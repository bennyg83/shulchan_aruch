(Collected note) But loss of earnings, etc. So too the Hagahot Ashri writes at the beginning of HaChovel, s.v. "u'Farach," that of the four primary categories in the Mishnah, apart from damage by the horn, payment is collected in Babylonia. The same applies to those listed by Rabbi Oshaya, apart from damage, as stated there concerning anything assessed as a slave, and apart from humiliation, which entails no monetary loss, as stated there; the same applies to pain. Of Rabbi Chiyya's categories, however, only three are collected: a thief, a robber, and conspiring witnesses whose liability is monetary, as stated there. The rest are penalties, as stated there regarding Rabbi Oshaya. In rape and seduction, although they include damage, pain, and humiliation, as stated there, we nevertheless do not collect them, as above. Regarding one who renders another's property impure, we rule that damage not physically apparent is not considered damage. Regarding conspiring witnesses, we rule like the first Tanna, specifically where the matter cannot be reversed, for then there is an actual monetary loss. Although Mahari Chaviv in Hagahot Ashri there was uncertain concerning fire and a pit, he himself writes in that author's name at the end of chapter five, s.v. "u'VaZeh HaZeman," that payment is collected, and so rule the Tur and Shulchan Aruch at the end of siman 410. Thus, of the thirty primary categories, together with an ox and a pit, a person and an ox, kofer, and the thirty shekalim for a slave, see Tosafot 5a, s.v. "leMa'utei," only fifteen are collected in Babylonia, including an informer; according to the Rav in the gloss, only thirteen are collected. (End of collected note.)
(Collected note) But loss of earnings, etc. This also appears from Hagahot Ashri at the beginning of HaChovel in the name of Rabbeinu Chananel. The wording there concerning humiliation contains a scribal error. He counts fifteen matters there, as explained above. This is also implied by the Gemara there, which says, "Therefore, a person who injures a person, etc.; humiliation, even though, etc." This indicates that the phrase "a person who injures a person" does not refer to the four additional payments. Likewise, when it says "these are damages of a person against a person," it refers only to damage itself. (End of collected note.)
(Collected note) But loss of earnings, etc. From the statement there, "anything assessed as a slave, etc.," Rava follows his own reasoning, which indicates specifically a matter subject to such assessment. Likewise there, when the Gemara answers, "When we perform their agency, it is in a matter that is fixed, etc.; a person against a person as well, go out and see, etc." Further, when it asks there, "and further, payment of double, etc.," it does not ask from loss of earnings and medical expenses. (End of collected note.)
