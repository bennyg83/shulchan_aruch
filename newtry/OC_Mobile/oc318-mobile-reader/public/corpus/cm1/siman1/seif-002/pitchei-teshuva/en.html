Regarding what he writes that, concerning the laws of fire and a pit in the present era, see Shevut Yaakov, I saw that Shevut Yaakov there cites the Mordechai and Hagahot Ashri at the beginning of HaChovel in the name of the Avi Ezri, who write simply that the laws of fire and a pit are adjudicated nowadays. Hagahot Ashri, however, writes in the name of Mahari'ach that he was uncertain whether payment for fire and a pit is collected in Babylonia. Maharshal there, siman 5, cited by the Shach in seif katan 2, therefore writes that it appears that these cases are not adjudicated in Babylonia because they are not common. His intent is that since Mahari'ach was uncertain, we rule leniently for the defendant. Shevut Yaakov writes that in his work Mishpetei Yaakov he proved that the laws of fire and a pit are adjudicated, and that this is also the implication of the Gemara in the chapter HaChovel. Moreover, the unqualified language of all the poskim, who record all the laws of fire and a pit without mentioning that they are not adjudicated nowadays as they do regarding damage caused by the horn, indicates that they are adjudicated. He adduces further proofs and concludes that it is clear that these laws of fire and a pit are adjudicated nowadays. Responsa Chavot Yair, siman 169, cited below at siman 378, seif 1, seif katan 1, likewise adjudicated a case involving fire that came before him. As for Maharshal's statement that such cases should not be adjudicated nowadays because the defendant might thereby come into danger to his person or property, since people may say that he was negligent, his words are plainly correct in places where such danger is a concern. Where there is no such concern, however, these cases are certainly adjudicated. Rabbi Akiva Eiger notes in his glosses to Shulchan Aruch: “See Tekfo Kohen, siman 40, and Responsa Shevut Yaakov, part I, siman 136. His proof from the incident in HaGozel, Bava Kamma 98b, where Rafram compelled Rav Ashi, is, with due respect, imprecise, for there the person burned the document with his own hands and was therefore a direct damager, and it is obvious that such a case is adjudicated nowadays. What connection does this have to his proof?” Nevertheless, one may say that Rabbi Akiva Eiger does not dispute Shevut Yaakov's principal ruling and only rejects that particular proof. Ketzot HaChoshen and Shaar Mishpat likewise agree that the law does not follow Maharshal, and that the laws of fire and a pit are adjudicated even nowadays. So too appears from the Tur and Shulchan Aruch below, siman 410, seif 37, from the words of the Raah in Sefer HaChinuch, mitzvot 53 and 56, and from Responsa Pnei Yehoshua, part II, siman 106.
