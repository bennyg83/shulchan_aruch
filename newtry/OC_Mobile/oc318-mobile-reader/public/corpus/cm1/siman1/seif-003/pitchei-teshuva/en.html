Shaar Mishpat is uncertain whether, if the litigants accepted judges upon themselves to adjudicate matters that are not ordinarily adjudicated nowadays, their acceptance is effective, just as in matters that are adjudicated nowadays acceptance is effective when they accept a relative or otherwise disqualified person, as in siman 22, or whether a distinction must be drawn. He discusses this at length, citing the Chiddushei HaRan at the beginning of Sanhedrin and Ramban on the Torah, Parashat Mishpatim, and concludes that the practical law requires study. See Beit Yehudah, who brings a proof that such acceptance is effective. See Shaar Mishpat further regarding whether, in cases of fines, the damager is obligated to pay in order to fulfill his obligation before Heaven, and see Ketzot HaChoshen on this matter.
