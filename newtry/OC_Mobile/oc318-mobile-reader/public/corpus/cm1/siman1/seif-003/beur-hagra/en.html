Or robbery. This follows from the discussion there and from the Rosh at the beginning of Sanhedrin. According to the Gemara's conclusion in Bava Kamma, where it says that injuries are uncommon, the implication is that other matters are common, including robbery. This means, as Tosafot explain there and elsewhere, that the 'robberies' mentioned in the Mishnah are robberies committed through violence, and are therefore included in the category of injuries. Tosafot note that in many places in the Talmud we find that the courts adjudicated cases of robbery. See the commentary of the Vilna Gaon on the source citations.

(Anthology) Some say, etc. Nevertheless, this does not resolve Tosafot's difficulty from Bava Kamma 96b, concerning the man who stole a field and was ordered to pay the increase in value. Rava initially understood that this was required by strict law. Several other passages are likewise difficult according to this view. Tosafot, however, explain that the 'robberies' in question arose through violence. The Rosh writes at the beginning of Sanhedrin that according to the Gemara's conclusion in Bava Kamma, robbery was removed from the list. The Tur and Shulchan Aruch likewise write at the beginning of siman 30 that the alternative view is strained, since it would render all the passages based on the Mishnah at the beginning of Sanhedrin and the Gemara at the end of Gittin contrary to the law.
