Or robbery. Bava Kamma says that robbery and bodily injury are uncommon and therefore unordained judges do not act as agents of the ordained judges regarding them. Yet throughout the Gemara we find courts adjudicating thieves and robbers. Tosafot therefore distinguishes robbery arising through bodily violence, which is uncommon and not adjudicated, from ordinary robbery, which is adjudicated. This is the intent of Rambam and Mechaber. Rama, in the alternative view, cites Beit Yosef's distinction between uncommon forcible snatching and common cases such as denial of a deposit. Sema adopts the first explanation. Shach objects that Beit Yosef did not cite that distinction in explaining Rambam and instead, in Kesef Mishneh, gives Tosafot's explanation. Shach understands Rambam differently: the statement that robbery is not adjudicated refers to doubled statutory payments, whereas the principal is collected everywhere. This explains Rambam's phrase that only the principal is collected. In Tumim I distinguish between the period when ordained judges still existed in Eretz Yisrael and our time. Then, unordained judges outside Eretz Yisrael did not adjudicate ordinary robbery. Now, when no ordained judges remain, ordinary robbery is adjudicated in order to preserve civil order. See there for the reasoning.
