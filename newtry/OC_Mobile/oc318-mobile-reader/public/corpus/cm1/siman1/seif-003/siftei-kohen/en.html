Likewise, before the owner's despair, when collection is made from the robber, the appreciation is not collected in Babylonia. So writes the Bach. In my humble opinion, however, the appreciation is also collected, and the ruling comes only to exclude the fine imposed upon a robber. This is why the Rambam first writes that laws of fines, such as robberies and injuries, are not adjudicated. One should not object that since he writes, 'such as robberies and injuries, and payments of double, fourfold, and fivefold,' it follows that 'robberies' does not refer to fourfold and fivefold payments, for otherwise why mention those payments again. One may answer that initially he refers to the fine arising from robbery, such as where one steals or robs from another's house in circumstances that obligate double, fourfold, or fivefold payment. He then mentions fourfold and fivefold payment generally, meaning, for example, one who falsely claims that a deposited article was stolen and swears to that effect, and similar cases.
Some say specifically robberies, etc. The Sema writes that the Mechaber holds that all robberies are adjudicated except robberies arising through bodily injury, in accordance with Tosafot in chapter HaChovel and at the beginning of Sanhedrin. The opening Mishnah of Sanhedrin deals with such a case, as is also evident from Tosafot there, contrary to Maharshal's commentary there. Therefore the Mechaber writes generally that all robberies are adjudicated. These are his words. This is difficult to me, for if so the Mechaber should have written explicitly, 'robberies arising through injury.' Furthermore, the Beit Yosef does not cite Tosafot's view on this matter. It therefore appears to me that the Mechaber follows the Rambam, who writes generally that all robberies are adjudicated. The statement in Gittin, chapter HaMegareish, that robberies and injuries are not adjudicated because they are uncommon, refers to the fine associated with robbery, such as double, fourfold, and fivefold payment, which is indeed uncommon. The Gemara could also have said that such cases involve no direct monetary loss, but it used the formulation because of injuries. This is precisely indicated by the Rambam's language in chapter 5 of Hilchot Sanhedrin: 'Laws of fines, such as robberies and injuries and double payment, are not adjudicated. One who stole or robbed is required by judges outside Eretz Yisrael to pay the principal, but the additional amount is not collected.' This resolves Maharshal's difficulty in chapter HaChovel, siman 5, where he wonders how the Rambam can first say that robberies and injuries are not adjudicated outside Eretz Yisrael and then say that one who stole or robbed must pay. It also appears to me that this is the view of the Rosh at the beginning of Sanhedrin and of the Tur, who write without qualification that all robberies are common and are adjudicated. This also appears to be the view of Rav Hai Gaon cited by the Tur at the end of siman 420, contrary to Maharshal, who says that in their view robberies arising through injuries are not adjudicated. The Mechaber therefore writes generally that one who stole or robbed is made to pay the principal, based on the Rambam cited in Beit Yosef. The Beit Yosef also implies that the Rosh and Tur agree. See Tur and Beit Yosef at the beginning of siman 30. This appears correct in practice, as Tosafot and other later authorities write, since courts adjudicate cases of robbery every day. The Bach's presentation of the Rosh's view is confused. He first writes that Rabbeinu Yerucham attributes to the Rosh the view of Tosafot that robberies arising through injuries are not adjudicated, and later writes that the Rosh follows the Rambam. In truth, one who examines Rabbeinu Yerucham will see that he writes this only in the name of Tosafot of the Rosh. See there.
