(Netivot HaMishpat, seif katan 5) The Shach himself proved, etc. Note: There is no difficulty. The Shach is speaking throughout of a case where the claimant seized without witnesses. Since the possessor has a valid claim, it is as though he produced witnesses. This is also clear from the Shach in siman 399 and from the Ramban in Sefer HaZechut, siman 27. The Ketzot HaChoshen writes that we have authority to adjudicate the matter. This requires study, because in truth we have no authority to adjudicate even after seizure. Our ruling is only that he must return whatever exceeds the amount he seized for his damage claim, as explained by the Rosh, Bava Kamma, chapter 1, ruling 20, cited by the Tur here. Nevertheless, the Ketzot's approach may be considered in light of the Rosh's explanation that seizure is effective because a person may execute judgment for himself. Since, in this case, he cannot recover through a beit din that lacks expert judges, perhaps his migo is effective even though he disclosed the seizure before such a beit din. Because a person may execute judgment for himself, he is, as it were, the judge in the matter. This is unlike the case in siman 72, seif 18, where he showed the property to witnesses outside beit din and later came to beit din. Here the beit din does not award him anything; rather, his seizure gives him the right to retain the property because the court does not intervene to remove it from him. Consequently, by exercising self-help he acquires the right from the moment of seizure.
