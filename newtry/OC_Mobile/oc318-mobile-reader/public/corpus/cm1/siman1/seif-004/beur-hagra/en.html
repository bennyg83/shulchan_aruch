The laws. Bava Kamma 115b and 117b.
(Collected note) The law of dina d'garmi, and likewise, etc. This is the Rambam. At first glance, it would seem that he follows his own view that one is liable by strict law, as written in siman 388, seif 2, that if the tortfeasor dies, the heirs must pay. Yet no one disputes this point. Proof is from Rafram, who compelled payment, etc., and likewise regarding an informer at the end of Bava Kamma, in the incident before Rav Nachman where he held him liable, and there in the incident before Rav Ashi. (End of collected note.)
Witnesses, etc. This is also for the same reason, there, and on 2a; Tosafot 4b, s.v. "Edim."
(Collected note) Witnesses, etc. This is unlike Rabbi Akiva. For in Makkot 3a, the Gemara is forced, according to Rabbi Akiva, to establish the case specifically where he says, "and you caused me to become liable for money," and it does not establish it in such a case. Several other sugyot also prove this. The Rashba, however, in Torat HaBayit, siman 288, rules like Rabbi Akiva, because Rabbah and the Ran explain his reasoning in Makkot there, and the Gemara says, "Know that this is so," indicating that the matter is clear; see there. (End of collected note.)
