And they were discredited, etc. This applies specifically to plotting witnesses whose testimony is disproved through hazamah. The Torah decreed that the later witnesses are believed more than the first even though the contradiction concerns the persons of the witnesses. But where the later testimony merely contradicts the substance of the first testimony, the first witnesses cannot be made liable monetarily, because they can say that they testified truthfully and there is no basis to prefer the second pair. If the first testimony had not yet caused payment, the liability of plotting witnesses is a statutory fine and is not adjudicated today. If they already caused actual loss, however, they can be liable under the law of direct consequential damage. Sema.
