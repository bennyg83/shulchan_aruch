They become liable only where money was actually paid out on the basis of their testimony, and not merely because they plotted to cause such a payment, since the latter liability is a fine. The text says that they were shown to be plotting witnesses, rather than merely contradicted, because where witnesses are merely contradicted, even if money was paid on their testimony, they are not made liable. Why should the later witnesses be believed more than the first? This is unlike a case of hazamah, as explained below in simanim 31 and 38, and in siman 46, seif 37. It appears clear that the Rama did not write at the beginning, "witnesses who testified," etc., merely to explain the laws of indirect causation, for those laws are independently set out in siman 386. Rather, the Rama stated this as a distinct rule. Thus concludes the Sema. However, this is somewhat difficult, for in siman 38 the Rama writes that the law of plotting witnesses is not adjudicated today because it is a fine. One may answer that here the case is where money was actually paid out on their testimony, in which event their liability is monetary rather than penal, as stated above in the name of the Sema.
