Likewise, if he seized, etc. See Shach, who writes that one who falsely claims a deposit was stolen is not liable to double payment before us because we are not an ordained beit din. At first glance this is difficult. If so, plotting witnesses should likewise not be liable to lashes or to 'as they plotted' even if experts later arise, because their testimony was not given before an ordained court, and they should not even become formally disqualified except rabbinically. Rather, in matters in which we act as agents of the earlier courts, our proceedings are treated as though they occurred before an ordained beit din. The special case of one who claims theft concerning a deposit is different. Liability for double payment requires that the oath itself be taken before beit din, as is evident from Bava Kamma. Although during adjudication we are treated as an ordained court for matters entrusted to us, at the time he takes the oath for the purpose of the penalty we are not acting as agents with respect to that penalty, because we do not adjudicate it. Therefore the oath is not one before a qualifying beit din for purposes of double payment.
