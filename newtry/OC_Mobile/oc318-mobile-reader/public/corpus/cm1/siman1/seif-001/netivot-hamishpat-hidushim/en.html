In the present time. The Sema writes in the name of the Tur that the reason the decisors were precise in writing "in the present time" is derived from the verse, "which you shall place before them." The Sages expounded "before judges," as written in that section, meaning ordained judges. Since we are lay judges, by Torah law we do not adjudicate these matters in the present time. Rather, we act as agents of the earlier ordained judges, and the conclusion is that we exercise their agency only in matters that are common and involve monetary loss:
And there is monetary loss. The same applies to inheritances and gifts which, according to the claimant's assertion, involve monetary loss. Only in monetary matters is monetary loss required. In other matters, everything depends on whether the matter is common. Therefore, we accept and compel a get in the present time, since such cases are common, Sema:
For example, an animal that injured. "Injury" here means damage by the horn, which is not its ordinary conduct and is not done for its benefit. By contrast, damage by tooth or foot is not termed injury but ordinary damage, and it is adjudicated in the present time, Sema. See the Shach, who holds that the same exclusion applies to fire and a pit, which are not adjudicated:
And likewise all fines. This is borrowed terminology. Matters that are not adjudicated in the present time because they are uncommon and do not involve monetary loss are called "fines," Sema:
Ordained judges in Eretz Yisrael. Ordination means that he is called Rabbi and is given authority to judge. The chain must be one person from another back to Yehoshua, who was ordained by Moshe.
