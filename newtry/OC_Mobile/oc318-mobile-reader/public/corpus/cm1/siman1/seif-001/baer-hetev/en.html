In the present era. The Sema writes in the name of the Tur that the reason the poskim carefully write "in the present era" is derived from the verse, "which you shall place before them." Chazal expounded that this means before the judges mentioned in the passage, namely ordained judges. We, however, are ordinary judges. Therefore, by Torah law we do not adjudicate these matters in the present era, but act only as agents of the earlier ordained courts. The conclusion is that we exercise their agency only in matters that are common and involve monetary loss.
Inheritances and gifts. According to the claimant's position these involve monetary loss. The same applies to other major matters, for converts are accepted in the present era even though this is uncommon; likewise, we find that courts compelled the giving of gittin in Babylonia even though there is no monetary loss, as the Ran and Beit Yosef write. Thus the Sema. The Ramban and Rashba questioned how converts may be accepted in the present era: are the present courts sufficiently authoritative to permit a convert to marry a Jewish woman? This is unlike admissions and loans, where we rule that property may be declared ownerless by beit din, and unlike a get, where Chazal said that anyone who betroths does so subject to rabbinic authority. They answer based on the statement in Keritot that a convert requires presentation of sacrificial blood, etc. See Responsa Bnei Shmuel, siman 1, where this is discussed at length.
That injured. The Mechaber calls a derivative of keren, where the act is neither normal behavior nor done for the animal's benefit, an "injury." This is not limited to an animal injuring another animal, but includes any damage done in an abnormal manner. What is normal behavior or done for its benefit is not termed an injury, but simply damage. This is why seif 3 states that where an animal damages with tooth or foot, etc., judges outside Eretz Yisrael collect payment. This applies even where it injured another animal, for the Gemara states explicitly that an ox damaging an ox is adjudicated when it occurs in its normal manner. Thus the Sema. Regarding the laws of fire and pit in the present era, see Responsa Shevut Yaakov, vol. 2, siman 136.
Double payment. The Sema writes that this requires study, since double payment is in any event not collected in Babylonia because it is a penalty. One can answer that the reason embarrassment damages and bodily damages are not collected is likewise that Chazal gave them the law of a penalty, because the case is uncommon or lacks monetary loss. Therefore the Mechaber mentions matters that are actual penalties as the straightforward case, and the same applies wherever Chazal assigned the law of a penalty. Maharshal writes that even in the language of the Gemara an uncommon payment is called a penalty, as it says regarding embarrassment, "Are you collecting penalties in Babylonia?" See there. Shach.
As one who struck. In siman 420, seif 41, the Mechaber writes that if one struck another with his palm he pays a sela, etc. Rashi in Bava Kamma 84b implies that a payment is not called a penalty unless its amount is fixed. Nevertheless, any claim that is uncommon and lacks monetary loss is not collected in our time, and Chazal called it a penalty because they gave it the law of a penalty for this purpose, namely that it is not collected now. Sema.
More. Such as double payment, fourfold and fivefold payment, or the additional fifth paid by one who stole and swore falsely. Sema.
Experts. Meaning a person experienced and proficient in the laws.
Ordained judges. Rambam, chapter 4 of Hilchot Sanhedrin, explains that the term semichah derives from the verse concerning Moshe and Yehoshua, "and he laid his hands upon him." Semichah in later generations, however, does not involve laying on hands. Rather, they call him "Rabbi" and tell him, "You are hereby ordained and have permission to adjudicate even laws of penalties." See Yoreh Deah, siman 242, seif 14, concerning the semichah practiced by Ashkenazim. Thus the Sema. The Shach writes that it is obvious this semichah is effective only for chalitzah and gittin, but not for laws of penalties.
Money. For this is a halacha transmitted to Moshe at Sinai. See below, siman 390, seif 3.
