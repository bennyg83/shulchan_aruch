In the Tur. The meaning is not that the world was created for the sake of three things, etc. It appears that these words should be explained as follows. Certainly, one of those two mishnayot speaks of the creation of the world and the other of its continued existence. At first glance, however, there is no indication which should be understood in which way, since the expressions “existence” and “stands” are equally capable of either meaning. Rabbeinu therefore proved that the mishna of Rabban Shimon ben Gamliel concerns the world's continued existence, whereas the mishna of Shimon HaTzaddik concerns its creation, because Rabbeinu HaKadosh placed the words of Shimon HaTzaddik at the beginning of the chapter. Rabbeinu then explained how the tanna's intention can be known, writing that this understanding is inherently reasonable. One must know that the things for whose sake the world was created had to exist at the time of creation. That is, when Hashem created the primary thing, He also created the other things attendant upon it, since that primary thing could not be created alone. We cannot say that the primary purposes did not yet exist at creation but would arise only later, such as justice, truth, and peace. Perhaps circumstances would never arise that required judgment, since human beings possess free choice and it is possible for the world to exist without litigation. How, then, could one say that the world was created for the sake of judgment? It must therefore be Torah, the Temple service, and acts of kindness for whose sake the rest of the world was created, and these existed at that time. Regarding Torah, he cites, “Hashem acquired me at the beginning of His way.” He then explains, “The Torah said,” meaning that the verse is interpreted thus: Hashem acquired me, for I, the Torah, am the principal possession of the Holy One, blessed is He. The proof is that I am “the beginning of His way.” Thus the Torah says: I was created before all other creations, which proves that all the others were created for my sake. The Temple service also existed at the creation of the world, namely, the site of the Temple upon which offerings would later be brought. Consequently, the Holy One, blessed is He, had to create the rest of the world as well. Should one object that only Israel, and not the nations, would perform the service, so that the nations have no connection to the place of the service, the answer is that Hashem chose Israel from among all the nations. The nations are therefore ancillary to Israel, for whose sake they were created.
