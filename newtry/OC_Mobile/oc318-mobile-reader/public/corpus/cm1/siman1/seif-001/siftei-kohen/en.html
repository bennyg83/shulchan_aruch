An animal that caused injury, etc. The same applies to damages caused by fire and by a pit. Maharshal, Yam Shel Shlomo, chapter 8, siman 5; see also Hagahot Ashri, chapter HaKones.
Such as payment of double, etc. Gloss: See Sema, seif katan 5, through the words, 'One may answer,' where he writes that the Sages assigned the law of a fine to something uncommon or to a case involving no monetary loss. Maharshal likewise writes in Yam Shel Shlomo there, siman 6, that in the language of the Gemara something uncommon is also called a fine. He proves this from the Gemara's statement, 'Do you collect the fine for humiliation in Babylonia?' See there. This is easily understood.
Those who are ordained. See Rivash, end of responsum 271, who discusses the form of ordination practiced among Ashkenazim. It is nevertheless obvious that this ordination is ineffective nowadays for adjudicating laws of fines. It is effective only for chalitzah and gittin, because it is not ordination for purposes of the laws of fines, as the Rambam writes in chapter 4 of Hilchot Sanhedrin. See there.
