It is further stated there. The wording “but medical expenses and loss of work are adjudicated” implies that depreciation is not adjudicated, and depreciation means bodily damage. This is difficult, because in the chapter HaChovel, Bava Kamma 84b, the Gemara asks that depreciation and humiliation are common, and therefore the judges of Babylonia should act as the agents of the ordained judges. It answers that this is indeed so, for Rav Pappa collected humiliation damages. The Gemara then challenges Rav Pappa's ruling from Rav Chisda and concludes that the judges of Babylonia act as agents only in a common matter that also entails a monetary loss. Humiliation, which does not entail a monetary loss, is therefore not adjudicated. This implies that regarding depreciation the questioner's assumption remains, namely, that it is common and is adjudicated. It appears that the word “depreciation” in the Gemara is a scribal error, and that the question was asked only from humiliation. Depreciation is certainly uncommon. This is also evident, for if the question included depreciation, how did the questioner know that it was not adjudicated, seeing that he himself considered it common? Regarding humiliation, however, the questioner relied upon the challenge subsequently brought from Rav Chisda. No such source exists regarding depreciation. It is therefore certainly a textual error, and this appears clear to me.
