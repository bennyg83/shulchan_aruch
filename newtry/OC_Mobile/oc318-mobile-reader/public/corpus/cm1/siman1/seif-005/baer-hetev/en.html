Amount. It appears that this does not mean the entire amount of his damages, but an amount close to that assessment. Sema.
Seized. This refers to seizure under Jewish law, but not through non-Jewish courts. Shach in the name of Maharshal. See below, siman 4, in the gloss.
From his possession. The Sema writes that even if he seized more than the amount of his actual loss, such as double, fourfold, or fivefold payment that would have been due had the matter been adjudicated, his seizure is effective. We also rule like the Rosh that whether he seized at the time of the damage or afterward, and whether he seized the damaging object itself or another object, the seizure is valid. The Mordechai, however, rules regarding keren damages in accordance with Rabbeinu Tam, that if he did not seize the damaging object itself the seizure is ineffective; regarding other penalties he agrees with the Rosh. The Mechaber and Rama do not state that an admission to a penalty makes seizure ineffective because we rule like the Ramban that in the present era a person is not exempted from a penalty by his own admission, even where witnesses have not yet come, as the Mechaber writes below in siman 348. The Shach writes that the same applies to a custodian who claimed that the deposit was stolen but was himself the thief. Since he pays double only after swearing before beit din, and we do not have an expert ordained beit din, he never became liable for double payment; therefore, even if the owner seized property, it is removed from him. So writes the Ramban in Sefer HaMilchamot. This case is worse than other penalties. With an ordinary thief, the act of theft itself creates the double-payment liability, and the only obstacle is that our courts are unauthorized to adjudicate penalties even though the legal liability is known. A custodian, by contrast, does not incur double payment until he swears before ordained judges. Therefore seizure is ineffective. See the Taz on this siman for his resolution of the Ramban's objection to the Raavad.
He may say. Maharshal rules like the Rif that in every matter that is monetary rather than a penalty, but is merely uncommon, the court hears his claim. Shach.
Return it. The Rosh and Tur conclude: if the person who seized cannot produce witnesses by whose testimony he would be entitled to prevail, he is instructed to return what he seized. Rama explains that this applies only where he seized in the presence of witnesses. If he seized without witnesses, he is believed through a migo that he could have denied seizing at all. See below, siman 87, seif 30, regarding monetary claims. A migo is also effective regarding a claim for a penalty, as the Mordechai writes at the end of HaManiach and Rama writes in siman 399, seif 4. Thus the Sema. The Shach writes that the Mordechai is not proof, since perhaps he holds that the sugya follows the opinion that half-damages are monetary compensation rather than a penalty. See there.
Penalties. The Mordechai writes that although penalties are not collected in the present era, they may nevertheless be adjudicated by the seven communal trustees. Sema.
They collect. The rulings of Mahara'i, siman 64, imply that one who refuses to obey a judicial ruling may be fined a sum payable to the king. See below, siman 388, seif 5. Sema.
