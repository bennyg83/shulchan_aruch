They excommunicate him. This applies specifically to one who physically injures another and similar cases. He is excommunicated, even though there is no additional monetary liability, because otherwise anyone could injure or damage his fellow. It is not imposed, however, on account of double payment, fourfold payment, or fivefold payment. So rules the Sema in seif katan 11, and so rules Maharshal in siman 6; see there.
Likewise, if the injured party seized. This means through Jewish law, but not through gentile courts. Maharshal, Yam Shel Shlomo, chapter 1, siman 43.
We do not remove it from his possession, etc. Even if the liable party admitted before the injured party seized, the seizure is effective, because nowadays his admission does not exempt him, even if witnesses never came. So writes the Tur in the name of the Ramban, and he rules without qualification in accordance with this below, siman 348. The Sema likewise writes this in seif katan 18. In the case of a custodian who claims that the deposited item was stolen, when in fact he himself stole it, since he is liable for double payment only if he swore before beit din, and we do not have an expert beit din, he never became liable for double payment. Therefore, even if the owner seized, we remove it from him. Ramban, Sefer HaMilchamot, chapter HaGozel Etzim, cited also by the Bach.
If the injured party says, etc. Maharshal rules in accordance with the Rif that whenever the obligation is monetary and not a fine, but the case is merely uncommon, we heed the injured party, because seizure is a relatively minor matter. See siman 2 there.
You shall retain such and such an amount, etc. See what I wrote concerning the Sema, seif katan 19. He writes that a migo is effective even with respect to a claim for a fine, as the Tur writes below in siman 399. The Ramah cited by the Tur there disagrees and holds that a migo is ineffective regarding a fine. Nimukei Yosef, at the end of chapter HaManiach, writes likewise. What the Sema writes, that Mordechai at the end of chapter HaManiach also says so, is no proof, for perhaps Mordechai holds that the sugya follows the opinion that half damages constitute monetary liability, as Nimukei Yosef writes there, and as Maggid Mishneh writes at the end of chapter 9 of Hilchot Nizkei Mamon.
