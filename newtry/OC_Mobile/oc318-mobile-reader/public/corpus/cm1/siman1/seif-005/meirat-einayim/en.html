Once he gives him the amount appropriate to him, they release him. This is Rambam's wording at the end of chapter 5 of Hilchot Sanhedrin. Rambam adds that they release the offender whether or not the injured party has actually been appeased. It appears that the Mechaber's phrase 'the amount appropriate to him' does not necessarily mean the full value of the damage, but approximately that amount, as the Tur writes in the name of Rav Sherira and the Rosh. Rambam follows Rif's wording concerning the yeshivah practice, and in the Derishah I proved that Rif too means approximately the amount. This explains why Rambam had to add that the ban is lifted even if the injured party is not appeased, meaning even if he insists on receiving the entire damage. It also explains the conclusion that if the injured party seized property, it is not removed from him. The novelty is that even if he seized the entire amount to which he would have been entitled had the fine been judicially collectible, his seizure stands. The Tur cites the Rosh that only anything beyond the proper amount is returned. The phrase 'what is appropriate for him to receive' may even include double, fourfold, or fivefold payment where applicable, because if he seized such sums they are not removed. Thus the word 'likewise' means: although contemporary judges do not themselves collect fines, the injured party has one remedy through a ban upon the offender until he provides appropriate compensation, and another remedy where the injured party has already seized property.
It is not removed from his hand. Meaning, even if he seized more than the amount of his direct damage, where he would have been entitled to that greater amount had the fine been adjudicated, such as double, fourfold, or fivefold payment, the seizure stands. The Rosh and Tur write likewise. This also follows from Rama below in siman 348, seif 4. Ir Shushan writes that even without seizure, when the offender is placed under a ban until he appeases the injured party, he is not released until he pays close to double, fourfold, or fivefold payment. That is incorrect. Siman 349 makes clear that absent seizure no authority requires remaining under a ban until paying more than the actual damage. Chazal imposed the ban in ordinary personal-injury fines and similar matters because otherwise people would injure one another with impunity, not to compel double, fourfold, or fivefold theft payments, since a thief knows he must at least restore what he stole. We also rule like the Rosh that seizure is effective whether at the time of the damage or afterward, whether of the damaging object itself or another object. The Mechaber and Rama therefore state generally that seizure helps. Mordechai, however, cites Rabbeinu Baruch that for horn damage we follow Rabbeinu Tam and for other fines the dissenting authorities. The Mechaber also omitted any rule that an admission to a fine defeats subsequent seizure, because we follow Ramban that nowadays such an admission does not exempt him even if no witnesses have yet come, as the Tur rules below in siman 348.
And this much you must return. This disagrees with Mahari Weil, siman 28, as explained in Darkei Moshe. The Rosh and Tur conclude that if the one who seized cannot produce witnesses showing that he was entitled to the amount, he is ordered to return what he seized. Rama explains that this applies only where he seized in the presence of witnesses. If he seized without witnesses, he is believed through a migo that he could have denied seizing at all. This follows the rule explained below at the end of siman 87, seif 30, concerning monetary claims. A migo is likewise effective in a claim for a fine, as the Mordechai and Tur write and Rama rules below.
Regarding the fines written in the Torah. Mordechai, chapter HaSholeach, writes in the name of Rabbeinu Yitzchak that although ordinary courts do not collect fines nowadays, the seven good men of the city may adjudicate them. See siman 2.
But fines, etc. Piskei Mahari, siman 64, implies that one who refuses to obey a Torah court may be fined by making him pay a penalty to the king. Darkei Moshe cites this at the end of siman 3. See also siman 388, seif 5, in the Sema.
