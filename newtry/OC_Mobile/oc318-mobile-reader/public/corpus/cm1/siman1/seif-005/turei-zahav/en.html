"Such as admissions and loans," meaning, etc. Our master needed to cite this explanation even though it is not his usual practice to cite an explanation of the Gemara unless there is a practical difference, because another explanation exists. According to that explanation, there is no testimony: the claimant makes an oral claim, the defendant admits part of it, which is "admissions," or denies everything, which is "loans." Rashi cites this explanation and rejects it because of the wording, since in both cases the matter concerns loans. It appears obvious that according to those "some say" cited by Rashi, who explain "loans" as complete denial, the meaning is that the claimant intends to bring witnesses afterward. If one were to explain that he has no witnesses and merely seeks a heset oath, as below in siman 24, the defendant is exempt by Torah law, and there would be no relevance to requiring expert judges, since the entire law is only a rabbinic enactment. Indeed, our master writes explicitly in Yoreh Deah in the cited siman, in the name of the Rosh, that rabbinic enactments do not require expert judges at all. Necessarily, then, the case concerns witnesses. Rashi therefore had to reject the interpretation because of the wording. Our master, however, wished to prevent the mistaken conclusion that complete denial without witnesses, merely for purposes of heset, requires experts because it is rabbinic. He therefore cited the explanation of "admissions and loans" that does not follow that approach.
