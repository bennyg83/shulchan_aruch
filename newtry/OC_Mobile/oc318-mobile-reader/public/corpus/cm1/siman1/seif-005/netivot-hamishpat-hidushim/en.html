And once he gives. Meaning, he need not pay according to the full amount of the damage, but only according to the judgment of beit din and their assessment of an amount close to the damage. Therefore he must pay that amount. Accordingly, the Rambam states, and on this basis the Sema emends the Shulchan Aruch to read, "whether the injured party was appeased," and so forth, because he need not pay the entire damage. If he were required by Torah law to pay it all, why would payment depend on whether the injured party was appeased? See the Rosh and Tur, who explain that this is only by virtue of an enactment of the Geonim and not at all by the law of the Gemara. It therefore appears that if the tortfeasor died, his son is not placed under ban, because they did not fine the son after him. The entire enactment was intended to prevent violent people and tortfeasors from proliferating. It is sufficient to penalize the wrongdoer himself, but his son, who had no hand in the wrongdoing, should not be fined. We should not add to the enactment, Urim VeTumim:
And likewise if he seized. This applies even if he did not seize the very object that caused the damage, but seized another item. However, regarding a tam animal, which pays only from its body, the Mordechai rules in accordance with Rabbeinu Tam that he may seize only the damaging animal, since that animal itself is encumbered, and not some other item. Likewise, one may seize for payment of double, fourfold, and fivefold fines. But if he did not seize and the wrongdoer was merely placed under ban, the ban is released when he pays an amount close to the principal damage. The Maharshal writes that one may not seize through a non Jew. His words do not clarify whether seizure through a non Jew is effective after the fact. From his possession. Even with respect to double, fourfold, and fivefold payment, seizure is effective, and even seizure of another item is effective. If one admits liability for a fine in the present time, then if he admits that witnesses were present at the time of the damage he remains liable and is not exempted by his admission. But if there were no witnesses at all, he is exempt. See Beurim, seif katan 5. A custodian who claims that the item was stolen but was himself the thief incurs double payment only if he swore before beit din. Since we do not have such a beit din, he never becomes liable for double payment, and even if the claimant seized, we remove it from him, Shach and Beurim. Where seizure is effective and the tortfeasor later seizes back from the claimant what the claimant had seized, we likewise do not remove it from the tortfeasor. Seizure from orphans is ineffective.
