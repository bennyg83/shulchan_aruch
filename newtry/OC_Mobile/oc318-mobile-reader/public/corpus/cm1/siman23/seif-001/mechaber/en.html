Until When a Judge Is Believed to Say Which Party He Favored. One seif. As long as the litigants remain before the judge, he is believed to say, 'I held this one liable and ruled for that one.' After they leave, he is not believed if they do not possess a written ruling. This applies specifically to a matter entrusted to judicial discretion. In a case determined by the parties' claims, the judge's special credibility is irrelevant: the party entitled under the claims prevails, provided that he does not contradict his original claims. All this applies to one judge. Two judges are always believed, even against two witnesses who contradict them. {Rama: The same applies when the parties accepted a compromiser for a set period: whatever he says during that period is believed. Once he has written and delivered the compromise, however, he may neither add nor subtract, even during the period, despite claiming that this was his original intent (Beit Yosef in the name of the Rashba). See also siman 80.}
