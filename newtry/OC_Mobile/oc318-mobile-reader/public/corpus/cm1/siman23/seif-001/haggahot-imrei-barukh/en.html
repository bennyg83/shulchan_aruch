(Siman 23, Mechaber, seif 1) Even if there are two witnesses. Note: See Responsa Rosh, klal 56, ruling 6. It appears there that the witnesses were not present when the claims were made, but only at the conclusion of the judgment. One can therefore say that this is why the judges were believed more than the witnesses, for the reason the Rosh gives there: 'This is not like two sets of witnesses contradicting one another. There, both sets heard the matter, one saying, "This is what we heard or saw," and the other saying, "This is what we heard or saw." Here, however, the judges heard the litigants' words and carefully examined them to determine where the law inclined. They therefore know and remember the truth of the ruling better than witnesses who heard it only once, and we attribute the error to the witnesses,' end of his words. In the question there it likewise states that two witnesses were present at the conclusion of the judgment, and afterward the matter emerged written and signed in one manner, while the two arbitrators stated it differently. It therefore requires study in a case where the witnesses were also present for the claims and say that they applied themselves to hear everything from beginning to end, yet they contradict the judges. If even then the judges are believed more because they were required to scrutinize the litigants' words, then in every case of two sets of witnesses contradicting one another, where one set was formally summoned for the testimony and the second happened to stand there and observe, the summoned witnesses should be believed more. The Bach addresses the point I raised at the end and distinguishes between the cases by a rationale that is open to question.
