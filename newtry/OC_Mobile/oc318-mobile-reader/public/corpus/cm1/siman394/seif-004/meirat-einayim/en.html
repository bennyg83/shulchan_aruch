They assess what it damaged as part of sixty, etc. This is learned from the verse "and it shall consume in another's field." The Torah could have said simply, "and it shall consume another field." Chazal therefore expound that the consumption is assessed as part of another field. We do not assess the value of the single se'ah that was eaten by itself, because then it would be valued very highly and the damager would lose excessively. Rather, we consider a person buying sixty times the quantity damaged. For example, if those sixty units were worth sixty gold coins, we ask how much less the buyer would pay because one se'ah was eaten. The ordinary buyer would not reduce the price by the entire stand-alone value of that se'ah. Nor do we assess the eaten se'ah as part of the entire field, because then the buyer would reduce the total by only a small amount and the injured party would lose too much. Chazal therefore fixed an intermediate measure, assessing it together with sixty times the amount damaged. Although this is derived from "and it shall consume in another's field," we also derive from that phrase that liability applies specifically where the damage occurred in the injured party's domain, excluding the public domain. The Torah therefore wrote this language by "and it shall consume" and not merely in the payment clause, "he shall pay from the best of his field." See Derishah.
Likewise, if it ate a kav, etc. We assess it as part of sixty times the amount eaten. This excludes the opinion that even one stalk is always assessed as part of a se'ah within sixty se'ah. See Perishah.
