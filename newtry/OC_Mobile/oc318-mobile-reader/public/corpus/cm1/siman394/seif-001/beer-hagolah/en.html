a) The language of the Rambam, chapter 3 of Hilkhot Nizkei Mamon, law 11, from the Mishnah at the beginning of the sixth chapter of Bava Kamma 55a.
bet) As Rav Kahana explains on 58a.
gimel) As the Gemara explains that Rav stated one case and certainly the more obvious one.
dalet) As Rabbi Yochanan says there.
heh) Ibid., in the Mishnah.
vav) Rabbi Yirmiyah's question on 58a. According to the view that initial negligence followed by an unavoidable accident is exempt, the question arises, but according to the view that such a person is liable it is obvious that he is liable, and we follow that latter view. Rashi explains that he should have guarded the animal so that it would not descend.
zayin) The ruling follows Rav Kahana. The Maggid Mishneh writes that the Rif rules so, and the Beit Yosef writes that the plain Gemara supports them.
chet) They rule like Rava, who explains the Mishnah's case of an animal falling into a garden as one where other animals pushed it. The meaning is that the owner is exempt from paying for the damage the animal caused, but must pay for the benefit it received. This is straightforward in the Gemara. The Rosh writes likewise in the name of Tosafot. This is unlike the incident of Ada Sabolaah in Bava Metzia 93b, cited above in siman 303, seif 11, where liability was imposed not because of negligence but because he was a paid guardian, and such a loss is treated like theft or loss. Just as the owner is exempt when another animal pushed it, so too his paid guardian is exempt, for the Mishnah in the fourth chapter of Bava Kamma at the end of 44b teaches that when the owner transferred the animal to a guardian, all guardians enter in place of the owner.
