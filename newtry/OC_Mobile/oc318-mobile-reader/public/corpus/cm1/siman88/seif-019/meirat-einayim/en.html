You have a deposit of mine in your possession, etc. This excludes the Raavad, whose opinion the Tur cites, that such a case is like one who claims wheat and receives an admission of barley. The Tur further cites the Raavad's even broader view that if the claimant demands both categories and the defendant admits one, he is exempt from a Torah oath because the legal character of a loan or borrowed item, for which one is liable even for unavoidable accidents, differs from a deposit or hired property, for which one is exempt from accidents. The Rambam, however, holds that even if only one was claimed, since both are monetary claims and liability for theft and loss applies in both, an admission of one is a partial admission of the same general type of claim. He therefore pays what he admitted and takes a Torah oath concerning the remainder.
