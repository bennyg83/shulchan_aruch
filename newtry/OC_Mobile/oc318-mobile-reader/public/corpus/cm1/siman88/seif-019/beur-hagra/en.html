A maneh, etc.; this is, etc. This is unlike Raavad, who treats a claim of a loan and an admission of a deposit as wheat and barley. Rambam holds that the Bava Kamma cases are not identical for oath purposes. In the mishnah there, the obligations arise from distinct animals and distinct causes of liability, just as one who claims a large slave and receives an admission concerning a small slave is not partially admitted upon the same claim. But a maneh claimed as a loan and admitted as a deposit is still the same monetary object and the same debtor-creditor relationship. Rambam's proof is explained below.
The same applies to borrowing and rental. This rejects Raavad's proof from Bava Metzia 98a. The cases there are arranged so that the admission and denial relate to separate animals and separate forms of liability. The other authorities answer that a deposited item still in existence is considered 'here it is,' and therefore no oath arises. The later discussion, where one says the animal was borrowed and the other says it was rented, asks not because this is automatically wheat and barley, but because on the defendant's version he owes nothing. The Gemara's answer that he must swear that it died naturally supports Rambam: if the mere difference between borrowing and rental were wheat and barley, even an admission of negligence would not create partial admission.
