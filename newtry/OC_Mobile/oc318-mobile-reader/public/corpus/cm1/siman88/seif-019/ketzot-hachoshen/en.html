Borrowing and renting. Raavad regards "borrowed" and "rented" as different species of claim. Even if the claimant asserts both and the defendant admits one, the claims are legally distinct because borrower liability includes unavoidable accident while renter liability does not. Ramban asks from the Mishnah in Perek HaShoel: where the owner says the animal died while borrowed and the custodian says it died while rented, the custodian must swear. The Gemara asks why, since what was claimed was not admitted, and Ulla answers that the oath comes through oath extension because the owner can at least require an oath that it died naturally. If borrowed and rented are wheat and barley, the custodian should be exempt even from the renter claim. The answer can be that the owner frames his demand from the outset in the alternative: "I say it died while borrowed, but according to your account swear at least that it died naturally while rented." Since he expressly asserts the renter position as a fallback, there is no waiver of that claim. The same language of the Gemara supports this. It says, "He says to him, swear to me at least that it died in its ordinary manner." A custodial oath ordinarily does not require the owner to demand it explicitly. The special formulation is needed because without the fallback demand the renter theory would have been excluded as a different claim. Rashba offers another distinction. Where the parties dispute the legal characterization of the same identified object, such as this exact ox or this exact sum, the claims can be treated as the same subject even if one calls it loan and the other deposit. The wheat and barley rule applies more strongly where the claimed objects themselves are different. This also explains several apparent contradictions.
