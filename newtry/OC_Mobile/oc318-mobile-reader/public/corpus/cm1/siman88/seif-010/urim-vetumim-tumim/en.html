And there is one who disagrees and says that the law of a deposit, etc. I am astonished that the Mechaber cites the Ran's view as the dissenting opinion, for he stands alone against the Rosh. Rambam also appears to hold otherwise, as the Baal HaTerumot writes, and this is likewise the view of the Baal HaTerumot, Ramban, Rashba, and Tur. This also appears to be the view reflected in the alternate reading cited in Gittin 13, s.v. it sefarim, which explains Rav's statement that if one says simply "a maneh," we are concerned that he may mean a buried maneh, as referring to a deposit. If so, it necessarily follows that even regarding a deposit, when one speaks without qualification, he does not mean an equivalent value. Otherwise, how could we even entertain the possibility of a buried maneh? He did not designate any particular maneh, as Rashba and the Ran himself write, that the concern for a buried maneh proves that the speaker specifically means that actual maneh and not merely its value. According to that alternate reading, this rule therefore applies even to a deposit. See Rashba's novellae to Gittin, where he writes that this was Rabbenu Yitzchak's reading, and it is clear that it does not accord with the Ran. Nevertheless, it appears that even those who disagree do so only with respect to an ordinary householder, whose law is unlike that of a money changer and who is prohibited from using deposited money even when it was left loose. In such a case one may say that the depositor intended specifically the money that he gave, which must be returned intact. In our time, however, when we have the status of money changers and use of such money is permitted, the depositor certainly intends equivalent value even in the case of a deposit, for the bailee was permitted to spend it and owes only its replacement, as with a loan. All the more so according to the Shach's view above in siman 73, that even if the deposited money remains intact when payment is demanded, the bailee may nevertheless exchange it. See there. If so, the depositor certainly intends equivalent value, and even the Rosh would agree in such a case.
