That which we say, etc.; some say specifically, etc. Gittin 13a discusses a deathly ill person who says, 'Give a maneh to so-and-so.' The Gemara's discussion whether one must suspect that the maneh is buried shows that he meant an actual maneh, not merely value equivalent to a maneh, even though the concern is ultimately rejected. See Ran and Tosafot there.
And there is one who disagrees, etc. This authority follows Rashi and Tosafot's reading of the conclusion and therefore maintains that the expression 'a maneh' means an actual maneh. Ran likewise writes that in a gift the wording is specific. See below, siman 253:19. A deposit claim cannot necessarily be inferred from a gift, however, because a claimant may be demanding equivalent value. See Ran in the chapter HaDayyanim.
