[Shach, letter 53] And so writes Nimukei Yosef in the first chapter of Bava Metzia in the name of the Ramban. Note: There the discussion concerns one who says “two,” where according to that answer he is exempt from an oath because the document supports him. The Ramban writes regarding this that he nevertheless owes a heset oath, while the Rashba and Ran reject his proof. The Shach therefore should not have cited this source regarding one who says “three,” where, according to the Beit Yosef, the document supports him. Perhaps the Shach means that if one who says “two” is exempt even from a heset oath, then one who says “three” is also exempt from a heset oath by a migo that he could have said “two.” Yet the Mechaber gives as the reason for exemption in the “two” case that the defendant admits only what is in the document, which follows the explanation that it is treated as a lien on land. According to that reason, he certainly remains obligated in a heset oath.
