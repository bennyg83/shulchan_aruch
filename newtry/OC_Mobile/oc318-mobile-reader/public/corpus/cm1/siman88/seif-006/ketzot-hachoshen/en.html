A hesset oath. The Tur cites Rabbi Yeshayah that even hesset requires a claim of two ma'ah, and Ran and other authorities agree. The Jerusalem Talmud concerning agents for kiddushin, however, indicates that when two agents perform money kiddushin they become financially interested witnesses because, after the enactment of hesset, they would otherwise face an oath. If hesset required two silver coins, one could establish ordinary money kiddushin at one perutah and there would be no such oath. Tosafot asks why the agents do not have a migo that the money was lost through unavoidable accident, and answers that such a plea itself requires an oath, making them witnesses who need an oath. Yet according to the view that custodial oaths also require two silver coins, the same problem returns for kiddushin of only a perutah. The Shulchan Aruch and Rama elsewhere rule that custodial oaths require two silver coins, yet in Even HaEzer they still treat agents for money kiddushin as interested witnesses. This supports the Shach's rule that when a defendant directly contradicts the claimant with a certain plea, hesset applies even though the defendant might have had a migo to a different plea not directly contradicting the claimant. The migo of unavoidable accident therefore does not remove hesset, and hesset itself need not require a perutah under this practical approach.
