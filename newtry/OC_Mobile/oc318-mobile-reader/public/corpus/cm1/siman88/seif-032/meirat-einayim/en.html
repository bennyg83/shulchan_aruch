And it turns out that he admitted only what is contained in the document. Meaning, it was already explained in seif 28 that an admission to an amount already established by a document does not create a Torah oath.
Even so, he is exempt. The Maggid Mishneh writes in the name of the Rambam that he is exempt only from a Torah oath, but must still take an oath of hesset. It may be that the Tur and Mechaber agree. Although they conclude by comparing this case to one who says, "You owe me one hundred," and is exempt even from hesset, the two cases need not be completely identical. The comparison is only that in both cases he is exempt from a Torah oath. In the first case a document still exists against him, so he is not entirely comparable to one returning a lost object. In the latter case, however, he is fully comparable to one returning a lost object and is exempt even from hesset because of the rabbinic policy encouraging return of lost property, as the Tur and Mechaber write in siman 267.
