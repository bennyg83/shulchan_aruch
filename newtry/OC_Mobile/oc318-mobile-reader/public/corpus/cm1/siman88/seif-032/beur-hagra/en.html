Therefore, if he said to him, etc. He is treated as one who returns a lost article. This appears in Shevuot and elsewhere. Rabbi Eliezer ben Yaakov does not accept the general exemption of one who returns a lost article in this case, but explains the dispute through Rabbah’s principle that a person does not brazenly deny his benefactor. That reasoning does not apply where the admission preceded the claim, for before he was sued there was no brazenness. Some versions in Gittin speak of two tied oxen and a claim that only one was returned. There too, once the claimant says, ‘You found two oxen of mine,’ it is as though he says they were not returned. Baal HaTerumot cites Ramban to this effect. This must be so, because the Gemara aligns the ruling with Rabbi Eliezer ben Yaakov, who speaks where the claim preceded the admission. The conclusion concerns one who can nevertheless act brazenly toward an adult claimant, and the same applies to comparable claims involving found property.
