For he is like one returning a lost object. He certainly remains obligated to take hesset. Since if he had said two he would be liable, he might scheme and say three in order to escape an oath that he cannot properly take because the document supports the claimant. See Tumim.
Had he said two. Ordinarily, in a case of partial admission we do not say that he has a migo to deny everything, because we do not employ a migo based on brazenness. Here, however, since the document assists him, we do employ the migo even though complete denial would involve brazenness, Sema. Although this is a migo used to exempt from an oath, and according to the Mechaber such a migo is ordinarily ineffective, here the enactment that one returning a lost object does not swear for the sake of social order applies. This case too falls within that enactment, because if an oath were imposed upon him he would refrain from coming forward and would simply say two. See my rules of migo for an extended discussion.
