And I repaid half. In Ketubot 19a the Gemara asks why the Mishnah does not teach that Rabbi Yehoshua agrees where one says, 'I owe your father a maneh and I fed him half,' that he is believed. According to whom? If according to the Rabbis, they already hold that he is merely returning a lost object. Tosafot explains that with a son a person is not brazen, and therefore he would always be believed through the migo that he could have said he repaid the entire amount. Thus the Mishnah could not teach that if witnesses exist he is not believed, because even with witnesses he would still have a migo of full payment. This is difficult. One can readily find a case where witnesses establish that he could not have paid, such as where they never left his presence or where the loan is still within its due term. See the novellae of the Ra'ah. It appears, however, according to Rambam that an admission concerning something the defendant cannot deny is treated as 'here it is.' Therefore, either there are no witnesses preventing the payment claim, in which case he is returning a lost object because a son is not one before whom a person is brazen, as Tosafot says, or the case is one where he cannot claim full payment, in which case the amount established by the witnesses is 'here it is' and does not create a partial-admission oath.
