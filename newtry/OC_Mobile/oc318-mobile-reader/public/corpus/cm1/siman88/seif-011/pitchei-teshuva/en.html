Netivot HaMishpat also writes that the Mechaber's view is primary. See there. See Responsa Beit Ephraim, Choshen Mishpat, siman 29, who writes: "In my view, the words of the Shach are correct. There, in the case of one who finds a lost object, both what he returns and what is claimed from him arise only because of the finder's own admission. Here, however, according to his present claim, he now remembers the matter himself, and this does not arise from the claimant's admission. It may therefore be that what he now remembers and asserts with certainty does not render him one who returns a lost object. This is evident to one who examines the Shach, who writes that it is difficult to say that because he said, 'I did not remember,' etc." Apparently, however, the words of the Taz in siman 267, which I cited there in seif katan 4, indicate that he does not accept this distinction.
