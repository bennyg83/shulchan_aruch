See Tumim, where I wrote that two requirements are necessary: the claim must concern a measurable or countable amount, and the admission must likewise concern a measurable or countable amount. Even "this house full" is not deemed a quantified claim. Although something is necessarily missing if it is less than full, it is still called "a full house." Which rotted through his negligence. They are still identifiable, but some rot has affected them. If they were entirely lost, this would not constitute an admission of more than one perutah, since it would supposedly be impossible to determine how much had been there. So writes Shach. His words do not appear correct to me. If he says, "There was wheat up to the window," why is it impossible to determine its value? We can measure how many kor of wheat fill the space up to the window and calculate the price per kor. Rather, regarding the rule, "What you left you may take," it must mean that the produce still exists but has rotted. If it were entirely lost, how could one say, "What you left you may take"? It must also be rotted, because otherwise, irrespective of the issue of quantification, it would be a case of immediate tender, and this is obvious.
