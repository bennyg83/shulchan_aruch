Here it is. The Sema writes that in the preceding case, where the claimant demands money and the defendant answers, "Whatever you left is there," the Mechaber did not say that the defendant is exempt from a Torah oath because of "here it is," even though there is no concern there that the deposited money was spoiled through his negligence. The reason is that with produce the owner presumably designated a particular place for it, so the defendant can say, "Here it is, for it remains in your domain in the place where you left it." With money, however, a specific place is generally not designated, so the depositary must return it into the owner's hand. Similarly, if one claims a kor of produce and the defendant says that he owes only half a kor, since half a kor is a relatively small amount, presumably no separate place was designated for it, and it is therefore not "here it is." The Bach writes similarly. The Shach disagrees and says that their words are incorrect. Even when the deposit is in the depositary's house it can qualify as "here it is," because wherever it is located it remains in the owner's domain, as stated in siman 87, seif 1, and as is also proved by Baal HaTerumot. The truth is that the preceding case likewise concerns money or produce whose form or condition was damaged through the depositary's negligence. This is also implied by Tosafot in chapter Shevuat HaDayanim, Shevuot 43a. I later saw that Gidulei Terumah explains the case as one where the property was lost through negligence. That does not appear correct, because if it were lost through negligence, the rule would be that one who is obligated to swear but cannot swear must pay, since the amount cannot be determined, as explained above. Rather, the explanation I gave is correct. See also siman 296, seif 4. Shach.
