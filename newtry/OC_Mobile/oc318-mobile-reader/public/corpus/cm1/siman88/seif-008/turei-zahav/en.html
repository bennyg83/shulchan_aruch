(There: but if after he admitted the barley.) It appears that this applies specifically where he admitted the barley within the immediate span of speech. He can then say, "Certainly, as you say," meaning: that is why I initially said that you have one kor of wheat in my possession. I intended to continue, "this is what I seek now, but I also have barley in your possession that I am not presently claiming," and you hurried to respond before I finished speaking. If, however, he had already completed his statement and only after the immediate span of speech admitted the barley, and the other party then said, "Certainly, as you say," this does not help. Why did he not mention the barley at the outset? Otherwise the law beginning, "but if after he admitted," should have been written above in seif 12. This appears to me correct.
