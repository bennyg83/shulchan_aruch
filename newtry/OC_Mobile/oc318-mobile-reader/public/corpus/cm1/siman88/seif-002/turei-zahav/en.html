(Seif 5) Regarding the oath of bailees, the Rosh wrote that some say the bailee is not liable to swear unless he denies a claim of two silver ma’ot, because the phrase “money or utensils” is written in the passage dealing with bailees. Others say that denial of two silver ma’ot is required only for partial admission, and although “money or utensils” is written there, it applies specifically to a loan. The first view is preferable, for when the Gemara states that a lender’s claim and the testimony of one witness do not require two silver ma’ot, it should also have taught this regarding the oath of bailees, which would be a greater novelty, since “money or utensils” is written in the passage of bailees. When the Sages said that the Torah passages are intermingled, they did not mean that the basic passages are uprooted. Rather, even though we combine the passages and treat what is written in one as though written in another, we do not entirely remove it from its own place. The Rambam follows the second view cited by the Rosh, namely that the passages are intermingled and two silver ma’ot are not required for bailees. It appears to me that according to the Rambam, just as two silver ma’ot are not required for bailees, neither do two needles suffice merely because together they are worth a perutah. Both rules are derived from “money or utensils,” and according to the Rambam that verse applies to a loan, not to bailees. Since there is no Scriptural derivation extending the rule to bailees, an item worth less than a perutah is not money, and a value of one perutah is required. According to the Rosh, whose view Rama cites as “some say” and declares primary, the rule concerning bailees and loans is certainly the same: in both cases the claim must be worth a perutah because, as a matter of logic, less than a perutah is not money. Although there is a derivation from “money or utensils,” it comes only to teach that when utensils are claimed the total claim need not amount to two silver ma’ot. Sema wrote that according to the Rambam even in the law of bailees the utensil need not be worth a perutah, and his words are exceedingly difficult. In any event, even according to the Rosh it is clear that “money or utensils” does not govern a case of one witness, for the Gemara states explicitly that two silver ma’ot are not required there. Accordingly, the rule that two needles worth one perutah suffice also does not apply there, since both rules derive from the same verse, “money or utensils,” and a derivation applies only where the Torah revealed it. Thus, in the case of one witness the Rosh agrees with the Rambam that one perutah is required, but not two silver ma’ot. This appears correct to me.
