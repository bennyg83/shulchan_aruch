The Shach concludes: All of this follows their reasoning that the defendant is exempt from paying the barley because the claimant is viewed as having admitted that he is not owed barley. According to what I wrote above, however, that the primary view is that of the Ramah, that the reason is that the defendant may have been jesting, there is no distinction. Any explanation the claimant gives for why he did not initially claim the barley is sufficient, because we do not remove money that the defendant clearly owes based merely on a weak inference of waiver. Only an actual and complete waiver would do so. But if the defendant claims, "I was merely jesting with you," then even if the claimant says, "Certainly, what you say is correct," the defendant is exempt even from the value of the barley. This appears to me to be the primary ruling. See what the Taz writes on this point.
