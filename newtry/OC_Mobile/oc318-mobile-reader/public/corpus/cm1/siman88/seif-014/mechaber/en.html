If the claimant first says, "You owe me a kor of wheat," and before he finishes adding, "and also a kor of barley," the defendant hastens and says, "I owe you only a kor of barley," and the claimant then says, "I also intended to demand the barley," then if the defendant acted deceptively by rushing to answer before the claimant finished, he is liable in a Torah oath, because we regard the claimant as though he had already demanded both. If the defendant answered innocently, he is exempt from the Torah oath and from payment, even if immediately, within the time of ordinary speech after his admission, the claimant says, "I also intended to demand the barley." But if, after the defendant admitted the barley, the claimant says, "Certainly, as you say, you do owe me barley, but I am not demanding it now," the defendant must pay the value of the barley.
