Two ma'ah. Tosafot appears to say that if the claim falls short of two ma'ah by less than a perutah, the deficiency is disregarded because it is not itself worth a perutah, so the claim still counts as two ma'ah. I do not understand why. Legal measurements are not given as approximations. Five sela for redemption of a firstborn son, and the fixed values for sin and guilt offerings, are not complete if even a small amount is missing. Why should the two ma'ah requirement differ? The Gemara's discussion of Rav and Shmuel likewise creates difficulty. Rav requires denial of a claim of two silver coins. The Gemara derives two from the plural "money" and then adds the requirement of partial admission. According to Tosafot's approach, one could imagine a claim of exactly two silver coins with an admission of a needle worth less than a perutah, leaving the monetary denial still effectively at two ma'ah. Rambam and the Shulchan Aruch treat even the needle as a valid admission. This would satisfy both requirements without reducing the measure. According to Tosafot, however, a utensil itself ordinarily must have at least a perutah of value. Perhaps their rule that a utensil must be significant arises only according to Shmuel, who reads "money" as teaching importance and extends that importance to utensils. According to Rav, who uses "money" for the amount denied, there may be no independent requirement that the utensil admission be worth a perutah. The matter remains difficult.
