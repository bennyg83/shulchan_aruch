(Shulchan Aruch, seif 4, where one witness contradicts him) The verse from which we derive that one witness obligates an oath treats the single witness like two witnesses with respect to the monetary consequence. Rashi explained: since, if there were two witnesses, they would obligate him to pay money on their testimony, one witness obligates him to take an oath. His wording implies that even where the claim concerns utensils, the single witness obligates an oath only if the utensils are worth at least a perutah, for only an item worth a perutah is legally considered money. The reason is that the rule that utensils suffice even when they are not worth a perutah is derived from the verse “utensils to guard,” and that verse concerns partial admission, not one witness, as we shall state below in the name of the Rosh. Sema wrote that with one witness, and likewise with bailees, the utensils need not be worth a perutah. See below, where his words are exceedingly difficult. In the case of one witness, according to all authorities, a value of one perutah is required, as explained below.
