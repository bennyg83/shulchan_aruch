Until he admits one perutah, etc. Scripture says, 'money or utensils.' Just as 'utensils' denotes two items of significance, so 'money' indicates two units of significance. The Sages assessed the significant minimum as a ma'ah. Why then was 'money' written at all, after 'utensils' already teaches two significant items? To teach that the denial itself must concern two silver ma'ah in addition to the admitted portion, which is at least one perutah. Sema from the Gemara. According to Rambam, as Maggid Mishneh understands him, the entire quantitative rule is rabbinic and the verses function as support. See Tumim.
The measure of a perutah, etc. Sema and Shach discuss the contemporary weight of a perutah, but this is not the place for a full treatment. I discussed it elsewhere regarding redemption of a firstborn son and similar laws. The principal calculation is that a shekel is one-half ounce, corresponding to one loth, approximately four and one-half grams and slightly more by the loth weight, or a little over nine grams by the ounce weight, and it must be pure silver. The stated measures of a perutah and ma'ah in silver appear to mean coined silver. Otherwise the cost of workmanship must also be deducted. Tosafot in Shevuot writes similarly.
