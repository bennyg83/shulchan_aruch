If one claims two items, such as wheat and barley, and the defendant admits one of them, he must give what he admitted and swear concerning the remainder. But if one claims wheat and the defendant admits barley, the defendant is exempt even from the value of the barley. Some say the reason is that the claimant is regarded as admitting that the defendant does not owe him barley. Therefore, even if witnesses testify concerning the barley, the defendant is exempt, because a litigant's admission is equivalent to the testimony of one hundred witnesses. {Rama: Even if the defendant knows he owes the barley, he is exempt from paying it, because it is as though the claimant forgave it, as explained above in siman 75, seif 11. This appears correct, contrary to those who disagree.} Some say this applies only when the claimant says, 'I lent you wheat on such-and-such day at such-and-such hour,' and the defendant says, 'It was barley.' Had he lent both at the same time, he would have claimed both; he has therefore certainly admitted that he did not lend barley. {Rama: Otherwise, the defendant certainly must pay, because a person does not forgive his other claims merely because he brought only one of them.} If the claimant seized the value of the barley, it is not removed from his possession. {Rama: Some say this applies only if he seized it before bringing the claim to judgment. If he seized it after they had already appeared for judgment, it is removed from him. This is the primary view.}
