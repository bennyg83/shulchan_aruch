But if he claimed wheat, etc., he is exempt. It appears that he must nevertheless take an oath of hesset, as written below in siman 296:4 and siman 400:4, because this is no less than any other denial, concerning which an oath of hesset is required.
And some say, etc., and therefore even if there are witnesses, etc. The phrase "some say" excludes the opinion of the Ramah, who gives a different reason. He holds that the defendant's admission of barley is not an admission that creates liability because he did not say "you are my witnesses" and did not admit before beit din, so he can say, "I was merely joking with you." If he does not make that claim, however, he is liable, and all the more so if witnesses establish the barley loan. The Rosh disagreed in chapter HaManiach. In the Perishah I cited his language. Although the Rosh also rejected Rashi and Tosafot, who explain that the defendant is exempt for the barley because the claimant waived it, do not infer that the Rosh rejects waiver or that Rashi and Tosafot reject the rationale that the claimant's wheat claim constitutes an admission concerning the barley. The Tur and Mechaber themselves use the language of waiver even while giving the rationale of implied admission, and Tosafot, who speak of waiver, also write that this follows because the claimant effectively admitted it. All agree that since he did not claim barley, his conduct indicates acknowledgment that no barley is owed, and this amounts to waiver. Therefore, even if witnesses later testify to the barley, he is exempt. The Rosh's objection is only that Rashi and Tosafot wrote too broadly, implying exemption from barley even where the wheat claim did not concern the same transaction and time. That seemed astonishing to him. See the Perishah.

But if he seized afterward, etc. The Tur writes that since the claimant already admitted that the defendant did not owe him barley, later seizure does not help. According to this rationale, even seizure without witnesses does not help, although the claimant could deny having seized anything, because such a migo is against clear evidence. We treat it as established that by failing to claim barley he waived it. Nevertheless, seizure without witnesses can help him collect the value of the wheat he claimed, like any claimant whose claim was denied and who later seizes without witnesses, who may retain the property upon an oath of hesset up to the amount of his claim, as the Tur and Mechaber write at the end of siman 87. But if the barley is worth more than the wheat, or he seized enough for both wheat and barley, or the claimant later admits that the defendant owes no wheat but only barley, the seizure does not help with respect to the barley.
