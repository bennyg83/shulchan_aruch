He claimed from him... See Beer HaGolah and Tosafos, Shevuos 40b, and Bava Metzia 5a.
Therefore, even... In the Bava Kamma case there must be witnesses that one of the animals caused the damage; otherwise the tortfeasor would be exempt as one who admits a fine, since we rule that half damages are a penalty. Thus the Mishnah there states....
For an admission... This applies even against witnesses, as shown in Shevuos 41b and Bava Basra 128b. The Rosh and Rashba likewise explain that the case involves witnesses. The Rambam, Hilchos Nizkei Mamon chapter 9, and the Shulchan Aruch below, siman 400 seif 4, rule that where there are two injured animals but no clear proof as to which animal caused which damage, the tortfeasor is exempt on the analogy to one claimed for wheat who admits barley. This indicates that there is some proof that damage occurred, only not which way it occurred. The Rama understood the Rambam similarly. The Maggid Mishneh's interpretation that the Rambam's latter case lacks witnesses is difficult, because without witnesses admission to a penalty would exempt him entirely, and the language "clear proof" implies that some proof exists. The Rashba, however, holds that where witnesses establish that either wheat or barley was lent, a defendant who is claimed for wheat and admits barley is liable; his concern is that otherwise the payment would ultimately rest solely on the defendant's admission. This depends on whether half damages are treated as monetary liability or a fine, as the commentators explain. See below in siman 400.
And even if he knows... As stated in Bava Basra 135a.
There is an authority who says... Since the Gemara challenges from the Mishnah in Bava Kamma, the case under discussion must be comparable to that Mishnah. See the Rosh there.
And if he seized... As stated there in the Gemara, where the Mishnah is established as a case in which he seized.
And some say... Specifically if he seized before the claim. See Rashi there, who explains that the seizure preceded the lawsuit; Baal HaTerumos and the Rosh read Rashi this way. Baal HaTerumos explains that such a seizure is as though the claimant had already claimed the barley, because when it is already in his possession no verbal claim is required. If he seized only afterward, however, his prior waiver prevents the seizure from helping. The Shulchan Aruch below at the end of siman 400 follows this view. The Rama there cites Tosafos and the Rosh that in a wheat-and-barley case seizure does not help, and likewise in the first clause of the Mishnah, where the claimant has a claim against only one of the two animals. The reason the Gemara does not answer the first clause by saying he is "fit to collect" is that if seizure cannot produce collection, such terminology is inapplicable. Rashi, however, explains that the first clause is not set up as a definite claim because Sumchus does not apply his rule to definite claims, and this is the basis of the Shulchan Aruch's view that seizure helps in all such cases, following the Rif.
(Collected notes) And some say, specifically... Nimukei Yosef at the end of the third chapter of Bava Kamma writes that seizure is effective even afterward, provided it occurred before the defendant retracted his admission. This is also the Rambam's and Shulchan Aruch's view. (End of collected notes.)
