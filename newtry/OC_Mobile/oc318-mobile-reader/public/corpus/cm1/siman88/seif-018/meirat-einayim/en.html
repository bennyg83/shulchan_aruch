For he is claiming oil from him. Since the claimant began by saying "filled," it is as though he said, "You have ten measures of oil of mine," and he mentions the jars only to indicate the quantity of oil. But if he says "ten jars filled with oil," his wording implies a claim both to the jars and to their contents. Likewise, if he does not use the word "filled" either at the beginning or end, we say that he intends to claim both.
But if he said, "ten vessels of oil." The Tur writes this. The Beit Yosef explains that when he says the construct form "vessels of oil," the vessels are grammatically attached to the oil and he is claiming only the oil; he mentions vessels merely as a measure. When he says the independent plural "vessels" followed by oil, the term is separate and implies that he claims both. In the Tur, the word "filled" does not appear in the first clause, because even without it the construct form itself indicates a claim only for oil. It is therefore difficult that Rama, commenting on the Mechaber's version taken from the Rambam, which does use "filled" in the first clause, introduces this distinction between the construct and independent plural as though undisputed. He should have presented it as "some say." In the Derishah I wrote that the Tur too should be emended to include the word "filled," and that there is no substantive distinction between the two grammatical forms. I brought proof from Rabbeinu Yerucham and from the abridged rulings of the Rosh. See there.
