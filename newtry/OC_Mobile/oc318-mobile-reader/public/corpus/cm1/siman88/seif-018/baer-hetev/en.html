With oil. Since he mentioned "full" at the beginning, it is as though he said, "I have ten measures of oil in your possession," and he mentioned the jars only to indicate the quantity of oil he claims. This differs from saying, "jars full of oil," for then his wording implies a claim to both the jars and their contents. Likewise, if he did not use the word "full" either at the beginning or at the end, we also say that he intended to claim both. So writes the Sema. The Shach disagrees. In his view, whether he said, "ten jars of oil," or, "a full ten," in every form he is liable for a Torah oath, unless he said, "I have ten jars of oil in your pit," in which case his intent is only the oil in the pit, and he mentioned jars merely as a measure.
