One who was sued, etc., the law is, etc. This is true not only of a Torah oath, which cannot be shifted, as stated in Shevuot, but even of a hesset oath. The Gemara’s statement that a rabbinic oath may be shifted means a hesset oath, as explained above in siman 87. Were that not so, the result would not rest on shifting the oath, since even one who is exempt by Torah law may say, ‘Swear a hesset oath and collect.’ This is certainly so where he is exempt by Torah law.
