And let him swear to me that he has a claim against me, etc. Meaning an oath of hesset. The Tur says so explicitly. If a Torah oath were involved, it is obvious that the defendant could not reverse it and impose it upon the claimant.
The law follows the claimant. In the Perishah I wrote that this is not comparable to the Tur's ruling in siman 24, where the defendant is given thirty days to free himself from the claim by producing witnesses or proof corresponding to the amount he owes. One might have argued similarly here that we should heed his request that the claimant first take an oath of hesset, perhaps the claimant will refuse and the defendant will immediately be freed. The difference is that here the claimant is not obligated to take that oath of hesset until the defendant first pays and then brings a separate claim against him.
