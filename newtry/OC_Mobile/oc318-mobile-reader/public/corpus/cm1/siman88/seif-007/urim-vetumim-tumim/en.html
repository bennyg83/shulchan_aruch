Except legumes. The Shach writes that wine and vinegar are considered one type with respect to an admission being of the same kind as the claim, because Rashbam writes so in Bava Batra 85. See there. It is true that this appears in Rashbam, but one may say that Rashbam wrote it only in the Gemara's initial assumption, when it held that even for purposes of a sale they are one type, so that if one bought wine and was given vinegar it would not be a mistaken transaction. On that assumption, they would likewise be one type regarding an admission being of the same kind as the claim. According to the conclusion, however, we say that they are treated as one type only regarding terumah, whereas for purposes of buying and selling they are two different types because people are particular about the distinction. If so, regarding claims and responses as well, we certainly follow ordinary human understanding, people's particularity, and their language. After all, in Torah usage are legumes not included within produce? Yet in ordinary speech they are not included, and therefore an admission concerning legumes is not considered of the same kind as a claim concerning produce. Likewise, a large menorah and a small menorah would not be considered one type but for the reason that it is possible to scrape it down to size. All the more so, according to ordinary human understanding wine and vinegar are distinct. Therefore this ruling requires study.
