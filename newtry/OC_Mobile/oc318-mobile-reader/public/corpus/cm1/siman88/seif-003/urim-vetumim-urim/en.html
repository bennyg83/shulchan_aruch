Ten needles, etc. The Torah singled out utensils separately from money, teaching that even utensils of minimal value can count, even where each is worth less than a perutah. Many authorities, including Ramban and Tosafot, disagree. They hold that utensils were separated from the money category only to teach that they need not equal two ma'ah in value. Nevertheless both the admitted and denied portions must each be worth at least one perutah. If the item is a utensil too insignificant to be used for a sudar kinyan, everyone agrees it lacks sufficient importance to generate an oath. This appears from Ran. See there.
And he admitted one, etc. The case must be a loan of the very object itself, with an obligation to return a needle like the one borrowed. If he could satisfy the debt by paying money, the special significance of the needle disappears. Nor can we simply say this is a deposit. If the needle remains intact, it is an immediate tender. If it was negligently damaged, the obligation becomes monetary, again losing the special significance. It is strained to say that we follow the nature of the original delivery rather than the current claim.
