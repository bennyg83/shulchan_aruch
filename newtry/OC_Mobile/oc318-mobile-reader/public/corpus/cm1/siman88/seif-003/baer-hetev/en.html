Obligated. The Sema writes that according to the Rambam and the Mechaber, who rule that when one claims utensils, three needles suffice for the requisite amount, it appears that when the defendant denies everything after being sued for a single needle, that too suffices. The same would apply to a watchman's oath in seif 5. The reason the Mechaber speaks there of a perutah is that he is not discussing utensils. The Bach disagrees and maintains that here, according to all opinions, the claim must equal at least a perutah even where the object is a utensil, both for an oath arising from one witness and for a watchman's oath. The Taz likewise disagrees with the Sema. See there. The Shach writes that the Bach's words are incorrect regarding an oath imposed by one witness. Since a person who partially admits a claim is obligated to swear even where he denies a utensil worth less than a perutah, as the Ran explicitly writes, he is likewise obligated in a Torah oath where one witness contradicts him. The reason is that wherever two witnesses would obligate him to pay money, one witness obligates him to swear, as stated in the Gemara and the authorities. Regarding a watchman's oath as well, the Sema's view appears correct, because utensils of even minimal value are significant. According to the Rosh and those who follow him, however, the utensil must be worth a perutah, and then even for a watchman's oath that suffices. The requirement that the denial equal two silver ma'ot applies only to money. See Responsa Radbaz, siman 185.
