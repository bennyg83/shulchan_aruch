For the oath of custodians as well. Shaar HaMishpat writes that if one claims a deposit worth two silver ma'ah and a perutah, the custodian admits the perutah, denies one ma'ah entirely, and claims unavoidable accident as to the other ma'ah, he is liable to a Torah oath on both matters. Although both partial admission and the custodian's oath ordinarily require a denial involving two silver ma'ah, here the two denials combine. The reason for requiring two silver ma'ah is that the Torah imposed an oath only concerning a significant monetary matter. Since the total disputed amount is two ma'ah, the two different defenses may be combined and he swears concerning both. Beit Yehudah disagrees, maintaining that the two-silver requirement is a Scriptural decree applying separately to the denial that generates each oath, so the two cannot be combined. See there.
