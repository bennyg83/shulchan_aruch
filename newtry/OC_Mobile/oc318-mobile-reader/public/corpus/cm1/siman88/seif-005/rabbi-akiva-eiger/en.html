[Sema, subparagraph 56] The reason is that in the first case he at least has a document against him. Note: In my humble opinion, it can be explained simply. Here, had he claimed two, he would have been obligated in a hesset oath. Therefore, even when he claims three he must take a hesset oath, because otherwise he might deliberately frame his claim in that way to exempt himself from hesset. For this same reason the Derishah’s question above, siman 84, seif 4, is not difficult. The gaon Chacham Tzvi writes virtually this in his glosses to Taz above, siman 71, seif 21.
