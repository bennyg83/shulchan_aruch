The same applies to these cases. Shach correctly explains that this refers to seifim 15 and 16. The Gemara explicitly states that where a person artfully admits a different item before it is claimed, in a case where there otherwise would have been a proper partial admission, that maneuver does not help. But here the exemption arises because the admission preceded the claim, and the theory is that at the moment of the later claim he becomes a complete denier, one who displays the brazenness of denying everything. One can therefore argue that even if he deliberately arranged the prior admission, the resulting denial remains complete, and perhaps his brazenness is even greater because he planned the tactic. Therefore some authorities hold that he is still exempt, while others hold that the stratagem does not help and he remains liable to the oath. That is why Rama writes this as a 'some say' ruling.
