And some say, etc. The Sema writes that the phrase “some say” indicates dissent, unlike the law above, seif 14, where all agree. He distinguishes that here the defendant's stratagem benefits him only by exempting him from an oath, while he must still pay the wheat, and in the preceding seif he must at least pay the barley. Therefore we do not suspect that he would scheme merely for that limited benefit. This is unconvincing. In seif 14 as well, the defendant offers to pay the barley and schemes only to escape the oath. Rashi and the Ran explicitly explain that if beit din sees that he rushed to admit in order to prevent the claimant from adding a barley claim that would generate an oath, the stratagem is disregarded. The real distinction is that there he admitted a different kind than the item claimed, whereas here he admitted the very item claimed. This is clear.
For in these cases as well, etc. The Rama's plural wording indicates that this rule also applies to the preceding seif. The Sema and Bach understand him this way. The Sema notes that the Tur's singular wording may refer only to the immediately preceding case. The Levush likewise uses the singular, and the Bach rejects the Rama, arguing that only where the defendant jumps in before the claimant finishes do we examine whether he is scheming, whereas if the defendant begins with an unsolicited admission we do not. In my view the Rama is correct. The Ran derives the preceding law from seif 14, where the exemption applies only if the defendant acted innocently and not as a stratagem. The Ran writes that this teaches that the claim must precede the partial admission; where the partial admission comes first, even if a claim follows, the defendant is exempt. Rabbi Yosef Halevi likewise deduces this. The Beit Yosef plainly presents the laws as linked. Thus, just as the earlier law does not protect a transparent stratagem, the same is true when the defendant volunteers the admission before the claim. The underlying logic is identical: if beit din sees that he is scheming, it makes no difference which procedural form he chose. See what I write below, seif 32.
