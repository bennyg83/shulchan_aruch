And some say. The Sema writes that the fact that the Rama introduces this opinion here with "some say" implies that there are authorities who disagree. This is unlike the law above in seif 14, where all agree, because there the defendant's stratagem benefits him only by exempting him from an oath, while he still must pay the wheat. Likewise, in seif 15 he must at least pay the barley. We therefore do not suspect him of deliberately using a stratagem merely for that purpose. The Shach writes that this is not convincing, because in seif 14 it appears that the defendant wishes to pay the barley and his stratagem is likewise only to exempt himself from the oath. Rashi and the Ran explicitly explain it that way in chapter Shevuat HaDayanim. Rather, the distinction is that there the defendant admitted a different species from the one claimed, whereas here he admitted the very item the claimant demanded. This is clear.
In these cases. The Sema writes that the Rama's wording, "in these cases," implies that the qualification also applies to the immediately preceding seif. The Tur, however, writes, "in this case too," implying that it refers only to the case immediately adjacent. The Shach writes that the Rama's version appears primary. The Ran derived the preceding rule from the law in seif 14, which says that if he acted innocently and not as a stratagem, the result differs. The Ran writes concerning that case: "From here we learn that the claim must precede the admission." The Beit Yosef likewise clearly implies this. It follows that just as there the defendant is exempt only where his admission was made innocently rather than as a stratagem, the same is true here. Reason also dictates this, because once beit din sees that the defendant is acting deceptively, there is no basis to distinguish between the cases. See below, seif 32. Shach.
