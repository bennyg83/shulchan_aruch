And some say that in these cases as well, etc. Rama's use of the plural "these" appears to include the preceding case where the defendant speaks first and says, "I owe you barley." According to this view he is liable, meaning liable to take a Torah oath that he does not owe wheat, as though the claimant had claimed both together and he admitted one. The Tur's wording, however, says "in this case as well," implying that it refers only to the immediately preceding case. In the Perishah I explained why Rama presents this as a disputed view here, whereas above in seif 14 all agree that where the defendant acts deceptively he remains liable. There, his deception cannot exempt him from payment itself, but only from swearing concerning the barley, while the wheat must still be paid. Likewise in the case immediately preceding, he must in any event pay the barley, and his maneuver is only to avoid the oath concerning the wheat. We therefore do not readily suspect him of such deception merely to avoid an oath. This is clear.
