But for a perutah he is liable. The Sema, seif katan 7, writes that according to Rambam and the Mechaber, who hold that a claim of three needles suffices for utensils, denial of even one needle should suffice when he denies everything. The same should apply to the oath of bailees in the next seif. The reason the Mechaber mentions a perutah is that this clause does not discuss utensils. The Bach objects that according to all opinions a perutah is required even for a utensil, both for an oath based on one witness and for the oath of bailees. His words are incorrect regarding an oath based on one witness. Since a partial admission denying a utensil worth less than a perutah creates liability, it follows that if he admitted it, or witnesses testified, he would owe that utensil. The Ran expressly writes that beit din adjudicates utensils worth less than a perutah. Therefore, when one witness contradicts him, he must take a Torah oath, because wherever two witnesses would obligate money, one witness obligates an oath. Regarding the oath of bailees as well, the Sema appears correct, since utensils of any minimal value are significant. According to the Rosh and those who follow him, however, the utensil must be worth a perutah. A perutah suffices even for the oath of bailees; the requirement of a denial worth two silver ma'ot applies only to money. This is implied by the Rosh and Ran, who derive “money or utensils” for the oath of bailees as well. See there carefully.
