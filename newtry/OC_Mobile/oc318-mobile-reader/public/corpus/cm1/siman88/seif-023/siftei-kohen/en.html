I have in my possession only a bundle of coins, etc. This is the Tur's wording, and the Semag likewise uses this wording. It implies that even though he certainly admits that there were coins in the bundle, so that his admission is certainly worth at least a perutah, he is nevertheless exempt because he did not admit to something defined by measure or number. This is even clearer from the Rambam at the beginning of chapter 5 of Hilchos To'en, who writes: one hundred dinars I entrusted to you; he replies, you entrusted to me only a bundle of dinars, and it is not before me and I do not know what it contained, and whatever you deposited you may take. He is exempt, and likewise in every similar case. I proved the same above in siman 72, seif 12, seif katan 48, and challenged the Rosh there. See there.
He is exempt from an oath. The Sema asks how this differs from the ruling of the Tur and Mechaber in siman 75, seif 19, where one claims, I lent you one hundred, and the defendant answers, it is true that you lent me, but I do not know how much. One opinion says that his admission necessarily includes at least a perutah and he is liable. The Sema answers that there the defendant responds to a claim concerning something measured or counted by saying, it is true that you lent me and counted it from your hand into mine, but I do not know how much. Therefore he is liable. Here, however, he says that he never received from the claimant individual counted items, but that they were handed to him together without measure or number. Therefore he is exempt. But what will the Sema do with the case of a loan secured by collateral above in siman 72, seif 12, and the case of produce deposited without measurement below in siman 292, seif 14? There too he never received individually counted items, yet he is liable. It therefore appears that the Sema overlooked the words of the Rosh and Ran in Perek HaDayanim and all that I wrote above in siman 72, seif 12, seif katan 50. I already clarified there that the Ramban's view is correct, and that the necessary distinction is that here the contents of the house can ultimately be fully determined. His words are clear, as I explained there. Thus the Sema's question does not arise at all. Examine carefully, for this is clear.
