How so, etc. There Abaye states that the rule applies only in a particular case, and Rava then responds. Baal HaTerumot explains that Rava does not hold that the claim ‘this house’ is inherently indefinite, for Tosafot already object that this cannot be so. Rather, Abaye holds that one of two requirements suffices: either the claimant must demand a measured item, or the defendant must admit a measured item. Thus, Abaye’s statement, ‘But if he said this house,’ refers back to the mishnah, where the defendant answered that he did not know except for a certain amount. Rava objects that if so, why does the latter clause teach ‘this one’? The mishnah should have stated explicitly that both the claim and the admission must concern measured quantities. Rava therefore explains that both are required. This also answers what Rava added beyond the mishnah itself. He did not merely say that the claim was indefinite; rather, he clarified that both the claim and the admission must be quantified.
