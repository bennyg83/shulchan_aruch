Until he claims something defined by weight, etc. The Torah's language of "money or utensils" concerns a claim involving a definite measure or weight, and the admission is described by "that this is it," likewise implying something defined and known.
You have nothing of mine except a bundle, etc., he is exempt from an oath. One might ask how this differs from siman 75, where if the claimant says, "I lent you one hundred," and the defendant answers, "It is true that you lent me, but I do not know how much," some say that his admission is presumed to be worth at least a perutah and he is liable. The answer is that there the defendant responds to a claim stated in a definite measure and count, admitting, "It is true that you lent me," meaning that he received from the claimant an item that was measured and counted, but he no longer knows the amount. Here, however, he says that he never received anything from the claimant item by item or by count, but rather a single undetermined bundle without measure or count, and that the claimant may simply take it back as deposited. Therefore he is exempt.
