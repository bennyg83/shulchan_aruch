Exempt. The Sema writes: One might ask how this differs from the ruling of the Tur and Shulchan Aruch in siman 75, seif 19. There, where one claims, "I lent you one hundred," and the defendant responds, "It is true that you lent me, but I do not know how much," one opinion holds that his admission is presumed to be worth at least a perutah and he is liable. The answer is that there, in response to a claim concerning a quantified and counted amount, the defendant answers, "It is true that you lent me and counted out into my hand something of that quantified and counted amount, but I do not know how much." He is therefore liable. Here, by contrast, he says that he never received from the claimant, item by item, anything that was counted. Rather, the claimant handed it over all together without measure or count, and the defendant says, "Take it in the same condition in which you left it." He is therefore exempt. The Shach disagrees and writes: What will the Sema do with the law of a loan secured by a pledge in siman 72, seif 12, or the law of one who deposited unmeasured produce in siman 292, seif 14? There too the defendant never received it item by item, yet he is liable. Rather, the Sema apparently overlooked the words of the Rosh and Ran in chapter Shevuat HaDayanim, as I wrote in siman 72. There I established the view of the Ramban as primary, namely that this case is different because it is possible to determine the entire amount by examining what remains in the house, or in the bundle. His words are clear, as I explained there, and the Sema's question therefore does not arise. The Taz also disagrees with the Sema and offers a different distinction. See there.
