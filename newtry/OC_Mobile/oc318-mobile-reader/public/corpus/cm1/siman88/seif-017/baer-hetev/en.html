Principal. The Shach discusses this law at length and concludes that the Mechaber's view is the primary law. At minimum, the defendant must pay the barley he admitted and cannot later retreat to, "I know nothing about any matter," or, "I owe you nothing at all." He takes a shevuat heset that he does not know that he owes wheat. If he wishes to satisfy his obligation toward Heaven, he must pay the wheat as well. Similarly, if he initially answered, "I know nothing about this," or, "There was never any such matter," and witnesses establish that he owes either wheat or barley, he must at least pay the barley and take a shevuat heset that he does not know of a wheat obligation. The same principle applies to comparable cases. See the Taz's discussion.
