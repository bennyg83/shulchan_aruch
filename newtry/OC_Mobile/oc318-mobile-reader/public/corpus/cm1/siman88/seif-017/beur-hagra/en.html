He claimed from him... and some say. So too at the end of the third chapter of Bava Kamma, where the Gemara discusses whether the injured party makes a definite claim. Rambam follows his own view, as he writes in Hilchos Mechirah chapter 20, and the Shulchan Aruch, siman 223 seif 4, rules that the seller takes a heses oath that he sold only the smaller item. This implies that the purchaser acquires the smaller item, since the text does not say merely that the seller swears he did not sell the larger one. In seif 5 there, if he says..., the claimant has only the smaller item. Yet the Gemara asks why this is so, since what was claimed was not admitted. It establishes the case where he claimed a slave together with his garment. The matter requires study.
Rather, one who wishes to fulfill his obligation before Heaven must pay the wheat, as in the case of one who says, "You owe me a maneh," and the other says, "I do not know," as stated at the end of Bava Kamma. But he certainly need not pay the barley, since the claimant is not demanding it. Even if the defendant knows with certainty that he owes it, he need not pay it, as explained above in seif 12 in the gloss. Although the plain wording of the gloss here may suggest otherwise, and likewise the source of the ruling, this is the principal law. See Shach.
