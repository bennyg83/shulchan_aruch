He pays barley. Sema explains that we may say to him: just as you are uncertain, so too the claimant is uncertain, and there was therefore no forgiveness. These words have no foundation. In Tumim I established the reason as follows: up to the value of the barley, the existence of the debt is certain, while payment or forgiveness is uncertain. A doubt cannot displace a certainty, and it is like a case of "I do not know whether I paid you." See there.
Only if he wishes to fulfill his obligation before Heaven. It appears that this concerns a case where he had no reason to know, such as an heir who says, "I do not know whether my father owed him wheat or barley," or the like. This parallels the case in the chapter HaManiach where one says the large ox damaged and the other says, "I do not know whether the small or large ox damaged." There too he had no reason to know, as I wrote in Tumim. Therefore, even to fulfill his obligation before Heaven, he is exempt from paying the value of the wheat. Although we rule above in siman 75 that where one says, "I do not know whether I borrowed from you," he is obligated to pay in order to fulfill his obligation before Heaven, there he should have known, whereas here he had no reason to know. Nevertheless, he must pay the value of the barley to fulfill his obligation before Heaven, because it is clear to him that some debt existed. But if he should have known, why discuss only the value of barley? He would be obligated even for the value of wheat in order to fulfill his obligation before Heaven. This resolves Shach's difficulty.
And this appears to me primary. Shach writes that Rama's determination is not decisive. I likewise concluded in Tumim that the Mechaber's ruling, which is Rambam's ruling, is correct. So too below in siman 223, where there were two slaves and the purchaser says, "I bought the larger one," while the seller says, "I do not know whether it was the larger or the smaller," the purchaser acquires the smaller one. That is precisely the Mechaber's rule here. Rama made no gloss there, perhaps relying on what he wrote here.
