And he pays him barley. The reason is that here we cannot say that because the claimant did not demand barley he certainly waived it. It is possible that the claimant himself was uncertain about the barley and therefore demanded only wheat, about which he was certain. The defendant admits that in any event he owes one of the two, so he pays the lesser, namely barley, and regarding the other claim swears that he does not know. The dissenting opinion holds that even here, since the claimant did not demand barley, we may say that even if it was owed he waived it. According to that view the defendant may therefore be exempt from paying the barley as well.
