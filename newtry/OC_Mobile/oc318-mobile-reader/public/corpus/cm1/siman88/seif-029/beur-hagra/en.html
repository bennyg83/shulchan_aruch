When does this apply, etc. Because the debt is not collectible from encumbered property, the reason of land lien does not apply. Although the debtor cannot deny the debt because the instrument contains a clause of trust, one cannot exempt him merely through the rule of ‘here it is.’ In the Gemara that rule itself is tied specifically to a lien upon land, as Rashi explains: the entire force of the document lies in the land. The Gemara then gives another answer, that even without ‘here it is’ he is exempt because the claim is supported by a document. Rif therefore does not cite the reason of ‘here it is,’ since that reason too depends upon the land lien. The argument that the document assists him applies only where the claimant’s demand arises from the document itself, such as sela'im or dinars. The Yerushalmi’s rule that everyone agrees in the ketubah case likewise applies specifically to an obligation created by an act of beit din, as explained above in siman 75. Thus, where there is no collection from encumbered property, the exemption does not follow automatically.
