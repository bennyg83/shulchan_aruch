Credibility. The Shach writes that this ruling requires study. It appears that the Rambam and other authorities hold that a handwritten acknowledgment containing a credibility clause is considered "here it is," because the debtor cannot deny it. In Even HaEzer siman 96, seif 15, the Rama likewise writes that regarding a matter the defendant cannot deny, there is no partial admission even where there is no lien upon land. See there. In my work on Even HaEzer there I established that this proof is not correct. The Ramah's statement there that the defendant takes a hesset oath is because in a place where people do not ordinarily write a ketubah, the ketubah itself is treated as a claim secured by land. See there. The same implication appears in Responsa Mabit, part 2, at the end of siman 205. It therefore appears straightforward as a matter of practical law that we should not obligate him in a Torah oath. At the very least this is a case of legal doubt, in which the defendant receives the benefit of the doubt. Shach.
