He is exempt from a Torah oath. For example, Reuven claims one hundred from Shimon, and Shimon admits one dinar. Shimon then claims a kor of wheat from Reuven, and Reuven admits half a kor. Shimon certainly must swear on the original one-dinar partial admission, even if half a kor is worth more than the dinar, because when he admitted the dinar Reuven had not yet admitted the wheat. That admission was therefore not an immediate tender. Reuven, however, is exempt if the half-kor is not worth more than the dinar, because Shimon already admitted the dinar and Reuven's half-kor admission is effectively covered by money already held by Shimon. It is therefore an immediate tender. If the half-kor is worth even one perutah more than the dinar, there is no complete immediate tender. It appears that even if Reuven must actually give the half-kor in kind, it is still considered an immediate tender. Although Mechaber holds that a pledge is not normally immediate tender because it requires appraisal, actual money already in hand is immediate tender according to everyone.
