For it is "here it is." I wrote in siman 87 that an offered object counts as "here it is" only when three conditions are met: it is given as complete payment rather than merely collateral, its value is fixed and does not require appraisal, and the debtor has no cash with which he could be required to pay instead. Here one party claims money and the other admits a dinar, while the reciprocal claim is wheat with partial admission of wheat. Why should the money already held by the first party be "here it is" for the wheat? The wheat creditor is entitled specifically to wheat, and the money in the other person's hand is at most collateral for that obligation, not complete payment. Perhaps the case is one where the debtor has no wheat available, so the law permits satisfaction through money and the fixed dinar can therefore function as full payment. Otherwise the characterization as "here it is" remains difficult.
