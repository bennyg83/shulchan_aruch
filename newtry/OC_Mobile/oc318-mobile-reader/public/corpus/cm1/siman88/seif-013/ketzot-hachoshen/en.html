That you gave those monies. Earlier, where one claims general value and the defendant admits a specific object that itself constitutes a debt, the admission can be of the same kind as the claim. Here, by contrast, the specific object admitted is not itself an owed monetary obligation but a particular thing belonging to the claimant. The two forms are therefore different. The Shach explains that if the specific object itself carries personal responsibility, it can more readily be treated as monetary value. A deposit ordinarily does not. Yet siman 88 later says that one who claims a maneh as a loan and the defendant admits fifty as a deposit can still be partial admission. This suggests the distinction cannot rest merely on responsibility. Rosh offers a different explanation. People sometimes confuse whether money held by another was given as a loan or as a deposit. Because that confusion is common, the legal forms can be treated as one type for partial admission. By contrast, one does not commonly confuse a claim that money was paid as consideration for a specific object with an admission that the object itself remains the claimant's property. Therefore the latter are different types. This seems the better explanation.
