Like an oral admission. Ramban holds that denial counts as denial of a land secured debt only where, upon admission, the claimant could actually collect from encumbered property. If the present admission would not support such collection, as with an oral admission, then even though liens may be biblical in theory the denial is not treated as denial of a land lien. This matters in custodial oaths. The Gemara asks where a Torah custodian oath can exist if every admitted documented deposit produces a land lien. Tosafot distinguishes ordinary deposit documents from certain commercial instruments. If a custodian presently pleads unavoidable accident, the claimant cannot collect from purchasers on the strength of that admission. The defendant's current plea itself prevents the document from functioning as a collectible land lien. Therefore the denial can still generate the Torah custodial oath. A similar question arises with a partially paid document. According to the Sema, a document impaired by partial payment may no longer collect from purchasers because of concern that the creditor could collect too much. If so, denial of its balance would not be denial of a land lien under Ramban. The Shach, however, holds that the unpaid balance can still collect from encumbered property. Under his view the land lien characterization remains.
