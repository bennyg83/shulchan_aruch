Just as one does not swear concerning a lien upon land. In the Perishah I wrote that the obvious rule that no oath is imposed upon denial of a land lien, meaning a document secured by real property, is derived from Bava Metzia 4b. Although Rashi there explains the case as denial concerning land itself, because land is excluded by the Torah's general and specific formulations, a document carrying a land lien is likewise treated as land. One should not say that the document itself is explicitly excluded from the Torah's general and specific formulations, because the "documents" discussed in siman 95 need not be documents that create a real property lien. They include handwritten obligations or formal documents where the claim concerns return of the document itself, such as a claim to two documents with an admission to one. See the end of siman 95. In the Perishah I also explained how a defendant can ever be made to swear regarding denial of a land lien, since ordinarily the holder of a document is the one who swears and collects, as the Tur and Mechaber write here and in siman 82.
It is regarded like an oral admission, etc. See the Tur in this siman, seif 38, who writes in the name of Tosafot and the Rosh that according to the accepted rule that property liens are Torah law, even one who partially admits an oral loan does not take a Torah oath unless he owns no unencumbered land, or the lender waived the lien upon his land. The Mechaber and Rama do not mention this view and rule generally in accordance with the Rambam and the other authorities who make no such distinction. See the Derishah.
