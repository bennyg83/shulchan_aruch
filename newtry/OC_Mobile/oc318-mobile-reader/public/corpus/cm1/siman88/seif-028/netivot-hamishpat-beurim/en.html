See Shach seif katan 48, who asks why the defendant is not believed by a migo: he could have denied the oral loan and admitted only what appears in the document. It can be answered that if he admitted the documented debt it would be heilech, leaving him effectively as one who denies the remainder entirely, so the proposed migo is a migo involving brazenness. The Shach follows his own view that a migo of brazenness may be used to exempt from money. The principal answer is therefore the Shach's own: we do not use a migo to impair a document. The reason one is believed to say, "I returned it," by a migo that he could have said the item was lost through unavoidable accident, is that whenever a debtor has some legally available defense to the document he may reasonably leave it in the other party's possession, so there is no force to the argument, "What is your document doing in my hand?" Tumim explains this in the rules of migo. Here, however, the debtor also owes an oral loan. He cannot plausibly say that he relied upon the possibility of denying the oral debt, because the creditor may demand and collect the oral debt first and only afterward present the written debt. Thus the migo does not undermine the force of the document.
For a document is like land. See Shach seif katan 49, through his statement that the document is heilech even when the defendant owns no land. On this point Tumim's view appears primary. It is considered heilech only where there is land subject to the document. Rambam gives the reason that the defendant cannot deny the document, rather than simply saying that it creates a lien on land, because after the enactment of the Geonim permitting collection even from movable property, as the Rosh writes, a document is no longer categorically a land lien. Rambam's wording itself indicates that inability to deny produces heilech specifically where land is actually encumbered. This is also evident from siman 400 concerning the larger and smaller ox, where a d'oraisa oath applies although the fact of the goring cannot be denied because witnesses saw it.
