Land. The Sema writes that even if the borrower owns no land at all, not even land already encumbered to others, a promissory note is nevertheless treated like land because the document was written for the purpose of enabling collection from land. The Shach writes that it does not appear correct to classify a document as land when the debtor has no encumbered property. According to the Rosh, an oral loan is not treated as land where the debtor owns no land, despite the rule that liens arise by Torah law. The same should therefore be true of a written loan. Tosafot at the beginning of Bava Metzia states this explicitly. The Beit Yosef writes, however, that a promissory note is considered "here it is" even where the debtor has no land at all. If he lacks even movable property, perhaps it is not "here it is." Although Rashi in Bava Metzia implies that the reason a document is "here it is" is that the debtor's land is encumbered by it, that passage concerns even a defendant who does not admit the debt. Where the defendant admits it, the Beit Yosef's view appears correct, namely that even without any land the document is "here it is." Many authorities hold that anything the defendant cannot deny is considered "here it is," as I write below in seif 29. See also siman 75, seif 4 in the gloss, Gidulei Terumah, page 54, and Responsa Rama, siman 95, question 4. The Shach further writes that the borrower is not believed through a migo that he could have denied the oral portion while admitting the amount in the document. Although a migo may operate even against a document, as stated above in siman 296, seif 2, this case is different because the document would remain continuously valid. In siman 296, by contrast, had he claimed that the deposit was lost through unavoidable accident, the document would no longer establish liability.
Orally. The Sema writes: See the Tur, who cites Tosafot and the Rosh that according to the established rule that a lien arises by Torah law, even one who admits part of an oral loan is not obligated in a Torah oath unless he has no unencumbered land, or unless the lender waived the lien upon the land. The Mechaber and Rama do not mention their view and instead rule without qualification like the Rambam and the other Geonim, who make no such distinction. This means that in their view denial of a claim secured by land applies only to a written document that allows collection from encumbered purchasers. Responsa Maharam of Lublin, siman 3, likewise rules that this is the established law. See there, and Tosafot to Bava Batra 175a, s.v. "the lender." Shach. See the Taz for his reconciliation of the Tur's words.
