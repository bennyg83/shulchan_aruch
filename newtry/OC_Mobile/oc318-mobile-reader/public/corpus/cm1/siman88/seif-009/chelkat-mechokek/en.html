She takes it together with its produce. With land that she originally brought in at an appraised value, it is obvious that the principle of preserving the honor of her father's house applies. The same is true of land the husband designated for her corresponding to the appraised dowry, as discussed by Rivash, siman 21. But even produce still attached to the land at the time she leaves belongs to her. It is not clarified here whether she receives that produce without appraisal, because the husband was never granted rights in produce that was not detached during the marriage, or whether the land together with its attached produce is appraised for her. Perhaps the novelty is only that one might otherwise think the land itself must return to her because of the honor of her father's house, while the produce belongs to him and need not even be transferred to her at an appraised value. In the mishnah on Ketubot 79 the dispute of Rabbi Shimon and the first tanna concerns produce of nichsei melog, and there the produce certainly belongs entirely to her because the husband was granted rights only to produce harvested during the marriage, as the Rosh writes. But here, where the land was brought in at an appraised value as nichsei tzon barzel and any appreciation ordinarily belongs to the husband, why should produce that is his be returned to her without payment? I do not know why this rule is stated specifically regarding land brought in at appraisal, which implies nichsei tzon barzel, rather than nichsei melog in accordance with the plain sense of the mishnah.
