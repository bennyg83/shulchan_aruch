And even if he admitted a coin worth half a dinar. Some versions read here, and in the Mechaber, "a coin worth half a gold dinar." This is not correct, for then there would be no need to repeat the case shortly afterward in seif 11. Here the point is only to distinguish between a claim for an ordinary dinar current in commerce, where even an admission of produce or merchandise is considered the same kind because the claimant demanded value, and a claim for a specific gold dinar, where we say that he demanded that very type. In the latter case, not only an admission of merchandise or produce but even an admission of a coin worth half the claimed amount is not considered of the same kind. Seif 11 adds the greater novelty that even when both claim and admission are of gold, they are not considered the same kind, for the reason stated there. Since that seif also teaches that even if the claimant did not merely say "dinar" but said one hundred dinarim in a specified coinage, one might have thought that mentioning the coinage was not intended precisely, as Rashi holds. Therefore it also teaches that even where he claimed one hundred gold dinarim and the defendant admitted half in gold coinage, it is not considered the same kind.
