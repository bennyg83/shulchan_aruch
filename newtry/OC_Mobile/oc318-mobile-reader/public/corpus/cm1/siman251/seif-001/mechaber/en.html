The laws of a deathly ill person's or healthy person's gift. It contains two seifim.
If a person gives a gift while deathly ill and the deed says, "during life and at death," or, "from life and at death," whether he gives all or part of his property, it is a deathly ill person's gift. The words "at death" mean that acquisition occurs only after death, while "during life" expresses his hope that he will recover from the illness. If a healthy person gives a gift and the deed says, "during life and at death," it is a complete lifetime gift. The added words "at death" mean "from now and forever" and are merely ornamental deed language.
