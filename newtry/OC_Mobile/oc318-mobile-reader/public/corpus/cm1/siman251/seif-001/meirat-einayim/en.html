Whether all or part. Meaning, even if a kinyan was made. Despite the usual law of a partial gift, if the giver recovers he may retract because the gift has the law of one who instructs because of death, since he mentioned death. The Tur states this explicitly. Rambam and the Mechaber omit mention of kinyan because from their earlier rules it is already clear that with a gift made because of death, the presence of a kinyan makes no difference. The Tur states it so one should not think that the kinyan converts it into a healthy person's gift from which he cannot retract even if he lives. The case assumes the kinyan includes an enhancement clause. See the Perishah.
This is a gift of a shechiv mera. Meaning, if he recovers he may retract, and even if he does not recover he may retract from this recipient and give it to another, as explained in the preceding siman.
To settle his mind that he will live. Rashbam explains that because he mentioned his death he did not want to "open his mouth to the Satan," and his wording is like saying, "May God save me from this death, for I shall live."
But a healthy person, etc. If the document says "in life and in death," it appears to me that the same applies if it says "during life and in death" with the preposition. I wrote this in the Perishah.
This is a gift, etc. Meaning, where there was a kinyan, whether he gave all or part, because a healthy person's gift is not effective without a kinyan. Specifically where he says "in life and in death" is it a complete present gift. But if he says "during life and after death," he reveals that the gift will not be complete until after death with respect to the produce, while the principal is acquired from today. If he writes, "during my life, if I do not retract before death," he may retract until his death even though a kinyan was made and he was healthy, as the Tur and Mechaber write at the end of siman 257.
