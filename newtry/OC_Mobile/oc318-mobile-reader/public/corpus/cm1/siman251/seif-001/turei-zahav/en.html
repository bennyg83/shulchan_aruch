(Seif 2: Until he brings proof.) The Sema's statement that this exempts the heir from an oath is surprising, because no oath is relevant to him. Even if the heir merely claims that perhaps the donor did not die from that illness, the recipient must bring proof. It appears to me that if the recipient claims, "You, the heir, admitted to me that you know he died from this illness," then indeed the heir must take a rabbinic oath that he made no such admission. The heir now becomes the recipient's litigant by virtue of the alleged admission.
