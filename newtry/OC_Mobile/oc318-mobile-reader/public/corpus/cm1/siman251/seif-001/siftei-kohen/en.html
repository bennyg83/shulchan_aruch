"In life and death," he said, "and if he were to die, and then in life, he said, "to him," and to him, he said, "and to him," and to him, and to him, he said, "And to him."
Whether with all, etc. See the Sema, seif katan 1, who writes that it has the law of instructions given because of impending death, since he mentioned death. The Maggid Mishneh did not explain it this way. See there. Maharib Levi and Ibn Sasson discuss this at length in Sefer 42, and Maharib Levi concludes that it is treated as instructions given because of impending death. See there.
But a healthy person, etc. The document states, 'during life and at death.' It appears to me that the same law applies if it states, 'in life and at death,' with the preposition beit. These are the words of the Sema. This is obvious, for Rav disputes Shmuel only regarding a dangerously ill person. Regarding a healthy person, Rav agrees with Shmuel that even the phrase 'in life' is effective, just as according to Shmuel it is effective for a dangerously ill person. This is clear.
