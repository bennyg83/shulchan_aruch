The recipient must bring proof. Rambam says that if the movables are in the recipient's hands, and he has a migo to say, "They are mine," he can take a hesset oath that the donor had been healthy. Maggid Mishneh suggests that even without such a migo, where witnesses know the property entered the recipient's hands specifically through this gift, the donor may be the one seeking to remove it and therefore bear the burden of proof. Shach rejects this and says that if witnesses saw the property and know it came from the donor, the recipient cannot simply claim a valid gift without proof. If it was a true deathbed gift, it never left the donor's ownership until death, and the recipient who now possesses it must prove that the donor was healthy. Maggid Mishneh can nevertheless be defended by distinguishing the way possession arose. If witnesses know the donor himself physically delivered the movables to the recipient as a gift, that delivery creates a present healthy person's transfer subject only to the implicit deathbed condition that the donor die. The donor cannot retract so long as he remains ill, and if he dies the recipient's title relates back. The remaining doubt is only whether recovery later cancelled the gift. In such a case the recipient entered lawfully and is presently in possession. Rashba rules similarly where a purchaser unquestionably entered property through a valid sale but the parties dispute whether an unstated condition would undo it: the current possessor keeps the property unless the condition is proved. This is so even with land, all the more with movables. By contrast, if the donor merely executed a document or kinyan while retaining the object and the recipient later seized it, then if the donor had been deathly ill the gift remained an ordinary revocable deathbed gift and the recipient's seizure was not a lawful present transfer. In that case he must rely on a migo or other proof. Rambam, Tur, and Rama can be read as addressing this latter case; Maggid Mishneh's separate suggestion addresses actual delivery by the donor. Thus the authorities can be reconciled.
