And if the recipient seized it. See Shach, seif katan 10, who challenges Maggid Mishneh. Ketzot HaChoshen correctly explains the distinction. Where the doubt is whether the donor was healthy or a shechiv mera, and the property was never delivered to the recipient, the uncertainty is whether it ever left the donor's ownership at all, because a shechiv mera gift takes effect only after death. We therefore leave it in the donor's chazakah. Here, however, the donor delivered the gift to the recipient during his lifetime. Siman 125 establishes that in such a case he cannot retract during the illness; recovery alone later defeats the gift, like a condition that the donor die from that illness. Rashba, responsum 972, cited by Beit Yosef in simanim 29 and 46, rules in a case of two sets of witnesses concerning a condition that the sale remains valid. Our case is similarly a doubt concerning whether a condition was fulfilled. Moreover, the recipient's possession is possession obtained with permission, and such seizure is effective. As for the rules that in a doubt whether one was healthy or a shechiv mera the property remains with the heirs, and that in siman 250, seif 10, we do not reduce the recipients' shares because of a possible hidden maneh, those cases rest on a different rationale. Where the facts known to us show a complete transaction, we do not disturb that clear transaction merely because of a speculative possibility. Only when a kinyan itself creates an indication that other property may exist do we take that possibility into account, as Rashba explains and Mishneh LaMelech cites.
