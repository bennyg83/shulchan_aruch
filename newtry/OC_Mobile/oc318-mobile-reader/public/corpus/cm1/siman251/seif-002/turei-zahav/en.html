(That he was healthy.) Regarding the Sema's statement in seif katan 5 that a strengthening clause also applies to a healthy person, it appears to me that a distinction must be made. The strengthening clause discussed in siman 250, seif 18, explained in our master's words in seif 34, uses wording such as, "Give, and also write for him." This applies to a healthy person as well, since people commonly write this to reveal that they are not among those who give but do not wish to execute a document, lest additional documents be issued against them. But the strengthening clause attached to a kinyan uses the formula, "And we made a kinyan from him in addition to this gift." There is no greater proof than this that the act was done while he was deathly ill, for what addition would this be in the case of a healthy person, whose gift is entirely ineffective without a kinyan? Therefore, it appears to me that in such a case the recipient has no claim against the donor. We are witnesses, as it were, that he was deathly ill, and the donor need not even take a rabbinic oath. This is clear in my opinion.
