The gift of a dangerously ill person, etc. See Responsa Rabbi Aharon Sasson, siman 165.
And if the recipient seized possession, etc. The same law applies when the dangerously ill person recovered and says, 'I gave it to you while I was dangerously ill, and I now retract,' whereas the recipient says that the donor was healthy. The recipient must bring proof. If the movable property is in the recipient's possession, then because he could have claimed that it was his, he takes a rabbinic oath that the donor was healthy. These are the Rambam's words in chapter 9 of the Laws of Acquisition and Gift. The Maggid Mishneh writes that even without a migo, since there are witnesses that the property came into his possession by reason of this gift, the donor should be treated as one seeking to remove property from another, because the movables are now in the recipient's possession. He therefore leaves the matter requiring study. In my humble opinion, there is no difficulty here. The Kesef Mishneh likewise writes that the Rambam's ruling is obvious, and this is also the view of the Tur and Rama here. In an ordinary case, if there are witnesses and the item was seen, the possessor cannot claim that it was given to him as a gift. The same applies here. Since the witnesses do not know whether the recipient acquired it through a valid gift, and we rule that the gift of a dangerously ill person takes effect only after death, as explained at the beginning of the next siman, if this was indeed such a gift the property never left the donor's ownership. The recipient must therefore prove that it left the donor's ownership. Otherwise, even in every ordinary case we could say that the possessor is believed to say that he purchased it or received it as a gift. Rather, since there are witnesses and the item was seen, it remains in the original owner's legal possession. The same applies here. This is clear. It is unlike the law above in siman 72, seif 23, seif katan 109, where witnesses know that the article came to him as collateral but do not know for how much, and the lender is believed. There, it was delivered to him as collateral, and therefore he is believed as to the amount for which it was pledged. Here, however, the situation is different. Examine carefully.
