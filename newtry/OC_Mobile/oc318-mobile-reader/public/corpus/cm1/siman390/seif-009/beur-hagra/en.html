But, etc. This depends upon the dispute cited above in seif 7, whether, after seizure, he receives half the damage or one quarter. Rosh. Rambam maintains that there is no uncertainty: it is a derivative of horn and pays half damages. Rashba writes that Rif's view in the case of seeds adhering to the animal is that full damages are paid, as implied by Rif's wording, unlike Rosh's forced reading. Once the Gemara establishes the case as one involving seeds, it is no longer a case of pebbles. This is also explicit in the Yerushalmi: a rooster that caused damage by the wind between its wings pays half damages; Symmachus says full damages. If it blew into a vessel and broke it, it pays full damages, indicating that all agree. Rif must therefore have read the Gemara, 'rather, where there are, etc.'
