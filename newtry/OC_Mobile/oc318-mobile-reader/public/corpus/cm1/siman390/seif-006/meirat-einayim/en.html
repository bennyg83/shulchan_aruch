It was walking in the public domain and kicked, etc. Wherever the law mentions pebbles propelled in the normal course, it means pebbles kicked up while walking, a derivative of foot. The owner pays half damages only in the injured party's domain, while in the public domain he is completely exempt, like ordinary foot damage. Whenever pebbles are mentioned together with kicking, however, this is pebbles propelled through an unusual act. Animals commonly walk and thereby scatter pebbles, but they do not ordinarily kick backward with force and thereby send pebbles elsewhere. It was explained above that kicking is an unusual act. The Gemara therefore leaves unresolved whether there is a category of unusual conduct within the law of pebbles. The Rambam understands that the doubt creates both a leniency and a stringency. If there is such an unusual category, then even in the injured party's domain the owner pays only one quarter damages, half of the ordinary half damages for pebbles. Conversely, even in the public domain he pays as for horn, because the act is unusual. If there is no special unusual category for pebbles, the act retains the law of ordinary pebbles, and therefore is exempt in the public domain and liable for half damages in the injured party's domain. Since the Gemara leaves the matter unresolved, the Rambam and Mechaber follow their general position that seizure is effective in a doubtful monetary matter. Accordingly, if the animal kicked and caused damage in the public domain, the owner is exempt because the burden of proof is upon the claimant and perhaps there is no unusual category for pebbles. If the injured party seized property, however, he has the upper hand to say that there is an unusual category and the law is that of horn. Yet once he relies upon that position, he must also accept its lenient side, namely that only one quarter damages is payable. Even that quarter is collectible only from the body of the offending ox, because the liability is then like that of an innocuous horn. This is why the Mechaber concludes that the matter is doubtful. If the kicking caused damage in the injured party's domain, the owner must in any event pay one quarter damages, because even if there is an unusual category for pebbles, that quarter is due. That quarter too is collectible only from the body of the offending animal. If the injured party seized the full half damages, even from other property, it is not removed from him because he may argue that there is no unusual category for pebbles and the case is therefore ordinary foot derived pebbles, for which half damages is payable from all the tortfeasor's property in the injured party's domain.
