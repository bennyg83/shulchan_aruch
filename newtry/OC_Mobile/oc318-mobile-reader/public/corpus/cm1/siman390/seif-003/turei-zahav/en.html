At the end of seif 12. The Tur objects: “This is not clear, for how could he become liable?” The Beit Yosef writes that the Tur was imprecise. I say that the Tur's objection is sound. The baraita stating that when the movement is from above downward they are exempt must refer to a reversed movement, as Rav Pappa explains. Thus one aspect follows the ordinary manner, namely movement from above downward, while the other aspect is unusual because the body is inverted. It is therefore difficult why the Rambam rules that one pays full damages when moving from below upward without inversion, for there too one aspect is unusual, namely the upward movement. This is the Tur's point: why should the unusual element of moving upward create greater liability than the ordinary downward movement, where there is also one unusual element because the body is inverted? We must conclude that the Rambam did not have the reading in the Gemara that those moving from above downward are exempt.
