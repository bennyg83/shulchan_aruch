There, law 6. The Maggid Mishneh writes that Rambam learned these laws from the preceding question. Rambam understands that if there is a legal distinction for pebbles thrown in an unusual manner, that creates both a leniency and a stringency: a leniency that even in the victim's domain liability is only one quarter, and a stringency that even in the public domain one pays one quarter damages, because it becomes a derivative of horn. When we say that pebbles are a derivative of foot and therefore exempt in the public domain, that is only in their ordinary manner. If there is a distinction for unusual pebbles, then when caused by kicking one is liable even in the public domain but pays only one quarter. If there is no distinction, then in the public domain he is entirely exempt even though the act was unusual, while in the victim's domain he pays half damages. The question remained unresolved.
