As a matter of law. This concerns an unresolved question in the Gemara. All agree that if the animal caused damage in the injured party's domain, no payment can be extracted unless the injured party seized the damaging animal itself. He may then collect half damages from its body, because the unresolved doubt permits us to say that the conduct was abnormal and therefore a derivative of horn, and horn pays only half damages from the animal itself. The Tur concludes that even if the same animal damaged the same person once in the public domain and once in the injured party's domain, the law remains the same. The claimant cannot argue: if it is horn, pay me also for the public-domain incident, and if it is foot, pay full damages for the private-domain incident. In both directions we rule leniently for the defendant. The Sema distinguishes this from siman 200 concerning a purchaser's utensils in the seller's domain, where a double doubt may establish acquisition, saying that our case involves a penalty because the owner's own body did not cause the damage, only his ox. This reasoning has no basis, and the Bach already objected. The Bach's proposal that the cases concern two animals is also unconvincing. Rather, even with one animal, since the doubt is whether its act was ordinary and unintentional or deliberate and therefore horn, it is possible that the animal sometimes acts one way and sometimes the other. Perhaps in the public domain it damaged in the course of normal walking without intent, while in the injured party's domain it intended to damage and the act was horn. This is clear. Shach.
