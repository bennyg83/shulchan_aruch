[Seif 2] Some say that in every unresolved legal question, and so forth. The Sema's answer, that a penalty is different, is exceedingly difficult. First, what source does the Tur have for such a distinction? On the contrary, there should be no distinction. Since the claimant is in possession and the defendant is liable for the penalty under Torah law, why should the claimant not prevail by a Torah-law argument of “either way”? Second, according to the Sema, this ruling is not connected to the Rosh's opinion where the Tur places it, whereas the arrangement of the Tur clearly indicates that it applies specifically according to the Rosh. It appears to me that the matter depends on the general question whether possession is effective in a legal doubt. The Ramah holds that possession is effective in an unresolved question of law, as is necessarily evident at the end of siman 242, seif 3, where his position accords with the Rambam. Therefore, the purchaser's right in that case was not extinguished by the first doubt, because even then he could retain money from the seller. When a second doubt later arose, he may assert an “either way” claim, and even without prior possession it is sufficient that one of the two parties pay him. Here, however, we are following Rabbeinu Yitzchak and the Rosh, who hold that possession does not help in a legal doubt. Once the first doubt arose, the injured party had no possibility of collecting from the tortfeasor as he wished, and that obligation was therefore extinguished as though it had never existed. When the second doubt later arose, it is treated as standing alone, without a prior doubt to create an “either way” claim. The Tur therefore placed this ruling specifically after the Rosh's view. This resembles the rule in Yevamot 37b concerning a doubtful son and a yavam, where the prior judgment stands and an “either way” claim does not help. This appears correct.
