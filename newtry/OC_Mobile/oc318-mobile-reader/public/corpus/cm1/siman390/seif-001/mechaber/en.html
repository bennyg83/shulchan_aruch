Regel, damage caused by an animal's normal walking, is a primary category of damages, as expressed in the Torah's phrase concerning letting one's animal loose. It occurs when an animal walks with its feet and causes damage in the normal course of walking, where this is its usual manner and the animal derives no benefit from the act. Anything similar is a derivative and has the same law. Thus, if it damages with its body while walking, with its hair, with a load-sack Bartenura explains in the first chapter of Bava Kamma that this means burdens carried in sacks and packs upon it, with the bridle meaning a rein in its mouth, with the bell on its neck, or with the load on its back; or if chickens fly from place to place and break utensils with their wings; or if they tread and dance meaning trampling and hopping on dough or fruit and soil it, or peck at it; or anything attached to the animal's body causes damage, such as a wagon being pulled over utensils and breaking them, all these are derivatives of regel and have its law.
