But, etc. This follows our text of the Gemara. The Rif and Rambam read instead: "but was it not taught, a dog and a kid that jumped, whether... are liable"; the Gemara explains the first baraisa as referring to an inverted manner. This resolves Tosafos' question there, s.v. "but was it not taught." Hence the Shulchan Aruch says, "they jumped," etc., "but," etc. According to Tosafos and the Rosh, jumping upward is entirely exempt even from half damages. See Tosafos there, s.v. "a person," concerning the alternate reading, which must also be supplied according to the Rif and Rambam.
(Collected note) They jumped, etc. This follows the Rif. According to his approach Tosafos' question is resolved, and the Gemara's words "if so," etc., are also readily understood, as Tosafos explains in s.v. "a person." Otherwise one might have said that the exemption was because jumping upward is an unavoidable accident. Under Tosafos' explanation, it is strained to say that jumping downward is also an accident. (End of note.)
