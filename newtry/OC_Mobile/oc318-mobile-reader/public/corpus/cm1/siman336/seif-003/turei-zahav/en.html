(Seif 2: Or it is in his possession) The Gemara earlier, codified in siman 166, rules that when a wall falls into another's garden, the garden does not acquire the stones merely because they entered his courtyard, since the wall owner did not intend to abandon them there; the recipient must physically clear them into his domain after the owner agrees that he may keep them. The Sema distinguishes our case because the worker has wages due from the employer, but the Gemara places both cases in the same Mishnah and explains why each is needed. That analysis shows that the final law is the same in both; otherwise there would be no true parallel. Here too, therefore, mere presence in the worker's domain is insufficient unless he actively moved the items there after the employer accepted them as wages. The text need not say so expressly because the case concerns collecting ownerless materials lying in the public domain; when it says they are “in his possession,” it necessarily means the worker removed them from the public domain into his own. The same is true where the work concerns the employer's materials lying in public. If the objects were already in the worker's domain before the employer accepted them as payment, the employer's acceptance alone does not transfer ownership, exactly as in siman 166.
