But if, etc. Tosafos there, s.v. "la'asos, etc." Although there 76a the Gemara cites the baraisa regarding a worker hired to work in another's property, that is because there the agent said, "your wages are upon me." That is why there the Gemara distinguishes whether he said, "your wages are upon me" or not. Here no such distinction is made. This is the meaning of Tur and Shulchan Aruch's phrase "even if he did not, etc.; and if he said, etc." This is also Tosafos's intent when they speak of an unspecified case in another's property. Specifically where nothing was said, but if the agent said, "your wages are upon the employer," even in the first clause the same rule applies. The distinction is only in an unspecified case.
