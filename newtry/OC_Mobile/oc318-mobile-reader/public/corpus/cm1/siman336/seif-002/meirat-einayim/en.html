To work with his own property, etc. This teaches that even where the artisan worked on the employer's own property and the owner is free to give his property to whomever he wishes, he still cannot say, 'Take what you made as your wages.' One might otherwise think that only where the work was performed on another person's property would he be unable to say this.

Or it is in his domain. Although in siman 166, where stones from a fallen wall lie in another's garden, the garden does not acquire them merely because they are there, that case is different. There the wall owner owes no labor payment until the gardener actually clears the stones, so his statement may merely be a way to push him off. Here the employer already owes wages. When he says, 'Take this as your wages,' the worker acquires it immediately.
