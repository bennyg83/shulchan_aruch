Take what you produced as your wage. Divrei Mishpat writes that according to Rashi in Bava Metzia 118, the reason a worker cannot be paid with the object he produced is derived from the verse requiring payment of his wage, which implies payment according to what was stipulated with him. Accordingly, if an agent hired workers and told them, "Your wage is upon the employer," in which case the agent does not violate the prohibition of delaying wages, as explained in siman 339 seif 7, the employer could seemingly say, "Take what you produced as your wage." Maharsha there states this explicitly. However, Shitah Mekubetzet cites Rashba that nothing can be inferred from the wording of the verse concerning the form of payment. Rather, the reason is that the worker hired himself because he needs money for his evening food and should not have to go to the market to sell goods. According to this reason, even where an agent hired him and the prohibition of delaying wages does not apply to the agent, the employer still cannot say, "Take what you produced as your wage." Rama in siman 332 seif 4 rules that where one is not required to give the worker an object as his wage, he may later pay him its monetary value, which indicates that Rama follows Rashba. This also explains Sema seif katan 3, who cites Mordechai that the rule is specifically with straw and stubble, but with edible items such as wheat and barley one may heed the employer and accept them. See there. Shach seif katan 2 appears not to agree with Mordechai. Rashash suggests that Mordechai may refer specifically to wheat and barley, which are not immediately edible as they are, whereas with baked bread and similar ready food even Shach would agree.
