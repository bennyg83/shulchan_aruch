Or to gather ownerless property for him. Bava Metzia distinguishes between a worker who performs an act of lifting ownerless property and one who merely watches it. If the worker lifts a found object for the employer, the employer can acquire through the ordinary rule that one who lifts a found object for another acquires for him. My son, Rabbi Berish Kohen, asked how Rava resolves the baraita according to the view that watching ownerless property can itself acquire. If mere watching acquires for the worker, why should the employer not acquire through him just as with lifting? Tosafot explains that "looking" in ownerless property does not mean bare eyesight but some minimal act, such as making a small fence. Rashi, however, explains that acquisition can occur because the worker's attention causes the property to be preserved. Bare viewing without preservation does not acquire. Even if the worker himself can acquire through such protective watching, the employer does not necessarily acquire through him. We rule like Shmuel that sitting and guarding is not legally equivalent to performing the work for the special Torah right of a worker to eat. Likewise, Rosh writes concerning agency for hearing a wife's vow that agency does not apply to matters that occur automatically rather than through a legal act. Therefore, lifting works for the employer because it is an affirmative acquisition act and agency applies. Watching, even if it gives the watcher some personal acquisition right because the property is preserved through him, is not an act capable of agency. The worker can acquire for himself, but not for the employer through the ordinary rule of agency.
