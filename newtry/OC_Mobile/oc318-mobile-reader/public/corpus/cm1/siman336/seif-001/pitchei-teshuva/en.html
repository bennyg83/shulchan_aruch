What you made shall be your wages. Divrei Mishpat writes that according to Rashi, Bava Metzia 118, the reason is connected to the verse concerning timely payment of wages, which implies that the employer must pay what he stipulated. Accordingly, where an agent hires workers and tells them that their wages are upon the employer, in which case the agent does not violate the prohibition of delaying wages, as stated in siman 339, seif 7, the employer could perhaps say, "Take what you made as your wages." Maharsha there states this explicitly. Shitah Mekubetzet, however, cites Rashba that the rule does not derive from the verse. Rather, a worker hires himself because he needs money for food that evening and does not want to go to the market to sell goods. According to that reason, even where an agent hired him and the technical prohibition of delayed wages does not apply, the employer cannot force him to accept the product in place of money. Rama in siman 332, seif 4, who rules that where an employer need not give an object as wages he may later pay its monetary value, appears to follow Rashba. This also explains Sema, seif katan 3, citing Mordechai that the rule applies specifically to straw and stubble, but with edible commodities such as wheat and barley the worker may be compelled to accept them. Shach, seif katan 2, appears not to accept Mordechai's distinction. Rabbi Shmuel Strashun suggests that perhaps Shach disagrees only with wheat and barley, which are not edible in their present form, while he would agree regarding baked bread or similar ready food.
