He gives him his full wage. See above, siman 37, Tur at the end of seif 4, where he cites Ramah regarding a sharecropper who comes to testify for a possessor against a challenger. Even though he labored in the field, "the challenger must pay him the wage for his labor, like one who hires a worker to perform work on his own property and then points him to another's property." I wrote there that "the challenger must pay" is a scribal error and should read that the one whose possession is challenged must pay. It is the hirer who pays the worker whom he hired for his own property and then directed to another's property, as the Tur and Mechaber rule here. This is an explicit baraita and the consensus of the authorities. See there and examine carefully.
And he recovers from the owner the amount by which he benefited him. See the beginning of siman 332, where the Tur and Mechaber discuss this, and what I wrote there in seif katan 2.
He cannot say, "Take what you produced as your wages." A practical case is where the product is worth less than the wage. Even if it is worth the full amount, the worker must ordinarily be paid in cash, in accordance with the laws of worker payment. See the Perishah. Mordechai at the beginning of Bava Kamma cites Maharam that this applies specifically to straw and stubble. With food items such as wheat or barley, the employer may say, "Take what you produced as your wage." Hagahot Maimoniyot, chapter 9 of Hilchot Sechirut, says the same; see Darkei Moshe.
But if he hired him without specification to work on another's property, etc. Meaning, he told him, "Work in this field," and the worker knew the field belonged to someone else. Even if the hirer did not expressly say, "Work in another person's field," since he did not say, "Your wage is upon me," and the worker knew it was another's, the hirer is not responsible for the wage. But if the worker did not know the field belonged to another, then even though the hirer did not say, "Work with me in my field," it is included in the case of one who hires a worker for his own property and shows him another's, because the worker assumed it belonged to the hirer. See the Tur below, siman 339, seif 6, where the Rosh explains Ramah's phrase "he hired him to work in a field without specification" to mean that he did not tell him it belonged to another, but the worker knew this independently. I explained this there in seif katan 15 and in the Perishah.
The same applies to one who hires a teacher for another person's son. Meaning, he does so in the presence of the child's father, and the father remains silent. Rama already wrote this in siman 81, seif 7, and explained the reason there.
One who said to his son-in-law, "Study, etc." Mordechai's case speaks of a wealthy son-in-law. Rama records the same law above, siman 81, seif 1, and Darkei Moshe discusses it both there and here. The reason is that since the son-in-law is not poor and is independently obligated to study with his son, he can say, "I was merely joking with you." Responsa Rashba, siman 643, discusses Reuven who hired a teacher for his own son and his fellow's son, and the fellow agreed to pay his share. Halfway through the term the fellow hired another teacher. He remains obligated to pay the first teacher. His claim that he hired him for the year only on condition that he perform properly and that the term would last only until Pesach constitutes an admission to part of the claim and obligates a Torah oath. See also Responsa Rashba, siman 1192.
