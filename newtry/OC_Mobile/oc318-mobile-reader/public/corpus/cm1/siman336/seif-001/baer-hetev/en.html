Without specification. Meaning, he told the worker, "Perform work in this field," and the worker knew that the field belonged to someone else. Although he did not expressly say, "Work in my fellow's field," since he did not say, "Your wages are upon me," and the worker knew it belonged to another, payment of the wage does not fall upon the one who hired him. Whenever the worker did not know that the field belonged to another, however, even if the hirer did not say, "Work with me in my own field," it is nevertheless included in the case of one who hires a worker to work in his own property but shows him property belonging to another, since the worker thought it belonged to the hirer. His fellow. The Rashba writes in Responsa, siman 643: Reuven hired a teacher for his son and for the son of his fellow, and his fellow agreed to pay his share. Halfway through the term, his fellow arose and hired another teacher. He must nevertheless pay the first teacher. As to his claim that he did not hire him for a year unconditionally, but only on condition that he perform his work properly, and that the period of hire was intended to extend only until Pesach, by this claim he admits part of the claim and is obligated in a Torah oath.
