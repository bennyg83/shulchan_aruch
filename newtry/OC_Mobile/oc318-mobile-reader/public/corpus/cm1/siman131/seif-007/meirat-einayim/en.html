Whatever he proves that he gave the gentile, etc., even if the gentile made a false claim, etc. In the Derishah I explained that this does not contradict siman 93:15 and siman 176, where in a comparable matter the party is not reimbursed until he proves that the principal debtor did not pay. There the gentile seller conducted his primary business with Levi and merely obtained Reuven's additional undertaking that if Levi did not pay, Reuven would. Since the gentile agreed to direct his first demand against Levi, Reuven had no reason to anticipate that the gentile would instead proceed against him, which was also an unusual event. Therefore Shimon was not required there to give Reuven a writing assuming responsibility for such a claim. Here, however, Shimon deliberately placed himself as guarantor for Reuven toward a gentile, and under their law they commonly proceed first against the guarantor. Therefore Reuven must fully indemnify him. See the Perishah here and in siman 93.
Or if he wrote between the lines, etc. The correct reading is without the conjunction "and." It refers back to the preceding clause. The meaning is that the gentile either wrote a separate note stating how much he received, or wrote in his own handwriting between the lines of the document how much he received. If the text read "and it was written between the lines," it would imply that such a notation suffices even when we do not know who wrote it. That certainly cannot be correct, because the promissory note is in Shimon's possession and we must fear that he himself forged a notation for more than he actually paid.
That the gentile released him. The Mordechai concludes that even if he has witnesses that the judge released him, so long as the gentile himself did not release him, the borrower remains responsible to remove the liability from him. Darkei Moshe cites this.
For then he collects without an oath, etc. The reason is that once a person placed a guarantor before a gentile, and gentiles commonly make false claims, the guarantor must be indemnified. It is therefore treated as though he incurred expenditures for his fellow with authorization, a situation in which, as explained several times above, he may take an oath and collect.
