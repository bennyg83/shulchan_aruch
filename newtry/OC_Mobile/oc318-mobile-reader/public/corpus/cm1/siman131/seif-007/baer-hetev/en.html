And he coerced him. One cannot object from siman 96 seif 15, where in a similar case it appears that payment is not required until it is established that the principal debtor did not pay. There, the non-Jewish seller's principal transaction was with Levi, and only as added security did he take Reuven's undertaking that if Levi did not pay, Reuven would. Since the non-Jew agreed that his first claim would be against Levi, Reuven did not anticipate that the non-Jew would proceed directly against him, and that is also an unusual occurrence. Therefore Shimon was not required there to give Reuven a separate indemnity. Here, however, Reuven deliberately made himself guarantor to a non-Jew, and under their law they proceed first against the guarantor. Reuven must therefore reimburse him in every event. This is the Sema's explanation. The Taz objects that in the other case too the partner became guarantor for Levi. He answers instead that there the claimant says, “Perhaps Levi paid the non-Jew and the non-Jew released him in your presence, but later made a false claim against you.” In such a case, it was the guarantor's own misfortune that caused the loss, as Rama states below. See Responsa Rama, siman 86, and Responsa Mahari ben Lev, book II, siman 66.

That is written. The Sema says the text should read “he wrote,” without the conjunction, referring back to the preceding clause: the non-Jew wrote a separate receipt, or wrote in his own hand between the lines how much he received. The reading “and it is written” would imply that this amount appears between the lines of the document without knowing who wrote it, which cannot suffice, for if the debt document is in Shimon's hands we must fear that he forged an entry for more than he actually paid. Bach disagrees, arguing that perhaps Shimon colluded with the non-Jew and therefore is not believed merely by an oath; he explains the case as one where the receipt or interlineation appears in the non-Jewish court records. The Taz also disagrees with the Sema and maintains that the reading “and it is written” is correct, meaning that the document remains in the non-Jew's possession. The Shach adds that the Sema's assumption that the debt document is in Shimon's hands is not compelled, for perhaps Shimon paid only part of it and therefore had to record the partial payment without receiving the document. See Responsa Rama, siman 15.
