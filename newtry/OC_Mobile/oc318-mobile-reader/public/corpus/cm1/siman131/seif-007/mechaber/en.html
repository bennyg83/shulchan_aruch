Shimon became guarantor to a non-Jew on Reuven's behalf. Reuven departed, and Shimon had to pay for him. When Reuven returned, Shimon sued him, and Reuven said, 'You should not have paid him, because I had already paid him and possess his receipt.' Shimon replied, 'I had to pay so long as you did not leave the receipt with me and left the debt instrument in the non-Jew's possession.' The law follows Shimon. Reuven must reimburse every amount Shimon proves he gave the non-Jew, even if the non-Jew demanded the money unlawfully and compelled him to pay. Proof may be through witnesses, a recognizable note from the non-Jew, or a notation written between the lines of the document. {Rama: The same applies whenever a Jew guarantees for another Jew to a non-Jew.
