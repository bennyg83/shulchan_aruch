Who obligated themselves conditionally, etc., because it is an asmakhta. The Tur concludes: Although every guarantor's undertaking contains an element of asmakhta, an ordinary guarantor is different because he did not make his obligation depend upon some separate event. Here, because he suspended his liability upon a condition, he does not fully resolve to obligate himself, relying instead on the expectation that the condition will not occur and he will never become liable.
Or if it will not be, etc. Our editions of Maimonides likewise read, "or if." On that reading the meaning would be that the rule applies whether the condition is phrased, "if such and such occurs," or, "if such and such does not occur." But this is unnecessarily verbose, for why should there be a difference? The better reading is that of the Tur: "I am guarantor if such and such occurs, and if it does not occur, I am not." The formulation then makes sense as a fully doubled condition. See the Perishah.
