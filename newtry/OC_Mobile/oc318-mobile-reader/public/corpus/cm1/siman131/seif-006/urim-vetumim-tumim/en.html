A Jew, etc. See Panim Meirot, responsa 20 and 101, who agrees with the Shach against the Sema. He further writes that even according to the Sema, the rule applies only with a non Jew, because fear of him is like fear of a lion. With a Jew, even the Sema would agree that the proposed guarantor should have said, "I do not wish to accept him." The matter requires study. If there was no fear, the guarantor himself could likewise have said, "I do not wish to become guarantor." Why should the principal party's failure to object be treated differently from the guarantor's own acceptance?
