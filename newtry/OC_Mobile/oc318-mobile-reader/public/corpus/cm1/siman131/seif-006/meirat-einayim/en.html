The warning that he gave him has no effect. The Tur explains that the guarantor could have evaded the gentile and refused to become guarantor. Since he failed to do so and instead acknowledged the guaranty before the gentile, the creditor could no longer evade, because everyone knew that this person was fit to be accepted as guarantor and he had already acknowledged to the gentile that the pledge would be returned on the strength of his guaranty. This might imply that if the creditor warned the guarantor before he acknowledged the guaranty, the warning would be effective, because then the guarantor should have avoided the gentile and said, "I will not put up my money on a guaranty, only against collateral." Yet the Mechaber and Rama omit this distinction. It appears that they hold that even then the warning is ineffective, because the owner of the pledge can answer: Why should I quarrel with the gentile more than you? You are unwilling to tell the gentile that you refuse to guarantee him, yet you expect me to say that I will not accept you as guarantor even though it is known that you are financially reliable. They understand that Rashi's responsum mentioned that he had "already acknowledged" merely because that was what occurred, not as a limitation of the law. The Mechaber likewise describes the ordinary sequence in which the gentile first approached the creditor and the creditor consented, because people ordinarily do not come with such a request without first knowing the likely response. But if they came together without prior consent, the warning would still be ineffective. This appears the better legal conclusion.
