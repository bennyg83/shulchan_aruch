(See there: Or it is written between the lines). The reading “it is written” is correct. The note remains in the gentile's possession, but a partial payment is recorded between its lines. At this stage any person's writing suffices, because the notation functions as a receipt. Proof appears from the Tur's wording: “or he wrote in the note, between its lines, that he paid such-and-such.” If the note were already in the guarantor's hands, why would it say “such-and-such”? It should say that he paid everything. There is therefore no need for Sema's emendation deleting the conjunction. According to Sema, if the gentile himself wrote it, that is identical with the earlier case of an authenticated receipt. What difference would there be between a separate receipt and a notation on the note itself?
