And he admitted. The Sema writes that the Tur implies that if the guarantor warned the pledge-holder before the latter admitted the facts, the warning is effective. The pledge-holder should have evaded the non-Jew by saying, “I do not give my money on a guarantee, only against collateral.” The Mechaber and Rama, however, appear to hold that even then the warning is ineffective, because the owner of the collateral can answer, “Why should I provoke the non-Jew more than you?” The Shach disagrees and rules that the pledge-holder must first have admitted the guarantor's version. The Mechaber therefore carefully writes “and he admitted his words,” and the Levush likewise implies this. Gidulei Terumah also writes that this requirement is clear. The Taz agrees with the Shach and writes that the Sema's position is very difficult.
Guarantor. The reason here is that he expressly said, “I am guarantor,” unlike the preceding case, where he said, “I remain as I was,” language capable of another interpretation. Darkei Moshe at the end of siman 129 cites a responsum of the Rosh: if Shimon served as administrator for a government official and refused to give Reuven an allowance that Reuven regularly received from the official unless Levi guaranteed that, should the official refuse to credit the payment, Levi would reimburse him, and afterward Shimon claims that the official did not credit it, while Reuven died and Shimon seeks payment from Levi, and Levi claims perhaps Reuven already paid you, the law favors the guarantor.
