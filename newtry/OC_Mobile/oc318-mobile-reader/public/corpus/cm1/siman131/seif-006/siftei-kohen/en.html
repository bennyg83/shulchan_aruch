The warning he gave him is of no effect. See Sema, seif katan 12. The Tur concludes with the reason that the guarantor should have avoided the non-Jew and put him off rather than become guarantor. The Sema says that because the Mechaber and Rama omit this reason, they apparently hold that the warning is ineffective in any event. In my humble opinion, nothing was omitted. On the contrary, the Mechaber precisely writes that the borrower acknowledged the guarantor’s words. This appears to me the practical ruling: the borrower must initially acknowledge the guarantor’s warning. The Levush implies the same. I later found that Gedolei Terumah also writes that it is evident that such prior acknowledgment is specifically required.
