And he admitted, etc. Sema inferred from Tur that if the guarantor warned the pledge-holder before admitting the debt, that warning may suffice, because the guarantor can say he could have denied the gentile's claim or insisted that payment be made only against collateral. Mechaber and Rama, however, imply that the pledge-holder's prior admission is essential, because otherwise he can say, 'Why should I quarrel with the gentile more than you?' The Shach, Taz, Gidulei Terumah, and Responsa Pnei Moshe all agree that the admission must precede the warning. See Tumim. The correct practical law is clearly with the Shach.
A guarantor. Here the guarantor expressly said, 'I am guarantor,' unlike the preceding case where he merely said, 'I stand as before,' which can bear another interpretation. Sema. See also Darkei Moshe at the end of siman 129, citing Responsa Rosh concerning one who acted as guardian for a prince and required another person to guarantee payment if the prince did not credit a certain allowance. When the recipient died and the guarantor claimed perhaps the recipient had already been paid, Rosh ruled for the guarantor.
