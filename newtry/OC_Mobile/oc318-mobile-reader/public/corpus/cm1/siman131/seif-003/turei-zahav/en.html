(Seif 6) A Jew who had in his possession. It is difficult, for guaranty is not enforceable despite its contingency except because the lender relied on the guarantor. Here the guarantor protested that the lender should not rely on him. What, then, creates his obligation? The gentile's earlier promise to act upon the guarantor's word is treated as though the lender relied upon and extended the credit at that time, because the gentile could no longer escape the commitment. It therefore appears clear that unless the gentile first acknowledged that he would act on the guarantor's instruction, no guaranty exists. For this reason the Mechaber carefully writes, “and he agreed to his words.” Sema's assertion that the Mechaber omitted an element is almost unintelligible, because the Mechaber expressly includes this admission, which is the foundation of the ruling. The authorities' explanation is correct. The Tur's statement that everyone knows the gentile to be financially secure applies only where no particular person was identified. If a specific person was identified and acknowledged the arrangement, it is unnecessary that his solvency be publicly known.
