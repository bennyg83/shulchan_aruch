Be released. If the guarantor expressly stipulated this condition when assuming the guarantee, the law follows his stipulation. See Responsa Raanach 60. Shach.
Blighted. All the more so if the assets were stolen from him, since violent persons too are liable to fall from power, as stated in siman 111:13. Sema.
Remember. The Tur and Shulchan Aruch write this at the end of siman 183. See the Sema, who cites the Rama's gloss in the name of Responsa Maharam Mintz concerning Reuven who guaranteed to a non-Jew that Shimon would appear in judgment. See there at length.
To wait. This applies specifically where the coercive non-Jew had forgotten the matter and it was uncertain whether he would ever demand the money. An ordinary guarantor is different. Therefore the Rama writes immediately afterward that the guarantor may compel the borrower to act. So writes Darkei Moshe, and the matter is straightforward, unlike the Ir Shushan's assertion that the Rama contradicts himself. Sema.
