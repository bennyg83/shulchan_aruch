The guarantor's ability to compel the borrower to obtain his release from the guaranty depends on the dispute following Ramban. According to the authorities who disagree with Ramban and allow the guarantor greater protection, he can compel release in the circumstances stated. According to the Mechaber's principal ruling that the creditor cannot be forced in the same manner, the guarantor's ability to compel the borrower is correspondingly limited. See the Shach.
The rule requiring the creditor's reassurance applies specifically in the unusual case where a gentile creditor may have forgotten the debt entirely and it is doubtful whether he will ever demand payment. An ordinary guarantor faces a normal outstanding obligation and does not receive that special protection. Therefore the Shulchan Aruch later says an ordinary guarantor can compel the borrower to act in the usual way. This is Sema's distinction.
