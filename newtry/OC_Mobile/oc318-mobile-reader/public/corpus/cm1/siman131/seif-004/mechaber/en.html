If, when the debt falls due, the guarantor goes to the lender and warns him to demand his money from the borrower, saying that otherwise he will be released from the guarantee, but the lender refuses and extends the borrower's time, some say the guarantor is not thereby released. Certainly an unconditional guarantor is not released. This applies even when the borrower has known property and the guarantor tells the lender, 'Proceed to judgment against the borrower and collect your debt from his property,' but the lender grants the borrower more time and the property meanwhile becomes devastated. The lender may collect from the guarantor. This ruling requires deliberation. {Rama: Some disagree. Nevertheless, if the borrower was prepared to give the lender his money and the guarantor sent for the lender to come receive it or send a document enabling collection of the debt, but the lender refused and the debt was lost, the guarantor is exempt, as stated in Mahariv 80. When the due date arrives, the guarantor may compel the borrower to release him from the guarantee, because he may say, 'I agreed to be guarantor only until this point.' If the lender is a non-Jew, the borrower gave the money to the guarantor to pay him, and the non-Jew forgot the debt while the money remained with the guarantor, the guarantor must return the money to the borrower. He cannot say, 'Perhaps the non-Jew will remember,' because the guarantor undertook the guarantee for the borrower and trusted him, and must continue waiting on his behalf. See note 40.}
