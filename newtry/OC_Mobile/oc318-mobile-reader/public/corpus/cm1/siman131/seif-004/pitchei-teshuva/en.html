For there are those who disagree. See Sema, Shach, and Taz for the identity of the dissenting authorities. Responsa Chacham Tzvi, siman 51, writes that the possessor may say kim li in accordance with the dissenters, as does Responsa Raanach and Knesset HaGedolah. This applies to an ordinary guarantor. A kablan, however, may be collected from first, like the borrower himself, and all agree that even if the creditor warned him at the due date he remains liable. Chacham Tzvi adds that the customary phrase in engagement agreements, 'arev kablan on the groom's side' or 'on the bride's side,' does not by itself create the halachic status of a kablan. In practice the scribes merely ask who the guarantors are, and the parties name them; no effective kablan formula is recited. This is especially so because guaranties for engagement penalties differ from ordinary guaranties. He further argues that according to the dissenting view, once the guaranty was limited to a stated term, the guarantor should be discharged after that term even without warning the creditor, because the original undertaking itself expired. It may even be that an express renewal requires a new kinyan, like a guaranty undertaken after the money was already advanced. Knesset HaGedolah, however, cites Maharshach that even according to the dissenters the guarantor is discharged only if he warned the creditor to collect at the due date and the creditor nevertheless extended the term. The Gra likewise appears to read the rule this way. Pitchei Teshuva concludes that even so, money should not be extracted from the guarantor who is in possession, because even a double doubt among authorities does not ordinarily remove money from a possessor.
And the guarantor may compel the borrower. The Shach understands this as following only the dissenting opinion above. According to the Ramban and the Mechaber, who say the guarantor cannot compel the creditor to collect at the due date, he likewise cannot compel the borrower to discharge him. Tumim disagrees and understands the Rama to state this universally. Responsa Beit Ephraim, Choshen Mishpat, siman 13, defends the Shach. According to the Ramban, the time fixed for the loan was not established for the guarantor's benefit. His undertaking is as though he guaranteed generally for as long as the borrower remains liable. If the creditor grants the borrower more time, the guarantor has no basis to compel payment. The Rama therefore follows the dissenters, who understand the guaranty as extending only until the fixed date; under that view the guarantor may also tell the borrower to remove him from the guaranty. Beit Ephraim notes that this can matter where the creditor is absent or is too powerful to be warned. He adds that even according to the Ramban, if the guarantor expressly stipulated orally at the time of the loan that he would be guarantor only until a specified date, and the creditor then refuses to extend the borrower unless the guarantor renews his undertaking, the borrower cannot compel the guarantor to continue. The fact that the guarantor could not have forced the borrower to pay earlier does not give the borrower power to force a new guaranty upon him.
