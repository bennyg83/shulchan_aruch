Because some disagree. Sema understands Mahari Weil's case, cited by Rama, as disagreeing with Ramban. Mahari Weil ruled that if the guarantor tells the lender to send the debt document and collect his money, and the lender fails to do so, the guarantor is exempt. See Tumim. This is not a true disagreement. Mahari Weil required the guarantor to swear that he knew with certainty that the borrower was ready to pay. I further wrote that the substantive rule itself depends on the two opinions discussed above in siman 120, and therefore Rama's unqualified adoption requires study. Perhaps Rama considered that here there is already serious doubt in the underlying guaranty law, and therefore ruled leniently. See Tumim at length.
To release him, etc. The Shach says this refers only to the opinion disagreeing with Ramban. According to Ramban, since the guarantor cannot compel the lender, he likewise cannot compel the borrower. In Tumim I established that Ramban indeed holds he cannot compel either the lender or borrower, but Tur, although following Ramban regarding the lender, holds that the guarantor can compel the borrower when the debt matures. This follows from the principle that a borrower is servant to the lender, and it is also implied by Perishah and Raavad in Baal HaTerumot. Rama's unqualified ruling is therefore justified. I further concluded that this power to compel the borrower applies only while the borrower is alive. If he dies, even where there is no concern for prior payment or bundles of money, the guarantor cannot compel minor heirs to pay merely to release him from the guaranty.
He must trust him. This is specifically where the gentile forgot the debt and it is uncertain whether he will ever demand payment from the guarantor. In an ordinary guaranty the guarantor can compel the borrower when the debt is due, as Rama states immediately afterward. Darkei Moshe says this explicitly. There is therefore no contradiction between Rama's rulings, unlike Ir Shushan.
