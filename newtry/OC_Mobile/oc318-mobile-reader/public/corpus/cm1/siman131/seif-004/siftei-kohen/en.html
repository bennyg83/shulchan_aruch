A guarantor who came to the lender. See Responsa R. Shmuel Kohen, part 2, simanim 101 and 156, and Responsa Maharashdam, simanim 57 and 218.
He should release him from the guarantee, etc. If this condition was stipulated when the guarantee was undertaken, the guarantor is entitled to demand release. See Responsa R. Eliyahu ibn Chaim, siman 60.

This ruling requires deliberation. The Sema writes that although the Rama says some disagree, he found no one who disagrees. Bedek HaBayit does disagree, as cited by the Bach. See Responsa Maharashdam, siman 57, which states that according to the Rif and Rosh, the first guarantor may say to the second guarantor, ‘Remove me from the guarantee,’ where the first guarantor may otherwise suffer loss. See there, and what I wrote in siman 86. See also Migdal David.
The guarantor may compel the borrower to release him. The Ramban disagrees, as I wrote in my work, and the Baal HaTerumot states this expressly in his name. This depends on the Ramban’s view cited by the Mechaber at the beginning of the seif: just as the guarantor cannot compel the lender after the due date, he likewise cannot compel the borrower. All this is evident from the Baal HaTerumot. The Rama must therefore be following the opposing view when he writes that some disagree. Consider this carefully.
