Even if they made a kinyan. Sema explains that a kinyan in ordinary guaranty, without a fully effective present obligation, does not cure the asmachta. Guaranty is enforceable at the time the money is advanced because the creditor relied upon the guarantor, and that reliance completes the undertaking. Here the guarantor retracts before the loan is made, so that reliance never arises. By contrast, a guarantor who undertakes after the money was already advanced requires a kinyan, and once that kinyan is made the undertaking is complete. This is correct. Perishah adds that according to Rashba, even if the borrower and lender themselves made a kinyan the lender can retract; and even according to Ramban and Tur who disagree, if the guarantor retracts the lender may likewise retract because he agreed to lend only on the assumption that the guarantor would remain bound. This is straightforward.
