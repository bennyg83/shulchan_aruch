(Seif 4) Because some disagree. It appears that the dissenter is the Tur, who writes that the guarantor may demand that the borrower release him from the guaranty because he agreed to guarantee only until the specified time. If so, he may likewise tell the lender that a person does not obligate himself beyond the period expressly stipulated. Although the Tur states this reasoning in relation to the borrower, perhaps he teaches the greater novelty that even when the lender is not presently suing, the guarantor may demand release, rather than being treated as still within the agreed term. Since the disagreement concerning the lender is not explicit, the Mechaber writes that the matter requires consideration, and Rama says only that “some disagree,” meaning that there is reasoning for disagreement. Sema identifies Mahari Weil as the dissenter, but that cannot be correct. Mahari Weil does not truly disagree, and even had he done so expressly his isolated late opinion could not outweigh Ramban, the father of the authorities. The reference is necessarily to the Tur. This also resolves another issue. Ramban here holds that even if the guarantor warns the lender to sue the borrower, the lender may refuse. This conflicts with Rav Nachshon Gaon, cited by the Tur in siman 129, who says that if the guarantor brings the borrower, the guarantor is exempt until the borrower swears, and if the borrower escapes the guarantor is exempt. The Tur cites Ramban here only for discussion but, as explained, disagrees with him.
