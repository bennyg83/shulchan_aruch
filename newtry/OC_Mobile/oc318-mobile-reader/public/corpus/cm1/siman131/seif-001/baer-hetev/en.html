A kinyan was made. The Sema explains that an ordinary kinyan not performed before an eminent beit din does not overcome an asmachta-like guarantee unless there is also the benefit of the creditor's reliance, namely that money was advanced on the strength of the guarantee. Here the guarantor retracted before the loan and no longer seeks that reliance. By contrast, when a guarantor undertakes after the money was already given, once a kinyan is made the matter is complete, and that kinyan is effective even without an eminent beit din because it combines with the creditor's reliance in accepting him as guarantor. Nevertheless, all agree that wherever the guarantor can retract, the creditor may likewise retract from his undertaking to lend even if a kinyan had been made from him to make the loan, because he only undertook to lend on condition that this person remain guarantor. See siman 39 seif 19.
Anything. Since the guarantor has the power to protest before the creditor and forbid him from lending in reliance on the guarantee, if the guarantor later asserts a defense that would exempt him, he is believed by a migo that he could have said, “I protested before you gave the money.” This applies even if the creditor and guarantor were not in the same place. If the guarantor sent a letter authorizing the loan on his reliance, he may claim, “Before you lent, I wrote that I no longer wished to be guarantor, and I know my letter reached you.” Maharshadam, Choshen Mishpat, siman 38.
