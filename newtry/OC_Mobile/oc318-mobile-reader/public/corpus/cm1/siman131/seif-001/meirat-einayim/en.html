Even if a kinyan was made from him. The reason is that a kinyan made outside an eminent beit din remains like an ordinary guaranty, which is an asmakhta and is not acquired except because the creditor relied upon the guarantor and advanced money on that reliance, causing the guarantor to resolve and obligate himself, as explained at the beginning of siman 129. Here the creditor has retracted and no longer seeks that reliance. This differs from a guarantor who enters after the money has already been advanced, where a kinyan is required and the obligation is completed as soon as the kinyan is made. Such a kinyan is effective even outside an eminent beit din when combined with the creditor's reliance upon the guarantor. In the Perishah I wrote that according to the Rashba, even if a kinyan was made both from borrower and creditor, with the borrower encumbering his property and the creditor undertaking to lend on that security, the creditor may still retract. Even according to the Ramban and Tur, who disagree there, it appears that if the guarantor retracts, the creditor may likewise retract because he agreed to the loan only on the understanding that the guarantor would remain obligated.
