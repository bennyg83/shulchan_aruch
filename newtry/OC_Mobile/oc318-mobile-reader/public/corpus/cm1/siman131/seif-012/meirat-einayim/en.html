See above, siman 129:8. It appears that Rama refers to his gloss there concerning one who borrowed an object from another, where he wrote that even though the borrower's own liability depends upon an asmakhta, the guarantor may nevertheless be obligated to pay. Rama there directs the reader back to the present law. It is straightforward that even an authority who obligates the guarantor there may still exempt the guarantor in the forms of asmakhta listed here. The asmakhta there is only like the ordinary asmakhta of guaranty, an undertaking to answer for the money without a separate condition. Such an undertaking is ordinarily binding because the creditor relied upon him and the guarantor fully resolved to obligate himself. The novelty there is only that although the borrower himself may be exempt because his liability was contingent, we do not apply the general rule that wherever the borrower is exempt the guarantor is exempt. The asmakhta cases here, however, differ from an ordinary guaranty for the reason explained above.
