Seif 8. In the gloss there, concerning one who borrowed an object from his fellow, it appears clear that even an authority who holds there that the guarantor is liable can agree that the guarantor is exempt in the cases of asmachta here. The reason is that there the obligation is only comparable to the ordinary asmachta inherent in an unconditional guarantee, where we say that because the creditor trusted him, the guarantor undertook the obligation. That ruling merely teaches that it is not included in the general principle that whenever the principal borrower is exempt the guarantor is also exempt. Although the borrower's liability there has an aspect of asmachta, the guarantor is nevertheless liable. Sema. See Responsa Rashach II, 51.
