There, seif 11, in the name of Rambam in chapter 25 of Hilkhot Malveh, who states this ruling in the name of his teachers. Maggid Mishneh writes that he does not know a source for this law in the Gemara. It appears that Rambam and his teachers distinguish a guarantee associated with a sale: when purchase money is given, it is given with the intent that it remain permanently invested, and the seller's warranty is not a warranty on the money itself but on the land in case it is later foreclosed. A guarantor's undertaking also contains an element of asmachta, etc. Beit Yosef writes similarly. See there.
Maggid Mishneh writes that the remaining point is certainly agreed upon. Rambam required a kinyan because he treats this guarantor like one who undertook after the money had already been given, since this money was not given with the expectation that it would later be collected back.
