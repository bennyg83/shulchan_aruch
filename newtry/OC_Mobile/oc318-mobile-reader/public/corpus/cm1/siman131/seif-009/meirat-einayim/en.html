If Reuven sold to Shimon, etc. The Tur explains that with an ordinary guaranty the guarantor becomes obligated because the creditor relied upon him and, due to that reliance, the guarantor fully resolved to obligate himself. Here that reason does not apply, because the purchaser did not buy in reliance on the guarantor. A person may buy land even for a short time. But if a kinyan was made from the guarantor that he would repay the purchase price whenever the purchaser demanded it, meaning whenever the purchaser chose to rescind the sale, even if no one had challenged title to the field, then it is clear that the purchaser took the guarantor primarily to secure his money. Therefore the guarantor is liable like any other guarantor. This is why the Tur and Mechaber say that if a kinyan was made from him "to pay the purchase price," not merely the value of the field as under an ordinary warranty. A kinyan is required because this is comparable to a guaranty undertaken after the money was advanced, since the original payment was not given as a loan. The Maggid Mishneh gives another explanation for the first clause. I cited it in the Derishah and explained that there are practical differences between the explanations. The Beit Yosef also gives another reason. See the Perishah and Derishah.
For with a guarantor, etc. The reason is that although the Gemara says every guaranty contains an element of asmakhta, nevertheless at the time the money is advanced the guarantor becomes obligated even without a kinyan because he fully resolves and obligates himself. Once that principle was established, the Sages did not distinguish between one form of asmakhta and another in these guaranty cases. This is the basis of the several "some say" rulings that Rama cites afterward. See the Perishah.
