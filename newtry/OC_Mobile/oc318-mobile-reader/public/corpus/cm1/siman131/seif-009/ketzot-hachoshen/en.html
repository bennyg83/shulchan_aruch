The reason a seller's guarantor can sometimes avoid ordinary guaranty law is that the purchaser does not buy the land in reliance upon the guarantor's personal trustworthiness. People sometimes knowingly buy land that may later be taken, because one may purchase land even for a single day. The usual guarantor principle, that the creditor parts with money in reliance on the guarantor and the guarantor therefore fully subjects himself, is absent. If, however, a kinyan was made from the guarantor that he would pay the purchase price whenever the purchaser wished to cancel the sale, even without an outside challenge to the land, the arrangement reveals that the guarantor's central purpose was to secure the purchaser's money. He is then liable like an ordinary guarantor. The kinyan shows that the money was intended to remain secure independently of any defect in the land.
