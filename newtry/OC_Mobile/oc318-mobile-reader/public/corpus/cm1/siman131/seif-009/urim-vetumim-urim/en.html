A field. Tur explains that ordinary guaranty works because the lender relied on the guarantor when giving money. In a sale, the buyer did not acquire in reliance on the guarantor, for a person may knowingly buy land even for a short time. Therefore an ordinary guaranty of title does not bind. If, however, the guarantor makes a kinyan obligating himself to repay the sale price whenever the buyer demands, even if no third party has challenged the field, it becomes clear that the guaranty's purpose was to secure the buyer's money, and the undertaking is enforceable like ordinary guaranty. Sema. Beit Yosef instead explains that a sale guaranty is more asmachta-like because at the time of sale no challenge to title is visible and the guarantor expects never to pay. See Tumim for the difficulty and possible answer.
