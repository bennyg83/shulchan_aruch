(Seif 10: or if there will not be.) It appears that even in our master's wording, "if there will not be," the meaning is "or if there will not be." This is Rambam's language. Meaning, he bound himself with an asmachta formulation, whether "if there will be" or "if there will not be," and in such a case the undertaking becomes effective under the stated conditions. What is written afterward, "we rely on whether the condition is fulfilled," refers to the phrase "if there will not be." According to the Sema's explanation in seif katan 21, the text should have said, "he did not obligate himself." Furthermore, the later statement that we rely on fulfillment of the condition is difficult, since the condition is explicitly phrased, "if there will not be," etc.
