His share. Responsa Mahari ben Lev, part I, rule 12, writes that the party in possession may say, “I rely on Rambam.” Responsa Maharshadam, simanim 82, 121, 124, and 179, says the same. This requires study, since later authorities disagree with Rambam, and the Mechaber and other later decisors appear not to follow Rambam at all here. The Sema writes that according to the Mechaber, Rambam follows his own view that one who undertakes an obligation of an unquantified amount does not become bound, as the Tur cites in Rambam's name in siman 60. Thus those who disagree there necessarily disagree here. The Derishah, however, writes that even one who disagrees there can agree here because there are two defects, both asmachta and guarantee. Rivash writes that even if the guarantor swore to this obligation, he need not perform it by force of the oath, because he swore in error, believing that the obligation was legally binding. The Shach concludes that Beit Yosef's view is primary. This is also explicit in the cited responsum of Mahari ben Lev, and Maggid Mishneh likewise treats the issue as dependent on the dispute in siman 60 and rules here like the Mechaber. Raavad's compromise was already criticized by Maggid Mishneh. See the Taz on this matter.
