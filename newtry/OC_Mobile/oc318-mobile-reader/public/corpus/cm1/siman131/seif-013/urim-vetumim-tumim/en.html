One who did not specify the amount of the obligation, etc. I have some difficulty with the Maggid Mishneh's explanation of Rambam's ruling that one who guarantees the warranty of a field is subject to asmachta. The Maggid Mishneh explains that the warranty does not relate to the purchase money but to the land itself. His proof is that if the field rises or falls in value and is later seized by the seller's creditor, the purchaser is reimbursed according to the value of the field at the time of seizure, not according to the original purchase price. Thus the guaranty is not over the money but over the land. According to his explanation, the guarantor undertook to pay whatever the field would be worth when seized. If it rises in value, the excess is treated as appreciation and the guarantor undertook that as well; if it falls, even an ordinary purchaser recovers only the lower value according to the Maggid Mishneh. See what I wrote above in siman 117. If so, why does Rambam need the reason that the undertaking is an asmachta? It should be invalid simply because it is an obligation of an unspecified amount. Who knows what the field will be worth at the time of seizure? Everyone would agree that this is not fixed, and according to Rambam an obligation of an unspecified amount is ineffective even with a kinyan, as explained above. Further study is required. Were it not for the Maggid Mishneh, I would say that the guarantor undertakes only the principal purchase price and not appreciation. Then he can never pay more than the fixed principal, so the amount is determinate. I would not follow Tosafot in Ketubot, chapter Na'arah, that a sum lacking only a lower bound is still called unspecified. But according to the Maggid Mishneh, who holds that the guarantor covers even appreciation, the amount is plainly indefinite. Perhaps this is why Rambam carefully wrote earlier, And so my teachers ruled, meaning that although his teachers disagreed with him regarding unspecified obligations generally, here they agree the undertaking is ineffective because of asmachta. Thus this ruling is necessary only according to his teachers, not according to Rambam himself. This is strained.
