And all who came after him disagreed with him. The Mechaber understands that Rambam exempts the guarantor because Rambam follows his own view that one who undertakes an obligation of an unspecified amount does not become bound, as the Tur cites in his name in siman 60 and at the end of siman 207. It would therefore follow that those who disagree with Rambam there disagree with him here as well. In the Perishah, however, I wrote and proved from the wording of the Tur and Raavad that even those who generally hold that an obligation of an unspecified amount is effective agree here that the guarantor is exempt, because there is an additional weakness here, namely the asmachta inherent in guaranty. Although each problem by itself would not prevent the obligation from taking effect, the combination of the two prevents full acquisition. Therefore Raavad, who there holds that such an undertaking is fully effective, writes here that it does not fully take effect except in accordance with an assessment of intent. See the Derishah for further proofs. This appears to me clear in practical halacha.
