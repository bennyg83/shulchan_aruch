And all who came after him disagreed with him. Responsa Maharan ibn Lev, book 1, at the end of klal 12, writes that the party in possession may say, 'I am entitled to rely upon the Rambam.' Responsa Maharashdam, simanim 82, 121, 124, and 179, writes similarly. This requires study, since the authorities who followed the Rambam disagree with him, and the words of the Mechaber and the other later authorities likewise indicate that the halacha does not follow the Rambam here. The Sema, seif katan 25, writes that the Mechaber understood the Rambam to follow his own view that one who transfers an obligation of indeterminate amount does not become bound, as the Tur cites in his name in siman 60. Accordingly, those who disagree with him there also disagree here. In the Perisha, however, he writes that even one who disagrees there concedes here, because two defects are present, namely asmachta and suretyship, neither of which effects acquisition. The Rivash writes that even if the guarantor swore to guarantee this obligation, he need not fulfill it by force of the oath, for the oath was taken in error, since he assumed he had become legally bound. See there, and see above siman 73 in the Sema. It appears to me that the Beit Yosef's view is primary. This is also explicit in Responsa Maharan ibn Lev, book 1, klal 12, and is the view of the Maggid Mishneh, who makes this depend upon the dispute in siman 60 and rules here in accordance with the Mechaber. As to the Raavad, who proposed a compromise, the Maggid Mishneh has already challenged him; see there.
