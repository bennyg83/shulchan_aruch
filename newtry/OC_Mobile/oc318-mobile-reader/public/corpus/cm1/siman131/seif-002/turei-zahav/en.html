(Seif 5). “A proof for the matter.” Even without the proof the guarantor should be exempt, because his words, “I remain as I was,” can reasonably be interpreted as preserving his exemption, and the burden of proof rests on the claimant. The next sentence also implies this by saying that he should have clarified his guaranty. The “proof” therefore teaches a greater point: despite the legal exemption, it is proper and upright for him to act accordingly under the principle of what is good and right.
