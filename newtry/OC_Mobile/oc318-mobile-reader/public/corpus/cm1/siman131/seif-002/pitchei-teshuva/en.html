And he says, 'I did not guarantee.' Shaar Mishpat writes that the same applies where one says, 'You became my guarantor as a kablan, so I need not trouble myself to collect from the borrower,' and the other says, 'I was only an ordinary guarantor; the borrower has assets, so collect from him.' The latter must swear that he was only an ordinary guarantor. We do not say that this is not a denial of money because the creditor can ultimately collect from the borrower; the avoidance of the burden and expense of collection itself has monetary value. He brings proof from Bava Kamma 108b.
