If one says to another, "You guaranteed my debt," and he says, "I did not"; or the guarantor says to the debtor, "You authorized me to guarantee you and to pay," and the debtor says, "You guaranteed voluntarily," or, "You never guaranteed at all"; or the guarantor says, "I paid the creditor in your presence," and the debtor says, "You did not pay," or says, "You did pay, but I reimbursed you"; or the creditor says, "You guaranteed two hundred," and the guarantor says, "I guaranteed only one hundred," then in all these and similar claims, the burden of proof is on the claimant. The defendant takes a shevuat heset, or a Torah oath when he admits part of the claim.
