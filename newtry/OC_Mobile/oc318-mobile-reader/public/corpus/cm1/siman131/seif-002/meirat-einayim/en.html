You authorized me to become your guarantor and to pay. The Rambam and the Mechaber follow their own view that it is not enough for the borrower to authorize the guarantor merely to become guarantor. He must also authorize him to pay, as stated in siman 130:2. Nevertheless, if the borrower authorized him only to pay, the borrower would be obligated to reimburse him. The wording here merely follows the ordinary manner of events. Likewise, when the text later says, "and he says, You became guarantor of your own accord," it could equally have said, "or, I did not authorize you to pay." It mentions only one of the alternatives. See the Perishah.
Indeed you paid, and I gave you what you paid, etc. The burden of proof lies upon the claimant. The Maggid Mishneh writes that this applies where the creditor did not give the guarantor a written receipt. If the creditor wrote him a receipt and transferred his rights to him, the guarantor stands in the creditor's place, and if the original loan was documented, the borrower cannot say, "I repaid you." This appears from the Rashba and is straightforward, for the guarantor should have no weaker rights than an unrelated person whom the creditor authorized. I have copied the Maggid Mishneh because of what I wrote in the Sema to the preceding siman, seif 4.
