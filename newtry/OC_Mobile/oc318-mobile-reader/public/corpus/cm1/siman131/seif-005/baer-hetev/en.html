Shabbat. The Sema explains that since there was in truth no Shabbat prohibition involved, especially during bein hashmashot, the creditor should have understood that the speaker mentioned Shabbat only because he was refusing to become guarantor. Moreover, his words, “I am undertaking no guarantee,” imply that even if it were not Shabbat he would assume no guarantee, and this constitutes a refusal. See Responsa Maharshadam, simanim 95 and 130. The Taz writes that even without this proof the guarantor is exempt, because his words “I remain as I was” can reasonably be interpreted as preserving his exemption, and the burden of proof rests on the party seeking payment. This is also implied by the later statement that he should have expressed his guarantee explicitly. The proof is brought only as an added reason that, even so, it is proper and upright to make the matter explicit.
