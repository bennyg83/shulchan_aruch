One who says to his fellow, etc. See Shach seif katan 16, who agrees with Gidulei Terumah that where a guarantor entered after the money had already been advanced, but did so with a kinyan, and that guarantor then procured a second guarantor for his own obligation, the second guarantor does not require a kinyan because with respect to him the case is treated as guaranty at the time the money is advanced. This is difficult to understand. What reliance of the creditor upon the second guarantor exists here? Even the first guarantor was not relied upon when the loan was made, since the money had already been given. He became obligated only because he made a kinyan. The second guarantor made no kinyan, so by what act does he become obligated? Had he refused to guarantee, what would the creditor have lost? It therefore appears that the case must involve a kablan. The first person became a kablan and is accordingly treated like the borrower. It is as though he obligated himself by kinyan to pay a person a sum he did not previously owe and then procured a guarantor for that new obligation. See above siman 129 concerning one who gives a gift and procures a guarantor. With respect to that newly created obligation, the second guarantor stands in the position of guaranty given at the time the obligation is created and therefore becomes bound without a separate kinyan. This works only with a kablan, because the creditor may proceed directly against him and he is therefore the creditor's debtor. With an ordinary guarantor, whom the creditor cannot sue first, there is no new moment of advance or direct debt merely because the first guarantor made a kinyan. If the second guarantor made no kinyan, by what does he become obligated? Gidulei Terumah's proof from guaranty in ma'amad sheloshtan is therefore rebutted. There, even according to the Rosh that the creditor may ultimately return to the original debtor, he may nevertheless collect from the new debtor, and indeed all agree he cannot first collect from the transferor. The new debtor is therefore the principal debtor, and the transferor resembles an ordinary guarantor. Consequently the new guaranty is indeed contemporaneous with the creation of the principal obligation. That reasoning does not apply to an ordinary guarantor. Because the matter is doubtful, money cannot be extracted. Examine this carefully.
