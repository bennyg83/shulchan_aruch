The Sema explains: he said, “Become guarantor for this matter for so-and-so.” The Shach writes that if one became guarantor for another after the money had already been given, and the creditor refused to accept him unless this guarantor produced a second guarantor who would guarantee the first guarantor's obligation, the second guarantor does not require a kinyan. Gidulei Terumah derives this from the Gaon's responsum cited by the Tur in siman 126 seif 19. Although some of his other proofs are not compelling, this conclusion is correct according to the Gaon, Sefer HaTerumot, and the Tur there. This applies only where the first guarantor actually became legally obligated, such as through a kinyan. Otherwise the second guarantor is plainly exempt even if his own undertaking involved a kinyan. See many additional distinctions concerning guarantors in Knesset HaGedolah.
