One who says to another, etc. If a person became guarantor for another after the money had already been given, and the lender refused to accept him until that guarantor produced a second guarantor who guaranteed the first guarantor's obligation, the second guarantor does not require a kinyan. This is stated in Giddulei Terumah, document 28, at the end of part 2, and is derived there from the responsum of a Gaon cited by the Tur above, siman 126, seif 19. Although the other proofs adduced there are not conclusive, his ruling is correct according to the Gaon, the Baal HaTerumot, and the Tur there. This applies, however, only where the first guarantor became legally bound, such as where a kinyan was made from him or the like. Otherwise it is obvious that the guarantor is exempt even if a kinyan was made from the second guarantor.
