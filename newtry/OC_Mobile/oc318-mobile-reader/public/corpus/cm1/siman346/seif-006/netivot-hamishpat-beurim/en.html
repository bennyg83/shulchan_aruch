The servant was borrowed without his master's knowledge. The wording implies that if the servant acted with his master's knowledge, it would count as shemirah b'baalim. Tosafot in Bava Metzia 99 prove otherwise. Shemirah b'baalim exists where the owner himself lends the animal and sends his servant in his place. But if the servant independently lends himself together with the cow, it is like borrowing two cows, not like borrowing the owner together with his animal. Shulchan Aruch itself in siman 340 uses the reason that a servant's hand is like the master's hand rather than saying the case is shemirah b'baalim. Therefore our case should be read as follows: the owner of the cow lent the cow, and the servant then independently volunteered to perform his master's work. Even there, if the servant acted without the master's authorization, it does not become shemirah b'baalim. If only the servant lent himself and the cow, it is not shemirah b'baalim even if the master authorized him to do so.
