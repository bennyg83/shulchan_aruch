And some disagree, etc. They follow Rabbi Yonatan, according to whom the phrase 'his agent, etc.' is generally included. Moreover, where the law is uncertain, we rule leniently for the defendant. They reject Rif's proof, since even according to Rabbi Yoshiya the response there would remain valid. Rosh writes so.
(Supplement) And some disagree. Rashba, Ran, and Nimukei Yosef also rule leniently here, while in vows the doubt is treated stringently. Rif's position can be defended: the Gemara's question is its own, not Rav Ilish's, and the later conclusion that an agent is subject to a tannaitic dispute indicates that only a slave's hand is unquestionably like his master's. There is also proof from Bava Metzia 99a. See siman 486. (End of supplement.)
He was borrowed for him, etc. Rambam derives this from Bava Metzia 99a.
(Supplement) He was borrowed, etc. A slave's hand is treated as the master's only when the owner consents. The Gemara therefore raises that point only against the latter clause. Some authorities nevertheless hold that even consent is insufficient unless the master himself enters the borrower's service. See siman 81. (End of supplement.)
