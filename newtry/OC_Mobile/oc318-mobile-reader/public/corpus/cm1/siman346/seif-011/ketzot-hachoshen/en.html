He borrowed from two partners. The Gemara's doubt concerns only the share of the partner who was present with the borrower. As to the other partner's share, whose owner was not with him, the borrower is certainly liable. The partner who was not with him should receive his entire half of the payment. The exempt partner does not share in that collected half, because the very exemption arose specifically from the other partner's personal presence. This differs from an ordinary impaired joint debt where whatever is collected may be divided. Here the first partner's personal exemption cannot reduce the second partner's independent full claim. The same applies in the converse case of partners who jointly borrowed and one owner was present with only one of them. The unresolved question concerns only the corresponding share to which the owner's presence might apply. The other share remains fully liable. Rambam and the Shulchan Aruch state the general exemption without spelling out this division, but the division is implicit.
