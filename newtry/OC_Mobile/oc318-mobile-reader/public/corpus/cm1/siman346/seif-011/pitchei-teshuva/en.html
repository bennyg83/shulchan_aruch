If one of them was employed for him, the matter remains doubtful. Tur writes that if one borrowed an animal owned by two partners and one partner was employed for him, the Gemara leaves unresolved whether the half belonging to that partner is considered a case of borrowing while the owner is employed. This wording implies that with respect to the other half it is obvious that the borrower is liable to the second partner, and Perishah states this explicitly. The wording of Shulchan Aruch, which follows Rambam, concludes, "Therefore, if it dies he does not pay," which could be read as referring also to this case and as exempting him entirely. Nimukei Yosef already questioned this wording and suggested that Rambam's phrase "he does not pay" was written because of the immediately preceding case of two partners who borrowed together; in the present case of borrowing from partners, the borrower is exempt only from half. Mishneh LaMelech likewise writes that Rambam's language does not decide whether he is exempt from the whole animal, since the conclusion may refer only to the immediately preceding case. Shitah Mekubetzet states plainly that the doubt concerns only the half belonging to the partner who was employed; as to the other partner's half, the borrower is certainly liable. Ketzot HaChoshen adds that the partner whose share is exempt does not receive any portion of the amount paid for the other half. Since the exemption arises specifically because that partner was employed with the borrower, the other partner should not lose because of him and therefore receives full payment for his own half. Although partners generally share equally in collection of a doubtful or impaired debt, here the Torah itself exempted the borrower from the first partner's share, so the second partner collects his own share in full and the first receives nothing from that payment. Ketzot further writes that in the reverse unresolved case, where partners borrowed and one owner was employed with one of them, the doubt likewise concerns only the relevant half; the other half is certainly payable. Nimukei Yosef and Tur indicate the same. See also Divrei Mishpat.
