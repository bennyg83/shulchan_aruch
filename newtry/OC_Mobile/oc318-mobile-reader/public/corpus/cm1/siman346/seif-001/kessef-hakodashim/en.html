See Shach, seif katan 14. The Shach at the end of siman 346 cites Yam Shel Shlomo regarding a woman who borrowed a garment that is 'important relative to her station.' The wording in Yam Shel Shlomo may mean that, considering her social standing and wealth, this garment is a particularly important one. Even if she has occasion to wear it, a garment of festival-like importance may not be treated as an ordinary necessity for purposes of presumed agency, just as festival clothing is treated differently in valuation because it is not a fixed everyday necessity. The Shach appears to have understood the phrase as meaning that the garment is more valuable than her proper social level. This requires study. At present I have not found the statement attributed there to Mahari.
