He is exempt for negligence. The Torah's law of unauthorized use by a custodian applies even where the owner was with him. The Gemara derives this expressly for unpaid and paid custodians. The same should apply to a borrower who uses the object for a purpose for which it was not lent. The statement that a borrower is ordinarily exempt when the owner was with him refers to use within the authorized borrowing. If he takes the borrowed item for a different unauthorized use, he invokes the same special misappropriation law that applies to the other custodians. A borrower is not better positioned than an unpaid or paid custodian. A responsum that treated a borrower as uniquely exempt from misappropriation when the owner was with him misunderstood the Gemara's derivation. The Gemara's comparison to a borrower refers to the borrower's ordinary authorized use, where the owner knowingly allowed the use and the borrower remains liable for accidents. Once Scripture independently imposed unauthorized-use liability on unpaid and paid custodians even with the owner present, the borrower likewise inherits that liability through the general custodial framework when he deviates from the permitted use.
