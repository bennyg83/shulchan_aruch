As it says, 'If its owner is with him.' In HaShoel the Gemara derives the rule from the paired verses. 'If its owner is with him, he shall not pay' implies exemption where the owner is with the borrower at the relevant stage, while 'if its owner is not with him, he shall pay' implies liability where the owner is absent. The Gemara concludes that the decisive time is the beginning of the borrowing. If the owner was with him when the borrowing began, the borrower is exempt even if the owner was absent when the animal later broke or died. If the owner was absent at the beginning, he remains liable even if the owner was with him at the time of the accident. From borrowing we learn the rule for all custodians.
But if he had done so much, he would have made it so far as if he had done so with him
