(Seif 3: Rather, he makes himself available, etc.) The Rosh proves that verbal availability suffices from the opening of HaShoel, which says that “the owner is with him” can arise through a statement, while the Gemara later calls the town scribe and slaughterer “with him” during the time of their work. The Rosh answers that “during their work” is not literal; once they make themselves available for the borrower's service, that is considered their working time. The Tur cites the Ramah as agreeing but distinguishing whether a time was specified. If the owner undertook to work whenever requested, he is presently borrowed for the work because he must act immediately upon request. If he agreed to work at a specified later time, he is not “with him” until he begins. The Sema says the Rosh also accepts this distinction. That is implausible. If so, the Rosh need not have distinguished availability from speech; the contradiction could be resolved simply by saying that speech suffices when no future time is fixed, whereas actual work is required when a time is fixed. The Rosh's answer therefore indicates that mere availability itself is enough even without the Ramah's limitation.
