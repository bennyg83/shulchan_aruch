The same law applies to a shaliach tzibbur and the congregation. Rashbetz writes that this applies specifically during prayer. It appears that the relevant period includes the ordinary time at which he is obligated to lead the prayers, even before he is actually standing at the lectern. This resembles the rule concerning partners and a hired planter above, where Raavad regards the period legally designated for the work as sufficient because the person is bound to perform it. After prayer, however, the shaliach tzibbur is no longer obligated to serve. Likewise, during the prayer period the congregation is bound to hear the shaliach tzibbur whom they cannot simply dismiss, and the relationship resembles students with their teacher as explained in seif 13.
