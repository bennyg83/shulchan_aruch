Then he is considered borrowed to them, etc. This applies even outside the actual study session, because he is obligated to teach them whenever they wish. The Tur likewise writes this. In the Derishah I noted that he says this regarding the teacher being borrowed to them, but does not say conversely that they are borrowed to him outside the time of learning. Similarly, Rashbatz is precise concerning a communal prayer leader, whose law Rama records below in the gloss, that the prayer leader is considered borrowed to the congregation throughout that day even outside the time of prayer, whereas the congregation is considered borrowed to him only during the time of prayer. I explained that the reason appears to be that the rabbi and prayer leader actively perform the teaching and leading of the prayers. Their labor is therefore more visibly their own act, so they are more readily regarded as workers serving the others. See there.
