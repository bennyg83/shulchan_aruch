But by verbal commitment. See Shach, seif katan 1, who infers that words without preparation can create shemirah b'baalim. I do not see how that resolves Tosafot's question at the beginning of HaShoel. One could say that when the owner explicitly says, "I will do work for you immediately," that very statement constitutes preparation. Likewise, in the case of a planter or similar worker, there may be a previously existing obligation to work. In my view Tosafot also require actual readiness. Tosafot in Chezkas HaBatim teach an additional novelty: even where a person prepares himself today to guard tomorrow, that does not help when a fixed future time was specified. They could have answered more narrowly as in HaShoel, but chose instead to teach this broader rule.
