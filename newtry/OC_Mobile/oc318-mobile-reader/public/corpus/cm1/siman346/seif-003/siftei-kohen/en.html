Through a statement, etc. The Tur writes this, but in my opinion it is not necessarily the Rosh's meaning. When the Rosh says, 'this is the statement mentioned above,' he means only that the earlier passage proves that the owner need not actually be engaged in the work at that precise moment. It likewise does not necessarily require physical preparation. The Gemara uses the phrase 'at the time of their work,' and the Rosh explains it in that setting. Tosafot at the beginning of HaShoel and Rabbeinu Yerucham also imply that borrowing with the owner begins when the owner prepares to go, because an owner's participation can begin by verbal commitment. Tosafot and the Mordechai in chapter Chezkat HaBatim distinguish the case, 'Guard for me today and I will guard for you tomorrow,' because there the owner does not obligate himself to begin the work immediately. Their language proves that where the verbal commitment is immediate, the law of guarding with the owner applies even without physical preparation.
