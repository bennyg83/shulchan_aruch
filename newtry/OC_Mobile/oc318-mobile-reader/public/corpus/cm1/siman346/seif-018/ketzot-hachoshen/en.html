One who purchases an animal for thirty days. Tosafot treats a temporary purchaser as an unpaid custodian. Rambam, however, says that a purchaser for a fixed term has the rights of an owner during that term: he may build, demolish, and alter the property in ways unavailable to one who merely bought fruit rights. If so, according to Rambam a temporary purchaser should not even be an unpaid custodian. If he may deliberately alter or even destroy within the scope of his temporary ownership, all the more he should not be liable merely for negligent loss. Tosafot's rule fits Raavad, who limits the temporary purchaser's power to damage the property. The Shulchan Aruch follows Rambam concerning temporary purchase, yet elsewhere cites Tosafot's rule that such a purchaser is an unpaid custodian. The rulings therefore sit uneasily together and require study.
He is an unpaid custodian. Bach asks that one who purchases an animal for thirty days ordinarily does so on the understanding that it will be returned afterward. If loss during the term prevents return, should the original purchase not be retroactively void, making the purchaser a robber liable even for accidents, like a gift on condition to return? There is no difficulty. A temporary purchase is not necessarily structured as a condition. The buyer acquires the body for thirty days, and at the end the ownership automatically reverts. If the animal is accidentally lost during the term, the original acquisition is not retroactively cancelled. A gift on condition to return is different. There the validity of the original gift itself is conditioned on actual return. Failure to return can undo the acquisition retroactively. A fixed-term purchase, however, simply ends at the stated time. The temporary purchaser is therefore not automatically liable for unavoidable accidents merely because the object will ultimately revert.
