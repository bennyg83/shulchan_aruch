Seif 6. Partners, one of whom borrowed, etc. Note: I saw in Machaneh Ephraim, Hilchot Shomrim, siman 36, that he disagrees with Maharam Alashkar, siman 4, and Maharit, volume 1, siman 123. He concludes that the exemption of guardianship while the owner is in the guardian's service applies only where, absent that circumstance, the guardian would have been obligated to pay by reason of his guardianship. In such a case it is deemed guardianship while the owner is in his service. But where even without the owner's being in his service he would not have been liable to pay, it is not deemed such guardianship. See there. In my view, with due respect to his great honor, an explicit Gemara in Perek HaShoel, together with all the poskim, escaped his attention. The students of Rava said to Rava, "Master is borrowed to us," etc., and this is how we rule: throughout the year the students are deemed to be in the service of the rabbi, while on the Shabbat of the festival discourse the rabbi is deemed to be in their service. Yet there is no monetary obligation of guardianship there. If they wish, they can refrain from studying altogether, and there is no liability for payment. Nevertheless, since as a matter of law they are obligated to study and the rabbi is obligated to teach them, this is considered a relationship of service for purposes of the exemption. Accordingly, the same is true of two partners who sit in a shop. Even while both are sitting there, each is in the other's service, because each is obligated to sit and conduct the business for the benefit of both, and whatever either one does is for the benefit of both. He cannot say, "I acted only for myself." In my view this is clear and straightforward. The law therefore accords with Maharam Alashkar and Maharit. Understand this well.
