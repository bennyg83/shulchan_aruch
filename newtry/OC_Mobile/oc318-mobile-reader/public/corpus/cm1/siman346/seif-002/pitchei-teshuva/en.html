And some disagree. See Baer Heitev and Shevut Yaakov, volume 2, siman 177, concerning one who rented a horse from Shimon and also hired Shimon's father to accompany him and show the road. At the time of the rental Shimon expressly said that he was reducing the rental price because his father was going along and would supervise the horse. On the return trip the horse became somewhat lame. The renter wanted to leave it on the road, but the owner's father told him to ride it home because doing so would not harm the horse. After reaching home the horse became seriously ill, and the owner claimed that the renter was negligent in riding after the lameness began. Shevut Yaakov writes that ordinarily this would be clear negligence under siman 309, seif 3. Nor could the renter necessarily invoke guarding while the owner works merely because the owner's father was present, because agency for this purpose is disputed. But in this case the father was not acting as the owner's legal agent. He was independently hired for wages. More importantly, the owner expressly said that he was reducing the rent because his father would accompany the renter and supervise the horse. He therefore relied upon his father. Since the father instructed the renter to continue riding and said there was no danger, the renter was not negligent and is exempt. In volume 3, siman 177, Shevut Yaakov discusses one who gave another a promissory note to collect and instructed him that if the road seemed dangerous he should hire another Jew from that place to accompany him. He did so, and the money was later lost. Shevut Yaakov rules that the collector remains liable as a paid custodian. The fact that the sender instructed him to hire another person does not create guarding while the owner works, because the sender never personally dealt with that companion and only told the collector to hire someone. No view treats such an independently hired assistant as the sender's personal agent for the exemption. Nor can one invoke the special rabbinic leniency for carriers of barrels in siman 304, because that enactment was limited to unusually heavy labor with frequent accidental loss. Ordinary paid custodians were not exempted from theft and loss. Finally, one cannot invoke the rule that documents are excluded from custodial liability, because the collector is not being charged for losing the document. He preserved and collected it properly; liability concerns the money received in exchange, for which he became a paid custodian. See there.
