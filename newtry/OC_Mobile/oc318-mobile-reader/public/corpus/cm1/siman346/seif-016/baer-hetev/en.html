The Sema explains that even if the husband borrows from his wife at a time when she is not actually working for him, he is exempt, since she is continually available and prepared to perform work for him. The reverse is not so: if she borrows from him, it is treated as though she borrowed from another person and she is not exempt unless she borrowed from him while he was actually working for her. The Tur concludes: Even if the items had been rented or lent to him by her before he married her, when the owner was not in his service, once he marries her, from that time onward the loan or rental is considered one with the owner in his service, and the prior loan or rental that was not with the owner terminates, so he is exempt. See Responsa Maharit, siman 330.
