On the day he sits to perform his work. This is difficult. Below, seif 16, concerning a husband and his wife's property, it appears that shemirah b'baalim applies even while she is not actively working. Sema, seif katan 26, says so. Likewise, with partners above in seif 7, the exemption can apply even while the partner is not presently working. It therefore appears that Rambam requires actual work specifically with a hired planter who is engaged successively for different owners. When he is working for one person, he is treated as fully hired to that person for that period. Ran in Nedarim explains that among partners, when one partner needs the common resource, bereirah can treat it as entirely his for that moment. Likewise here, when another employer currently needs the planter, the planter is fully hired to that other employer and is not considered simultaneously in the borrower's service. Accordingly, even at a moment when no one else presently needs him, he is not automatically considered bound to the borrower, because at any moment another employer's need could remove him completely. Rambam therefore requires that he actually be engaged in the borrower's work. Ordinary partners are different. Each partner remains continually obligated to work in the other's jointly owned interests, and therefore the owner is considered "with him" even when not actively working at that exact moment. Raavad disagrees. Shitah Mekubetzet in Bava Metzia explains that even while the planter works for a different owner, that work benefits all the employers because by completing the other person's task he becomes free sooner for the borrower's work. He can therefore be regarded as engaged in everyone's service even while physically working for only one. This also explains Raavad's comparison to partners who jointly hire a worker for a month. Sema's reading makes the comparison difficult: why should partners hiring a worker be simpler than the planter, who is likewise hired for a period? According to what I have explained, Raavad's comparison is exact. A worker hired by partners to work continually for the partnership is certainly in the service of each because all his work benefits the partnership as a whole. Raavad says the planter is analogous: even when serving one employer, he is clearing his obligations in a way that benefits all of them and remains continually bound to their collective work.
