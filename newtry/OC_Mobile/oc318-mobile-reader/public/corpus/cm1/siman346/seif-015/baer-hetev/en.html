The second. Meaning, one might think that since the exemption of guarding while the owner is in his service requires that the beginning of the rental or loan be with the owner, and if it did not begin with the owner, the bailee remains liable even if the owner is later with him at the time of breakage or death, so too here all the periods should be regarded as one rental, whose beginning was without the owner. The ruling therefore teaches that once the first rental period ends, the second is considered a new rental. If the owner is then with him, the bailee is exempt. This is so even though where the first rental began with the owner and the second did not, we regard the periods as one rental and exempt him. In the laws of bailees we follow the more lenient treatment. The Tur likewise writes this. Sema.
