tzadi) Ibid., seif 12, from Rami bar Chama's unresolved questions there. The Maggid Mishneh writes in law 9 that Rashi explains the first four questions where the owner was not present. His wording is: 'If he borrowed it for intercourse with it himself, is he liable for unavoidable accidents?' The issue is whether the borrowing for which the Torah imposed liability for unavoidable accidents is specifically ordinary borrowing, and similarly for the other cases. According to this explanation, if he borrowed it while the owner was present he is certainly exempt. In my humble opinion, Rashi's words imply that he remains liable for theft and loss like a paid guardian, as the Tur writes in the name of the Ramah, that if there is a benefit worth a perutah he is liable as a paid guardian.
The explanatory phrase means that he wishes to appear wealthy and important, so that householders will not refrain from extending him credit, Rashi.
