heh) The language of the Rambam there, chapter 2, law 10. The Maggid Mishneh writes that this is an if-you-say question on 98b, and the Rambam rules in accordance with the if-you-say formulation, as is his general practice throughout the Gemara; the other questions remain unresolved. Rashi explains: he borrowed the item while its owner was working for him, and before returning it he then rented it from the same owner, while at the time of the rental the owner was no longer working for him, and it was stolen or lost. What is the law?
vav) The language of the Gemara there is that rental is connected to borrowing, as Rashi explains, because every liability of a renter for theft and loss also exists in a borrower.
zayin) Rabbi Abba bar Memel's question there. The Beit Yosef writes that it appears the Rambam did not have the words 'if you say' except in the first question, and all the others remained unresolved. The Maggid Mishneh likewise writes this.
chet) This follows the reading of Tosafot there and of the Rosh in his rulings, who read in this question, 'if you say,' because once the two arrangements are connected in part, they are treated as connected in full. He writes that according to the Geonim, who hold that every 'if you say' resolves the preceding question, the question of one who rented while the owner was present and then borrowed while the owner was present is resolved. The Beit Yosef already expressed surprise at the Tur for writing this, since the Rosh's method is not to rule this way, and according to the Rosh the question remained unresolved; so too in the Remezim.
