But if he first rented it, etc. The Gemara asks whether, once the legal category changes from rental to borrowing, the fact that the rental began while the owner was present continues to exempt an accident occurring during the later borrowing when the owner is no longer present. On one hand, borrowing has a distinct law because a borrower is liable for unavoidable accidents while a renter is not. On the other hand, rental and borrowing overlap regarding liability for theft and loss, and perhaps that connection is enough to treat the later status as continuous.
And then borrowed it when the owner was absent, etc. Rashi and the Rosh explain these later questions on the assumption that rental and borrowing can sometimes be legally connected. The new issue is whether we can extend continuity through two transitions, rental into borrowing and then another change back, or whether that is too remote. Rama's alternative view, that if one rented with the owner present and later borrowed without him it is still considered custody with the owner present, follows a textual version in which the Gemara says 'if you say' before the third question. Such a phrase generally treats the preceding question as resolved affirmatively. The Rambam did not have that reading and therefore treats the matter as doubtful.
