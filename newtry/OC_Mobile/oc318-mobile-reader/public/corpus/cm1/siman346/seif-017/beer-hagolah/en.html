The Maggid Mishneh writes that he did not find this stated explicitly in the Gemara, but Rambam's reasoning is correct. Since the purchaser is like one who buys from the woman, his position is no better than hers, for he derives his right through her. Since she informed him and he then used the item, he is liable. This is unlike heirs, as explained above in siman 341, seif 3, who are not liable for unavoidable accidents even if they used it, because inheritance comes automatically and they can say that they inherit their father's right to use it without accepting his liability for unavoidable accidents.
