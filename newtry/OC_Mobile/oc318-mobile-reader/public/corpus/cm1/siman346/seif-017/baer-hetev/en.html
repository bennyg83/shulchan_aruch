Himself. See Maharshal, chapter 10 of Bava Kamma, siman 40, and what I wrote in siman 291, seif katan 45.
Money. See below, siman 424, seif 10. If she owns melog property, tzon barzel property, or an addition beyond the basic ketubah, she must sell her contingent interest and pay. Here the case is where she owns none of these, relying on what was written there. This also appears from Darkei Moshe. The Gemara, Bava Kamma 89a, asks concerning personal injury that she should sell her melog property and pay, and answers that she has none. The questioner's assumption that if she has melog property she must sell it and pay implies that the same applies to other obligations. Sema.
Entered. If someone lent her a precious stone for medicinal use and it was stolen, the husband is exempt. But if he lent her ordinary clothing appropriate to her station and wealth, the husband presumably consents and is liable even for unavoidable losses. This does not apply to clothing of unusually great value unless she regularly conducts business in the home. Maharshal, Bava Kamma, chapter 8, siman 31. See there. Shach.
