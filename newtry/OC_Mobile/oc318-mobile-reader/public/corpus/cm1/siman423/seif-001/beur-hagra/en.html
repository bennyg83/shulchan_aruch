(Supplement) Damage and pain, etc.; likewise loss of employment and medical expenses, etc. The Tosefta, Bava Kamma chapter 9, teaches: one who strikes a pregnant woman and causes miscarriage pays damage, pain, medical expenses, loss of employment, and humiliation to the woman, and the value of the offspring to the husband.
(Supplement) Damage, etc. This requires study, because the baraita follows the first tanna, whereas we rule like Rabbi Yehuda ben Beteira, as stated in Even HaEzer 83, seif 1. Rashi's explanation in Ketubot chapter 6, limiting Rabbi Yehuda ben Beteira's share to humiliation, resolves the difficulty, and Rosh writes likewise. Rashi in Bava Kamma, however, s.v. 'we evaluate,' includes damage as Rambam does, and Rambam too expressly includes damage.
And pain, etc.; likewise, etc. See Rosh there and Tosafot 42b, s.v. 'he gives,' and 59a, 'because she will ultimately, etc.' See Rashi there, s.v. 'deduct,' etc.
(Supplement) And how, etc. The law does not follow Rabban Shimon ben Gamliel. The statements attributed to him here are Amoraic disputes concerning Rabbi Yehuda's rule. The Gemara's broader method follows the other view, as seen at the end of HaMadir and in Bava Metzia chapter 3. The ruling at the end of HaShoel that the intercalated month is divided according to Rabban Shimon ben Gamliel depends on his acceptance of Sumchus, while we consistently reject Sumchus. Rif and Rosh in Perek Get Pashut likewise do not accept a general rule in favor of Rabban Shimon ben Gamliel.
(Supplement) And how, etc. This also rejects Rabbi Yochanan's general rule that the law follows Rabban Shimon ben Gamliel in a Mishna. Rif explains in Get Pashut that this is not an absolute rule. Rava in Avodah Zarah 69b states independently that the law follows Rabban Shimon ben Gamliel, which would be unnecessary if Rabbi Yochanan's rule were universal. Rava also relies there on an anonymous Mishna. Shmuel likewise rules separately like Rabban Shimon ben Gamliel in Shabbat chapter 14 and at the beginning of Yom Tov chapter 3, and in Bava Batra 133b. Later Amoraim follow Shmuel's limited approach, as stated at the end of Ketubot chapter 4.
And some say that it is, etc. Even Rabbah could accept this, as Tosafot there, s.v. 'is it then,' Rosh, and Raavad explain. The sugya on 43a, however, follows Rambam and Shulchan Aruch. Tosafot there, s.v. 'a divorced woman,' remains with a difficulty, and Rashba resolves it only with difficulty.
