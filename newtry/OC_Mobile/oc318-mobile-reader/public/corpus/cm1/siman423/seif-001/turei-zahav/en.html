Seif 1, damages and pain belong to the woman. Rashi explains in Bava Kamma 49a that damages are divided according to the rule in Ketubot distinguishing between injury in a concealed and exposed place. The Maggid Mishneh writes, however, that the Rambam implies all damages belong to the woman. The Rosh in HaParah likewise indicates that the distinction between exposed and concealed applies only to humiliation, not to damages, and the Tur also appears to rule so. Maharshal writes that the Rashba agrees: in ordinary bodily injury the husband receives a share because he must be appeased for the loss, but here he has already been compensated through payment for the fetuses, and the Sages did not grant him a further share in the damages. This is the practical ruling. The Sema was unaware of these sources and therefore offered a strained distinction. The Even HaEzer rule is not based solely on work disability, since physical diminution is also counted there. Rashi explains that regarding humiliation “concealed” and “exposed” means whether the act occurred privately or publicly, whereas regarding damages it refers to whether the bodily injury itself is in a visible place. This is contrary to the Sema.
