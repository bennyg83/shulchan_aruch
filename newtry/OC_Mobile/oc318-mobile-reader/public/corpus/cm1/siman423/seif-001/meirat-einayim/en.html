Although he did not intend, etc. Scripture says in Mishpatim: "If men fight and strike a pregnant woman and her children come out, but there is no fatality, he shall surely be punished as the woman's husband imposes upon him." From "if men fight" we learn that the blow to the woman was unintended. From "but there is no fatality," meaning no death of the woman, we learn that only then is he liable for payment for the fetuses. If the woman dies, he is exempt from fetal payment because the more severe punishment absorbs the monetary liability, as the Tur and Mechaber write at the end of this siman. From "the woman's husband" we learn that the Torah awarded the fetal payment to the husband.
And the woman's damage and pain. See Even HaEzer siman 83, where the Tur and Mechaber write that damage and diminution belong partly to him and partly to her. There is no contradiction. That law concerns permanent bodily damage, such as cutting off her hand or another organ, which reduces her ability to perform work for him, and therefore he has a share. Here we are speaking of weakening her body without permanently diminishing her capacity for work. The husband's financial loss is limited to her loss of work while ill, and that is paid to him.
