The value of the fetuses belongs to her. Since kiddushin does not take effect with a maidservant or non-Jewish woman, she is not described as "the wife of a man" with respect to this Israelite who impregnated her, and he has no right to the value of the fetuses even if he is alive at the time she is struck. This differs from an Israelite woman impregnated by an Israelite outside marriage, where we rule that the value of the fetuses belongs to the man who impregnated her, because in that case they are persons with whom kiddushin could have taken effect. So writes Maggid Mishneh. Therefore the payment is hers, because according to this view the Torah grants it to the woman where there is no husband with a right to it.
Some say that if her husband is alive, the payment belongs to the husband. Meaning, where she became pregnant from an Israelite, as explained. Their reason is that they compare this case to an Israelite man who has relations outside marriage with an Israelite woman.
If not, he is exempt. These authorities follow their stated reasoning, that the woman herself has no proprietary right to the value of her fetuses.
