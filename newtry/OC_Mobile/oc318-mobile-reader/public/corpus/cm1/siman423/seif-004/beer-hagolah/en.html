The Maggid Mishneh writes that this accords with the explicit conclusion in Ketubot chapter 3, 35a, and with the teaching of the academy of Chizkiyah that there is no distinction between inadvertent and intentional conduct in this respect. Rambam follows Rav Adda bar Ahavah, who concludes in Bava Kamma chapter 1, 42a, that if men intended to strike one another, even if disaster befell the woman, they still pay the fine, and Rabbi likewise says so in Sanhedrin chapter HaNisrafin, 79a. This differs from the academy of Chizkiyah on that point, and the issues are not dependent upon one another. Rambam has already ruled like Rabbi Shimon that one who intended to kill one person and killed another is exempt. It appears Rambam derived this from Rav Adda's straightforward challenge against Abaye and Rava, and because the conclusion of the sugya is anonymous like Rav Adda. Other approaches exist.
