One who strikes, etc.; when does this apply, etc. See Be'er HaGolah and Lechem Mishneh.
(Supplement) When does this apply, when he intended, etc. This follows 26a: 'Let a person be liable, etc.' See Tosafot there. Rashba, citing some authorities, explains that the case concerns lack of intent, because we rule like Rabbi Yochanan that ransom is due even without intent, unlike Chizkiyah.
(Supplement) When does this apply, etc. Be'er HaGolah cites Maggid Mishneh and says Rambam follows Rav Adda bar Ahavah and Rabbi rather than the School of Chizkiyah. That cannot be correct, for Rambam himself rules in Laws of Murder chapter 4, and in Laws of a Maiden, like the School of Chizkiyah rather than Rabbi. Nor can Rambam understand 'intended' as Tosafot does, namely intent regarding that same property, because Rambam expressly speaks of different property, like Rashi. Rambam must hold that although the law generally follows the School of Chizkiyah, fetal damages are different because Rav Adda bar Ahavah disputes them, and we follow him.
And some say, etc. Raavad rules like the School of Chizkiyah, as Ketubot 35a indicates. See Tosafot there, s.v. 'and is there,' and Sanhedrin 79a.
