As it is stated, "and there shall be no fatality," etc. The correct reading is: "he shall surely be punished." Scripture did not distinguish between inadvertent and intentional conduct in a matter that carries beit din's death penalty with respect to exemption from monetary payment. When does this apply, etc. This is also Rambam's text there.
When he intended to strike the woman, etc. Although the verse itself discusses two men fighting, where one intends to strike the other and inadvertently strikes the woman, as written at the beginning of this siman, the inference from "and there shall be no fatality," namely that if there is a fatality he is exempt from paying for the fetuses because of kam lei bid'rabba minei, does not apply according to this view when he did not intend to strike the woman, because there is then no possible capital liability. But when he intended to strike the woman, even if her death was inadvertent, the verse teaches that whenever intentional conduct would have incurred the death penalty, even inadvertent conduct resulting in that death exempts him from monetary liability. So write the Tur and Mechaber at the beginning of the following siman.
Like a matter that does not involve beit din's death penalty. This is likewise Rambam's wording in chapter 4 of Hilchot Chovel. Although Rambam holds that where one intended to kill one person and killed another there is no beit din death penalty, as written there, he says "like a matter" using a comparative expression because even an inadvertent killing carries no beit din death penalty, yet we nevertheless say that the killer is exempt from the value of the fetuses because there would have been a possible death penalty had the act been intentional. Here, where he did not intend to strike the woman at all, the novelty is that we treat it as though there were no possible capital liability whatsoever.
