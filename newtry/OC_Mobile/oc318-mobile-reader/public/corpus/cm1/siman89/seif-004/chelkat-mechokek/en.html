The heirs of her ketubah are obligated in her burial. This wording implies that if she is poor and leaves nothing to inherit, no relatives are obligated in her burial and she is buried from communal charity. See Tosafot at the beginning of chapter Almanah Nizonet, passage beginning "and they are not obligated in her burial," which implies that even if she left only movable property and, under Talmudic law, her ketubah could not be collected from it, nevertheless those relatives who would have inherited from her had she possessed ketubah assets are obligated to bury her. Raavad likewise writes in his gloss that the heirs of her dowry and nichsei melog bury her even if they inherited nothing. One can perhaps reject that inference, as I will write below. I later found in Responsa Maharil, siman 64: if one undertook to bring her into marriage entirely unclothed, would he then not be obligated to bury her? Rather, the Sages did not distinguish in their enactment. See there.
