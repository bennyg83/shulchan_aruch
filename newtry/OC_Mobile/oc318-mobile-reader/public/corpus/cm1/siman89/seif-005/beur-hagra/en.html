He must therefore take an oath while holding a sacred object, etc. Since he does not claim ownership of the object itself, this follows from the mishnah concerning one who recognizes his utensils and books. Although Shevuot states that as long as the cloak remains in the artisan's possession, if it was delivered to him at the proper time, and so forth, this indicates that while the cloak is in his possession he does not require an oath. The distinction there is only between a claim made within the proper time and one made afterward. The expression that he 'swears and collects' is not difficult, because the term 'collects' is inapplicable while the object remains in his possession. The Yerushalmi, however, states explicitly that when the pledge is in his possession he collects without an oath. The Yerushalmi therefore cannot disagree with the mishnah concerning one who recognizes his utensils, and the plain wording of the Gemara likewise indicates collection without an oath. See Milhamot, which distinguishes this case from an artisan holding an object as security for his wages. Even according to the view that an artisan does not acquire the improvement made to the utensil, the object is nevertheless treated as his for purposes of the oath, since he holds it for his wages. See also Ran.
