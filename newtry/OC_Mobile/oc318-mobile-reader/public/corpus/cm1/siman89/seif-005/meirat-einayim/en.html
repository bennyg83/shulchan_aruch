And the other says, 'I fixed only one.' Meaning that regarding that one he says, 'here it is.' Otherwise, even when the garment is not in the artisan's possession he would be obligated in a Torah oath as one who admits part of the claim.So that he could claim, 'I purchased it.' Below, siman 134, this is explained.
The case is where, at the time of the lawsuit, witnesses did not see this garment in the artisan's possession, so he has a migo that he could have said, 'I returned it to you.' But if witnesses currently saw it in his possession, even though it was not originally delivered to him before witnesses, he cannot use the same migo in the same manner.
The law therefore turns on the artisan's available possessory claims and whether the object was seen in his hand.
