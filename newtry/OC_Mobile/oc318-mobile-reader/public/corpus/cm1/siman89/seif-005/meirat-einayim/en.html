So that he could claim, 'I purchased it.' Below, siman 134, this is explained. The case is where, at the time of the lawsuit, witnesses did not see this garment in the artisan's possession, so he has a migo that he could have said, 'I returned it to you.' But if witnesses currently saw it in his possession, even though it was not originally delivered to him before witnesses, he cannot use the same migo in the same manner. The law therefore turns on the artisan's available possessory claims and whether the object was seen in his hand.
He swears while holding a sacred object. Although had he claimed, 'I purchased it,' he would have been required only to take a hesset oath, once he admits that the garment belongs to the householder and merely seeks to retain it until his wage is paid, the Sages imposed the stronger oath applicable to this artisan's claim.
