While holding a sacred object. The Sema writes that if he claimed, "I purchased it and it is mine," he would take only a shevuat heset. Nevertheless, since he admits that it belongs to the employer and merely wishes to retain it until the employer pays his entire claim, his law is like that of others who swear and collect, who swear while holding a sacred object, as stated in siman 62. The Shach challenges this ruling at length and concludes: It appears to me that whenever one of those who ordinarily swear and collect is already in possession of the property and has a migo, he collects without the severe oath while holding a sacred object, and takes only a shevuat heset.
Swears. The Shach writes that what the Beit Yosef states here is incorrect. Since, as a matter of law, the primary ruling in his view is that the employer takes only a shevuat heset where he does not admit part of the claim, as explained above in seif 20, there is no need to lengthen the discussion. See the Taz and Tamim De'im, siman 195.
