If the worker was... Yerushalmi there, halachah 1, where the case of a slave and of a suspected oath-taker is discussed. Beit din imposes the oath on the slave or on the suspected party's counterpart. The implication that the employer swears follows from the ensuing dispute where both are suspected. The same reasoning applies to a minor, to whom an oath is not administered.
The worker... but... Meaning, he swears against the employer. The Yerushalmi says explicitly that if the employer dies, the worker swears to the heirs. But if the worker dies, do his heirs swear to the employer? Rabbi Levi asks this and the Yerushalmi answers that the enactment was made for the worker, not necessarily for his heirs.
