Even if his wage was only one perutah. Know that the Rambam writes, and the Tur agrees, that even a hired worker swears and collects only where the amount denied is at least two ma'ot of silver, like the other oaths discussed at the beginning of siman 88. The Mechaber, however, rules like the Raavad and the Rambam's teachers that a hired worker may swear and collect even when the denied amount is only a perutah. The reason is that the worker's very life depends upon his wage. What is a worker who hired himself for only a perutah supposed to do? Should he leave empty handed merely because his wage was small?

The hired worker swears and collects, etc., but not the worker's heirs. Rama's wording proves that the worker's heirs do not swear and collect even from the employer himself. First, he states without qualification that the worker's heirs do not swear and collect. Second, had they been able to collect from the employer himself, he should also have stated in the first clause that the worker's heirs swear and collect from the employer. This is also clear from Darkei Moshe, which cites the Rashba's responsum as the source. The Beit Yosef quotes the Rashba somewhat differently, citing the Yerushalmi: if the employer died, the worker certainly swears to the heirs and collects; the question is whether, if the worker died, his heirs may swear and collect. The Yerushalmi responds: Was the enactment made for the worker, or also for his heirs? The meaning is that while the worker himself certainly collects from the employer's heirs by oath, it remained doubtful whether the worker's heirs could collect even from the employer himself, and all the more so from the employer's heirs. The answer is that the enactment was made for the worker alone, not for his heirs, so they do not collect even from the employer himself. Darkei Moshe indeed cites the Rashba in abbreviated form: if the employer dies, the worker swears and collects from his heirs, but the worker's heirs do not swear and collect. I later saw the Yerushalmi itself, which frames the question concerning the worker's heirs collecting from the employer's heirs. This wording compelled the Rashba to explain that the question includes both whether they collect from the employer himself and, if so, whether they collect from his heirs. The answer is that the enactment was not extended to the worker's heirs. This appears completely clear to me.
