And collects. The Sema writes that the reason is explained in the Gemara. An employer is generally preoccupied with his workers, and because of that distraction he may think that he paid this worker when in fact he paid another. Therefore the Sages believed the hired worker to swear and collect, and they did not distinguish further. Even where the employer is not actually preoccupied with workers, the worker may swear and collect. The Shach challenges the Mechaber's ruling that even if the wage was only a perutah the worker swears, and after lengthy discussion concludes that in cases of those who swear and collect, the defendant must deny at least two ma'ah of silver. Since many leading authorities agree with the Rambam on this point, that is how we rule, particularly because it is a leniency for the defendant. See the Taz, who resolves the Tur's words on this point. See also Responsa Chacham Tzvi, siman 125.
The minor. Although ordinarily money is not extracted from a minor, as the Tur and Shulchan Aruch state at the end of siman 91 and the beginning of siman 96, here it is extracted for the minor's own benefit, since otherwise workers would not agree to perform his work. Sema.
Swears. The Shach writes that the wording implies that the employer takes only a shevuat heset. However, the Ran, from whom this ruling is derived, indicates that the oath reverts to the employer, meaning the same oath that the worker would otherwise have taken, namely the Mishnah oath while holding a sacred object. Nevertheless, the Rambam appears to hold that with all who swear and collect, including a hired worker, if the claimant is disqualified from taking an oath, the opposing party swears only a shevuat heset. The Maggid Mishneh likewise states this explicitly. See there for the Shach's lengthy discussion.
Heirs. See the Sema, who discusses at length and proves from the Yerushalmi that the heirs of a hired worker do not swear and collect even from the employer himself. The Shach writes that this lengthy proof was unnecessary, because the Tur and Shulchan Aruch explicitly state at the end of seif 14 of siman 108 that heirs do not swear and collect, because a person cannot bequeath an oath to his children. This is from Baal HaTerumot in the name of the Ittur, who derived it from this Yerushalmi.
