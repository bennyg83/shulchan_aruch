These swear and collect. The Shach writes in Tekfo Kohen that where the Sages instituted an oath for a claimant who then collects, such as an employee, robbed person, or injured person, if the defendant refuses to pay, beit din does not descend to his property. He compares it to rabbinic theft of a lost object of a minor. I do not agree. In the minor's lost object the very monetary ownership is only rabbinic. Here the underlying damages or wages are true money obligations. The Sages merely transferred evidentiary credibility to the claimant upon an oath. Once they did so, the claimant's right is fully enforceable through the power of beit din to transfer property. Many rabbinic acquisitions, such as meshichah and lifting, are enforced by descending to property once the Sages made them legally effective. The same must be true here. Ran says that once an injured party has taken the special oath, that established amount can function like witness testimony and create a Torah partial admission oath on the rest. That result would be impossible if the first amount were not truly collectible. Therefore one who takes one of these claimant oaths can collect through ordinary judicial enforcement.
