All oaths imposed by the Torah, etc. The Torah imposes three basic oaths in three settings: first, one who admits part of a claim; second, one contradicted by a single witness; third, the oath of custodians. In all three, the defendant swears to the claimant and is exempt, as the verse in the section of custodians says, "and its owner shall accept it, and he shall not pay," which the Sages interpret to mean that the claimant accepts the defendant's oath and the defendant need not pay once he has sworn. This is what is meant by "they swear and do not pay." Apart from these three forms, the Torah itself does not impose an oath. The Sages of the Mishnah, however, instituted in certain cases that the claimant swears and collects, including a hired worker, one who was robbed, and others, for reasons explained here and in the following simanim in their respective places.
