(Shulchan Aruch, seif 2) “Even if his wage was one perutah.” The Tur writes here, “The Rambam’s view appears reasonable.” This is difficult, for the Rambam himself later retracts, as stated in seif 11: “If his wage was one perutah and the employer says, ‘I paid it,’ the worker collects only with an oath; and similarly all those who swear and collect,” implying that with an oath he does collect. Kesef Mishneh explains there that for practical law the Rambam resolved the objection in accordance with his teachers, who follow Raavad. This is exceedingly difficult. Moreover, the Tur himself writes the same at the end of this siman. One cannot say that the Tur too merely intended a stringency, for then he should have written immediately, “Nevertheless, one should be stringent.” The Rambam himself presents no difficulty. He first stated his general view that all who swear and collect require a claim of two silver ma’ot, and afterward ruled specifically regarding a worker that even one perutah suffices in practice, following the view of his teachers, as Maggid Mishneh wrote. The Tur, however, can be explained as follows. He understood Raavad’s objection, “What shall the poor worker do?” as referring necessarily to a case where the employer denies the entire claim and the whole wage is only one perutah. In such a case the Sages certainly enacted that the worker should swear and collect, for otherwise he would leave empty-handed. But the same does not follow in a case of partial admission. Even if the whole wage is one perutah, the reason of “leaving empty-handed” shows that the requirement of two silver ma’ot is disregarded only where the entire claim is denied. In partial admission, where the worker does not leave entirely empty-handed, two silver ma’ot are still required. Therefore, the Tur writes that he does not accept Raavad’s proof as establishing a general rule. Although where the entire wage is only one perutah we certainly disregard the requirement of two silver ma’ot, in partial admission the ordinary rule remains. At the end of the siman, which concerns complete denial and a wage of only one perutah, the worker does swear even on one perutah, so that he not leave empty-handed. This resolves the Tur’s words correctly.
