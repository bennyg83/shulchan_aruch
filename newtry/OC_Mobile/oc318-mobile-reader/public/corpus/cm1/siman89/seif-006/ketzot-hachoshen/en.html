He swears while holding a sacred object. The Gemara concerning an artisan holding a garment says that while the garment remains in the artisan's possession, the owner must bring proof. Baal HaMaor and Ramban ask how this accords with the Geonim who require a claimant holding collateral to take a severe oath, since the baraita appears to allow the artisan to collect without an oath. Ramban answers according to the view that an artisan acquires the improvement in the object. The artisan is therefore claiming through a substantive property right in the garment itself. Ran gives a different explanation: in ordinary wage disputes the employee would be believed to collect by the special enactment, and the additional oath exists only to appease the employer. While the artisan still holds the garment, the employer is already sufficiently appeased by the security and no further oath is needed. The Shach argues that this proves artisans never need a severe oath while holding the item. Yet Ramban's answer works only according to the opinion that an artisan acquires the improvement, while Rambam rules that an artisan does not. Ran's answer likewise follows Rabbi Yehudah's view that even wage amount is not remembered well and the employee can collect, whereas our practical law does not necessarily follow that view regarding every wage dispute. Nevertheless, if the dispute is only whether the employer already paid, and the artisan still holds the garment, there is strong reason that the artisan collects without an additional oath. The ordinary employee enactment already favors him during the proper time, and the possession of the garment removes the employer's need for further appeasement. This also helps explain the baraita's distinction between while the garment remains with the artisan and after it has been returned. So long as he holds it, the wage is effectively still within its payment period because the prohibition of delayed wages does not begin while the artisan retains the item. Once he returns it, the ordinary distinction between within and after the payment period applies. This resolves several difficulties in Tosafot and also helps reconcile Rif's view that an artisan is not believed through ordinary migo. The baraita need not rest on migo at all. The artisan's credibility can rest on the wage enactment and the fact that the owner is already appeased by the garment's continued possession.
