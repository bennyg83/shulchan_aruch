Except for the hired worker, concerning whom we are lenient and open for him, etc. In the Perishah I demonstrated that the Rambam and Mechaber teach three points here. First, although in ordinary cases beit din may impose an extended oath on its own initiative even without the claimant's request, it does not do so to a hired worker. Second, even if the claimant asks that another matter be included in the worker's oath and the worker remains silent, beit din is lenient and itself tells him to swear without the extension, explaining that additional claims are not rolled onto a hired worker. Third, just as beit din is lenient by speaking on his behalf concerning extension of the oath, it also tells him, "Do not trouble yourself. Swear and collect," even though judges may not ordinarily coach another creditor in that way. See the Derishah, where I explained the rationale.
