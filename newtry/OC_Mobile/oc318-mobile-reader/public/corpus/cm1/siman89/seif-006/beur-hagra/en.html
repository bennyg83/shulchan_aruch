A hired worker, etc., and beit din opens the claim for him, etc. The Gemara explains that the statement that there is a practical difference regarding whether beit din opens the claim for him refers to the hired worker. Rav Huna says that beit din does not impose additional stringencies upon him, while Rav Hisda says that beit din affirmatively assists him. The Gemara asks what practical difference exists between them. It answers that according to Rav Huna, beit din does not open the claim for him, but merely refrains from imposing additional oaths through oath aggregation; according to Rav Hisda, beit din opens the claim for him. The questioner initially assumed that Rav Hisda too spoke only about oath aggregation, and therefore asked what difference existed. The answer is that Rav Hisda also means that beit din opens the claim and assists him by stating the relevant argument on his behalf. The ruling follows Rav Hisda, because according to the Gemara's conclusion, once the issue is defined as opening the claim, Rav Hisda does not necessarily dispute Rav Huna's statement; the apparent dispute arose only under the questioner's initial assumption.
