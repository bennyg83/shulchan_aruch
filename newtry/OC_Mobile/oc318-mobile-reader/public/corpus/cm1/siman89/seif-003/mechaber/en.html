When does this apply? When there are witnesses that the employer hired the worker and that the worker performed labor for him. If there were not two witnesses, the employer is believed to say, 'I hired you and paid your wages,' through a migo that he could have said, 'I did not hire you,' and the employer takes a heses oath. If he admits part, he takes a Torah oath. {Rama: Some say that even if he denies the entire claim, when one witness testifies that he hired the worker, the employer must take a Torah oath.} Similarly, if the worker sues after the prescribed time, even if he was hired before witnesses, the burden of proof rests upon the claimant. If he brings no proof, the employer takes a heses oath. What is the prescribed time? A day worker has the entire following night; a night worker has the entire following day. If the worker proves that he continuously demanded payment throughout the prescribed time, he swears and collects throughout the day of that demand. For example, if he worked until Monday evening, his time is the entire Tuesday night, but on Tuesday he no longer swears and collects. If he brings witnesses that he demanded payment throughout Tuesday night, he swears and collects throughout Tuesday. From Tuesday night onward, however, the burden of proof rests upon the claimant. Similarly, if he brings witnesses that he continued demanding payment through Thursday, he swears and collects throughout Thursday.
