They swear and collect. It appears that even where the heirs of a worker make a definite claim that their father was not paid, they do not swear and collect. The enactment was made for the worker himself and not for his heirs, as the Yerushalmi says. Since no enactment was made for heirs, even a definite claim does not help. Beit Shmuel in Even HaEzer siman 89 writes that if the worker's heirs make a definite claim they do swear and collect, and that only where they are uncertain do they fail because a person cannot bequeath an oath to his children. But if that were the sole reason, the Yerushalmi would not have needed to say that the enactment was made only for the worker and not for his heirs. In an uncertain case the general rule that one does not bequeath an oath would already suffice, especially where the worker himself could collect only by oath. The Yerushalmi therefore teaches that even definite heirs do not receive the special enactment. This is straightforward.
