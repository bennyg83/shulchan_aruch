Witnesses that he hired him and that he worked. Witnesses only to the hiring are insufficient, because perhaps he never performed the work. Witnesses only to the work are likewise insufficient, because where someone performed work without a hiring arrangement the Sages did not institute the worker's oath to collect. In that case the employer swears and has a migo that he could have denied hiring him at all. See Beurim: even if those witnesses merely traveled overseas afterward, the employer retains the migo.

And he hired him before one witness. This is not a case of one obligated to swear but unable to swear. Even if two witnesses had established the hiring, the worker would collect only with an oath. Therefore, with only one witness the employer is not treated as one who must swear and cannot, but must take an oath patterned after a Torah oath. According to the Mechaber, where the hiring is established by one witness the employer takes only hesset. Although he is believed only through a migo that he could have said, "I never hired you," and such a denial would itself have created a Torah oath because of the one witness, nevertheless on his actual claim he takes only hesset. See Beurim for the reason.
After its time. There is a presumption that an employer does not violate the prohibition against delaying wages, so he places the matter upon his mind and remembers. There is also a presumption that a worker does not delay collecting his wage. Gemara. See Beurim: even an artisan who, according to the opinion that an artisan acquires the improvement in the object, may not violate the wage delay prohibition, nevertheless does not receive the special worker's oath when the garment is not in his possession. See Beurim further: if witnesses establish that the employer delayed part of the wage beyond the prohibited time, the worker is then believed regarding the remainder too and may swear and collect. If he demanded payment within the time before witnesses and the employer said, "I paid you," and he later sues in beit din after the time, the employer cannot claim that he had paid either before the witnesses' denial or afterward. But if he sued in beit din within the time, beit din ruled that the worker should swear and collect, and the worker actually swore, the employer is believed to say he afterward paid him. If the worker had not yet sworn, the employer cannot say, "He already swore and I paid him," because oaths are taken before the court attendant, as explained in siman 87. See also siman 78. If they dispute whether the period has passed, the employer says it has and the worker says it is still within the term, the employer is believed. But if there are no witnesses that it is within the term and the employer himself admits that it is still within the term, yet says, "I paid you within the term," he may be believed through a migo that he could have said the time had already passed. This requires study.
A day laborer. A worker hired by the hour during the day collects throughout the day, and one hired by the hour at night collects throughout the night. One hired for a week or month, if his term ended during the day he collects throughout that day, and if it ended at night he collects throughout that night. See siman 339. Whatever is described there as beyond the time for the prohibition of delayed wages is likewise considered here to be after the time.
