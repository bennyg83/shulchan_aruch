Derivatives of a pit, such as one's stone, knife, or burden that caused damage. It contains seven seifim:
The derivatives of a pit include one's stone, knife, burden, and similar objects that one left in the public domain and that caused damage where they lay, whether he abandoned ownership of them or not. The same applies if he placed them on top of his roof, they fell in an ordinary wind, and after coming to rest they caused damage where they lay, for this resembles a pit: his property causes damage in the place where it rests. They therefore have all the laws of a pit, including liability for full damages from the outset for the death or injury of an animal and for injury to a person, whether the victim tripped over the stone and was injured by the stone or tripped on the ground and was injured by the stone. But if he tripped over the stone and was injured by the ground, the owner of the obstacle is exempt and is exempt from liability for the death of a person and for damage to utensils. Likewise, if he left these objects on his own property, then abandoned his property but not the objects, and someone tripped on the ground and was injured by this obstacle, the owner of the obstacle is liable.
