The place of the water channel. The buyer needs to dig an irrigation channel through the seller's field in order to water his irrigated field, which requires much water. The seller must therefore give him a strip of land through the field four cubits wide along the entire length in which the channel is to run: two cubits for the channel itself and one cubit on each side for its banks. So writes the Rambam. The Tur, however, says that he gives one cubit for the width of the channel and two cubits for the banks. Rashbam likewise records a version that reads this way and writes that this is the primary text. According to that version it is called an amah because the channel itself is one cubit wide. I wrote in the Perishah that the Rosh's textual version also appears to support this. It is surprising that Rama did not mention this opinion at all.
To irrigate with it by a small channel. The Tur likewise reads “by a kilon,” while Maimonides reads “by a silon” with a samekh. The Maggid Mishneh notes this and writes in the name of Ibn Migash that it means a channel through which the wastewater and laundry water of the courtyard passes. Rashbam similarly explains that it is used to water animals and to wash clothes and utensils, and is therefore not as large as an ordinary irrigation channel. The Maggid Mishneh's wording implies that both explanations apply to the reading “silon.” Were it not for his explanation, it would appear that Rashbam's explanation belongs specifically to the reading “kilon.” See Bava Batra 99b, where Shmuel's statement reads “kilon,” and Rashbam writes, “the channel of the silon, some say kilon, and for watering animals,” etc. The conjunction suggests that his explanation follows the reading immediately preceding it.
