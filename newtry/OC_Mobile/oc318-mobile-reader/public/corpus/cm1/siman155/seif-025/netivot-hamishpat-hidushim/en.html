To distance four cubits. The reason is that people plow around trees. The length of a plow in Eretz Yisrael is four cubits, and outside Eretz Yisrael two cubits. Therefore, if one planted his trees directly adjacent to the boundary, his neighbor must distance the entire four cubits, even if he wishes to erect a fence and plant trees alongside it. The first owner can say, "I already established a right to bring my plow into your field as needed." See the Beurim, where I explain that this requires three years of chazakah together with a claim. But if the first owner's tree already stands two cubits from the boundary, the neighbor need distance only another two cubits to complete the four, just as when both come to plant trees simultaneously. We say that they originally agreed to provide the required four cubits between them. Sema:
Between vines. This refers to where the neighbor already has vines and the first party now seeks to plant ordinary trees. In the reverse case, he need distance only as much as between one tree and another, because he is then harming himself. This is according to those who explain that the damage results from birds flying from the tall trees to the vines, or to a low grain field, and that this bird damage requires more than four cubits. According to the Rif and Rambam, who require only four cubits, they apparently rely on another reason. Perhaps according to their rationale vines also damage trees. The matter requires study. Sema:
He plants a seed. According to the Rosh, who writes that this is deemed his direct force because he himself drives the birds from the tree and they then settle on the vine, even when planting from seed he must distance. Sema:
The garden owner need not distance. This applies not only to trees already present; he may even plant new trees. Although the roots will extend beneath the wall and damage it, the halachah follows Rabbi Yose that each person may plant within his own property, because the roots travel on their own and are not considered his direct force. This differs from a window in siman 154, where by sealing the window he directly damages the other person's established use, so we say that the seller sold generously. Roots, by contrast, come on their own. The Rama alludes to this by writing, "See below, siman 156." Sema:
A fence between them. Even between a tree and a vine, where the concern is birds, the fence interrupts their flight pattern. Even if the fence is lower than the tree, the birds first alight on the fence, so their movement is not considered the planter's direct force. Sema:
