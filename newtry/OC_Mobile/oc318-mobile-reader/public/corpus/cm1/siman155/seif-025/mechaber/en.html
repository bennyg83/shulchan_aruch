When another person's field is planted with vines or other trees, and one comes to plant in his own field, he must distance vines from vines, or trees from trees, by four cubits. Rama: This is because that amount of space is needed for the plow to pass between the trees. Where it is not customary to plow between the trees, no separation is required. Where separation is required and both parties come to plant at the same time, each distances himself by half the required measure. This applies in Eretz Yisrael. Outside Eretz Yisrael, one distances vines from vines, and trees from trees, by two cubits; between vines and other trees, or between vines and a grain field, the distance is four cubits. Rama: Some say that between vines and trees one must distance them according to an assessment sufficient to prevent birds from flying directly from the trees to the vines. Some say this applies only when one plants trees, but if he plants a seed and the tree grows on its own, no separation is required. If Reuven sold his house to one person and his garden to another, the owner of the garden need not distance his trees, even if the house was sold first; we do not apply here the principle that a seller sells generously. See above, siman 154, seif 28. Wherever there is a fence between them, each may plant adjacent to the fence on his side.
