Likewise, a stove. Meaning, even a baker's stove. Alternatively, the novelty is that a stove is used constantly for cooking, unlike an oven used for bread only intermittently. See the Perishah.
Nevertheless, initially he can prevent it. In the Perishah I wrote that the wording should be understood as 'because initially he can prevent it.' Since he could have protested initially and failed to do so, he later loses the right to protest. Otherwise, wherever there is no right of protest there can be no chazakah.
Some say that with smoke that is not constant, etc., and so I rule. Darkei Moshe writes that Mahari Isserlein derived this carefully from Mordechai, Hagahot Ashri, and Maimoniyot, and concludes that this is the proper ruling because Mahari Isserlein was a later authority and a reliable sage. This is unlike Beit Yosef, who wished to reject their position in favor of Ramah because Ramah was a famous authority.
