(Supplement) One may not dig, etc., beside his wall, etc. This is Rambam's wording. The Tur objects that the Gemara concludes that the mishna refers to the wall of his fellow's cistern and all the listed items concern distancing from a cistern wall, not an ordinary wall. Nevertheless, reason dictates that they must also be distanced from an ordinary wall, for they cause more damage than urine, which requires three handbreadths. Bedek HaBayit explains that 'the wall of his cistern' does not exclude other walls; it merely adjusts the mishna's language, which began with a cistern. (End of supplement.)
