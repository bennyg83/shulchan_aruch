Some authorities say one of the two protections is enough. Rosh writes that the Talmud asks whether the Mishnah requires both a distance and lime, or either one, and the question is unresolved. Therefore, if one already performed one of them, beit din does not compel him to do more. This should be understood only after the fact. Initially he should satisfy both possibilities, just as Rama rules in the earlier unresolved questions concerning preparing a place for a storeroom. Therefore one should not initially dig a cistern beside another's cistern unless one both distances three handbreadths and plasters with lime. Indeed, even if he has already dug the pit at the required distance, he should not initially fill it with water without also plastering it, because Tosafot explains that the principal concern in the cistern case is dampness and water. Once water has already been placed there after a three handbreadth distance but without lime, however, the court does not compel additional protection because of the unresolved doubt.
