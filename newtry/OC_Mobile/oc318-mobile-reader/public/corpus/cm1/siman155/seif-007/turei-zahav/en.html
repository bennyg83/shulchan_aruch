(Seif 6: If the stones were upon exposed bedrock). The wording implies that the entire wall from bottom to top is one rock. In the Tur, however, the text reads, “If the stone wall was built upon rock.” It appears that the Tur's reading in Rambam was, “If the stones were upon exposed rock.” This is also compelled by the language, for how can the plural “stones” refer to one rock? This is the correct reading. It also appears that for the other required distances no separation is necessary when the wall is built upon bedrock. This resolves a difficulty in the Tur's ninth clause. Rambam's rule concerning a stone wall there requires three handbreadths only for an excavation, and the Tur brings proof from the baraita. The main proof concerns the Tur's statement that one handbreadth suffices. Why did the Tur insert between these points the rule that if it was built upon rock no distance at all is required? Further, why does he mention plaster at the end regarding an excavation but not at the beginning? The intent is that the baraita's statement, “for a stone wall, one handbreadth,” concerns mere placement without excavation. If it referred to an excavation, why would it differ from a water channel, which requires three handbreadths and plaster, with no distinction between brick and stone? This accords with the Tur's conclusion nearby in the name of Rabbeinu Yonah. Nor can one answer that the baraita concerns a wall built upon rock, because then no distance at all would be required. It must therefore concern a case without an excavation. If it concerned an excavation, it would be exactly the first clause, which according to Rambam requires both three handbreadths and plaster, as explained nearby.
