And so it appears to me to rule. See Baer Heitev, who notes that the Shach disagrees. Responsa Rabbi Akiva Eiger, end of siman 151, whose words I cited in part above, siman 153, seif 2, seif katan 4, indicates that his practical view follows the Rama. He distinguishes this from Shulchan Aruch above, seif 3, concerning the unresolved question of sweeping and sprinkling, where initially the owner of the storehouse can prevent the other from acting because out of doubt he may not damage his fellow. There, with a cattle shed, the harmful force is certainly direct, since the damage renews itself each day, and the act is certainly damaging; the doubt is only whether the Sages exempted one who established it first. One may not cause such certain damage on the basis of a doubt. Here, by contrast, the authorities dispute whether the act is considered damage at all. Since he is doing everything within his own property, he may do so initially. See there.
