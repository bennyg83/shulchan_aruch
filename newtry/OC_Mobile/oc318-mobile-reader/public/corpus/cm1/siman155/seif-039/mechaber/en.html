If a person established in his premises an occupation involving blood, carcasses, or similar matter, thereby attracting ravens and other birds that come because of the blood and eat there, and they distress his neighbor with their noise and chirping, or with blood carried on their feet when they alight on trees and ruin the fruit, then, if the neighbor is especially sensitive or ill and the chirping harms him, or if his fruit is damaged by the blood, the operator must cease that occupation or distance it sufficiently to prevent the harm. This injury resembles the odor of a privy and similar harms, concerning which no established right can be acquired. The same applies to any severe harm that a person cannot tolerate.
