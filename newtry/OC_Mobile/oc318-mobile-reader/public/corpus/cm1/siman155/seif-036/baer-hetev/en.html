This applies specifically where he says, 'You waived your right after you saw what I had done, remained silent, and did not object.' But if he told him beforehand, 'I consent to your opening a window into my courtyard,' this is not a waiver until he actually opens it. Mabit, part 1, siman 96. The compiler comments that there the Mabit writes that if one tells his fellow, 'When I later have a debt owed by you, I hereby waive it from now,' then even if he subsequently lends him money without qualification, the waiver is ineffective. One must explain why advance waiver of property damage is effective in a comparable form. It appears to me that waiver of damage is different: by telling him 'open,' he has granted permission to open, and if he then opens, the opening was made pursuant to that permission. But where one says, 'When I later have a debt against you,' he presently has no lien or claim to waive, and the future waiver is merely words and ineffective. Accordingly, if one says, 'When I later acquire land adjoining yours, I give you permission to open a window into my courtyard,' and he subsequently acquires the land and the other opens the window, there is no waiver, just as with money.
