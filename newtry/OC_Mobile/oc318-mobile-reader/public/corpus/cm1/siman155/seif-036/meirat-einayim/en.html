It follows the Rosh's approach, ruling like Rabbeinu Tam that all harmful uses may initially be placed next to the boundary when no susceptible object is yet present, and that wherever the neighbor had no right to protest at the outset, no prescriptive right arises from his silence. This is as the Mechaber writes in siman 154:7. The Tur writes the same here, unlike Rabbi Yonah, who disagrees. One might distinguish that Rabbi Yonah speaks where the harmful use continued for three years before the susceptible object appeared and the possessor claims that he purchased the right, whereas Rama here discusses only a presumption arising from observation and silence. In that form perhaps everyone would agree that no presumption arises. The Tur's language, however, does not appear to support this, because after citing the Rosh he writes that the Rambam said the same, while in chapter 11 of Hilchot Shekhenim the Rambam states only the rule copied here by the Mechaber, which applies even to a three year presumption. Examine this carefully.
