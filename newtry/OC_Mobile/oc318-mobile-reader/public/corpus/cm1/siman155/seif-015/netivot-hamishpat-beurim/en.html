The courtyard was built. Although the general rule is that one who initially placed a use permissibly need not later distance it when a vulnerable use is introduced nearby, Hagahot Ashri explains that here he must distance because he can remove the damage easily. Beit Yosef gives another reason: with smoke and a privy, which are harms for which no chazakah can be established, even one who began permissibly must later distance himself, and this case resembles smoke and a privy. It further appears that here, after the courtyard is built, he is actively causing the damage with his own acts. One should not ask against the Rosh why, if the neighbor may later require him to distance himself, the neighbor cannot object in advance and say, "I do not wish to have to litigate with you later," as in siman 154, seif 15. The Rosh does not accept that argument, as explained there. Even according to Ramban there, however, it appears that the use may initially be established here. Ramban's concern applies where olive refuse or a similar object is placed permanently and removal may later be delayed. Here the potentially harmful activity is renewed each day through fresh pounding. No existing object needs to be removed later; the user simply must refrain from beginning the activity once the law changes. His legal position at that later time will therefore be exactly what it is then, and there is no reason to prevent his current permissible use.
