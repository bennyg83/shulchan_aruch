Tur writes that Rambam, following Rif, requires distancing soaking water and leeks, mustard from vegetables, onions, and bees by three handbreadths or a little more, enough that the damage is no longer direct. Beit Yosef understood Rif's mention of soaking water as merely one example and assumed the same law for leeks and mustard. Sema distinguishes among them, and his distinction is supported by Ramban. Ramban writes that Rif's statement requiring soaking water to be distanced enough that it is no longer direct force means approximately three handbreadths. Even though the water itself first stops and then travels, moisture from the soaking pit can reach the neighboring vegetables within that distance just as moisture reaches a wall. Leeks and onions, however, require no such distancing according to Rif, because their damage is not direct in the same manner. Ramban therefore rejects Rambam's broader equation of all these cases. Sema's distinction is thus preferable to Beit Yosef's assumption that they are all alike.
