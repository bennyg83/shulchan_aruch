A permanent threshing floor. See the Perishah and Derishah, where I proved that according to the Rosh, Tur, and Mechaber, the word 'permanent' excludes a temporary threshing floor. They did not require distancing for a harm that occurs only occasionally and temporarily. This is unlike Maggid Mishneh's explanation of Rambam; see there, where I discussed the matter at length.
He removes it, and the townspeople then pay, etc. Meaning, he first removes it and only afterward is paid. The reverse order is not used, because otherwise the threshing floor might remain for many days. Where many people share responsibility, no one necessarily tends to the matter, like the pot of partners that is neither hot nor cold, while the owner of the threshing floor prefers that it remain. Therefore he must first remove it and afterward pursue the townspeople for its value. In the Gemara and Tur this law is stated regarding distancing a tree from a city. The Mechaber omitted that case because it does not apply outside Eretz Yisrael, as the Tur writes in seif 27.
From this some derive, etc. Just as Chazal were concerned enough to require cutting the tree and distancing the threshing floor even though each preceded the city, and not only required removal but required it before payment, so as not to prolong public damage, the same applies to individuals living in villages whose residence causes public danger. They must first remove themselves to eliminate the danger to the public, and only afterward litigate with the community. This is explicit in Hagahot Mordechai and cited in Darkei Moshe.
And afterward litigate with the community, etc. It is explicit there that even if the villagers suffered loss, the merits are afterward adjudicated. If they too had been in danger, the public need not compensate them merely because they said, in effect, 'Let me die with the Philistines.' They cannot impose that choice upon the many. See there and Darkei Moshe.
Likewise, one should not make a permanent threshing floor on his own property, etc., so that the chaff not harm plantings, etc. The Tur presents this in two clauses. After writing that a permanent threshing floor must be distanced from the city, so that the chaff not harm the townspeople, he adds that one should likewise not make it on his own property unless he has fifty amot in every direction, and that the same distance is required from a neighbor's plantings and plowed field. The Mechaber copied Rambam's wording. They apparently understand 'on his own' to mean in his own field where others have plantings and plowed fields around it. The Tur understands it as within one's own property in the city because of harm to the residents, and therefore does not restate the reason.
From his fellow's plantings. The chaff enters the blossoms of the fruit and causes rot. From a plowed field, because applying more fertilizer than needed burns and damages the soil. See the Perishah.
