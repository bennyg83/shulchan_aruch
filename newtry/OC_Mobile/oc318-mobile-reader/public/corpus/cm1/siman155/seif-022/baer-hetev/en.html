Permanent. This excludes a temporary installation. The Sages did not require distancing for something occasional, contrary to what the Maggid Mishneh writes in explaining the Rambam. See there. Sema.
He removes it. Meaning, he first removes it and only afterward is he paid, not the reverse. Otherwise the threshing floor might remain for many days because many people share responsibility for paying, as in the expression concerning a pot belonging to partners. The owner of the threshing floor would be content for it to remain. Therefore they required him first to remove it and only afterward to pursue the townspeople for its value. In the Gemara and the Tur this law is stated regarding distancing a tree from a city. The Mechaber omitted it because it is not applicable outside Eretz Yisrael, as the Tur writes. Thus far the Sema. See what the Shach writes on this and his emendation of the Mishnah in Arachin. See there.
And to adjudicate. It is explicit in the Hagahot Mordechai there that even though they suffered damage, the matter must still be judged between them. If the villagers were also in danger, the larger public need not pay them anything, even if the villagers said, "Let me die with the Philistines." They have no authority to impose such a loss on others. See there. The Darkei Moshe cites it. Sema.
