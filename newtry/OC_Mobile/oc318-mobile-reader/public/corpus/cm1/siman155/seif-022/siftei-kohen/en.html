He removes it, and the townspeople pay, etc. The Sema writes in seif katan 48 that in the Gemara and the Tur this law is stated regarding distancing a tree from a city, and that the Mechaber omitted that law because it does not apply outside Eretz Yisrael, as the Tur writes. It is true that the Tur, the Maggid Mishneh, the Nimukei Yosef, and Ramban in his novellae all write that the Mishna applies specifically in Eretz Yisrael when it was settled. Yet this is difficult, for if so, how does the Gemara answer that the Mishna follows Rabbi Eliezer? Rabbi Eliezer explicitly states at the end of Arakhin that one may convert a field into an open area, but not an open area into a field, because of the settlement of Eretz Yisrael. Rather, the Mishna must concern the Diaspora. The Sages at the end of Arakhin hold that one may never convert an open area into a field, whereas Rabbi Eliezer holds that this is prohibited only in Eretz Yisrael, but permitted in the Diaspora. Examine this carefully. It is strained to say that the text of the Mishna in Arakhin is corrupt and should read: Rabbi Eliezer says that one may convert a field into an open area and an open area into a field, for it is exceedingly difficult to emend all editions of the Mishnayot and the entire Talmud. This requires study. I later saw that the Yalkut on Parashat Behar cites two versions of the Mishna at the end of Arakhin. The first reads: Rabbi Eliezer says that one may convert a field into an open area and an open area into a field. Another version reads: Rabbi Eliezer says that one may convert a field into an open area but not an open area into a field. There is no doubt that the first version is correct, as is demonstrated by the Gemara in Bava Batra, chapter Lo Yachpor, and as is stated explicitly in the Tosefta at the end of Arakhin. All the rulings of the authorities then follow straightforwardly. The text is mistaken in all the editions of the Talmud and Mishnayot before us. The author of the Yalkut, who wrote that there is another version, also had the readings found in our editions, but the Gemara at the end of Lo Yachpor escaped his notice. It is also surprising that the other commentators on the Mishna did not take care to correct the text throughout the Talmud and Mishnayot. This is clear.
