Who exempts him from payment. This exemption concerns olive refuse and similar cases, where the damage is only indirect direct causation. But in the case of pounding grain in seif 15 and a mill in seif 7, where the harmful force itself proceeds directly from the defendant's activity, everyone agrees that he is liable. Even the authority who generally imposes payment nevertheless agrees that in the dovecote case of seif 16 he is exempt, because the animals themselves contribute to the damage. This resembles seif 37, where wind contributes to the harm. See the Tur. Likewise, preventing the stabilizing pressure against a wall in seif 12 is only indirect causation and creates no payment liability. Once the prescribed distance was kept, liability for resulting damage applies only in the case of fire. Although the Tur cites Baal HaIttur as holding that one may remain liable even after distancing, the Mechaber did not rule like him on that point.
