[Ibid., gloss: And it is proper to rule accordingly]. It appears to me that Rama's intention is as follows. He cited this dispute many times without deciding it, and here he finally rules. The Sema labored to distinguish this ruling from Rama's decision in siman 153, seif 16. I am astonished at his holy words. It appears that Rama waited until the final citation of this dispute in order to rule that the burden of proof rests upon the claimant and that three years are not required in any of these cases. Consider the Sema's own position above, that for required distances three years are unnecessary, such as olive refuse from seif 4 onward. Why, then, would Rama state his ruling only here and not regarding olive refuse, where even according to the Sema three years are unnecessary? Necessarily, he waited until the end. We should therefore apply this statement without distinctions: in all the cases this is Rama's intention. This appears plainly correct, without any doubt or contrary possibility. Incidentally, we may explain the Tur's wording in siman 52: “But according to my father the Rosh, who requires three years for every matter, so long as he does not establish clearly, and so forth.” Its intention is difficult. If it teaches that the three years must be established by witnesses, that is obvious; and why state it here more than elsewhere? The word “but” is also inappropriate. If he means that when there is uncertainty between the one causing damage and the injured party whether a protest was made, the possessor must prove that none was made, unlike the Rambam, that is incorrect, for in every case the challenger must prove that he protested. It appears that the Tur means this: even according to the Rosh, the injured party cannot remove him immediately where he assisted him, as stated nearby in seif 35; and in the third category, where he saw and remained silent, the Rosh also concedes that he must tolerate him for some period. The Tur therefore writes that although according to the Rambam the waiver permits the use permanently, according to the Rosh it is effective only for a fixed period. Consequently, if uncertainty later arises from his having seen and remained silent, each side giving a different account, the injury must be removed immediately in accordance with the injured party's claim, for he is considered the party in possession.
