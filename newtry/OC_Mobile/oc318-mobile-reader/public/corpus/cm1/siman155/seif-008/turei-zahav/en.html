(Seif 12: Reuven had, and so forth). The case is as follows. Reuven already had a wall adjoining Shimon's wall in the form of the Greek letter gamma. He now wishes to construct another wall opposite Shimon's wall, so that the breadth of the new wall will face the breadth of Shimon's wall from the beginning of Reuven's old wall. Reuven must distance the new wall from opposite the breadth of Shimon's wall because of the need for foot traffic that strengthens the ground. Had Reuven not already possessed the old wall shaped like a gamma, he would not now need to distance the new wall, because there would be a passage open through between them. Even if narrow, people would walk between the walls. Now, however, there is no passage at all. Rambam's reading of the Talmudic discussion may be explained accordingly. The Mishnah says: one whose wall was adjacent to his neighbor's wall may not place another wall beside it unless he distances it four cubits. The Gemara asks how the first wall could have been placed, and answers that the Mishnah means: one who comes to place a wall may not do so. Rava objects that the wording “one whose wall was adjacent” implies that it already existed. Rambam understands that the first wall was permitted because a passage remained open, while the later wall closes the passage entirely. The Mishnah therefore speaks precisely of an existing first wall and a second wall now being added.
