Where one protection is enough, there must nevertheless be a real wall of the ordinary minimum width between the two cisterns. Plastering with lime is meaningful only when there is a wall to plaster, whether the foundation wall of the building or the wall of the cistern. A cistern wall cannot reasonably be thinner than three handbreadths. Therefore, if both owners dig at the same time and each wishes to rely upon plastering with lime, each must leave one and one half handbreadths so that together there is a complete three handbreadth wall. Plastering a mere sliver of earth cannot suffice. This helps answer Rosh's question at the beginning of Perek Lo Yachpor. The Mishnah says one may not dig a cistern beside his neighbor's cistern unless he distances three handbreadths and plasters with lime. According to the initial assumption that the distance is measured from the hollow of the cistern, Abaye's view that the first owner may dig at the boundary seems difficult, because the second should then need to distance a full six handbreadths from the first hollow. On the understanding developed here, the Mishnah can be teaching two cumulative requirements, so that each side's three handbreadths together form the required six handbreadth separation. The Talmud asks whether the phrase means "and plaster with lime" or "or plaster with lime." Some texts infer from Rabbi Yehudah's statement about crumbly rock that both are required. Tosafot questions that inference because Rabbi Yehudah's own statement could itself be read as requiring one or the other. According to what I have written, the inference is stronger. Rabbi Yehudah says each one distances three handbreadths and plasters with lime. Since the law applies to both parties, if lime alone sufficed without any minimum distance, the baraita should have specified how much space remains for the wall. The absence of a separate lime measurement implies that the three handbreadths are part of the requirement. The Gemara answers that crumbly rock is different and requires both protections. The same reasoning works under Abaye when both parties come to dig simultaneously. If lime alone were sufficient, there still would need to be a minimum combined thickness sufficient to form a wall. Since the source gives no such separate measurement, the natural reading is that both distancing and plastering are required in that case.
