Or if the shop owner made an intervening space, etc. This is the Rambam's language. The Maggid Mishneh explains that the Rambam understood the Gemara's question, "If he built an upper story over his house, what is the law?" in this manner, and so did Rabbeinu Chananel and the Rambam's teachers. Rashi, however, explains the question regarding priority of the storage room: people ordinarily used upper stories as storerooms, and this person built such an upper story before the shop existed but had not yet stored produce there. The question is whether the construction alone gives him priority. Some later authorities agree with this explanation, and the Tur likewise follows Rashi. According to Rashi and the Tur, this question resembles the other cases and all concern the owner of the storeroom, who attempted to establish priority by building the upper story, adding windows, or storing apples there. According to the Rambam, however, the other cases concern the storeroom owner, while this question concerns the shop owner, who built an intervening story between his shop and the storeroom after the upper owner had already established his storeroom and would otherwise have required the shop to distance its harmful activity. He built the story so that the heat would not pass directly from the shop to the storeroom, and the question is whether this barrier suffices. See below, siman 214 at the end of seif 3, where the Mechaber, following the Rambam, writes of acquiring the intervening space between the plastered ceiling and the floor above. It is possible that there is no practical disagreement between these explanations.
