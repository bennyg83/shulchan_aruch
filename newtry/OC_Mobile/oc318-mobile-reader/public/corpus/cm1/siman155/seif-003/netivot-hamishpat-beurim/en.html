The owner of the upper story revealed his intention. A question arises whether the Gemara requires the same kind of priority regarding a cattle stall. Suppose the owner of the lower room revealed his intention to use it as a cattle stall, for example by installing feeding troughs or making another alteration that clearly shows it is being prepared for cattle, and only afterward the other owner placed wine in his storeroom, before any animals were actually brought in. Is this considered a case where the cattle stall preceded the storeroom? The wording of the Shulchan Aruch, which states that if the shop was first established as a cattle stall or bakery, implies that even if animals had not yet been brought in and baking had not yet begun, so long as it was publicly established and visibly prepared for that use, it counts as prior establishment. This also appears from the Rosh on page 20, who writes that if the cattle stall preceded the storeroom it is permitted even though the damage is direct, because residential use is different. At first glance this is difficult. Immediately above, the Rosh concludes that the primary text is that of Rabbeinu Chananel, according to which Ravina retracts the earlier answers and even Rava permits placing the potential source of damage next to the boundary when the object that could be damaged is not yet present. The Rosh further writes on page 22 that all the prescribed distances are cases of direct damage. If so, why does the prior cattle stall need the special reason that residential use is different? According to what I wrote above, the matter is clear. With other sources of damage, the actual damaging object must first have been permissibly present before the neighbor's vulnerable object appeared. A cattle stall is different. Even if only the stall itself was prepared and the animals had not yet been brought in, so that the actual source of damage did not precede, the owner need not distance it later because residential use is treated differently. This also resolves Tosafot's question there, s.v. davka nami, why the Mishnah needed to teach this specifically according to Abaye. It was necessary specifically in the case of a cattle stall to teach that mere prior preparation of the stall suffices because residential use is different. With other sources of damage, the actual damaging object must have been present first, and according to Abaye that result would follow without a special Mishnah. Nevertheless, the precise law still requires study, and at present I have not found an explicit source resolving it.
