They divide its fruit. The Tur and Mechaber rule likewise below at the end of siman 167. This follows Shmuel, who says that we do not follow the direction of the branches and award whatever bends to each side to the corresponding landowner. Rather, we follow the roots. Since the roots are divided equally because the tree stands on the boundary, they divide all of the fruit as well. The Tur cites here a responsum of a Gaon ruling like Rav, who follows the branches, so that what extends to one side belongs to that side and what extends to the other belongs there. In the Perishah I wrote that the Tur did not cite this as the controlling law, but only to teach that where the local custom follows it, that custom is valid even though it conflicts with Shmuel, whom we ordinarily follow in monetary law.
