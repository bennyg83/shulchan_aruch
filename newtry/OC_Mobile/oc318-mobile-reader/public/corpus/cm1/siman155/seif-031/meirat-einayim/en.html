From the force of the hippoche that entered the mouths of the sweet honey bees in order to dispel the humidifis from which the charcoal is found to be called harmful, but not because the bees are not harmful to the puzzle itself that it grows in a harsh and other leafy clip that is planted in which the P.A. is not eaten from them
And leeks from onions, etc. This is the Rambam's view. The Rosh, Tur, and Nimukei Yosef disagree and hold that a distance of three handbreadths is required only for a soaking pit used with vegetables, because such a pit is filled with water and is no less harmful than an ordinary pit. It is not required between leeks and onions or between mustard and bees. Their reason is that the injured item is not harmed immediately when the harmful activity is established, but only after time, and therefore the person subject to injury must distance himself. The Mechaber follows his own view, because in the Beit Yosef he questioned the Tur and understood the Raam too to agree with the Rambam. He therefore rules here accordingly. Rama also appears to follow the Beit Yosef, because he draws no distinction in Darkei Moshe or in his gloss here. In my view, however, the distinction is compelled, as I wrote in the Derishah and Perishah.
