This is not comparable to the Tur's ruling at the end of siman 154, which the Mechaber likewise states there in seif 28, that if one sold or gave the courtyard but retained the house for himself, the person who acquired the courtyard may build in front of the windows even though the original owner had opened the windows permissibly. One cannot distinguish simply by saying that there the window owner causes continuing visual intrusion, because I explained there and in siman 153 that the law applies even without visual damage. Rather, there the courtyard owner who builds a wall does not directly damage the window owner through the act of construction; the harm is only indirect. Here, however, with every spadeful that the second person digs within the final six handbreadths, he directly damages both his own pit and his neighbor's. Since the law requires six handbreadths between the pits, and the first pit was dug permissibly, strict law requires the second person to provide the entire six handbreadths of separation. So writes the Rosh. See what I wrote in the Perishah at the end of siman 154.
