This is unlike the ruling in siman 154, seif 28, where if one sold or gave away the courtyard but retained the house for himself, the person who acquired the courtyard may build in front of the windows even though the first owner opened them permissibly. One cannot answer that there the window owner continuously harms him through visual intrusion, because I wrote there and in siman 153 that the case applies even where there is no visual intrusion. Rather, there the courtyard owner who builds a wall does not directly damage the window owner with his construction; the harm results only indirectly. Here, however, with each blow of the spade by which the second person digs within the final six handbreadths, he directly causes damage to his fellow. Since six handbreadths must separate the two, strict law dictates that because the first person already dug his pit permissibly, the second must distance the entire six handbreadths. So writes the Rosh there. Sema.
