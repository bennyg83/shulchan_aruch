And likewise it is permitted to do, etc. Responsa Rabbi Akiva Eiger, siman 151, surveys the authorities. Rama's wording might imply that where one placed a potentially harmful use on his own land before the neighboring vulnerable structure existed, he never has to remove it, just as in the Mechaber's rule concerning prior excavation of a pit. Rabbi Akiva Eiger explains that this is not necessarily the intent of Rosh and Tur, who may only permit placement while there is presently nothing to damage, while requiring removal once the neighbor later builds. Nevertheless, his final ruling is that even after the neighbor builds, the first owner may assert kim li in accordance with Tosafot, Baal HaMaor, Maggid Mishneh, and Nimukei Yosef that prior placement need never be removed. He considers whether the neighbor may therefore object before the placement occurs, lest the first owner later become the possessor and refuse to remove it. He initially compares this to Rashba's rule that where the existence of injury is certain but the right to maintain it is doubtful, one may prevent the new act. He ultimately distinguishes the cases. Here the very classification as legally actionable damage is disputed, and according to several authorities it is not one's direct force and not legally treated as damage at all. In such a fundamental doubt, the owner acting within his own property may rely on the favorable authorities. This is analogous to Mahari Ben Lev concerning raising an established drain, and to Terumat HaDeshen concerning intermittent smoke. It also differs from the case of sweeping and sprinkling, where the harmful force is certain and the only doubt is whether the Sages imposed a duty to distance it. Rabbi Akiva Eiger therefore concludes that one may initially place olive refuse and similar uses at the boundary even intending never to remove them.
