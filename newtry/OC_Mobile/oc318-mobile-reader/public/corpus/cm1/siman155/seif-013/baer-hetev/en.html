This also appears from the Darkei Moshe and is found in the Mordechai, but it is clearly a scribal error and should read sixty years, for the source is the second chapter of Ketubot, where sixty years is stated. (The Taz writes likewise.) As for the Tosafot Yom Tov's question on the Mordechai in the chapter Lo Yachpor, it seems to me that there is no difficulty. Since Ketubot states there, "Rav Chisda said: learn from Rabbi Meir," it implies that he rules like Rabbi Meir rather than Rabbi Yehudah. Nevertheless, the ruling written by the Rama requires study, because the Rambam and Rav Ovadiah of Bartenura rule in chapter 17 of Ohalot like Rabbi Yehudah, and the same should apply here. According to them one must explain Rav Chisda as follows: according to Rabbi Yehudah it is obvious, since the matter depends on memory, that when they do not remember they cannot testify. According to Rabbi Meir, however, since he does not make it depend on memory, one might have thought that only with respect to tumah does the matter depend on sixty years, whereas testimony depends on memory. The Gemara therefore rejects this and teaches that with respect to testimony Rabbi Meir also agrees. In any event, the Rama's ruling requires study. The Tosafot Yom Tov also appears to hold that something is considered old here whenever no person remembers it. See there. Shach.
