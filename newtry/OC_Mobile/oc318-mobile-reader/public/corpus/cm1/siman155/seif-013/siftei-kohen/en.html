For up to fifty years the city is considered new. The Darkei Moshe writes likewise, citing the Mordechai. Plainly, however, the Mordechai contains a scribal error and should read sixty years, because it derives the rule from the second chapter of Ketubot, where the period stated is sixty years. The Tosafot Yom Tov’s objection to the Mordechai is not compelling. Since the Gemara says that Rav Chisda inferred the rule from Rabbi Meir, it implies that Rav Chisda follows Rabbi Meir rather than Rabbi Yehudah. Nevertheless, the Rama’s ruling requires study, because the Rambam and Bartenura rule in chapter 17 of Ohalot like Rabbi Yehudah. According to them, Rav Chisda must mean that for Rabbi Yehudah the law is obvious, since the matter depends upon remembrance and witnesses cannot testify when no one remembers. According to Rabbi Meir, one might have thought that sixty years applies only to ritual impurity, while testimony depends upon actual remembrance. The Gemara rejects this by teaching that even Rabbi Meir agrees concerning testimony. The Rama’s ruling therefore remains difficult. The Tosafot Yom Tov also appears to understand that a city is “old” here whenever no person remembers its establishment.
