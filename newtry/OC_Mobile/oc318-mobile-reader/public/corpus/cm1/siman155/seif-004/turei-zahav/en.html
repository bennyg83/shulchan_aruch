This is stated explicitly in the Mishnah in the chapter Lo Yachpor, concerning olive refuse and manure: he must either distance them three handbreadths or plaster with lime. Tosafot write that even if the text reads “and plaster with lime,” its meaning is “or plaster,” because their harmful effect is not so severe. The Gemara's question whether both three handbreadths and plaster are required concerns the opening clause of the Mishnah, namely distancing from a cistern, cited in seif 18. Beit Yosef's statement that the question concerns olive refuse is exceedingly difficult, and he writes the same in the name of Rashi nearby. I am astonished that such words issued from his holy mouth, for Rashi never intended to explain the question except regarding the first clause, the cistern. Rambam also proves this, for regarding a cistern he writes “and plaster with lime,” as the Tur cites in seif 18, because the Gemara responds, “It is obvious, for the Mishnah says ‘and plaster with lime,’” as Beit Yosef cites from Maggid Mishneh. Regarding olive refuse, however, Rambam writes here “or plaster with lime,” and Beit Yosef himself says this in Rashi's name.
