All these distancing requirements. See Sema, seif katan 25, who says that seeds and plowing are harmful because they undermine the foundation rather than because they damage the wall surface. According to him this applies even to a stone wall. He then expresses surprise that he did not find such a statement in Rambam. All of this is difficult. Tur explicitly writes that this appears to be Rashi's view, and Rashi says clearly in the Mishnah concerning seeds that the case involves a brick wall and that the reason is damage to the wall. From that Tur properly infers that Rambam disagrees and applies the rule even to a stone wall. Rambam explains the Mishnah's rule concerning urine, which is grouped there with seeds, as wastewater collected in a pit. That can only concern a stone wall, because with a brick wall even ordinary urination requires distancing of three handbreadths, as Sema himself writes in seif katan 20. Thus Rambam's broader application is indeed evident.
