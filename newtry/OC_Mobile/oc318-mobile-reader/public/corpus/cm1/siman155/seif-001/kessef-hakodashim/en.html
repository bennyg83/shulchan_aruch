(See Sema, seif katan 15, and Taz, s.v. according to the circumstances.) The Taz in siman 155 can be understood as answering the Sema's difficulty by distinguishing rainwater from water directly discharged by the upstairs resident. The expression "his arrows" does not refer to ordinary rain penetrating from above. Rather, it refers to the case discussed in siman 164, where liquids poured or used by the upstairs resident pass downward through the floor or fill and are treated as direct harmful force. The Sema's question apparently arose because siman 164 places responsibility for the floor fill and roof related needs upon the upstairs owner. As to the roof itself, however, there is no difficulty. In countries where the ordinary construction consists of a sloped roof above the ceiling and fill, everything from the ceiling upward, including the roof, belongs to the upstairs unit. Since the roof provides enhanced protection from rain and thereby benefits the lower resident, the upstairs owner remains responsible for it as though the parties expressly stipulated that responsibility, because that is the ordinary form of construction. There is no need to explain that duty through the doctrine of direct harmful force. The Taz may therefore have written briefly. In siman 155, where buildings are ordinarily designed so that rain is absorbed by the fill, as in places that rely on such fill rather than a sloped roof, an accidental penetration of rainwater through the fill does not make the upstairs owner liable because it is not "his arrows." In siman 164, by contrast, the duty to repair the fill concerns water coming from ordinary use in the upstairs dwelling. Where that water is not ordinarily absorbed and instead passes downward, it can be treated as direct harmful force. The matter requires further study.
