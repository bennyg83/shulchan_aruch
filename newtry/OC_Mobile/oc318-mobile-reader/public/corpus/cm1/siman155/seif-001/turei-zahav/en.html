(Seif 1: And for a stove, one handbreadth, and so forth). The Mishnah does not state the required vertical height. The Tur writes in the name of Ramah that it is eight handbreadths, one third of four cubits, corresponding to the ratio used for the thickness of the ceiling layer. This also clarifies the Tur's presentation. Rashbam and Rivach, whom the Tur cites, likewise address Ramah's question. Rashbam understands that the Mishnah's stove is open at the side. Its flame does not rise directly upward, and the only concern is heat passing through the ceiling. There is therefore no distinction between a stove above and one below, and even above, a ceiling layer one handbreadth thick is sufficient. One cannot object that Rashbam requires three handbreadths, for he states a broader rule: even in a baker's oven, where three handbreadths are required, three handbreadths above are sufficient. He therefore writes that three suffice, not that three are always required. There are plainly cases in which one handbreadth suffices. The Tur therefore concludes that according to Rashbam our ovens, being open at the side, resemble the Talmudic stove rather than the Talmudic oven, and at least three handbreadths suffice. The Rosh disagrees because their large size prevents comparison to a stove. Rivach also explains why no height was stated for a stove: the Talmudic oven was open at the side and had enclosing walls, all the more so a stove, and the Mishnah relied upon the requirement of four cubits. Our ovens, which are open at the side, are learned from this. On this explanation there is no need to distinguish our ovens because of their size, as the Rosh does, and all is resolved.
