Beneath it. Regarding the required height above a stove, the Tur records a dispute, while the Mechaber states the law without qualification. It therefore appears that in his view all the cases listed require the same vertical distance of four cubits. Because the concern is the flame rising upward, a substantial distance was required, while beyond four cubits there is no concern in any of these cases. Sema. See the Taz, who explains the Tur's words differently.
He pays. The Shach cites the Rif, who asks in the chapter HaKones, as quoted by the Tur and Mechaber in siman 418, seif 2: if one maintained the prescribed distance for fire damage he is exempt, so why is the law different here regarding an oven? He answers that there the person lights the fire only as an occasional necessity, gave it the proper space, and the fire nevertheless spread beyond its expected measure and caused damage. He is therefore considered compelled by circumstances, for what else could he have done, and the event is deemed an act of Heaven, so he is exempt. Here, however, an oven is lit regularly; he should have examined whether it was causing damage to his fellow. Since he failed to do so, he is negligent and liable. See Yam Shel Shlomo there, siman 28.
