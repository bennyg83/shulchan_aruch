He pays for the damage it caused. See Shach, seif katan 2, who asks in the name of the Rif from siman 418 and answers that there the person lights the fire for a temporary need, so the Sages established a broader distance. In my view his intent is as follows. In the fire case, the Sages fixed a distance at which damage should not occur even if the person does not supervise the fire continuously. Since the activity is only temporary, they could require a relatively broad safety margin. If damage then occurs because of an unusual wind, it is an unavoidable accident and he is exempt, because the Sages did not impose guarding against such an uncommon event after the required distance was kept. Here, by contrast, the activity is constant. The Sages did not require such a broad separation, because doing so would make ordinary residence in a house impossible. They required only the distance at which damage can normally be prevented through proper supervision. Since he acts within his own property and can prevent harm by guarding carefully at that distance, no greater distance is required. But if he fails to guard and damage results, he is liable.
