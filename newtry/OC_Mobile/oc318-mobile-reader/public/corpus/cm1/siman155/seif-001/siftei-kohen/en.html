If it caused damage, he must pay for the damage, etc. See below, siman 418, seif 2. The Rif in the chapter HaKones and the Yam Shel Shlomo there require analysis, though the matter can be reconciled. The point is that siman 418 rules that one who kept the required distance concerning fire damage is exempt. The Rif addresses the apparent contradiction with an oven, where the Sages prescribed a distance yet the owner remains liable if damage occurs. He explains that one who kindles a fire for an occasional need and properly enlarges the safety distance is considered compelled when the fire travels beyond that measure, for there was nothing further he could reasonably do. The event is treated as an act of Heaven and he is exempt. An oven, however, is used continually. Its owner must repeatedly examine whether it threatens the neighbor. Failure to do so is negligence, and he is liable. This is confirmed by the fact that the Sages dispute Rabbi Shimon’s prescribed exemption distance for an oven, while they themselves establish a distance that exempts in the case of an ordinary fire. See Yam Shel Shlomo, siman 28.
