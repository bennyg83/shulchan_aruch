The rule requiring both distancing and plastering with lime beside the wall of a cistern is understood differently by the authorities. Sema writes that Rambam applies the same requirement even beside an ordinary building wall, while Rosh and Tur require only a three handbreadth distance from a building wall and do not also require lime. Shach objects that according to Rosh and Tur, who rule leniently because the Talmudic question remained unresolved, even beside a cistern wall one of the two protections should suffice. According to what I wrote below, the distinction is clear. Since the question was unresolved, initially one should satisfy both possibilities, by distancing three handbreadths and plastering with lime. Only after the fact, if one of the two was done, beit din does not compel more because of the unresolved doubt. By contrast, with an ordinary building wall there is no unresolved question requiring both. At the outset one of the recognized protections is enough. This is Sema's intention when he says that according to Rosh and Tur the additional lime is not required initially for a building wall.
