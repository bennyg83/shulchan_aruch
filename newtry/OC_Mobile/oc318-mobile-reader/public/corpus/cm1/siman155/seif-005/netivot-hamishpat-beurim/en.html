The seeds. Ramban writes in Perek Lo Yachpor that even Rabbi Yose, who permits planting a tree near a neighbor's property in the relevant case, agrees that seeds must be distanced because their roots draw moisture directly from the surrounding soil. This is considered his direct damage. His words are cited by Ketzot HaChoshen.
And in a pit he must plaster with lime. The Rama disagrees with the Mechaber's interpretation. The Mechaber understands the Gemara's statement about poured urine as referring to urine collected in a pit. On that reading, even a pit does not require lime plaster, because the Mishnah lists it together with olive refuse. According to the Mechaber, ordinary poured urine without a pit requires only a distance of one tefach, like ordinary urination. The Rama interprets the Gemara's reference to poured urine as meaning even without a pit. In that case a distance of three tefachim suffices without lime plaster, because this is the case addressed by the Mishnah. But where the urine is collected in a pit, lime plaster is also required. Only occasional urination near a stone wall requires merely one tefach; regular pouring requires three tefachim.
