A privy in the very ground of his fellow. Meaning that both the pit and the seat upon which one sits to relieve himself are located in the neighbor's house, and he made an opening from his own house through which to enter that privy standing in the neighbor's property. The alternative case, beginning with "or," concerns the drainage trench beneath the privy. The excavation begins broadly under his own property and extends into his neighbor's domain. It is unnecessary to explain it as a sloped trench through which the waste rolls into the neighbor's field, like the Persian privies discussed in Orach Chaim siman 83. In the Perishah I proved that the Tur and Mechaber did not intend those privies.
