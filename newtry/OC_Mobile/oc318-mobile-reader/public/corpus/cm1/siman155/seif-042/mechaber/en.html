The rule that smoke and a privy cannot acquire an established right applies only when the person causing the harm operates them within his own domain. If, however, he established and maintained a privy in the actual ground of his neighbor, opening toward the house of the person causing the harm, or he made a privy in his own domain but its drain runs through his neighbor's property, then, because he is exercising possession in the actual land, he can acquire an established right. If he claims that the neighbor sold that right to him, he is believed upon taking an oath. Nevertheless, he must cover the privy or drain so that the odor is eliminated, even if he had maintained it uncovered. If he does not eliminate the odor, there is no established right at all. Even if he says, 'I will remove the privy and use the place for another purpose,' he is not entitled to do so, because his possession was established only for a privy; since he acquired no right to the land itself, he acquired no right to another use. Some say that once he has acquired an established right in the land itself, it belongs to him for any use he wishes, provided no harm reaches his neighbor.
