The rule concerning placing a bakery, dye shop, or cattle stall beneath another person's storeroom is stated by Rambam and Tur. Their wording might imply that the prohibition applies only beneath a storeroom, while beneath another person's dwelling such uses would be permitted. Ramban, however, citing the Jerusalem Talmud, writes that they are forbidden beneath a dwelling as well. The Jerusalem Talmud asks about a use opposite another's residence, and Rabbi Acha answers that a residence is all the more protected. Thus the Mishnah's reference to a storeroom states a novelty, and a dwelling is certainly included. When the Mishnah says that if the cattle stall preceded the storeroom it may remain, this can mean that the upper premises had previously been used only for wood and straw and were later converted into a storeroom for grain and fruit. Although Beit Yosef and the later authorities did not cite Ramban here, the practical law should follow him because his ruling is explicit in the Jerusalem Talmud.
