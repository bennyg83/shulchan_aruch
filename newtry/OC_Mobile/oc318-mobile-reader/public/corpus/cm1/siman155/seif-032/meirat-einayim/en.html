This is damage that comes by itself only later. This is unlike olive refuse and similar substances, which according to all opinions may not be placed close to a neighbor's boundary when the susceptible wall already exists, and according to Rif and those with him may not be placed there even before the wall exists. There, once the neighbor builds his wall, the damage comes from the very refuse that the first person placed there. It is therefore regarded as though he then damaged him directly, since the injury comes from the same substance he deposited. A pit damaged by a tree is different. The pit is not harmed by the tree itself as planted, but by roots that later spread from it on their own. Those roots arise only afterward, and he did not directly perform the damaging act. See the Derishah.
Just as this one digs within his own property, etc. See the Tur here, seif 47, who records the dispute. Rashi and Rif hold that one may not place a damaging use near the boundary even before the neighboring susceptible object exists. Rabbeinu Chananel, Rabbeinu Tam, and the Rosh hold that one may do so whenever the neighboring susceptible object does not yet exist, except for digging a pit next to a field normally used for pits. The Mechaber abbreviated the matter here. Rama already mentions the dispute above in this siman, seif 19. The Mechaber himself also writes above at the end of seif 4, in the language of the Mishnah, that olive refuse and manure must be distanced three tefachim from a neighbor's wall. The Gemara infers from that wording that specifically where the wall exists distancing is required; otherwise one may place it there. See the Derishah.
