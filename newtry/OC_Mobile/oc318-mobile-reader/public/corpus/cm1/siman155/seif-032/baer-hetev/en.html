Why is this different from seeds, which must be distanced even though the damage does not occur until the roots penetrate the soil, and likewise from leeks and mustard? It appears that only in damage caused by one planting to another do we say that the person who planted first acted somewhat improperly by planting so near the boundary, since he should have anticipated that his fellow might also wish to plant. The same applies to a pit dug adjacent to the boundary, and therefore the second party may be required to distance himself. A wall is different, because ordinary practice is to erect it directly at the edge of one's boundary. Taz.
