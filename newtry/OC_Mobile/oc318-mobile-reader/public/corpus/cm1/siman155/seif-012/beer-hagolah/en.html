This too is there, chapter 9, law 9. Maggid Mishneh writes that the Mishnah and Gemara on Bava Batra 22b establish the case as involving two directions, and Rambam follows his teacher's explanation. The Gemara's question, "How did the first one place his wall?" initially assumed that Reuven's wall stood directly opposite Shimon's rather than at a right angle. The Gemara answers that it stood only at a right angle, meaning from the side, and therefore it was permitted to place it there. This is straightforward according to this approach. They further explain that the Mishnah specifically concerns one who purchased from the king, because ordinarily four cubits of land outside the wall are deemed assigned to the purchaser for foot traffic around it. Therefore another person purchasing from the king must distance four cubits, because those four cubits were already allocated to the first purchaser. Rama cites this below in the gloss.
