(Seif 24: and if they purchased, etc.) The reason is that we plead for a purchaser that the seller appeased his neighbors with money. It appears to me that this is said only regarding a matter in which the seller himself could have been believed through a chazakah accompanied by a claim. But in matters for which chazakah is ineffective, such as smoke and a privy, we do not plead that the seller acquired the right by a kinyan and possessed proof of it. Although such a kinyan would be effective even in matters for which there is no chazakah, as stated at the end of this siman, we do not advance such an unusual plea for a purchaser. First, heirs and purchasers are treated alike in this respect in the Gemara, and regarding an heir we say in many places that we do not advance an uncommon claim on his behalf, as in siman 108 concerning an investment. With a wall, where people ordinarily place the wall at the very edge of the boundary, it is certainly uncommon to make a separate kinyan regarding the neighboring rights. If such a kinyan were common, chazakah itself should also be effective. Second, the Gemara challenges the statement, "if they purchased it, it remains in its chazakah," from Rav Nachman's rule that there is no chazakah for damages. If we were to plead for a purchaser that the seller had a formal kinyan, what difficulty would there be from the absence of chazakah? Necessarily, in matters for which chazakah itself is ineffective, we do not advance such a plea. From this it also appears to me that regarding a permanent threshing floor, which causes bodily harm and has no chazakah, as implied by the Tur in seif 28, we likewise do not plead for the purchaser. I am therefore surprised by the Sema, seif katan 56, who writes that we plead for a purchaser even regarding a threshing floor and tannery. What I have written appears clear.
