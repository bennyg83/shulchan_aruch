If one maintained a type of damage for which no established right can be acquired, such as smoke, a privy, or the like, and the person causing the damage claims that the injured party formally transferred to him the right to do so, the burden is upon the person causing the damage to prove the transfer. If he brings no proof, the injured party takes a heset oath that he made no such transfer, and the other must remove the harm. Rama: Above, siman 154, seif 19, it was explained that one who derives title from a non-Jew has the same status as the non-Jew regarding harms that arise automatically. For the harms discussed in this siman, however, which must be distanced because they are regarded as direct injury, the non-Jew's established use does not permit one to cause direct injury. If two neighbors live together and the house of one is breached, so that thieves thereby enter the other's house, and the second says, 'Fence your house or sell it to others, because you are causing me harm,' some say his claim is valid and the first must repair the condition. Others disagree, because it is not considered his direct force. According to the Rosh, if he failed to fence it and damage occurred, he is exempt from payment.
