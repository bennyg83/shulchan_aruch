The land from the king. Beit Yosef cites Rabbeinu Yerucham here: "Regarding an ordinary person we do not say this law, for if so, one who wishes to build a wall adjacent to his neighbor's courtyard, merely to avoid acquiring a servitude over the neighbor's land, would always have to distance four cubits." The meaning appears to be that an ordinary person who owns a house and does not know whether it was acquired from the king does not automatically obtain the four cubits. Otherwise, whenever a person wished to build a wall adjacent to his neighbor's courtyard, even though the law of treading near the wall would not ordinarily require four cubits, he would nevertheless have to distance four cubits because the neighbor could say, "Perhaps later I will build a house and you will then compel me to distance four cubits by claiming that you purchased this right from the king, thereby burdening my land retroactively." Necessarily, therefore, one who asserts such a royal grant must prove it.
