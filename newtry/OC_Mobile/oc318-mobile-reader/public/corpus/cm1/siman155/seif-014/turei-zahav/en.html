[Seif 31, and leeks from the onions]. The Tur writes, “This does not appear so from the Gemara.” Beit Yosef writes that this is no objection, because Rabbi Yose concedes where the damage is caused by one's direct arrows. I am astonished, for what direct arrows are present in mustard, when the damage does not arise until later? It appears to me that this itself is the Tur's objection. With respect to a field, one can say that the pits he digs are his direct arrows, and regarding a cistern all agree because digging immediately loosens the soil. Regarding leeks and mustard, however, the Tur's difficulty remains. The Rambam can be explained as discussing leeks and mustard where one first plows in order to sow. This is therefore also comparable to a cistern, as the Gemara asks regarding seeds: let the obligation follow simply from the plowing. For that reason he required a distance of three handbreadths, or slightly more, in place of the lime required when distancing a cistern. Although regarding an actual cistern the Rambam requires both measures, certainly three handbreadths and lime, as stated in seif 6, here, since it is not a true cistern, he used the uncertain expression “or slightly more.” The Tur, by contrast, discusses seed scattered by hand, without plowing before sowing. This appears correct in my humble opinion.
