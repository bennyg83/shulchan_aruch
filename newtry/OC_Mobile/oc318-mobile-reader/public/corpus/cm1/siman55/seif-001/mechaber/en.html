One Who Paid Part of His Debt and Deposited His Document with a Third Party. Two seifim. If a person paid part of his debt, deposited the document with a third party, and said, 'If I do not pay you by a certain day, give him his document,' then when the day arrives without payment the custodian must not give it to the creditor, because this is an asmachta and does not effect acquisition. If a formal act of acquisition was made before an eminent beit din and he deposited his rights there without acting under duress, it is effective. Thus, if he deposited his document or receipt with the beit din and formally undertook that it be given to the opposing party if he failed to appear on a certain day, and he did not appear, it is delivered. If a river or illness prevented his arrival, it is not delivered. {Rama: Some say physical deposit of the document is unnecessary when the acquisition was made before an eminent beit din, meaning three judges expert in asmachta law. See below, siman 207, seif 15. If a custodian wrongly returns a document to the creditor, he is placed under a ban until he accepts liability for every loss this causes the borrower. If the beit din knows the return was improper, it does not adjudicate upon the document. If it did not know and collected the entire document before learning the truth, the custodian is exempt because his act only indirectly caused damage.}
