Provided that he was not prevented by circumstances, etc. If a river prevented him, the rule depends on the nature of the event. An event that is uncommon, or partly common and partly uncommon, is treated as unavoidable for vows and monetary law. A common event is not, because he should have stipulated for it. The Rosh and Rabbeinu Yerucham explain that a river obstruction means that rainwater or melting snow caused an unusual rise. If the ferry simply ceased operating or a similar common difficulty arose, he should have anticipated it. Maharshal disagreed, but Maharan ibn Sasson, siman 61, argued at length for the view stated here. See also Maharam Alshich, siman 60.
