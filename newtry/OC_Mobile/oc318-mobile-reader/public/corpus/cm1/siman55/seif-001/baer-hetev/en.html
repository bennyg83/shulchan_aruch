Acquired. See the Tur, who cites a responsum of the Raavad concerning one who lent money to another, received a sale deed to the borrower's field as security, and the borrower sold the field to someone else before the due date. See there. Rama cites this at the end of siman 211. Sema.
A river. See my Shach to Yoreh Deah, siman 232, seif katan 20, where I demonstrated that an event that is neither common nor extraordinarily rare, and certainly one that is wholly uncommon, is considered ones for monetary law. A common event is not ones, because the party should have stipulated for it. I cited the Rosh and Rabbeinu Yerucham that "the river prevented him" means that the river rose because of heavy rain or melting snow, an uncommon event. If the ferry service ceased or a similar predictable obstacle arose, that is a common event that he should have anticipated. I have now found Responsa Maharashach, part 2, siman 45, stating the opposite and disagreeing with the Rosh and Rabbeinu Yerucham. I later found Responsa Mahara ibn Sasson, siman 61, which discusses Maharashach at length, rejects his position, and agrees with my view. I was pleased to find this support. See there and Responsa Maharam Alshich, siman 60. Shach.
Who receives. So long as the creditor has not collected on the document, the third party is placed under nidui until he removes the harmful situation, because one who causes indirect damage must remove the cause. Once the creditor has already collected, however, the third party is exempt, as Rama concludes. The same rule appears in siman 175, seif 40, in the gloss. Likewise, if the lender is present and admits that the document came into his possession improperly, the court compels him to return the document or to write a receipt on it. Thus the Sema.
Became known. See below, siman 65, seifim 11 and 16. One must say that here everyone agrees the creditor may not collect because it is certain no debt is owed. There, although the document was impaired by suspicious circumstances, the underlying debt may nevertheless still be genuine. Shach.
Exempt. Responsa Maharam Brisk, siman 85, rules that the exemption applies specifically where the third party returned the document to the lender, which is only indirect causation. If an unpaid document was instead returned to the borrower, that is dina d'garmi and the third party is liable, as demonstrated by a responsum of the Rosh. It resembles burning another person's promissory note. See below, siman 386, seif 2. Shach.
Indirect causation. The Sema writes that this does not contradict the Tur and Mechaber in siman 46, seif 37, where witnesses who admit they signed falsely must pay, nor the similar laws in siman 29, seif 2, and siman 38. One must distinguish between actionable dina d'garmi and more remote grama, as the Tur and Mechaber explain in siman 386. See there.
