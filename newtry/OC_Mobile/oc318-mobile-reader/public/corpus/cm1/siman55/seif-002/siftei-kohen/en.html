He collects nothing with it, etc. The Rama and Sema’s ruling that a deed partially paid cannot collect anything from encumbered property is not convincing. Why should the unpaid balance lose its lien merely because another part was paid? No such decree appears in the Gemara or authorities. Even those who invalidate an interest bearing or antedated deed do so because the instrument was defective from inception, or because the prohibited and permitted amounts are intertwined. A partial payment is a separate later event. The Gemara concerning a borrower who says half was paid while witnesses say all was paid indicates that absent the purchasers’ reliance on those witnesses, the unpaid half could be collected from purchasers. The Rosh’s first explanation in Nedarim, that the entire sum may be collected from unencumbered property but not from purchasers, rests on concern that the lender might collect the paid half as well. His final explanation, however, is that the earlier payment was stipulated to become a gift if the balance was not paid by the appointed time. Without such a condition the lender cannot recollect the paid half, since debts represented by documents are not acquired by delivery alone. Once the paid half is unavailable even from the borrower, there is no reason to fear that it will be collected from purchasers. Moreover, collection from purchasers requires an oath that the debt was not paid, and we do not suspect both wrongful collection and a false oath. Therefore the unpaid balance remains a valid secured debt and may be collected from encumbered property. The paid portion is entirely extinguished and cannot be collected even from unencumbered property. This is the correct ruling.
Only from unencumbered property. The Sema limits this to the unpaid balance. He understood the Rosh to say that the amount already paid could be collected again only by written assignment and delivery. That cannot be correct. Once the lien was discharged to the extent of the payment, an assignment cannot revive it, since the transfer language must convey the instrument and all its existing liens. Nor can the paid amount be restored by mere delivery. The Rosh means that the explanation treating the earlier payment as a gift is preferable because documentary rights ordinarily require writing and delivery, and that mode cannot operate here after the lien has been extinguished. See also siman 207, seif 12.
