He collects only from unencumbered property. Urim VeTumim asks: if a kinyan is required, why should the creditor not collect from encumbered property through that kinyan? The answer follows the rule concerning a guarantor who undertakes after the money was advanced. Although such a guarantor requires a kinyan to create the obligation itself, the kinyan does not thereby create a lien on purchasers. A kinyan creates purchaser liability only when the underlying debt already exists without needing that kinyan. Here the kinyan is needed to create the very obligation, so it does not additionally reach encumbered property. Sema's statement that one cannot collect even from unencumbered property is therefore difficult. The case must be one where the kinyan was expressed conditionally, such as that if he did not repay he would give over this document. A person can obligate himself by creating and transferring a valid debt instrument through sudar kinyan, but here the document had already had its lien extinguished and was legally worthless. A conditional transfer of worthless paper therefore creates no personal obligation.
