Nothing. It appears that this refers only to the unpaid balance. As for the portion already paid, the Rosh writes there that the creditor cannot collect it again unless the debtor transferred it to him by writing and delivery, in accordance with the law governing transfer of documentary rights, as stated at the beginning of siman 66. Even the unpaid portion cannot, according to that approach, be collected from encumbered property, because we are concerned that he may also collect the half already paid, just as with an antedated document. With respect to unencumbered property, however, the Sages did not make one case a decree because of the other. Rama's words, 'he collects nothing,' therefore mean even the unpaid portion from encumbered property, while his continuation, 'except from unencumbered property,' means only the unpaid portion. This requires study, however, because Darkei Moshe writes that he collects the entire amount from unencumbered property, and should have explained that this is only through writing and delivery. The Shach extensively disputes Rama and the Sema here. He concludes as a matter of law that the unpaid half may be collected even from encumbered property, and we do not impose such a decree in this case, whereas the half already paid cannot be collected even from unencumbered property. This is the primary ruling. The Sema's question on Darkei Moshe, that it should have said this applies only through writing and delivery, is no question. According to the Sema's understanding, writing and delivery would also permit collection from encumbered property. Rather, the Rosh certainly did not mean that writing and delivery can revive a lien that was already discharged, for a transfer of a document requires the formula, 'Acquire it and all its liens,' as stated at the beginning of siman 66, while here the lien was already discharged to the extent of the money paid. Furthermore, the document would thereby be antedated. Rather, the Rosh means that the debtor must stipulate that the money already paid shall be treated as a gift, analogous to the acquisition of documentary rights which requires writing and delivery and the appropriate language of acquisition. That mechanism does not apply here. See further siman 207, seif 12. The Taz also writes that the Sema's words here are strained; see what he writes regarding this case.
