Which are not acquired through sudar kinyan. Tosafot in Arakhin writes that anything destined to return to its owner, such as a gift on condition of return, a loaned right, or a rental, is not acquired through barter because it resembles an intangible benefit that is not monetary property for barter. This raises a question from a Hebrew servant. Scripture specially excludes his acquisition through barter. Why is a verse needed if, since the servant necessarily returns at six years or Jubilee, he is already like any temporary right that cannot be acquired by barter? One can answer that the verse itself teaches the general principle that a temporary right is not acquired through barter. Just as Rabbi Yosef derives from the servant verse that money or money value must be fixed, so too the exclusion of barter can reveal that a right destined to return is not within barter acquisition generally. This also explains Rambam's omission of barter from the acquisitions of a Hebrew servant while listing it for a Canaanite slave. Although the Gemara's final explanation of the baraita follows Rav Nachman, who reads the exclusion in another way, a Hebrew servant is independently not acquired by barter because his right is temporary. Ritva adds that a Hebrew servant leaves by the same categories through which he is acquired, which further supports the exclusion. There is, however, an important difficulty. The very source of barter is the transaction of the redeemer and Boaz, where the relevant land interest was itself destined to return at Jubilee. This suggests that a temporary property right can indeed be acquired through barter. Moreover, Rosh appears explicitly to disagree with Tosafot's broad rule. In Bava Metzia he writes that even if a loaned right were not acquired through barter, one could still have transferred the land to the sages for a fixed term by barter. This shows that a temporary gift can be acquired through sudar kinyan. The dispute is therefore real, and the temporary nature of the right does not universally bar barter.
For this reason Rama omitted Tosafot's opinion regarding a temporary gift and mentioned only loan and rental. Even according to those who question barter for loan or rental, a gift for a limited period can still be acquired by barter. There is also evidence that even loan and rental may be acquired through barter. The Gemara concerning one who lends an object "for its benefit" says that the arrangement is effective if a kinyan was made. Rashi understands this as a formal kinyan, apparently sudar kinyan. Likewise, the case of borrowing a watering place and being entitled to dig successively can be made binding through a kinyan even though the right is temporary. One might argue that the Talmudic phrase "they made a kinyan from him" need not mean sudar kinyan and could refer to whatever acquisition is legally effective. Tosafot in Ketubot distinguishes formulations in this way. Yet hazakah is difficult in the watering place case, because possession of one location would not naturally transfer a right to dig later in other locations, and the wording does not suggest a written deed. Therefore these sources strongly support that loan and rental too can be acquired through sudar kinyan. See also siman 341.
