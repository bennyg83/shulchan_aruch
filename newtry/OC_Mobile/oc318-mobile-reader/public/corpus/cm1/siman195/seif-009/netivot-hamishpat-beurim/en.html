And some say they are not acquired by kinyan sudar. Ketzot HaChoshen challenges this from the law that a person may borrow an item "for its benefit" and from the irrigation trough discussed at the end of Bava Metzia, HaShoel, and in Choshen Mishpat 341. In my humble opinion there is a distinction. Borrowing land, where the substance is not diminished and the borrower receives only the intangible benefit of residence or use, does not transfer any monetary substance in the land itself. It resembles a mere right of benefit and therefore cannot be acquired by kinyan sudar. Borrowing movable property, however, physically diminishes the object through use. It is as though the owner conveys to the borrower an interest in the body of the object, permitting him to use and diminish it. This is shown by the Gemara's statement there that he returns it "when it breaks." There can be no greater transfer of an interest in the substance than the right to use the item until it is worn out. Likewise, borrowing land for digging pits, comparable to the irrigation trough case, transfers a right in the substance of the land and can be acquired by kinyan sudar. Ketzot's question from a Hebrew servant is similarly answered, for the servant's body is acquired for service and is itself regarded as monetary substance.
