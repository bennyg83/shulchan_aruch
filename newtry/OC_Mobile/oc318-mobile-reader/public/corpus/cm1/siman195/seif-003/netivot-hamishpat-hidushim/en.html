The Sema cites Mahariv that this law depends on the dispute brought by the Tur and Shulchan Aruch in siman 171, seif 10. One opinion holds that even a gift may be unwanted because "one who hates gifts will live"; according to that view, acquisition would not occur even with a gift. The Rosh and Tur, however, rule there that we do not ordinarily consider this concern. Still, if we knew that the recipient wanted the acquisition earlier and he also accepts it when later informed, perhaps he retracted in the interim and only later agreed again. But if the purchaser himself sent witnesses to the transferor to perform the kinyan, we need not fear an intervening retraction because they act under his direction. See the Beurim. The principal ruling follows the Rama that a kinyan sudar for a gift is effective in the recipient's absence. Even with a sale, a kinyan sudar can sometimes be effective, such as the case in siman 295, seif 23, where the seller transfers the purchased item through another person and tells him, "Acquire it on behalf of the purchaser." The purchaser can then retract but the seller cannot, because the option lies only with the purchaser and therefore the transfer is considered beneficial to him. But if Reuven wishes to give one hundred as a gift to Shimon and says to Levi, "Take this hundred and let your field be acquired by Shimon," so long as the seller did not say, "Acquire on Shimon's behalf," the seller can also retract. With money, we specifically require agency on behalf of the owner of the money. Since the seller did not say, "Acquire," but only used language of an ordinary acquisition, there cannot be a half acquisition. If the purchaser later retracts, the seller would need to return the money to the giver; therefore the seller may retract as well, because an acquisition cannot operate only halfway. The special rule that one who buys clothing for his wife cannot retract on the ground that he transferred it to her rests on a rabbinic acquisition, as the Shitah Mekubetzet writes. Finally, the rule that a gift can be acquired by kinyan sudar in the recipient's absence applies specifically where the sudar belongs to the witnesses.
