He transferred ownership, etc. As stated in Bava Batra 152b, “and we made a kinyan from him,” and in many places in the Gemara, “they made a kinyan from him”; these formulations refer to the witnesses.
Even if he transferred ownership to him, etc. As stated in Bava Metzia chapter 1: the Mishnah permitting one to write, etc., refers to a deed of acquisition. The Rif explains it as a deed effecting acquisition. Likewise in Kesubos, in the chapter HaNosei, and in Gittin, in the chapter HaNizakin, concerning daughters who were not in existence at the time of the kinyan, the defect is specifically that they had not yet come into existence. In Kiddushin chapter 1, according to the standard text: “Rather, if it is for their value, let him transfer ownership to him through meshichah, which is unavailable, etc.”
Therefore they became accustomed, etc. As above, from the formula “and we made a kinyan, etc.”
And that which he acquired, etc. As stated by the rule that one may confer a benefit upon a person in his absence.
But, etc., and both of them, etc. The seller does not rely conclusively upon the transaction, as stated in Bava Batra chapter 5 and Avodah Zarah chapter 5: “If he did not fix the price, etc.” The same applies here. Although afterward he says he is satisfied, the sudar has already returned to its owner, as stated at the end of chapter 5 of Nedarim. Even if we know at the time of the act that he is satisfied, since the buyer could still retract, the seller transferred ownership only conditionally upon the buyer’s desire; this resembles an asmachta, as stated in Bava Metzia chapter 5: every conditional undertaking of this type does not acquire.
