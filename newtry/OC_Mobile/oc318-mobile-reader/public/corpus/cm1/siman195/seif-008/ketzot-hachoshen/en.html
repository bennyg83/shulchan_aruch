After the time of immediate speech. Taz in Even HaEzer siman 38 inferred from the Tur that once a gift has been handed over unconditionally, a condition added afterward is ineffective even if stated within the brief interval ordinarily treated as continuous speech. He brought proof from the case of one who lifts a found object for another and afterward says, "I acquired it first for myself." Rashi explains that the word "first" is almost unnecessary, because once the object has already been handed to the other person the lifter can no longer say that he is acquiring it now. I am surprised by this proof. The authorities cited by the Tur and Shulchan Aruch here state that a person may retract within the brief interval even from ordinary modes of acquisition. Rosh says that the Sages especially extended that retraction period to sudar kinyan, but his language implies that even lifting, meshichah, and similar acquisitions can be retracted within the ordinary interval. Beit Yosef likewise rejects the minority view that meshichah and hazakah cannot be retracted even immediately. The found object case is different. The lifter never owned the object. Once he lifted it solely for the other person and then handed it over, the recipient acquired it directly from ownerless status. A later retraction by the lifter cannot help, because the object was never his to reclaim. That is why the Gemara can say that he must mean he had acquired it for himself first. A seller or donor dealing with his own property is different and may retract within the recognized interval. Thus Taz's proof does not stand.
