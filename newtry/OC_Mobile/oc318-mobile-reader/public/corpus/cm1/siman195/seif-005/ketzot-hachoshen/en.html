On condition that you acquire it for me from now and after thirty days, he acquires. Tosafot asks how deferred sudar kinyanim can work according to Rabbi Yochanan, who understands "from now and after thirty days" as retaining part of the acquisition until the later date. By then the sudar has already returned to its owner. Rabbi Meir therefore suggests that using the expression "on condition" makes the wording a true condition rather than a retained future component. It is therefore preferable to say "on condition." The Shach understands Sema as saying that "on condition" alone is enough and the words "from now" merely publicize that acquisition begins immediately. He then argues that the purchaser is the one saying "on condition" and that "from now" alone should suffice. In my view the practical authorities appear to follow Rabbi Yochanan that "from now and after thirty days" is a partial retained acquisition rather than a simple condition. This is indicated by the law that an animal acquired in this language works if it stands in a meadow at the end of thirty days, but not necessarily if it stands in the public domain. According to Shmuel, who reads the expression as a pure condition, the original acquisition would remain effective regardless of the animal's location at the later date. Thus the Shulchan Aruch's comparison to an immediate conditional acquisition is not exact in every respect. The specification of the meadow shows that a future element remains. Rama's ruling elsewhere that even if the document is torn before the thirty days the acquisition remains effective is therefore difficult if we follow Rabbi Yochanan. Taz already objected. Rama can perhaps be understood where the parties expressly formulated the arrangement as a condition rather than merely using the ambiguous phrase "from now and after thirty days." The Shach's comment that it matters that the purchaser said "on condition" is unclear. The essential question is the legal structure of the words, not which party uttered them. Where the expression is truly "on condition," it is a condition rather than a retained future acquisition.
