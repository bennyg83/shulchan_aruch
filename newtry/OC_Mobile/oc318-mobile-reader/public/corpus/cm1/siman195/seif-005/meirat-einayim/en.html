But if he said to him, "Acquire on condition that, etc." In the Perishah I explained the reason. The phrase "from now" can imply a reservation in the acquisition, as though he said, "From now the acquisition shall begin, but it shall not be completed until after thirty days." If so, the item through which the acquisition is effected must still exist in the transferor's possession at that later time, whereas with a kinyan sudar the cloth has already returned to its owner. When he says, "on condition," however, there is no reservation in the acquisition itself, only an external condition. It is as though he said, "Acquire it now through this sudar, except that I stipulate that you shall not take possession until after thirty days." Strictly speaking, "on condition" is effective even without adding "from now"; the practice of also saying "from now" was instituted to publicize that he is transferring ownership immediately. Beit Yosef writes likewise. See the Perishah.
He certainly would not have done so for nothing. Mahari writes in his rulings there that if a man accepted a kinyan to divorce his wife, the kinyan is ineffective. See Even HaEzer siman 135, seif 9.
