From now. The Sema explains that the phrase "from now" leaves part of the acquisition incomplete, as though he said: the acquisition begins now and will not be completed until after thirty days. The object through which the acquisition is made must therefore still be in the transferor's possession at that time, whereas with kinyan sudar the cloth has already returned to its owner. By contrast, when he says "on condition," there is no reservation in the acquisition itself, only an external condition. It is as though he said: acquire now through this cloth, but I stipulate that you may not take it until after thirty days. Strictly speaking, "on condition" works even without adding "from now"; the latter is merely used to make clear that the transfer is immediate. The Beit Yosef writes likewise. The Shach writes that these words are difficult, because here it is the buyer who says "on condition," as is clear from the poskim, and the end of siman 191 proves that saying "from now" alone suffices. The Tur here also implies this. The phrase "on condition" in the Tur is merely habitual wording, because every sudar is given on condition that it be returned after effecting the acquisition. Tosafot in Kiddushin also take this position according to Shmuel, whose ruling we follow. See the end of siman 157, the Rosh in chapter Mi Shemet, Tur siman 210, and Responsa Maharit, siman 46. The Taz likewise writes that the Beit Yosef's explanation is strained and explains Tosafot differently. See there.
