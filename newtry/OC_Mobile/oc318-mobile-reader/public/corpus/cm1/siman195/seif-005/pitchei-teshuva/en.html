Ketzot HaChoshen responds that he does not understand the Shach's point, because why should it matter whether the purchaser or the transferor stated it? Ketzot also questions the Shach's statement that Tosafot in Kiddushin follows Shmuel and that we rule like Shmuel. The rulings of the authorities appear to follow Rabbi Yochanan, who treats the formulation as a reservation. Taz below, siman 197:7, likewise writes that it is clear we do not rule like Shmuel on this issue. The authorities in Even HaEzer 40 dispute whether the law follows Rav or Rabbi Yochanan, but all agree that Shmuel's position is rejected. Dagul MeRevavah also challenges the Shach's statement that we rule like Shmuel. On the practical law, however, Dagul MeRevavah agrees with the Shach that the words "from now" alone suffice. He argues that the Sema's contrary ruling is difficult. We rule in siman 258 like Rabbi Yose that the date of a deed itself proves present effect even when the deed says the transfer will occur after death. Surely an explicit statement "from now" cannot be weaker than the date of the deed. He therefore explains that the dispute of Rav, Rabbi Yochanan, and Shmuel concerns gittin, kiddushin, and similar matters where one cannot divide principal ownership from future fruits. In property where such a division is possible, everyone agrees that "from now" gives the principal immediately and leaves the fruits until the later time, as taught in Bava Batra 136a. Tosafot in Kiddushin likewise deals with subjects where principal and fruits cannot be separated, such as kiddushin and betrothal arrangements. Thus, where principal and fruits can be distinguished, "from now" alone is effective, unlike the Sema. Taz also rejects the Sema in practice. See Netivot HaMishpat below, siman 197, and Ketzot HaChoshen there and in siman 258 at length.
