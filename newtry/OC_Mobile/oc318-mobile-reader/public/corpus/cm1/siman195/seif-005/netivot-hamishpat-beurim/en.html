And without qualification we say. At first glance the clause is difficult. If it concerns a sale made without qualification, why should we assume the kinyan is deferred until thirty days? If that were so, even with money, which remains effective after the coins are consumed, the seller could claim that he did not intend the acquisition to take effect until some distant time. Rather, the rule concerns a case where the language itself connects the acquisition to a future time, such as, "Acquire after thirty days," or, "Do not acquire until after thirty days." Then the ordinary kinyan does not work unless framed properly. But if he simply makes the kinyan now, even if he verbally says that the buyer should not use or take the item until after thirty days, and the subject matter presently exists so the kinyan can take effect immediately, we interpret the transaction as effective now with deferred use. Ramban's responsum cited by Beit Yosef in siman 27 states similarly.
