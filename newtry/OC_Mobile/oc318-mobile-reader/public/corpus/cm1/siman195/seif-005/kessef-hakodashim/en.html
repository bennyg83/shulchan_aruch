I further found. I wrote elsewhere that perhaps the rule in siman 195, that when parties make a kinyan sudar accepting judges to compromise they cannot retract after the normal time for retraction even while still engaged in the same matter, applies specifically before three judges, for even if they are laymen the status of a beit din and judges rests upon them. With two, even if they are established judges, perhaps not. The inability to retract may depend specifically upon the standing of three judges. I have not now found a decisive proof. At first glance, according to Maharshal in responsum 4, cited in siman 12, that before three unlearned men no kinyan is required, one might say that the Gaon’s language, cited by the Baal HaIttur and in Beit Yosef here, speaking of accepting “judges for compromise,” must refer to two, for with three no kinyan sudar is required. This can be rejected, however, because perhaps a kinyan sudar is still required in order to prevent retraction while they remain engaged in the same matter, and that special effect may exist only before three. It is also possible that according to Maharshal, where they are actual judges, even if the parties expressly accepted that they should compromise, a kinyan sudar is required; only where the three are laymen and their very conduct shows that they were accepted solely for compromise did he hold that no kinyan sudar is necessary. This requires further study.
