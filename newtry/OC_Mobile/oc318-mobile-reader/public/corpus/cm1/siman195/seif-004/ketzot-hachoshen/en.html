For an ordinary kinyan sudar is on condition that it be returned. See Ran at the end of chapter HaShutafin in Nedarim concerning one who wrote his property to another only so that he could transfer it to his grandson. The Gemara calls this 'acquisition in order to transfer.' Ran explains that it does not resemble a gift on condition that it be returned, where the recipient acquires the object for the interim. In this case the intermediary acquires no beneficial right in the property at all, even temporarily, and receives it only in order to transfer it. The Gemara compares this to a sudar used for chalipin, saying that the sudar too is acquired only in order to transfer. We therefore learn that in kinyan sudar the recipient does not acquire the sudar for any substantive use, even temporarily. It is not like a gift on condition of return. Rather, the sudar is transferred solely in order to effect acquisition of the corresponding object. See what I wrote in siman 103, seif katan 4.
