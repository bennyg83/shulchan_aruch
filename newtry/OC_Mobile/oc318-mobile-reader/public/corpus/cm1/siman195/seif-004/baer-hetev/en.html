Three fingerbreadths. Meaning, three fingerbreadths by three fingerbreadths, because at that size it has the status of a garment utensil with respect to tumah. Rashi and Rashba write likewise. The Tur and Mechaber allude to this by using the term 'measure,' meaning the measure stated for garments in other laws. Since an ordinary kinyan is made with a sudar, this measure is stated, and from it we learn the law for one who acquires with other utensils. Ibid.
To pull away. The Bach writes likewise. Specifically for a kinyan sudar made in order to transfer ownership, it suffices that he can pull the object away toward himself. But for acquiring the entire utensil itself, this does not suffice, as stated in siman 269, seif 5. Alternatively, perhaps that limitation applies only to a found object, whereas in a sale, where another person's intent transfers ownership, being able to pull it toward himself may suffice to acquire the whole utensil even though he grasps only part of it. The first approach appears primary. Shach.
All. It appears that 'the entire utensil' is not literal. Rather, it means the measure of three by three fingerbreadths stated immediately before. In the Tur's quotation of the Rambam, the words 'the entire utensil' do not appear. See there. Sema.
Than three. Meaning, it is not enough that he can pull it and bring it toward himself. Ibid.
