Baer Heitev explains that this means three fingerbreadths by three fingerbreadths, because that size has the legal status of a garment regarding tumah, and Rashi writes similarly. Responsa Be'er Yitzchak 5 was asked by Rabbi Eizel of Shavel that the rule of three by three fingerbreadths applies only to wool and linen, while fabrics of other materials require three by three tefachim for tumah. If so, when the cloth used for kinyan sudar is of another material, should it not require three tefachim? Be'er Yitzchak answers that Tosafot, Bava Batra 173b, indicates that there is no distinction between leather and sackcloth in the rule derived from Bava Metzia concerning a cloth of three by three fingerbreadths. The same appears from Piskei Tosafot and Hagahot Ashri. Several explanations are possible. First, although fabrics of other materials do not acquire tumah at the smaller size, the fact that wool and linen of that size are legally treated as a garment because they are useful to poor people shows that this minimum size can carry the halachic name of a utensil. Rashi's reference to tumah is therefore only a proof of the absolute minimum below which no object is called a utensil anywhere. Second, Zevachim 94a teaches that a cloth smaller than three may still be considered fit if its owner assigns significance to it. Third, from the discussion there concerning a small piece attached to a larger garment, one can explain that attachment itself gives it significance. Be'er Yitzchak develops these approaches at length with sharp analysis and broad learning. See there.
