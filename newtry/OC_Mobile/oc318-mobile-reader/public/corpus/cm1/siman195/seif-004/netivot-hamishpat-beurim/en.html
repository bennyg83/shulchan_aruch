To detach the entire utensil. See Shach, seif katan 6, who asks from siman 269. His answer is exceedingly forced, because there is no Gemara source for Rambam's distinctions. In my view Rambam follows his own ruling in siman 271 concerning a found animal: if one person first holds the lead rope, the person holding the rope acquires that portion himself. Sema explains that this is because he can detach it. Shach objects there that it conflicts with the Gemara. Rambam may have been troubled by the rule that one who lifts only half an object from the ground does not acquire. Why should he not at least acquire the half actually raised by his force when that is the only half he wishes to acquire? Likewise, in a sale of half an object, if the buyer physically lifts that half, why should failure to lift the unsold half defeat acquisition of the sold half? The answer must be that so long as the object remains bound to another person's hand or to ownerless ground, the lifted part is not considered independently acquired. Rambam therefore holds that where the holder can detach the portion he controls from the rest, it is no longer legally "bound in another's hand." This is analogous to gittin, where ability to detach can satisfy separation. The Gemara in Bava Metzia 9 rejects R. Abbahu's statement only insofar as he wished to say that the person acquires the entire object, including the portion not yet raised by his own force, merely because he could detach it and thereby raise it. That is indeed too much. But the part already physically raised by his force can be acquired. This is somewhat suggested by the Gemara's formulation: "If another came and lifted half of it from the pillar, would the latter not acquire?" The challenge is that the second should not be excluded from the part the first never raised. The Gemara does not necessarily deny the first person's acquisition of the portion actually elevated by his force. Accordingly, Rambam can be speaking where the holder grasps less than three handbreadths while the remainder is elevated through his force. With a small object, the portion beyond his hand is naturally elevated by his grasp. That can be acquired because it is no longer bound to another. Likewise, in siman 271 one who alone holds the lead rope acquires the part actually elevated through his hand. Where one person rides and another holds the lead rope, however, Shitah Mekubetzet explains that each one's kinyan interferes with the other. One has meshichah and the other hagbahah, so neither gains the disputed part. But when there is no competing acquirer, the holder can acquire the portion actually elevated by his force.
