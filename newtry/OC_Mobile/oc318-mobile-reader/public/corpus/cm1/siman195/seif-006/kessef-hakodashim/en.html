For a lease, money is sufficient. A lease of land is acquired by payment, since the practice is not to write a deed for such a lease. This requires consideration where one leased it before the right came into existence. Payment can be effective even after the money has been consumed, as discussed in siman 191 in Sema, seif katan 9. Even in a sale, where the practice is to use a deed, one may say that with respect to the acquisition of the usufruct for the term of the lease, payment alone takes effect. As for acquisition of the underlying land itself, it is considered to have come into the owner’s control even while it is leased to another. Under the local practice, while premises are leased to another they are considered outside the owner’s practical control, yet a deed delivered later can effect the acquisition once that obstacle ends. Likewise, a kinyan sudar can immediately operate upon the underlying title, and it is not said that at the end of the other lease the property simply reverts to its original owner without effecting the present acquisition. Since a kinyan sudar is treated as standing for writing, perhaps it is equivalent to a deed for purposes of allowing the earlier payment to complete the acquisition after the other lease expires. Still, perhaps the buyer does not fully rely upon it, because the seller may retract from writing the deed while the property is not presently in his control, and ordinary agency can certainly be revoked.
