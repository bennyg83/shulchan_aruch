To retract after the time required for a brief utterance. See Responsa VeShav HaKohen, siman 19. The rule stated here, that within the time required for a brief utterance one may retract from every kinyan, is not universally agreed upon. Rambam, Hilchot Maaseh HaKorbanot, chapter 15, halachah 1, implies that with delivery and similar forms of acquisition one cannot retract even within that short period. Rambam rules that one cannot retract from consecration within that period once he has verbalized it. The reason must be that a declaration to Heaven has the legal force of delivery to an ordinary person. If the reason were merely the special stringency of consecrated property, then even a decision made only in one's heart should likewise be irrevocable. Tosafos in Menachot 81b expressly write that the reason one cannot retract from consecration even within that period is because a declaration to Heaven is like delivery to an ordinary person. See Shach below, siman 295, seif katan 5. It follows that actual delivery to an ordinary person likewise cannot be retracted even within that period, and the same should apply to meshichah or hagbahah. According to this, it is possible to explain that even the Rosh in Bava Batra does not necessarily hold that one may retract within that period from meshichah or delivery. His statement that "there is no retraction after the time required for a brief utterance" may mean only that after that period one cannot retract from any kinyan other than kinyan sudar, even while the parties are still discussing the transaction. Within the brief period, however, some forms of acquisition may still be retractable, such as money, deed, and chazakah, whereas from meshichah, delivery, or hagbahah there may be no retraction even immediately.
