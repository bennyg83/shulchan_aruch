Any utensil, even of minimal value. As stated in seif 2.
And he should say, etc. As stated, “and he should say, etc., acquire for yourself.”
And there is no, etc. This is evident at the end of chapter 5 of Nedarim, where the dispute is whether the kinyan is made on condition that the utensil be returned or not. This shows that the ordinary kinyan is made without an express stipulation.
And once, etc., wherever it is. Bava Metzia 10b, and there 45b: “Afterward, by exchange, that means, etc.”
Even though, etc., and there is no, etc. As stated in Bava Batra chapter 9, Sanhedrin chapter 3, and elsewhere: after a kinyan nothing remains to be done, even for a shechiv mera and a partial gift.
(Collected note) Even though, etc. This rejects the view that treats it like a document, so that in a sale acquisition would not occur until the money is paid, as in Kiddushin 26a. That is incorrect, for Bava Metzia chapter 1 states in the name of Shmuel concerning one who finds a deed of conveyance, etc.; if the concern is that he wrote it intending to borrow, etc. Ran in Kiddushin there, and Rashba likewise. (End of note.)
Even if there are no, etc. As stated in Kiddushin chapter 3, “It was not created, etc.”; Sanhedrin chapter 1 regarding compromise by one judge, even though it requires a kinyan; and Bava Metzia chapter 4, “Did the Tanna trouble himself to teach us regarding a man, etc.?” One cannot answer that no witnesses are present, for that is more common than all the other cases mentioned there, especially at the threshing floor, as stated in Yevamos 100a regarding seclusion, showing that ordinarily witnesses are not common there. When Bava Batra chapter 3 says “a kinyan in the presence of two,” it excludes the need for three: if one wishes to record the kinyan, two suffice.
