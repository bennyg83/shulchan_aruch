That he pulled. See the Rosh's responsum concerning hides. I saw one who wrote that if the seller explicitly mentioned slaughter, such as saying, "I sell you the hides of these animals, which I will give you after slaughter," he sold specifically the hides of slaughtered animals, and therefore the buyer does not bear the loss if an animal dies, since the hide of a carcass is not worth the same as the hide of a slaughtered animal. In my view this is incorrect. Even in such a case the loss is the buyer's. There is an explicit proof from siman 199, where one who gave a dinar to a butcher for slaughtered meat loses the dinar if the ox dies. Here too, the item entered the buyer's risk immediately, even though the seller could not deliver it until after slaughter. Taz.
To retract. It appears that if the buyer had not yet accepted the sale, or in a comparable circumstance where he could still withdraw on the ground that the merchandise was not satisfactory to him, the seller may likewise retract. So writes Bnei Chiya in his glosses to the Tur, letter 5, citing proof from Mishpat Tzedek, part 1, siman 1. See there, and see Responsa Maharit, simanim 19 and 124.
