With other methods of acquisition, one cannot, etc. The reason is that the other modes of acquisition operate upon the purchased object itself, such as chazakah, meshichah, mesirah, and hagbahah. Likewise, with money the purchaser gives the full purchase price and the seller or transferor removes himself from the property. According to this, if he acquired through a perutah that serves as the act of acquisition in some places, as discussed at the beginning of siman 190, the law may be that either party can retract so long as they are still engaged in the same matter. This requires study. Similarly, with a deed: if the seller sold desirable land, the law is that a deed alone does not acquire and he must also give the money, at which point the seller has completely removed himself. If he sold it because of its poor quality, we are witnesses that the seller intends to remove himself and transfer it entirely, as the Tur writes in siman 191. The same applies to ma'amad sheloshtan, where the transferor immediately removes himself entirely. Kinyan sudar is different, because the utensil he gives does not represent the value of the purchase. See above at the beginning of siman 126. The Levush gives another reason of his own and did not pay attention to the Tur's explanation, even though the Levush himself cites it at the beginning of siman 126.
