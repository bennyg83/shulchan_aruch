With other modes of acquisition. Sema explains that hazakah, meshichah, and lifting operate upon the object itself, while full payment removes the seller's connection to the property. If only a perutah was paid, he says retraction can remain possible while the parties are still engaged in the transaction. A three party arrangement likewise removes the transferor's right immediately. Sudar kinyan is different because the utensil given is not the value of the property. The explanation of Ir Shushan is more convincing. If the problem with sudar kinyan were merely that the utensil is not the value of the transaction, the rule should not apply in a gift, where there is no purchase price at all. Yet the Talmudic source for the right to retract while the parties are still discussing the matter concerns a gift as well. The better distinction is that sudar kinyan is a formal act external to the body of the property. It can be performed suddenly and without the same settled physical deliberation. Therefore the law allows retraction while the parties remain engaged in the same matter. Meshichah, lifting, and hazakah are acts performed directly upon the acquired object and ordinarily reflect settled intention. They therefore complete the acquisition more decisively. Thus the special retraction rule belongs to the formal nature of sudar kinyan, not to the relative monetary value of the utensil.
