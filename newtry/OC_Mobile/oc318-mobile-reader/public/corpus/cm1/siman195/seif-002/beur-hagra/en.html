Even though, etc. There, Kiddushin 13a, and Eruvin 49a.
Provided, etc. The Gemara there, according to Rashi’s explanation.
But not with produce. In accordance with Rav Nachman, for the halachah follows him in monetary law. There: “with a utensil, excluding, etc.”
An animal, etc. There and Kiddushin chapter 1, according to the Gemara’s initial understanding, “This works according to Rav Sheshes, etc.,” and according to the reading of all poskim and manuscripts, “he exchanged the meat of an ox.” See Tosafos there. Likewise there: “If one was holding his cow, etc.” See Rashi, Bava Metzia 100a, s.v. “one who exchanges a cow, etc.”
(Collected note) An animal, etc. Yerushalmi: “There we learned, whatever becomes payment for another item, once one acquires it the other becomes obligated in its exchange.” Rabbi Yochanan said this applies only to an ox for a cow or a donkey for an ox, but not one pile for another. Rabbi Yirmiyah in the name of Rav says even pile for pile, meaning piles of produce. Their dispute is whether produce can serve as the object of exchange. It follows that an animal can serve for exchange according to all opinions. Likewise in the chapter HaMadir, “one who exchanges a cow.” (End of note.)
(Collected note) An animal, etc. See Rashi, Kiddushin 28b, and Tosafos there, s.v. “This works, etc.” The Baal HaMaor and Ramban also disagree on this point in Bava Metzia chapter 4. Rashi’s words are more compelling, for it is very forced to say that the entire discussion, “This works, etc.,” follows only Rabbi Shimon and merely reflects the initial assumption. Tosafos’ challenge from one who exchanges a cow for a donkey is answered according to Rabbeinu Tam, who writes that where items are of equal value, exchange works even with produce, and Tosafos writes that this applies even to land. (End of note.)
But all other, etc. Meaning that anything that is not a utensil is included in “produce,” as stated there, “with a utensil, excluding, etc.” There they say that acquisition is made with a utensil. See Rashi 45b, s.v. “What is the reason of the one who says, etc.,” and Beis Yosef.
A coin that was invalidated, etc. There regarding an anka dinar, etc.; see Tosafos 45b, s.v. “It is not, etc.” There: “both refer to perutotos, etc.,” meaning fully invalidated, as explained below in siman 203 seif 8.
And one does not, etc. In accordance with Rav, who follows the first Tanna. There: “to acquire, excluding, etc.” And Gittin 39b: “That is not so, etc.”
