But with any valid item. See Baer Heitev, which cites Terumat HaDeshen that if the deed does not state that the kinyan was made with a valid utensil, we do not extract property. Although the Sema states this simply, in my opinion this is not the halachah. Darkei Moshe writes that nowadays, when the practice is to record a kinyan sudar, it is as though the deed expressly stated that the parties used an item valid for acquisition. Beit Yosef in Bedek HaBayit likewise writes that we presume the kinyan was made with a valid object, and that the expanded formula in deeds is merely added for greater clarity. Above, siman 42:5, the Sema again cites Terumat HaDeshen that where the formula does not specify a valid chalipin object one must be concerned that another type of kinyan was used. Taz there disagrees and rules that a deed simply stating "we made a kinyan from him" is valid, because the ordinary meaning is kinyan sudar. He also cites Bedek HaBayit. Chacham Tzvi explains that Terumat HaDeshen would agree in an ordinary case. His case concerned a subject that cannot be acquired through chalipin, such as money. Tumim and Netivot HaMishpat agree: where the deed simply says "we made a kinyan from him" without adding the full formula, we presume an ordinary chalipin was used because that is how most kinyanim are performed. Thus, where kinyan sudar is effective, the kinyan is valid. But for something that cannot be acquired by kinyan sudar, such as money, we do not invent a different effective acquisition such as meshichah or acquisition together with land. Rather, we presume the ordinary kinyan sudar was used and the acquisition is invalid. It is therefore surprising that Netivot HaMishpat in siman 42 adopts the Taz's position but here reproduces the Sema's statement without mentioning Darkei Moshe or Bedek HaBayit. This requires study.
