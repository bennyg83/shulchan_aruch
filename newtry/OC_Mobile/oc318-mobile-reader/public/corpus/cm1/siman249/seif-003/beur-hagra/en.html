The Nathan is a. The Holy One is in many places:
And the husband, etc. Because it is her melog property.
(Supplement) One who gives, etc.; and the husband, etc. Kiddushin 23 states that all agree that there is no acquisition, etc. Ran writes there that this does not mean that a wife is identical to a slave. If a master or another person gives something to a slave, the slave acquires nothing at all. Regarding a wife, however, the Yerushalmi in chapter 6 of Ketubot states: although according to Rabbi Meir a slave's hand is like his master's, when a woman acquires, her husband acquires only the right to consume the produce. In chapter HaKotev it likewise states: if another wrote it for her, does he have the right to consume the produce? If he himself wrote it for her, all the more so, etc. In chapter 6 it states: why did they say that a wife's find belongs to her husband? Rabbi Yose said, so that a woman should not conceal gold coins from her husband and say, 'I found them.' Consider a case where another gave her a gift. A gift becomes publicly known, etc. This is cited in Haggahot Ashri there, chapter 6, and it is unlike Rashi's words in Sanhedrin 71a, s.v. on condition, etc. (End of supplement.) See 331.
