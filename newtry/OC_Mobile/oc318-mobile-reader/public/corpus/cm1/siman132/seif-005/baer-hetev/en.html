The creditor. The Tur reads “the creditor,” while Rambam reads “to the creditor,” as does the Mechaber in siman 77 seif 3. In either reading, the Sema explains that the creditor must tell the guarantor against which debt he accepted the payment, so the guarantor can return to that particular borrower and recover from him. The choice belongs to the creditor to say, “I accepted the money toward this debt,” as stated in siman 83 seif 3. The Shach objects that the language “for which of them he is paying” does not imply this. Rather, the creditor is indifferent for which debtor payment is made, and the ruling teaches that the guarantor must tell the creditor for whom he is paying. Otherwise he cannot recover from either debtor, since each can deflect him to the other. Notice by the guarantor to the creditor is sufficient. The Taz explains similarly and writes that if the guarantor paid without designation, he cannot sue either debtor until he also pays the second debt or obtains authorization from the creditor.
