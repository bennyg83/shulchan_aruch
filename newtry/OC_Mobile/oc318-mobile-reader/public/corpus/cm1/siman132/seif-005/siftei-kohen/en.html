He may return and claim his share from his fellow. Responsa Mabit, part 1, siman 22, and part 2, siman 228, discusses Reuven, Shimon, and Levi, who purchased merchandise together and executed a promissory note to Yehudah for the entire debt, each becoming an arev kablan for the others. When payment became due, Reuven and Shimon were present to pay their shares, while Levi was absent. Yehudah released Reuven from the guarantee for his associates on condition that he pay his own share, and he collected from Shimon both Shimon's share and Levi's. Shimon later sued Reuven for half of what he had paid on Levi's behalf, since both had guaranteed Levi. Reuven answered that Yehudah had already released him from that guarantee. The Mabit ruled that because Yehudah could initially have collected the entire debt from either guarantor, Shimon, after paying, could not recover a share from Reuven. Although the Tur in siman 132 states that when one guarantor pays the entire debt he may recover his fellow's share, the Mabit understood this only according to the Ramban, who holds that where the creditor can collect from both, he may collect only half from each. In that case, when one paid the full debt, he paid a portion the creditor could not have demanded from him alone, so the other must reimburse his share. But where the creditor could originally collect the entire debt from either one, as here, the one who paid did not pay anything on the other's behalf, and cannot recover from him. The Mabit adds that this is certainly so where Yehudah released Reuven. He elaborates there. His words do not appear correct to me. The opposite is more reasonable. Where his fellow was himself liable for the entire debt, the paying guarantor may recover the other's share. Where the fellow was not liable for the whole debt, payment may be treated as paying another's debt without authorization according to those who require the debtor to have said, 'Guarantee me and pay.' The Baal HaTerumot, sha'ar 44, part 2, states explicitly in this very case that since the lender may collect the whole from whichever one he wishes, they entered the arrangement on the understanding that they would share every loss and payment, and one may therefore recover half from the other. The Sema also states explicitly here that even according to the Rambam one need not have said, 'Guarantee me and pay,' because the creditor may collect from either one. It is as though the debtor had said, 'Guarantee me,' and the creditor had already litigated with the debtor, in which case even if the guarantor pays voluntarily the debtor must reimburse him. The same applies here, and it follows the law of an arev kablan stated by the Mechaber in siman 130. This is also proved by the Tur and Mechaber, who state without qualification that he may recover his fellow's share. The Tur appears to follow the Rambam that the creditor may collect from whichever guarantor he chooses. The Tur also cites in siman 77 a responsum of the Rosh that follows the Rambam, and the Mechaber in seif 3 here and in siman 77, seif 3, initially rules in accordance with the Rambam, yet both state here that the paying guarantor may recover his fellow's share. The Sema likewise writes explicitly in siman 77, seif katan 11, that when two guaranteed, the sages did not trouble the creditor to collect half from each, even if both can pay. He may collect all from whichever he chooses, and the one who paid may recover half from the other. The Bach also assumes this plainly in siman 77, seif 4. The Mabit's further argument from Yehudah's release is also incorrect, as I wrote in siman 77, seif katan 16, that releasing one guarantor in such a case does not release the other. Moreover, even if the creditor released the other, that would only mean that the creditor may not sue him; it would not prevent the paying guarantor from recovering from him. Other aspects of the Mabit's responsum are likewise unsound. It therefore appears that in this case Shimon may recover Reuven's share. See also Responsa Mabit, part 2, siman 220, at length.
