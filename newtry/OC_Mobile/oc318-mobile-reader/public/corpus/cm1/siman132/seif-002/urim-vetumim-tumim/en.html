An unmarried woman, etc. She is not obligated to pay. See Shach, who holds that if the guaranty was written in a document but the creditor did not write that it binds property she would acquire in the future, he may nevertheless collect from her husband after she marries, because in a case of loss the husband is treated like an heir. This follows the Shach's view above regarding future property in siman 112. According to what I wrote there, however, the creditor caused his own loss because he should have written an express clause binding after acquired property. All the more so here he should have written such a clause. Even if we say the husband is legally an heir, by Talmudic law movable property inherited by heirs was not subject to a creditor's lien. Therefore there is certainly no collection here and no doubt. I am even more surprised by the Shach's statement that if the movable property she brought into the marriage remains identifiable, the creditor may collect from it. This is difficult to understand. In the guaranty case she received nothing from the creditor, so what can it mean that the movables are still intact? Even where the woman herself borrowed, this formulation has no basis. The Geonim said that if the very money she borrowed remains intact, although a loan is generally given for expenditure, the creditor may collect that money. But if she has other money, even thousands of dinars, the creditor does not collect from it, and certainly not unrelated movable property, since those movables were never received from the lender. Whatever lien may have existed against her property is terminated upon marriage. Even land that she owned, which by the rule that liens are d'oraisa was subject to an oral loan, cannot be collected from by the creditor once she marries even if it remains physically intact. All the more so movable property, which was never subject to the lien at all. I can find no explanation for the Shach's words.
