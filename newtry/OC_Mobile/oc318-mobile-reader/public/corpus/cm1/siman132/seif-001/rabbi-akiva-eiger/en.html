[Shach, letter 2] For it is no worse than her borrowing. Note: This is the view of Rambam, Ri, and Mordechai, cited in the Rama, Even HaEzer 91:4. Nevertheless, I do not understand the comparison. There, the money she received as a loan from the lender is still extant, and we say that the husband does not acquire it, so it is as though it never left the lender's possession and he merely takes what is his. Here, however, her movable property that she brought into the marriage remains extant, the husband acquired it and has the status of a purchaser, and the creditor cannot collect it from him.
