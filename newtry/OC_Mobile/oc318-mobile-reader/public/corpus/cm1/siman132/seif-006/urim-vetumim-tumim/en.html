That the creditor may demand, etc. If the second guarantor paid, Mabit holds that he may not return and demand half from the first guarantor, because the creditor forgave the first guarantor. The Shach in seif katan 5 holds that he nevertheless may recover half from him. Practical law requires study. Reason and the unqualified language of the decisors appear to support Mabit. If the second guarantor had a claim against the first, the decisors should have stated it. Otherwise this creates an endless circular series of claims: the second guarantor demands from the first, and the first then turns to the creditor and says, You discharged me. Reason likewise supports exemption, for the first guarantor can say to the second, What relationship do I have with you? I guaranteed only to the creditor, and he discharged me; I am as though I had never been a guarantor. Nevertheless, some proof for the Shach can be brought from brothers who divided an estate and a creditor collected from one brother, where the others must contribute their proper shares even if the creditor could have chosen differently. Rambam, according to the Maggid Mishneh, compares guarantors to that case. Still, the situations may be distinguished. The matter therefore remains doubtful, and money cannot be extracted from the one in possession.
