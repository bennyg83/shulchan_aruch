Although he did not say to him, "Become guarantor for me and pay." The correct text is "and pay," without an alef in the Hebrew spelling. The meaning is that although regarding an ordinary guarantor who paid the borrower's debt the Rambam holds that the guarantor cannot recover from the borrower unless the borrower originally said, "Become guarantor for me and pay on my behalf," as the Tur and Mechaber state in siman 130:2, here the second guarantor need not have been expressly told both to guarantee and to pay. Even according to those who disagree there and hold that once the borrower said, "Become guarantor for me," no separate instruction to pay is needed, one might have thought that here, where the second guarantor was not directly asked by the first guarantor to become guarantor but was persuaded by the borrower himself to do so, even they would require the words, "and pay." The ruling teaches the opposite. Even according to the Rambam, no such separate instruction is required here. Since the creditor may collect from whichever guarantor he chooses, the situation is comparable to a guarantor whom the borrower instructed to guarantee and against whom the creditor has already obtained judgment after the borrower was found unable to pay. In such a case, even if the guarantor pays voluntarily, the borrower must reimburse him. This is also analogous to an absolute guarantor, as explained in siman 130:2.
