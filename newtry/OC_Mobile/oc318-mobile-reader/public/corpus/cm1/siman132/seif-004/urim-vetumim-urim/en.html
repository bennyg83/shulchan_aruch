Although he did not say, 'Become guarantor for me and pay.' This is the correct reading. Rambam ordinarily requires that a guarantor who paid be able to say the borrower expressly told him, 'Become guarantor for me and pay,' as explained above in siman 130. Even authorities who say 'pay' need not always be explicit agree that here some authorization is needed, because the borrower never expressly said, 'Become my guarantor,' but merely induced him to act. The novelty is that even according to Rambam no additional express instruction is needed in this case, since the creditor may collect all from whichever guarantor he chooses. It is therefore as though the borrower expressly authorized him. This resembles a kablan guarantor, who may recover after paying without additional instruction. Sema. This is correct.
He returns and claims his fellow's share. Mabit argues that this follows only the dissenting view, but according to Rambam, who allows the creditor to collect the entire debt from either guarantor, the guarantor who paid cannot recover a share from the other. The Shach rejects him from logic and from the language of Tur and Mechaber. See Tumim. There is no need for extended argument. Rambam learned this very rule from the case of brothers, and there the Gemara explicitly says that the one whose share was taken recovers proportionally from the other brothers. The matter is therefore clear against Mabit.
