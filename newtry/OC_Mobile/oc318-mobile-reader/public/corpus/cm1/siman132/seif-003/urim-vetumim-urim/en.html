He may collect from whichever of them he wishes. Beit Yosef derived this from Rambam, chapter 25 of the laws of loans. I already discussed the issue at length above in siman 77. Rambam's proof, as Maggid Mishneh and Gidulei Terumah explain, is from brothers who divided and a creditor took one brother's share. Rambam understood that the creditor may proceed against either obligor, and therefore two guarantors are similar. Tosafot, however, answer that the brother case involves an apotiki or a situation where the other brother has no intermediate land. Mechaber above in siman 107:7 rules in accordance with Tosafot and Rosh, which makes his present adoption of Rambam difficult. I left the Shulchan Aruch's ruling requiring study. See Tumim for the broader analysis.
