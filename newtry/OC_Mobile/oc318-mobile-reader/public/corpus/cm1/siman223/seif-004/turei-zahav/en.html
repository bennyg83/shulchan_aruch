The Sema's explanation in seif katan 3, that this is not a complete case of “here it is,” is surprising, for the Gemara treats it as “here it is” and finds liability for an oath only where the animal was cut off from his hand. This shows that it is “here it is.” What the Sema further writes, that the oath is due to a rabbinic enactment, is likewise surprising, for if so the Talmud would not have needed to say that it was cut off from his hand. Moreover, one who studies the sugya there will see that it concerns a Torah oath. It appears to me that the Rambam speaks here where the cow is standing in the marsh, and in such a case it is not “here it is,” as stated in Bava Kamma 105. From there the Baal HaTerumot, gate 3, derived this rule, as cited by the Beit Yosef in siman 87. This is also evident from the Rambam, who states this law that the seller must swear immediately after writing that the cow is in the marsh, and so forth. One should not ask why the Talmud established the case as one where it was cut off from his hand, for the Talmud preferred to establish the case even where the animal is before us, as explained above.
