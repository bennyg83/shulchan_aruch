Proof. At first glance it would appear that here, where the purchaser also claims uncertainty, if he has no proof the money should remain with its original owner even without an oath. This would also explain why the Mechaber repeated this law instead of relying on his immediately preceding ruling, where even when the purchaser claims certainty and the seller claims uncertainty we say that the burden of proof rests upon the claimant. There, the seller may collect only with an oath because the purchaser claims certainty. According to this, however, Rama should not have added that some say this applies even when the claimant asserts certainty, because no one disputes that in such a case the seller may collect only with an oath. This requires study. Sema. Taz explains the matter differently and thereby resolves Sema's difficulty. See there.
