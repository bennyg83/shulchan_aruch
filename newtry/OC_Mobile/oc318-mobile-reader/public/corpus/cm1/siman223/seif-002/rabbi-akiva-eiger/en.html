[Shulchan Aruch] And if the purchaser is in possession. Note: Tosafot, s.v. "and let us see," hold that this applies specifically where both parties make definite claims, but where both make uncertain claims, the purchaser's possession does not help. The view of the Ramach in Shitah Mekubetzet appears to be that even where both are uncertain and the purchaser is in possession, the burden of proof is on the seller. He is uncertain only where the seller makes a definite claim and the purchaser an uncertain one, for perhaps even when the purchaser is in possession, a definite claim prevails over an uncertain one. From the language of the Rif and Rosh, who write that where both are definite, or where one is definite and the other uncertain, even if the item stands in a marsh or alleyway we place it in the possession of the original owner, it would appear that even in the definite versus uncertain case this is only where it stands in a marsh. If it is in the purchaser's possession, we leave it with the purchaser.
