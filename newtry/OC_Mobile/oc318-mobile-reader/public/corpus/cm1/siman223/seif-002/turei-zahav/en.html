(See there.) The owner of the cow claimed, etc. The wording implies that the only dispute between them is when the birth occurred. This is difficult in light of siman 220, seif 10. One who sells a cow without qualification does not sell its offspring, while one who says “a pregnant cow” sells the offspring. Here, therefore, one way or the other there is a problem. If he sold a cow without qualification, the offspring was not sold even if born afterward. If he sold a pregnant cow, he sold the offspring even if it had already been born beforehand. One cannot answer that the case here concerns an unspecified sale and differs because the offspring was still in its mother's womb at the time of pulling, unlike the earlier case where it had already been born. Rabbeinu writes there in the Rosh's name that when the cow is still pregnant, the offspring is sold, which implies that according to the first view there, even a fetus is not sold. It therefore appears that Rabbeinu did not present the two statements there as a dispute at all.
