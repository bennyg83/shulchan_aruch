If the purchaser is in possession. Tosafot asks why the Gemara says to examine in whose domain the disputed animal presently stands and make the other party bear the burden of proof. Livestock is the type of property for which ordinary possession does not establish title. Tosafot answers that the case contains an objective monetary uncertainty independent of the parties' claims. If the purchaser is in possession and makes a definite claim, we do not remove the animal from him. But if he claims only uncertainty, possession obtained under doubt should not help, because he was never certainly established as owner. This differs from a case where a depositor leaves one live lamb with a householder who already owns another live lamb and one later dies. There, even if the householder is uncertain, his prior certain possession of his own live lamb makes the depositor the one seeking to extract. Here the purchaser was never certainly established in the disputed animal. The seller, however, if presently in possession, benefits even with an uncertain claim because he was the original certain owner. The Shulchan Aruch does not state that the purchaser must claim with certainty. This suggests that it follows Rambam's broader view that in an unresolved legal doubt, seizure can help even with an uncertain plea. Tosafot and Rosh, who do not allow doubtful seizure except with a definite claim, require the purchaser's claim to be certain. The omission is therefore consistent with the Shulchan Aruch's general approach.
