Object. The reason for this stringent oath is that a fetus is considered part of its mother. The claimant demanded a cow that was pregnant at the time of meshichah, while the defendant admits liability for the cow but not its offspring. This resembles a partial admission, without a complete case of 'here it is,' because he is not returning the cow in the condition in which it was claimed. Although the Gemara calls this a case of 'here it is,' it is nevertheless not a complete instance of that rule. I further suggest that just as the Sages instituted a severe oath when two people are holding a garment, so that everyone should not simply seize another's garment, they instituted one here as well. Sema. Taz writes that Sema's words are astonishing and rejects them from the Gemara. He explains that Rambam is speaking where the cow is standing in a meadow or a similar place, which is not considered 'here it is,' as stated in Bava Kamma 105. Baal HaTerumot learned this from there, as cited by Beit Yosef in siman 87. If so, why did the Gemara need to establish the case as one where he cut off its hand? Because the Gemara preferred to establish the law even where the animal is before us. Taz. See below siman 88:12.
