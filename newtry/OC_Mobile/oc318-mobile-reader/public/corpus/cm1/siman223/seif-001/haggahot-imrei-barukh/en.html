(Sema, seif katan 3) For he is not giving him a cow like, etc. Note: This requires study. According to the defendant's own words, this is a case of 'here it is.' How can he be obligated to swear merely because the claimant asserts that it is not 'here it is'? See above, siman 87, seif 2. However, according to what Beit Yosef writes on the Tur, letter 5, it appears that he holds that if, according to the claimant's version, the defendant's admission is not 'here it is,' the defendant is obligated in a Torah oath. He explains there why, according to this, the Tur needed in the cited siman evidence or an admission that its value had diminished. Without that, it would be enough for the defendant to swear that it had not diminished, making it 'here it is.' But Sema and Bach in that siman explicitly hold that if he does not admit a loss in value, he takes only a hesset oath that it did not diminish and is exempt from a Torah oath. Beit Yosef's words therefore require study.
