(Seif 4: His taking possession is of no effect.) This is difficult in light of Rabbenu's and the Mechaber's ruling at the end of siman 125. When a deathly ill person tells an agent, "Bring one hundred zuz to so-and-so," and the recipient was alive when the instruction was given, the donor cannot retract, because "bring" said by a deathly ill person is equivalent to "acquire," and the recipient acquires through the agent's taking possession. That case necessarily concerns one who gave instructions because of impending death, since it states that if he recovers he may retract. This contradicts the ruling here that taking possession is entirely ineffective. One cannot answer that the possession here was taken independently, without the deathly ill person's instruction. The Mordechai in the chapter Mi Shemet describes Leah, Rachel's attendant, taking bundles of Rachel's property at Rachel's direction, according to Leah's account. This is also evident there, for it says that had Rachel not made the gift because of impending death, she could not have retracted, since a gift by a deathly ill person accompanied by an act of acquisition is effective, and Leah's taking possession constituted such an act. Had Leah seized the property on her own, how could she have acquired even a partial gift of a deathly ill person, where no valid act of acquisition occurred? Taking possession must be with the donor's consent. Thus she necessarily took it with Rachel's consent, as is also explicit in Maharam's responsum 34, which appears to be the same responsum cited by the Mordechai. This also shows that the Sema's distinction is untenable. He writes that if the deathly ill person had handed it over so that the recipient could bring it home, the donor could not retract. Yet here Leah took it home at Rachel's direction, and Rachel could nevertheless retract. It therefore appears to me that the Rama's phrase, "taking possession is of no effect," includes every act of acquisition: none prevents retraction. He thus disagrees with the Ramah's ruling in siman 125. Although the Rama offered no comment there on the Mechaber's ruling in accordance with the Ramah, truth must guide the ruling as I have written. The matter, however, still requires study, as I will explain in seif 13.
