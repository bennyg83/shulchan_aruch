And the gift was found. A written and signed will was found in the deceased's clothing, but at death he was not mentally settled and said nothing about the will. If it was written while he was healthy, it has no force unless every disposition was made through a form of acquisition legally sufficient for that property. If the document states that all details were conveyed in the most effective manner recognized by Chazal, it may operate through an admission, particularly if he signed it himself and certainly if the signatures of witnesses are clearly authenticated. Yet delivery of the instrument is ordinarily essential. Where there was no separate kinyan and the document itself functions only as an admission, perhaps even a witnessed document has no effect until delivery. It may therefore be necessary that the will state that he prepared it because he did not know whether he would later be able to issue instructions. Such wording could show a present intent that the writing itself serve as the operative preparation. The fact that he did not immediately deposit it with another person may suggest that he still contemplated changes, but if he made none perhaps the presumption is that he remained satisfied with the prepared instrument. This requires further study. If the will states that it was written because of anger toward his son and it is known that they later reconciled, the ordinary law of inheritance stands strongly. If the will states that its purpose was to save the son from punishment or to cause him to receive his deserts through temporary deprivation of inheritance, perhaps the intent was that he be excluded only until judgment or correction, especially if the wording points that way. In one matter there had been an earlier will containing such language, and the local rabbi ruled by his considered judgment that it should be disregarded, presumably on evidence before him. When the guardians wished to ask me, I instructed them in my name to rely upon that local rabbi. If the first will states that it is made in every effective manner, and afterward the testator changed some provisions and wrote a later will without equivalent formalities, then because he retracted before the first instrument was delivered for implementation, the first will may be cancelled. The later one, lacking a kinyan or other valid mechanism, may likewise be ineffective, leaving the Torah law of inheritance in place.
