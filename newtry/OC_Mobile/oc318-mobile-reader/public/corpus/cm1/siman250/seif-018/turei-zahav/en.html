(Seif 20: For he thought, etc.) I do not understand this. Why did he write and make a kinyan for the first recipient? If he did so because a gift without a kinyan would be deficient, what does it mean that he thought to do the same for the second? And if he erred when giving to the first and thought that a kinyan could take effect after death, we should say the same regarding the second. Why attribute it to his unwillingness to give the second less than the first? It appears to me that the case is not one in which he retracted the first gift. Rather, he divided his property, wrote part of it to the first, and made a kinyan. There the kinyan was effective because he intended to retain the remainder. Later he changed his mind and gave the remainder to the second with a kinyan. It is then reasonable to say that he thought that, just as a kinyan was effective for the first, he should not treat the second less favorably, not realizing that the second gift completed the disposition of the entire estate. This is proven by our master's wording, "and he again wrote also to the second," rather than, "and he retracted and wrote to the second." This also resolves the apparent contradiction in our master's words. In seif 23 he writes that the second recipient acquires only where the donor strengthened his power, for otherwise even the second does not acquire, whereas in seif 32 he writes that the second requires no strengthening clause. According to my explanation, the earlier case is different because he rewrote the same gift to the second, so the reason stated here does not apply. Although the Sema's language does not imply my interpretation, for he explains that the first gift lacked a strengthening clause, since otherwise the donor should have made one for the second as well, indicating that he understood the case as a retraction of the first gift, I have written what appears to me the primary explanation.
