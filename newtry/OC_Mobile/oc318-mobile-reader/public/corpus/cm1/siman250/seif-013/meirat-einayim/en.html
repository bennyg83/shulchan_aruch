A shechiv mera who wrote to one person, etc. See Beit Yosef in seif 17, where he writes at length in the name of Rashba concerning Yaakov, who gave one parcel of land to his son Reuven as a gift of a healthy person, "from today and after death." Years later Yaakov made a deathbed disposition and divided his property, giving that same land to his son Shimon, and instructed that the will conclude that anyone who challenges the will shall take nothing from his property. Rashba ruled that Shimon acquired nothing. Although Reuven cannot take possession of the land during Yaakov's lifetime because of his father's condition, Reuven's heirs will nevertheless acquire it. Darkei Moshe cites this.
Whether all or part. The Tur comments on Rambam's wording: "This does not appear to me, because a partial gift of a shechiv mera has the law of a healthy person's gift for every purpose, and he cannot retract." The Maggid Mishneh and Beit Yosef explain that Rambam speaks where he expressly stated that he gives it with the status of a shechiv mera gift. This is exactly what Rama adds here, "if he expressly said that it should be a gift of a shechiv mera, as explained in seif 9." A printing error placed that gloss earlier and out of position. In a handwritten Shulchan Aruch with Rama's glosses I found it explicitly placed on the words "whether part," as stated here. The Ir Shushan omitted the gloss entirely, perhaps because he intended the explanation I gave in the Derishah, that Tosafos too interpret the ruling as applying even to a partial gift. See there for my answer to the Tur's objection.
Whether for himself or for another. See above at the end of siman 125, where the Tur cites Ramah that once the shechiv mera gives the property to an agent, he cannot retract and give it to someone else. We must say that there the giver handed it to the agent for immediate delivery to the recipient. Since he removed the money so fully from his own domain, Ramah holds that he cannot retract even before it reaches the recipient. Here, however, the giver told the person holding the property not to give it to the recipient until after the giver's death. See the Perishah.
Even if he wrote and conferred possession upon the first recipient, etc. This is also the Gemara's language. The Tur gives two explanations. One is that of Rabbeinu Yitzchak and the Rosh: even if he wrote and delivered the document to the recipient and also conferred possession of the gift upon the recipient through another person, such as movable property delivered to another to acquire for the recipient, or land where he told another, "Go take possession and acquire it for so-and-so," he may nevertheless retract until he also performs a kinyan for the recipient. Once he goes that far, we say that he would not have done all these acts unless he intended a complete present transfer. The same applies that a kinyan alone, without conferring possession through another, is insufficient even if he wrote and delivered the document. Examine this carefully.
The latter recipient acquires, etc. The Tur writes in seif 22 that this is specifically where he enhanced the latter recipient's power, but the Mechaber and Rama omit that. It appears their reason is as I wrote in the Perishah: enhancement of the latter recipient's power is needed only where the first recipient's power was also enhanced. If neither recipient's power was specially enhanced, the latter still acquires, as the Tur explicitly writes in seif 32. See there.
Seizure does not help, etc. It appears this means that seizure by the recipient himself does not help and the giver may retract. Rama then says, "however, if the shechiv mera or his agent delivered it into the recipient's hand," which the Tur seems to imply does help. Rama says he does not know the Tur's source. The implication comes from the Tur's words in seif 22: even if he wrote a document for delivery to the recipient and conferred possession through another, he may retract if there is no kinyan so long as the gift has not reached the recipient. This expressly implies that if it did reach the recipient, he cannot retract. As for Rama's question, see the end of siman 125, where the Tur cites Ramah that even if the shechiv mera's agent already delivered it to the recipient at the giver's instruction, the giver can still retract upon recovery. One can distinguish, because here the case includes both writing and delivery of the document and conferral of possession through another; the Tur may hold that everyone agrees this is like an additional kinyan, so he can no longer retract. Darkei Moshe itself makes this distinction in seif 6. Another distinction is that there the giver retracts after recovering, whereas here the discussion is retraction while still ill, as explained in seif 4 regarding Mordechai's position. See the Perishah.
