One on his deathbed who wrote a gift to one person. If Yaakov gave a parcel of land to Reuven's sons as a gift made by a healthy person, and later divided his assets and issued a deathbed directive giving that same land to Shimon, and at the end of the will wrote that whoever contests the will shall receive nothing from his property and everything shall go to his other brothers, the Rashba ruled that Shimon acquires nothing in the land. Reuven, however, may not contest Shimon's possession during his own lifetime, though Reuven's heirs ultimately acquire the land. See Beurim for many laws concerning this:

Whether for himself or for another. Although the passage concludes that this applies even where he caused another to acquire it on behalf of the recipient, and siman 125 explains that after such acquisition he cannot retract during his illness, there the case is where he instructed the agent immediately to bring it to the recipient's house. Here, however, he caused the agent to acquire it merely as an act of acquisition and did not instruct him to deliver it until after death. In such a case, even an added expression of strengthening is required, because without it the transaction is a deathbed gift accompanied by a kinyan:
And he wrote and caused acquisition for the latter recipient. If he made a kinyan for the first recipient without an added expression of strengthening, it nevertheless works for the second recipient even without such strengthening, as explained below in seif 20:
It is implied by the Tur that it is effective. This is where the gift actually reached the recipient at the giver's direction. In that case it is effective to prevent the giver from retracting during his illness. But if he recovers, he may retract.
