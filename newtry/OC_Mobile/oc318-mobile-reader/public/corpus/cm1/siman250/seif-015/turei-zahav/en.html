The Rivash wrote that it is reasonable that the concern, "perhaps he did not fully resolve to transfer ownership except through the document or the kinyan," was said only regarding gifts. An appointment as guardian is different, for what is he giving them that we should say he did not fully resolve to appoint except through the document? The document serves only as evidence that he appointed him guardian. This is difficult, for siman 246, seif 8, states that without a strengthening clause he did not appoint him guardian either. Since the appointment was expressed as a transfer, we say that perhaps he did not fully resolve to appoint him guardian except through the document, and a document cannot take effect after death, as our master wrote there in the name of Rabbeinu Yonah. It therefore appears that the Rivash did not see these words of Rabbeinu Yonah. Had he seen them and disagreed, he should at least have cited them, and it is not the Rivash's usual practice to disagree with the early authorities. Nor do I understand the Rivash's objection that the principal transfers nothing to the guardian. We find that a healthy person who grants another authority to control his property requires a kinyan, as stated in siman 122, whereas for a deathly ill person a verbal declaration suffices. When he says, "Write and sign," his intent is to appoint him in the manner of a healthy person. Therefore, in my humble opinion, one should not rule according to this gloss, which follows the Rivash.
