And if he did not, etc. As stated on 126b: 'they acquired,' specifically regarding one who distributes his property orally. On 148b, 'he shall take,' 'he shall acquire,' etc., only these formulations are language of inheritance or gift. Even if he was pleased with their conduct, it is ineffective, as stated on 152a regarding a deathbed gift containing an act of acquisition, etc.; as Rabbi Elazar sent, etc. Likewise on 147b, regarding a deathly ill person who said, 'So-and-so shall reside, etc.' On 135a, regarding a testamentary document that was found, etc. In chapter 9 of Bava Kamma, Rabbi Abba was owed money, etc., and the acknowledgment 'I have received payment' of Rabbi Abba was ineffective, etc. See 6 and 100.
And some authorities say, etc. Appointing the guardian is equivalent to his instruction, and his words are effective. This is as stated in Gittin 52a: 'or one who appointed for them, etc.' The Gemara's distinction concerning who appointed them, etc., here, etc., shows that his words are effective even regarding the prohibition of untithed produce, which carries death liability, and all the more so in monetary matters. We even follow an indication of his intent, as stated in Ketubot 68a: Rabbi Yehuda says, if he married off, etc., for support we assess, etc. See Mordechai there.
