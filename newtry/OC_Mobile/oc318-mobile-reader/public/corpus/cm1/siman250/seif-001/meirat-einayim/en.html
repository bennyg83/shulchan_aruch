A gift of a shechiv mera does not require a kinyan. This refers back to what the Tur and Mechaber wrote at the beginning of siman 241: one who gives a gift to another, whether land or movable property, the recipient acquires only through one of the modes by which a purchaser acquires. Here he teaches that this applies to the gift of a healthy person, but a gift of a shechiv mera does not require a kinyan. This is a rabbinic enactment, out of concern that the mind of the shechiv mera might become distraught if he fears that he will die and his instructions will not be carried out, since not every shechiv mera has the time or opportunity to execute each transfer through the legally appropriate kinyan. Precisely because no kinyan is required, if the shechiv mera nevertheless makes a kinyan for the gift, that can actually weaken it, and the gift will not be effective, as the Tur and Mechaber write below in seif 17. The two rules depend on one another. Since no kinyan is needed and he nevertheless made one, he revealed that he intended his gift to operate like a gift of a healthy person through a kinyan. It is obvious, however, that he intended the gift to take effect only after his death, and there is no kinyan after death.
It does not require a kinyan. This applies specifically where the shechiv mera distributed all of his property, or where he expressly instructed because of impending death, as explained below in seif 7.
They are considered as though written and delivered. Meaning, if the subject is land, it is as though he wrote, "My field is given to you," and delivered the document to him. If it is movable property, which is not acquired through a document, it is as though it were actually delivered into the recipient's hand. For this reason he can also transfer promissory notes to others, because his speech is treated as though he wrote, "Acquire the note itself and all its liens," and delivered that writing to the recipient, as explained above in siman 248 and in the Perishah there.
For immediately upon his death his property falls, etc. Here it is not relevant to say that his words are treated as though written, because even if written, he gave nothing during his lifetime. Before the contemplated transfer could take effect, the property already descended to the heirs. This is unlike the explanation offered by the Ir Shushan; see my discussion below.
