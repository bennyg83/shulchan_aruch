The words of a shechiv mera are as though written. Ketzot HaChoshen discusses Tur's statement that the recipient acquires retroactively. Beit Yosef objects that a shechiv mera gift is acquired only after death, and Ketzot asks from Bava Batra 137, which says that in a shechiv mera gift the recipient does not acquire until after death, and therefore the later recipient has already preceded him. Ketzot overlooked Maharit, Choshen Mishpat siman 74. Maharit writes that Chazal strengthened a shechiv mera gift as though it were a gift from today and after death: the lien begins from today while the proprietary right is completed after death. Nevertheless, when the shechiv mera gives property elsewhere in an "after you" situation, the second can prevail because the later recipient does not come through the donor directly but through the first recipient, while the earlier lien and right of the shechiv mera beneficiary preceded him. See Maharit there. His reasoning directly undermines Ketzot's discussion. See also the end of siman 125, where the primary rule is that there is no retroactive acquisition except where the donor said "bring it," because in a shechiv mera context "bring" is like "acquire."
