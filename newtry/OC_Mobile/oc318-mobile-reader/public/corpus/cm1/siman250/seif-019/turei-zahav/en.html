(Seif 21: And he fully resolved to transfer ownership immediately.) This wording indicates that the document need not reach the recipient during the donor's lifetime, and the Sema wrote likewise without explaining the reason. It appears to me that this differs from seif 19, which requires a testamentary document to reach the recipient during the donor's lifetime. There, the writing nevertheless uses the form of a legal document, although it is apparent that it is a testamentary instruction to his household to carry out the gift while the document remains in their possession. Here, however, he records the entire matter in general terms and uses no documentary formula at all. He therefore does not make the transfer conditional upon the writing reaching the recipient. This also explains why our master placed this law after already stating the rule of a writing composed in testamentary language. The Rama's words require study, for he wrote here as well that the testament must reach the recipient. Our master has already written that rule, so why repeat it? Moreover, at the beginning of this seif it is obvious that the disposition is effective. The Sema therefore wrote that the Rama's gloss refers to the opening clause. Yet this is difficult, because the Rama mentions a testament while the opening clause mentions a document. Nevertheless, the truth itself points to the proper interpretation.
