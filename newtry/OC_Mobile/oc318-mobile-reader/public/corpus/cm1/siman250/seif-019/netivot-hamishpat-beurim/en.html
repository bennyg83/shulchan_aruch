That which we say. See Sema, seif katan 57. In Perishah I wrote that according to Rosh and Tur, even if they made a kinyan sudar from him, if he afterward told them to give the gift recipient the written document together with the gift, and they delivered that document during his lifetime, but did not record the kinyan in the document, the recipient acquires. Shach challenges this and concludes that the opposite is primary. That is correct. What I wrote in Perishah was intended to resolve Tosafot's questions, but careful examination shows that the explanation is very difficult.
Likewise, if he wrote it in his own handwriting. See Sema, seif katan 26. His intent is that we infer a desire to transfer by a healthy-person kinyan only where he caused a deed to be written in the form, "My field is acquired to you," meaning he instructed witnesses to write and deliver a deed. But if he himself merely wrote, "My field is acquired to you," it is only testamentary language and not an admission. This is how Perishah explains it.
