What we say, even if it reached, etc. This refers back to seif 17. The rule there applies to a deed containing the acquisition language “my field is given to you,” which is one of the acquisition methods of a healthy person. Since the donor revealed that he wishes the gift to be acquired through such a healthy person's kinyan, merely delivering that deed during life does not revive the deathbed enactment. But if the dangerously ill person simply instructed the witnesses to write his will and deliver that written memorial to the recipient, the law differs. If they wrote it but did not deliver it until after death, the recipient does not acquire, because the donor's instruction “write and give” suggests that he intended acquisition only through the deed, and there is no deed after death. If they delivered it during his lifetime, the recipient acquires. In the Perishah I wrote that according to the Rosh and Tur, even if the donor had made a kinyan sudar on the gift, if he then merely told them to give the recipient the gift together with the written will, and the writing did not itself record the kinyan as the method of acquisition, delivery during life is effective.
