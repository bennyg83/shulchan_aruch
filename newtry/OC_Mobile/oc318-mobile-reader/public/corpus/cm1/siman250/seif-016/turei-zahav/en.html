The reason is that, since the document states "from now," it has the status of a gift by a healthy person. It is stronger than an ordinary gift of all one's property by a deathly ill person accompanied by a kinyan, where we say that a kinyan cannot take effect after death, because here the acquisition begins today. It appears obvious to me that even if he retracts while still deathly ill, the retraction is ineffective if he later dies, whenever that may be, because this is fully a gift by a healthy person. Thus the first chapter of Bava Metzia states that a gift by a healthy person resembling a gift by a deathly ill person is one written "from today and after death," and plainly no retraction during his lifetime is effective there. Accordingly, the Rama's wording, "and no strengthening clause is required," is imprecise. It implies that without a strengthening clause the law is nevertheless the same as with one, but that is not so. Wherever this siman speaks of a strengthening clause, its effect is that the recipient acquires after death under the law of a gift by a deathly ill person, and the donor may retract during his lifetime. The Beit Yosef likewise wrote in siman 11, citing a responsum of the Ramban, that a strengthening clause does not make the gift effective from now. Here, by contrast, the law is fully that of a healthy person because the document says "from today," and there is no right of retraction. This is clear in my opinion. It further appears to me that if the document says only that he transfers from today, without stating "after death," and he gives the instruction because of impending death, then if he recovers he may retract even though a kinyan was made. We find in seif 3 that even a gift of part of the estate accompanied by a kinyan may be retracted when it was expressly given because of impending death. This is also implied by our master's words in seif 25: "But if he expressly stated that it was because of death, then even if he transferred it through every form of acquisition, he may retract." One should not object that, according to this, whenever a gift by a deathly ill person is accompanied by a kinyan, instead of saying the kinyan weakens it we should say that it was written so that the recipient would acquire from now, since an ordinary kinyan takes effect from now, as the Rama wrote in siman 258, seif 2. Nor should we say, according to the Ritva, that it is as though he wrote "from now and after death." For here, had he intended present effect, he would have stated "from now" explicitly.
