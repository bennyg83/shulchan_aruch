The Sema writes that the Rosh proves this from the rule that even where a dangerously ill person gave all the property known to us, we still suspect that he possesses additional property, and therefore a kinyan is required. If a kinyan was made, he cannot retract, as the Tur and Shulchan Aruch explain in seif 10. Thus, once an effective kinyan has been made, we do not remove the property from the recipient merely because it is uncertain whether the donor owns something more. The same should apply to any declaration to Heaven for consecrated property or charity, and likewise to abandonment, since declaring property ownerless also makes it available to the poor. Such a declaration is treated as though the property had actually been delivered into their possession and is stronger than an ordinary kinyan. Therefore, he should not be able to retract merely because of the possibility that he did not fully resolve to consecrate it under all circumstances. The Mechaber and Rama, however, rule here contrary to the Rosh while in seif 10 they rule that one who gave all his property and had a kinyan made cannot retract. They must therefore hold that the two laws are not analogous. Sema.
To retract. The Sema explains that while he is still a shechiv mera, even after he consecrated the property he may retract the consecration, remove it from sacred status, and give it to another, just as a shechiv mera may retract a gift to one person and give it to another. If he subsequently dies, the latter recipient retains it. See Responsa Maharashdam, simanim 273, 397, and 431, and Sefer Imrei Emet, p. 99a.
