From now. Consider a partial deathbed gift made with a kinyan sudar and the words "from now." The Taz at the end of siman 197 leaves unresolved whether "from now and after thirty days" may be treated as a retraction rather than a condition. Responsa Maharam Padua, responsum 54, likewise writes that when the wording of an instrument shows that it is a command effective because of death, it resembles "and after thirty days," and he discusses the dispute concerning "from now and after thirty days." According to this, the phrase "from now" may not always supply the expected benefit. The Shulchan Aruch, following the Rambam, says that when a dying person writes "from today" he cannot retract. Perhaps that rule applies only where the language does not reveal that he intended the gift to take effect only after death. Even though the case there concerns the whole estate, perhaps the donor intended the transfer to be irrevocable even if he recovered, and in any event an unexpressed thought has no legal effect. Where the text of the will itself shows that the gift is due to death, the problem is greater. The Sema at the end of siman 195 indicates that one should write both "on condition" and "from now," perhaps because otherwise the language may imply retraction. At the end of siman 197 he writes that if the recipient is standing in a public domain at the later time, acquisition may fail. Nevertheless, if the instrument states that it is made in every legally effective manner, there should be no further concern. The dispute whether the wording means a condition or a retraction concerns only ambiguous language. All agree that if the parties expressly make it a condition, it is a condition and not a retraction. Therefore, where treating it as a retraction would invalidate the deathbed instrument, while the document expressly declares itself effective in every valid manner, that language itself indicates that the intended construction is a condition. Maharam Padua, whose ruling is cited by the Rama below in seif 17, likewise treats a clause such as "in every effective manner" as a strengthening of the instrument. Even though he also relied there on an additional clause authorizing every language of reinforcement, the Shulchan Aruch cites the general principle that such wording is itself a form of reinforcement. Siman 60 likewise indicates that language requiring the instrument to operate in the most effective manner can preserve its validity. Thus the will should be construed as containing a valid condition rather than a retraction.
