Vav) This is the language of the Tur, seif 3. Rambam likewise rules there, chapter 9, halachah 9, from the question resolved by the incident of Issur Giyora in Bava Batra 149a. Rambam writes that the deathly ill person also said, "Give it to him." The Maggid Mishneh and Kesef Mishneh explain that without such an instruction the money is not given, because perhaps he made the statement merely so that his children should not appear wealthy. If the admission was made before the claimant, however, this concern does not apply. Even if the claimant demanded payment, there is no need for the dying person to say "You are my witnesses," because a person does not make jesting admissions at the time of death. Even if he did not say "give," the money is given. Tosafot explain that the Gemara's question involved an admission concerning property that was presumed to belong to the dying person, and it concludes that the recipient fully acquires it. Rabbenu Chananel and the Rosh explain similarly.
Zayin) There, chapter 9, halachah 19. The Maggid Mishneh writes that all of these are unresolved questions in Bava Batra 148b. Rambam therefore rules that if the giver recovers, he may retract, because property is not removed from his possession without clear proof.
Chet) There, halachah 20. The Maggid Mishneh writes that this is explicit on 149a.
Tet) There, halachah 21, and the Tur, seif 5. The Gemara resolves the question from a statement of Rav Yehudah in the name of Rav. The Maggid Mishneh writes that Rambam's explanation is correct, as Rabbenu Chananel and the other commentators, including Rashbam, explain. The reason is that when the actual money is still present, the case resembles one who sold his courtyard expressly because he intended to purchase wheat. Even without a formal condition, if he later has no need for the wheat he may retract, as explained above in siman 207.
