But if he acknowledged concerning his property, etc. Meaning, if he said "you are my witnesses," so that we cannot say he spoke merely to avoid appearing wealthy before himself or his sons, as explained in simanim 32 and 81; or if he said "give," as explained in siman 255; or he used another expression that prevents the explanation that he spoke only to avoid appearing wealthy. There is no concern for jesting even if the acknowledgment came in response to a claim, because a person does not jest at the time of death. Even if we know that the property never belonged to the person in whose favor he acknowledged, we nevertheless say that he deliberately expressed his gift in the form of an acknowledgment so that he would be unable to retract it. In the Perishah I proved this and that Tosafos likewise write so. This is unlike the Ir Shushan, who writes that there is no need to say "you are my witnesses" because a person does not jest at the time of death. There still remains the concern that he said it merely to avoid appearing wealthy.
If he consecrated all his property, etc. This is an unresolved question in the Gemara: should we compare it to a gift to another person, where he did not fully intend to transfer until after death, or perhaps since he lies on his sickbed he fully intends to consecrate it now, hoping that the merit of the consecration will stand for him and God will send him healing. The Mechaber rules that the party seeking to extract from the heirs bears the burden of proof.
Unlike the dissenting authorities. In the Perishah I gave a substantial explanation of the dissenting view, namely the Rosh and Tur, with Mordechai agreeing. The Rosh proves it from the rule that even where a shechiv mera gave all the property known to us, we are concerned that he has more property elsewhere, so a kinyan is required. If he makes a kinyan, he cannot retract upon recovery, as the Tur and Mechaber write below in seif 10. Thus once there was a kinyan, we do not remove the property from the recipient even though there is a doubt whether he had additional property, and if he had no other property the law would allow retraction upon recovery despite the kinyan sudar. The same reasoning applies to a declaration to Heaven, meaning consecration or a gift to the poor, and likewise to hefker: the declaration is treated as actual delivery and is even stronger than a kinyan. He therefore cannot retract merely because of a doubt whether he fully intended to consecrate in every circumstance. The Mechaber and Rama, who rule here unlike the Rosh but below in seif 10 rule that if he gave all his property and a kinyan was made he cannot retract, hold that the cases are not comparable. In the Derishah I suggested a basis for distinguishing them.
The law of hekdesh is likewise like that of a gift, so that he may retract, etc. Meaning, while he remains a shechiv mera, after consecrating the property he may retract the consecration, return it to ordinary status, and give it to someone else, just as a shechiv mera may retract from one gift and give it to another. If he then dies, the latter recipient keeps it.
His sale is valid like that of a healthy person. Meaning, if he sold the property through one of the regular modes of acquisition. This is obvious.
If the actual money still exists, etc. Then we are witnesses that he retained the sale proceeds themselves specifically so that, if he recovered, he could return the buyer's money.
