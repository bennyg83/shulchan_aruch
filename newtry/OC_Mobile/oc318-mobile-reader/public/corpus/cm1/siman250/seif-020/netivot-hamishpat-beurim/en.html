The second recipient acquires even without added strengthening language. See Shach, seif katan 27, who explains the case as one where the first recipient received a kinyan and the second did not. This is difficult. Since the first recipient did not acquire, the portion intended for him remains a retained portion of the estate as against the second recipient, just as in seif 12 where he retracted from part and gave that part to another: the second gift also fails because the first recipient's portion counts as a retained remainder. One cannot distinguish by saying that there the donor knowingly retracted from the first gift and therefore knew that portion remained his, whereas here the first gift failed only as a matter of law and perhaps the shechiv mera did not know that his kinyan invalidated the first gift. First, who says he did not know? Perhaps he knew and intended to mock both recipients. Second, Perishah in seif 11 writes that where a person distributes his estate and pauses between one recipient and the next, even the later gift can fail, although the first gift's defect is only legal. Unless one wishes to say that the rule that a partial shechiv mera gift without the proper form is more widely known than the rule concerning a gift of the whole estate made with kinyan, there is no meaningful distinction. Therefore, in my view this ruling has no sound basis. The gifts to both recipients are void.
