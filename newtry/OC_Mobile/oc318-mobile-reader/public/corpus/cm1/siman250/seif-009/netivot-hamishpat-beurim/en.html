And he explains that he gives everything from now and transfers it during his lifetime. The phrase "and transfers it during his lifetime" appears redundant. It seems Mechaber intends the rule Beit Yosef cites from Ritva: if the donor explicitly stipulates that the gift shall remain permanently effective and makes a kinyan from now, it has the law of a healthy-person gift. Ritva's language shows that a kinyan "from now" alone is insufficient unless the donor expressly states in a manner showing that he intends an immediate healthy-person gift. There is proof. Nimukei Yosef, who is a principal source for this law, writes that if the donor recovers he may retract even where he said "from today"; only while he remains ill may he be unable to retract. Tosafot in Gittin 9 likewise explain a case as saying "from today," and the Gemara says specifically with a slave he cannot retract because the slave has already acquired the public status of a free man. This implies that in an ordinary monetary gift he may retract upon recovery. Beit Yosef's view is only that during the illness he cannot retract. The poskim therefore require him to state explicitly that he is giving as a healthy person, so that even if he recovers he cannot retract. I would add that there are circumstances where even during the illness he can retract although he said "from today." Gittin 72 says concerning a get, "his get is like his gift," showing that the same general presumptions apply. Gittin 73 says that even if he says, "from today if I die," it is treated as though he said, "from the final moment that I am alive." Tosafot explain that a shechiv mera delays the get as long as possible. Beit Yosef in Even HaEzer siman 148 says the same applies even with "from now." Why should a gift differ? Ordinarily, a shechiv mera gift can take effect after death, so if he says "from today" there appears to be no reason for those words unless he truly intends some immediate effect. This supports the view that "from today" in a gift is meaningful. But there are important practical differences between a shechiv mera gift taking effect after death and a healthy-person gift taking effect a moment before death. For example, a widow's or daughters' maintenance can be collected from a shechiv mera gift but not from a healthy-person gift effective before death. Likewise, in "after you" arrangements a shechiv mera gift can have different priority consequences. Where such practical differences exist, the donor may have said "from today" only to secure those particular advantages while still intending to delay full ownership until the final moment of life. In such a case even while ill he should be able to retract unless he expressly says that he transfers it during his lifetime as a present healthy-person gift. This, in my view, is Mechaber's intent in adding "and transfers it during his lifetime." He requires an express indication like Ritva's that the donor is making a true present gift. Rama's addition should likewise be read against Mechaber's wording. Rama does not state a general rule that any "from today" gift is irrevocable. He is speaking where the document itself contains clear language of a healthy-person transfer. Therefore the practical rule is: If a shechiv mera merely says "from today," he may retract if he recovers, as Tosafot and Nimukei Yosef write. During the illness he ordinarily cannot retract where no other consequences indicate a delayed intention. Where the special consequences of a shechiv mera gift matter, even during the illness there is room for retraction unless he explicitly states that the property is transferred immediately during his lifetime. If the gift consists of debts that cannot be acquired by kinyan sudar in a healthy-person gift, they are not acquired merely because he said "from today." But if the deed contains every strengthening formulation of transfer and makes clear that the shechiv mera intended both present legal force and the shechiv mera mechanism, the gift can be treated as having both advantages and the debts can be acquired upon death if he did not retract. Maharit, Choshen Mishpat siman 50, explains this.
