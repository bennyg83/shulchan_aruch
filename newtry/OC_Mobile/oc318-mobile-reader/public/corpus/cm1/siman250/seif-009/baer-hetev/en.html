When is this said. The Sema writes that the Mechaber repeats this rule here after stating it in seif 7 because two new points are taught. First, if the giver expressly says that the partial gift is a 'gift of a person dangerously ill,' it is as though he expressly says that it is given because of death. Second, this is the place to add the concluding rule, 'Likewise, if he specifies that he gives everything from now...' See Responsa Maharashdam, simanim 265 and 313, and Responsa Rama, beginning of siman 48.

And some say. The Mechaber speaks where we personally heard the person dangerously ill command that the gift take effect 'from now.' The Rama adds that even if we did not hear this from him but the will before us contains such language, we do not say that it was merely a scribal error inserted by the scribe while the testator intended the ordinary deathbed form effective only at death. This excludes what the Beit Yosef wrote, cited by the Darkei Moshe: If a will states that he gives 'as the gift of a healthy person,' it has the law of a healthy person's gift. This is simple in my view. I mention it only because I saw a responsum of the Beit Yosef ruling that he acquires in such a case because we attribute the phrase to scribal error. I answered him on this point. Our respective arguments came before Maharam Padua, who agreed with me that the recipient does not acquire, as the proofs in the responsa on this matter demonstrate.
