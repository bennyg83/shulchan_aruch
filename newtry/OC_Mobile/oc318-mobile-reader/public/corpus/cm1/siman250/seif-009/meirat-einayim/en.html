When do we say that an unstated partial gift of a shechiv mera requires a kinyan, etc. The Mechaber already wrote this law in seif 7, following the Tur in seif 11. Here he repeats it from Rambam, whose formulation the Tur also cites in seif 14. He repeats it because two points are added here. First, if the giver expressly says that the partial gift is to have the status of a shechiv mera gift, this is equivalent to expressly saying that he gives because of death. Second, it introduces the next law, "likewise, if he expressly gives everything from now, etc."
If it contains a kinyan, he does not acquire, etc. This too is written by the Tur and Mechaber below at the end of seif 17, even regarding one who instructs because of death.
As an enhancement of his power. See below for the reason and meaning of this enhancement.
Some say the same applies to a shechiv mera gift in whose document it is written, etc. The Mechaber speaks of a case where we actually heard the shechiv mera instruct that the gift take effect "from now." Rama adds that the same applies even if we did not hear this from him, but the will presented to us contains those words. We do not say that it is a scribal error, inserted by the scribe on his own, while the shechiv mera intended the transfer to take effect only if he died, like ordinary shechiv mera wills.
We do not say that this is a scribal error. Rama writes this to exclude what the Beit Yosef wrote, cited in Darkei Moshe: "If the will states that he gives it as the gift of a healthy person, it has the law of a healthy person's gift. This is obvious in my view, and I write it only because I saw a responsum of the author of Beit Yosef who ruled that in such a case he acquires because we attribute the wording to a scribal error. I responded to him on this point. Our two positions were presented to Maharam Padua, who agreed with me that he does not acquire, as the proofs in the responsa on this matter show."
