And the witnesses write. Sema explains that because the law distinguishes different formulations, witnesses must be careful not to record that the testator said, “Write and give,” when he used language carrying a different legal effect. Nachalat Shivah, siman 37, rejected an old testamentary form stating that the testator “asked us to receive his will and to write and sign it,” because such wording may require an express strengthening of the beneficiary's power. He further argues that even where such strengthening is present, Shulchan Aruch and Sema imply that it helps only if the instrument was written during life, whereas the old form itself ended by stating that the testator died of that illness, apparently indicating that it was written afterward. Chatam Sofer, Choshen Mishpat siman 138, respectfully disagrees. In his view, additional language of empowerment is required only where the testator says “write and give to so-and-so” or “give and write to so-and-so.” If he merely says, “Hear my words and write and sign,” without saying that the writing is to be delivered to a beneficiary, his intent is that the witnesses create a memorandum for themselves, not that the document itself serve as the instrument of acquisition. Chatam Sofer uses this to reconcile apparently inconsistent passages in the Tur. Pitchei Teshuva notes, however, that Chatam Sofer's own responsum 121, cited above, seems at first glance difficult under this distinction, because the document there likewise did not expressly say “to so-and-so,” yet he invalidated it if signed after death. The matter requires study.
