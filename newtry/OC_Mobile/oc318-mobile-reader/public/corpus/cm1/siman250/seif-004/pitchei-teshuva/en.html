Panim Meirot, part II, siman 133, discusses an earlier will made while the testator was healthy and framed as gifts of a healthy person. Such gifts ordinarily require a kinyan even if distribution is to occur after death. Although siman 258 rules that a dated gift instrument saying that a person shall acquire a field after death may be effective because the date proves a lifetime conveyance, here there was still reason to doubt whether the document had been delivered through the third party as a present acquisition or merely deposited with him so that the donor could still retract. The decisive fact, however, was that several years later, while dangerously ill, the testator again instructed the scribe to write all those same provisions, with certain additions, word for word. That later act was a gift of a dying person of his entire estate and required no kinyan. The fact that the earlier text described the transfers as gifts of a healthy person did not diminish the later deathbed disposition, just as an ineffective earlier gift does not prevent a later valid conveyance of the same field. Even if one insists that the scribe was told to copy the earlier language exactly and therefore retained the phrase “gift of a healthy person,” the evident intent was to confirm the substantive gifts, not to insist on a technically ineffective formula. Where the donor's intent to make a real gift is clear and only the legal phraseology is mistaken, the wording may be treated as a scribal or legal error. In this case, moreover, the testator was dangerously ill with a stroke from which almost no one survived and physicians expected his death. He therefore qualified as one commanding because of impending death, whose gift requires no kinyan even if only partial. Panim Meirot concludes that the later will was fully effective as the words of a dying person, which are treated as written and delivered.
