And with a gift of a healthy person. See Baer Heitev and Rabbi Akiva Eiger, responsum 146. The rule here applies specifically where the document was delivered to a third party. But where an instrument of conveyance is found in the donor's own possession and there are no witnesses before us that he actually made the kinyan, the document has no effect. The same applies to a document of dedication found in his possession. Mabit, part III, siman 99, expressly rules that where a writing is found among a man's papers stating that he dedicated specified property, nothing is extracted from the heirs. Orach Chaim siman 153 seif 19 cites an opinion that where, after a person's death, a writing is found concerning a dedication, it may be effective; Rabbi Akiva Eiger suggests that this concerns a document written in the donor's own hand, where it is less plausible to say that a scribe merely happened to prepare an unused draft. A writing prepared by someone else is different. If the donor was a dying person, however, even a writing in his own hand may have been only a draft prepared in anticipation that his strength would fail before he could later formalize the gift. See there further, and VeShav HaKohen, siman 8, for an extended discussion.
