There is no distinction in a deathbed gift of all one's property between giving it to one person and giving it to two people together. Therefore, if a person on his deathbed distributed all his property among two or three recipients without pausing between one and the next, but said, "This to so-and-so and this to so-and-so," and then died, they all acquire even without a kinyan. If he recovers, he may retract from all of them even if a kinyan was made, because the entire distribution is regarded as one gift of all his property without retaining anything. If, however, he paused between one recipient and the next in a way that shows he reconsidered after the earlier gifts, then all the earlier gifts, except the last one, are treated as partial deathbed gifts and require a kinyan even if he dies. Therefore, if he recovers he cannot retract from them, but if no kinyan was made they do not take effect even if he dies. The final gift is treated as a deathbed gift of all remaining property, and if he dies it takes effect even without a kinyan; consequently, if he recovers he may retract from it.
