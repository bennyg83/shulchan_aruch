Their statements are of no effect. See siman 66, where Rama writes that the defendant may swear that the donor did not own the property at the relevant time, and we do not say “since it is here now, it must have been here then.” Here there is room to distinguish because the defendant himself is alive and makes a definite claim that the donor did not own it then. A harder question is from siman 112, where the Mechaber records a dispute and Rama prefers the view that the recipient bears the burden of proving that the donor owned the property at the time of the gift. Some distinguish land from movables, arguing that ownership of land would have public notice, but that is strained. A better distinction is that the presumption “it was found here, therefore it was here before” applies most naturally to movables now found in the donor's own house. Their present location supports an inference that he acquired and brought them there before the gift. Land situated elsewhere is not “in his domain” in the same factual sense. Ownership must be established by deed or witnesses.
