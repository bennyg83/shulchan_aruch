This is difficult, because in siman 112, seif 3, the Mechaber records a dispute on this point, and the Rama writes there that the first view, which places the burden of proof on the recipient, appears primary. Here he writes nothing. Some wish to distinguish by saying that here the donor gave movable property, whereas there the subject is land, because if he had owned additional land at that time it would have become publicly known. This is forced. It appears more correct that the principle, 'since the items are found here, they are presumed to have been here previously,' applies here only to movable property presently found in his house and under his control. In that setting one may properly infer from the fact that they are now in his possession that he had already acquired them and brought them into his home at the time of the gift. By contrast, land located elsewhere cannot be called 'in his possession' for this purpose unless witnesses or a deed establish that he acquired it. See above, siman 112, for what I wrote further on this point. So writes the Sema. See also the Bach and Responsa Maharit, simanim 39 and 128, end of p. 170.
