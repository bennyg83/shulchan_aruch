Seif 4. These rules apply only where one gives all his property and leaves nothing for himself. Note: I was uncertain whether leaving even a small parcel of land counts as a reservation when that land is already encumbered to a creditor. At first glance, Even HaEzer siman 141, seif 6, implies that even a dinar encumbered to a creditor counts as a remaining dinar, so perhaps the same applies here. Yet there the reason may be that repayment of a creditor is itself a mitzvah, whereas a gift of a dying person depends upon the assessment that he did not intend to leave himself with nothing. If the retained property is fully encumbered, perhaps he has in fact left himself nothing. On the other hand, one may say that the encumbered asset still counts as his because he could appease the creditor and obtain forgiveness, analogous to the rule that one who pays another's debt may be exempt because the debtor can say, "I could have appeased him and he might have forgiven me." If so, the retained encumbered property may indeed count as a reservation. The practical ruling requires study.
