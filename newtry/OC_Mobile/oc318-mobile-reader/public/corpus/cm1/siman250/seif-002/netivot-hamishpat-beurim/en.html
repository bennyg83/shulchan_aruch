The gift is void. See Shach, seif katan 1, who explains the case of R. Huna as one where the property was delivered to hekdesh to become consecrated from today. This is difficult. If he expressly said "from today," it would be the consecration of a healthy person and delivery would be unnecessary. Rashba's words are understandable without that. Even if he did not say "from today," once he physically delivered the property, although he can still retract if he recovers from the illness, during the illness he certainly cannot retract, as explained at the end of siman 125. Bava Batra speaks of one who acted while still ill. See what I wrote in siman 258. Sema, seif katan 5, writes concerning waiver of a debt that if the shechiv mera recovers he can retract, because waiver of a debt is no stronger than a kinyan regarding other property. There is room to question whether he can retract during the illness. Perhaps waiver is like actual delivery of the gift, in which case he cannot retract during the illness. I later found Ketzot HaChoshen stating simply that he cannot retract during the illness. Still, Sema's language in siman 128, seif katan 34, is significant. He writes that there the donor did not remove money from his own hand at the time of the instruction. This implies that actual removal of the property from his possession is necessary. Without it, even during the illness he may retract. This is reasonable. Every shechiv mera gift rests on the presumption that the donor intends it to take effect only at the completion of death. Only when he physically removes the property from his possession and places it in another's domain do we have evidence that he intended an immediate transfer. With a debt that was already in another person's hand before the instruction, there is no such new evidence. Therefore the ordinary presumption is not displaced merely by verbal waiver.
