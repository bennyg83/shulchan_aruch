Automatically. This follows from Bava Batra 153a, the incident of that gift, etc. If the rule depended on explicit retraction, then even if he died and was buried, etc., he would have retracted. Yet we rule there 151a that any deathly ill person who recovers, etc. In Gittin 72b to 73a Rav Huna says, "his get is like his gift, etc." Regarding a get the Mishnah there states that it is not a get, and there Rabbi Elazar says regarding a deathly ill person who recovered, etc. If explicit retraction were required, he could retract even while remaining in the same illness. Likewise, the Mishnah in Bava Batra 146b, "if he retained nothing, etc., his gift is not valid," implies that it lapses automatically. See Tosafos there 153a, s.v. "velo, etc.," and Gittin there, s.v. "amar Rav Huna, etc."
Even if he stipulated, etc. See Sema. A proof is the incident at 151a involving his sister, etc., where he did not make such a stipulation with her.
Unless, etc. Rivash writes that since his intent was to convey permanently, it is like a gift of a healthy person. His words require study, for we should still assess his presumed intent, as in the incident cited above.
Although he walked, etc. The Mishnah says "he walked in the marketplace." Likewise the verse states, "if he rises and walks outside."
(Collected note) If he walked in the marketplace without, etc. This is Rav Huna's law in Gittin there. See Tosafos there, s.v. "amar Rav Huna, etc.," in the name of Rabbeinu Tam. Rabbah and Rava disagree with him only regarding a get, because there the Gemara says it is a decree, etc. Rav Huna says "like his gift," showing that with a gift the rule is clear that he retracts in such a case.
