For all of his possessions as a gift, he seems to have a brother-in-law and wish him his debt at all, and he is appointed as a result of Hashem’s forgiveness in other things:
Even if a kinyan was made from him, if he recovers, etc. The novelty is that although a kinyan was made, the gift of a dangerously ill person who gave all his property is still revocable upon recovery. One might think the kinyan makes the transaction like a healthy person's irrevocable gift. The ruling teaches otherwise. Since he distributed all his property, we are witnesses that his intention in making the kinyan was that it take effect only if he dies from the illness. If he recovers, he retracts. This differs where he did not give all his property, as explained immediately below.
And even if he stipulated, etc. The Sages treated the words of a dangerously ill person as written and delivered only for the case in which he dies, so that his mind will not become agitated. They did not create a power enabling him to make the gift irrevocable even if he recovers. If he wishes to do that, he must make an explicit complete kinyan effective in that manner, in which case it is like any ordinary gift of a healthy person.
