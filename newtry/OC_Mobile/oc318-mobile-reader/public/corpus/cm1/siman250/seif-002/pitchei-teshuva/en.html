But if he leaves anything over. Noda BiYehudah, second edition, siman 45, discusses a will in which a sum of seventy-five gold coins was set aside for a female relative. Because the witness concerning that gift was related, she could not establish her claim. Nevertheless, the final residuary beneficiary, to whom all remaining estate was given, did not acquire those seventy-five either, because the will expressly said that he should take the residue “apart from all the inheritances and gifts mentioned above.” Thus the seventy-five remained with no designated recipient, apparently turning the other dispositions into partial deathbed gifts that would require a kinyan. There was an additional problem: twenty-five gold coins that the testatrix directed her Torah heir to “inherit” were not a gift at all but inheritance. If so, she necessarily left that amount within the hereditary estate, again seemingly making all other gifts partial gifts requiring a kinyan. Noda BiYehudah then examined the text and found that the testatrix had opened wisely by declaring, “I make this will as one commanding because of impending death.” In that form, even a partial gift does not require a kinyan, as ruled in siman 250 seif 7.
