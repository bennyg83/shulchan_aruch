The gift is void, etc. This applies even if the gift reached the recipient before the donor recovered, as appears below, seif 4, in the gloss. It is likewise clear from the Tur and the Mechaber above, at the end of siman 125, and from the Tur and Rambam in chapter 9 of the Laws of Acquisition and Gift, which I cite below at the end of siman 251. Accordingly, when the Rashba writes in Responsa Rabbi Betzalel, siman 15, page 50d, that the ruling of Rav Huna at the end of the chapter Get Pashut, Bava Batra 174b, concerning a dangerously ill person who consecrated property, refers to a case where he delivered the gift into the possession of the Temple treasury, his meaning must be either that he delivered it so the gift would take effect immediately, or that since the question concerning a dangerously ill person who consecrated property in the chapter Mi SheMet remained unresolved, he could not retract and remove the property from the Temple treasury on account of the doubt. See there and examine carefully. See Responsa Mabit, part 1, second collection, siman 83, page 139.
