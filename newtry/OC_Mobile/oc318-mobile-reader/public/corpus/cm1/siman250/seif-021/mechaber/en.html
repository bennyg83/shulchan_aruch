The rule that when a person on his deathbed orders both that a gift be given and that a deed be written for the recipient, we are concerned that he intended acquisition to occur only through the deed, applies in that form of instruction. But when a person on his deathbed arranges his affairs, completes his will, and then orders that it be written, the writing is presumed merely for memorial and evidentiary purposes, and he intended the recipient to acquire immediately. No additional language strengthening the gift is required. Rama: It was already explained in seif 19 that the will must reach the recipient during the giver's lifetime. A will containing a kinyan is like any other deed of gift and requires language strengthening the recipient's power. In a place where the custom is that everyone makes a kinyan in a will merely as a formality and does not intend acquisition to depend on the deed, no such strengthening language is required. Rivash, siman 168. One who asks his heirs to do such and such with his property is considered to have made a deathbed instruction, because the fact that he phrased it as a request was only to reinforce his wishes. Piskei Mahara'i, siman 99.
