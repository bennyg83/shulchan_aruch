(Seif 25: It is ineffective.) The Rashbam explains that even if the recipient took possession after the donor's death by means of that document, he did not acquire. The document is no stronger than the donor's testament, which itself is treated as though written and delivered, because the donor did not fully resolve to transfer ownership except through receipt of the document, and a document cannot take effect after death. This implies that even a gift of part of the estate accompanied by a kinyan is not acquired so long as the document did not reach the recipient. The Nimukei Yosef likewise rules in the first chapter of Bava Metzia, from the mishna concerning one who found a promissory note containing a lien on property, in the names of the Rif and Rambam. Our master, however, concludes here in the name of the Rosh that in a gift by a healthy person, once the recipient acquired by kinyan, he has acquired, as the Rama writes below. The Maggid Mishneh likewise writes this even according to the Rambam, whose words the Mechaber copied here. Consequently, the statement here, "although it is witnessed and they made a kinyan from him," must refer to a gift of the entire estate by a deathly ill person. A partial gift is treated as a gift by a healthy person, as the Mechaber explicitly wrote in presenting the Rambam's view. Nevertheless, our master had to explain that the law concerning a found testamentary deed applies where the donor used wording permitting retraction, namely, "from today if I do not retract." For even according to our master it may concern a partial gift, which requires a kinyan. Since a kinyan was made, had he not used wording preserving a right of retraction, the recipient would have acquired from the time of the kinyan even though the document never reached him. Thus this seif concerns two cases: either a gift of the entire estate by a deathly ill person, or a partial gift in which he expressly reserved the right to retract. It is also clear from our master's words that once the document reached the recipient during the donor's lifetime, we no longer suspect that the donor retracted, for had he done so he would have informed him. This appears to be the reason.
