He arranges his affairs and his testament, and instructs that it be written. The same should seemingly apply if he instructed them to write and deliver, as Sema, seif katan 60, writes. It is difficult, however, to define the distinction. If he gave the instruction to write and deliver only after the interval of toch kedei dibbur, seif 22 already teaches that the earlier gift is not canceled. The present case must therefore concern an instruction given within toch kedei dibbur of the gift. If so, when does a testamentary instruction to write suffice even though the document did not reach the recipient during the donor's lifetime, and when must the document specifically reach him before death? Perhaps the distinction is this. If he distributes to several people and, after each individual gift, says, "Write and give that recipient the deed," the deed must reach that recipient during the donor's lifetime. But if he first completes the entire series of dispositions and only afterward says, "Write and give each of them a deed," the gifts are effective even if the documents did not reach them during his lifetime. Still, it appears more likely that the word "deliver" in Sema is a scribal error. The true distinction is between merely instructing that the testament be written and instructing that the deed also be delivered.
