The apparent property being all his property, etc. Meaning, even though we know of no other property belonging to him, we nevertheless say that he may possess property elsewhere. It is therefore treated as a partial gift of a shechiv mera, and according to all opinions, if a kinyan was made and he recovered he cannot retract. But if he died without a kinyan and the heirs seek to retract on the ground that it was a partial shechiv mera gift without a kinyan, the Maggid Mishneh writes in Rambam's name that we do not listen to them to diminish the recipients' rights. The Rosh and Tur, however, write that in every respect we treat it as a partial shechiv mera gift. Rama intended this when he added the two words "and it requires a kinyan" to the Mechaber's wording, which follows Rambam. He means that if no kinyan was made and the giver died, the heirs may retract. See the Perishah.
We are concerned, etc. This is the Gemara's language. We find the word "concern" used both for leniency and stringency, as when we are concerned that a person may have lodged outside the wall. See the Perishah. See also what I wrote in seif 3 concerning the Rosh's comparison to one who consecrates his property while a shechiv mera and the Mechaber's distinction between the cases.
Until he says, "All my property consists of these." Meaning, it is not only where he says without qualification, "I give you all my property," which plainly includes whatever property he has anywhere. Even if he merely says, "I give you all my property, namely these," and proceeds to enumerate his known fields here, once he used comprehensive language we do not suspect that he has other property elsewhere. See the Perishah.
Or he is established as having no other property. This is the Gemara's wording. The Tur writes that Rashbam explains it to mean that witnesses establish that he has no more property, while Rabbeinu Yitzchak holds that an ordinary chazakah suffices. The Mechaber's unqualified wording implies that he likewise follows Rabbeinu Yitzchak. Moreover, otherwise the preceding clause, where saying "all my property consists of these" suffices, would already cover the stronger case and make this clause unnecessary.
