[Be’er HaGolah, subparagraph 9] This is like one who sold his courtyard. Note: At first glance, this can be explained as Rashba writes in siman 158, that the reason there is the evidentiary force of the fact that he performed a kinyan. According to this, one can argue as a practical matter that where a person consecrated property in circumstances making clear that it was all of his property, we do not suspect that perhaps he has other property overseas and therefore can retract. This case is weaker than an ordinary kinyan to a private person, because although we say that a declaration to the sacred domain is like a kinyan, it lacks the evidentiary implication created by an actual kinyan, and therefore we should not remove property on the basis of doubt. Nevertheless, one can argue that there is a double doubt: perhaps even one who consecrates all his property intends a complete transfer, and perhaps he has other property overseas. The practical ruling requires study.
