(Seif 13: If he specified that it should be, etc.) This resolves the Tur's objection that a partial gift by a deathly ill person requires an act of acquisition, in which case he is treated as healthy in every respect. Rabbenu therefore explains that the case involves no act of acquisition, yet the gift is nevertheless effective because he expressly stated his intent, and for that reason he can retract. The Sema asks from the end of siman 125, but his objection is puzzling, because that case does not concern delivery to an agent, but a written instrument alone. If he means to challenge the Mechaber's necessary implication that even writing and transferring possession through another person are ineffective, his answer is even more difficult. He says the case is one where the donor told the agent not to deliver until after death. How, then, can the ruling continue, "But if he wrote, transferred possession, and they made an act of acquisition from him, nothing remains after that act of acquisition"? Where the donor expressly directs that delivery occur only after death, the gift is equivalent to one given because of impending death, for which neither an act of acquisition nor transfer of possession is effective, as stated in seif 14. Nevertheless, the wording of the earlier editions, followed by the Mechaber, requires study in light of the end of siman 125. There we say that even, "Bring it to so-and-so," said by a deathly ill person is equivalent to, "Acquire it for him," and the donor cannot retract, whereas here he expressly said, "Acquire it for so-and-so," and also wrote an instrument, yet it is ineffective.
