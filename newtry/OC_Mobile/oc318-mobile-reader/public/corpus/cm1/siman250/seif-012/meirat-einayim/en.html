Even if he gave to two people in one document, etc. Meaning, we do not say that the force of the document is strong enough that his later retraction from the second recipient cannot reveal a retraction from the first as well. He carefully writes "in one document," because with two separate documents the retraction from the second would not constitute retraction from the first, who has his own document. This is implied by the Nimukei Yosef.
And gave that retained portion to others, etc. The Nimukei Yosef writes that when he later gives the retained portion to others, he ultimately reveals that he intended to dispose of all his property. The first recipient therefore acquired the initial portion even without a kinyan, because from the outset it was a gift of all the shechiv mera's property. But if he retained that portion for himself, he revealed that he did not give the first portion as a shechiv mera gift, but rather as the gift of a healthy person. The first recipient therefore cannot acquire without a kinyan.
The gifts of the others are not void, etc. It appears that for this reason they have the law of partial shechiv mera gifts and require a kinyan. Yet one can say that even here it helps that he originally distributed all his property between the recipients and his heirs, making the initial disposition a gift of all his property. We have already explained that where a full-property gift came first, if he later retracts from part but explicitly does not retract from the rest, the first recipient acquires his portion without a kinyan. Here too, when he later retracts from the portion given to his sons or heirs, we say that retraction from his sons and heirs is not treated as retraction from the other gifts. If after retracting from his sons he retained that portion for himself, however, it seems obvious that the first recipients would need a kinyan as well. This requires study.
The second has the law of a partial gift of a shechiv mera. Because the giver retained for himself the portion from which he retracted from the first recipient and did not give it to the second.
The first acquires even if the giver does not die. In the Perishah I wrote that it appears that even if he did not interrupt between the first and second gifts, the first nevertheless acquires even if the giver recovers, because the first gift was made with a kinyan and is not treated as a gift of all property from which he could retract despite the kinyan. Since he described the second gift as "the remainder," the first is treated as a partial shechiv mera gift. Likewise, if he transferred the first gift by kinyan and the second without a kinyan, this itself reveals that he intended the first as a partial gift and the second as a gift of all the remaining property.
