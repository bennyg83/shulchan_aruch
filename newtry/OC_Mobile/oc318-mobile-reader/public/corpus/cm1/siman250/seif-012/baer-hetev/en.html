In one deed. Meaning, we do not say that the force of a written deed is so strong that his retraction from the second gift does not reveal that he also retracted the first. The text carefully says 'in one deed.' If they were in two deeds, retraction from the second would not affect the first because the first has an independent deed. Nimukei Yosef makes this clear. Ibid.
To others. Nimukei Yosef explains that when he gave the remainder to others, he ultimately revealed that he wanted to give away all his property. Therefore the first partial gift is effective without a kinyan, since from the outset the overall disposition was a gift of all property by a person dangerously ill. But if he reserved that remaining portion for himself, he revealed that the first gift was not made as a deathbed gift of all property but as a healthy person's gift, and then the first recipient cannot acquire without a kinyan. Ibid.
Acquires. It appears that even if he did not pause between the first and second gifts, the first recipient nevertheless acquires because that gift was made with a kinyan. It is not treated as a gift of all property by a person dangerously ill, from which the giver may retract even after a kinyan, because by using the language 'the remainder' for the second recipient, he characterizes the first gift as a partial deathbed gift. Thus far the Sema. The Shach writes that the Bach disagrees and is correct.
Kinyan. Meaning, all the more so if the kinyan was made with language strengthening the recipient's power. A bare kinyan can actually weaken a deathbed gift, because there is no kinyan after death. This is explained in the Maggid Mishneh cited by the Beit Yosef. Shach.
