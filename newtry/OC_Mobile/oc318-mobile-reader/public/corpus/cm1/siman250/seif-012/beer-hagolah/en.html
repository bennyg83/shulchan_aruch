Yod) There, seif 8, from an unresolved question in the Gemara that was resolved.
Kaf) Terumat HaDeshen, rule 13, seif 15, discusses Reuven, who held books belonging to Shimon. Shimon gave all his property to his daughter as a gift of a deathly ill person, while Reuven claimed that Shimon had instructed him to consecrate the books. The ruling was that the daughter acquired the books. See Sema in the name of Darkei Moshe.
Lamed) The baraita there and the Gemara's conclusion establish that a partial retraction is a retraction. The first clause is explained so that whether the giver dies or recovers, the first recipient does not acquire, because retracting part of the gift retracts the entire gift. The second recipient acquires even if the giver recovers, because this is a partial gift of a deathly ill person accompanied by a kinyan. The latter clause applies only where the giver recovers, for if he dies the second recipient also acquires. Rambam writes similarly in chapter 9, halachot 17 and 18, except that in the first clause he writes that all the property was given to the first recipient and a kinyan was made. The Maggid Mishneh explains that the law applies even though a kinyan was made, as explicit in Shmuel's statement on Bava Batra 151b.
