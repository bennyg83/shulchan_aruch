A gift document tied, etc. Meaning, a document of a shechiv mera's gift. This is the Tur's wording from chapter Yesh Nochalin. Rashbam explains that the Mishnah says it was tied to his thigh to teach that even where there is no concern that someone else wrote it and placed it there in order to acquire the dead man's property, nevertheless they do not deliver it to the named recipient.
And a kinyan was made from him to enhance, etc. Meaning, the kinyan written in the document enhanced the recipient's power, stating that this kinyan was in addition to the gift. Without such enhancement, it would obviously not be delivered, because he revealed that he wanted the recipient to acquire through the kinyan and there is no kinyan after death, as Rashbam explains. No enhancement is needed with respect to the mere writing, however. Such enhancement is required only where he orally said "give" and also instructed them to write a document. If instead he himself wrote in the document, "My field is given to so-and-so," as Rama writes in seif 19, no separate enhancement of the writing is needed.
All the matters in it are effective. The Maggid Mishneh, at the end of chapter 9 of Hilchot Zechiyah, comments on Rambam's identical wording that Rambam appears to interpret the Mishnah's phrase "his words are effective" to mean that if he conferred possession upon one recipient, all the other gifts are likewise effective. For example, if the will gave to three or four people and he delivered the document to one, the gifts to the others are effective as well because he thereby revealed that he wants the will carried out. The Maggid Mishneh says he did not see other commentators explain the Mishnah this way. The Tur cites two interpretations in the names of Rashbam and Raavad, neither of which is Rambam's. Rambam's explanation nonetheless shows that when the Mishnah says "if he conferred possession through another," Rambam understands that the named recipient himself is the one to whom the document is delivered, following Rashbam rather than the Raavad, who interprets it as conferral through this person to another. The words "whether from the heirs or one who is not among the heirs" could mean that the novelty is that even if the person to whom he delivered the document is one of the heirs, his delivery to that heir reveals intent that the gifts to the others also take effect, which accords somewhat with the Raavad's approach. Alternatively, Rambam may mean that even if the one to whom the document was given is not an heir, he himself acquires by receiving it, which accords with Rashbam. See the Perishah.
Likewise, one who wrote a promissory note, etc. Just as we are concerned in the shechiv mera gift above that perhaps he wrote it and reconsidered, we are likewise concerned with this promissory note that perhaps he wrote it intending to borrow but never borrowed. Therefore it is not delivered to the lender named in the note. The same applies to a sale document: perhaps he wrote it intending to sell but never sold.
But with the gift of a healthy person accompanied by a kinyan, etc. Meaning, if such a document is found in a third party's hands, it is delivered to the recipient. This is from a Responsum Rosh cited by the Tur at the end of this siman. The reason is that the recipient acquired immediately at the time of the kinyan, and the document serves only as evidence. Although Rabbeinu Yitzchak and the Rosh hold that a person's witnesses acquire for him through their signatures, as the Tur writes nearby and at the end of siman 39, that applies specifically where the document eventually comes into the lender's or purchaser's hands with the debtor's or seller's consent. Here the giver has already died.
