And likewise one who wrote a promissory note, etc. Regarding the gift of a healthy person, etc., the Sema writes that in the case of a promissory note we are concerned that perhaps he wrote it intending to borrow but did not borrow, and therefore it is not given to the lender named in the note. The same applies to a bill of sale, for perhaps he wrote it intending to sell but did not sell. His words imply that even a promissory note or bill of sale containing an act of acquisition is subject to this concern, since he distinguishes between a promissory note and sale document on the one hand and a deed of gift on the other. He states this explicitly in siman 243, seif katan 23. The Bach likewise writes here that the Rosh refers to a case where it is known that the transfer was made without specification, and therefore with a promissory note we are still concerned that perhaps he never borrowed, whereas with a gift the recipient ordinarily acquires. In truth, however, this is incorrect. The Gemara and all the poskim explicitly indicate that where a loan document or bill of sale contains an act of acquisition, we are not concerned that perhaps he wrote it intending to borrow but did not borrow, or intending to sell but did not sell, because from the time of the act of acquisition he obligated himself. This is as explained above, at the end of siman 39 and the beginning of siman 38. There is no distinction in this regard between a promissory note, a bill of sale, and a deed of gift, as is explicit in the first chapter of Bava Metzia and throughout the poskim. It is therefore clear that a bill of sale is also returned to the recipient just like a deed of gift. The Rosh, in responsum, principle 66, siman 3, mentions a deed of gift only because that was the actual case before him. When he explains the Rambam as applying specifically to a promissory note, he means one without an act of acquisition, in accordance with the Rosh's view that follows Abaye, that a person's witnesses acquire on his behalf through their signatures, and therefore one must be concerned that perhaps he wrote the document intending to borrow but did not borrow when the document contains no act of acquisition. According to the Rambam, who rules unlike Abaye, the distinction may instead be that a promissory note is different because we are concerned that it was repaid, as stated explicitly in the first chapter of Bava Metzia 13a. The Rosh abbreviated his explanation of the Rambam, but the practical law is clear as I have written. This appears correct to me. The Ir Shushan likewise appears to follow the Sema, for he writes that this applies specifically to a promissory note, because a note may be written for a borrower even when the lender is not present, and perhaps he wrote it intending to borrow but did not borrow. He then distinguishes the gift of a healthy person made through an act of acquisition. This implies that he speaks even of a promissory note containing an act of acquisition. Moreover, without an act of acquisition, the Ir Shushan himself writes at the end of siman 39 that a document may not be written for a borrower when the lender is absent. He therefore should not have written that perhaps he wrote it intending to borrow but did not borrow. With a promissory note containing an act of acquisition there is no such concern, as he himself writes at the end of siman 39. Rather, according to the Rambam, the Mechaber, and those who rule unlike Abaye, the concern here is a different one, namely repayment, as I have explained. Examine carefully.
And regarding the gift of a healthy person, etc. See above, siman 56, seif 5. This requires study, because it is explicit here that even though it is known that the donor delivered the document to the third party, the recipient acquires. This is also explicit in Responsa Rosh, principle 66, siman 3. The reason is that from the time of the act of acquisition the donor obligated himself, and the document serves only as evidence. See what I wrote above in siman 45, seif katan 15, and in siman 243 on the Sema, seif katan 23.
