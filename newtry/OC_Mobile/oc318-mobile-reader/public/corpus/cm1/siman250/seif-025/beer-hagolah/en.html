Alef) This is the language of the Rambam, chapter 9 of Hilkhot Zekhiyah, halachah 24, from the Mishnah in Bava Batra 135b.
Bet) The Maggid Mishneh writes that this applies to a gift of all one's property. With a partial gift, however, the recipient certainly acquires because there is a kinyan, even if the deed never reached him. The Ramah likewise writes this. The Kesef Mishneh notes that Rivash, siman 168, agrees with the Maggid Mishneh and proves it from Rambam, chapter 8, halachah 18, where Rambam writes that if the deed reached the recipient or a kinyan was made from the giver, the recipient acquires.
Gimel) There, halachah 25, from the Mishnah. Rashbam explains that the giver caused the recipient to acquire during his lifetime.
Dalet) The Maggid Mishneh understands Rambam to mean that if the giver transferred part of the estate to one person, the rest of the disposition remains valid, because he revealed that he truly intended the transfer. He writes that he did not see another commentator explain this way. The Bedek HaBayit notes that the Nimukei Yosef disagrees, while Rivash, siman 364, proves from Rambam chapter 8 in accordance with the Maggid Mishneh. The Bach concludes that this is the practical ruling according to Rambam. Rashbam, however, explains that even if the document was not written specifically in that person's name, if the giver said, "The property described in this document I transfer to you through accepting this deed from me," the recipient acquires, because this is no worse than an oral deathbed instruction. The Tur cites the Raavad as explaining that the giver gave it through another person, saying to Reuven, "Acquire this deed for Shimon." Shimon acquires even if Reuven, acting on his behalf, is himself the heir, and we do not say that because Reuven is the heir the deed never left the giver's control.
Meaning, without a separate kinyan, because the words of a deathly ill person are regarded as written and delivered.
Heh) There, halachah 25. The Maggid Mishneh writes that this too is explicit. The Kesef Mishneh says he did not find it in the Gemara. In my humble opinion Rambam learned it from the Mishnah at the end of the first chapter of Bava Metzia 20a: one who found a deed among his documents and does not know its status must leave it unresolved. We are concerned that the borrower deposited it temporarily with him until giving further instructions, as the Tur writes in the name of the Rosh, because a deed may be written for a borrower even when the lender is not present.
