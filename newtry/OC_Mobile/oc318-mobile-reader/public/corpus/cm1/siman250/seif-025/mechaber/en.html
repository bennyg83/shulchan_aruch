If a person dies and a deed of gift is found tied to his thigh, even if it was witnessed and he had made a kinyan merely to strengthen the rights of the named recipients, the deed is ineffective, because we say that he wrote it and then changed his mind. If, however, during his lifetime he transferred the deed to another person, whether one of the heirs or someone else, all the provisions written in it are effective like any deathbed gift. Likewise, if a person wrote a debt instrument against himself in the name of another person or one of his sons, placed it in the hands of a third party, and said only, "Keep this with you," without explaining further, or said, "Hold it until I tell you what to do," and then died, it is ineffective. With a gift made by a healthy person and accompanied by a kinyan, however, the recipient acquires. Tur in the name of Rosh.
