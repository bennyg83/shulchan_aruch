One who embarks on a sea voyage or sets out with a caravan, etc. The Rosh, cited briefly by the Tur, writes that there is no clear reason to treat one who travels by sea or caravan and gives only part of his property as one who gives because of anticipated death, so that he could retract upon returning. Only regarding a get were travelers equated with a dangerously ill person and one being led to execution, because they are anxious and preoccupied with the journey and therefore may omit the word “give.” For gifts, however, why should they be treated like people expecting death when their intention is ordinarily to return? Nimukei Yosef, in the name of Rabbeinu Yonah, explains that many travelers ultimately settle in the place to which they go. They therefore give because they are uncertain whether they will return or settle there. If they later do return, they may retract even from a partial gift made with a kinyan, like one who gave because of anticipated death.
