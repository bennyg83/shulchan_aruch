(Seif 12: He retracted for his own benefit.) By doing so he revealed that he did not intend the first transfer as a deathly ill person's gift, but as the gift of a healthy person, which requires an act of acquisition. One should not object that, since the initial allocation to his heirs is not legally deemed a gift, what he gave to others should be treated as a partial gift and therefore require an act of acquisition. Since he mentioned the heirs at the same time, it is as though he expressly stated that the partial gift was given under the law of a deathly ill person's gift, which is effective without an act of acquisition, as explained in seif 9. This is the correct text.
