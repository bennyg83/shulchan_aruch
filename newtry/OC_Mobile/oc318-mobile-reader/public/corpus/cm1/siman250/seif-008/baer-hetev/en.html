With a caravan. The Tur writes in the name of the Rosh that there is no basis for the view that one who sets out to sea or departs with a caravan and gives a partial gift should be treated as one who gives because of impending death, so that he may retract the gift if he returns. Only with respect to a get were such persons equated with one who is dangerously ill or is being led out in chains: if he says, 'Write a get for my wife,' they deliver the get even though he did not say, 'Give it,' because he is frightened and preoccupied by the journey before him and therefore did not complete his instructions. But with respect to a gift, why should he be treated like them when his intention is to return? This is the Rosh's position in brief. The Nimukei Yosef, in the chapter Mi Shemet, writes in the name of Rabbeinu Yonah that many such travelers intend to settle at their destination. They therefore make the gift because they are uncertain whether they will settle there. Consequently, if they do not settle and instead return, they may retract even a partial gift made with a kinyan, like one who gave because of impending death. Responsa Rashach, part III, siman 165, likewise writes that even according to the Rif and Rambam, who treat these four categories as giving because of impending death, that rule applies specifically if they die on that journey, meaning that one who departed with a caravan or set out to sea did not reach his intended destination. Moreover, even according to those authorities, the rule applies only where the person gave his instructions at the time he set out to sea or departed with the caravan, comparable to one being led out in chains or one in mortal danger. If he gave the instructions several days before setting out to sea, the gift is not one made because of impending death but the gift of a healthy person, and unless a kinyan was made from him his words have no legal effect.
