One who departs by sea. Rif and Rambam treat the four dangerous travelers listed by the Sages, including one departing by sea and one leaving with a caravan, like one who gives because of imminent death. Their words can have the special force of a deathbed disposition. Rosh reports that Rabbi Yonah disagrees, and Maggid Mishneh likewise says Rabbi Yonah and Ramban dispute Rif and Rambam. Yet Nimukei Yosef cites Rabbi Yonah in a way that appears partly supportive: many who depart for distant journeys intend to settle there. Since the donor may be giving only because of that possible permanent departure, if he later returns home he can retract even from a partial gift made with a kinyan, just as one who expressly gives because of impending death can retract upon recovery. The likely reconciliation is that Rabbi Yonah agrees these travelers may retract when their assumed reason for the gift disappears, but disagrees that their mere words have the full acquisition force of a deathbed gift without any ordinary kinyan. The Sages dispensed with formal acquisition for a true deathly ill person lest anxiety worsen his condition. A traveler in danger does not necessarily receive that same extraordinary verbal power. Rosh's language, however, suggests a broader disagreement. He says there is no reason to treat a partial gift by one departing by sea or caravan as automatically motivated by death so that it would be cancelled merely because he returned. Thus even the retraction rule itself remains disputed.
