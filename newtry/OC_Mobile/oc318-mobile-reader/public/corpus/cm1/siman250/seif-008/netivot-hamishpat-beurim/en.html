One who sets out to sea or departs with a caravan. See Sema, seif katan 22, who writes that there is no reason to distinguish one who sets out to sea or leaves with a caravan where he gives only part of his property. His wording is imprecise, for in truth the Rosh and Tur disagree even where he gives all his property, as is clear from their words. The Sema further cites Nimukei Yosef's reason that many who depart do so intending to settle elsewhere, and therefore if they do not settle and instead return, the gift is void. According to this, the Mechaber's statement that such a person is treated as one giving because of impending death does not apply in every respect. If the transfer was made with a kinyan in a manner where the problem that the sudar has returned to its owner does not arise, such as by deed, there is no need for additional language strengthening the gift, because the rule that a deed cannot take effect after death is inapplicable. Likewise a sale is not comparable, as shown by the case of one who sold property intending to move to Eretz Yisrael. He is treated like one giving because of impending death only regarding a kinyan that would otherwise fail because the sudar has returned to its owner, since his intent is not to transfer until later, and regarding his right to retract if he recovers. His words imply somewhat that if the acquisition was made by deed, where the issue of the sudar returning does not apply, and he retracted before settling elsewhere but subsequently did settle there, the retraction is ineffective. The transaction resembles a transfer subject to a condition, and once the condition is fulfilled, the acquisition takes effect retroactively. By contrast, where there was no formal kinyan, one may say that the Sages enacted their special mode of acquisition only to take effect after the departure actually became permanent.
