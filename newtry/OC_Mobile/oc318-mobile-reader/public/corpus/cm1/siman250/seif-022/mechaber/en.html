Some say there is no distinction between saying, "Write and give it to him," and, "Give it to him and write for him." Others say strengthening is required specifically when he says, "Write and give one hundred maneh to so-and-so," which implies writing the gift and delivering the deed as the means of acquisition. If he says, "Give one hundred maneh to so-and-so and write a deed for him," without directing that the deed itself be delivered, the writing is merely for memorial and evidence, not acquisition. Likewise, if a deathly ill person says, "Give one maneh to so-and-so," and after the interval of immediate speech adds, "Write and give him the deed," he is presumed not to retract the first gift but to strengthen it by providing proof. The witnesses write what he commanded, but should not write, "He told us to write and give," because that wording would invalidate the gift.
