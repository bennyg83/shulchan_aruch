(Seif 19: For then delivery of the document is effective.) The Beit Yosef challenged our master: since the transaction contains a kinyan, why is delivery of the document necessary? Moreover, if it was written in testamentary language and delivered during his lifetime, what does that accomplish? In my humble opinion, our master's words should be understood as follows. His opening statement, "if it contained a kinyan," does not refer only to a symbolic kinyan, but to every relevant mode of acquisition, such as the formula, "My field is sold to you," by which land is acquired when written in a document. He then concludes that there is no difference whether the mode of acquisition is specified in the document, each according to its applicable form, whether the document expressly records that a symbolic kinyan was made or states, "My field is sold to you," and no difference whether he gave it orally with a kinyan. In all such cases we are concerned that he did not fully resolve to transfer ownership, and delivery of the document to the recipient during the donor's lifetime is ineffective unless there is a strengthening clause. The reason is that perhaps he intended the transfer to take effect only after death, and no kinyan takes effect after death. This also includes a case where there was no independent kinyan, but he merely instructed that a document be written and given to the recipient. There too we are concerned that he did not fully resolve to give until the document reached the recipient, and a document cannot take effect after death. Therefore, our master distinguishes: when the document is written in testamentary language, delivery during the donor's lifetime is effective, because the gift contains no mode of acquisition. He merely ordered that the document be written, which is only a condition, and once it was written his instruction was immediately fulfilled. But if the document uses language of acquisition, whether a symbolic kinyan or "My field is given to you," delivery during his lifetime is ineffective. Since the principal acquisition does not depend solely on the testamentary instruction, we remain concerned that he intended to transfer ownership only through the kinyan at the time the gift was to take effect, namely at death. This is demonstrated by our master and the Rosh, who wrote that a document stating, "My field is given to you," resembles a document of a deathly ill person's gift that contains a kinyan, precisely as I have explained.
