A gift made by a kinyan does not take effect. I already explained the reason at the beginning of this siman, seif 1. The Sages enacted that the words of a shechiv mera are as though written and delivered. If he was unwilling to give except through a kinyan sudar, a document, or another mode of acquisition used by a healthy person, he revealed that he is not relying on the rabbinic enactment but wants the gift to function like an ordinary kinyan. It therefore does not work unless he wrote or said that the kinyan or document accompanying the will comes to enhance rather than diminish the recipient's power. In that case he acquires.
Even if the gift was written in a document and delivered, etc. Below in seif 18 it will be explained that this refers specifically to a document containing operative acquisition language, such as "My field is given to you," making it the form of a healthy person's kinyan. See there.
"And we made a kinyan from him, in addition to this gift." This is the Gemara's and authorities' formulation. It concerns a kinyan sudar: the document must state that this kinyan is an added strengthening beyond what he already conferred through the rabbinic rule of writing and delivery, which is "this gift." The same applies if the document says, "My field is given to you," and adds that the acquisition language in the document is in addition to what he already gave orally. That too is effective. See the Perishah.
As though he wrote an enhancement of power. Meaning, as though he wrote that this kinyan is additional.
As though he transferred movable property together with land belonging to the recipient. Darkei Moshe also writes this in Rashba's name, that it is as though he transferred movable property together with land of the recipient. Rashba's wording in Responsa 882 does not appear to me to mean this. There he writes: "A shechiv mera who gave land and, together with it, all his movable property did not acquire the movable property, because he did not acquire the land until after death, and this is like transferring movable property together with land of his own, which does not work." Rama apparently understood "his own" to mean the recipient's. I do not understand this. If so, why would Rashba's reason be that the recipient did not acquire the land until after death? On the contrary, he should have said that precisely because he then acquires the land after death, the movable property should also be acquired together with it. Nor were the recipient's lands his before the movable property; both acquisitions would arrive together, like any transfer of movable property together with land. Moreover, the pronoun "his own" cannot naturally refer to the recipient, who was not mentioned immediately beforehand. And why would it be obvious that one who transfers movable property together with land already owned by the recipient fails? A person's land normally acquires for him property he purchases or receives from others; why should the seller's statement "acquire it together with your own land" make matters worse? It therefore appears to me that "his own" means the giver's. The explanation is this: a shechiv mera who ordered that Reuven receive his lands and the movable property together with them, if he intended an ordinary shechiv mera gift he should simply have said, "Give Reuven the lands and movable property," in which case he would acquire the movable property as well because the giver's words are as though delivered. By instructing acquisition together with the land, he revealed that he did not intend to give the movable property by the special law of shechiv mera. We must then ask whether he wanted the movable property acquired immediately during his life together with the land, or only after death together with the land. If after death, it plainly fails because there is no kinyan after death. If immediately, it also fails, because before death the land still belongs to the giver; he does not acquire it until after death. Movable property can be acquired together with land only when the recipient acquires both together. I therefore believe Darkei Moshe and the gloss should read "land of the giver" rather than "land of the recipient." In manuscript copies it indeed reads "of the transferor."
No enhancement of power is required here. Rivash writes that there is no independent acquisition here about which we could say he intended to rely only on the document or kinyan.
