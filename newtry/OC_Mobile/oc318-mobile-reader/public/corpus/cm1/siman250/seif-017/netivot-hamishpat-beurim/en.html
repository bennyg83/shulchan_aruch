The same applies to one who gives because of impending death together with a kinyan. This is contrary to Responsa Maharit, Choshen Mishpat, siman 50; see there.
It is as though he transferred it together with land. See Sema, seif katan 54, who writes that there is reason to doubt whether the donor intended the movable property to be acquired immediately before his death. In my humble opinion this is difficult. Why should we entertain that doubt when he is making a gift of a shechiv mera of all his property? How is this different from other modes of acquisition, such as a sudar kinyan or chazakah, where we say that perhaps he intended to transfer only through the deed and a deed cannot take effect after death? Moreover, Rashba could simply have said that just as other modes of acquisition weaken the special gift of a shechiv mera, acquisition together with land does so as well. Rather, the shechiv mera certainly intends transfer only after death. Rashba teaches that even where the concern that a kinyan cannot take effect after death does not apply, such as where he expressly transfers one hour before death, or says "from today," which, according to what I wrote above, can sometimes mean while I am still alive, namely one hour before death, the acquisition together with land is still ineffective. The reason is that it is then comparable to transferring movable property together with land that already belongs to the recipient. Tosafot at the end of the first chapter of Gittin write that a gift of a shechiv mera takes effect retroactively from the time of the transfer. The acquisition together with the land begins one hour before death, and at that time the land is already regarded retroactively as the recipient's own land. Therefore the movable property cannot be acquired through it.
Even though he expressly stated that the gift should exist only after death. Ritva, cited by Beit Yosef, concludes that we combine "from today" with "after death," treating it as though he said "from today and after death." See there. In my humble opinion, this cannot mean that the gift is like an ordinary gift of a healthy person made "from today and after death." When a healthy person gives in that manner, the body of the property belongs to the recipient from today and the fruits after death, whenever death occurs. By contrast, a shechiv mera who expressly says that the gift shall exist only after death necessarily means death from this illness, since one giving because of impending death refers to death from that illness. If he recovers, the gift is not effective. Thus his expression "from today and after death" must mean a present transfer subject to the condition of death. If he recovers, he may retract, as Nimukei Yosef writes and as I cited above in his name.
