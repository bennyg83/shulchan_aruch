(Seif 4: enough for his support.) The Sema's distinction in seif katan 12 between land and movable property is surprising. If so, land too should be valued only after deducting all necessary expenses, just as merchandise cannot generate support without expenses. Likewise, one should deduct the cost of hiring a worker to manage the land. It therefore appears to me that in every case we value what remains above the necessary expenses. It is also clear that if he customarily supported members of his household, this too is included in the assessment, because we presume that a person does not abandon his household and give everything to others, as stated at the beginning of siman 246 concerning one whose son died, etc. Yet the Rama writes simply, "enough for his support," meaning according to his prior standard. Our master's initial mention of his household applies equally to merchandise; he merely spoke of the most common case first.
