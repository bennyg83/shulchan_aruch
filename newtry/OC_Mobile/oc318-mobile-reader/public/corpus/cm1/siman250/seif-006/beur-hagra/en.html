A dying person, etc. As stated at the beginning of Masekhet Semachot: a dying person is considered alive for all matters, forever, etc.; he inherits and transmits inheritance. Likewise in Arakhin 6b, in the Mishna: Rabbi Yehuda says, he may vow, etc. Gittin 28a says only that a dying person is presumed, etc., implying that without that concern his acts are effective. There on 70b, 'they cut through him, etc.,' and it was taught, 'they saw him, etc.' There is no greater dying condition than that. See Rashbam, Bava Batra 127b, regarding property, etc., where he explains that the person cannot speak, etc. See 40.
