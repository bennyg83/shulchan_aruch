Lest he did not fully intend to convey. Noda BiYehudah, simanim 26 and 28, observes that this language sounds like a doubt whether the recipient acquired. His learned son-in-law, Rabbi Yosef, argued that Rivash, siman 345, treats the defect as certain rather than doubtful, comparing it to the case of one who falls upon ownerless property and thereby fails to acquire it. Noda BiYehudah responds that Rivash is not inconsistent. Here there is doubt whether the donor performed the kinyan only because he did not know that mere words would suffice. With acquisition by four cubits, by contrast, lack of knowledge itself defeats the acquisition, since four cubits acquire only with the person's intent. See there.
