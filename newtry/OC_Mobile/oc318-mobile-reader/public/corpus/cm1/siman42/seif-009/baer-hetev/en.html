He erred. The Sema writes that this concerns expressions by which a person attempts to convey gifts, such as "I will give him this field," as discussed in siman 245. We say that he did not intend to nullify the gift, but merely erred and believed that this wording was effective, whereas in truth it is not. The Shach writes that it would have been more appropriate to cite the case of Maharik involving a testament that states, "I leave to so and so," and that the Sema's case of "I will give him" requires study. Maharik seems to have spoken only of language of leaving, because the Rosh holds that such language is effective, and one can therefore say that the giver erred and thought it valid. But regarding "I will give him," no authority holds that it is effective, so one should not presume such an error. Therefore, where a document can be interpreted as "I will give him" or alternatively in another wording that is legally effective, we do not apply the rule that the holder of the document has the weaker hand. See siman 49, seif 2 in the gloss, and seif 6.
