Maggid Mishneh and Rivash write that whenever one possible interpretation would invalidate the document entirely, we do not adopt that interpretation. Nimukei Yosef similarly writes that we do not apply the weak-hand principle where doing so requires adding something to the condition. Darkei Moshe cites these authorities.
But only until a Pesach several years later. For the same reason, the debtor cannot say, "I will pay at the Pesach after next year." If the intended date were not the immediately approaching Pesach, the document would not identify which Pesach was meant and the debtor could keep postponing payment for many years. That cannot be the intent, because it would effectively void the document. Therefore we interpret it as the first coming Pesach. The Rosh writes this in responsa.
Because it is possible that the donor erred in this, etc. This appears to concern language by which the gift itself is conveyed, such as "I will give him this field." In such a case we cannot say that a person never intends language that will void his own document. Perhaps he did intend to make the gift and simply erred in thinking this formula was legally effective, when in fact it was not.
