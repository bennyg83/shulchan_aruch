And it is not explained which coinage. Rama appears to interpret the Mechaber as saying that even if the document states that he owes one hundred selaim, it does not explain which current coinage is to be used, whether the most readily accepted coins or otherwise. Thus in Bavel one would collect one hundred selaim in the currency commonly used there. I rejected this explanation in Perishah. The Mechaber's intent follows Maggid Mishneh at the end of chapter 17 of Hilchot Malveh. Even where the document expressly states the unit, such as one hundred dinars or selaim, a Babylonian dinar or sela is not equal in weight to an Eretz Yisrael dinar or sela, and similarly among other countries. Rashi explains this explicitly at the end of Ketubot. Therefore, if the document does not specify of which locality the unit was, one collects using the coin of the place identified in the document as the place where it was written and signed, because we presume the loan occurred there and used that locality's currency. If no place is stated in the document, one collects using the currency of the place where the document is produced for collection, again presuming the loan occurred there. At the end of the seif, where the document says merely "one hundred silver," the case is different. There the place of the loan is known, but the denomination itself is unspecified, so the creditor receives whichever local coin fits the wording. It appears this means the smallest coin of the place stated in the document, or if none is stated, of the place of collection, not the smallest coin from some other country. Although the Mechaber already ruled at the beginning of seif 13 that if a document says "a silver coin" and perutot circulate in that place, etc., that earlier rule concerns one coin, while this clause teaches the law of one hundred coins.
