If a person produces a promissory note against another for one hundred dinars or sela'im, without specifying which currency, then if the document was written in Babylonia he collects in Babylonian currency, and if it was written in Eretz Yisrael he collects in the currency of Eretz Yisrael. If no place is written in the document and it is produced in Babylonia, he collects in Babylonian currency; if produced in Eretz Yisrael, he collects in the local currency. If the creditor seeks to collect in the currency of the place where the document is produced and the borrower claims, "The money I owe him was of a currency worth less than this," the creditor swears and collects. Rama: If the borrower brings proof that they originally lived in a place where the currency was worth less, he pays in the lesser currency. If the document says merely "one hundred silver" and does not specify whether sela'im or pundeyonim, the borrower may pay whichever he chooses.
