A broader phrase is interpreted to enhance the recipient's right, etc. Beit Yosef cites Rashba: one who mortgaged a house with several upper stories wrote that he mortgaged "from the lower parts of my house until the end of the uppermost." He later claimed that he had mortgaged only one upper story directly above the house, while the scribe said the intent was all the upper stories. The law follows the creditor, because the broad wording is interpreted in the manner that gives it full effect.
According to the understanding of those who hear it. Rivash likewise rules that documents are interpreted according to ordinary human speech. Darkei Moshe cites him.
