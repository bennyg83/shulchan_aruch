Many commentators wrote, etc. This law requires careful study. Its source is Ramban in Perek Chezkas HaBatim and Baal HaTerumot, Shaar 43. The Gemara says that one who gives a creditor a lien on movables together with land must write that the arrangement is "not as an asmachta and not as a standard document form." Ramban, in the name of Rav Saadiah Gaon, explains that the phrase "not as a standard document form" means that in any later ambiguity, the holder of the document has the upper hand. On that reading, the phrase is not intrinsically necessary to create the lien on movables together with land; scribes merely became accustomed to adding it. Many authorities disagree with this explanation. Rashbam explains that the phrase is necessary because otherwise the clause concerning movables together with land may merely have been inserted by the scribe as standard embellishment, without the borrower's actual instruction. The words "not as a standard document form" show that the borrower deliberately authorized the clause. Tur below, siman 113, adopts this. Ritva is cited the same way, Maggid Mishneh writes similarly in Hilchot Malveh 11, and Rabbenu Yerucham himself elsewhere says that for a lien of movables together with land, one needs this phrase so that we do not say the scribe inserted it merely as routine wording. Accordingly, Mechaber's ruling here appears difficult. He should not have stated the Gaonic interpretation without qualification when so many authorities disagree, especially since below, siman 60, he himself writes that for a lien of movables together with land one must say "not as an asmachta and not as a standard document form," which sounds like Rashbam's explanation. Perhaps Mechaber intended to reconcile the views. Baal HaTerumot asks what good the phrase "not as an asmachta" accomplishes if the underlying arrangement really is a full asmachta. Merely declaring "this is not an asmachta" cannot validate a genuine asmachta, as Mechaber himself rules below, siman 207. The phrase therefore works only where the legal structure is not a classic asmachta but there is uncertainty whether the borrower intended a firm present lien on the movables together with land or merely relied on the land lien and treated the movable clause conditionally. By saying "not as an asmachta," he indicates that the lien is absolute. Yet even then, the words can themselves be read in two ways: perhaps he means, "I acknowledge that this arrangement was never an asmachta in substance"; or perhaps he means only, "I stipulate that the defense of asmachta shall not invalidate it." The second formulation would not help if the arrangement were truly an asmachta. This remaining ambiguity is exactly where the phrase "not as a standard document form" becomes useful under Rav Saadiah Gaon's explanation. It tells us to construe the document in favor of the holder and therefore to read "not as an asmachta" as an acknowledgment that the lien itself was absolute. At the same time, the same phrase also shows, as Rashbam says, that the movable-lien clause was not inserted casually by the scribe. Thus both explanations can be true together. One might still ask why the parties could not simply write clearer language, such as, "I hereby subject the movables together with the land by an absolute present lien and not conditionally." Of course they could. But scribal practice adopted the phrase "not as an asmachta and not as a standard document form" because it serves both purposes at once: it confirms deliberate borrower authorization and also resolves doubts in favor of the document's operative effect. This can reconcile Rabbenu Yerucham's apparently inconsistent passages and likewise Mechaber's rulings here and below. The matter still requires study, but there is no necessity to treat the sources as contradictory.
