Unlike an asmachta. This phrase is not really needed here except in connection with movables, because a person's movable property does not automatically become subject, as stated in Bava Batra 174. There 173b it says that whether a guarantor becomes obligated is disputed, etc., until Rabbi Ashi states the rule. That concerns land, unlike movables. See Rashi, Bava Metzia 67b: a creditor does not rely upon movable property. "Unlike standard forms" is written for the reason stated here. The Gemara there 44b did not require this wording intrinsically; it was only because that was the standard formula of their documents. Here there is similarly no need to write "unlike an asmachta."
And every expression. Avodah Zarah 11, concerning the official document writer, etc.; Bava Batra 31 and 159b: since he said, "the members of the house of Bar, etc." Even to extract property, because the holder of the document has the weaker hand, as explained above.
(Collected note) And every expression that includes more, etc. Even where part was erased. In Get Pashut the Mishnah teaches: "selas that are such and such," and if the amount was erased he receives only two. We do not say perhaps it had written seven dinars. Ramban's novellae; see there. (End of collected note.)
