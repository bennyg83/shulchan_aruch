So long as the document has not been authenticated. This implies that if the document is authenticated, or can be authenticated, the kiddushin are established. But Rashba in responsa 1209-1210 writes that if the witnesses wrote and signed the document without the woman's authorization, it is not a valid evidentiary document. It is written testimony rather than oral testimony and is no more than a memorandum. His words imply that if the woman told them, "Write, sign, and deliver it to the man who betrothed me as proof that he betrothed me," it would be a valid document. Hagahot Mordechai to Gittin, however, implies that even a writing made with the woman's knowledge is ineffective, because such an evidentiary document was not authorized by the Torah to be written and the Torah requires testimony from the witnesses' mouths, not from their writing. A debt document is different because Scripture itself established written documents, and a document of kiddushin is different because it effects the kiddushin and is compared to a get. But a mere proof of kiddushin effected by money was not given to be written.
