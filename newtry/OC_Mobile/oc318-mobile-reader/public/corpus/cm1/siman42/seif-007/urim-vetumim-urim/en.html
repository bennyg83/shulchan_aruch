We do not make the argument for him. For example, if the judge senses that the document originally said three and was altered to thirty, but the litigant does not make that claim and instead argues total denial, trust, or another unrelated defense, the court does not invent the forgery defense for him. But if the litigant cries out, “I never owed more than three. What is this thirty?” then, even if he cannot identify the method of alteration, he is challenging the substance itself and the judge should raise the possibility of forgery for him. This is the category of “open your mouth for the mute.” Rabbi Shlomo Kohen writes similarly, and the ruling is correct.
