Because kaphal is less than sefel. Tosafot and Rashbam imply another reason. A fly usually shortens a letter, turning a kuf into a samekh, rather than lengthening a samekh into a kuf. Therefore even if "kaphal" below would yield a larger amount than "sefel" above, we still follow the lower writing. Either the upper word was originally sefel and the writer later wrote kaphal below, in which case the lower wording governs; or the upper word was originally kaphal and a fly altered it to sefel, in which case kaphal is again the correct amount. Rambam apparently holds that blotting by a fly can also lengthen a letter and turn samekh into kuf. He therefore needs the separate reason that kaphal denotes the smaller amount.
