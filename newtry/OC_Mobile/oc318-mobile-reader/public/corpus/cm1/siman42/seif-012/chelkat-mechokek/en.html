One who performs kiddushin before witnesses who are disqualified by Torah law. Rambam writes in chapter 13 of Hilchot Edut that paternal relatives are disqualified by Torah law, while maternal relatives and relatives through marriage are disqualified rabbinically. Ramban and Rashba, however, hold that all relatives derived through derashah are disqualified by Torah law, whether paternal or maternal. The Maggid Mishneh cites this in their names. Rabbeinu Yehudah son of the Rosh likewise writes that many authorities disagree with Rambam and hold that relationship through marriage is a Torah-level disqualification. See Rambam from chapter 9 of Hilchot Edut onward, where he explains the Torah and rabbinic disqualifications for testimony. See also the Beit Yosef, who cites a case where a man performed kiddushin before disqualified witnesses, whose disqualification was based on testimony that they had violated their oaths concerning the future, and their disqualification had been accepted outside their presence. One authority wished to permit the woman, but others disagreed and imposed a ban upon him until he retracted his ruling, for several reasons. See there, because this has practical legal ramifications.
