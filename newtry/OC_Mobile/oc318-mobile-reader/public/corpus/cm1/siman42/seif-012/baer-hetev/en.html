Heset. In siman 88, seif 32, the Mechaber rules similarly where the lender claims five and the borrower says three: the borrower does not swear a Torah oath concerning the remainder, because to the extent he admits more than the document itself implies he has the status of one returning a lost item. Thus the Sema. The Taz writes that if a document presented to beit din says merely "sela'im" without any number and a blank space was left beside the word, nothing may be collected on that document. See there for his reasoning.
Seized. This refers to seizure in the presence of witnesses. If he seized without witnesses, he is believed through a migo. This differs from seif 8, where in cases governed by the rule that the document holder has the weaker position, property seized even before witnesses is not removed. Here the wording of the document itself supports the borrower, because an unspecified reference to "sela'im" ordinarily means no more than two. According to this reasoning, in the preceding case where the document stated "which are" and the numerical amount was erased, seizure should be effective. Yet the Beit Yosef and Darkei Moshe do not appear to hold so, and the matter requires study. Thus the Sema. The Shach answers that an erasure is different, because otherwise anyone holding a document could seize a large amount and erase the written total. He adds that the rule allowing one who seized without witnesses to rely on a migo applies where the lender asserts with certainty that he lent more. If the document merely created an obligation without an underlying loan and the holder cannot state the larger amount with certainty, even seizure without witnesses is reversed because no migo is available.
We remove it. Sefer Giddulei Terumah asks from the rule in HaMekabel, explained in siman 317, seif 3, that where a document says merely "years" without specifying how many, property already consumed by the lender is not taken from him. The Shach answers that this presents no difficulty according to Nimukei Yosef's explanation there: because the borrower allowed the lender to consume the fruits of a third year, the document's apparent evidence in the borrower's favor was weakened. Ritva in his chiddushim to the beginning of Bava Metzia writes similarly. One also cannot object from the Mechaber's ruling at the end of siman 54 that a receipt stating merely "dinars" cancels every document produced against it. Baal HaTerumot distinguishes between loan documents and receipts. With loan documents we say that because the witnesses know the holder has the weaker position, if the amount exceeded two they would have stated it explicitly. With a receipt we reason oppositely: perhaps the witnesses did not know the exact sum in the debt document and therefore wrote generally that whatever amount is later produced against this receipt is impaired. Thus the Shach.
