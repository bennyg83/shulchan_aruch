Gidulei Terumah objects from the rule in Bava Metzia, explained below in siman 317, that where a lease states merely “years,” the landlord may retain produce of a third year if he already consumed it, because the matter is one that will become known. There is no difficulty. The Nimukei Yosef explains that the lease's wording, which ordinarily indicates two years, does not assist the tenant because the tenant allowed the landlord to consume the third year's produce, thereby weakening the evidentiary force of the document. The Ritva writes similarly.
He seized. This means that he seized in the presence of witnesses. Otherwise, he would be believed through a migo that he could deny the seizure, as the Sema, Maggid Mishneh, Beit Yosef, and other authorities explain. It appears to me that this applies when the creditor claims with certainty that he lent more. If, however, he is a bailee whose asserted liability arises only from this document, then even a seizure without witnesses is removed from him, for he cannot claim certainty and therefore has no relevant migo.

The Sema infers that in the preceding case, where the amount following “which are” was erased, seizure should be effective. This is incorrect. Erasure is different. Otherwise, every holder of a note could seize a large sum and erase the written amount. An erasure is interpreted only to weaken the extracting party. The Tur and Mechaber apply the same principle in siman 54 to a receipt in which the amount after “which are” was erased, ruling according to the smallest amount supported by the wording. There is no stronger form of possession than a receipt, yet the erasure still works only against the party relying upon it. This is the reason given by the Ramban in Baal HaTerumot.
From him. At first glance, this would imply that a receipt stating that the debtor paid “dinars” discharges only two, following the Ritva and unlike the Raah, as I wrote above. The Bach likewise understood the Maggid Mishneh and Mechaber this way. Yet this cannot be correct, for the Mechaber expressly rules in siman 54 that a receipt stating “dinars” cancels the entire note, and the Maggid Mishneh rules likewise. The distinction is that in a loan document, witnesses know that the holder's hand is inferior. Had the sum exceeded two, they would have stated it explicitly. In a receipt, however, the witnesses may not know the amount of the original note. They therefore write generally so that whatever note the creditor later produces will be impaired by it. Accordingly, even the Raah may agree that in a promissory note, where the creditor seized more than two, the excess is removed. The Bach's contrary inference is therefore incorrect.
