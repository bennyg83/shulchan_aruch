And if he seized. See Shach, Gidulei Terumah, and Urim VeTumim. The best distinction is between possession obtained by right and seizure not obtained by right. In ambiguous receipts or a mortgage whose fruits have already been consumed, the possessor came to hold the object or benefit lawfully, and because the doubt is a genuine monetary uncertainty he may sometimes retain. In an ordinary loan, however, a creditor who later seizes property has only a lien claim, not ownership of the object itself; such seizure does not help merely because he asserts certainty. This is especially so where the uncertainty is a true *darra de-mamona*, arising independently of the parties' claims. Ketzot HaChoshen correctly cites the case of a disputed repayment date: money of a loan was given for expenditure and the borrower remains the possessor until proven otherwise. That is unlike a borrowed object that remains legally in the owner's domain throughout the loan.
