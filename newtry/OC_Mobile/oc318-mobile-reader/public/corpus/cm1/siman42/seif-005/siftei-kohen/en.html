If the two formulations can be reconciled, they are reconciled. The Sema writes that the Darkei Moshe explains that even a strained reconciliation is accepted, because people do not ordinarily contradict themselves within the time required for speech. That is not what the Darkei Moshe says. He cites the Responsa Maimoniyot only for the rule that if reconciliation is possible, it is made even when the two formulations are far apart and other material intervenes. Certainly this is so when they are adjacent, because one does not ordinarily contradict oneself immediately. Nothing there establishes that a forced or strained reconciliation must be adopted.
And the document does not state, “Everything is firm and established.” The Bach uses this to defend Rashi's explanation at the beginning of Bava Metzia against Tosafot's objection from the rule that the final line is not relied upon, suggesting that Rashi discusses a document containing the phrase “firm and established.” This is incorrect. The requirement to repeat the document's substance in its last line exists precisely because the last line is otherwise disregarded, as the Gemara and all the authorities explain in siman 44. If Rashi's case contained “firm and established,” so that the last line could be relied upon, there would be no need to repeat the substance. That is exactly Tosafot's objection. See the cited responsa of Maharshal, Maharshadam, and Maharit.
For the holder of the document has the inferior hand. Terumat HaDeshen discusses an instrument stating that the party accepted all the foregoing through a complete act of acquisition, but omitting the formula “with an instrument fit to effect acquisition.” He rules that one must fear that another type of acquisition was used rather than exchange, and the holder of the document has the inferior hand. This requires explanation, for the case must involve an acquisition that is possibly ineffective; otherwise there is no practical difference. Yet where that interpretation would nullify the clause entirely, the rule generally is not to construe the document against its holder. It cannot be answered that the document contained other matters not requiring an acquisition, for the particular clause requiring acquisition would still be entirely void. The same issue arises where one obligation is payable at a known date and another “after Pesach”; the latter is construed as the immediately following Pesach because otherwise that clause would be emptied of effect. The proper explanation is that the scribe believed the other acquisition to be effective in a matter where the law itself is uncertain, like the case addressed by Maharik and discussed below in seif 9.
