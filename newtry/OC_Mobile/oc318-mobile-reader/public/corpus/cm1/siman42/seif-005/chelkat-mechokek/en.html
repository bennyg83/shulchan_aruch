And he alone saw. This wording implies a case where the witness does not say that everyone else saw, but only that other witnesses were present and he does not know whether they also observed the act. Mere presence is irrelevant if he alone actually saw. But if he says that the kiddushin were properly effected before himself and another named witness, both having been designated as witnesses, and the other witness contradicts him and says the kiddushin were not completed, this resembles one witness saying the kiddushin were close to her and one saying they were close to him. There we are initially concerned for the stricter testimony, as explained below in siman 47:3. One can distinguish, however, between a case where many witnesses were present and all later departed except two, and a case where only two were present from the outset, because then the testimony of each depends upon the other and one expressly contradicts him. Terumat HaDeshen, siman 212, distinguishes along these lines and concludes that we ordinarily maintain her presumption of being unmarried unless that presumption has been weakened. Thus, where both witnesses agree that they assembled for kiddushin and one says the kiddushin occurred before both while the other denies it, she should not marry another initially. All this concerns a case where neither party admits the testimony of the witness. If the woman herself admits that there were two witnesses and the kiddushin occurred, she has rendered herself prohibited even without the second witness.
