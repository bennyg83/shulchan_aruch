Last method. This is a sign of this:
The hand of the holder of the document is on the lower side. If the document involves a prohibition arising from an oath, then if the party asserts a definite interpretation he is believed. If the language itself is doubtful, the doubt is treated leniently regarding the oath. See Netivot and Taz. If a document says merely, "and we made a kinyan from him," without saying that it was made with an instrument fit for acquisition, then where the subject is something ordinarily acquired by symbolic kinyan, the document is valid because we presume the usual form of acquisition was used. But where the subject is not acquired by symbolic kinyan, such as coins, we do not assume an effective alternative mode such as meshichah. Rather, we assume the ordinary symbolic kinyan was used and the transaction is invalid. This is the primary law, unlike the Sema.
