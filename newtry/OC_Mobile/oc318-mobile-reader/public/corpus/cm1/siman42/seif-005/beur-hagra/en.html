(Anthology) And there are not two in the document, etc. This implies that the passage does not concern current practice, where a document is invalid without the words 'valid and established.' Even so, if those words are written, we derive law from the last line. Siman 44, seif 4, likewise states, 'where,' etc. See Shach there, seif katan 12, who struggles with Tosafot's question that perhaps the words were not originally present and were fraudulently added. He answers that Bava Metzia's case where 'valid and established' was written concerned only a folded document, which is otherwise invalid, and likewise documents in current practice. See Shach, siman 45, seif katan 20. He then writes, 'rather, it appears to me that the Rosh and Tur,' etc. His words are puzzling, for if so, how can the Tur write, 'nevertheless,' etc.? In the first case the document would in any event be invalid because 'valid and established' is absent. It must therefore concern earlier practice, and even there, where these words were written, forgery was not suspected. As to Tosafot's difficulty, the meaning is that where 'valid and established' was written, it was known through witnesses or admitted by the borrower that those words appeared in the document from the outset. Rambam's ruling in siman 44, seif 8, concerning an erasure, is thereby correct. The objection that the document should not be invalid because one does not derive law from the last line is answered, even where 'valid and established' was written as above. The same question applies to Shulchan Aruch, since all the seifim in siman 44 through seif 9 concern Talmudic law, as shown by seif 9, 'nowadays,' etc. See Shach there, seif katan 17. According to this explanation everything is resolved.
