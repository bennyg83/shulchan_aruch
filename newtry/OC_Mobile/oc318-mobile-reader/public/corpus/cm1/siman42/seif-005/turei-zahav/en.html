(Seif 7: the word “kefel” indicates the smaller amount, etc.) The Sema infers from the Rambam’s reliance on the smaller sum that the Rambam attributes even an elongated letter to damage by a fly, unlike Rashbam and Tosafot. This is incorrect. The Gemara says it is obvious that where the upper line says “sefel” and the lower “kefel,” one follows the lower line; the question is only where the upper says “kefel” and the lower “sefel,” whether to suspect a fly. Thus in the former case there is no doubt: one follows the lower line whether the upper letter was erased by a fly or the writer retracted the first amount. A proof appears in Sotah, where Rabbi Yishmael warned the scribe that his work was heavenly work, and the scribe replied that he did not even fear a fly erasing the crown of a dalet and making it a resh, because he mixed vitriol into the ink so it could not be fully erased. If a fly could add to a letter, the vitriol would not solve the danger. The Rama’s ruling in Even HaEzer regarding a fly dragging ink is distinct. The Rambam’s statement that “kefel” is less than “sefel” merely explains the practical difference and that one follows the lower entry whether it favors or harms the holder. The Tur’s phrase that the holder’s hand is on the lower side explains only why, absent a fly, he receives the smaller amount, not why we adopt the remote possibility of a fly. Similar expressions by scribes are treated differently only when adjacent, as the Nimukei Yosef explains.
