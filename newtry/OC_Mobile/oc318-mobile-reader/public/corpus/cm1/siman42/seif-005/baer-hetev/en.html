To reconcile them. The Sema writes that Darkei Moshe explains that even where the reconciliation is strained, we nevertheless reconcile the two clauses because people ordinarily do not contradict themselves within the brief span of toch k'dei dibbur. The Shach says this is not what Darkei Moshe states. Rather, Darkei Moshe cites Responsa Maimoniyot that whenever the two clauses can be reconciled, they are reconciled even if they are far apart in the document with intervening material, and all the more so when they are adjacent, because people do not ordinarily contradict themselves. But where the clauses can be harmonized only through a strained interpretation, no such rule was stated.
The lower one. The reason is that where two distinct clauses directly contradict one another, the latter is certainly a retraction from the former. Sema.
The lower interpretation. Pesakim U'Ketavim of Mahara'i discusses a document that stated, "All of the above he accepted upon himself by kinyan," but did not say "with a utensil fit for effecting acquisition." Mahara'i ruled that one must be concerned that some other type of kinyan was used rather than chalipin, and therefore the holder of the document has the weaker position. Thus the Sema. The Shach objects that if the alternative kinyan was certainly ineffective, the document would be wholly void, and where the entire document would otherwise be void we do not apply the rule that the holder has the weaker position, as explained in seif 9. One must therefore say the case is where the scribe mistakenly believed the alternative kinyan was effective and the legal effectiveness of that form of kinyan is itself doubtful, similar to the case described in the gloss to seif 9.
