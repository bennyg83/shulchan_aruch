We follow the lower entry. See Baer Heitev and Chut HaShani, end of responsum 38, who writes that one may question whether this law applies in every type of contradiction. Perhaps it applies only to one hundred and two hundred, where one hundred is included within two hundred. But if the upper line says “a barrel of wine” and the lower line says “a barrel of oil,” perhaps the document is invalid. He further writes that it appears one should distinguish as follows: we say that the lower wording represents a retraction only where the retraction depends upon the parties themselves. Where the purported retraction depends upon the witnesses, however, as in his case where the discrepancy concerned the date, we do not say so. For if the witnesses first began to write an earlier year, that writing was false and antedated, and one cannot say that they later retracted, since from the outset they had no authority to write it that way. This is the principal ruling. See there.
The holder of the document has the inferior position. See Baer Heitev, through his citation of the ruling that we must be concerned that the kinyan may have been made by some other mode. See the Taz, who disagrees and rules that the document is valid because ordinarily a kinyan is made by the usual form of kinyan. Chacham Tzvi writes in his glosses that Terumat HaDeshen also agrees to this, and that in the case before him the subject was one for which chalipin could not be effective, such as money. Tumim and Netivot HaMishpat agree with this, and it will be discussed at length below in siman 195, seif 2, seif katan 2. Chacham Tzvi further writes that an unspecified kinyan chalipin plainly means a valid sudar kinyan, and we do not suspect that it was made with something worth less than a perutah, with produce, or with something that is not a utensil. He wrote this last point in passing, however, because we rule that a kinyan may be made with a utensil even if it is worth less than a perutah, as stated below in siman 195, seif 2. See Shevut Yaakov, part II, responsum 152, who writes that although standard documents contain formulas invoking an actual oath, ban, and handshake, and in cases of doubt concerning an oath, ban, or handshake one should be stringent, nevertheless for monetary consequences we say that the holder of the document has the inferior position and money cannot be extracted from the possessor. Moreover, although the document states that these acts were actually performed, everyone knows that in practice no oath or handshake is administered. This does not render the witnesses false, because the party accepts upon himself to perform as though he had actually sworn, so that there should be no defect of asmachta or mere words, but not so as to impose the full severity of an actual oath. See there. See also Rabbi Akiva Eiger, end of responsum 129, concerning a half-zakhar document containing a clause that everything is to be construed in favor of the holder. He writes that one may distinguish: such a clause applies to a doubt in interpreting the language of the document, but not to a doubt in law. He proves this from Shev Yaakov, so that the latter’s responsum 14 will not contradict responsum 21. He then writes that perhaps even in a doubt in law, where the root of the doubt depends upon interpretation of the document, the holder has the superior position, except where that interpretation conflicts with established custom. See there.
