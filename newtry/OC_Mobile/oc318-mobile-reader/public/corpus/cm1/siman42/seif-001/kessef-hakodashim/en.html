We follow the final expression. If a document obligates a sum described in one place by one form of gold coin and later by another current monetary description, this is not necessarily a case where the final wording controls. The intent may simply have been to exclude lighter coins or inferior coins from another country. Nor should we apply the rule that the holder of the document has the weaker hand, because we do not presume the parties made an internally contradictory obligation. The obligor certainly did not agree to a later reduction in value, and at the time of signing the parties did not anticipate the newly arising difference in currency. This appears reasonable.
