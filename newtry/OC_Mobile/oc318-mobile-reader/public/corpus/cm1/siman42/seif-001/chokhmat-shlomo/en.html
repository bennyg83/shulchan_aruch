Seif 9. We do not say that the holder of the document has the inferior position except in cases like those discussed above, where the document is not thereby entirely nullified. At first glance this is very difficult in light of Rashi in Gittin 42, who explains that perhaps one half was written for both recipients, leaving a remainder. The Rosh adds that the holder of the document has the inferior position, and Tur in siman 267 follows this view. Yet if interpreting the document one way would nullify the transaction entirely, we do not place the holder in the inferior position but rather in the superior position. In that case, if one says the document was intended to free only one half, the entire transaction fails according to the Sages there, for neither slave becomes free and consequently neither acquires the property. A case in which one interpretation nullifies everything should therefore favor the holder of the document. Perhaps the answer is that Rabbi there disagrees and holds that one who frees half of a slave effects a valid half-manumission. The master may therefore have erred and believed that such language was effective. This resembles Rama's ruling in the name of Maharik that where the donor could plausibly have been mistaken, we place the holder of the document in the inferior position. If so, the words of Rashi, Tosafot, and the Rosh provide a proof for Maharik's rule. For practical law, however, it requires study why the authorities did not derive a rule for gifts. If a person writes two gift documents, in each saying “half of my property,” and gives both documents to one person to acquire on behalf of two recipients together, we should suspect that he intended the same half in both documents. Each recipient would then acquire only one quarter. Indeed, in such a case everyone should agree, because the transaction is not entirely nullified: each recipient acquires at least one quarter. The rule that the document holder has the inferior position should therefore apply, as appears from Rashi, the Rosh, and Tur. This presently requires study.
