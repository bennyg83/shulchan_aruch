Against the holder of the document. See Baer Heitev, through his citation of the ruling that one must be concerned that the acquisition was made through another form of kinyan, etc. See Taz, who disagrees and rules that the document is valid, because ordinarily it was executed through the usual form of kinyan. See there. The Gaon Chacham Tzvi, in his glosses, writes that Terumat HaDeshen also agrees to this, but that the case he addressed involved property for which chalipin is ineffective, such as money. See there. Tumim and Netivot HaMishpat agree with this explanation. This will be cited below in siman 195, seif 2, seif katan 2, at length. Know that when Chacham Tzvi writes that it is obvious that an unspecified kinyan of chalipin means a sudar kinyan with an object fit for acquisition, and that we do not suspect that it was made with something worth less than a perutah, with a piece of produce, or with an object that is not a utensil, he wrote the reference to an item worth less than a perutah in passing, for we rule that acquisition may be made with a utensil even if it is not worth a perutah, as stated below in siman 195, seif 2. See Responsa Shevut Yaakov, part II, siman 152, who writes that although standard document formulas say that the obligation was undertaken with an oath, severe ban, and formal handshake undertaking in actual effect, and in a doubt concerning an oath, ban, or formal undertaking it is proper to be stringent, nevertheless regarding money we say that the hand of the holder of the document is on the lower side and we do not extract from the party in possession. Furthermore, although documents state that these acts were done in actual effect, everyone knows that no oath or formal handshake undertaking is in fact performed. This is not false testimony, because the person executing the document accepts upon himself to fulfill the obligation as though he actually swore, so that the undertaking not be invalid as an asmachta or mere verbal kinyan. It does not mean that the actual severity of an oath applies. See there. See also Responsa Rabbi Akiva Eiger, end of siman 129, regarding a half male inheritance document in which it is written that every ambiguity shall be interpreted for the benefit of the holder of the document. He writes that one may distinguish and say that this applies only to uncertainty in interpreting the wording of the document, but not to a doubt in law. He proves this from Responsa Shav Yaakov, so that his words in siman 14 not contradict his words in siman 21. He then writes that one can perhaps say that even in a legal doubt, if the root of the doubt depends upon interpreting the document, we say that the holder of the document has the upper hand, except where such an interpretation conflicts with established custom. See there.
