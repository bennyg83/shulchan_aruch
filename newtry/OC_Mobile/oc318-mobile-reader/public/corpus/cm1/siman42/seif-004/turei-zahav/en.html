(Seif 5: the hand of the document-holder is on the lower side.) The Sema cites Terumat HaDeshen, responsum 206, concerning a document stating, “All this he accepted by kinyan,” without the usual phrase “with a valid instrument of acquisition,” and rules it invalid lest the kinyan have been made by some ineffective method. This is not the halachah. Tosafot state that an unspecified “they acquired from him” means a valid kinyan sudar. Terumat HaDeshen’s proof from Tosafot in Ketubot is not compelling. Rivash likewise writes that a document stating “they acquired from him concerning this” should be interpreted as an effective acquisition. The Beit Yosef in Bedek HaBayit, siman 195, expressly rejects Terumat HaDeshen and writes that an unspecified kinyan is presumed valid, while the customary formula is merely added for clarity. One should therefore not rely on Terumat HaDeshen’s ruling. The gloss argues that the later authorities misunderstood Terumat HaDeshen. He did not invalidate the document merely because it omitted the standard formula. Rather, the subject matter itself could not be acquired by ordinary exchange, such as current coinage, and required acquisition through land or another effective method. The questioner argued that unspecified kinyan means exchange, which would invalidate the document. Terumat HaDeshen answered that “they acquired from him” can sometimes mean another acquisition, such as possession, and the phrase “a complete kinyan” may mean an acquisition effective for that item, perhaps through land, pulling, or possession. Nevertheless, he ultimately invalidated it because the holder of the document bears the burden, and the ordinary meaning of unspecified kinyan is exchange. This resembles the Rif’s responsum cited by the Tur regarding coin acquired by kinyan: coin is not acquired by exchange. Although the Raavad disagrees there because the wording can mean an admission, we generally follow the Rif, and in any event the doubt is governed by the rule that the claimant bears the burden. On this reading, Terumat HaDeshen’s ruling remains sound.
