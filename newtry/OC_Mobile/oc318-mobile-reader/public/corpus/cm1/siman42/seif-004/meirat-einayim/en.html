He repeats the matter several times. Meaning that if, on the second attempt, the relevant expression again falls at the end of a line, he repeats it a third time, and similarly from the third to the fourth, until it appears in the middle of a line. In Perishah I wrote that the reason is that one cannot write the masculine forms "three" or "ten" for a count of cubits or fingers, which are feminine nouns. Otherwise there would be a simpler correction: add a final heh to the word at the end of the line.
Such as two or four. The same applies to other letters or numerals susceptible to alteration, such as turning a six into a seven, or a heh into a chet or kuf. Beit Yosef writes likewise regarding the Tur. The Ir Shushan says that if he writes numerals or abbreviations such as twelve or thirteen, the document is valid because they cannot easily be altered. He adds that we need not fear that a two or three was originally written and a yod later added, because witnesses are presumed to act properly and would not have signed if such space had been left. His wording is not precise. There is no reason one may not write the numeral three. Rather, he should have said that we do not fear that a two or three was written and a yod later added, because the witnesses would not have signed while leaving enough space for such addition. If the yod visibly appears squeezed in, the document is not collected upon, as already explained.
