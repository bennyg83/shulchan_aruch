Line. One may write "eleven" at the end of a line. We are not concerned that someone will falsify it into "twenty-one," because that would require adding a vav to make "one and twenty," and the alteration would be evident. Sema in the name of the Rashba.
Three. The Mechaber's phrase "from three through ten" is not necessarily precise, because with the word "eight" there appears to be no concern for forgery. The Ramban, however, writes explicitly that even in masculine-number wording such as "three" or "eight," one should not write it at the edge because the final heh could be erased and the word altered. Sefer Torat Chaim writes that his reasoning is correct. Shach.
It happened. The Bach writes that even if the scribe wrote the masculine form "three" earlier in the middle of the line, if at the end of the line he then wrote the feminine form "three," the document is invalid because it remains possible to forge the amount and the rule is that the lower wording controls. The Shach says this conclusion is not compelling.
For example. The Ir Shushan writes that if the number is written as abbreviated letters, such as 12 or 13, the document is valid because it cannot be forged. We do not fear that it originally said 2 or 3 and that the letter yod was later added, because witnesses are presumed to have acted properly and would not have signed a document written as merely 2 or 3 in a form susceptible to alteration. The Sema objects that there is no problem writing the numeral 3, since one seemingly cannot transform that letter into another. The Shach answers that this is not a valid objection because in Ashurit script the letter gimel can be altered into a nun. See Responsa Maharam Isserles, siman 74, and Mabit, vol. 1, second section, siman 155. The Taz writes similarly.
