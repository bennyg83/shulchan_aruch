All the more so regarding a receipt, the holder of the document, etc. Some emend the text to read, "the holder of the receipt has the stronger hand." That is unnecessary, because the holder of a receipt is also called the holder of a document. Rama calls it an a fortiori because where a person seized property without the owner's permission after the doubt arose, possession can sometimes help; all the more so with a receipt, where the debtor already stands in possession of his own property and we do not extract from him. This is Rama's view. It is difficult, however, because an ambiguous receipt should perhaps not prevail against a clear debt document in the creditor's hands, which creates a lien on land. This is implied by the law below in siman 71 concerning a creditor who made the borrower credible, where the borrower must still swear that he paid because the holder of the document has the weaker hand only to the extent of canceling the debt document and treating the debt as oral. There the reason is that the debt itself is clear in the creditor's document. This also raises a difficulty against Beit Yosef, who writes at the end of seif 10 concerning sefel and kaphal that if the document is a release, we do not attribute the ambiguity to a fly because the burden of proof rests on the one seeking extraction.
