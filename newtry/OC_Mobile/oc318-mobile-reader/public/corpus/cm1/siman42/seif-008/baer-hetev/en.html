In a receipt. The Sema asks why an ambiguous receipt should prevail against a clear debt document held by the lender, which also creates a lien on land. This appears contrary to siman 71, seif 34. The Shach writes that there is no difficulty. Ordinarily the holder of a document has the weaker hand against a receipt, because a receipt is written specifically to cancel the document. Consequently, the holder of the receipt has the stronger hand against the debt document. Once the document has been canceled, however, the receipt holder becomes, with respect to any remaining oral claim, the party relying on a document; there we again apply the rule that the holder of the document has the weaker hand, for example regarding whether he must take an oath. Siman 71 likewise proves against the Sema. The Taz resolves the matter differently. See there.
