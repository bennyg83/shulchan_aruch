(Seif 12: and if he seized, we remove it from him.) This refers to seizure in the presence of witnesses, where no migo is available. The Sema leaves unresolved the prior case in which the word “dinars” was written and the amount erased, asking whether seizure before witnesses helps. In my opinion, because the document contains an erasure, it is treated as though no document exists, leaving only an oral claim, and seizure before witnesses plainly does not help. This differs from uncertainty in the meaning of an intact document, whether favorable or adverse. There, the rule that the document-holder’s hand is on the lower side applies only because he seeks to extract money; if he already seized it, even before witnesses, his hand is on the upper side. For this reason the Beit Yosef notes that the Tur states the effectiveness of seizure before discussing a document that merely says “dinars,” and does not place it before the case of an erased amount, where seizure obviously fails. A similar distinction appears at the end of siman 24. The gloss explains that if seizure were effective where the amount was erased, anyone holding a document for a small sum could erase the amount and seize a much larger sum, destroying orderly justice. In a document that merely states “dinars,” by contrast, the obligor caused his own loss by undertaking an ambiguous obligation. This distinction also appears in the Maggid Mishneh cited by the Beit Yosef in siman 54 and in the Shach.
