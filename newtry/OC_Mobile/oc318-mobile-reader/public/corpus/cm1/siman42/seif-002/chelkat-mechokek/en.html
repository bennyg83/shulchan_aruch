And she did not throw away the kiddushin, she is betrothed. The plain language sounds like definite kiddushin, but that cannot be correct. Her silence occurred only after the money had been given, and such silence is ineffective, as explained above in siman 28:4-5, especially here where the initial giving was against her will. The responsum itself says only that because she did not throw it away she requires a get, which sounds like a stringency. Yet this too is difficult, because above in siman 30:6 it is ruled that even if the money was thrown into her lap and she remained silent, where she never initially consented there are no kiddushin. The same appears in siman 28:4. The Bach there explains the Ramah's case as one where after it was thrown into her lap she shook out her clothing, in order to reconcile that ruling with the present gloss. It seems more correct, however, to read the Ramah according to its plain meaning and say that it disagrees with the Hagahot Mordechai cited here. Rivash, siman 170, likewise says that where she performed no act showing consent, there are no kiddushin. Her failure to protest expressly proves nothing, because not all women know the law and she may fear that throwing the money away would make her liable for it. Thus Hagahot Mordechai follows Rav Huna stringently, that if she did not want the kiddushin she should have thrown them away, whereas the Ramah elsewhere follows the contrary rule. The two rulings therefore cannot simply be reconciled.
