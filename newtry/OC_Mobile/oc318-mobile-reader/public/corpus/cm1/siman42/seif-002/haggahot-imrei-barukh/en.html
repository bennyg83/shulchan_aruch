(Rama, seif katan 9) Some say that this applies specifically, etc. Note: This requires study in the Ran on the Rif, chapter 4 of Gittin, in the discussion of one who writes all his property to two slaves. The Ramban explains why writing one half to each does not free them: because the holder of a document has the weaker hand, the master can say that he intended to give each slave property equal to half the value of all his assets, but did not free the slave's own person. In other words, the value represented by half of the slave himself was included in the gift, yet the slave's body remained enslaved, and therefore the attempted disposition is ineffective. This is difficult, because where that interpretation would make the entire document void, we ordinarily do not invoke the principle that the holder of the document has the weaker hand. Nor can we say that the master merely made a legal mistake and thought he could give each slave the equivalent of his value in the rest of the estate while retaining the slave himself, because he executed both documents at the same time. If the non-slave assets were worth two hundred and the two slaves together another two hundred, each document purported to convey two hundred. If the slaves remained enslaved, there would not be enough non-slave property to satisfy both gifts. Even if the non-slave assets were worth two hundred and the slaves only one hundred, each slave's half-share would be worth one hundred fifty. If neither slave were freed, satisfying the first slave with one hundred fifty would leave only fifty for the second. To satisfy the second as well would necessarily require freedom of at least one slave. In short, if the master's asserted intent was to give the slaves property while retaining their bodies in servitude, what coherent allocation could he have contemplated that would give each one the full half-share stated in his document?
