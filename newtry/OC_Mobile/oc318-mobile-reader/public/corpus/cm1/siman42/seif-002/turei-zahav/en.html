(There: the defendant did not plead the particular defect, etc.) It appears unnecessary for him to plead the exact defect. Whenever he advances any claim and the defect pertains to that matter, the court raises it for him. Although the Tur concludes, “It appears to me that even if the defendant does not plead it, the judge must raise it,” implying the first opinion disagrees, the Tur’s disagreement concerns a defect wholly unrelated to the defendant’s own claim. Where the defect directly fits his claim, even the first opinion agrees that the court raises it. Maharshal objects that a litigant cannot plead that a single witness is not believed, as stated in siman 17. Perhaps forgery differs because the single witness has no legal force, whereas another single witness imposes an oath. According to my earlier explanation, however, the Tur did not mean the judge should decide the case on that basis, but that he should confront the litigant and say, “This witness has testified against you,” so the objection disappears.
