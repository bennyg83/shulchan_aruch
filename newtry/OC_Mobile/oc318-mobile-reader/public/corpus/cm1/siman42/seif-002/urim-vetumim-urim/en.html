But an acquisition document. This means a document written on erasable pottery saying, “My field is given to you,” which was delivered before delivery witnesses. Everyone agrees it can acquire the field, although witnesses will still be needed later to prove the acquisition, because the writing itself is not designed as lasting evidence. If it was delivered privately and the seller admits the delivery, Sema holds that the acquisition is effective, Bach disagrees, and Shach rules like Sema. In Tumim I explain that this depends on an early dispute among the authorities. If the buyer later produces a shard stating, “My field is given to you,” and the seller says, “That never happened; I never sold it,” Shach says the seller becomes established as a false denier. In Tumim I disagree. Who knows where the buyer obtained the shard? Perhaps it previously contained unrelated writing, which he erased and replaced with these words. See there.
