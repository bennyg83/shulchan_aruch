And it is possible to reconcile both statements. Sema cites Hagahot Maimoniyot that even if the reconciliation is somewhat strained, we preserve the document because people do not ordinarily contradict themselves. Sema explains the distance as physical distance in the document, such as one statement near the beginning and another near the end. Hagahot Maimoniyot more naturally means a strained interpretive reconciliation. Still, Sema's approach can be supported by the rule in siman 29 concerning two witnesses whose statements appear contradictory: we reconcile them even through a somewhat remote explanation. Yet one can distinguish. There, failure to reconcile would make one witness a liar, and witnesses are not presumed to lie. Here, one can simply say that the writer retracted an earlier formulation. No one becomes disqualified. Why should we prefer an unnatural reading over ordinary retraction? See Tumim.
