Since he raised him. See Tumim, who cites Knesset HaGedolah as distinguishing that this applies only where the person who raised the child has no sons of his own. Tumim writes that he does not know the source for this distinction, for the reason given in Responsa Maimoniyos is that one who raises an orphan in his home, etc., and also because of affection, as in the verse, 'My son, incline your ear,' and both reasons apply even where he has sons. See there. See Responsa Chatam Sofer, Even HaEzer, siman 76, who writes that the reasoning of Knesset HaGedolah is sound. When a person has no sons, people sometimes call a beloved child whom they raised 'my son,' and he is not concerned that this wording will cause future harm by allowing the foster child to inherit, because he does not care so greatly whether his wife's beloved son inherits his property rather than his brothers or other relatives. Everyday experience supports this. If, however, he has sons of his own, he is very concerned that the wording not create error and loss by depriving his own sons of their inheritance. We have a presumption that a person does not abandon his own child and give his property to others. Therefore, a document written in such a form is invalid. On this basis Chatam Sofer resolves the words of Tosafos in Pesachim 54a. See there further. I cited this in Pitchei Teshuva to Even HaEzer, siman 19, seif katan 3.
