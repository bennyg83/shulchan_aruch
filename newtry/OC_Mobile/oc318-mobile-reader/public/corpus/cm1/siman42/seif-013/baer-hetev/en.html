Dinarei. The Sema writes that the general rule in these laws is as follows. The plain term "dinarei" refers to gold dinars, while the plain term "dinarin" can refer either to silver or to gold. Since the holder of the document has the weaker hand, he receives only silver. This applies where the wording is unstated. If the document expressly says "silver dinarei" or "silver dinars," or "gold dinarin" or "gold dinars," we follow the explicit metal named. This is where no prefix meaning "in" appears. If it says "silver in dinarei," the phrase is interpreted as silver worth two gold dinars. Likewise, "gold in dinarin" means gold worth two silver dinars. According to this, the Sema emends the Mechaber's wording in the first clause and in the latter clause to preserve this grammatical distinction. The Shach writes that the text should not be emended.
