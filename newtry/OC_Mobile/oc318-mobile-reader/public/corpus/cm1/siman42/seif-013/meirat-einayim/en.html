If it says "silver dinrei," etc. The general rule is this. The unqualified word "dinrei" ordinarily refers to gold dinars. The unqualified word "dinarin" can refer to either silver or gold, and because the holder of the document has the weaker hand, only silver is paid. But if the writing expressly says "silver dinrei," "dinrei silver," "gold dinarin," or "dinarin gold," we follow the material specified next to the noun. This applies where "dinrei" is written without the prefixed bet. If it says "silver in dinrei," the bet separates the expressions, and we interpret it as silver worth two gold dinars. Likewise "gold in dinarin" means gold worth two silver dinars. Based on this, I wrote in Perishah that in the Tur and Mechaber the first clause should read "silver dinrei" or "dinrei silver," both without a final nun, while the latter clause should read "gold dinarin" or "dinarin gold," both with a final nun. Each formulation teaches that when the material is explicitly stated, the precise form dinrei or dinarin is not decisive.
