(Seif 8: concerning a receipt, the holder’s hand is on the upper side.) The Sema asks from siman 71, where a lender trusted the borrower and we say he trusted him only with an oath, because the debt is certain. Why not say the same here? There, the borrower’s right depends entirely on the lender’s words, and he cannot know with certainty what the lender intended. Therefore any plausible limiting interpretation advanced by the lender is accepted. The same logic appears in siman 65 concerning “one document of yours is forgiven.” A receipt, however, is made to impair the document. Its language is uncertain, and the borrower asserts a definite interpretation, so the case resembles an erasure in the document where the erased matter is unknown: the document itself has been impaired. The real difficulty is from siman 49, where a document identifies the debtor by three generations but the receipt does not, and the Ramah rules the receipt-holder’s hand is on the lower side. That appears to contradict the present rule. The Tur disputes that ruling and favors the receipt-holder. The opinion here, from the Nimukei Yosef, likely disagrees with the Ramah there, while the dissent here follows him. The Rama should therefore have ruled in siman 49 against the Mechaber. Independently, the Tur’s view is correct because the claimant bears the burden of proof.
