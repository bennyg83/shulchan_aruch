And it shall be given to Chanani. Urim VeTumim concludes, against Sema, that even if the deed is in Chanan's possession he cannot collect. If the deed is in the hands of a shalish who says Chanan lent the money and the name 'Chanani' was written by mistake, he cannot contradict the signed witnesses and turn them into mistaken witnesses. If the deed itself is in Chanan's hands, Chanan still cannot simply collect because the borrower can say, 'I borrowed from Chanani and repaid him, and the document fell from me and you found it.' At first glance Chanani likewise could not collect because the paper is in Chanan's possession and the borrower may claim it fell from him. Yet according to the principles I explain later in siman 66, even where the paper is in Chanan's hands, Chanani can in certain circumstances collect. The Yerushalmi text cited by Sema regarding 'Chanan' above and 'Chanani' below contains a textual error. The real unresolved question is how to read two incomplete or differing forms where scribes commonly omit letters. If the two possible recipients give one another authorization, collection can proceed regardless of which form is intended.
