The lower wording is learned from the upper. Scribes commonly omit or erase a single letter. See Netivot: this rule applies only to the name of the lender or borrower. If the amount of money is missing or erased, even by one letter, we do not reconstruct it from the other line. See also my comments. Regarding the lender's name, if the upper line says Chanan and the lower says Chanani, payment is given to Chanani even if the document is presently held by Chanan. But where two words are missing or erased in the upper line, the lower line controls. If two words are erased in the lower line, the entire document is invalid because perhaps they were erased deliberately in order to invalidate it. Even if the document is held by the person favored by either the upper or lower text, he cannot use it to extract money. Nevertheless, because he possesses the physical document, it is not removed from him. Urim VeTumim.
Two. If the upper line says Chanan and the lower says Chanani, we do not say that each teaches the other, treating the upper as missing the yod and the lower as missing the chet so that both become Chanani. This is an unresolved Talmudic question, and the burden of proof rests upon the claimant. The document is given to neither Nani nor Chanani. If they write authorizations to one another, they may collect. Chanan, however, cannot collect even through authorization.
