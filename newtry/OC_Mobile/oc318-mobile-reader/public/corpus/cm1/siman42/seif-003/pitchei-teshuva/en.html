We follow the lower wording. See Baer Heitev. See Responsa Chut HaShani, end of siman 38, who writes that there is room to question whether this rule applies in every matter. Perhaps it applies only to one hundred and two hundred, because one hundred is included within two hundred. If, however, the upper text states a barrel of wine and the lower text states a barrel of oil, perhaps the document is invalid. He further writes that one should distinguish as follows: only a retraction that depends upon the parties can be treated as a retraction. A discrepancy that depends upon the witnesses, such as in his case where the date differed, cannot be treated in this way. If the document began by recording the year 5347, that is already a falsification and an antedated document, and one cannot say that the witnesses later retracted, since from the outset they had no right to write it in that form. He concludes that this is the primary view. See there.
