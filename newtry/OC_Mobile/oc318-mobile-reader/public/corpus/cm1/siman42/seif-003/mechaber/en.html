One of the requirements in properly drafting documents is to examine carefully the letters vav and zayin so that they are not compressed between words, lest someone have forged the document by adding a vav or zayin. They also should not be overly separated, lest someone have erased another letter, such as a heh or chet, and left one leg appearing as a vav. The same care applies to similar possibilities in every language, as stated by the Tur here, and in every script. Therefore the scribe must be careful that the letters in the document resemble one another and that the writing is straight and consistent throughout. He should not space letters excessively, compress them excessively, compress in one place and spread them out in another. The witnesses therefore may not sign until they have carefully examined all the letters. Likewise, when a document comes before a judge, he must inspect all its letters and compare one letter with another. If he sees any irregularity, he may not collect on the document until he has investigated the matter carefully. Whenever an irregularity can reasonably be attributed to an erasure, we attribute it to an erasure. If necessary, the holder of the document may be compelled and even struck until he admits the truth, in order to establish the correct judgment. Some say this applies only where he seeks to extract property from heirs or purchasers, because we advance claims on behalf of an heir or purchaser. Where, however, he seeks to extract from the borrower, seller, or giver himself, and the defendant does not raise a claim relevant to the defect found in the document, beit din does not raise that claim on his behalf.
