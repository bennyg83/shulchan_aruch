[Seif 7] Both entered with permission. The Tur explains, following Tosafot, that where both entered the owner's courtyard either with permission or without permission, each is liable for direct damage even without intent. Each should have anticipated that just as the owner gave him permission, he may have given another person permission, or that just as he entered without permission, another may also have done so. Nevertheless, each is exempt for damage that occurred indirectly so long as he did not intend it, because it ultimately turned out that the other had no right there. One who entered without permission is liable for direct damage to one who entered with permission even without intent, since he should have anticipated that another might also enter without permission, and all the more so when the other in fact entered with permission. For indirect damage, however, he is exempt. This is Tosafot's approach as explained by Maharshal. Maharshal further writes that the rule that two persons who are both there with permission or both without permission are liable for direct unintentional damage applies specifically when people injure one another. If one entered with permission and the other brought his property in with permission, or both entered without permission, and one person's property damaged the other's property unintentionally, he is exempt. The Rambam rules accordingly. The Raavad objected from the statement that where they damaged one another, they are liable even without intent, but it appears that there is no objection, because one may distinguish between bodily injury to a person and damage to property. This is preferable to the Maggid Mishneh's different reconciliation.
