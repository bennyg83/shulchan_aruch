Whether he fell because of an ordinary wind, etc. In the Derishah I discussed the Tur's statement that if he fell because of an unusual wind he is exempt. This is difficult, because the Tur himself writes later in siman 421 that one who falls because of an unusual wind is liable for actual bodily damage, and the Gemara says the same. I distinguished the cases. There the victim is a person injured by the fall from the roof. If the fall occurred through an ordinary wind, the one who fell is liable for four categories of damages. With an unusual wind we reduce the liability by one level and impose only actual damage. Here, however, the case concerns damage to utensils caused by the fall. Even with an ordinary wind, liability is only for actual damage. Therefore, where an unusual wind caused the fall, the Tur holds that we reduce liability one level further and impose no payment at all. This explains the Mechaber's seemingly repetitive and unclear wording. In seif 1 he writes generally that one who falls from a roof and damages utensils must pay, without saying that this applies even to an unusual wind. In seif 2 he again discusses a fall from a roof and expressly says that liability exists even where the wind was unusual. According to the distinction just stated, seif 1 concerns damage to utensils and therefore does not include unusual wind, while seif 2 concerns injury to a person and therefore does. Although the Beit Yosef did not accept this distinction and challenged the Tur's exemption, it is possible that in the Shulchan Aruch he reconsidered and accepted it.
