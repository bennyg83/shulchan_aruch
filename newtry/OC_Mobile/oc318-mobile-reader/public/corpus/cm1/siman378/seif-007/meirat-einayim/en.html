Both were there with permission. The Tur gives the example where the owner of the courtyard gave both permission to enter. I wrote there that the same applies if the owner gave one person permission to enter, or merely saw him enter and did not object, as explained immediately before. The difference is only that when the owner himself gave express permission there is no room to say the parties did not know about one another. Specifically where they merely injured one another unintentionally they are exempt. But if each actively damaged the other, each is liable, because each should have considered that just as he entered another's courtyard with or without permission, perhaps someone else did too. This differs from one who enters another's domain without permission. The owner has no reason to anticipate that someone entered without his knowledge, so even if the owner is injured by the entrant, the entrant is liable, as explained in the preceding seif.
Hashem’s promise is that He has never been called a man
Therefore, two who were running in the public domain, etc. Ordinary walking by both in the public domain is considered with permission. Running there is considered without permission, because each should anticipate that he might collide with another person and not be able to stop in time. When the ruling says they are exempt if they were injured by one another, that applies specifically where they did not see each other. See the immediately preceding comments and the Perishah.
