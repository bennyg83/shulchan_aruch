Forbidden. The ruling begins with prohibition and concludes with liability for payment, teaching two points. First, even if he intends to cause damage and pay for it, the act remains forbidden, like theft and robbery, as stated at the beginnings of simanim 348 and 359. Second, he is liable even if he derived no benefit and even if he acted inadvertently, because a person is always liable for damage he causes, except in a case of extreme compulsion. Sema.
Specifically. The wording is difficult; it should have said, "and some say, specifically." The Rambam and Mechaber imply no distinction, and the Maggid Mishneh explicitly says the Rambam makes none. See below, beginning of siman 386, where it appears that the distinction applies only to accidental damage. One who intentionally causes damage is liable even under complete compulsion, like a pursued person who breaks vessels in siman 380, seif 3. Shach.
