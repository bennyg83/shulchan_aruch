It is prohibited to damage another person's property, etc. The Tur begins with the prohibition and concludes with the obligation to pay in order to teach two separate points. First, even if a person intends to damage and then pay, the act itself is prohibited, just like theft and robbery, as the Tur and Mechaber write above at the beginning of simanim 348 and 359. For this reason the Tur links the prohibition of causing damage here with the prohibitions of theft and robbery. Second, one who damages must pay even though he derived no benefit and even though he acted unintentionally, because a person is always forewarned regarding damage unless the event involved an extraordinary compulsion. See the Perishah. Nimukei Yosef in chapter HaManiach writes that the Gemara's statement, "One who wishes to be pious should fulfill the laws of damages," includes all the laws taught in the three Bava tractates, namely damages, robbery, returning lost property, interest, and ona'ah. See Darkei Moshe at the beginning of this siman.
