If one came riding on his horse, etc. The Tur cites the Rosh's responsum. Reuven erred in thinking he was exempt merely because both parties were riding in the public domain and the injured person should have protected himself. That is no defense. A person may run in the public domain even on foot, where he can stop whenever he wishes, yet if he causes damage he can still be liable. All the more so one riding a horse should not gallop in a place where people ride, because he may be unable to stop the horse when he wishes. His conduct is negligent, and because he rides the animal, damage caused through the horse's body or saddle is treated as damage he caused and he must pay. If the injured horse belonged to a gentile and the gentile imposed additional loss upon its owner because of the incident, the rider who caused the damage is not liable for more than the amount assessed by beit din, since he did not intend that additional loss. The Mechaber therefore concludes that the tortfeasor pays no more than the amount assessed by the judges. See also siman 72 concerning a pledged knife lost after being lent onward, and see the Perishah.
