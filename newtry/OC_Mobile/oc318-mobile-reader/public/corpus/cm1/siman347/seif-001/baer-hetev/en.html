Be damaged. It appears that even if he did not spell out the condition, since ordinarily the borrower's obligation is to guard the animal both from causing damage and from being damaged, and here he specifically accepted responsibility only that it not cause damage, it is as though he expressly stipulated that he did not accept responsibility that it not be damaged. This is also implied by Rashi's wording at the end of the first chapter of Bava Kamma. As a matter of practical law, however, it appears that even if he explicitly stipulated that he would not guard the animal from being damaged, he must nevertheless pay half-damages if his own innocuous ox injures it, because the borrower is obligated to guard his own ox from causing damage. The lender's ox is no worse than any other person's ox. Sema.
To pay. It appears that he pays according to the liability that would have rested upon the lender himself: for an innocuous ox, half-damages, and for a forewarned ox, full damages. He accepted only to stand in the lender's place, and therefore assumes the lender's measure of liability. Shach.
Accepted. Here too the meaning is as explained above: whenever he specifically says only that it not be damaged, it is as though he expressly said, "but I do not accept responsibility that it not cause damage." Shach.
