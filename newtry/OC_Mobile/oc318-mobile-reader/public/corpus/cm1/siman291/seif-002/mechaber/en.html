A gratuitous bailee is one to whom money, utensils, an animal, or any object is entrusted for safekeeping and who accepts its custody. Even without explicit acceptance, saying 'Place it before me' makes him a gratuitous bailee. Saying 'Place it before yourself,' 'Put it down,' or 'The house is before you' does not, and he owes no bailee's oath, though a ban may be declared against whoever took the deposit and does not return it. On the road, however, one who directs that shoes be placed on his donkey may become a gratuitous bailee because the setting itself requires custody; leaving the donkey unattended by the roadside can constitute negligence. Some disagree and require the words 'Place it before me' even in an unguarded place.
