(And his mother shall swear that she concealed them.) The Gemara states: the mother shall swear that she placed those coins in her basket and that they were stolen. It is difficult why she need not swear only that the money her son gave her was stolen, and why she must mention that she placed it in a chest. Even had she left it exposed, which would be a far more serious act of negligence, she would nevertheless be exempt toward the depositor, since she did not know that it was deposited property. One may answer that the mother must still present a valid claim according to her own understanding that the money belonged to her son. She thought the money was intended for expenditure and therefore did not require burial, as the Rosh writes there. Had she not placed it in a chest at all, she would at least have damaged her son's property according to her own understanding and would be liable to him. Consequently she would pay the depositor in any circumstance in which she would be liable to pay her son. I was asked about one who gave another an article to sell in his shop and fixed his compensation. The recipient later left the shop and the article was lost. The recipient claimed: "When I left the shop, I told you to watch my shop and you agreed." The giver denied accepting responsibility to guard. The recipient argued: "According to your version, you admit that you did not guard at all. According to my claim, you became my unpaid custodian and are liable for negligence. Since I am in possession, I should swear to my claim and be exempt." I answered that the recipient is liable. Even according to his own account the giver is exempt. Although the giver became an unpaid custodian, this was safeguarding while the owner was in his service. Safeguarding is considered to be while the owner is in his service whenever, at the time the custodian accepts responsibility for the article, the owner of the article is obligated to provide the custodian with some service, however slight. The Gemara in the chapter HaShoel states that one who wishes to borrow an item from another and be exempt should say to him at the moment of borrowing, "Give me water to drink." Here too, the giver received a benefit from the recipient, because at the moment the giver accepted responsibility as an unpaid custodian, the recipient was already obligated to him as his paid custodian. The giver as an unpaid custodian would be exempt from theft and loss, whereas the recipient as a paid custodian is liable for theft and loss. This was therefore borrowing while the owner was in his service, and he is exempt even for negligence, as stated at the end of siman 251. This appears correct to me.
