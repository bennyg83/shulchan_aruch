Seif 6. If he was negligent with it and did not guard it properly. Note: I saw that Machaneh Ephraim, Hilchot Shomrim, questions a case where one deposits property with another, the custodian sees that it is deteriorating, and he fails to sell it through beit din. Is he liable because this is negligence, or, since the loss occurred by itself and not through the custodian's act, perhaps although he should initially have acted to save it, his failure is not included in his custodial liability? He discusses the case of a paid custodian who should have confronted thieves with shepherds and staffs, rejects its relevance, and also cites Tur, siman 443. With due respect, however, he overlooked the explicit ruling in Shulchan Aruch, Choshen Mishpat 72:27: if the creditor admits that he placed the pledged item somewhere not protected from mice, or failed to air it properly, he must pay the resulting depreciation. Thus, even where the deterioration occurred by itself, failure to air it properly creates liability because he was obligated to do so. One cannot answer that there the owner was absent, while Machaneh Ephraim distinguishes whether the owner was present. If that distinction were correct, the Shulchan Aruch should have taught that even if the custodian admits that he failed to air it, he is exempt when he can say, 'You were in my city when the loan was made, and I never accepted responsibility for spreading and airing it.' The omission shows that there is no such distinction. Nor can one distinguish a deposit from collateral, for even with collateral the debtor could come to the creditor's home to spread and air it, while the creditor could guard it personally or through members of his household. There is also no basis to distinguish whether the deterioration had already begun before the negligence or began only because of it. Whatever further deterioration occurs after he negligently fails to sell is a new loss. Machaneh Ephraim himself compares the matter to chametz before Pesach, where the loss begins only because of the custodian's failure to act, showing that he too does not maintain such a distinction. It therefore requires study how all this escaped his attention.
