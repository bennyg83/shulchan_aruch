Leave it. If he receives payment, he becomes a paid bailee even with this wording. This is implied by Bava Metzia 81b, Tosafot there 99a, and the Rosh. According to the latter view in seif 5, however, the Maggid Mishneh writes in the name of the Rashba and Raavad that the case here is where the owner placed the item before him in a side alley and within his four cubits. The Nimukei Yosef likewise rules in their names. They did not explain that it was placed in the bailee's courtyard because the Gemara implies that the case occurred in the marketplace; they therefore explain that 'marketplace' means a side alley. The Maggid Mishneh and Nimukei Yosef also write that the case involved an animal, and the owner told him, 'Strike it with a stick and it will come.' This answer does not accord with the practical halachah, as stated in siman 360, seif 7; see what is written there. See Responsa Ibn Lev, part I, general rule 17, beginning of siman 95. Ibid.
Or. The fact that the two cases are equated applies only to damage that comes from an external source. But where the damage is caused by the bailee or his animals, if the owner merely said 'leave it,' the bailee is liable according to Rabbeinu Yonah, the Rosh, and the Tur, who rule like the Sages. According to the Rif, who rules like Rabbi, he is not liable at all. This is also the Mechaber's ruling in seif 3. These are the words of the Sema. His intent is that the cases are equated only in a courtyard. In the marketplace, however, there may be no distinction: according to the Sages, even damage caused by the bailee himself may be exempt, while according to Rabbi, even externally caused damage may create liability. This is explicit in Bava Metzia there, 81b. Ibid.
Before you. Even though he accepted payment from him, he nevertheless said, 'The house is before you.' So writes Bedek HaBayit in the name of the Ritva. Meaning, although he received payment that would compensate him if he actually guarded the item, since he said, 'The house is before you,' he never accepted responsibility for guarding and is not even an unpaid bailee. This is demonstrated by the Gemara. See Responsa Mahara Sasson, siman 144, and Responsa R. Levi Ibn Chaviv, siman 94. Ibid.
On the road. The Sema writes that in his Perishah and Derishah he proved that even if, in the marketplace, the owner merely said, 'Leave it,' the recipient does not have the status of an unpaid bailee. See there. The Shach writes that this is indeed the Gemara's ruling in chapter HaUmanin.
