"Money or tools," he said, "let's give a man money or tools to keep, and so on, I will give him a certain amount of money, and he will be able to save him, and he will not be able to do so."
"Place it before you," or simply "place it." Even the law of an unpaid custodian does not apply in these formulations. In the Perishah I proved that equating the two expressions is specific to damage that comes from outside. But if the deposit is damaged through the custodian himself or his animals, then according to Ri, the Rosh, and Tur, who rule like the Sages, he is liable where the owner said, "place it there." According to the Rif, who rules like Rabbi, he is not liable at all. The Mechaber follows that view in the next seif.
But on the road, etc. In the Perishah and Derishah I proved that even if the owner says in the marketplace, "place it," without more, the recipient does not become an unpaid custodian, because he can say that the owner meant, "put it down and watch it yourself." The case is different when the recipient is departing on a journey and the owner remains behind. If the owner says, "Place these shoes on the donkey," he clearly intends the traveler to guard them, because otherwise who will guard them once the owner remains behind? Rama writes that some disagree, and the Ir Shushan follows him. Darkei Moshe too discusses this at length. I did not find anyone who disagrees in the actual case where the item is given to him to transport on the road while the owner remains behind. The dispute concerns only an unspecified "place it" in the marketplace where both people remain present. Although responsum of the Rosh uses wording similar to Rama's, saying that the exemption was stated only in the recipient's house, which is a guarded place, I proved in the Derishah that he does not mean to limit the rule literally to a house and exclude a marketplace. On the contrary, where both remain in the marketplace there is even stronger reason to say he did not accept custody. The Rosh means only to exclude the case where the item is placed on the recipient's donkey and transported away, even though the donkey too is under his control and might be compared to his house. There the recipient plainly assumed responsibility.
