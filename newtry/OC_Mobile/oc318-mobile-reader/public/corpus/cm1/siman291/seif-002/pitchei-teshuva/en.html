And he accepted upon himself to guard it, etc. Tzemach Tzedek, responsum 23, discusses a woman who carried her sister's infant into the street. The mother placed a dinar in the child's hand to play with, and it was later lost. The mother argued that she relied on her sister to watch it. Tzemach Tzedek exempts the woman, because the mother said nothing to her when giving the coin to the child, so this is worse than merely saying 'leave it' and no custodial obligation was accepted. Even if the woman briefly took the dinar from the child and returned it to him, she remains exempt because the original placement was a knowing exposure of the coin to loss. Shaar HaMishpat questions this from the rule concerning a husband and borrowed property, where liability may arise without an express acceptance of guardianship because acceptance is presumed. Pitchei Teshuva answers that Bach explains the husband's rule through his special legal status as purchaser of his wife's property and the Rambam's rule that once he is informed that an item is borrowed, he steps into her position. That rationale does not apply here. This is especially significant because Tzemach Tzedek was a disciple of Bach and generally followed his rulings. See further in siman 349.
