But if he said, 'Place it before me,' he is an unpaid custodian. If he receives payment, he is a paid custodian even though he used the expression, 'Place it before me.' This is implied by the Gemara in Perek HaOmanin, Bava Metzia 81b, and by Tosafot in Perek HaShoel, 99a, and the Rosh there. According to the final view cited below in seif 5, the Maggid Mishna writes in the name of the Rashba and Raavad that here the depositor placed the item before him in an alley and within his four cubits. The Nimukei Yosef likewise writes this in their name. They did not explain the case simply as occurring in the custodian's courtyard because the Gemara in Perek HaOmanin implies that it occurred in the marketplace. They therefore explain that 'marketplace' means an alley. The Maggid Mishna and Nimukei Yosef further write that the case concerns an animal, and the owner said, 'Strike it with a stick and it will come.' This answer, however, does not accord with the final halacha, as explained below, siman 340, seif 7. See what I write there. See Responsa Mahari ibn Lev, part I, klal 17, beginning of siman 96.
'Place it before yourself,' or simply, 'Place it.' The law regarding the unqualified expression, 'Place it,' remained unresolved. The Sema writes that in the Perisha he proved that the reason the two expressions are equated applies only to damage coming from an external source. His meaning is that even in a courtyard they are equated only regarding external damage. In the marketplace, however, there may be no distinction. According to the Sages, he is exempt even for damage caused by the custodian himself, while according to Rabbi he is liable even for damage coming from elsewhere. This is stated in the Gemara, Perek HaOmanin, Bava Metzia 81b. See there.
The house is before you. Even though he received rental payment from the depositor, he nevertheless said, 'The house is before you.' Bedek HaBayit writes this in the name of the Ritva. Meaning, although he would receive payment for guarding if he actually undertook to guard, since he said, 'The house is before you,' he did not accept responsibility for guarding and is not even an unpaid custodian. This is proved by the Gemara. See there. See Responsa Maharan Sasson, siman 144, and Responsa Rabbi Levi ibn Chaviv, siman 94.
