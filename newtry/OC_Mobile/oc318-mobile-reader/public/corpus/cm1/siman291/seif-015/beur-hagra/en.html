And bars of metal, etc. Rambam, because they share the same rationale as money: people risk themselves for them, they are easy to carry, and they are not damaged by burial in the ground, as the Rosh and Rambam write there.
They have no, etc. This follows the Rif's reading, which does not read the passage "nowadays, etc.; feelers, etc.; Persians, etc." Rather, it reads: "money for separation, Rav said, and Shmuel concedes, etc.; and nowadays, etc." This fits the Gemara's later statement, "there we learned, etc.," which then applies even to the conclusion. See Lechem Mishneh.
(Collected note) They have no, etc. According to the Rif's reading, the entire passage from "nowadays" until Rava's statement is omitted. According to our reading, Shmuel's rule is that placing money in a wall always suffices, while nowadays burial in the ground does not suffice. According to the Rif, Rambam's wording is clear, because "nowadays" refers only to a wall. The Rif's reading is correct, for according to our reading Rava's statement, "there is no difference here," is difficult. The Rosh is forced to explain it as even when not between the rows of bricks, which is strained. It is also strained to say that "between the rows of bricks" does not count as being in a wall. Moreover, the Gemara should not have said "and Shmuel concedes"; it should have said, "Rav said, and regarding a wall even when not between the rows of bricks." According to the Rif, the question in chapter 2 of Pesachim 31b also applies nowadays.
