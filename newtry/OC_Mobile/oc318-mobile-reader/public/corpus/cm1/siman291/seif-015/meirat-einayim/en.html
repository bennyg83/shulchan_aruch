Money and dinars, etc. Ordinary "money" means silver coins. "Dinars" here means gold dinars, as the Tur and Mechaber use the terms in siman 42. "Tongues" means long, broad pieces of silver or gold shaped like a tongue. Rabbeinu Tam writes, as cited by the Beit Yosef, that the requirement to guard money in the ground applied in their time because their houses were unstable. In our time, such guarding is not necessarily required. See Terumat HaDeshen siman 153 regarding a roofed room constructed like a vault.

Even if he does not put them in the middle of the wall's thickness. Meaning that the Sages did not burden him to place the money precisely in the center. But if he buries it more deeply in the ground or places it farther within the thickness of the wall than one handbreadth, that is better. This is unlike the Ir Shushan, who writes that one should not bury money deeply because deceivers probe the ground and can detect a hollow space, and therefore one should cover it with only one handbreadth of soil. The Gemara in HaMafkid proves otherwise. Rav Acha asked Rav Ashi about the Mishnah that when chametz is buried under a collapse, Rabban Shimon ben Gamliel requires a depth a dog cannot dig through, namely three handbreadths. Do we also require three handbreadths here? Rav Ashi answered that there three are needed because of scent, while here the concern is only concealment from sight, for which one handbreadth suffices. The Gemara shows that more than one handbreadth is certainly not worse. The Rif, Rosh, and Nimukei Yosef also indicate this.
