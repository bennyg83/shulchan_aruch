[Shulchan Aruch] Without a kinyan. Note: See Bava Metzia 58a and Tosafot there. One practical difference is whether an unpaid custodian may stipulate to assume the liability of a paid custodian. According to Tosafot's second answer, a kinyan is required. See Mishneh LaMelech, chapter 2 of Hilchot Sechirut. Another practical difference concerns an unpaid custodian where the owner is with him who stipulates to be liable for unavoidable accidents. According to Tosafot's first answer a kinyan is required, whereas according to their second answer it is not. The same issue applies to land and slaves where one stipulates to be liable for unavoidable accidents. Maharit's derashot, Parashat Chukat, derush 2, writes that with land and similar property, even according to Tosafot's second answer a kinyan is required. See there. See also Sema below, siman 301. Shitah Mekubetzet cites Geonim who explain that with ordinary private transactions, where a person would normally take precautions to guard his own property, the depositor's entrusting it to the custodian and relying upon him gives significance to the undertaking. This does not apply to consecrated property, where the idea of personally guarding it is inapplicable. According to this explanation, with slaves and land no kinyan is required. See also Machaneh Ephraim, siman 8, who concludes that in every case a stipulation is effective to impose liability for negligence. The Gaon who authored Birkei Yosef writes in Shem HaGedolim, in his discussion at the beginning of chapter 5 of Avot, as a simple matter that the same applies if one gives another a silver dinar and stipulates that if it is lost he will be reimbursed with a gold dinar: if it is lost, he must pay a gold dinar. In my humble opinion, this ruling is not at all simple, and the stronger inclination is to exempt him. Examine carefully.
