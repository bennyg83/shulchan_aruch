[Shach, subparagraph 34] It is obvious that he can require an oath. Note: In my opinion, when the Rosh writes that she is not believed against an uncertain claim, his intent is that she can say, "You are not my litigant, and I need not believe you that this money is yours. You cannot make me swear on an uncertain claim merely because perhaps you know that you are my litigant because my son told me that the money belongs to you." According to this, a gilgul oath also cannot be imposed upon her, because she says, "You are not my litigant. My oath is with respect to my son, and there is no basis for gilgul." However, this is difficult according to Chelkas Mechokek, who holds that if she was negligent her son must pay. Since the son must swear that he actually gave that money to his mother, why need he not also swear by gilgul that he did not tell her it was a deposit? Had he told her, her negligence would be attributable to him and he would have to pay.
