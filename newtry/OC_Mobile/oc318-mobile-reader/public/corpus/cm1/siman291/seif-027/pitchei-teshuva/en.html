Even by words alone. See Baer Heitev citing Responsa Mahari Sasson, who was uncertain whether an unpaid custodian who stipulated that he would be liable as a paid custodian and later claims unavoidable accident must prove the accident. See Ketzot HaChoshen below, siman 301. Tosafot, Bava Metzia 58a, write in one explanation that when one obligates himself as a borrower, people assume that the item was actually lent to him, and he obligates himself in return for that benefit. On that explanation one might have thought that an unpaid custodian cannot become liable as a paid custodian without a kinyan, and that the rule was said only regarding becoming liable as a borrower. The poskim, however, cite only Tosafot's first explanation, as Sema writes below, siman 301, seif 4, and above, siman 66, seif katan 90. Accordingly there is no such distinction.
