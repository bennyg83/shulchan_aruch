If he entered at the time, etc. A paid custodian is liable in every case, as stated below, siman 303, seif 10. See Responsa Rabbi Shlomo Kohen, siman 110, and Responsa Rabbi Yosef of the House of Levi, siman 28, folio 149.
But otherwise he is exempt. See Sema, seif katan 20. The Raavad, as cited by the Nimukei Yosef in his conclusion, regards this as an unavoidable accident resulting from prior negligence, because had the custodian been present, the verse, 'The fear and dread of you,' would have been fulfilled. This is also implied by Tosafot in Perek HaMafkid, Bava Metzia 42a, and Tosafot state it explicitly in Perek HaOmanin, 78a. Although the Nimukei Yosef and the Mechaber rule like the Rif and Rambam, the Maadanei Melech and Bach agree with the Tur. It appears to me that the law should be decided by the rule that the burden of proof rests upon the claimant. The Rama's words below, siman 303, seif 10, where he cites the Raavad, also support this. His silence here therefore requires study. Perhaps he distinguishes between an unpaid and a paid custodian, but in truth no such distinction exists. It is more likely that his reference there to siman 291, seif 12, means that the dissenting authorities there disagree here as well. Examine carefully.
