By his own hands. For one may say to him: What right did you have to cause him this loss? This applies specifically where the depositor proves that he deposited a gold dinar. If he cannot prove it, the custodian need not pay him the value of gold even if he destroyed it by his own hands. This is also evident from the Tur's statement in siman 90. So writes the Sema. I do not know of any such proof. On the contrary, since the Tur writes there that the enactment for one whose property was robbed applies to one who causes damage, it implies that here too the injured party swears and collects. Maharshal likewise writes in chapter HaKones, siman 32. Still, one may distinguish here, because once the depositor initially said that it was silver, he is not later believed by oath to say it was gold. Accordingly, if he deposited it without specifying, the custodian says perhaps it was gold-plated, and the depositor says with certainty that it was gold, the depositor swears and collects according to all opinions.
