Tied and held in his hand. Maharit, Choshen Mishpat 115, writes that in a place where soldiers are common and seize property from people, it is negligent to carry silver or gold openly in one's hand. Sensible people tie the money securely and place it in the bosom, adding further protection by tying the end of the garment into the belt so that it cannot fall. In this way it is guarded both from theft and from loss.
And held in his hand. See Baer Heitev and Chatam Sofer, Choshen Mishpat 168, cited below in siman 346, regarding a partner's son who carried a bundle of partnership money on a business journey together with his own money and placed it in a pocket sewn into his stockings, where all the money was lost. Chatam Sofer treated this as close to negligence because normal practice was to keep money in a pocket sewn into a garment on the chest. If, as the wording of the question may imply, he placed the money in the stocking pocket in the partners' presence, one might argue that they saw and accepted that lesser form of safekeeping. Maharshal, cited by Knesset HaGedolah, states that even where a lender on collateral is a paid guardian, if he showed the borrower the place of safekeeping and the borrower accepted it, this is equivalent to an express stipulation that the lender is exempt if that level of care is used. The borrower's silence may suffice as acceptance. The Chatam Sofer case must therefore have involved placement outside the partners' presence, and the presence of another partner's son would not necessarily constitute consent. The matter requires study. See also above siman 176, seif 8, seif katan 10.
