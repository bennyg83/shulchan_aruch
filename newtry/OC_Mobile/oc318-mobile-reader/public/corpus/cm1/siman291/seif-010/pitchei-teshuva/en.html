Noda BiYehuda, siman 36, gives a sound explanation why a paid custodian is liable. At first glance, the unpaid custodian's argument that had he thought the property was his own he would have guarded it even more carefully should apply equally to a paid custodian. The explanation follows Shach in siman 63, seif katan 7, in the name of a responsum of Rama. For a paid custodian, failure to inform the family member that the item is a deposit is itself negligence, because if a loss threatens both the deposit and her own property, she must save the deposit first. An unpaid custodian, by contrast, has no such priority obligation even if she knows it is a deposit. See what I wrote in siman 303, seif katan 4. In Noda BiYehuda, second edition, siman 55, a rabbi objected that Nimukei Yosef gives a different reason, namely that with an unpaid custodian the son can argue that he assumed his mother would bury the item, as she ordinarily buried things she did not need, whereas a paid custodian must provide active guarding and burial is insufficient. The author's son answered that his father needed the other explanation because according to Shach's own position, cited in siman 303, seif katan 4, a paid custodian need not literally sit and watch constantly. Thus the reason of Nimukei Yosef does not fit Shach's approach. See there.
