Wait for me, etc. Terumat HaDeshen, siman 333, rules that a custodian who does not know whether the item was stolen through his negligence or without negligence is one who is obligated to swear but cannot do so, and therefore must pay. Hagahot Sema, seif katan 12, cites this. Responsa Mahari ibn Lev, part I, folio 122, rules likewise, and writes that the same applies where the custodian claims that he understood from the wording of the document that he had permission to act as he did, so that he was not negligent in deviating from the original arrangement. See Responsa Rabbi Shlomo Kohen, part III, siman 87, and Responsa Rashdam, simanim 48 and 429. Terumat HaDeshen proves this from the gloss below, siman 340, end of seif 3. Hagahot Sema comments that this applies specifically here, because he admits that the item was stolen. This differs from the end of siman 298, where the Rashba's view is cited that one who swears that he does not know whether it was stolen is exempt. See Responsa Mahari ibn Lev, part III, siman 115.
