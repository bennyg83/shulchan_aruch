Negligent. Terumat HaDeshen, siman 333, discusses a bailee who does not know whether the item was stolen through his negligence or not through his negligence, and applies the rule that because he cannot swear he must pay; see there at length. Responsa Ibn Lev, part I, page 122, rules similarly, and writes that the same applies where the bailee claims that he understood from the wording of the document that he had permission to act as he did, so that his deviation was not negligent. See Responsa Rashach, part III, siman 87; Responsa Rashdam, simanim 48 and 429. The Terumat HaDeshen brings proof from siman 340, seif 3, in the gloss; see there. Hagahot Sema writes that this applies specifically here because the bailee admits that the item was stolen. By contrast, at the end of siman 298 he swears that he does not know whether it was stolen and is exempt. This is the Rashba's view, because he does not know that anything was stolen from him at all. The Rashba states explicitly that for this reason it is unlike the case of a tied sack. The Darkei Moshe cites him there. See Responsa Ibn Lev, part III, siman 115. Shach.
