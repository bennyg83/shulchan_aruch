"Where did I place it?" According to the Mechaber's explanation that this constitutes negligence, if the owner was present with the custodian one might think he should be exempt, since negligence while the owner is present is exempt. In my view, however, this negligence is treated as direct damage, and he is liable even while the owner is present. It resembles the rule in siman 300 where two wrapped deposits were entrusted to him, one containing two hundred and the other one hundred, and he is liable because he was negligent, even where the owner was present. A proof is that otherwise, when a custodian says, "I do not know whether I returned the deposit," he should be exempt if the deposit had been entrusted while the owner was present. A claim that he does not know whether he returned it necessarily includes a case where the object remains somewhere in his house and he does not know where. If liability for not knowing its location were merely ordinary negligence, the exemption for negligence while the owner is present should apply. Rather, this form of negligence is in the category of direct damage and creates liability even then. The reason is that even one who was never a custodian is liable in such circumstances. If Reuven enters Shimon's house, takes Shimon's money and buries it in the ground for Shimon's benefit so that it will not be stolen, without thereby becoming a thief, but then forgets where he buried it, Reuven must certainly pay. Since he performed an act upon another's property and that act caused the loss, he is a direct damager. The same applies to this custodian, who removed the property from an exposed place and hid it in a location where it can no longer be found. Since his act itself caused the loss, he must pay. Seif 11.
