They were hidden in the thickness of the partition. Even though if money were hidden this way in a substantial building wall, even a wooden one, the custodian could be exempt from theft, as the Tur explains later, and although the Gemara requires only that one bury valuables in a wall under one handbreadth of covering, a reed partition is different. A built wall made of beams does not burn so quickly, and even if fire catches it one may be able to rescue a deposit hidden within it. A reed partition is highly flammable, so hiding the deposit there is considered negligent from the outset. In Bava Metzia 42a the Gemara does not explicitly say that the deposit was inserted into the thickness of the reed hut. It merely says it was placed in a hut of reeds. Rashi explains that such a location is guarded from thieves even without burial because thieves do not generally seek valuables there. The Rambam, however, states the case as the Mechaber does. It is possible that Rashi too means that it was hidden in the thickness of the partition, and by "without burial" he means without burial in the ground. Even so, liability follows because the original placement involved negligence regarding fire.
