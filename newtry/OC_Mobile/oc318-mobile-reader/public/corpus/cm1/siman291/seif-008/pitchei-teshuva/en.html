And they are deposited in his possession. See Chatam Sofer, Choshen Mishpat, siman 168, in the statement of the case, cited below in siman 346, seif katan 1. A partner's son was carrying a partnership bundle while traveling on partnership business. He put it together with his own money in a pouch sewn into his stockings, and all the money was lost on the road. Chatam Sofer writes that this resembles negligence, because money is ordinarily carried in a sewn pocket in the garment over the chest. At first glance, if, as the wording of the question somewhat implies, he placed the money in the stocking pocket in the presence of the partners, one might say that they saw and accepted this inferior method of guarding. Maharshal, volume 2, siman 169, cited in Knesset HaGedolah to siman 72, Hagahot Beit Yosef, letter 19, writes that even according to the view that one who lends on collateral is a paid custodian and liable for theft and loss, if the lender showed the borrower where he would guard it and the borrower accepted this, it is as though they expressly stipulated that this level of guarding would suffice. The word "accepted" apparently means that the borrower merely remained silent, not that he expressly consented, for otherwise the ruling would be obvious. One must therefore say that in Chatam Sofer's case the act was not done in the partners' presence, and the presence of the other partner's son did not help. This requires study.
