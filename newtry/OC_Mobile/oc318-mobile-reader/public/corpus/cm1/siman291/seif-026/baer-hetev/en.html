Even. Nevertheless, if the second custodian was negligent, the owner may collect from the second custodian, who cannot say, "You are not my litigant," just as in the case of stolen property where the owner has not despaired of recovery. Even if the first custodian borrowed the item while the owner was with him, the second is still liable; it is not considered a borrowing while the owner is with him, since the property does not belong to the first custodian. So writes Maharshal, chapter 10 of Bava Kamma, siman 1, with proofs. See below, siman 346, seif 17, in the gloss, and Responsa Rabbi Moshe Alashkar, siman 39. Ibid.
To entrust. The same applies if it is known that this custodian ordinarily does not guard deposits himself but delivers them to another; the depositor's claim is then against the second custodian. Maharshal, chapter 1 of Bava Kamma, siman 32. See there. Ibid.
Always. This does not exclude a case where the owner once entrusted property to someone else even though most of the time he entrusts it to this person; that is unreasonable. Rather, it excludes a case where most of the time he entrusts it to others. Proof is that in siman 305 the Tur writes this rule regarding a paid custodian and does not use the word "always," and all the more so here regarding an unpaid custodian. Sema.
From paying. If the person to whom the owner commonly entrusts property was negligent and has no money with which to pay, see siman 72, seif 30, in the gloss, and what I wrote there. Shach.
While the owner was with him. Meaning, the first custodian borrowed the item while the owner was with him, so that had an unavoidable event occurred while it remained with the first custodian he would have been exempt. This does not exempt him from an unavoidable event occurring with the second custodian. So writes the Sema. See Responsa Rashdam, simanim 144 and 221. See the Rosh, chapter 1 of Bava Metzia, in the discussion beginning "there are those who ask," from which it follows that where there is one witness, the second custodian joins with that witness and together they are two witnesses. According to other authorities, however, who answer that the custodian is an interested party, two other witnesses are specifically required here. This appears primary. Ibid.
Witnesses. The Shach writes that this implies that even if he reduced the level of guarding, he is exempt where there are witnesses. The Bach states this explicitly, and it is also explicit in the Rambam and Semag. Maharshal, chapter 1 of Bava Kamma, siman 32, disagrees and holds that where he reduced the guarding, even witnesses do not exempt him because he was negligent. I say that even by oath he should properly be exempt even in such a case; see there.
Properly. If the article was stolen or suffered an unavoidable loss in circumstances for which the first custodian would have been exempt, then even though it is not certain that such an event would have occurred in the first custodian's house, he is exempt. We do not say that delivering it to the second custodian was negligence and therefore constitutes negligence at the outset followed by an unavoidable loss. A paid custodian who gives an item to an unpaid custodian is not deemed negligent merely because he gave it to a responsible person. This is clear from the Rosh, chapter HaMafkid, and Tosafot there. Shach.
To swear. The Sema explains that the first custodian can swear that the second custodian was not negligent and did not make unauthorized use of the item. The Shach writes that this does not correctly explain the Rambam and Mechaber. They hold in siman 294, seif 2, that the primary oath is that the item was stolen, while the oaths that he was not negligent and did not make unauthorized use are only through gilgul. If he has witnesses that it was stolen, he is exempt. Accordingly, here too, if he has witnesses, or if he himself can swear only that it was stolen while with the second custodian, he is exempt. This is explicit in the Rosh, chapter HaMafkid, which makes this rule dependent on the law of siman 294. The Shach further writes that if a paid custodian redeposited the entrusted item with the owner himself, and it was stolen or lost while with the owner, the paid custodian must pay. This is likewise derived from Rabbeinu Yitzchak cited by the Mordechai, chapter HaUmanin. Even Rabbi Baruch, who disagrees in Hagahot Mordechai at the end of Bava Metzia, agrees here, as I wrote at the beginning of siman 72, seif 3. See Responsa Mabit, part II, simanim 196 and 304.
