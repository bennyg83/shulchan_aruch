Mem) A dispute between Rav and Rabbi Yochanan in the second chapter of Bava Metzia 36a. The halachah is known to follow Rabbi Yochanan, and Rava explicitly rules so on 36b.
Nun) There in Rava's statement.
Samekh) The Tur, seif 25. The Rosh likewise writes this in his rulings.
Ayin) Rambam, chapter 8 of Hilkhot Sekhirut, halachah 4, explicit from the incident involving gardeners there on 36a, where they themselves, etc. The Maggid Mishneh writes that even if the second custodian has nothing and never swore, so that there is nothing to collect from him, the first custodian is nevertheless exempt.
Peh) The Kesef Mishneh explains that this excludes a case where the depositor was accustomed to entrusting property to the second custodian and only later the second custodian became poor.
Tzadi) This excludes a case where the property was worth more than the relevant amount. Kesef Mishneh.
Kuf) There. The Maggid Mishneh explains Rambam's view: once the depositor has revealed that he now desires a higher level of guarding and the first custodian reduced that level, there is no greater negligence.
Reish) The Maggid Mishneh writes that in his view the reason is that exemption through shemirah beba'alim applies only to the custodian who borrowed the owner's services, not to a second custodian who did not. The first custodian therefore remains responsible for having placed the property under the second custodian's authority. This requires study. The Kesef Mishneh writes that the reason must instead be that handing the item to another custodian is considered closer to direct damage than ordinary negligence.
Shin) There, and it is straightforward.
Tav) The Maggid Mishneh there, halachah 4, in the name of some authorities.
