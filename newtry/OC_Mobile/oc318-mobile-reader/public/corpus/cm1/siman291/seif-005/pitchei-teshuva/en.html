And in a guarded place. See Responsa Shivat Tzion, siman 99, concerning a custodian who placed paper currency, imperial redemption notes, wrapped merely in paper and then put them in the breast pocket where he kept his own money. He rules that this is negligence. Ordinary practice is to place such notes in a leather wallet and then put that wallet in the breast pocket. If instead the notes are wrapped only in paper and put directly into the pocket, this is negligent because a person frequently reaches into his pocket and, given the thinness and light weight of the notes, they can slip out and fall without being noticed. This is not the superior level of guarding required of custodians. A person may choose such a method for his own property, but even an unpaid custodian is liable for this negligence. See there.
