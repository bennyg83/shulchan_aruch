In a place where meshichah effects acquisition. Rashi and Rambam hold that custodial responsibility begins through meshichah in a place where meshichah is a valid acquisition. This creates a difficulty concerning debt instruments. The Torah excludes documents from some custodial laws, which implies that absent the exclusion they could enter custody. But a debt instrument is not acquired by meshichah alone. It ordinarily requires writing and delivery, and a custodian cannot write the seller's transfer formula. One can answer from the law of ownerless debt instruments. Maharam of Lublin explains that the requirement of writing exists because an ordinary seller retains an underlying right unless he expressly transfers the claim and its liens. Where there is no remaining seller interest, such as an ownerless document, physical taking can suffice. Custodianship is similar. The owner is not selling the debt right. The custodian needs only possession sufficient to establish the custodial relationship, not a transfer of the underlying claim. Thus meshichah of the document can suffice for custody even though a purchaser would require writing and delivery. The matter nevertheless warrants further study.
