Ritva writes that the rule exempting a custodian who transfers property to one with whom the owner regularly deposits applies only if that second person did not become poor or otherwise lose his trustworthy status in the meantime. Beit Yosef cites this and notes that Rambam's wording implies the same. See Responsa VeShav HaKohen, siman 17, who writes that Ritva's phrase "became suspect" is not limited to formal disqualification. If the person's established reliability has deteriorated, the owner can say, "He is no longer trustworthy to me." See what I wrote above in siman 71, seif 13, seif katan 11, and siman 91, seif 3, seif katan 4. VeShav HaKohen further discusses Reuven, who came to Shimon and said that Levi had sent him with silk for Shimon to repair. After the repair Reuven returned and asked Shimon to give the silk back to him. Levi later sued Shimon, saying that Reuven had been appointed only to deliver the silk and had never returned it, and asked why Shimon returned it without Levi's knowledge. Shimon answered that since Levi trusted Reuven to bring the silk initially, he was entitled to trust him again and return it through him. VeShav HaKohen rules for Shimon. Since Levi sent the silk through Reuven, Levi demonstrated trust in him, and Shimon did not reduce the level of guarding because both Shimon and Reuven were paid custodians. Even if we require that the owner regularly entrust property to the second custodian, and one prior occasion is generally insufficient, here all authorities agree because the very same item was sent through Reuven and no relevant change occurred in the meantime. He then cites Hagahot Ashri at the end of the first chapter of Gittin concerning Reuven who sent one hundred zuz to Shimon for safekeeping and Shimon returned it through the same messenger, where it was lost on the way. According to Rabbi Simcha there, Shimon is certainly exempt because Levi never dealt directly with him and Shimon can say that Levi is not his litigant. Even according to Or Zarua there, that a bailee becomes the depositor's litigant for all laws of bailment, Shimon is exempt because this is equivalent to returning the property through one with whom the owner regularly deposits. If Reuven cannot pay, the issue depends on the dispute cited above in seif 24, and we cannot resolve it, so money cannot be extracted from the possessor. See there. Responsa Maharam of Brisk, at the end of the volume, siman 7, cites an analogous case in the name of the Geonim and rules for the sender. The matter therefore requires study.
