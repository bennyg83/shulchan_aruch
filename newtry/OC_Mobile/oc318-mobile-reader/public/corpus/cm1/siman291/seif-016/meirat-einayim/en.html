If he is a Torah scholar, etc. In the Perishah I explained the reason. Ordinary people rely upon the havdalah they hear from the cantor in the synagogue, while a Torah scholar ordinarily seeks to recite havdalah himself over wine. The Gemara says, "If he is a young Torah scholar, perhaps he needs coins for havdalah," which appears to refer to the depositor being a Torah scholar. Yet the reasoning also fits if the custodian is the Torah scholar. Because he knows the importance of reciting havdalah over wine, he assumes that the depositor likewise intends to do so and may send for the deposited money for that purpose. This is the basis of the alternative opinion Rama records. Similar wording appears in siman 28 regarding whether the witness or the litigant reminding him must be a Torah scholar. The Rif and Rambam do not contain this reason, which suggests they did not have this text. Indeed it is somewhat strained, because the owner gave the money as an ordinary deposit, whose law is that it be buried in the ground. Why should one assume he expects the custodian to take money from it for havdalah? Nevertheless, even if the text is accepted, I explained the matter according to the Rambam and Mechaber.
