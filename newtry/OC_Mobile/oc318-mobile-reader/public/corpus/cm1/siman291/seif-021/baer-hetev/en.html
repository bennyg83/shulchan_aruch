The depositor. Maharik, shoresh 131, writes: Reuven hired Shimon to go after merchandise and gave him his money. Shimon placed it together with his own money. On the way he slept in a room and the money was stolen from him. Maharik ruled that Shimon must pay, because it was negligence to leave the purse in the room; he should have placed it beneath his pillow or tied it beneath his arms, etc. See there, Darkei Moshe 18. The rule applies specifically where it was stolen during nighttime sleep. If the money was lost while being transported, see below, siman 292, seif 10, in the gloss. This is the Sema's language. The Shach writes that Responsa Rabbi Yosef of the House of Levi, siman 35, questions Rashakh, part II, siman 85, who writes simply that this applies specifically to an unpaid custodian, since the Beit Yosef cites Rabbeinu Yerucham that most authorities questioned the Rambam: because owners commonly entrust property with the understanding that the custodian's wife may hold it, etc., even if he reduces the level of guarding, he is exempt. See there, and Responsa Rabbi Moshe Alshich, siman 39.
Exempt. Others disagree, as stated in siman 72, seif 98. See there. Shach.
To his servants. Meaning Canaanite servants, who are presumed to be thieves. Hebrew servants, however, are comparable to members of his household. This is explicit in the Mordechai there. So writes the Sema. See the aforementioned Responsa Rashakh and Responsa Rashdam, siman 222.
Was negligent. Meaning, negligent initially. Otherwise, even if the ultimate loss was unavoidable, he is liable, as stated above. See below, seif 26.
