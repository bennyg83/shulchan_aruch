If a maidservant's belongings are kept inside her employer's chest, it is doubtful whether this creates safeguarding while the owner is present, because it is not necessarily like saying, 'Place it before me,' and then removing oneself from responsibility. It is also doubtful whether safeguarding while the owner is present can arise through one's wife. If one originally became an unpaid custodian when the owner was not present, but later became a paid custodian while the owner was present, as may occur with collateral given for an investment, the enhanced paid-custodian obligation may be regarded as arising while the owner was present even though the original unpaid-custodian obligation did not. In another formulation, if the original arrangement itself already made him an unpaid custodian, the presence of the owner when the enhanced duty arose may not exempt the basic duty. If there is some paid-custodian status because of a future benefit expected from entering an investment arrangement, this may be no stronger than the benefit received by craftsmen, and perhaps weaker. A stipulation that one will be liable even where the owner is present should be effective because, once one is within the category of custodian and has been trusted with the property, it resembles an unpaid custodian stipulating to assume the liability of a borrower, for which no formal kinyan is required.
