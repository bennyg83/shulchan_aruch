He requested, etc. See the beginning of siman 353 and the end of siman 398.
(Supplement) He requested, etc., into his courtyard, etc. This applies specifically to a courtyard, but if he invited the objects into his house he is liable. This is stated in Hagahot Ashri and in the Yerushalmi: all agree that when one says regarding a house, 'Bring it in and I will guard it,' he accepts custody; all agree that a field remains like a field. They dispute a courtyard, Rabbi treating it like a field and the Sages like a house. The Yerushalmi resolves the related Mishna by distinguishing stacks in a field from ordinary objects. The decisors may have omitted this because the Bavli appears to disagree.
(Supplement) He requested, etc. Tur cites Tosafot and Rosh, who rule like the Sages. This limitation matters particularly where damage arises on its own. Rama's omission indicates that he follows Tosafot's first resolution, under which there is no practical difference here concerning custody; according to their second resolution, it would matter even if the objects were broken by wind. Hagahot Ashri, Rosh, and Tur follow the latter view. (End of supplement.)
