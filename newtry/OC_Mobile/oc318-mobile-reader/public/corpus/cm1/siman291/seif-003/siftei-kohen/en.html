Into his courtyard, etc. This applies specifically to a courtyard. If, however, he brought his property into the homeowner's house with permission, the ordinary assumption is that the homeowner accepted responsibility to guard it, even according to Rabbi. This is stated in Hagahot Ashri, Perek HaParah, in the name of Or Zarua, and this is the primary ruling. What is written in the responsum of the Rosh cited by the Tur, that this applies in the custodian's house, does not disagree with this. Rather, its meaning is as the Bach explains. The words of the Sema, seif katan 5, may likewise be understood in this manner. See Responsa Mabit, siman 92.
He is not liable to guard them at all, etc. Others disagree and hold that he is liable for damage that comes from his own property, as explained in the Tur and the other authorities. The Rama himself cites their view below, at the end of siman 398. The Rama's omission here requires study, and even more so that of the Ir Shushan. Maharshal likewise rules in Perek 5 of Bava Kamma, siman 6, that he is liable. As to damage caused by an outside force, the matter remains unresolved, and if the injured party seized payment, it is not removed from him. See there.
