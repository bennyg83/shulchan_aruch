[Shulchan Aruch] But if he stipulated. Note: See Mishneh LaMelech, chapter 1 of Hilchot Sechirut, halachah 9. He writes that according to one of Tosafot's answers at the end of the chapter HaZahav, the rule that no kinyan is required applies specifically when one undertakes the liability of a borrower. If he stipulates to have the liability of a paid custodian, a kinyan is required. In a case of borrowing while the owner is with him, if he stipulates that he will nevertheless be liable for payment, it appears that even according to Tosafot's first answer a kinyan is required, since by law he is exempt even from an oath. If an unpaid or paid custodian is guarding while the owner is with him and stipulates that he will be liable for unavoidable accidents, according to Tosafot's second answer no kinyan is required, whereas according to their first answer a kinyan is required.
