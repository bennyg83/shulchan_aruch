Some say he is exempt. This is the plain meaning of the Gemara there: "let him say to his mother, etc." Likewise Bava Kamma 56b: "rather, in place of a shepherd, and a keeper who transferred to another keeper, etc.; to his hired shepherd, which is his usual practice, etc." The Sar of Coucy rejects the proof, because there it is known that the owner never grazes the animal himself, whereas here the depositor nevertheless assumes that the first bailee will also guard personally. See Mordechai there, siman 362, and Hagahos Ashri there, s.v. "but where, etc."
(Collected note) Others say he is exempt. This is also Rambam and Shulchan Aruch's view. Mordechai and Nimukei Yosef there write that this is Rambam's opinion, as does Maggid Mishneh. In siman 72:30 in the gloss, however, Rama rules without qualification like the first opinion. See Mordechai and Hagahos Ashri, seif 23, s.v. "but where, etc."
