[Shach, letter 46] For he wrote: this constitutes negligence. Note: This inference is not so compelling. Rambam, in the wording reproduced by the Shulchan Aruch above in seif 21, writes: "And needless to say, if he delivered them to another person, this constitutes negligence." There, it is necessarily a case of negligence. Accordingly, here too one can explain the wording in the same manner. However, there is grounds for wonder from what Rambam writes afterward, that even though he borrowed, if he hired while the owner was with him, and so forth. See there. From this wording, and from the Kesef Mishneh there, it can be inferred that this is complete negligence. See also the Maggid Mishneh, and examine carefully.
