All the more so she would have been careful. Baer Heitev states that this reasoning applies to a gratuitous guardian, whereas a paid guardian is liable. Noda BiYehuda 36 explains why. The same argument, that the guardian assumed the household member would be even more careful, might seem applicable to a paid guardian as well. The distinction follows the Shach's discussion in siman 63: a paid guardian is negligent if he fails to tell the household member that the item is a deposit, because in a situation of competing danger she may have to save the deposited property before her own. A gratuitous guardian has no such preference duty. Noda BiYehuda II, 55, records an objection that Nimukei Yosef gives a different rationale: a son may assume that his mother will bury money she does not presently need, while a paid guardian must provide more active guarding. The son of Noda BiYehuda answers that his father needed the alternative explanation because the Shach rejects the premise that a paid guardian must literally sit and watch continuously.
