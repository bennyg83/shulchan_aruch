All the more so she would have been careful, etc. The reason she did not bury the money in the ground is that she thought it belonged to her son and that he would deal with it, and he would not want it buried in the ground. See the Perishah.
For he did not tell her that it was a deposit. The depositor cannot make her swear that her son did not tell her that it was a deposit, because one does not swear on an uncertain claim, and the depositor cannot assert with certainty that the son told her it was a deposit.
That she hid it. Meaning, that she placed it in a guarded place, such as a chest.
With an item that is not individually identifiable. Meaning, in such a case she cannot swear that the very money deposited with her son was the money that her son gave her and that was stolen. Therefore her son, the bailee, must swear concerning this. But with an individually identifiable item, the son need not swear; rather, the mother swears concerning the entire matter.
