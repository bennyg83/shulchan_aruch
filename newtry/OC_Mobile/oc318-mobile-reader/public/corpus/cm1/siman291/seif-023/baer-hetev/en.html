Was careful. This applies specifically to an unpaid custodian. A paid custodian is liable, because he should have explained that the money was a deposit. So writes the Nimukei Yosef in the name of the Ran, cited by the Bach. The Sema writes that the reason she did not bury it in the ground is that she thought the money belonged to her son, who would use it, and that he would not want it buried. Meaning, she did not wish to trouble herself because it had not been given to her to guard on behalf of another. Only a custodian is obligated to exert such effort, and she thought her son would not object and that this level of guarding sufficed for him. See Responsa Maharit, simanim 44 and 105. Ibid.
For. The depositor cannot require her to swear that her son did not tell her the money was a deposit, because one does not swear on a claim of uncertainty, and the depositor cannot assert with certainty that the son told her it was a deposit. This is the Sema's language, based on the Rosh and Tur. Therefore, since the son says he gave it to her without explanation, she is exempt from this oath. If the son says he told her it was a deposit, she must take a shevuat heset against the son's claim even though he is a relative, as above, siman 75, seif 21, in the gloss. It is not reasonable to regard the son as interested testimony, because even if he says that he did not tell her, he is exempt. This applies where there are witnesses that an unavoidable event occurred to the money, or where the depositor believes her, so that she is exempt from the primary oath. Otherwise, it is obvious that he can require her, by gilgul shevuah, to swear that she concealed it. Ibid.
That she concealed it in a chest. This is the wording of the Gemara and Tur. It appears that the reason she does not merely swear that it was stolen is that perhaps she did not conceal it at all. In such a case, even if the money had belonged to her son, he would certainly have objected, so she would have been negligent toward her son and liable to the depositor. Therefore she must swear that she concealed it. Ibid.
