He may claim. Certainly she would have exercised greater care, etc. This applies only to an unpaid custodian. A paid custodian is liable, because he should have told her that the money was a deposit. So writes the Nimukei Yosef in the name of the Ran, as cited by the Bach. The Sema writes that she did not bury it in the ground because she believed it belonged to her son, who intended to use it and would not want it buried. Meaning, she did not wish to undertake such effort because the money had not been given to her as another person's deposit. Only a custodian is obligated to make that effort. She assumed that her son would not object and would be satisfied with this degree of care. See Responsa Maharit, simanim 44 and 105, beginning of folio 126.

That she hid them, etc. The Tur and Gemara likewise state that she must swear that she hid them in the chest. It appears that the reason she may not simply swear that they were stolen is that perhaps she did not hide them at all. In such a case, even if the money belonged to her son, he would certainly object, and her conduct would be negligence toward him. She would then be liable to the depositor. She must therefore also swear that she hid the money. Examine carefully.
