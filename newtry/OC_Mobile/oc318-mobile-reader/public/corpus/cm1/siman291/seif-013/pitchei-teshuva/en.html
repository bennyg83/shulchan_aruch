Even if he is an unpaid custodian. See Baer Heitev, who writes that even if the first custodian had a case of guarding while the owner was working for him, the second custodian is nevertheless liable, because the money does not belong to the first custodian. These words are difficult to understand. In Yam Shel Shlomo, the source of the ruling, the text says that if the first custodian is working for the second, the second is not exempt, because this is not called guarding with the owner. The property remains in the domain of its actual owner, and the Torah's exemption applies only where the owner of the property is working for the custodian. He proves this from Bava Metzia, chapter HaShoel, concerning a woman who rented a cow and then married. Accordingly, there is a scribal error in Shach and Baer Heitev, and the text should read: even if the second custodian borrowed while the first custodian was working for him. The law of the first custodian where the original arrangement itself was guarding while the owner worked is not discussed here. See below, seif katan 19.
