(Mechaber, seif 18) And fraudulent people who search for it. Note: Tosafot, Bava Metzia 43, passage beginning "why specifically," at first glance appear to disagree with this rule. Their words can be reconciled based on Ritva's novellae to Bava Metzia, cited in Shitah Mekubetzet on 47, which explain the concern that one may say, "your wheat burned in the attic," not as a literal unavoidable accident but as an event resembling an accident, namely theft or loss. Accordingly, where thieves and swindlers are not common, money is sufficiently guarded from theft and loss even without burial in the ground. Shitah Mekubetzet cites the Ritva explicitly: with money, the degree of guarding required of an unpaid guardian suffices; theft, loss, and accident are not common where the money is properly locked away, or buried in the ground at a time when thieves are common. Thus Tosafot can be understood in accordance with Ritva. The concern of burning does not apply literally to money. The relevant concern is theft or loss, and ordinary guarding suffices unless thieves are common, in which case burial is required. See also Tosafot, Gittin 52b, passage beginning "lest he come to say."
