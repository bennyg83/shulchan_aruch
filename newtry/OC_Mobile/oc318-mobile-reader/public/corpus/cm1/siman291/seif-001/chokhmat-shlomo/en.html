Seif 5. 'Some say that he is not liable until he performs meshichah, in a place where meshichah effects acquisition.' Note: I saw that Ketzot HaChoshen asks why a verse is needed to exclude documents from the laws of bailees, especially according to Rambam, who in fact obligates for negligence. A document cannot be acquired through meshichah alone, but requires writing and delivery, while writing has no relevance to a bailee. He leaves the matter unresolved. In my opinion there is no difficulty. Writing is required for a purchaser of a document because the purchaser seeks to acquire the underlying debt itself. Meshichah or delivery of the paper does not demonstrate acquisition of the debt. This is further illuminated by the Ran at the beginning of HaKotev, who explains that seizure of a document is ineffective because possession of an ordinary object itself demonstrates ownership, whereas possession of a document does not demonstrate acquisition of the underlying debt. For this same reason, delivery or meshichah alone does not acquire a document, since the act must itself demonstrate acquisition of the object, and taking the paper does not demonstrate acquisition of the debt. But with a bailee, the owner gives him only the paper to guard. The debt itself is not capable of being physically guarded and is not in the bailee's possession. Even if the laws of bailees apply to documents so that failure to guard the paper properly creates liability for the value of the debt, the duty of guarding concerns only the paper. For bailees, meshichah is required only of the object that he undertakes to guard. Here, taking possession of the paper itself clearly demonstrates receipt of that paper for safekeeping. Delivery or meshichah therefore suffices for the bailee's undertaking, even though it would not suffice to transfer ownership of the underlying debt. There is thus no difficulty.
