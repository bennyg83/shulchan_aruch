He is liable to pay only for negligence, and according to appreciation or depreciation. Maggid Mishneh states that custodians pay according to the value at the time of the event that creates liability, such as negligence. Shach likewise assumes that all custodians pay according to the value at the time of the negligence. This differs from a thief or robber, who generally pays according to the value at the time of the theft. Ramban explains that a robber becomes fully liable from the moment of taking, and the object is removed from the owner's effective domain with respect to sale and consecration. The moment of taking is therefore the moment that fixes the monetary liability. A custodian, by contrast, does not become fully liable merely by receiving the object. So long as it remains intact, it belongs to the owner and can be sold or consecrated. The custodian's monetary liability arises only when the relevant breach or accident occurs. Even according to the opinion that a custodian's assets become encumbered retroactively from the time of meshichah, Ritva explains that this does not mean a full monetary debt exists from that moment. It means only that once the later breach creates liability, the lien relates back to the earlier receipt. The amount of the debt is still measured by the value at the time the liability actually arose. Therefore appreciation and depreciation are measured at the time of negligence or other obligating event, unlike a robber, whose liability is fixed when the robbery begins.
