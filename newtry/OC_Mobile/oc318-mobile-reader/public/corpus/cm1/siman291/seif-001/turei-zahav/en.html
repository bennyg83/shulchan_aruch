(Or he said merely, "Leave it.") In the chapter HaOmanin the Gemara proposes that this matter depends upon a dispute among Tannaim. That is, whether one who says merely "Leave it" becomes an unpaid custodian is the dispute between Rabbi and the Sages cited by the Tur. The Gemara answers with difficulty that even according to the Sages, they disagree with Rabbi only in a courtyard, which is fit for safeguarding. When he says, "Bring it in," he means, "Bring it in and I will guard it for you." In the marketplace, however, which is not fit for safeguarding, when he says, "Leave it," he means, "Sit and guard it yourself." It follows that where one says merely "Leave it" inside the prospective custodian's house, the matter itself is the dispute of Rabbi and the Sages. The Gemara's unresolved question whether saying merely "Leave it" creates the status of an unpaid custodian concerns the marketplace. Accordingly, the Mechaber and the Tur here also discuss the marketplace. We therefore do not require the Sema's statement that where the damage came about through the custodian, he is liable even when he merely said "Leave it," according to the Sages who dispute Rabbi. According to what I have written, since the case here concerns the marketplace, even the Sages agree that merely saying "Leave it" does not make him even an unpaid custodian. This is also evident from the Tur, who writes here that he is not even an unpaid custodian, yet shortly afterward rules like the Sages that ordinarily one accepts responsibility to guard. The necessary distinction is that here the case concerns the marketplace, whereas there it concerns a courtyard. However, I find difficulty in the Tur's ruling in the name of the Rosh, copied by the Rama below, that the unresolved question regarding one who merely said "Leave it" concerns the prospective custodian's house, and since it was unresolved we rule leniently that he is not an unpaid custodian. In the prospective custodian's house, the Rosh himself ruled like the Sages, as the Tur writes below. One cannot answer that here even the Sages agree because the damage to the shoes came from elsewhere and not through him, as explained below, for the Gemara's unresolved question concerns only the marketplace. This requires study. We also learn from the Rosh's responsum cited here by the Mechaber that saying "Leave it here" does not have the same meaning as "Leave it before me."
