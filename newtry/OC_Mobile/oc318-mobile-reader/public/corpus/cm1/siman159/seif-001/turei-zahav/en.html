(Seif 1: when I stand.) This is difficult in light of siman 154, seif 3, where one may open a doorway to the public domain because he can say, "I am merely like one of the people in the public domain." Why can the neighbor not answer, "The public does not see me at night," as the Gemara and Tur say here? There is also a third reason in the Gemara: the public does not see me except when they deliberately look, as the Beit Yosef explains. That too should apply in siman 154. A similar difficulty exists regarding the Tur's ruling in the name of Rabbeinu Yonah, cited by the Rama, that if the roof is low, etc. One must say that the Sages were concerned mainly with the reason that the public does not see him while he is seated. The first reason, that this person sees him at night, is not a major injury because people do not ordinarily conduct their private affairs at night. Likewise, the third reason, that the public sees him only when it deliberately looks, is not a major concern, because just as the public will not commit a wrongdoing openly merely to look, so too this individual can be expected to distance himself from looking as much as possible. The Gemara lists three reasons by way of additional support. Thus the Tur writes, "when I stand during the day," mentioning the day as an added factor. The principal point, however, is whether he is standing. The Gemara's third reason is likewise only supplementary.
