Rivash distinguishes between two roofs on opposite sides of a public domain and an opening opposite an opening across a public domain. In the roof case, one owner can sometimes see the other at times when passersby ordinarily cannot, such as at night. In the doorway case, however, a person does not stand constantly in his doorway. The occasional times he does so are no different from occasions when passersby also happen to see inside. According to Rama's ruling, if the roof is low enough that people in the public domain can ordinarily see it, there is no additional visual harm. Maggid Mishneh likewise explains that the Gemara's roof case concerns roofs at least four amot high. If they are lower, the owner can say, "You already must protect your privacy from the public." Although the Gemara mentions that people see by day but not by night, Lechem Mishneh explains that on a roof lower than four amot the public can see sufficiently even at night, whereas a higher roof may be exposed only in daylight.
