He remains liable for his own personal warranty. For example, the case in the preceding seif, where the seller himself later becomes the party seeking to remove the field. The right in question existed already at the time of sale. When Reuven sold the field to Shimon without warranty, Reuven had the benefit that if an existing creditor of his later seized the field from Shimon, Shimon could not return against him. Even so, we say that by selling without warranty he did not reserve the right to become personally the seller and the remover. But if Shimon owed a debt to Reuven's father Yaakov, and after Reuven sold the field Yaakov died and Reuven inherited that debt, Reuven may seize the field from Shimon. This right came to him only afterward. Although he is both the original seller and the one now removing the property, it is as though Reuven lent Shimon money after the sale and Shimon's lands became encumbered to him, in which case he plainly may collect from them.
