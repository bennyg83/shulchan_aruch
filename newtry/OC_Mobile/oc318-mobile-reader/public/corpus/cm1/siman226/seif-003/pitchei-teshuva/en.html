He accepted responsibility for his own claim. Responsa Rabbi Akiva Eiger, siman 148, considers whether, where stolen land is recovered by the true owner from the first seller, that seller may recover against the person who sold it to him. Nimukei Yosef distinguishes three forms of warranty: a creditor's collection is treated as responsibility arising from the seller, because the creditor collects through the seller's lien; recovery by the true owner of stolen property is treated as an outside claim, because the owner simply takes his own property wherever found. Rabbi Akiva Eiger therefore suggests that the rule that one accepts responsibility for claims arising from himself may apply specifically to a creditor, while in stolen property the true owner's recovery is always treated like an outside claim. Tosafot in Bava Metzia 14 appears to support this, but he leaves the practical ruling requiring study.
