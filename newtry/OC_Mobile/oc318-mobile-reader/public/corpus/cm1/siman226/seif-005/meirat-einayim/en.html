Before he makes use of it, etc., through the phrase, and the seller returns the money, etc. This is the Rambam's language in chapter 19 of Hilchot Mekhirah. The Rosh and Tur disagree. They explain the Gemara's case as one where the purchaser had not yet paid the price of the field, although he had acquired it through another method. The Rambam and Mechaber make the purchaser's ability to return the field depend on whether he has used it at all, including the act described in the Gemara as treading the boundary. They interpret that act as pressing down the raised earth of the boundary and leveling it with the field, which improves the field and constitutes an act of possession, as the Rambam writes expressly in chapter 1 of Hilchot Mekhirah and the Tur cites in siman 192. The Rosh and Tur interpret the phrase differently, as walking along the boundary to inspect whether the field matches what the seller represented and to see what work it needs. In the Perishah I explained at length why return depends on that act according to their view. Under their approach, if the purchaser already paid the money he cannot return the field and recover his money merely because beit din sees a genuine adverse claim against the field. The seller may say, Produce your deed of seizure and I will reimburse you, as the Rosh rules in the responsum cited by the Tur. It is surprising that Rama mentions none of this here.

If the purchaser retracted because of the adverse claim, etc. This comes from a responsum of the Rosh cited by the Tur. It appears, however, that the matter does not depend upon the purchaser's unilateral retraction, but upon retraction with authorization of beit din. If the adverse claim is resolved before beit din has permitted him to retract, he can no longer retract. An exception is where the adverse claim arose because a promissory note appeared against the seller and that note is not presently available to be torn up.
