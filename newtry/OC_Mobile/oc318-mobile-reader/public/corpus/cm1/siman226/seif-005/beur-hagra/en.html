(Collected note) And he said that he purchased, etc. Rashba writes that this was before he purchased it. See there. (End of note.)
(Collected note) Through one of the methods. Tosafos there, s.v. “once he made chazakah, etc.,” and all the poskim rule this way, unlike Rashi. (End of note.)
(Collected note) And he returns, etc. Tosafos there, the Rosh, the Tur, and other poskim write that this applies specifically when he has not yet paid the money. (End of note.)
Even if he trod, etc. According to the Rif’s explanation there, for example where that field was, etc. See what was written above in siman 192.
(Collected note) And if he used it, etc., even, etc. This follows the Rif’s explanation. Tosafos and the Rosh in the first chapter of Bava Metzia, the Tur, and other poskim instead explain that he walked along the boundaries of the field to inspect the condition of the field. (End of note.)
(Collected note) And if he used it, etc. Tosafos, the Rosh, Rashba, Nimukei Yosef, and other poskim explain that he went to inspect the field, etc. (End of note.)
All this, etc., but if he did not, etc. The Tur writes this in the name of the Rosh, in Responsa, klal 97, seif 4, where he explains that there are three distinctions. If there is a plausible basis for the challenge, such as where the challenger produces an unverified document and says that he wishes to authenticate it within thirty days, then even if the buyer already trod the boundaries, so long as he has not yet paid the money, the money is not taken from him. This is also clear from Tosafos there, 46b, s.v. “for there is not, etc.” Rama combined the rule of a plausible basis with the first category, treating them as only two distinctions, but that is not correct. Bach writes likewise.
(Collected note) All this concerns, etc. See the Rosh there and his responsum at the end of klal 97, as also copied by Rabbeinu Yerucham. The Tur’s reading yields three categories. A mere rumor never permits cancellation. A plausible claim permits cancellation so long as the money has not yet been paid. A formal challenge is governed by the stated distinction. If beit din sees a genuine basis in the challenger’s words, even after the transaction has been fully completed the buyer may cancel. The Rosh and Tur also discuss a challenger who merely delays bringing his witnesses, distinguishing according to whether the seller is financially reliable. That case too concerns a buyer who already made chazakah but has not yet paid, for the Rosh says that the judge should not remove the money from him. Rama relied upon what he wrote in siman 73, seif 10. The same appears to apply to a formal challenge, as indicated by the responsum of the Rif cited there. (End of note.)
And if he retracted, etc. As stated in Bava Metzia chapter 4: with a one-sixth disparity the sale stands and the ona'ah is returned, while with more than one-sixth the sale is void. This shows that returning the ona'ah does not itself prevent retraction where the disparity is greater.
He owed, etc., and need not, etc. As stated in Bava Batra 171b, ordinarily one does not write a receipt because if the receipt is lost the other party could collect again. Even according to our ruling that a receipt may be written because the borrower brought the difficulty upon himself, this case is different. All this applies before the buyer trod the boundaries of the field.
