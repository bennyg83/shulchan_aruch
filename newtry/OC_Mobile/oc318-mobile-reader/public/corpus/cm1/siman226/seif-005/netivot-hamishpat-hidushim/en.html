Where there is a challenge that has not yet been clarified. Rambam and Rosh disagree about this rule. Rambam holds it applies even after the purchaser has paid. Rosh and Tur hold it applies specifically before payment. According to them, if the challenge has not yet been clarified and someone merely says, "I have witnesses and a document," the purchaser need not pay. If the challenge has been clarified to the extent that the challenger produces a document, even if it is not yet validated, then even where the property already has clear boundaries the purchaser need not pay. But if the purchaser already paid, then even where beit din sees a strong basis for the challenge, the purchaser cannot recover the money until he produces a formal certificate of eviction, unless the challenger's documents are already validated. According to this, Rama's gloss is difficult because he places it upon the Mechaber's words, which concern a purchaser who already paid. In such a case, even a clarified challenge in the sense that an unvalidated document was shown does not allow the purchaser to recover his money until validated documents are produced. Sema.
If the purchaser retracted. This applies even if he has not yet actually retracted, but beit din already gave him permission to retract. If the challenge disappears before beit din gives permission to retract, the purchaser may no longer retract, unless he has a claim that would require him to preserve a receipt or similar proof.
