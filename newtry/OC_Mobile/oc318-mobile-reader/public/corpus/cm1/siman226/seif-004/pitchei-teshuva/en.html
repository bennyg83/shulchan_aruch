Afterward. Baer Heitev explains with a case where Shimon owed Yaakov and the right later came to him. Shevut Yaakov I, siman 165, questions this explanation. In such a case, even if Yaakov died before Reuven sold the field to Shimon, Yaakov's successor could still later collect from the field, because the seller knowingly sold property subject to that exposure, as appears from siman 85 and Ketubot, chapter Shnei Dayanei Gezerot. Shevut Yaakov therefore says that Sema apparently did not review the source in Ran, chapter Mi SheHayah Nasui, concerning one who sold his mother's ketubah for its speculative value. Even if the lien is on the field itself, comparable to a widow's ketubah against heirs, the seller cannot later seize this particular field by means of that ketubah. See Ran there.
