Guardians who sold, etc. The correct reading is: One who sells land to another, etc. This is the language of the Rambam in chapter 19 of Hilchot Mechirah. The Rosh and Tur, however, do not agree. They explain the Gemara's law as referring to a case where the purchaser had not yet paid the seller for the field, but had acquired it through one of the recognized modes of acquisition. The Sema, seif katan 9, also cites their position. The phrase, 'Show me your deed of dispossession and I will pay you,' accords with the Rambam. See what the Sema writes in the names of the Rosh and Tur, who explain the Gemara's phrase 'walking along the boundary' to mean that the purchaser walks around the boundary to determine whether the field is as the seller represented and what work it requires. Maharshal likewise rules in chapter 1 of Bava Kamma, siman 19, and proves this interpretation from the Gemara.
