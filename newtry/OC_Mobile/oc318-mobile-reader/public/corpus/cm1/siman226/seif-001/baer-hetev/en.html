He wished. Maharshach, part 3, siman 10, writes that the truth of the matter is that he is obligated to go and litigate with him. When the Gemara says that he 'may' do so, it means that the other party cannot reject him, but he is in fact obligated to go and litigate. Bnei Chayei.

The Tur explains the practical difference resulting from Reuven entering the litigation as a party (apart from the fact that he could otherwise act as Shimon's authorized agent, or beit din itself could advance Shimon's claims, since we rule that the court pleads on behalf of a purchaser). If the borrower designated this field as a general apotiki for the creditor, Reuven may nevertheless remove the creditor by paying money, whereas Shimon, the purchaser, cannot do so. There is another difference if Reuven made the field an apotiki for the creditor but had borrowed only an amount equal to the value of the land without its appreciation. If the creditor collects from Shimon the purchaser, he also collects the appreciation, and must pay only for the purchaser's expenditures, as in the law of one who descends into another's field without permission, as stated in siman 115:2 and siman 375. If, however, he collects from Reuven, he collects only up to the amount of his debt. So write the Rosh and Nimukei Yosef. Sema. I have already written in siman 115 that some disagree and hold that a creditor never takes more than the amount of his debt. Shach.
