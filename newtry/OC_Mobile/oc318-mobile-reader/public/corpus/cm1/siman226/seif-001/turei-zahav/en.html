(Seif 5: before he used it, etc.) This wording implies that the later clause, "and if the purchaser used it," also concerns a case where he had already acquired the field through one of the recognized methods of acquisition, because this clause is presented as the converse of the opening phrase, "before he used it." If so, this is difficult, because use itself can acquire a field, as stated in siman 192. It appears to me that indeed use can be an act of acquisition. The present rule, however, addresses a separate question: when is the threat of third-party claims considered a defect? So long as the purchaser has not yet benefited from the field, the adverse claim is treated as a defect, as the wording here indicates, "before he had yet benefited and claimants came," etc. Once he has already derived some benefit, he is deemed to have accepted the possibility of later adverse claims. Therefore, two separate matters are required here: first, the basic acquisition; second, removal of the defect concern, meaning that after acquisition he used the property and thereby accepted some exposure. If there was no prior kinyan and he merely walked along the boundaries in a manner constituting use, that conduct may itself serve as the kinyan, but the defect concern remains unresolved because there has not yet been any subsequent benefit after acquisition. This, in my view, explains Rambam's words.
