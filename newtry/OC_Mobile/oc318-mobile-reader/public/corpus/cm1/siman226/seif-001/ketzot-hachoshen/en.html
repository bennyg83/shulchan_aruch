Reuven wished to litigate with Levi. Tosafot writes that there is an additional practical difference. If Reuven has another Torah oath against Levi, he can attach by gilgul a Torah oath that Levi did not repay this debt. This implies that even a party who is ultimately collecting can sometimes become subject to a Torah oath through gilgul. I discussed in siman 94 whether a Torah oath can be imposed through gilgul upon one who collects, in light of the verse, "its owner shall accept and he shall not pay," which appears to characterize Torah oaths as those of one who swears and does not pay. Tosafot here suggests that gilgul can create a Torah oath even in a collection context. Perhaps they speak after payment, analogous to the strategy in Perek HaKotev. The Mishnah's general language, "All Torah oaths are sworn and do not pay," still leaves the question requiring study.
