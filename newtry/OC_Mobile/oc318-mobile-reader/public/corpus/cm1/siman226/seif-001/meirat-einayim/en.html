If Reuven wishes to conduct the litigation, etc. Meaning, he may make himself the litigant and assert that the field is his and that he has standing to defend it.
That belonged to Shimon, etc. The Tur explains the practical benefit Shimon derives from Reuven becoming the litigant. Do not say that even without becoming a party Reuven could simply serve as Shimon's authorized representative, or that beit din would advance for a purchaser every claim the seller could have made. One practical difference is where Reuven, the borrower, made this field an apotiki to his creditor. Reuven can nevertheless satisfy the creditor with money, because he said only, "If I do not repay you with money, you shall have no collection except from this field," and now he is repaying with money just as he borrowed money. Shimon the purchaser, by contrast, cannot necessarily remove the creditor by offering money once the field was designated as an apotiki. Another difference is where Reuven designated the field as an apotiki and the creditor lent only the amount equal to the field's original value, not the later appreciation. If the creditor collected from Shimon the purchaser, he could also take the appreciation, giving the purchaser only reimbursement for his expenses, like one who enters another's field without permission, as the Tur and Mechaber write above in siman 115, seif 2, and below in siman 375. If the creditor collects directly from Reuven, however, he can take only the amount of the debt. The apotiki means that if Reuven does not pay money, the creditor's collection is limited to this field, but Reuven can satisfy the debt by giving from this land only an amount equal to the debt. The Rosh and Nimukei Yosef write likewise.
