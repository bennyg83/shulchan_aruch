What have I to do with you? Baer Heitev cites the Tur concerning the practical difference. Chatam Sofer, Choshen Mishpat, siman 46, gives another. Where a creditor has two deeds against one debtor signed by two groups of witnesses who contradict one another, some advise burning the smaller deed so the debtor cannot require an oath concerning the existence of two conflicting deeds. If the creditor comes first against a purchaser with the smaller deed, the purchaser cannot object that another deed exists, because the creditor can say: "What have I to do with you? Once I collect this field from you I have no further claim against you, so why does the other deed concern you?" If the original seller could not litigate directly with the creditor, the creditor could collect from the purchaser with the small deed and later proceed against the seller with the large one once the seller acquired assets, with no remaining small deed to support an oath demand. Since the seller may litigate, however, the creditor's position is weaker. See also Nachalat Tzvi on Shaar Mishpat to siman 20.
