D) Rambam is named after the Court of Justice and as such, the High Court of Justice and the Holy Spirit:
There, like the Tur, seif 10. The Ran likewise writes in the name of the Geonim that anyone who retracts before the accident occurs receives the curse of Mi Shepara. The incident involving the wine that was about to be seized, 49b, where Mi Shepara is not mentioned, is no proof otherwise, because they came only to ask whether the buyer could retract. In truth, he would still have to accept Mi Shepara. Rabbenu Chananel writes so, and the same is reported in the name of Rav Hai, the students of the Rashba in the name of Rabbi Yitzchak bar Reuven, and the Mordechai at the beginning of HaZahav.
Tur in this siman, seif 70, in the name of the Rosh, from the cited incident, since the Gemara did not say that he had to accept Mi Shepara. Rather, Mi Shepara applies only when one retracts because of a rise or fall in price. Tosafot likewise write so, 47b, and Rabbenu Yerucham writes that this is the view of Rav Hai and Rabbenu Tam. The same applies where he retracts because the goods were wine and became vinegar, since there is some loss. The Ran likewise writes in the name of the Raavad. The Bach writes that where the authorities dispute the matter, we rule leniently for the defendant and stringently for the claimant, and he need not accept Mi Shepara.
