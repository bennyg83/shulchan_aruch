Because he fears losing the entire purchase. See Sema, seif katan 5, who precisely notes the phrase "the entire purchase." This excludes a case where he fears only a partial loss, such as one who purchased wine and fears that it may sour. Shaar Mishpat writes that from Rabbeinu Yerucham cited by Beit Yosef it appears that this distinction applies even after the loss has already occurred. If the property was entirely lost, the party does not accept Mi Shepara, but if only a partial deterioration occurred, such as wine that soured, he does. It appears that this applies specifically to wine that soured. If part of the merchandise was destroyed by an unavoidable event and no longer exists, even if much of it remains, he does not accept Mi Shepara with respect to the lost portion. Since that portion is entirely gone and no longer extant, it is comparable to a total loss. This is unlike price fluctuation or wine that soured, where the object remains but has deteriorated.
