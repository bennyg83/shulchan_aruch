With a small gift. Bava Metzia says Rabbi Yochanan agrees that one may not retract from a small promised gift because the recipient relies upon it. The Gemara proves this from one who tells a Levi, "A kor of tithe belongs to you in my possession," whereupon the Levi may designate it as terumat maaser for other produce. Rashi explains that the Levi may rely on the promise because upright Jews do not act deceitfully and the donor will not give it to another Levi. The difficulty is that even if the donor is morally bound not to retract, the Levi still has not formally acquired the tithe. How can he treat it as his and separate terumah from it? Shitah Mekubetzet answers, citing Rosh, that the case concerns recognized priestly or Levitical recipients. Such a recipient is treated as though the gift had already reached him. But if that recognition were itself full acquisition, it would not prove the law of a small gift. Tosafot elsewhere explains recognized priestly recipients merely through the rule that one may not retract from a small gift. Thus formal ownership still seems lacking. The better explanation follows Rosh in Nedarim, citing Rabbi Eliezer of Metz. If ownerless property lies before a person and no one else is near enough to take it, he may consecrate it because it is within his power to acquire. Likewise, property that he can redeem or otherwise certainly obtain is treated as within his power. Here, since the donor is certain not to retract because upright Jews do not act deceitfully, the tithe is effectively within the Levi's power to acquire. Although one formal acquisition remains, that missing act does not prevent use of the broader principle that something presently within one's control can be designated for terumah and tithes. This explains how the Levi can separate from it even though the small gift itself was not yet formally acquired.
