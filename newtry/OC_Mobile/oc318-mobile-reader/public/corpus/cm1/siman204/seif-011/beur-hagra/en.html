Some say even, etc. As stated on 49a in the incident of Rav Kahana, who was given, etc. The other opinion said that a verbal commitment, etc., and it was stated, etc. This indicates that Rabbi Yochanan disagrees even in this type of case. Tosafos there, s.v. “he concedes, etc.,” writes likewise regarding a price increase. See Rashi there, s.v. “that he should not speak, etc.,” whose language implies that Rabbi Yochanan applies the rule even where the price changed, although Rashi’s proof is not conclusive, since the case need not involve a market change. The incident of Rav Chiya bar Yosef is also not proof, for perhaps he followed Rav, just as he followed Rav concerning earnest money. The Yerushalmi in HaZahav appears to support the first view. It teaches that if one sold an item worth five for six and, before they completed the transaction, it rose to seven, Rabbi Yochanan rules that just as the sale is voidable by one side, it is voidable by the other. The Yerushalmi initially assumes that money had been paid and challenges from the Mishnah of the four measures in sellers, because the party who was not deceived cannot retract without Mi Shepara. It answers that no money had yet been paid. The novelty is that where the price changes before payment, there is not even a breach of faith.
