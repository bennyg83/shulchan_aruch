The movable property. See Beit Yosef, seif 5, concerning whether the sanction of Mi Shepara applies to land where the sale was not completed, such as where money was given in a place where deeds are customarily written and money alone does not acquire. Beit Yosef cites a dispute among the early authorities, and Darkei Moshe above, siman 190, letter 1, discusses the same issue. See Responsa Rabbi Akiva Eiger, siman 134, who writes simply that Mi Shepara applies even to land where money did not complete the acquisition, as is evident from Tosafos, Ketubot 93a, s.v. Ad. He expresses surprise that siman 190, seif 7, states that in a place where deeds are normally written, money does not acquire, yet no authority there mentions that the party who retracts must accept Mi Shepara. It is surprising that he did not mention the discussion of Beit Yosef and Darkei Moshe cited above. Perhaps when he writes that no authority mentions it, he means the later commentators on Shulchan Aruch. His proof is also found in Tosafos, Bava Metzia 14a, s.v. Ad Shelo Hechzik, although Tosafos in Ketubot states the matter more explicitly. See Responsa Mishkenot Yaakov, siman 46, who discusses this law at length and concludes that because many great early authorities hold that Mi Shepara applies even to land, including the Rivash, Mordechai, Baal HaItur, Baalei Tosafos, Ran, Ritva, and Hagahot Ashri, and no authority expressly rejects their view other than Rambam and Rashba, who only express doubt, the practical ruling should follow the majority.
