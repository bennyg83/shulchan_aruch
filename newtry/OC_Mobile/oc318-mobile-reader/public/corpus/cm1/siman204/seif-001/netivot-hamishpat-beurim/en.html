I later obtained Machaneh Efrayim and saw that in Hilchot Kinyan Ma'ot, siman 5, he rules that if one bought movable property without cash and gave the seller his own promissory note, he acquires completely. His reason is Tosafot in Bava Batra 46, who explain that in kinyan sudar there is no concern that the seller will say, "Your wheat burned in the attic," because the buyer has not yet given the money and the seller therefore exerts himself to save the merchandise. Machaneh Efrayim says the same should apply where the buyer only gave a promissory note. In my view this is certainly incorrect. If so, why does silver not acquire gold? With money itself, Tosafot say the concern of "your money burned" does not ordinarily apply. Likewise, if after a kinyan sudar the money has already been paid, Tosafot's reason would no longer apply, yet surely the kinyan remains effective. Necessarily the Sages did not distinguish case by case. Anything legally categorized as acquisition by money was stripped of full acquisition power. Other acquisition modes remain effective even in occasional cases where the original fire concern could arise, because in the ordinary form of those acquisitions the concern is absent. Sema writes this explicitly in siman 250, seif katan 15, and Shitah Mekubetzet in Bava Metzia 48 says similarly. A promissory note given by the buyer is plainly a form of monetary consideration. It therefore does not complete acquisition, and the Sages did not distinguish. Indeed, it is even stronger than partial cash payment. A promissory note can be sold to another and converted into cash according to its assessed value. Shach in siman 39, seif katan 49, also implies that no full acquisition occurs. Machaneh Efrayim further writes that if the seller says, "Give one hundred to so and so," the buyer fully acquires movable property. In my view this too is incorrect. Ran in Kiddushin compares only the special case of "Take one hundred" involving an important person to chalipin, because the recipient receives not money itself but the benefit that the distinguished person accepted from him, and that benefit functions like chalipin. Merely saying, "Give one hundred to another person," is no stronger than "Give one hundred to me." It is still acquisition by money and produces only mi shepara.
