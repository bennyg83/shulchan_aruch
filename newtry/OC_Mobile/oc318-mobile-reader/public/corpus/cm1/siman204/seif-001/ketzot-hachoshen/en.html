And he must accept the curse of "Who exacted payment." If the seller denies that he received the money as purchase money and says instead that it was given as a loan or deposit, he returns the money. As I wrote in siman 88, where there is no actual denial of an outstanding monetary obligation, beit din does not pronounce the curse directly upon him by name merely because he disputes the legal character of the payment. Rather, beit din states the general curse upon whoever does not keep his word.
