Shiltei HaGibborim in chapter Shenayim Ochazin writes that Hagahot Ashri there implies that although we rule that a sale is not completed by words alone, nevertheless after the enactment of the oath of hesset, if Reuven claims to Shimon, "You sold me such and such utensil," and Shimon denies it, then even though the utensil remains in Shimon's possession and had he admitted the sale but said "I retract" he would have no liability, once he denies the sale Reuven can impose a hesset oath upon him. These words do not appear correct. Since Shimon could retract, why should he have to swear? Hagahot Ashri can be explained as speaking where a valid act of acquisition was performed, as stated at the beginning of siman 222. Shach.
Mi Shepara. If one party wronged the other by one sixth and the buyer paid but had not yet performed meshichah, the Bach rules in siman 227 that the aggrieved party may retract without accepting the curse of Mi Shepara, even if the other party offers to return the amount of the overcharge. The party who committed the ona'ah, however, must accept Mi Shepara if he wishes to retract. The Bach attempts to prove this from the Gaon's words cited by the Rif in chapter HaMokher Et HaSefinah. He did not penetrate the Rif's intent, and the ruling itself is incorrect. Rather, both parties must accept Mi Shepara. The general rule is that payment of money, regarding Mi Shepara, has the same status as meshichah regarding the law that the sale stands and the amount of ona'ah is returned. Ramban states this plainly in his novellae there and proves it at length. Shach.
