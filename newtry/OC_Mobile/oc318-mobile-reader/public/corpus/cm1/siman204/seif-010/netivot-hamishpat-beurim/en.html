Through the benefit of forgiving a loan. Some explanation is needed, because even when the lender transfers the loan itself as payment for movable property, that too might seem equivalent to forgiving the loan. It appears that the Shitah Mekubetzet in Bava Metzia 20 distinguishes between saying, "I forgive the loan to you," and saying, "I transfer the loan to you." Therefore, if he merely says that he transfers the loan in exchange for the movable property, the loan itself is not acquired as a transferred asset. He must first use the language of forgiveness, and then acquire the movable property through the benefit the borrower receives from that forgiveness.
That he extended the time for repayment. Ketzot HaChoshen asks that this is interest, and therefore resembles a transaction effected through a prohibited act, for which the sanction of "He Who exacted punishment" does not apply, as explained in siman 218. His question is strong. Perhaps the case is one where the parties fixed a full price for the object, for example one hundred, and the buyer intends to pay the entire one hundred. The extension of time on an existing loan is used only as the small benefit that effects the kinyan, comparable to a perutah that the buyer would otherwise have had to give a third party to persuade him to extend the time. Such a benefit can function as the money of acquisition, just as even a perutah given on condition that it be returned can effect acquisition, as explained in siman 190. The lender receives no additional monetary benefit from the buyer beyond the acquisition itself. According to this, the case can also be a transaction whose sale is burdensome to the seller, so that the relevant benefit is the seller's benefit rather than the buyer's, as explained in Nedarim 31. The Sema's statement in seif katan 16 that before the original due date arrives, an extension of time is not considered a benefit, is very difficult in my eyes and requires substantial study.
