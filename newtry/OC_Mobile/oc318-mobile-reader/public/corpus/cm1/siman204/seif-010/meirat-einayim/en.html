For this is like giving money, etc. Some disagree. Their view is that since he is not giving him anything now, he cannot acquire through the preexisting loan. Even if the loan money were still physically in the borrower's possession, since it had been given to him for expenditure it is regarded as though it no longer exists. Rambam disagrees. In his view, even though the loan money is no longer in the borrower's hand because he already spent it, we treat it as though it were still present. If he acquires land, which can be acquired through money, the land is fully acquired through the debt. With movable property, the transaction is effective to the extent that retraction subjects the party to mi shepara, just as if he were presently giving actual money to purchase the movable property or land. Each side has support from the Gemara. Their dispute applies specifically where the debt arose from a loan. If the debt arose from a sale, it was already explained in siman 199, in the Tur and the Mechaber, seif 2, that even the Rosh agrees that one can acquire through it, including movable property as a complete acquisition. Since a debt arising from a sale in this form is uncommon, the Sages did not enact their decree out of concern that the seller might say, "Your wheat burned in the attic."
