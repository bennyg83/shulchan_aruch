(Collected note) Some disagree, etc. Likewise siman 199 seif 2 in the gloss states, “Nevertheless some say, etc.” This resolves Tosafos’ question in Bava Metzia 62b, s.v. “Behold,” and the same ruling is stated without qualification in Yoreh Deah, siman 160 seif 3. (End of note.)
(Collected note) And they say, etc. This wording appears to imply that the benefit of forgiving a loan does not effect full acquisition, only Mi Shepara. That is incorrect. According to those authorities there is no distinction between purchase money and loan money. The Rambam alone distinguishes them, as explained above in siman 199 seif 2. According to the others, the money itself does not acquire at all, whether it is purchase money or loan money, and Mi Shepara does not apply merely because of the money. But the benefit created by forgiving the debt effects a complete acquisition in either case. See Maggid Mishneh, Hilchos Mechirah chapter 5, who writes that the Rambam appears to distinguish, etc., and then contrasts the Ramban and Rashba, who rule the same in every case. See Tosafos, Bava Metzia 62b, s.v. “one who is owed, etc.,” and 46b, s.v. “there is, etc.” (End of note.)
