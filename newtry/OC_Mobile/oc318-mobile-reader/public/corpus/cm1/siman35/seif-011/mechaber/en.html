A deaf person is disqualified from testifying, whether he speaks but cannot hear or hears but cannot speak, even if his understanding is sound. A witness must testify orally in beit din and must be capable of hearing the judges' words and their admonition. Likewise, if a person became mute, then even if he was examined in the manner used for a bill of divorce, his testimony was found coherent, and he testified before us in his handwriting, it is entirely invalid, except for testimony permitting a woman to remarry, concerning which the Sages were lenient to prevent her from remaining an agunah.
