A deaf person is disqualified, etc. See Beit Yosef and Kesef Mishneh, who suggest that Rambam may derive this from the verse, "and he hears." They did not note the Tosefta in Shevuot, Perek Shevuat HaEdut. The first Tanna derives: "and he hears," excluding one who is deaf and cannot hear; "if he does not tell," excluding a mute who cannot speak. Thus the deaf person mentioned there is one who can speak but cannot hear. R. Akiva derives the rule from "you shall inquire and investigate," excluding a deaf person who is not within the full framework of interrogation and examination. These are exactly Rambam's words. He combines the teachings of the first Tanna and R. Akiva into one rule: the witness must testify orally, or at least be capable of oral testimony, and must be able to hear the judges and the warning they give him, which is part of the process of interrogation and examination. This is straightforward.
