Likewise, if he became mute, etc. Meaning he originally could speak and later lost the ability. This explains the conclusion that he testifies before us in writing, because having once been able to speak and still being able to hear, he learned to write. The phrase "although he was examined in the manner used for gittin" also relates to one who can speak but not hear, or hear but not speak, and "his mind is sound" means sound according to that examination. It is also possible that the Mechaber states the examination specifically regarding one who became mute because in the laws of gittin that examination is required when he married before becoming mute, so that his marriage was a full Torah marriage, and only afterward lost speech. One born deaf-mute or with the comparable defect may sometimes divorce in the same form by which the marriage was created, as explained in Even HaEzer.
