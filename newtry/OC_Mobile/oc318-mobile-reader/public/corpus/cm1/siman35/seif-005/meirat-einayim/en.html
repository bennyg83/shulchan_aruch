That the customs of virgins were performed for her. Meaning that when she entered marriage she was treated in the customary manner of a virgin, such as having her head covered with the bridal veil. This testimony is accepted to collect the principal ketubah of two hundred, because most women marry as virgins. Nevertheless, this testimony is required and we do not rely on the majority alone, because there is a slight weakness: the Gemara says most virgins marry with public notice, and here there was no public notice. Rosh and Ran write that here too another witness who was already an adult at the time must join him. The Tur agrees. Rambam's wording is unqualified and the Maggid Mishneh and Kesef Mishneh disagree over his view.
This place is a beit haperas. Rashi explains that one who plows a grave creates a beit haperas extending one hundred cubits, because the plow may drag human bones that distance and a person passing there may touch them. Ran explains the case as a valley known to contain a beit haperas, with uncertainty over the extent of impurity. If he testifies that this particular location is the beit haperas, his testimony also establishes that what lies outside it is pure. Raavad explains differently. See Ran.
