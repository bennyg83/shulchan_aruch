For example, in the women's section of a synagogue. See Panim Me'irot, part 2, end of siman 180, by my grandfather, concerning a case where beit din sent two men to take testimony from a woman regarding seats in the women's section of a synagogue. Beit din received the testimony outside the litigant's presence and issued a ruling on its basis. The woman later retracted her statement, and one scholar wrote that the judgment should be annulled. Panim Me'irot ruled that it should not be annulled because judgment had already been rendered. Since there are several circumstances in which testimony may be received outside a litigant's presence, one beit din does not scrutinize the conduct of another beit din, and we presume they acted properly. As for the testimony not having been received before three, Beit Yosef in siman 28, and Shulchan Aruch there, seif 23, cite Meisharim that a community may enact that two scribes receive testimony. It has already become the practice of Jewish courts to send trusted communal representatives to distinguished women so they may give testimony. Beit Yosef in Even HaEzer siman 17 also writes, according to several Geonim, that where testimony need not be given before beit din a witness cannot retract after stating it. I do not know the location of that passage, and the words of Chelkat Mechokek there, seif 8, appear explicitly contrary; see what I wrote in Pitchei Teshuva there, seif katan 45. Panim Me'irot concludes that in his view no beit din should annul the ruling of another beit din. See there.
