The Shach writes that Responsa Mahara Sasson rules regarding testimony of anusim that they are not believed even after they later become fully qualified, because testimony requires both its beginning and end to occur while the witness is qualified. The Shach disagrees. If that were the reason, why would the Gemara need to explain regarding a non-Jew that he is not precise? According to the Shach, anusim who later become qualified should be valid because inwardly they remained Jews and would presumably have paid close attention. However, the Tosefta also implies that the reason regarding a non-Jew is the requirement that both beginning and end be in a state of qualification. Accordingly, the Gemara's reason that a non-Jew is not precise may refer specifically to a rabbinic matter. See above, siman 34, seif katan 36, and what was written there. This passage is also a strong proof for that view and against the Shach.
