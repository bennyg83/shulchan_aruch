A non-Jew or slave, etc. He is not believed. At the end of Orach Chayyim siman 399, however, the Tur and Mechaber write that regarding Shabbos boundaries a slave or maidservant is believed even before emancipation. Therefore, when the Tur writes this law here, he carefully says, 'all these matters regarding which the adult testifies.' I explained in the Perishah that he says 'testifies' because this section concerns matters requiring formal testimony. Regarding boundaries, the credibility of a slave is not based on the law of testimony. If it were, a slave or maidservant could not be believed because they are disqualified as witnesses. Rather, they were believed because the matter is merely clarification of a fact. The Mechaber likewise alludes to this by saying, 'and he testified after converting,' teaching that only where formal testimony is required are they not believed, unlike boundaries.
He is not believed. While he was a non-Jew the matter was not incumbent upon him, and a person does not retain in mind a matter that was not relevant to him. Similarly, while he was a slave, he lacked control to free himself, and in addition a slave is not obligated in all the mitzvot. This is the Gemara's reasoning.
