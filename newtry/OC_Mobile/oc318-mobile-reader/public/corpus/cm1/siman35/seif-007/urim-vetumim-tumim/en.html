If he was a non-Jew, etc. The Gemara says that he is not sufficiently precise. Shach concludes that for Torah testimony this point is not needed, because a non-Jew is plainly disqualified. It matters only for the limited testimony discussed above concerning document authentication, where we do not require that the witness have been qualified both at the beginning and the end. Even there, however, a non-Jew is not believed because he is not precise. See Shach's discussion regarding persons who had converted under compulsion and later returned to qualification, and see what I wrote in siman 34. In Even HaEzer siman 42, seif 5, it is explicit that Jews who converted under coercion are not disqualified from testimony even though they failed to fulfill the mitzvah of sanctifying Hashem's Name. This is difficult from Rashba, responsum 1237, where it appears that even one compelled by non-Jews to commit adultery is treated as wicked because he was obligated to be killed rather than transgress. This requires study.
