Responsa Maharan Sasson, siman 90, rules that forced converts are not believed even after returning to full qualification, because testimony requires qualification at both its beginning and end. I am not convinced. If so, why does the Gemara explain the disqualification of a non-Jew by saying that he was not careful? It should simply say that he lacked qualification at the beginning. Rather, since conversion was in his power, this resembles a monetary disqualification that can be removed. Thus the Gemara's concern applies specifically to a non-Jew, who was not careful, whereas forced converts who later return are qualified, since inwardly they remained Jews and were careful. The responsum of Mahari ben Lev, part I, siman 23, cited by Maharan Sasson is no proof, because it concerns testimony given while they were still under coercion. His proof from part III, siman 75, however, requires study. A Tosefta at the end of chapter five of Sanhedrin indicates that even for a non-Jew the reason is lack of qualification at the beginning. Accordingly, the Gemara's reason of inaccuracy may apply only to a rabbinic matter. This is also implied by the Ritva at the end of the second chapter of Ketubot. Careful examination of the authorities at the end of siman 33 likewise shows that the law does not depend upon the witness's power to become qualified.
