A blind person. See Be'er HaGolah, who writes that the Rambam concludes with the verse, 'and he is a witness, whether he saw,' etc. See Kesef Mishnah there, chapter 9 of Hilchos Edus, who writes that the proof brought from the words 'whether he saw' requires study, for where is this derivation found? A gloss printed there suggests that the Rambam derived it from Arachin 18. See there. Birkei Yosef, entry 8, cites that the same is written in Mayim Chayim by the author of Peri Chadash. He adds in the name of a certain sage who challenged that proof, since no complete proof can be brought from there, but the rule is explicit in the Tosefta, chapter 3 of Shevuos. See there. He further writes in the name of Tashbetz, part III, siman 6, that even if the blind person can see somewhat, if he cannot distinguish one person from another even when looking at them, his testimony is void by Torah law, even though he can identify the person by voice. This is straightforward.
