Even if he is alone. The Ran writes that the requirement that an adult accompany him applies only to the first two matters in the Mishnah. Tosafot and the Rosh there imply the same. Rama adopted Tur's wording, which omitted the case of a beit haperas. In Shulchan Aruch's wording, taken from Rambam, Rama should have placed this gloss beside the beit haperas case, because from that point onward the minor is believed alone.
(Anthology) Even if he is alone. Ketubot 24a states: 'Come and hear: I am a priest and my companion is a priest,' etc. The Gemara explains that Rabbi Yehudah and the Sages dispute whether one elevates a person from terumah status to genealogical status. On 25a it limits this concern to biblical terumah, while the present case concerns rabbinic terumah, as stated on 28b. A single witness therefore suffices. Rav Huna son of Rav Yehoshua's statement cannot refer to this ruling of the Sages.

(Anthology) And that he shared with us, etc. See Tosafot there, s.v. tnan keman. Their answer depends on the existence of terumah. In the present era, when terumah is not practiced at the biblical level, their question returns. One might answer that their discussion follows Rabbi Meir, whereas according to the Sages all families are presumed fit and we do not suspect slave lineage. This answer is not correct, however, because regarding terumah and genealogy the commentators do not apply the general presumption of family fitness.
