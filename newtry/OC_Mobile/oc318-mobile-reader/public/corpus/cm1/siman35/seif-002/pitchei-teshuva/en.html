See Noda BiYehuda, siman 4, who writes that although Shulchan Aruch states this law without qualification in accordance with a responsum of the Rosh, and no dissenting view is cited, the matter is not unanimously accepted as practical law. Ramban's novellae to Bava Batra 154, in his explanation of the incident of Bnei Brak where one sold property and died immediately, appear to disagree. Even if one can reconcile Ramban with the Rosh, the Rosh's proof from that passage is at least undermined. The Rosh inferred that the reason the posthumous examination was ineffective was that the person had died, whereas had he been alive, when physical signs are not ordinarily subject to change, the examination would have been effective. According to Ramban, however, one might say the reverse: had he remained alive the examination certainly would not have helped, because after his death the signs could have developed. Beit Yosef himself, in Even HaEzer at the end of siman 43, cites this Rosh and writes that his teacher Rabbi Yaakov Bei Rav held that the Rashba and Rabbi Shimon Duran disagreed. Noda BiYehuda discusses the matter at length and concludes that the Rosh's ruling is rejected where there is a strong presumption of possession. In any event, how can money be extracted when Ramban, Rashba, and Rabbi Shimon Duran disagree, and Beit Yosef's teacher appears to have agreed with them? At minimum the possessor may say kim li. See there. In the Even HaEzer section, siman 61, Noda BiYehuda later writes that there is reason to uphold the Shulchan Aruch's ruling, because Beit Yosef's statement that Rashba disagreed appears to be a slip of the pen and should read Ramban, and Ramban's words do not conclusively prove that he disagrees in law. See there. Agudat Ezov, by Rabbi Moshe Ze'ev, in the discourse Emek Halakha, debates Noda BiYehuda at length and then writes that there is another serious difficulty with the Shulchan Aruch's ruling, because the Rosh himself appears to have retracted it in his rulings to Kiddushin, chapter HaOmer, siman 9. Beit Yosef in Choshen Mishpat siman 110 already cites Rabbi Yehudah son of the Rosh that one follows a decisor's later rulings. Agudat Ezov reports that he presented these arguments to Rabbi Chaim of Volozhin, who replied that he too had already addressed the matter in a special treatment, citing additional early authorities who disagree with the Rosh's responsum, and concluded that one should not rule in practice according to the Shulchan Aruch here. Get Mekushar, order of the third get, note 13, likewise writes that he had already demonstrated in a responsum that this ruling of Shulchan Aruch does not stand; that responsum was later printed in Meshivat Nefesh, Even HaEzer, siman 91. Rabbi Akiva Eiger notes that Beit Yosef, Even HaEzer siman 43, cites his teacher Rabbi Yaakov Bei Rav that Rabbi Shimon Duran and Rashba disagree, and Maharchash, part 1, siman 23, writes that the matter remains a doubt of law. See Shev Shema'teta at the end of Shema'teta 5. Sha'ar Mishpat also discusses Noda BiYehuda at length and concludes that although Beit Yosef cites authorities who disagree, we cannot decide among these great early authorities, and the rulings of the Tur, Shulchan Aruch, and the unqualified Rama, upon whom all Israel relies, should prevail. Nevertheless, the words of Maharchash appear correct that the matter remains a doubt of law.
