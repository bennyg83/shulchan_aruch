And the matchmaker went and betrothed her for him, etc., she is betrothed. This wording implies definite kiddushin. We must then say that witnesses were present when he disclosed his intent to the matchmaker, because it was already explained that, according to some authorities, even an express appointment of an agent requires witnesses. Yet one who examines the Rosh's wording will not find it clear that he regards these as definite kiddushin. The Gemara in Kiddushin 45a discusses two men drinking wine together, where one says to the other, "Betroth your daughter to my son with this cup of wine." Ravina says that even according to the view that we are concerned that a father may later have consented to his daughter's unauthorized kiddushin, we do not say that perhaps the son also consented to the father's act. The Gemara asks, perhaps the son had appointed his father as agent, and answers that a person is not so presumptuous as to appoint his father as agent. On this the Rosh writes: "It would appear possible to infer from this passage that if the son had revealed to his father that he desired her, etc., she is betrothed to him." The phrase "it would appear possible to infer" shows that he was not entirely certain that these are definite kiddushin.
