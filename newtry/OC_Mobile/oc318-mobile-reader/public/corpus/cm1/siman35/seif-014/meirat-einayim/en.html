A woman is disqualified. This is derived from the verse, 'the two men shall stand,' which refers to witnesses, as explained above.
And anyone whose status is doubtful, etc. In siman 34, seif 28, the Mechaber writes that if two testify that a person is disqualified and two others contradict them, he remains doubtfully disqualified. The reason is that testimony generally comes to extract or obligate money, and the burden of proof rests upon the claimant.
All this is according to strict law, etc. See siman 408, where the Tur cites Rambam regarding the statement that damages are paid only upon valid testimony. The purpose is to teach that one should not say: since people ordinarily present in stables and sheepfolds are slaves, shepherds, and similar persons, if they testify that one ox gored another or injured a person we should accept their testimony. The law teaches otherwise. Yet neither the Tur, Mechaber, nor Rama there says that by communal enactment their testimony may be accepted, as Rama says here. One must distinguish that ordinary damages are common, as Terumat HaDeshen, siman 353, and Darkei Moshe write. See what I wrote there in siman 408.
Provided the claimant, etc. This excludes a case where he claims only 'perhaps' and his entire claim rests on these witnesses, for then their testimony is not accepted. See below, siman 28, seif 15 in the gloss, Yoreh Deah siman 127 regarding women witnesses, and Even HaEzer siman 17 concerning the relative strength of two women and one witness.
