A woman is disqualified. This is derived from the verse that speaks of "men" and not women:
Even in a place where qualified witnesses are uncommon. Bava Kamma teaches "free men" and "members of the covenant," excluding slaves and non Jews. The Mishnah states this specifically in the context of damages so that one might not think that in horse stables and sheepfolds, where such qualified people are not usually present, the ordinary disqualifications should be relaxed. It therefore teaches that they remain disqualified, Rambam in his Commentary to the Mishnah:
And all this is according to strict law. The Sema asks that below in siman 408 the Mechaber cites the Rambam that with respect to damages neither slaves nor similar disqualified persons are believed even where qualified witnesses are uncommon, and neither the Mechaber nor the Rama adds that they may be relied upon by communal enactment as here. He answers that damages are common, whereas the present cases are occasional. No such enactment was made for common events, because otherwise every wrongdoer could hire non Jews or disqualified people to testify that his fellow caused damage, and qualified people could not live normally:
For example, in the women's section of the synagogue. The Maharshal concludes in Yam Shel Shlomo, Bava Kamma, that if a person established possession in a place there for three years, testimony of women does not suffice to remove him from that chazakah. Likewise, one qualified male witness has greater evidentiary force than two women, Shach:
Provided that the plaintiff himself claims with certainty. If he himself does not know the facts and sues only on the basis of these witnesses, their testimony is ineffective.
