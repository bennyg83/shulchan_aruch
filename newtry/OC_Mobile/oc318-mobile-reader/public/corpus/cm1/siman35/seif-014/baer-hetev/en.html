The enactment. In siman 408, seif 1, concerning damages, where valid witnesses are required, the Tur writes that one might have thought that because only servants, shepherds, and similar people are commonly present in horse stables and sheep pens, they should be believed in testimony; the law therefore teaches otherwise. See there. The Tur did not say that they may testify by force of an enactment, as stated here. One must therefore say that cases of damage are different because they are common occurrences. Terumat HaDeshen writes likewise. Thus writes the Sema.
Women. The Shach writes in the name of Maharshal that this applies specifically before one has established possession for three years. If he has possessed the property for three years, we no longer remove it from him on the testimony of women. In all cases, one male witness is more credible than two women.
A relative. Maharshal rules that a relative is never believed unless his testimony is against his own interest, in which case he is believed. See Bava Kamma, chapter 1, siman 41, where he discusses these laws at length. Shach.
