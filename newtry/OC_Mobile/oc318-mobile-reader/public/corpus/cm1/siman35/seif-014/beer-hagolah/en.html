d) Rambam there, from the Mishnah, Rosh Hashanah 22a. In Shevuot 30a it derives this from "and the two men shall stand." The Rambam writes there that Scripture says "by the mouth of two witnesses," using masculine language rather than feminine. The Perishah writes that he does not know why the Rambam left the first proof and brought this one, which he finds less satisfactory, since the entire Torah is expressed in masculine language.
e) He concludes there that money is not extracted on the basis of doubt and punishment is not imposed on the basis of doubt, as a matter of Torah law. The Kesef Mishneh writes that this is agreed throughout the Gemara: if money is not extracted on the basis of doubt, all the more so punishment is not imposed on that basis.
Meaning, this excludes a case where the claimant himself is uncertain and seeks recovery solely on the basis of the testimony of these witnesses, whose testimony is not valid. See above, siman 28, seif 15 in the gloss; Yoreh Deah siman 127, seif 3 regarding testimony and the credibility of women in prohibitions; and Even HaEzer siman 17 regarding whether two women or one male witness has greater weight.
