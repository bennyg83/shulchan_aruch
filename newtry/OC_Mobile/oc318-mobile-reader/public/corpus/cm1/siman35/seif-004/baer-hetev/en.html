Believed. This applies specifically when adult witnesses are signed on the document, because validation of documents is rabbinic, while by Torah law their testimony is regarded as though it had already been examined in beit din. But where the persons themselves are litigants and obligated themselves in their own handwriting to pay a sum of money or undertake some other obligation, the person is certainly not believed to validate their handwriting based on what he saw as a minor, even if he says that he saw the signature again after reaching adulthood, for this falls under the prohibition that fathers shall not be put to death on the testimony of children. Sema.
Who says. The Sema writes that although there is no dispute on this point, the Mechaber nevertheless introduces it as "some say," in accordance with his customary style, as in siman 16, seif 2. The Shach writes that in Bedek HaBayit, siman 46, the Beit Yosef records a dissenting view.
