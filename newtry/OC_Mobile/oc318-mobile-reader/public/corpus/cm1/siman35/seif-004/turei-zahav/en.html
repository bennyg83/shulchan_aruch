(See the Beit Yosef, seif 6, citing Tosafot: testimony that this field was valued at such-and-such, etc., yet he valued it above its actual worth, etc.) This appears contrary to reason. The case may involve two brothers or partners who divided property, one taking land and the other money, and we now seek to determine at what valuation the land was assessed. Because the minor’s mind was close to them, and he saw one party take the land in exchange for money, he may assume the land must have been worth substantially more than the money, for otherwise why would that person have preferred land to the superior liquid asset? This explanation is from Mahari.
