This is my father's handwriting, etc. This applies where an adult joins him in identifying the signature, even if that adult is related to the signatories. Sema says that the leniency applies where his father signed as a witness to another person's document, but not where he identifies a relative's personal handwritten obligation, because his testimony would directly impose liability upon the relative. I do not understand why this was stated categorically. Below, siman 46:19, there are two opinions whether witnesses authenticating signatures may be relatives of lender or borrower. One can distinguish only if one says that authentication of personal handwriting has Torah-level force while authentication of ordinary signatures is rabbinic, but that distinction itself requires study. See siman 46 and Tumim, where I also leave unresolved the case where he identifies his father's signature not by handwriting recognition but by saying, “He signed before me.”
