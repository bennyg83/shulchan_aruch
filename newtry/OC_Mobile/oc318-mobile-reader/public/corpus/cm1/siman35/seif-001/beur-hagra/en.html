And if it became full. The Beit Yosef derives this from Tosafot in Yevamot, chapter 2, s.v. "deHevi." This is mistaken, for nothing there indicates it, and if anything the opposite seems implied. It appears that the Tur derived this law from the Rambam, who writes in chapter 2 of Hilchot Ishut: a boy thirteen years old, etc., and signs appeared on him, etc.; if he was not examined below, etc. They objected from Niddah 48b: "with a man there is one sign, etc.," namely the lower sign and not the upper. It seems to me that this means: for a male, certainly we rely on one sign, the lower sign, and not the upper, from what is stated at the beginning of chapter 8 of Sanhedrin: "the lower and not the upper, etc.; 'a son' and not a man, a minor is exempt, etc." If the upper sign alone were required, how could there ever be a ben sorer u'moreh, since the relevant period is only three months and occurs well before the upper beard develops? This is unlike a female, where the verse says "son and not daughter." Nor can one say that for a male it is impossible for the upper sign to precede, because Yevamot there implies it can happen. Nevertheless, usually the lower sign comes first, as with a woman according to Rabbi Meir there, and as is evident to the senses. Thus the Rambam's wording "a boy thirteen years old, etc." refers to where we see that the lower sign has not appeared; "if he was not examined, etc." This is the meaning here of "and if it became full, etc."
