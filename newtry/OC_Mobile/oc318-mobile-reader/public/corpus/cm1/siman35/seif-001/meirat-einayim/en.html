A minor is disqualified from testimony. We derive this from the verse, "And the two men shall stand," which refers to witnesses. If it referred to litigants, it would imply that only men come for judgment and not minors or women, which is incorrect, as explained in siman 7.
Two hairs after thirteen complete years. Meaning thirteen full years. One should not say that after thirty days into the thirteenth year he is already counted as having completed a year. Regarding testimony he becomes an adult only after full thirteen years and the required hairs. Regarding judges, however, the Tur and Mechaber in siman 7 cite a view that a thirteen-year-old may judge even without having produced two hairs. That view holds judicial qualification depends on understanding and therefore testimony and judging are not fully equated. Both disqualify minority, but each defines the end of minority according to its own requirements. I also wrote there that another view requires a judge to be eighteen and to have two hairs, because in that opinion the status of an adult man for judging begins only then.
If they delayed examining him, etc., and later examined him and found, etc. It appears the same, and all the more so, according to the view that a person may not judge until eighteen and two hairs. If he judged at eighteen without examination and was later examined and found qualified, his judgment stands.
If his beard became full. The Tur writes, "if his upper beard grew." The Mechaber changed this to "became full" because in Beit Yosef he distinguishes between growth and fullness. I wrote in Derishah that they are the same concept. "Became full" is the Talmudic language and "grew" is the Tur's language. The Gemara says "his beard became full" only to teach that two isolated hairs in the upper beard do not suffice. "Full" means many hairs, not that each hair reached some particular length. Once there are many hairs, their mere growth suffices, unlike the lower signs where two hairs must reach the required size.
