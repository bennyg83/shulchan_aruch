Seif 1. Note: See the Shach’s lengthy discussion whether exact hours are required when calculating years, and see Urim VeTumim. It appears to me that there is a proof from the Gemara that exact hours do matter. Our Sages expounded Moshe’s statement, “I am unable to go out and come in today,” as meaning, “Today my days and my years were completed.” The commentators ask that elsewhere our Sages say concerning “because you did not believe in Me” that had Moshe and Aharon fully sanctified Hashem there, their time to depart the world would not yet have arrived. There is no contradiction. “My years were completed” does not mean that Moshe had a fixed heavenly allotment of exactly that many years which could not have been extended. It means that Hashem filled his years from day to day, so that he did not die halfway through a year. But if so, why did the Sages say both “my days” and “my years”? Either phrase should have sufficed. Moreover, our Sages say that Moshe was born on the seventh of Adar and died on the seventh of Adar. How then could he say on that day, “I am one hundred and twenty years old today”? As soon as the night of the seventh began, the sixth had ended and he had entered another year. This is especially difficult according to the principle that even one day of a year can sometimes count as a year. It must be that for this purpose years are completed by exact hours. Moshe did not become exactly one hundred and twenty full years old until the corresponding hour on the seventh of Adar at which he had been born. This explains the doubled language of the Sages: “Today my days and my years were completed.” Just as certain calculations of days require a complete twenty-four-hour period and therefore depend on hours, so too his years were completed only at the corresponding hour. The point is not that a predetermined quota of life-years happened to expire, but that both the anniversary day and the full measure of the years reached completion on that day, not on the preceding night. This appears to me, with Hashem’s help, a clear proof that exact hours can be relevant in the calculation of years.
