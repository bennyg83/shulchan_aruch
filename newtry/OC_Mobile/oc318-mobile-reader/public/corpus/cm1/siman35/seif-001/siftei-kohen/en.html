Two hairs after thirteen complete years. This means thirteen full years. One should not say that thirty days in the thirteenth year count as a year and make him an adult. The Sema appears to mean that one born in the middle of the night of Rosh Hashanah becomes thirteen at the beginning of Rosh Hashanah of his fourteenth year. The Lechem Chamudot questions whether the years must be counted hour for hour and seeks proof from Tosafot in Rosh Hashanah and from the Rambam. That proof is inconclusive. When Tosafot speak of a full period, they mean complete days, not necessarily complete hours. This is evident from the end of the chapter Yotzei Dofen, where many Torah periods are described as complete periods, yet Tosafot distinguish sacred matters and walled houses, which require complete hours, from the other cases. Thus Tosafot in Rosh Hashanah may likewise refer only to days. The Bach in Orach Chaim, end of siman 53, also rules that complete hours are unnecessary, and one answer in Tosafot in Niddah supports this. Another answer there does require thirteen years measured to the hour of birth. See what I wrote in Orach Chaim 53:1.
