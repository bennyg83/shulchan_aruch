And collect. The Maggid Mishneh and Ran write that Ri Migash asked why the claimant should swear and collect. Is this not like one who says, 'You owe me a maneh,' and the defendant answers, 'I do not know,' in which case the defendant is exempt? He answers that there is no established monetary basis there, whereas here the defendant admits that he owes property but does not know what it is, so the claimant swears and collects. Even so, it is difficult from siman 88, seif 17, where the Mechaber rules that if one claimed wheat and the defendant replied that he did not know whether it was wheat or barley, the defendant swears that he does not know and is exempt from the wheat. Why, then, here does the owner of the deposit swear and collect? See what I wrote there, seif katan 26. It must be that here, although by strict law the custodian would swear and be exempt, the Geonim enacted the rule of a robbed claimant for a deposit so that everyone would be careful in safeguarding deposits and not act negligently. The Rosh writes that one should not depart from their ruling, and the Tur cites him here and in siman 298. It must also be that admitting possession of the sack itself is not considered an admission of part of the claim. The Raavad and Rosh disagree and consider it a partial admission. See above, siman 72, seif 12. Shach.
And some say. The Shach writes that this alternative opinion necessarily refers back to the first clause, as the Sema explains here, and not as the Sema's second explanation in siman 298. Baal HaTerumot cites the Raavad: If one deposited a gold ring with another without weighing it, and the custodian, who was negligent, does not know its weight, while the depositor says it weighed two gold dinars and the custodian says, 'I do not know whether it weighed one dinar, and I also do not know whether it was copper inside with gold only on the surface; you yourself know that I have no knowledge of it,' the depositor swears and collects. It appears that all authorities agree in this case because the claimant himself concedes that the defendant does not know, as explained above, siman 72, seif 12. Shach.
