The owner of the deposit shall swear. See Shach seif katan 15. He explains that the Gemara can properly ask whether the Mishnah refutes Rabbah bar Natan, since the injured party collects according to the damager's statement even though the damager could say that his earlier admission was made in jest. Ketzot HaChoshen objects that Raavad's fundamental question from HaManiach concerns Rambam's rule that where a defendant says, "I do not know whether I owe wheat or barley," and there is derara demamona, the plaintiff swears and collects the wheat. If so, in HaManiach, when the owner of the damaging ox is uncertain, the injured party should swear and collect according to his own claim. The Shach's jesting answer does not seem to address that. There is no difficulty. The Maggid Mishneh already answers the objection from the Mishnah of tam and muad itself by saying that damages are different, as explained in Rambam. The remaining difficulty is only that the Gemara compares the Mishnah to wheat and barley, implying that ordinary wheat and barley follows the same rule. To this the Shach answers that where the defendant retracts his admission to the barley and says he was merely jesting, the case is no longer "I do not know whether it was wheat or barley." It becomes an ordinary case of "I do not know whether I ever became obligated," with no derara demamona. The plaintiff therefore does not swear and collect the wheat. But where the defendant remains by his admission and says only, "I do not know whether it was wheat or barley," he certainly acknowledges one of the two obligations, there is derara demamona, and Rambam's rule applies: the plaintiff swears and collects the wheat.
