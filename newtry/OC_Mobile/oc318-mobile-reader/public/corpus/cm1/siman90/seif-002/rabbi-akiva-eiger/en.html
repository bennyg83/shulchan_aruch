[Shulchan Aruch] Since he contradicts the witnesses, he is exempt. Note: The Beit Yosef writes that the reason is a migo, since he could have said, “I entered but took nothing.” The Tur’s view is that this is a migo against witnesses, while the Rambam’s reason is that this is not truly a contradiction of witnesses because a person does not necessarily focus upon such a detail, and it is therefore a migo not opposed by witnesses. I am astonished why any migo is needed. Once we say he is not established as a denier because the matter is not something a person necessarily places upon his mind, then since his statement “I did not enter” also includes the assertion “I did not take,” he should be believed directly regarding the latter point, just as one who admits that he entered is believed to say he did not take. The fact that he lied about not entering should not matter. We treat him as having entered, but he should still be believed that he took nothing. For this reason, the Tur’s characterization of the case as a migo against witnesses, which is also cited in the gloss, is likewise astonishing. It is obvious according to all opinions that he is not believed to say he did not enter. Their dispute is only whether he is believed that he took nothing. This requires great study.
