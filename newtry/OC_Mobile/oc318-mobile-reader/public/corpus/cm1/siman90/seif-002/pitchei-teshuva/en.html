If it is clear to the witnesses, etc. See Responsa Shevut Yaakov, part 1, siman 113, who cites Divrei Neva as explaining the words of the Ramah and the Shulchan Aruch here as referring to a case where the other person is upright and God fearing, and therefore the injured party collects without an oath. Divrei Neva expresses surprise at the Ramah and the Shulchan Aruch, since this appears contrary to the sugyot in Sanhedrin 36b and 80a and to the authorities in siman 408, which establish that we do not follow such an assessment. He leaves the matter with a question. Shevut Yaakov himself writes that there is no difficulty. It is possible that the Ramah follows the Rambam in chapter 5 of Hilchot Chovel U'Mazik, who explains that an injured party swears and collects because the Sages imposed a penalty. If so, one can say that this too is a special innovation enacted by the Sages as part of the penalty. Even if we say that he follows the Ra'avad, who disputes the Rambam there and maintains that the reason is not a penalty but that people do not ordinarily injure themselves, there is still no difficulty. The meaning of "it is clear to the witnesses" may be that the other person is physically weak and incapable of inflicting such an injury, or that his hands were bound, or the like. Since there is such a compelling circumstantial indication, it is certainly effective. See there.
