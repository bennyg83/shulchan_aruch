Even, etc., and some say that since, etc. This follows the discussion in the third chapter of Bava Batra. Rambam applies the principle that every statement of this kind is interpreted comprehensively, as explained in Shevuot 41b. Rambam follows his own interpretation of the first Tanna's ruling in the mishnah, namely that the defendant says either, 'I did not enter,' or, 'I entered but did not take anything,' and the Gemara challenges both possibilities by asking that perhaps he did not take a pledge. The alternative opinion follows Rif's explanation in She'arim, first gate, which reads the mishnah according to its plain meaning: the defendant says, 'I took nothing.' Rambam declined to explain it that way, because if so, why would Rabbi Yehudah disagree with the first Tanna? Even in the first Tanna's case, the witnesses contradict part of the defendant's statement, and testimony should not be weaker than his own admission. Rather, the mishnah teaches two distinct clauses, as explained above. It cannot state only one of them, for it would then have had to say explicitly, 'I did not enter,' or, 'I took only my own property.' Instead, it uses wording broad enough to include both claims.
