Anything. The Sema writes that the text should read "utensils" instead of "anything." See there. If one told another, "Take the key to the shop, but do not open it except together with so-and-so," and the other opened it alone, and the shop owner now claims that a silk garment is missing, then if the owner's claim is definite, the person who opened the shop takes a shevuat heset and is exempt. If the claim is uncertain, a general cherem is proclaimed. Responsa Maharashdam, Choshen Mishpat 308. See also Tamim De'im, simanim 225 and 226. Knesset HaGedolah.
Exempt. This appears to be the primary ruling because of the principle that a person does not carefully remember a matter that he was not required to keep in mind. Thus, if he is contradicted on such a matter, it is not treated as a formal contradiction. This is comparable to one who says, "I lent you a maneh beside a certain pillar," and the defendant responds, "I never passed that place," as stated in siman 79, seif 11. The Bach writes likewise. See simanim 32 and 81, Responsa Maharashdam 174, and Responsa Rashach, part 2, siman 29. Shach.
Who entered. The Gemara explains that a person sometimes makes an exaggerated threat and does not carry it out. Tosafot, citing the Rokeach, write that this does not apply to one who has already established a pattern of such conduct. Taz.
