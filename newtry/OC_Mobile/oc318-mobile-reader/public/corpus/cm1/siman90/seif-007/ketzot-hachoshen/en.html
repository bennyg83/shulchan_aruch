That he is reasonably capable of possessing. Rosh distinguishes between retaining money already in a person's hands and extracting money. In the case of the tenant or sharecropper whose estate contained money beyond what he appeared able to accumulate, the court can leave it in possession of those who already hold it even without proof of ordinary means. Extraction is different. The Shach suggests that in the robbed person's enactment the reason is simply that the entire right to collect is rabbinic, so the Sages limited their enactment to amounts the victim was reasonably capable of possessing. That cannot be the full explanation. In siman 75, the rule that one obligated to swear but unable to swear pays applies as actual law, not merely rabbinic enactment, yet the claimant still cannot recover an implausibly large amount beyond what he could have owned. Thus the decisive distinction is indeed between maintaining possession and extracting money, not merely between Torah and rabbinic liability.
