(Ibid.) “Since he has witnesses.” Sema, seif katan 33, explains that there are no witnesses that the object belonged to the claimant; rather, it is presumed to be his. This is difficult, because the following seif expressly states that there are witnesses that it belonged to him, and there too Sema emends the text to mean that the witnesses merely saw him remove articles. The language cannot support this. In the Remezim it is stated explicitly: “Even if he removed them openly, where there are witnesses that they belonged to the claimant and the witnesses now see them,” etc.; “but if there are no witnesses that they belonged to him, he has no claim,” etc. These are clearly two separate requirements. Moreover, in Bava Batra 46b, the Rashbam’s interpretation shows that every case of “seeing” means that the witnesses had first recognized the object. Sema’s argument that no witnesses were mentioned earlier is no difficulty. One who examines chapter 9 of the Rambam’s Laws of Pleading will see that immediately before the words “since he has witnesses,” the Rambam writes: “Articles ordinarily lent or rented remain in the possession of their owners, as we have explained.” He thereby refers to the preceding chapter concerning possession of movables, where the law of articles ordinarily lent or rented requires witnesses who know that they belonged to the claimant. The phrase “as we have explained” points precisely to that requirement. Thus the Rambam properly says, “since he has witnesses that these articles are known to belong to him,” using the same language as in the preceding chapter. It follows that the witnesses must testify that these very objects were originally his. Although siman 364 states that whatever is found in a person’s house is presumed to be his, that rule applies only where it is taken in his absence, as explained there. When the taking occurs in his presence and the other party claims purchase, no such presumption applies. This appears clearly correct to me.
