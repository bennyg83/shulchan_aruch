All this does not apply, etc. Bava Batra 36a states that wandering livestock do not create a presumption of ownership, and discusses the incident involving the goats. The Gemara asks from Reish Lakish's rule concerning wandering livestock. Were one who seized them before witnesses and claimed that he purchased them believed, then where the seizure itself is certain he would be believed, all the more so where it is doubtful. Likewise, Bava Batra 33b states, 'If he claimed and said, etc., did Rabbi Yehudah not say that one who, etc.?' The Gemara requires that additional reason. See Tosafot there, beginning, 'And if,' and Hagahot Maimoniyot, Laws of Claimant and Defendant, chapter 9, section 5. Ritzba writes that there is no difficulty at all, etc. His position is supported by the case of Rabbi Abba's silver ingot, for if witnesses had testified that he seized it, etc. See there. This is the basis of the ruling here. See also citation 5.
