[Sema, letter 14] And the homeowner’s seizure is ineffective in this case. Note: In a case of doubt concerning the scope of an enactment, seizure is ineffective. In my humble opinion, this is because the seizure itself rests only on an uncertain invocation of the enactment, since the homeowner does not know what happened and claims merely that perhaps the enactment gives credibility to his hired worker. But where he seizes on the basis of a definite claim, and the only doubt is whether the enactment makes him credible, his seizure is effective. This explains siman 388, seif 6. There the seizure is not based upon the enactment. Rather, he seizes on the basis of a definite claim that the informer caused him a specified loss, and the only doubt is whether the Sages enacted for a victim of informing the same credibility as for one who was robbed. The same applies to the law in siman 388, seif 1. This resolves the difficulties of the Derishah and Mishneh LaMelech from the cited case in siman 388. I later found that Beit Meir, siman 112, seif 5, writes likewise.
