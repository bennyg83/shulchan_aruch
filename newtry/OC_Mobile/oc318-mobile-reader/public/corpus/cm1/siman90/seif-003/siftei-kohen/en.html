There was one witness, etc., that he entered to seize a pledge, etc. This seif follows Rambam, Hilchot Gezelah, chapter 4, law 4. Rambam's text says only that one witness testified that he entered and took utensils, without expressly saying that he entered to seize a pledge. Raavad objects that even with two witnesses there are circumstances where the entrant may claim purchase, as below, seif 11, and therefore he should certainly be believed to say, “I did not rob; I took my own property,” or, “I seized for my debt,” through a migo of purchase. Raavad did not object to Rambam's preceding law because there Rambam expressly said that he entered to seize a pledge. The Maggid Mishneh explains that here too Rambam relies upon the earlier statement. Once a person reveals that he entered to seize a pledge, he may no longer claim purchase. The Mechaber therefore writes expressly here that one witness testified that he entered to seize a pledge.
He must swear that he did not rob. The Sema correctly explains that this case is not identical to the “piece of metal” case of Rabbi Abba. See Agudat Ezov, page 83b. The Sema's earlier remarks, seif katan 7, answer the objection.
