And he or I did not steal anything: P., but the tools I had made are that I had been encoded in his hand, and as a result of it:
Or I say at my heart. and near:
He swears that he did not rob him. The Tur concludes with the reason that one does not become established as a robber through a single witness. The Rambam and Semag require an oath while holding a sacred object, whereas the Tur requires only an oath of hesset. The reason for the Tur is that the entrant claims, "I took what was mine," or "You gave and transferred the utensils to me in satisfaction of my debt," so this is not the ordinary case of one who swears and collects while holding a sacred object. Only one who claims against a pledge up to its value, while admitting that the object belongs to the other party, is required to swear while holding a sacred object, as explained in siman 72. In the Derishah I explained the dispute. This case is also unlike the case of the silver ingot of Rabbi Abba discussed in siman 75:13 and below in seif 15, where one is obligated to swear against a witness but cannot swear and therefore must pay. There, if two witnesses testified that he seized the object from the other person's hand, he would have to pay, and wherever two witnesses create monetary liability, one witness creates an oath. If the defendant cannot swear, he pays. Here, even if two witnesses saw him carry utensils out, the owner still could not recover merely on their testimony without first taking his own oath. The Tur explicitly makes this distinction in the name of the Rosh in siman 75:17. See the Derishah.
