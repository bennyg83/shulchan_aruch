He swears that he did not rob. See Sema seif katan 13, through his statement that the owner does not collect unless he first swears. This cannot refer to the additional utensils that the defendant says he never took, because regarding those all agree that there is no d'oraisa oath upon the defendant: the single witness does not testify about them and the defendant has not been established as a robber by two witnesses. The dispute concerns the very objects that the single witness says he took, while the defendant says, "I took them for my debt." Nor can the case be one where the witness merely says he took unspecified utensils without identifying them. Even with two witnesses the law requires that the items be seen, as Shach seif katan 3 writes, so the witness must have seen the particular objects whose taking he describes. If the defendant simply denied taking those objects, he would ordinarily have to take the d'oraisa oath required to contradict one witness. Yet because if there had been two witnesses to the taking and he made his present claim, he would still have to swear according to the Tur cited by Shach in seif katan 7, this is not the classic case of Rabbi Abba's piece of metal and he is not one who must swear but cannot and therefore pays, as Shach explains at the end of siman 75 seif katan 47. The reason is that the case is one where the owner says the utensils were stolen. Seif 11 establishes that even with all the surrounding suspicious circumstances, an owner is not ordinarily believed to call the possessor a thief. Here, however, because the possessor entered specifically to seize collateral, that conduct is treated like an established basis for the theft claim, so the owner can be believed upon an oath, as Shach and Sema explain. With only one witness, even if the defendant admits an unauthorized taking, he is not legally established as a thief. The owner therefore cannot rely on the theft claim to shift a d'oraisa oath and needs at most a shevuat heset. This appears to be the root of their dispute. However, the Shach himself writes earlier in seif katan 3 that according to the Mechaber the owner need not swear at all, which makes the Shach's later approval of Sema's explanation difficult.
