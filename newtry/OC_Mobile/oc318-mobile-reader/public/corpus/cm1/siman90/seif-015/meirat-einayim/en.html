Likewise if he snatched, etc., he must return it. Meaning without an oath. This differs from the case of the robbed householder above.
There the witnesses do not testify with certainty that he seized the property as collateral; it remains possible that he took his own property. Here, however, they saw him snatch the object directly from the other's hand, and it therefore has the presumption of stolen property.
The case also requires that witnesses presently see it in his possession, for otherwise he could be believed through a migo that he returned it, according to the view that one who stole before witnesses need not return before witnesses.If he does not deny, etc., he must, etc. Where he admits the taking but disputes the legal consequence, the ordinary rules governing one who holds another's property apply. His admission removes the factual uncertainty, and the remaining issue is whether he has a valid claim of right or must return it.
