The Sema writes that in the Derishah he proved that the Tur and Mechaber follow the opinion that even one who robbed in the presence of witnesses need not return in the presence of witnesses. Therefore, even if one entered to seize security without permission and witnesses saw him remove concealed utensils, thereby establishing him as a robber, if there are no witnesses who presently see the items he is nevertheless believed to say, 'I returned them,' or, 'I bought them,' through a migo that he returned them. The Shach responds that this proof is not compelling, because even according to the owner the property came into his hands by borrowing, so he is not equivalent to an ordinary robber. The Maggid Mishneh writes that the Rambam holds that a robber must return before witnesses, yet in the laws of claims he understands the Rambam here to require witnesses who presently see the item, for otherwise the possessor is believed through a migo that he returned it. Nevertheless, as a matter of law we rule that a robber need not return before witnesses. See the end of siman 361; this is the conclusion of most authorities, and the Sema likewise rules so in siman 107, seif 5. Shach.
