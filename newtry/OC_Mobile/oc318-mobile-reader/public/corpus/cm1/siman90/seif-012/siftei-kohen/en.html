The Sema writes: “In the Derishah I proved that the Tur and Shulchan Aruch follow the view that one who robbed in the presence of witnesses need not return the property in the presence of witnesses. Therefore, even if he entered to seize collateral without authorization and was seen removing concealed utensils, so that he became established as a robber, nevertheless, where there are no witnesses who later saw the utensils in his possession, he is believed to say, ‘I returned them to you.’ The same is true that he is believed to say, ‘I purchased them,’ through the migo that he could have said, ‘I returned them to you.’” In my humble opinion, this proof is inconclusive. Since even according to the homeowner the objects originally came into his possession by way of a loan, he is not regarded as a robber. Proof of this is that the Maggid Mishneh in chapter 4 of Hilchot Gezelah writes that the Rambam holds that a robber must return the property before witnesses, whereas in chapter 9 of Hilchot To’en he explains the Rambam here as requiring that the utensils have been seen in his possession, for otherwise he is believed through the migo that he returned them. Necessarily, the distinction is as I have written. Nevertheless, as a matter of law we rule that a robber need not return before witnesses, as I wrote at the end of siman 361. A robber is therefore believed through the migo that he returned the property. The Ba’al HaTerumot likewise writes in gate 49 that even a confirmed robber, and likewise one who entered to seize collateral without authorization, is believed in whatever claim he makes through the migo that he returned it, when there are no witnesses who subsequently saw the property. This is the consensus of most authorities. The Sema likewise writes in siman 107, seif katan 16, that this is the accepted ruling.
