The Shach explains that this wording teaches that if the robber dies, the penalty was not imposed upon his son after him, as stated in siman 385 regarding one who ritually defiles another's produce, mixes terumah into it, or pours libation wine. Although regarding a hired worker the employer's son remains liable after his father, as stated in the gloss to siman 89, seif 1, that case is not a penalty. Utensils. If the witnesses do not know that he removed utensils, then even if the homeowner is a person of means, he does not swear and collect, because perhaps the intruder carried only stones or pebbles beneath his garments. It is then as though the witnesses did not see him remove anything, whose law is explained immediately below.
Know. This implies that whatever the witnesses know with certainty he must return without an oath, and this also appears explicitly from the Rif. Although he claims, "I took my own property," and the witnesses do not contradict that assertion, that claim would help him only through a migo that he could have claimed, "I took nothing." The witnesses, however, testify that he did take property. Once witnesses have established him as a robber regarding part of the property, even though he has a migo regarding the remainder that he took nothing or returned it, the Sages enacted that the victim swears regarding the remainder and collects. Shach.
Ever. This applies even if he does not contradict the witnesses, but says: "It is true that I entered empty handed intending to take collateral, but after I entered I changed my mind and took nothing belonging to him. The utensils the witnesses saw me remove were my own property that had been deposited with this homeowner." The Sema suggests, however, that the Mechaber may simply have followed the wording of the Rambam and Rif, who understand the case even where he contradicts the witnesses. The Shach writes that it appears the victim swears and collects only where the witnesses now saw the property in the intruder's possession. Otherwise he is believed through a migo that he returned it. He is similarly believed to say, "I took it for my debt," or, "It is mine," through the migo that he returned it. A claim that he purchased it is believed even where there are witnesses who saw it, in the circumstances discussed below in seif 11, except that here a purchase claim is not applicable. The Taz writes similarly.
And collects. See Tur, seif 20, who writes that the homeowner swears and collects only where the homeowner is not accustomed to selling his utensils, the person leaving the house is not a modest person, he carried the utensils concealed, and it is not his ordinary manner to do so. If any one of these conditions is absent, the homeowner does not swear and collect; rather, the person leaving is believed. Here, however, the Tur and Shulchan Aruch state without qualification that the victim swears and collects. The Sema explains that there the person claims, "I purchased them," whereas here he claims, "They are mine," contrary to the presumption that whatever a person has in his house belongs to him. Therefore the homeowner is believed even without the other circumstances. The Shach objects that this does not explain why the intruder should not be believed here to say, "I took them for my debt," which is not contrary to that presumption. They impose a general cherem. The Sema explains that the intruder is regarded as disqualified from taking an oath. Since by strict law he would have been exempt without an oath, his inability to swear does not create liability. Rather, he remains exempt and a general cherem is proclaimed, as the Tur and Shulchan Aruch state in siman 82 and below in siman 92. See also seif 5 and siman 359, seif 6. Knesset HaGedolah cites Responsa Radbaz, siman 179: If the victim died before he could bring the robber to beit din, and his heirs come to claim their father's stolen property, saying that their father instructed them that a certain amount was stolen, while witnesses testify to the robbery but do not know the amount, then although the heirs of a deceased hired worker were granted no special enactment, the heirs of a robbery victim swear and collect. If the heir does not make a definite claim, a general cherem is proclaimed.
