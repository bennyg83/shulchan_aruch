(Seif 12) The law concerning articles ordinarily lent or rented is stated specifically in those terms because, according to what I have written, the witnesses mentioned here actually recognize the articles as having belonged to the claimant. With articles not ordinarily lent or rented, where the suspicious circumstances described above are present, there is no need for witnesses to recognize them as his, because those circumstances themselves show that the possessor did not purchase them. Sema wrote that the same applies to articles not ordinarily lent or rented, but this does not contradict my explanation, for he follows his own view that the witnesses did not actually recognize the objects, as he wrote earlier. He therefore necessarily treated the two situations alike. “But if they did not see.” It was unnecessary to add, “or if they did not testify that the objects were his,” because that is included in the requirement of seeing. Unless the witnesses recognized them beforehand, they cannot be said to have now seen those objects in his possession, since they do not know their identity.
