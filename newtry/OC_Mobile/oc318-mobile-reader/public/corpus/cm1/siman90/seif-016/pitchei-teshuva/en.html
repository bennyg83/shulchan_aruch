If it is clear to the witnesses. Teshuvot Shevut Yaakov, part I, siman 113, cites an interpretation that this means the other person is known to be upright and God-fearing, and therefore the injured party collects without an oath. He questions this because ordinary halachah does not follow such conjecture. He answers that the Ramah may follow the Rambam's view that the injured party's oath and recovery are a rabbinic penalty, in which case the Sages could have extended their enactment to this unusual circumstance. Even according to the Raavad, who explains the rule because people ordinarily do not injure themselves, the case may be one where the other person was physically incapable of causing such an injury, such as being too weak or having his hands bound. A compelling factual presumption of that kind is sufficient.
