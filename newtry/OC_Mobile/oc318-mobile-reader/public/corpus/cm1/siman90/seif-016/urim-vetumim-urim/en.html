The injured person, etc. See above, seif 1, regarding which laws of personal injury are adjudicated nowadays. See there at length.
He swears and collects. See Tumim, where I remained uncertain whether the tortfeasor is believed to say, 'I paid for the injury,' in which case he would be believed through a migo that he could have denied injuring him. One would then have to say that the injured party warned him, 'Do not pay me except in the presence of witnesses.' See there. I also remained uncertain whether the injured person may say, 'I do not wish to rely upon the rabbinic enactment; you swear hesset,' or, where the tortfeasor admits causing one injury and the injured party claims two, require a Torah oath. See there. I also concluded that even where the tortfeasor has one witness who supports his claim that he caused only one injury, the injured party nevertheless swears and collects.
And collects. If there are no witnesses that he injured him, but he admits the injury and says that the injured party struck him first, he is believed through a migo, as stated by Mahari in his writings.
And if there are, etc. The Tur explains that the rule that the injured party swears and collects applies where witnesses testify that there was a quarrel between them. If there was no quarrel, on what basis should we say that this person injured him? He does not swear and collect unless there is some proof of the type stated there, in which case he always collects even without an oath. Beit Yosef writes that this is also implied by Rambam, who writes that there was a dispute between them. I am surprised that this condition was not brought in Shulchan Aruch, although Sema cites it.
There are, etc. If the injured person died, his heirs do not collect through this oath, even if they were present at the injury and can swear that he injured him in this manner. The Sages did not institute this enactment for his son, as appears from Responsa Radbaz. With respect to a robbed person, however, if his son was present, whether the son has the status of a custodian for the owner requires study. See Tumim.
