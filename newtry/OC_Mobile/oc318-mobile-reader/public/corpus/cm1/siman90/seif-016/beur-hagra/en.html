And even if there are, etc. See Bava Metzia 26b, where one version states that Rava said, etc. Rambam writes in chapter 14 of the Laws of Robbery and Lost Property that this applies even though each person's share is worth less than a perutah. This implies that even two perutot are unnecessary. Maggid Mishneh writes there that Rambam may reject Rav Natan's rule, while Rif states explicitly that Rava disputes it and applies the rule even where each share is worth less than a perutah. Rif adds that one of them may have waived his share. That explanation would seem to require two perutot. It must therefore be understood in accordance with Tosafot's question there, beginning, 'No': why not be concerned that, etc.? This shows that in such a case the claim is treated as certain. Even according to Tosafot and Shulchan Aruch below, siman 262, seif 2, who disagree with Rambam, the reason is as Tosafot explain there: certainly, etc.
