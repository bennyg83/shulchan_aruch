And the employer takes a heses oath... and they return... The wording should be reversed. See Shach.
Items made for... always. This follows his view that their initial manufacture is for lending and renting, and he reads the expression "made for" literally. See siman 72 seif 19.
And some say that even... From the Gemara's phrase, "And in all of these cases we said only..." which appears to refer to all the preceding categories, including a claim that the items were stolen. Otherwise that qualification should have been stated earlier. See Beer HaGolah.
And some say that not... Once the owner claims that the items were stolen, the presumption that they are items normally lent or rented is weakened. One cannot rely on a migo to restore that presumption, because the general presumption that people are not robbers operates like evidence. This is also reflected in the gloss to siman 357. However, that gloss applies only where no theft was publicly reported, as Tosafos and the Rosh explain in Shevuos. They write that books are ordinarily lent or rented and discuss the Mishnah of one who recognizes his articles. So too the Tur and Shulchan Aruch, siman 133 seif 6, distinguish the case where a theft was reported. The objection to Rambam in siman 357 therefore concerns only a case without such a report.
And if it was established... In that case the presumption that people do not ordinarily steal does not apply, and he is believed through his migo.
