One who entered the house of the householder, etc.
In the Derishah and Perishah I explained the sugya and its variant readings.
The Mechaber follows the version of Rif, Rambam, and Rosh.And the witnesses see him.
Meaning that witnesses saw him enter empty-handed and also saw him leave with utensils concealed under his garments.
This entrance alone did not establish him as a robber, because the case is where they did not hear him announce that he was entering to seize collateral without permission.
The combination of circumstances, however, creates the special evidentiary rule described in the Gemara.The claimant's credibility depends on several adverse indications concerning the person who entered: whether he entered in a manner associated with unlawful seizure, whether the utensils were concealed, whether such utensils are ordinarily lent or rented, whether witnesses currently see them in his possession, and what the possessor claims about them.
The Mechaber's formulation follows the precise structure of the Rif and Rambam.
Where the necessary adverse indications are present, the householder may be believed regarding ownership and value with the oath specified by the law.
Where one of those indications is absent, the ordinary rules of possession and migo may instead govern.
