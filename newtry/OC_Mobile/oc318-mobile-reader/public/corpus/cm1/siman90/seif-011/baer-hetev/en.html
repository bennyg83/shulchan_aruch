Borrowed. This means items ordinarily lent or rented, concerning which the owner is believed. According to the Sema, however, the same adverse circumstances listed above must be present here too, namely that the person leaving the premises is established as a thief and the owner claims that the items were stolen. At the beginning of this siman the Tur and Mechaber state without qualification that the owner is believed because there the entrant claims, 'I took my own property,' so they did not distinguish and simply wrote that the owner swears and collects. Here the entrant claims, 'I bought them,' and therefore the owner is not believed to say that they were stolen, even for items ordinarily lent or rented, unless all those adverse circumstances are present. The Shach disagrees, as stated in seif katan 6. The Rambam writes that where the entrant is established as a thief and the owner claims theft, the owner must swear while holding a sacred object even when all the adverse circumstances exist. The Mechaber began with the Rambam's language that when the owner claims the items were borrowed he is believed with a hesset oath, and concludes that if the entrant is established as a thief his law is like one who claims they were borrowed. According to the Rambam the two are not entirely equal, because a borrowing claim requires only hesset while a theft claim requires the more stringent oath. Perhaps the Mechaber meant only that the owner is believed in both cases, not that the oaths are identical. Sema. The Shach argues that since the Rif, Rosh, Tur, Mechaber, and other authorities state without qualification that one established as a thief has the same law as one who claims the items were borrowed, they imply complete equivalence and only hesset is required. He further argues that this must be the Rambam's view as well. See there.
