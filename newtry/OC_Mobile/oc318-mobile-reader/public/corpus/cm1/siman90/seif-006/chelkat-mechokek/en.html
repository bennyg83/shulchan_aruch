A loan in the hands of others when he married her. The Bach already writes that the words "when he married her" are an error; they do not appear in the Rosh's rulings. There is no difference whether she already had a debt owed by others when he married her or whether she lent money to others after the marriage. In either case it is considered prospective property with respect to the husband if she did not collect before her death. This applies specifically to a loan. If she had deposits in the hands of others, wherever the property is located it remains in the owner's domain and is therefore not prospective. So writes the Bach.
