[Shulchan Aruch] We do not administer an oath to the robber because. Note: The Rambam states this regarding the robber’s hired worker or gatherer. This explains why the reason that the robber is disqualified is needed. Since the claimant makes a definite claim based on another person’s statement, it is a definite claim sufficient to impose hesset, as explained at the end of siman 75. The reason we do not apply the rule that one who is obligated to swear and cannot swear must pay may be that the hired worker is his relative, or because where the opposing party cannot swear we do not apply that rule, as with a custodian who is disqualified from swearing below, siman 92, seif 8. Nevertheless, hesset would ordinarily arise from a definite claim based on another person, even a relative, and therefore the reason that the robber is disqualified is necessary. It further appears that even if he claims, "I took property from others in satisfaction of my debt," we do not apply the rule that one who is obligated to swear and cannot swear must pay. Had he said, "I did not take," he would not have had to swear, because he is disqualified, and since the opposing party cannot swear he would have been exempt. He is therefore believed to say, "I took it in satisfaction of my debt," by virtue of a migo that he could say, "I did not take." Consider this carefully.
