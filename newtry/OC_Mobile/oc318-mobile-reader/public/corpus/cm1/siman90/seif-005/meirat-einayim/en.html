If the owner was not there, etc. Meaning, even if the owner claims, "So many utensils were stolen from my house on that day, and these witnesses testify that you were in my house and carried out utensils in my absence, so you certainly took them all," he still does not swear and collect. There is no definite claim against the entrant, neither from the owner's personal knowledge nor from the witnesses. Rama concludes that even if the robber admits part, he is not treated as one obligated to take an oath by partial admission who cannot swear because witnesses have rendered him suspect. The rule that a person suspected on account of witnesses must pay rather than swear applies only where the owner makes a definite claim against him, as the Tur and Mechaber explain in siman 92.
