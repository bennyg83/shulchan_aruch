Seif 1. In the gloss: 'And so Maharam ruled regarding a teacher whom the ruler decreed should not teach, that this is a regional calamity.' Note: See Sema and Shach, who ask how the two responsa can be reconciled. It appears to me that the distinction is this. In the responsum where the ruler decreed that the teacher not teach, the intention of the decree was directed only at the students, to prevent them from learning, not at the teacher. In that case the loss falls upon the employer, because his mazal caused it. Maharam Padua, however, discusses an unavoidable circumstance caused by a change in the air, which affects the teacher just as it affects the students. In that case he rules that the employer is exempt. Even if the wording of the responsa does not clearly support this distinction, as a matter of practical halachah it is reasonable to distinguish in this way.
