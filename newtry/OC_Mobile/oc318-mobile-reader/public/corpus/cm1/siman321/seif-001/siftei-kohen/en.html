In every case he deducts from him, etc. The Sema writes at length to refute the Rama, but his arguments are not compelling. In particular, he interprets Maharam’s ruling regarding a teacher to mean that the loss is divided between the parties and uses this to reconcile Maharam Padua. That interpretation is plainly incorrect. He also overlooked Hagahot Ashri in the name of Or Zarua and Maharam Padua’s own responsum, siman 86, which expressly state that the employer pays the teacher his full wages. Nevertheless, there is another basis for distinguishing Maharam’s ruling concerning a teacher from Maharam Padua. In the teacher’s case he did not teach thereafter and could no longer do so; once the period ended, the matter was wholly past. By contrast, where a shop or similar property remained in the tenant’s possession, one may say that he should have withdrawn and, by failing to do so, waived the claim. This reasoning does not apply to a teacher or to a house that burned. Maharam Padua himself, in responsum 86, rules regarding a teacher in accordance with Maharam. See Responsa Rabbi Eliyahu ibn Chaim, siman 38, and Responsa Maharshach, volume II, siman 98.
