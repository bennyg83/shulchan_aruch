One who leases an irrigated field or orchard and it dries up. It contains two seifim.
If one leases or receives a field under a crop-sharing arrangement, and it is an irrigated field or orchard, and the local spring dries up while the main river remains and water can still be brought by bucket, or the orchard tree is cut down, he may not deduct from the rent. If it is a regional calamity, such as the main river drying up, he may deduct. Rama: Under a crop-sharing arrangement he deducts nothing, but they divide whatever remains according to their agreement. The rule permitting a deduction for a regional calamity applies similarly wherever the entire purpose is lost through a regional disaster. If it can be remedied through effort and measures, there is no deduction. Where a deduction is permitted, there is no distinction between past and future rent. Thus, if a ruler decrees that a teacher may not teach, it is a regional calamity and the employer bears the loss. Others hold that for the future the worker may withdraw, as with a rented donkey that dies, and if he does not withdraw he causes his own loss and waives it. The first view appears primary.
