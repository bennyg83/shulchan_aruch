(Seif 1: Rather, it is possible), etc. Tosafot ask why the tenant may not deduct rent even when irrigation is impossible, since the loss is not a calamity affecting the whole region. They answer first that even if the small channels of other fields also dried, making it a regional calamity, he still may not deduct where water can be brought with additional effort. Thus, so long as irrigation remains possible through exertion, the event is not treated as a regional calamity. They answer alternatively that this reasoning is needed only for a sharecropper. A fixed-rent tenant would not deduct absent a regional calamity even where irrigation is impossible. A sharecropper, however, might otherwise refuse to work, and the Gemara teaches that where water can be brought from the main river by buckets, he must continue, though he is not required to bring water from extremely distant rivers. It follows that where irrigation is truly impossible and the sharecropper voluntarily undertakes extraordinary labor to bring water from elsewhere, he is no worse than a fixed-rent tenant: if the event is a regional calamity, his share must be increased proportionately to the additional yield he preserved. This explains the Tur's statement that if one accepted a field for one quarter and the spring dried, the owner's share must be adjusted. The Maggid Mishneh, citing the Ramban and Rashba, writes that the Gemara's question concerning a regional calamity applies specifically to fixed rent, whereas under sharecropping whatever grows is divided according to the agreed proportion. This refers to a case where the sharecropper did not bring water from elsewhere but left the field as it was. There is therefore no dispute. The Rama's statement that sharecropping is different accords with all opinions. The Nimukei Yosef misunderstood the Maggid Mishneh as contradicting the Rambam, but the Rambam merely states the Mishnah's general rule for both rental and sharecropping. The distinction concerns only the deduction inferred when the main river dries, and only where the worker did not undertake extra irrigation. The Darkei Moshe and Sema followed the Nimukei Yosef in treating this as a dispute, but there is none. Nor is there any reason to distinguish this regional calamity from the locust case in siman 322.
