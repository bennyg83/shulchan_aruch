There is no distinction concerning what has already passed. See Sema, seif katan 6, who challenges Rama. Maharam Padua writes that reduction applies specifically where the damage concerns what already occurred, such as locusts consuming what was planted or a spring drying after the cultivator invested in sowing. But where the loss concerns only the future, the tenant can simply retract, like one who rents a donkey that becomes unsuitable. In my view Rama is correct. The Mishnah states generally that one who leases an irrigated field and the spring dries reduces the rent when the event is a makkat medinah. That includes a multi-year lease where the spring dries before sowing the second year, or even immediately after the kinyan before any sowing. The damage is then entirely future, yet the rent is reduced. This proves that when the loss results from a makkat medinah, it falls on both parties. The transaction remains in force and the rent is adjusted. Only a private accident, such as a particular rented donkey becoming unfit, is governed by ordinary cancellation rules. The difference from locusts that consume produce in a private event is straightforward. There the accident occurred only to fruits already acquired by the tenant, not to the body of the landlord's property. The tenant's own fortune is implicated. When the spring of the field dries, by contrast, the accident affects the land itself. Even where the lease was only of a generic field and did not expressly include the spring, a regional disaster prevents attributing the loss solely to the tenant. Sema's comparison to a hired teacher is also difficult. If the teacher arrangement simply ended through a private accident, the teacher should bear the loss no less than where a child dies. Yet in a makkat medinah the loss is divided or adjusted, which itself proves that the category applies even to future performance. The analogy to a ship and wine is also not controlling, because Tosafot explain that the ship case differs where one side itself caused the loss. Thus Rama's distinction between an individual accident and a makkat medinah applies both to past investment and to future performance. The detailed application to a teacher will be explained in siman 334.
