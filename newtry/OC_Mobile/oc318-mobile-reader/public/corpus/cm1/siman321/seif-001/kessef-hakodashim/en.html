A calamity affecting the province. The precise definition of a regional calamity is not entirely clear. It is uncertain whether it suffices that some neighboring owners share the loss, or whether all persons connected with the relevant area must be included. If the neighbors suffered only a slight loss while this tenant suffered greatly, perhaps it is not considered a calamity affecting the province. Siman 322 speaks of a majority of the townspeople. Yet in the case of a river ceasing to flow, perhaps the relevant majority is those dependent upon that river rather than the city's population as a whole. So too in other events one may need to identify the group connected to the specific cause. Alternatively, the test may be the majority of the surrounding area as a single economic unit. The discussion of four fields surrounding a property may provide some indication. Likewise, the Sema states that even where neighbors suffer less severely, their losses can combine with the renter's greater loss to create the character of a regional calamity. It remains doubtful whether a wholly trivial loss suffices, or whether a meaningful degree of loss is required. Regarding rental or sharecropping, complete or near-complete destruction may be necessary for a reduction in rent, as implied in siman 321. If only half or less of the enterprise was harmed, ordinary additional expense, such as drawing water after a spring dries, may not justify deduction because the damaged element remains secondary to the overall enterprise. Conversely, where the parties expressly identified a particular irrigated field and the loss affects an identified feature, even something quantitatively smaller may become central because of the express designation. Perhaps two of the factors noted by the Sema are sufficient. Years ago, householders here rented stills and equipment from one another for the winter to produce liquor. Grain later became expensive and scarce, so only wealthy people could easily continue production. At the time I did not cancel those rentals or exempt the tenants from payment. I now have some hesitation whether such market conditions might qualify as a regional calamity, at least against one who is in possession. Still, because most householders continued to produce, the concern appears limited.
