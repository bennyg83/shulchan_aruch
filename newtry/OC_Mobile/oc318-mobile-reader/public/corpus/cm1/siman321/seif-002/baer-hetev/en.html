Even if he stands outside and says, "I rent you this irrigated field," the designation is specific. Otherwise he could simply have said, "I rent you this field." Since he deliberately added both "this" and "irrigated field," it follows that he meant the designation precisely and as a condition. Seif 1 concerns a case where he did not call it an irrigated field, but merely said, "I rent you this field." In that case, even if at the time of rental it was in fact irrigated and had a spring in it, he may not have made that feature a condition, and the tenant therefore receives no deduction if the spring dries. This also explains why the Tur and Mechaber conclude here that if the lessor was not standing inside it, and so forth, but do not state the stronger proposition that even if he stood inside and said "I rent you an irrigated field," there would be no deduction unless he also said "this." Rather, saying "this" has the same effect as standing inside it, and standing inside has the same effect as saying "this." The purpose of standing inside is only so that the tenant sees that it is an irrigated field with a spring, and the same is true when he says "this" while the field is visible. Sema.
