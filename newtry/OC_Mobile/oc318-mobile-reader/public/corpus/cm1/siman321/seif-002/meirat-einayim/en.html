He was standing in the field and said, "I rent you this irrigated field, etc." In the Perishah and Derishah I proved that all three elements are not required, namely that he stand inside it, say "this," and call it an irrigated field. Even if he stands outside and says, "I rent you this irrigated field," it is a specific stipulation. Otherwise he could have said simply, "I rent you this field." By adding both "this" and "irrigated field," he shows that those characteristics are material. In the preceding seif the case is where he said only, "I rent you this field," not "this irrigated field." Then, although it happened to be irrigated at the time and contained a spring, we may say he did not make that feature a condition. This also explains the Tur's and Mechaber's conclusion, "Therefore, if he was not standing inside it, etc." They did not state the greater novelty that even if he stood inside and said "I rent you an irrigated field," the tenant gets no reduction because he failed to say "this." Rather, saying "this" while both see the field is equivalent to standing inside it, and standing inside it while pointing it out is equivalent to saying "this." The only value of standing there is that the field's irrigated condition and spring are visibly before them.

In that direction of speech we say that because the landlord himself ordinarily has no need to emphasize the field's irrigated status, his phrase "I lease you an irrigated field" may merely be a descriptive name for the property. But if the tenant says to the landlord, "I lease from you an irrigated field," it is ordinarily a stipulation made for the tenant's benefit, even if he is not standing inside it and does not say "this."
