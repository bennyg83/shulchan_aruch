Seif 1. Whoever gives his property to others, etc. Note: I saw in Maharsha, Chiddushei Aggadot to Sanhedrin, Perek Chelek, regarding the claim brought by the descendants of Yishmael against Israel before Alexander of Macedon, that he cites the question of Rabbi Eliyahu Mizrachi: how could Avraham divert the inheritance from his other sons when the Sages warned that one should not transfer an inheritance even from a bad son to a good son? Maharsha answers that the other sons were sons of concubines and therefore Avraham was not required to give them an inheritance together with Yitzchak. His words imply a practical rule that if a man has a son from a concubine, he may divert that son's inheritance to his daughter from a full wife. This is surprising. Since a son of a concubine is legally his son in every respect and inherits him, from where do we know that the father may divert his inheritance? Perhaps the basis is the verse, 'If a man has two wives... he may not make the son of the beloved the firstborn,' where the word 'wives' excludes concubines, implying that where one is a concubine he may prefer the other son for the firstborn share. Yet even then, perhaps this applies only to the double firstborn portion. From where do we know that he may divert the ordinary inheritance share as well? This requires study.
