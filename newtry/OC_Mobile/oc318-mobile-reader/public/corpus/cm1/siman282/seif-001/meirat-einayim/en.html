Even though the heirs do not conduct themselves properly toward him. The reason given in the Gemara is that one does not know what offspring may ultimately descend from them. The Tur does not have the word "toward him." The Mechaber copied Rambam at the end of chapter 6 of Hilchot Nachalot, who does include it. This implies that the heir does not honor the person leaving the estate properly, even though he otherwise behaves properly in religious matters. The same reasoning applies: the owner should not say, "Since he does not treat me properly, I will give my money to another," because he must consider the offspring who may descend from this heir.
The spirit of the Sages is not pleased with him. Meaning, because of him the spirit of the Sages is not calm or content, for he causes them to become agitated against his conduct. Rashbam explains this. See the Perishah.
And the others acquired. Meaning, despite his improper act, the recipients nevertheless legally acquire.
For there is nothing better than this. Rama writes the same above at the end of siman 252. Mordechai concludes that preserving the inheritance order is preferable even to consecrating the property, because the Torah itself granted the heirs rights in the chapter of inheritance, so they stand in place of the deceased with respect to his estate and inheritance.
