A deed, etc., is valid. Why should it be valid where the custom is to record repayment on the reverse? In such a place the visible erasure is an actual defect, and one cannot simply preserve the deed's presumption of validity. Ramban validated the case only because in his view even an explicit repayment notation on the reverse is ineffective; he understood the Gemara's statement about writing 'on it' as referring to the face of the deed after the signatures. Rabbeinu Yerucham says the same. But the Mechaber rules in siman 65 like Rambam and those who hold that a notation on the reverse is effective. He therefore should not cite the validating opinion here. The correct law is that the deed is invalid under such a custom. Ramban's proof from a receipt found in the creditor's possession does not apply, because that case concerns a separate receipt, not one written on the instrument itself.
