It is valid. The Shach cites Ramban to explain why an erasure or scraping on the back of a document need not invalidate it in a place where people do not ordinarily write receipts there. Ramban's reason is that even if an explicit receipt were written on the back, it would not necessarily defeat the document under his view. The Shach therefore asks against the Mechaber, who later in siman 65 follows Rambam and other authorities that an explicit receipt written on the back can be effective. How can the Mechaber here validate a document with an erasure on the back? It appears that even according to the authorities who give effect to an explicit receipt on the back, a mere erasure or scraping after the witnesses' signatures does not invalidate the document. According to the Sema in siman 65, even an explicit receipt can sometimes be overcome by the creditor's migo that he could have cut off that portion. The Shach disagrees there where the receipt is actually legible, treating the migo as opposed by strong evidence. But where there is only an unexplained erasure, the creditor can say there never was any receipt there, and his migo of cutting it off remains strong. Therefore the Mechaber can validate an erasure after the signatures even while accepting that an actual written receipt on the back can be effective. The disqualifying cases are erasures on the face or between the lines where no such migo exists.
