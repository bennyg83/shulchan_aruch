It is valid. We do not create a defect merely because perhaps a repayment notation had been written there and erased. The existence of the deed itself is certain, while the suspected repayment is only conjecture. Rashba, consistent with his view that an erasure in the standard form of a deed does not itself invalidate it. The same principle applies to an erasure on the back. See Tumim.
And some invalidate. Although Mechaber earlier ruled that an erasure in the standard form does not invalidate, the back of a deed is a place where repayment notations are commonly written. Therefore an erasure there creates a stronger concern of repayment. By contrast, in the body of the standard form the most plausible concern would be some unusual condition, and such conditions are not common enough to create invalidity.
