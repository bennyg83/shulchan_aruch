A document that has scraping, etc. Shach, seif katan 15, argues at length that Ramban's responsum validates scraping outside the essential body of the document only because Ramban holds that a receipt written on the back of a document is ineffective, since we can say it was written jokingly. According to our accepted law, however, such a receipt can be valid. If so, an erased or scraped area on the back creates concern that a notation of payment had been there and was removed. Shach's substantive concern is real. But Rashba, responsum 1035, gives a different and shorter rationale. Any erasure or scraping that is not in the essential body of the document does not, by itself, impair the document's existing presumption of validity. Rashba explicitly addresses the statement at the end of Perek HaShoel: "If he repaid, he should have written it on the document." He explains that this statement does not establish that every unexplained scraping on the back must be treated as a removed receipt. The court does not create a defect merely from the possibility that something once stood there. Thus Rashba clearly accepts that a receipt written on the document can matter if it is present and unaltered, yet he still validates an unexplained scraping outside the essential text. He is following his general position from the preceding seif: we do not manufacture a presumption of fraud or erased conditions where the defect is outside the operative body of the document. Accordingly, Shach's objection does not refute Rashba's ruling.
