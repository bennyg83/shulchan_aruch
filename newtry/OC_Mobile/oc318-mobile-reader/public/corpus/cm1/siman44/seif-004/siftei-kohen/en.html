'Firm and established,' etc. I am astonished that the Mechaber rules simply that this phrase permits reliance on the final line, based on Rashbatz, without noting that almost all authorities disagree. Ramban, Hagahot Ashri in the name of Or Zarua, Tosafot, Mordechai, Ri, the Rosh, Tur, Rambam, Semag, and others indicate that even where 'firm and established' is written, the final line is not relied upon unless the prevailing custom makes that phrase indispensable to every valid deed. Tosafot and Mordechai explain that under our custom, because omission of the phrase invalidates the deed, matter written before it in the final line can be used; that reasoning itself proves that merely including the words in an isolated deed would not suffice. The Tur's decree in a case with a blank line likewise assumes that a forger could add material and then write 'firm and established,' which would be impossible if the phrase alone validated the last line. The Rosh's responsum must concern the present custom or reflect a position later abandoned in his rulings. The other authorities state without distinction that nothing is learned from the final line. That is the correct law, contrary to the Mechaber and the Bach's preference for Rashbam.
One may learn from the final line. This is difficult: the witnesses may have left part of a line because they could not sign immediately adjacent to the text, after which the holder inserted whatever he wished and wrote 'firm and established.' The Ramban raised this objection against Rashbam. The Ir Shushan and Sema answer that a full blank line presently remains, but then the same deed would be valid even without 'firm and established,' and if one decrees the spaced case because of the unspaced case the decree should apply here too. The wording also indicates an unqualified rule, and seif 8 implies that the preceding discussion concerns a deed without a blank line. The best defense of Rashbam and the Mechaber is that reliance upon the last line after 'firm and established' applies only where the text above did not already repeat the deed's substance. Since the Sages ordinarily required such repetition, failure to repeat suggests that the witnesses themselves wrote the concluding phrase. But if the substance was first repeated and then 'firm and established' appears, we presume the witnesses completed the deed properly and left an unavoidable gap, into which the holder may have written. Even so, the law should not follow Rashbam and Rashbatz. The baraita is stated without qualification, and because the Mechaber himself holds that failure to repeat does not invalidate the deed, omission of repetition cannot prove that the witnesses wrote the phrase. Many authorities cited above disagree, and their view is primary.
