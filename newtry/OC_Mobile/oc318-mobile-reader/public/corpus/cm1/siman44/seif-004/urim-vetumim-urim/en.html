Where the closing phrase is written. See Tumim. Even though a document lacking the closing phrase can still be valid, there remains concern that originally the closing phrase was absent, the final line was left blank, and the holder later inserted a favorable clause and then added the closing phrase. Nevertheless, because most deeds customarily include the closing phrase, we presume this deed originally did as well and do not assume forgery merely because the phrase could theoretically have been inserted. This requires that the line before the final line not itself contain the closing phrase. Otherwise perhaps the scribe originally ended there, the holder inserted whatever he wished in the final line, and then repeated the closing phrase. Likewise, if the preceding line contains an erasure large enough to have held the closing phrase, and all the more if the erasure is larger, the deed is invalid. Perhaps the original phrase was erased, the holder used the final line for an addition, validated the erasure there, and then wrote the closing phrase again. Sema and the Shulchan Aruch in seif 8.
