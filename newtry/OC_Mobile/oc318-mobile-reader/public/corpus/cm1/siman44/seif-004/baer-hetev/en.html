Valid and enduring. The Shach writes that the Sema appears to think the Mechaber derived this law from the Tur, but that is mistaken. The Tur expressly discusses the present custom of writing "valid and enduring" in all documents, so that a document lacking those words is invalid. Under the Talmudic law, where a document is valid without that formula, the opposite is true: nothing is derived from the final line. This is clear from the Tur. There is also a concern that the witnesses may have left one blank line, because they cannot always sign immediately adjacent to the text. The holder could then write whatever he wished in that line and add "valid and enduring" afterward. The Sema answers that the case is one where there is presently a blank line between the document and the signatures. The Shach finds this unsatisfactory. If, even without the formula, the Sema holds that we decree a spaced case because of an unspaced one, the same decree should apply where "valid and enduring" was added. The Mechaber also states without qualification that the final line is effective. The Shach therefore explains that the case is one where the earlier text did not repeat the subject matter at the end. Since the Sages enacted that the subject should be repeated, why did the witnesses not follow the enactment? We infer that the witnesses themselves wrote "valid and enduring," and therefore did not repeat the subject. But if the subject was repeated and the formula "valid and enduring" also appears, we presume that the witnesses followed the proper procedure by repeating the subject and no longer needed the formula. Since they could not align their signatures exactly, they may have left a blank line, and we are concerned that the holder inserted text there and then added the formula.
