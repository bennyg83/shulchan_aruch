Even his offspring born after he became an apostate, their kiddushin are kiddushin. One cannot explain that the text refrains from calling them definite kiddushin because an apostate's wife is presumably promiscuous, so that even regarding an Israelite woman who is exceedingly promiscuous we suspect that her children may not be her husband's, and here too perhaps the child is not his offspring. In any event, the child follows the mother even if the father were a non-Jew or a slave, as stated immediately afterward in the gloss. There is nevertheless some difficulty as to why the law was phrased as 'even his offspring whom he fathered,' since the child's Jewish status does not derive from him but from the mother.
