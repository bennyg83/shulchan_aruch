If the document ended at the end of a line, etc., we do not learn from it. See siman 45:10, where Rama writes that if the document ends in the middle of a line, the witnesses may sign side by side in the remaining half-line, and we do learn from the half-line before their signatures. We rely on the assumption that if that text had not already been present when the document came before the witnesses, they would not have signed. That ruling is from Ramban, responsum 94, cited by Beit Yosef and Darkei Moshe. At the end of that siman Rama writes that if they signed one beneath the other, the entire document is invalid in that configuration. Therefore, the present case must involve a document where it is known that when the witnesses originally signed, they left a gap of one and one-half lines between the document and their signatures, but when the lender later comes to collect, the court sees that the gap is now filled. The Mechaber and Rama then teach that if the witnesses signed one beneath the other, the document remains valid even for collection from encumbered property, but nothing is learned from the last one and one-half lines because the court knows that material was added after the witnesses signed. If the witnesses signed side by side, the entire document is invalid because the holder could have cut off the upper document and written in the half-line before the signatures, 'So-and-so borrowed one maneh from so-and-so,' creating a document and its witnesses in one line, which is valid. All the more so, if the document comes before beit din while the gap is still empty, the entire document is invalid. That is the case discussed in siman 45:10 and 11. See what I wrote there. Ir Shushan abbreviated and altered the language here in a way that obscures this distinction, but what I have written is compelled by the sources.
