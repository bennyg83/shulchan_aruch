Specifically a complete mentally incompetent person, but if his mind is clear, etc. One who studies Rabbeinu Yerucham will see that where his understanding is clear, the kiddushin are valid. The Gemara in Yevamot 113 expressly says according to Rabbi Eliezer that if his understanding is clear, although weak, his acts are valid and his kiddushin are kiddushin. It therefore appears that there is a scribal error in this gloss and it should be corrected in accordance with Rabbeinu Yerucham and the Beit Yosef: one whose mind is clear and who understands matters properly, even if his intellect is weak, has fully valid kiddushin and gittin. If his mind is confused and he understands nothing properly, he is presumed mentally incompetent and his kiddushin are not full kiddushin. If at times he is lucid and at times confused, and we do not know his state at the relevant time, it is a doubt. Even Rabbeinu Yerucham's wording requires precision, because calling him "presumed mentally incompetent" sounds like complete incapacity, while saying that his kiddushin are not "full" kiddushin sounds as though some doubt remains.
