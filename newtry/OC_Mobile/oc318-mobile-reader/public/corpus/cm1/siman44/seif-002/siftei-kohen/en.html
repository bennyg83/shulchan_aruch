If the entire document, etc. This is the language of Darkei Moshe: In Responsa Rashba, siman 1013, it is stated that even regarding a document written in the borrower’s own handwriting, we do not derive anything from the final line. Beit Yosef, however, wrote in the name of Rashba that in a responsum he ruled that we do derive from the final line if the entire document is in his handwriting, but if the document itself is not in his handwriting and he merely signed below, we do not derive from the final line. This concludes his words. Yet this does not appear from the responsum that I cited in Darkei Moshe. These are the words of Darkei Moshe. His words are surprising, for it may readily be explained that when Rashba wrote in Responsa, siman 1013, regarding one who produced the debtor’s handwritten document, from which one may collect from unencumbered property, and asked whether anything may be derived from its final line, and answered that it appears that even in this case nothing may be derived because he too leaves a line of space, he was referring to a case where the debtor signed his name. This accords with the Tur below, at the beginning of siman 69, which states: Whether he wrote it in his own handwriting, or another wrote it and he signed below, this is what the Mishnah means by one who produces the debtor’s handwriting may collect from unencumbered property. Moreover, below at the beginning of siman 69, seif 6, Beit Yosef writes that the abridger of the printed responsum omitted most of this responsum. See there.
