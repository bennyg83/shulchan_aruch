[" After ‘and we effected a kinyan.’ This must appear before ‘firm and established,’ because they became accustomed, etc. But after ‘firm and established,’ certification of erasures is ineffective, since nothing written after ‘firm and established’ is legally derived from. Those who certify erasures after ‘firm and established’ are mistaken:"]
[" Therefore, a document that does not contain ‘firm and established’ is invalid. The witnesses should not be attached immediately one to another. Meaning, the witnesses may not sign when they see that the document lacks ‘firm and established,’ even if they intend to sign immediately adjacent to the text. Since exact alignment is impossible, we are concerned that they may leave one blank line, allowing him to write whatever he wishes there and then add ‘firm and established.’ Certainly, however, if a document comes before us without ‘firm and established,’ and we see that the witnesses signed immediately adjacent to the text and there is no blank line, the document is plainly valid, except that nothing is derived from the final line alone. In Tosafot, the Rosh, and the Tur, however, this expression may be understood literally, for they maintain that if the substance of the document is not repeated at its conclusion, the entire document is invalid even where there is no concern for forgery. The same would apply for us where ‘firm and established’ is absent, because now that ‘firm and established’ is written in all documents and we derive from the final line, ‘firm and established’ takes the place of repeating the substance of the document. According to the Mechaber and the other later authorities, however, it must be explained as I have written, namely that this invalidity refers to the witnesses and means that they should not sign such a document, for we rule above in seif 1 that only the final line is not relied upon. Examine this carefully:"]
[" Which does not contain ‘firm and established,’ etc. Responsa Maharshdam, siman 30, writes that if it contains the phrase ‘all is true,’ this is equivalent to ‘firm and established.’ The practical ruling requires study:"]
[" And if ‘firm and established’ is written twice, it is valid. This is difficult. If so, what is gained by the practice of writing ‘firm and established’ in every document? We should still be concerned that after they wrote the first ‘firm and established,’ they left a blank line, and he wrote whatever he wished and then wrote ‘firm and established’ again. Ir Shushan writes that we need not be concerned that the original document ended at the first ‘firm and established,’ that the witnesses then left a large space and signed, and that the holder later added text until the second ‘firm and established,’ because we do not suspect the witnesses of leaving so much space. His words are surprising, for we certainly are concerned that they left one line, and the entire Gemara and the poskim are replete with this concern, as explained several times in this siman and elsewhere. Sema, seif katan 16, explains that the case is one where a blank line is presently before us, but this is difficult because the Mechaber should have stated so rather than ruling without qualification. I also find the Tur difficult. He writes: According to this, it would appear that if ‘firm and established’ was written twice, the document should be invalid, because perhaps after the first ‘firm and established’ he forged additional text and then certified it a second time. But Rabbi Yehudah Barceloni wrote that with an ordinary document, since its witnesses sign within it, one may properly write ‘firm and established’ many times. This concludes his words. Yet Rabbi Yehudah Barceloni plainly validates only where the second phrase is not in the final line, for his expression ‘since its witnesses sign within it’ means that in a folded document we are concerned that he himself may have written the second ‘firm and established,’ unlike an ordinary document, whose witnesses sign within it and do not leave two blank lines, but only one. Beit Yosef likewise writes in the name of Rashbetz, Rashba, and Responsa Ramban, siman 94, that Rabbi Yehudah Barceloni refers to a case where these phrases are not in the final line. The Tur, however, necessarily discusses a case where it is in the final line, since he writes that perhaps after the first certification he forged text and certified it a second time. The same difficulty applies to what the Mechaber wrote in Bedek HaBayit: For practical law, since Rabbi Yehudah Barceloni, Ramban, and Rashba validate it, we adopt that ruling. This requires study. See what I write below in siman 45, seif 18, in my comments to the gloss, seif katan 29, that some poskim maintain that two occurrences of ‘firm and established’ should not be written even in the lines preceding the final line, because the Sages did not distinguish. See there:"]
