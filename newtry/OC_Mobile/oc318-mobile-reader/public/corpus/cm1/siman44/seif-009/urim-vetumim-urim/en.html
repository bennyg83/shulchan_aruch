After "and we made a kinyan," etc. See Tumim. Although the Mechaber already wrote above in seif 4 that if the repetition at the end of the document is written, the document is valid, one might still have thought that the validation must appear before the document's concluding repetition, namely before "and we made a kinyan," or that we should at least avoid entering a dispute among the great authorities. We are therefore taught that a validation may be written even after the concluding repetition, without any concern.
Because they became accustomed, etc. Shach writes that after the concluding repetition nothing may be validated at all, and this is obvious. In Tumim I concluded from Tosafos that even a matter detrimental to the holder of the document is ineffective there, because at that point there is no longer any document at all.
For that reason, a document without the concluding repetition is not invalid, etc. See Tumim. Shach holds that if a document comes before us with one blank line and without the concluding repetition, the document is entirely invalid. But if there is no blank line and the witnesses signed immediately after the text, the document itself is valid, only we do not derive from the last line. I wrote that the accepted practice is already to invalidate a document lacking the concluding repetition, and that this is precisely the rule that validates the last line. Accordingly, in every case such a document is entirely invalid. See there.
Invalid. Shach holds that when a validation written in the last line is accepted because the concluding repetition appears afterward, this is only where the witnesses are established as persons who know the rule that a document without the concluding repetition is invalid. Otherwise it is not valid. See Tumim, where I wrote that this does not appear necessary, because there is a distinction between a matter dependent on the witnesses and one dependent on the scribe, especially according to the Mechaber above in seif 4, who holds that even under the strict law of the Gemara the document is valid. See there.
If two concluding repetitions are written, etc. Sema explains that the case nevertheless contains one blank line. There is therefore no concern that the document originally ended in the preceding line and that the holder later added another concluding repetition, for had that been so the witnesses would have needed to leave a distance of two lines, which they do not do. See Tumim, where I wrote that this ruling is not discussing whether we derive from the last line at all. The initial rule is that a document without the formula "valid and established" is entirely invalid. The present ruling teaches that if this formula appears twice, the document is not invalidated, because it was prepared in accordance with the enactment of the Sages. Whether one may derive from the last line is a separate issue not addressed here. This is obvious. See there.
