Invalid. Meaning that the witnesses should not sign such a document. Certainly, however, if a document comes before us without the words “firm and established,” and we see that the witnesses signed immediately adjacent to the text with no blank line, it is obvious that the document is valid, except that no inference is drawn from the final line, as stated above in seif 1. In the Rosh and the Tur, however, their words may be understood literally, that if the matter of the document was not repeated at the end, the entire document is invalid even where there is no concern of forgery. The same applies for us to the formula “firm and established,” which takes the place of repeating the substance of the document. Responsa Rashdam, siman 30, writes that if the document states “everything is true,” this is equivalent to “firm and established.” The practical ruling requires study. Shach.
