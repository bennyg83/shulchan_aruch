Both. If afterward only one witness signed, that is insufficient. This differs from siman 45, seif 21. There the document itself is written on ordinary paper, so its text is straightforward, and any writing inserted between the two witnesses is only for the borrower's protection against forgery by the lender. Since there is no visible defect, and no realistic concern that the lender will falsify it, one witness can suffice in that circumstance. Here, however, the erased areas work for the lender's benefit. Who can tell us that those erasures were originally present? Perhaps the erased area contained wording unfavorable to the lender, and the first witness signed the document in its ordinary form. This is clear in my view. Thus the Shach; see there.
