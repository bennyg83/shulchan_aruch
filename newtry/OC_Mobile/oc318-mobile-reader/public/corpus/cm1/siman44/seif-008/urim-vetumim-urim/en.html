And it is the size of the closing phrase. I explained above that Rambam and the Shulchan Aruch hold as follows. Although a deed without any closing phrase is not automatically invalid, where the phrase is present we derive from the final line because we presume the deed was prepared in the customary manner from the outset. But if the line before the final line contains an erasure as large as the closing phrase, and certainly if it is larger, even if the erasure is formally validated in the final line, we suspect that the original closing phrase was erased, the witnesses had left the final line blank, and the holder later erased the closing phrase, inserted a favorable statement in the final line, validated the erasure there, and wrote a new closing phrase. If the erasure is only exactly the size of the closing phrase and the final line contains nothing favorable to the holder, there is no obvious forgery. One cannot say he erased some adverse clause there because the erasure is no larger than the closing phrase that must already have occupied that space. Sema nevertheless invalidates even such a case. Shach disagrees and validates it. Shach's view appears correct, because any alleged forgery would be apparent rather than hidden.
