For this reason the Sages instituted that nothing be learned from the last line of a document, because perhaps it was fraudulently added after the witnesses signed. They therefore instituted that the final line repeat something already written above. Then even if the final line is ignored, the matter is already learned from what precedes it. In Perishah and Derishah I explained that the standard concluding phrase, 'and we made a kinyan from so-and-so for so-and-so,' is itself considered a repetition of the document. Although the earlier text may say only that the borrower instructed the witnesses to perform a kinyan and does not state that they actually did so until the final line, it is not an entirely new matter. Presumably the witnesses carried out what he requested, and the document was written upon that basis. It is therefore included within the concept of repetition.
