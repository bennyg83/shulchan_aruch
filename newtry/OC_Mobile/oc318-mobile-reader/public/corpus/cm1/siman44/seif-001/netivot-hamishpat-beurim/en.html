From the last line. See Shach, who says that even if the witnesses themselves come and say, 'We intentionally wrote the relevant clause in the last line,' the effect is only that of an oral loan because the necessary term is not proven from the face of the deed. Urim VeTumim asks from siman 49, where the witnesses can identify which of two people with the same name was the one whose obligation they witnessed, and the result is still treated as a written loan. In my view the invalidity here rests on another ground. A clause written in the last line resembles writing on material vulnerable to alteration. Even if the signers now testify that no fraud occurred, the Sages can invalidate such documentary form because later courts may rely only on signature authentication and not investigate the last-line problem. This resembles the rule in Even HaEzer regarding a get written on erasable material, where appearance of the original witnesses after the fact may still not cure the structural defect. By contrast, in the two-same-name case there is no risk that ordinary authentication itself will validate a fabricated clause. The distinction is therefore between an evidentiary ambiguity that outside witnesses can clarify and a structural location in the document that is intrinsically vulnerable to fabrication.
We learn it to the detriment of the holder of the document. Urim VeTumim asks against Tosafot in Bava Batra, who say that if the document were cut there would be a problem because the document must be returned in its last line. He suggests a new law from the possibility that a disadvantageous clause might have been written after the return clause. In my view there is no difficulty. Tosafot follow their own position that a proper return clause must itself be in the last line. If any additional line appeared after the return, then the return was not in the last line and would not validate the document. Thus there is no room to posit a further disadvantageous clause after it in a properly constructed deed.
