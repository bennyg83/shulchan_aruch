He must repeat, etc. The Sema writes that the concluding formula 'and we made a kinyan from so and so for so and so' counts as repetition of the deed's substance, even where the opening merely records that the debtor instructed the witnesses to make a kinyan and the actual performance is mentioned only at the end. Since it is not wholly new matter and the witnesses presumably did what was requested, it is treated as repetition. Rashbam and Rashi indeed regard 'and we made a kinyan' as repetition, but Tosafot and Mordechai hold that it is not. The Sema's rationale may mean that the final phrase merely explains what precedes, paralleling the Hagahot Maimoniyot in the name of Maharam, but I explain below that Maharam's position requires study.
Because nothing is learned from the last line. The Beit Yosef cites Maharam that this rule applies only to a new matter or one wholly contradicting the preceding text, but a final line that merely explains what precedes may be relied upon, and where upper and lower clauses can be reconciled the lower governs even in the last line. His principal proof is the standard closing formula concerning a valid implement for kinyan. That proof is inconclusive. First, in Talmudic times the formula may have been written before the final line, followed by a repetition of the deed, while today it appears last because our deeds conclude with 'firm and established.' Tosafot and Mordechai themselves say that 'and we made a kinyan' is new matter rather than repetition, which would force this explanation. Second, even if the formula appeared in the last line, it may have been legally unnecessary because the opening already recorded a full kinyan; the Gemara's discussion merely identifies which opinions the formula was intended to exclude. The other authorities state without qualification that nothing is learned from the final line, which includes even explanatory matter unless the same conclusion follows necessarily without it. Maharam's distinction therefore requires study.
Rather, nothing is learned from the final line. The Sema says this remains true even where a full blank line separates the text from the signatures, because the enactment was applied uniformly. That is correct only for the Tur, Tosafot, Rosh, and those who decree a spaced case because of an unspaced one. Rambam, Semag, the Mechaber, and most authorities explain the rule solely by concern that the final line was inserted fraudulently; where a full blank line removes that concern, the final line may be used. Ramban and Nimukei Yosef support this, and it best fits the Gemara. According to most authorities repetition is only prudent advice to avoid losing the final line, so where there is no risk of forgery there is no reason to disregard it.
