The Beit Yosef cites Hagahot Maimoniyot that the rule that nothing is derived from the final line applies only to a new matter or to a matter that wholly contradicts what appears above. If the final line does not wholly contradict the earlier text, an ambiguous earlier phrase may be interpreted from the explicit final phrase; indeed, the final line may be primary. Hagahot Maimoniyot brings proofs. The Shach writes that he examined those proofs and found them inconclusive. All the other authorities state the rule without qualification, implying that even where the final line merely explains the earlier text, if the earlier text would not necessarily have that meaning without the final line, nothing may be derived from it. The ruling of Hagahot Maimoniyot therefore requires study.

The Sema writes that if the subject matter was not repeated in the final line, then even if a blank line equal to one full line separates the body of the document from the witnesses' signatures, nothing is derived from the final written line, because the Sages did not distinguish in their enactment. The Shach writes that this is only according to the Tur and those who agree with him, who extended the decree from a case without spacing to one with spacing. According to the Mechaber and other authorities, the reason is simply concern for falsification. Therefore, where there is a full blank line and no concern for falsification, one may derive law from the final written line.
