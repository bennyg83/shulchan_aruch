(Shulchan Aruch, seif 1: except that nothing is learned from the final line.) This is Ramban’s view as cited by the Tur. Maharshal explains that it applies only where there is no blank line between the text and the signatures; if a blank line remains, one may learn even from the final written line. The Sema says the rule applies even with a blank line because the Sages made no distinctions. According to him, Ramban treats the rule of not learning from the final line like the Rosh treats invalidity of the whole document: whenever the Sages’ prescribed form was not followed, the rule applies uniformly. In my opinion Maharshal is correct. For the Rosh, repetition of the document’s essential content was a formal rabbinic enactment, so “they did not distinguish” is appropriate. Ramban, however, holds there was no formal enactment; as the Nimukei Yosef writes, repetition is merely good advice. Therefore no blanket rule applies, and where no concern exists there is no reason to disregard the final line.
