It is like a document without a date. See above, siman 43, seif 1, that a document without a date cannot be used to collect from encumbered property, but may be used to collect from unencumbered property. See Responsa Shevut Yaakov, part III, siman 146, who writes that the ruling here in Shulchan Aruch requires great study. In Responsa Rashba, siman 1035, which is the source of this law, it appears that the document is entirely invalid. The reason must be that although a document originally written without a date is valid, where the date has been erased it is like a document that contains an internal appearance of falsification and is invalid. Why then does Shulchan Aruch rule only that one cannot collect from encumbered property? Perhaps the Beit Yosef follows his own view in siman 43, seif 7, that even an antedated document may be used to collect from unencumbered property. If the date has been erased and this defect appears before us, the concern is merely that the original document was antedated. Therefore he rules that it is like a document without a date and may collect from unencumbered property. According to the Rama there, however, whose ruling we accept that an antedated document is entirely invalid, an erased date should likewise invalidate the document. One can further say, and this is the principal point, that the Beit Yosef follows his view in siman 65 in the name of the Maggid Mishneh, end of chapter 14 of Hilchos Malveh VeLoveh, that the essential part of a document consists of the names of lender and borrower and the amount of money, whereas the date is merely part of the standard form. The Sema there, seif katan 51, disagrees and writes that the date is part of the essential text, as Rashi explains and as stated in the Aruch under tofes, and this is also explicit in the Gemara in the chapter Kol HaGet. According to that view, the entire document is invalid and has only the force of an oral claim, against which the opposing party swears and is exempt. This appears correct in my humble opinion.
