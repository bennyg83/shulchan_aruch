He must repeat, etc. The practice is to write in the last line, "And we made a kinyan from him, etc.," even though this was not stated above in the body of every document. Rather, the document begins by saying that he instructed the witnesses to make a kinyan from him, without expressly stating that the kinyan was actually made, and only in the last line is it stated that it was done. Nevertheless, this is not considered new matter, but merely the completion of the document, and there is also a presumption that the witnesses acted accordingly. Therefore the document is valid, Sema. Shach, in seif katan 2, discusses at length whether the principle that the last line is valid when it merely completes what came before and introduces no new matter is subject to a dispute among the poskim. He explains that our practice of writing it in the last line is because we do not write the entire document in one continuous formula, and we learn the completion from the last line. See there.
Except that we do not derive, etc. Sema holds that even if one blank line was left, we still do not derive legal effect from the last line. Shach disagrees and writes that according to Ramban the document is valid and one may even derive from the last line when there is one blank line. In Tumim I established the view of Sema, that according to everyone we do not derive from the last line even when there is one blank line. See there.
We derive, etc. Even if the witnesses themselves say, "We wrote the last line and we know that it contains no forgery," their statement is only oral testimony and they are not believed to convert it into documentary evidence, Shach in the name of Rashba. See Tumim, where I concluded that if the document contains a kinyan, its law is that of a written loan, because the witnesses can write a new document since they have not completed their agency. See there.
To the detriment of the holder of the document, etc. See Tumim, where I discussed at length that it appears from Tosafos that if something was written after the repetition at the end of the document, in a manner unrelated to the document, then we do not derive even a detrimental matter from it because it has no connection to the document at all. This requires study. If a favorable clause was written in the last line but before the repetition, see Tumim. It appears from Ran that it is valid, whereas Tosafos indicates that it is invalid. The practical law requires study.
