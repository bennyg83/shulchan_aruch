It is like a document without a date. See above, siman 43, seif 1, that a document lacking a date cannot be used to collect from encumbered property, although it may be collected from the debtor’s free assets. See Shevut Yaakov, part III, responsum 146, who writes that the ruling of Shulchan Aruch here requires great study. The Rashba’s responsum, number 1035, which is the source of this law, implies that such a document is entirely invalid. The reason would be that although a document originally written without a date may be valid, when the date has been erased the document is internally defective as though falsified. Why, then, does Shulchan Aruch rule that it remains valid except for collection from encumbered property? Perhaps the Beit Yosef follows his own ruling in siman 43, seif 7, that an antedated document may still be collected from the debtor’s free assets. If the date has been erased, creating a visible defect, we need only suspect that the document had been antedated. He therefore treats it like a document without a date and permits collection from free assets. According to our ruling like the Rama there, however, that an antedated document is entirely invalid, an erased date should likewise invalidate the document. There is another answer, and it is the principal one: the Beit Yosef follows his position in siman 65, citing the Maggid Mishneh at the end of chapter 14 of Hilchot Malveh VeLoveh, that the essential portion of a document consists of the names of lender and borrower and the amount, while the date belongs only to the standard form. The Sema there, seif katan 51, disagrees and holds that the date is part of the essential text. Rashi explains likewise, as does the Arukh under the entry tofes, and this is also the plain implication of the Gemara in the chapter Kol HaGet. On that view, erasure of the date invalidates the entire document, leaving only an oral claim, against which the defendant may swear and be exempt. This appears correct in my humble opinion.
