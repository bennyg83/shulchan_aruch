Rabbeinu Tam writes that erasures and interlineations must be authenticated before the phrase 'and we made a kinyan,' meaning before the last line, otherwise nothing can be learned from them. The Rosh writes that nowadays the practice is to authenticate erasures and interlineations after 'and we made a kinyan,' because it became customary to write in every document 'all is valid and enduring,' which prevents forgery, and therefore we even learn from the last line. The Mechaber records the Rosh's rule later in seif 9. Here he teaches only that erasures and interlineations require authentication and that failure to authenticate does not invalidate the entire document. He also writes briefly that under our practice the authentication appears before 'all is valid and enduring.' In Talmudic times, when documents did not universally conclude with that formula, authentication appearing before it was effective only where beit din found a full line of blank space between the document and the witnesses, for the reason explained in the preceding seif.
