And some say, if there was an erasure. See Shach, who interprets the Tosefta as speaking even where the original writing remains recognizable under the erasure, yet says that in the essential part of the deed the erased clause is not relied upon. Urim VeTumim asks that if the original remains recognizable, an erasure should be valid even in the essential clause, as with an amount that was written and later erased. I do not understand the question. Shach agrees that whatever has not been erased remains valid and is learned from. His point is that the erased writing itself is not treated as existing writing even if traces remain and one can see what had been written there. If the entire sum is under an erasure and no un-erased sum remains, the court does not adjudicate from the erased letters. Erased writing is not writing.
