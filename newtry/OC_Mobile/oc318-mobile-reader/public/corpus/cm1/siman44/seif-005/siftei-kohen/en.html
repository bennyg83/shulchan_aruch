Some say, etc. The Nimukei Yosef likewise rules that an unerased or scraped passage not properly authenticated invalidates the entire deed, citing the Yerushalmi and many leading authorities. This is the better rule, for otherwise one must always fear that the holder erased an obligation, limitation, or condition. Rashba's proof from the Tosefta, which distinguishes an erasure or interlineation in the essential body from one outside it, can be rejected. That source concerns a case where the erased content is evident from the remaining text. Even then, erasure of the names, money, or other essential terms invalidates. Where the erased matter cannot be reconstructed and may have contained a liability, defect, or condition affecting the whole instrument, it is itself part of the deed's essence. This reconciles the Tur and Rama here with their adoption in Even HaEzer of Rambam's rule for a get: there too the surrounding text reveals what was erased, yet erasure in the essential portion remains invalid unless authenticated. The Maggid Mishneh's inference from Rambam that erasures and interlineations share every consequence is likewise not compelling; Rambam equates them only in requiring authentication.
