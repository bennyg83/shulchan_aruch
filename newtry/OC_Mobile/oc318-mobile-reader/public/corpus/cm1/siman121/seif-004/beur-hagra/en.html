The lender sent... See Shach, seif katan 19.
Or the borrower does not... even if he was... Rambam explains that money should not be sent where the borrower could later deny authorization. But if the borrower admits the authorization, witnesses are unnecessary, as shown by the Mishnah in Perek HaShoel. The Bava Kamma case where agency was made before witnesses is specifically where the principal did not personally tell the agent the identifying message, as explained above in seif 2.
After he pronounces a ban... and there is an authority... Rambam writes this. See Beer HaGolah and Beit Yosef. The latter opinion can be understood by analogy to the shopkeeper on his ledger: where there are circumstances supporting the claim, an oath can sometimes suffice even though the other party denies. Here too there are grounds for belief because witnesses attest to the identifying sign. Maharik writes similarly. See Shach. The first opinion distinguishes the shopkeeper case because there the two claimants contradict one another, while here the agent does not directly contradict the borrower.
