The agent is not believed. Just as a hesset oath renders the agent interested and therefore disqualified as a witness, the same may apply where a general acceptance of a ban is imposed upon him. Likewise, if his fellow could seize property from him and thereby collect, perhaps that too renders him interested, even if seizure would help only without witnesses. On the other hand, perhaps he can protect himself from such a seizure and therefore is not regarded as interested. If there is a single independent witness who supports the agent, the agent is no longer interested, because that witness exempts him from a hesset oath. Even if a general ban would otherwise render him interested, Urim VeTumim writes that no such ban is imposed where the claim is only uncertain. There is also no concern that three quarters of the money is being established through one witness, because that principle applies only to a direct legal effect, not to remote causation. Once his interest has been removed, we do not recreate it merely because the supporting witness arrived afterward. Nor do we presume that he first made his statement in order to preserve a later position, especially where he knew that a witness would support him. We do not presume an interest unless it is established.
