To him. Whether the creditor's handwriting is presently before us or not, if the debtor claims with certainty that the creditor's handwritten authorization reached him, the debtor is exempt. In the latter case, where the debtor does not recognize the handwriting, he is liable even if the writing is before us. The Sema writes likewise. Ibid.
Who ruled. Maharshal writes that the first opinion should be accepted, because one does not administer an oath at all on the basis of an uncertain claim. Moreover, the Tur and Shulchan Aruch write in seif 6 that the depositary or creditor is subject only to a general ban. The Shach responds that this is not decisive. There the oath would be used to exempt a person from payment, whereas here the claimant seeks to collect; one may therefore require him to swear even in response to an uncertain claim, as stated in seif 9.
