If a lender or depositor sent the borrower or custodian a letter saying, 'Send the one hundred you hold for me through Levi,' and he sent it through Levi, but the lender later claims, 'I did not write or send you that letter,' the borrower takes a heses oath that the lender's handwriting reached him and that he therefore sent the money, and he is exempt. If the letter was not in the lender's handwriting, or the borrower did not know it was his handwriting, then even if it contained private signs and letters used between them, the borrower or custodian remains responsible if the lender says, 'I sent no letter; others deceived you.' He must pay after imposing an unspecified ban upon anyone who knowingly sent this letter and does not admit it. Some have ruled that the lender swears and then collects, like all claimants who swear and collect. See note 140.
