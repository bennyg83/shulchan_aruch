He cannot require him to swear. Certainly, if no one is demanding payment from the borrower, it is obvious that he cannot require the messenger to swear. Rather, even if the lender died and his heirs are demanding payment from the borrower because they do not know whether payment was made, the messenger is exempt from an oath, because there is no definite claim against him. Whether this must specifically be a loan documented by a note, or whether it applies even to an oral loan, where the borrower's claim is, 'I do not know whether I repaid you,' depends on the dispute among the authorities above in siman 91 concerning a shopkeeper on his ledger.
