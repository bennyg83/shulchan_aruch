He cannot require him to swear that he performed his mission. This is so even though the borrower must pay the creditor’s heirs a second time. If the heirs were not demanding payment, their father was evidently already paid, and there would plainly be no basis to make the messenger swear. Thus the case necessarily involves a demand by the heirs, yet an uncertain claim does not impose an oath on the messenger. Terumat HaDeshen, siman 308, writes this correctly. On that understanding, the case should be limited to a documented loan. Otherwise it resembles Rambam’s ruling concerning deceased workers, where the shopkeeper does not swear and collect because the employer makes only one payment and the debt is oral. According to my explanation there, however, this case can also involve an oral loan.
Likewise, if the messenger died or went overseas. This applies where the messenger never definitely asserted before beit din that he paid the creditor. Even if the borrower says the messenger told him with certainty that payment was made, the messenger did not present that claim in court against the creditor. If he had definitely asserted payment before us, then even if he later died or went overseas, the creditor must swear. This follows from the rule concerning a deceased shopkeeper or workers: there, no oath is required only because the employer makes one payment. Here the borrower already paid the messenger and must now pay the creditor or his heirs, making two payments. See above, siman 91, seifim katanim 11 and 13, and Responsa Rabbi Shlomo Kohen, vol. 3, siman 51.
