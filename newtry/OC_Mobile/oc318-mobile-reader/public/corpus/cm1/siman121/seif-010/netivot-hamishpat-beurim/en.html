Who says that they were lost through unavoidable accident. See Shach seif katan 35 through his words "and he is not a shopkeeper or moneychanger." His language implies that if the messenger is a shopkeeper or moneychanger, he bears responsibility because he may use the money. Shach apparently overlooked Beit Yosef in Yoreh Deah siman 169, who writes that whenever a person accepts no greater responsibility than the ordinary law imposes, he has no permission to use the money and is merely a guardian, even if he is a moneychanger and the money is loose. Since the money was not given to him to remain with him for any period, but only to pass immediately to the borrower, and likewise on the return trip immediately to the lender, he may not spend it. This is clear. All the more so where the debtor gave him the money with the language "bring" or "acquire," so that the money becomes the creditor's, while the creditor never entrusted loose money to him, there is no basis to infer permission to use it. But where the creditor himself appointed an agent to receive the money from the debtor, the agent may use loose money, because it is as though the creditor deposited loose money with him. Rashba, cited by Tumim in siman 128, writes similarly. It is possible that Shach too referred only to an agent appointed by the creditor, though his wording does not imply that limitation.
