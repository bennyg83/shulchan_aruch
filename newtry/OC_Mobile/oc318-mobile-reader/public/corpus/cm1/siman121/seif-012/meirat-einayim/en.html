Shimon must pay after the lender takes an oath, etc. The reason is that Reuven the lender has a definite claim: 'I did not send him to receive the entire amount, and I received only twenty.' Shimon's claim is uncertain, and a definite claim prevails over an uncertain one. The Tur disagrees and distinguishes this case. Since Reuven admits that he appointed the person as his agent and Shimon therefore gave him the money with authority, Reuven caused his own loss by choosing an agent who cannot reliably establish what he delivered. Accordingly, Shimon swears that he gave the full fifty and is exempt. See the Beit Yosef and my discussion in the Derishah. The Beit Yosef adds another case: Reuven sent Levi to borrow money for him from Shimon. Shimon says, 'I gave you fifty through him,' while Reuven says, 'I received only twenty.' Since Shimon seeks to extract money from Reuven, even the opposing view agrees that Reuven takes a Torah oath as one who admits part of the claim and is exempt.
