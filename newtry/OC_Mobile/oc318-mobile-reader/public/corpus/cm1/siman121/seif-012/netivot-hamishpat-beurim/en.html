Bring it to so and so. See Shach seif katan 44 through his statement that once they said "give" or "acquire," they can no longer say "we returned it to you." There are textual errors there. It should read, "if they say, 'we paid,' they are not believed," and later, "alternatively, the poskim are not presently discussing the law of acquisition." At first glance, according to the opinion in siman 291 that when a guardian gives a deposit to his wife or children, even if they were negligent and cannot pay the first guardian is exempt, it would seem that if they later deny receiving it, the first guardian should likewise be exempt upon swearing that he delivered it to them. Since delivery to them is considered proper guarding, he has sworn that he guarded properly, and their later denial should make no difference. If so, even where the sender said "acquire," the messenger should be able to say, "I returned it to you as a deposit," and be exempt, as Taz explains in siman 125 and as I wrote there. Once he returned it to someone with whom the owner regularly deposits, even if that person later denies it, the messenger should be exempt. It nevertheless appears that the oath sworn by the guardian who says that he delivered the item to his mother, in Perek HaMafkid, Bava Metzia 42, cited by Shach, is itself the shomrim oath. It is equivalent to swearing that the item is not in his possession and that he guarded it properly by delivering it to his mother for safekeeping. Since he remains subject to the shomrim oath, he again has the status of one obligated to swear, and one who is obligated to swear cannot serve as a witness in the matter.
