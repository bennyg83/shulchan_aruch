As Beit Yosef and Kesef Mishneh write in rejecting Tur's view: Reuven definitely says, 'You did not repay me.' Since I appointed the agent to bring only twenty, anything beyond that which you gave him was not given at my direction. Shimon, however, can say only that perhaps the lender made him an agent to bring the entire debt. This is therefore like one who says, 'perhaps I repaid you.'
Bach writes that if it was known through witnesses that he appointed him as agent, or he told him orally or in writing, then Tur's words are correct. Everyone agrees that the borrower takes a hesset oath and is exempt, because he acted with authorization in giving the money to the lender's agent, and the lender caused his own loss. Beit Yosef explains Rambam's words above as follows: if Reuven sent Levi to Shimon to borrow a sum of money for him, and Shimon sent the money through Levi, and Shimon says there were fifty while Reuven says there were only twenty, Reuven takes a Torah oath that there were only twenty, like anyone who admits part of a claim. Levi takes a hesset oath and is exempt.
