And if he did not reach that degree, it remains a doubt. The Gemara says that one who has not reached the drunkenness of Lot is treated as fully competent, and if he commits an offense punishable by death he is executed. There is therefore no ordinary doubt. Maggid Mishneh was forced to explain that Rambam's doubt concerns whether the drunk person has the status of a healthy person who must say explicitly, "Give," or perhaps the status of one dangerously ill who need not use that wording. This would be a case where he was very drunk, but one only slightly drunk is certainly treated as healthy. Admittedly, this explanation is strained, because Rambam writes simply, "this is a doubt," and it is not natural to understand that only the need for the word "Give" is doubtful. The Tur's text in Rambam is better: "If the matter was doubtful whether he had reached that degree, it is a doubt." See Beit Yosef.
