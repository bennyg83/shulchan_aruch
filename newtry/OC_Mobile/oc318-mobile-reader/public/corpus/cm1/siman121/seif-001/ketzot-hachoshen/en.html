If the creditor told the debtor to send payment through a gentile messenger, the debtor is exempt once he follows the instruction, even though a gentile is not an agent in the ordinary Torah sense. Sema says all the more where the creditor expressly says, "Send and be discharged." The Shach cites Ramban as requiring that express release. In my view the ordinary instruction to send through the gentile already shows that the creditor accepted the risk of that method. The creditor cannot direct a mode of transmission that lacks ordinary agency and then hold the debtor liable for using it. This differs from sending through a Canaanite slave where the surrounding facts may not show that the creditor accepted full risk. The governing question is whether the creditor's command itself shifted the risk of delivery.
