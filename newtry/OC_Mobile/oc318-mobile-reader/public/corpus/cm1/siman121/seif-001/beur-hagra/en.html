He sent... even if he did not say... See Tosafos, Bava Kamma 104a. Tosafos in Bava Metzia 98b, Nimukei Yosef, the Rosh, and the poskim support the broader rule. The contrary formulation in Bava Kamma is rejected because we rule like Shmuel, as the Rif explains, and also rule that an agent appointed before witnesses is a valid agent. Bava Basra and Tosafos there provide further support. The dispute concerning a written identifying sign likewise shows that written authorization can be effective even outside the principal's presence when the writing or witnesses authenticate it. Tosafos ultimately presses its earlier language because of the difficulty, but Bava Metzia yields a coherent reading.
Provided that he recognizes... See Tosafos, Bava Kamma, concerning oral agency.
Even if the agent... A deaf-mute, mentally incompetent person, and minor share the relevant incapacity and are excluded by Scripture through the law of a minor. See Rashi, Gittin 23a. A non-Jew likewise lacks ordinary Jewish agency, and Bava Metzia treats him comparably to a minor for acquisition in this context. See Bava Basra.
Even if he did not designate... So the Rif writes according to the Tur's text. The Mishnah in Perek HaShoel says "in the hand of your agent," and its plain wording implies no specifically named individual; otherwise it would overlap with the case of one's son.
And provided... See Tosafos, Bava Kamma. Even if the creditor showed willingness to have the money sent through someone generally considered trustworthy, he may still have intended that it be sent only through a secure channel.
Through someone... In the first chapter of Gittin even a person previously trusted with the writing can become problematic once established as a denier. All the more so here.
Unless... As stated in Bava Basra concerning a minor and the need to send through a legally effective representative.
