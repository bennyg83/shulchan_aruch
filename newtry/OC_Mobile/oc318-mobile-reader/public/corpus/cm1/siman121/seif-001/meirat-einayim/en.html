Send me my debt, etc. This siman continues the rule begun in the preceding siman, that the money remains at the borrower's risk until it reaches the lender or his agent.
If he merely sent him a written instruction, etc., he is exempt. Levush says the reason is commercial custom. Although similar language appears in Rif and is quoted by Rosh and Beit Yosef, I proved that this is a scribal error. Where the lender writes, "Send through so-and-so," or "Send through whomever you wish," the borrower's exemption is strict law. Custom matters only where the lender wrote simply, "Send me my money," and the borrower selected a carrier.
Even if the agent is a non-Jew. The lender need not add "send and be exempt," for the reason explained in the preceding siman.
In the manner people ordinarily send, etc. This limitation applies specifically where the lender said, "Send through whomever you wish." If he designated a particular carrier, the complaint about choosing a dangerous route is against that carrier rather than the borrower.
