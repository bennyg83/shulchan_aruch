The language of Ran: the lender's oath is a Mishnah-level oath taken while holding a sacred object, like the oath of workers, because the lender swears and collects just as they do. The agent's oath, however, is only a hesset oath, because the Mishnah's oath applies only to those who swear and collect. Rif agrees, and this is the primary view. This is also Kesef Mishneh's textual reading in Rambam there, chapter 3, law 7, and Tur writes likewise.

Rama emended the text this way based on the Mechaber's question in Beit Yosef against Tur and Ba'al HaTerumot, who wrote this. Sema already writes that no emendation is necessary, because the Mechaber retracted what he wrote in Beit Yosef. We find several instances where he raises a difficulty in Beit Yosef against Rambam or Tur but nevertheless in Shulchan Aruch rules according to their words. Only one word must be corrected here, and it should read: 'their agency is believed to render them,' etc. Bach likewise rules this way, reasoning that wherever one witness is believed to impose an oath, two witnesses are believed to impose monetary liability. Responsa Mahari Weil, siman 173, writes that if the agent claims he gave the money to the lender through the lender's wife, and the lender says he did not receive it, Shimon the agent swears that he delivered it to the wife. There is no problem of a custodian entrusting an item to another custodian, because anyone who deposits does so with the understanding that the custodian's wife and children may handle it. The agent's wife then swears that she gave it to the lender and is exempt. The lender swears that he did not receive it, and the borrower must pay him. Sema.
