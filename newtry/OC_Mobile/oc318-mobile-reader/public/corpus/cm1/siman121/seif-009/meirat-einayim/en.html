And he can require the lender to take a Torah oath. In the Perishah I explained that although one witness does not ordinarily require a claimant to take a Torah oath in order to collect, but only requires a defendant to swear in order to be exempt, nevertheless the borrower can bring the lender into a situation where a Torah oath is required. He can pay the lender a second time in the presence of the first witness and one additional witness, so that two witnesses attest to the second payment and the lender cannot deny it. The borrower can then sue to recover the first payment, concerning which only the first witness testifies. The lender will then need to take a Torah oath in order to be exempt from repayment. This is similar to the ruling of the Tur and Mechaber in Even HaEzer siman 92:13.
They are witnesses and the borrower is exempt. These words too are missing from the Mechaber and were supplied by Rama, and this is also the Tur's text at the end of seif 12. Rama then writes, 'It appears to me that he is not believed.' Here too Rama intends to emend the Mechaber, because the Mechaber's phrase that the borrower can render them witnesses rests on this point. The Beit Yosef himself expresses surprise: where there is one witness, it is understandable that the borrower is believed so as to require the lender to take a Torah oath, because no money is directly extracted from the lender. But how can the borrower be believed where this would actually remove the lender's money that remains in the borrower's hands? In the Perishah I wrote that according to the Beit Yosef's own reasoning, even one witness should not be sufficient to impose a Torah oath, because if the lender refuses to swear the borrower will not have to pay him. The Tur writes similarly in the name of the Rosh in siman 93:7 that there is no real distinction between money and oath, because an oath can ultimately produce a monetary consequence. As to the underlying difficulty, it appears that the Tur's ruling that the borrower is believed concerns a case where, before any protest from the lender was known, people heard the borrower state, 'I saw the agents carry out their mission.' This resembles the preceding ruling, based on the Ran, that if the borrower released the agents at the outset they are believed and we do not suspect collusion. I wrote other resolutions in the Perishah. According to this, there may be no need for Rama's broad emendation of the Shulchan Aruch. It is possible that the Mechaber in the Shulchan Aruch retracted from the difficulty he had raised in the Beit Yosef, as he does elsewhere, and ruled in accordance with the Rambam and Tur. Only one word need then be corrected, so that the text reads that regarding performance of their mission he is believed to render them witnesses. The Beit Yosef's phrase 'to remove the lender's money' rather than simply 'to exempt the borrower' may then be understood as emphasizing the novelty that they are believed even though the consequence is to remove the lender's monetary claim.
