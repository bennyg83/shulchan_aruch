The messenger is not considered financially interested merely because his testimony may affect whether the creditor can collect. Ran's discussion of a creditor claiming that he seized during the debtor's lifetime shows that a witness can become interested where his testimony directly affects his own potential oath or liability. Here, however, the messenger's position is not automatically improved by the substance of his testimony. So long as the messenger himself has no direct monetary gain or avoidance of liability dependent on the testimony, he remains a valid witness.
