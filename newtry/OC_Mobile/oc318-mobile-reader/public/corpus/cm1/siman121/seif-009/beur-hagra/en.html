Or if he came... and the lender... Learned from there and from seif 5 above. The cases here and in the preceding seif concern a loan documented by a note.
The agent shall swear... He takes a heses oath because he swears to be exempt. One who swears to be exempt does not ordinarily take the more stringent oath while holding a sacred object, except in enumerated Mishnah cases. The lender, by contrast, swears as one who swears and collects, as the Rif and Ran explain. This differs from seif 8, where both swear to be exempt.

And even if he releases... Mordechai asks why the borrower cannot release the witnesses from liability and thereby remove their interest, as in the law of two interested witnesses who withdraw their interest. Ran discusses the distinction: there the released witnesses gain no benefit from the release, whereas here they may. Sema and Ran explain the competing views. If their relevant status was established before the dispute, the second opinion in siman 140 may govern. According to the first opinion there and siman 37 seif 12, release can sometimes remove the interest.
And if there is no... The Yerushalmi recounts a person who deposited a barrel with another, the custodian denied it, and Rabbi Yirmiyah required an oath through an agent or porter. Baal HaTerumos reads it as the present type of case. The Yerushalmi's continuation differs from our Bavli, saying that once the Torah believed the agent he is not considered interested in his testimony. Ran limits part of that conclusion to Kiddushin, where an agent may become a witness at least to require a get stringently. Still, the underlying rule here is correct where the agent has no personal interest, comparable to acquisition by document and to the Gemara's rule that the agents themselves become witnesses.

And it appears to me that he is not... See Beer HaGolah. This is the primary view. Beit Yosef's objection is not compelling, because the borrower does not turn them into witnesses by designation; they are witnesses by law and are disqualified only insofar as they remain financially interested. Therefore, where that interest is absent, or was released before or afterward according to the relevant opinion, they may testify.
