When does the preceding rule apply? When the creditor appointed the person as his agent to bring his property. If he did not appoint him, but the borrower or custodian instead said, 'Bring so-and-so the money of his that I hold,' or a person came to the borrower or custodian and said, 'So-and-so appointed me as his agent to bring his money,' and the borrower trusted him and delivered the money, then if the agent says, 'It was lost through unavoidable accident,' or, 'I delivered it to the lender,' while the lender says, 'I did not appoint you and did not receive it,' the agent swears that he gave it to the lender, and the lender or depositor swears while holding a sacred object that he did not receive it and collects his debt from the borrower or custodian. The agent is not believed because he has an interest in the testimony, since he must swear to exempt himself. Even if the borrower or custodian releases the agent from the oath afterward so that he will no longer have an interest, it is ineffective. Some say, however, that if they exempted him from an oath at the outset when they delivered the money to him, the exemption is effective. If the agent need not swear, such as where the sender accompanied him, stood at a distance, and saw him carry out his agency, the agent has no interest and is a single witness who obligates the lender to take a Torah oath. If there were two agents and the sender accompanied them, stood at a distance, and saw them carry out their agency, they are witnesses and the borrower is exempt. It appears to me, however, that the sender is not believed to confer the status of two witnesses upon them by his word alone and thereby remove the lender's money.
