If the claimant admits that this is his handwriting. Meaning, he admits that he wrote the instruction to send through this messenger. The Bach correctly notes that writing is not essential. The same rule applies if the creditor orally told the borrower, face to face, “Send it through so-and-so,” but later says that he does not believe the messenger. Since the creditor admits appointing the messenger and the borrower says he complied, only a general ban may be imposed. These rules apply even to a documented loan, and so do the rules in seifim 6 and 7, because the creditor admits creating the agency.
The messenger takes the guardian’s oath. Beit Yosef cites the Mordechi: Reuven owed money to Shimon and sent it through a messenger, but it was lost. Shimon cannot compel the messenger to swear until Reuven appears. Raavan similarly ruled that one who sent maintenance to his wife through a messenger, and it was lost, need not swear to the wife without the husband’s authorization. The reason appears to be that the messenger may say, “Perhaps the sender will choose not to make me swear, will pay you himself, and will trust my claim that it was lost.” Accordingly, if the borrower dies or travels overseas, the messenger must immediately take the guardian’s oath vis-à-vis the creditor.
