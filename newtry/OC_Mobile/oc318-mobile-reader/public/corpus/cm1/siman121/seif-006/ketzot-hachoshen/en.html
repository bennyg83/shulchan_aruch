The messenger takes an oath. Beit Yosef cites Mordechai concerning Reuven, who owed Shimon and sent the money through a messenger; the money was lost. Shimon cannot directly require the messenger to swear until Reuven comes and litigates, because the messenger's custodial obligation runs to Reuven, his principal. Once Reuven sues the messenger or otherwise places the matter in litigation, the appropriate oath can be imposed. The rule preserves the distinction between the creditor's claim against the debtor and the debtor's separate claim against the messenger.
