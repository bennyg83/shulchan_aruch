And he swears. Beit Yosef cites the Mordechai in the name of Avi HaEzri: if Reuven owed a gold coin to Shimon and sent it through an agent, and it was lost, Shimon cannot compel the agent to swear until Reuven appears. The Mordechai concludes that Raavan likewise ruled regarding one who sent maintenance to his wife and it was lost, that the messenger need not swear to the wife without her husband's authorization. The apparent reason is that the messenger can say, 'Perhaps the sender will prefer not to make me swear, but will pay you himself and trust my statement that it was lost.' Accordingly, if the debtor has died or gone overseas, the messenger must immediately take the oath of bailees vis-a-vis the creditor. See Responsa Rashdam, simanim 416 and 424. Ibid.
