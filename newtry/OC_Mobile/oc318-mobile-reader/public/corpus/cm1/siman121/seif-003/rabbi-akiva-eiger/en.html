[Shach, letter 27] This too requires study. Examine this carefully. Note: In my humble opinion, with respect to a loan, if the money was lost through unavoidable circumstances while in the agent’s possession before the authorization letter reached the agent, the borrower certainly remains obligated to pay. Even if it is later revealed that the agent was trustworthy, what does that accomplish? Since he had not yet become the lender’s agent, delivery to him was not payment, and it was therefore the borrower’s own money that was lost. If the money was lost after the letter reached the agent, then even though there was no retroactive revelation, from the moment the letter reached him he presently became the lender’s agent. It is as though the money now came into the lender’s hand, and payment was completed. See Tosafot, Bava Kamma 104a, s.v. “Thus,” who wonder why the borrower is liable. Their words are astonishing to me. Since the person was not the lender’s agent, what difference does it make that the lender trusted him? The money never left the borrower’s ownership, and it was his own money that was lost. Only with a deposit does the rationale that “you trusted him” apply. This requires very great study.
