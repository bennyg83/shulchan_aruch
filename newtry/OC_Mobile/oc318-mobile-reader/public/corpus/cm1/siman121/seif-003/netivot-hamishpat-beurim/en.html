And by the route ordinarily used by people. See Shach seif katan 5 through his words that there is no distinction between appointing a particular person and saying "by whomever you choose." See Tumim, who gives a simple case: from Jerusalem to Damascus, the route through Tzefat may be dangerous while the route through Hebron is safe. If the creditor said "send it by so and so," and it is known that this person's regular route is through Tzefat, the debtor is exempt; but if he said "send it by whomever you choose," the debtor remains liable if he chooses the dangerous route.
Unless he told him before witnesses. See Sema seif katan 6. The plain meaning of Sema is that if the creditor told the messenger that the debtor should send the money through him, the debtor is thereafter exempt even if the messenger was not appointed before witnesses. The Perishah says this expressly: if the creditor told the messenger, "Tell the debtor that I sent you to tell him to send it through you," it is effective even without witnesses. Shach in seif katan 15 attempts to force Sema to mean that witnesses are required even here. According to Shach, Sema's phrase "he did not tell him to send it through him" must mean that the creditor did not tell the debtor directly. But Sema's intent is straightforward, as explained above and as the Perishah writes. Shach simply did not have the Perishah before him, as is known.
