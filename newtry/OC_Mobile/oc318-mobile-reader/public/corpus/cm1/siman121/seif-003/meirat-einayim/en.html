He did not appoint him as agent before witnesses, etc. I proved that the case is where the lender used the weaker language from the previous seif, such as, "I have money with so-and-so; tell him to send it to me, and if he gives it to you bring it," but did not say this before witnesses and also did not expressly say, "Send through so-and-so." Instead the lender gave the messenger a recognizable seal so the borrower would know he came from the lender and trust what he reported. The seal does not make the messenger equivalent to one appointed before witnesses.
So-and-so sent me to you so that you should send him his money. The messenger says that the lender told him to ask the borrower to send the money because the lender needs it. He does not falsely say that the lender instructed that it be sent specifically through him. There is no factual contradiction among lender, borrower, and messenger. The ruling teaches only that handing over a recognizable seal plus saying "bring it to me" is not as strong as an appointment before witnesses. For this reason the Mechaber here says nothing about an oath or ban upon the lender. Rama's addition citing views that he should accept a ban or swear is difficult, because Rambam and Tur state those remedies in the different case of the next seif, where the lender denies having sent the written instruction.
