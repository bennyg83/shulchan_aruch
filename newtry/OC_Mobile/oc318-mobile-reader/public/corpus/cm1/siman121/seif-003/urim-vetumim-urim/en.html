But he said to him that he had been sent to the sign of the letter, and that he was written in another hand, and he made a painting and a sign that Hashem had signed and me like a messenger who had done for the sakes:
Go and bring it to me, etc. Sema holds that saying, 'Go and bring it to me,' and the messenger's claim, 'So-and-so sent me,' amount to the same thing. Even if the messenger's statement is true, it is ineffective when it was not stated before witnesses, because the sender did not trouble himself to establish witnesses. In Tumim I wrote that the case is different. The lender who sent the message says, 'I never used language appointing him as an agent.' Therefore, without witnesses, the messenger's claim does not help. But if the messenger carrying the identifying token lies and says that the lender did use agency language, such as, 'Tell him to send the money, and if he gives it to you, bring it to me,' then he is in fact an agent even without witnesses. See Tumim.
And he should impose a ban. Sema questions this. There is seemingly no contradiction here, because absent witnesses the agency is ineffective. Why then is a ban needed? The Shach answers that Rama follows Rambam's view in the next seif. According to what I wrote above, however, if the messenger's version is true and the lender did use effective agency language, then there is a genuine contradiction between the parties. A formal ban or oath is therefore appropriate. See there.
