If the lender or depositor claims, 'This is not my handwriting,' no witnesses are available to authenticate it, and the custodian says, 'I recognize it as your handwriting,' then if the money was deposited with him without witnesses, or even before witnesses but only orally, he is believed upon taking a heses oath: 'I recognize this as your handwriting, and on its authority I delivered the money to your agent.' He has a migo that he could have said, 'This never occurred,' or, 'I returned it directly to you.' If the deposit was recorded in a document, he is likewise believed to say, 'I delivered it to your agent,' through a migo that he could have said it was lost through unavoidable accident, but he must take a severe oath resembling a Torah oath. If it was a documented loan, the borrower is not believed and must pay after the lender takes a severe oath that the writing is not his. {Rama: This distinction applies specifically to a loan. With a deposit, even one recorded in a document, the custodian is exempt through the migo that he could have claimed unavoidable accident. If the borrower demands that the agent swear to his claim that the money was lost through unavoidable accident, the agent must take a custodian's oath.}
