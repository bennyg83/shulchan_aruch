The agent who says that the money was lost through unavoidable accident, etc. The Shach in seif katan 32 questions why this ruling belongs here and says it is obvious that the agent, as a paid guardian, must take the guardian's oath. It appears that the novelty is that this is so even where the creditor does not demand a guardian's oath from the agent. The creditor is unconcerned with the agent, for he proceeds directly against the debtor. The debtor admits that the writing is the creditor's handwriting and says only that the creditor is making a false claim and denying his own handwriting. One might therefore ask who can compel the agent to swear. The creditor is not suing him, and to the debtor the agent can say, "You are not my litigant. You admit that the truth is with me, that I came as the creditor's agent, so you were in fact discharged when you gave the money to me. I therefore became the creditor's guardian, not yours. If the creditor now makes a false claim against you, that does not make me your guardian." The ruling therefore teaches much: the agent must nevertheless swear. Since the debtor now needs to pay, the matter reverts so that the agent is treated as the debtor's guardian. Even though, according to the debtor's claim, he is paying unjustly, he is effectively standing in the creditor's place by authorization and the agent must take the oath of a paid guardian. This is precise.
