The borrower is not believed to say he repaid the messenger where the messenger says otherwise. Maharshal argued that the borrower should be believed with hesset, relying on Rambam's nearby ruling. That comparison is mistaken. Rambam's case concerns an oral loan, where the debtor retains the ordinary payment plea. Here the messenger's role and the particular circumstances of delivery create an evidentiary structure that removes that simple migo. The borrower's denial therefore does not receive the same credibility as an ordinary claim of repayment of an oral loan.
