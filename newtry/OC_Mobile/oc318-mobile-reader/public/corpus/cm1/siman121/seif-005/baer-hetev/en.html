On his instruction. The Bach explains that the oath must specify, 'I delivered it on the basis of his instruction,' because perhaps the debtor gave the money to the messenger before the creditor's handwritten instruction reached the debtor, in which case the debtor was negligent. The Shach disagrees. Even if the debtor gave it to the messenger before the writing reached him, if at that very time the writing was already in the messenger's possession, we can see retroactively that the creditor had trusted the messenger, and in the final analysis the debtor was not negligent. The phrase 'on his instruction' merely states the actual course of events. Perhaps the Bach agrees and intended only to exclude a case where the debtor delivered the money before the creditor's writing had even reached the messenger. Although the creditor later gave him the writing, until then the messenger had not been established as trustworthy by the creditor. Even this point requires study. Shach.

A stringent oath. This means the oath ordained by the Mishnah, as in the law of one who swears and collects, discussed above in simanim 82 and 87. The oath imposed on a defendant concerning a deposit evidenced by a document, mentioned immediately before, is according to the Sema an actual Torah oath, because the defendant is exempt only through the migo that he could have claimed that the deposit was lost through circumstances beyond his control. Such a claim would require the Torah oath of bailees. The distinction between these two oaths is explained in siman 87. Maharshal also appears to understand the matter this way. The Shach rejects this. Baal HaTerumot and the Tur write that the oath is 'similar to a Torah oath,' which implies a rabbinic oath modeled on a Torah oath. This terminology is used that way above in simanim 89 and 91 and in many other places. Thus the defendant's oath concerning a documented deposit is also rabbinic, taken while holding a sacred object, as explained in siman 296, seif 2. The Shach adds that it is difficult to imagine an actual Torah oath based on a claim of 'I returned it,' since such a claim has no Torah-level oath attached to it.
