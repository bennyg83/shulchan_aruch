If it is an illness whose cure is in our hands, etc. Bach correctly writes that since Rabbi Yochanan does not accept this distinction, there is no difference between an illness with an available cure and one without. It also appears that if the husband was of sound mind when he ordered the get written, and only afterward lost his mind, the defect is not Torah law. Reish Lakish too agrees that where there is no available cure, writing the get before recovery creates only a rabbinic defect. Everything depends primarily on his being of sound mind when he gives the instruction to write, and that the instruction be heard directly from him. Even if he afterward became fully insane, it is possible that Torah law does not invalidate the get, only rabbinic law. The Yerushalmi cited by Ran also implies this. Therefore, the ruling here that with an illness whose cure is not in our hands "it is not a get," implying complete Torah invalidity, conflicts with Rambam's language, "it is invalid," and follows the Tur's stronger wording, "it is nothing." This requires practical study. Rambam's language appears correct.
