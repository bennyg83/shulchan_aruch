Say. Whenever one tells or writes another, even privately, "Send it to me through so-and-so," and the debtor sends it and it is lost, the debtor is exempt if the lender admits that he gave that instruction. By doing so the lender placed the agent in his own stead, and witnesses were created only to prevent false denial. Sema.
Witnesses. The Sema writes that the rule applies specifically where the lender said this before witnesses. Since he took the trouble to designate witnesses, we presume that he appointed the named person as an agent. If he did not designate witnesses, then even if he admits telling the proposed agent, "Tell so-and-so to send the money to me, and if he gives it to you, bring it to me," the named person is not legally the lender's agent because the lender never told the debtor to send through him, and the debtor is not discharged if the money is lost in the agent's hands. The Gemara in Bava Kamma 104a proves this explicitly. Shach.
To him. Meaning that even if the lender told the intermediary before witnesses, that does not exempt the debtor. This is clear from the Gemara and Tosafot there. Although we rule that an agent appointed before witnesses is a valid agent, one who merely points out a possible agent is not treated as an actual agent according to any view. The Gemara implies that because the lender merely says, "He is a trustworthy person," the debtor is liable only where he gave the money to that person for transport on a dangerous route. The Shach writes that the authorities do not appear to accept that limitation, since they state simply that the debtor remains liable in all circumstances. There is no difficulty. The lender's statement means: "He is generally trustworthy, but I am not appointing him as my agent and I am not personally relying upon him. If you wish to rely on him, you may do so." This is unlike the rule that one who normally deposits with a certain person cannot later say, "I do not want my deposit in another's hands," because that exception applies where he regularly entrusts property to that person, as stated in siman 291, seif 26. Here we have no such demonstrated reliance. The Shach suggests that even Tosafot may agree that a person merely indicated as possible agent leaves the debtor liable in all circumstances.
