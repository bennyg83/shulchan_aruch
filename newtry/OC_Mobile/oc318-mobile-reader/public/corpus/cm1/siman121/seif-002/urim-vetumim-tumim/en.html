But he said to him and so on, and he said, “If he did not bother me, he would witness that he would not have done so, and that he would not be able to do so with him, and that he would not be able to do so with him, and that he would not be able to see him, and that he would say to him.”
Rather, he said, "I have money," etc. The Shach in seif katan 11 cites Tosafot that this applies specifically where the person traveled through a dangerous place. He ultimately seeks to distinguish that Tosafot wrote this only according to the opinion that an agent appointed before witnesses is not considered an agent, whereas according to our ruling that such a person is an agent and only one who merely points out a possible agent is not an agent, perhaps the sender is always liable. His proof is that the poskim did not make the distinction stated by Tosafot. This is no proof. Tosafot's question concerns a deposit, which is the subject of the Gemara there, and they properly ask that it is a guardian who transferred the item to another guardian. The owner cannot say, "I trust you but not him," because he himself said that this man is trustworthy; see there. Here, however, all the poskim are discussing a loan. What relevance does the law of a guardian who transfers to another guardian have to a loan? So long as the lender did not instruct the borrower to deliver it to the agent, even if the borrower gives it to an exceptionally trustworthy person, it remains at the borrower's risk. This is obvious. The Shach wrote otherwise incidentally. Regarding a deposit, however, the matter indeed requires study whether, after the owner stated that the person is trustworthy, it remains a case of a guardian who transferred to another guardian.
