That he send through you. I proved that whenever a lender says or writes, even privately, "Send me the money through so-and-so," and the borrower sends it and it is lost, the borrower is exempt if the lender admits giving that instruction. The lender thereby placed the carrier in his own position, and witnesses are needed only to resolve falsehood.
Unless he said to him, etc. Meaning he did not say, "Send through him," but only, "If he gives it to you, bring it to me." In that case it must have been said before witnesses. Since he troubled to designate witnesses, we infer that he intended to publicize that this person was his proper agent so the borrower would rely on him. Therefore the borrower is exempt even if the lender used no technical language of agency, merely "bring it to me." But if this was not said before witnesses, even if the messenger was told, "Tell so-and-so to send it to me, and if he gives it to you bring it," there is no complete agency because the lender never said, "Send through him."
Rather, he told him, "I have money, etc." Even if said before witnesses, that is not enough to exempt the borrower because he did not say "bring it to me.
