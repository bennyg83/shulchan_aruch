The agent swears to the borrower, etc. Mahari Weil rules in a case where Reuven sent money through Shimon to Levi, his lender. Shimon claimed he delivered it to Levi through Shimon's wife, while Levi denied receiving it. Shimon swears that he delivered it to his wife. This is not improper transfer from one bailee to another, because one who deposits does so with the understanding that the bailee's wife and children may handle the property. Shimon's wife then swears that she delivered it to Levi and is exempt. Levi swears that he did not receive it, and Reuven must pay him. See siman 291.
The borrower or bailee swears to the lender. Although the lender's claim against him is technically uncertain, the supporting statement of the messenger, while not full witness testimony because the messenger himself must swear, combines with the lender's claim sufficiently to require a hesset oath.
