To the borrower or depositor, etc. Sema cites Responsa Mahari Weil. Reuven sent money to Levi, from whom he had borrowed through Shimon. Shimon claims he gave the money to his wife, and she delivered it to Levi. Levi says he received nothing. This is not treated as a custodian who transferred to another custodian, because everyone who deposits does so with the understanding that the custodian's wife may handle the property. Therefore Shimon swears that he gave the money to his wife, his wife swears that she gave it to Levi, Levi swears that he did not receive it, and Reuven must pay. See Shach. The case must be one where Shimon's wife did not know the money belonged to Reuven and was intended as repayment to Levi, but merely received an unspecified sum. Otherwise perhaps it was different money, and therefore Shimon too must swear that he gave her Reuven's money. But if she knew and swears that she received money from her husband specifically to repay Levi on Reuven's behalf and that she did so, then Shimon plainly need not swear. This is correct.
In the position of the Levites or the tongue is better than the tongue that the psalmist or the depositor has already stated:
A hesset oath. Although the claimant himself does not state certainty, the agent's statement makes the claim definite. The Shach cites Terumat HaDeshen: Reuven sent money through Shimon to Levi to buy merchandise. Shimon says, 'I gave it to Levi,' and Levi says, 'I never received it.' Shimon may require Levi to swear because Shimon definitely says he delivered the money. Reuven, however, cannot require Levi to swear merely because Levi denies receiving anything. Nor can Reuven force Shimon to sue Levi and impose an oath upon him unless Reuven exempts Shimon from his own oath. See Tumim. I concluded that Reuven can require Levi to swear. Also, even if Shimon has already sworn to Reuven, Reuven can compel Shimon to bring Levi before beit din so that an oath can be imposed. But if Shimon sees that Levi is willing to swear, Shimon may waive Levi's oath, and Reuven cannot prevent him. See there.
And he is exempt. The same rule applies even to a written loan. Once the lender admits that he instructed the borrower to send payment through the agent, the document has been weakened. Shach. If Reuven says to Levi, 'Pay me,' and Levi replies, 'I sent it to you through someone, but I do not know through whom,' Mahari Weil requires Reuven to swear. Knesset HaGedolah explains that Levi's claim is that the agent told him he completed the mission. That is treated as 'I was told,' and the witness need not personally appear in beit din. But if Levi merely says, 'I sent it and do not know through whom,' there is plainly no definite claim and no basis for this oath.
