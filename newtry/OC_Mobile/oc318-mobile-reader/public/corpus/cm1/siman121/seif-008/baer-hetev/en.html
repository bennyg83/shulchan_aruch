The agent. Responsa Mahari Weil, siman 172, rules as follows. Reuven sent money with Shimon to Levi, who had lent Reuven money. Shimon claims that he gave the money to Levi through Shimon's wife, while Levi says that he never received it. Shimon swears that he gave it to his wife. This is not considered a bailee who transferred the deposit to another bailee, because anyone who deposits with a person does so with the understanding that the person's wife and children may handle it. Shimon's wife then swears that she gave it to Levi and is exempt. Levi swears that he did not receive it, and Reuven must pay Levi. This is cited in Darkei Moshe at the end of this siman. See also siman 291, seif 23. The Shach challenges the requirement that Shimon swear that he gave the money to his wife. Since his wife swears that she gave it to Levi, Shimon is in any event exempt, and the Shach therefore questions the purpose of Shimon's oath.
The depositary. This rule applies here even to a loan evidenced by a document. The authorities state the law without qualification, indicating that it applies in all cases. One cannot argue that with a documented loan, since the messenger contradicts the debtor, the creditor should swear and collect. In the case of a shopkeeper relying on his ledger, the shopkeeper swears and collects from the employer even though the workers contradict him, because the employer authorized him to pay them. All the more so here, where the debtor is exempt because the creditor authorized him to give the money to the messenger. The statement that the messenger and the debtor swear applies when both are present and contradict one another. Otherwise only a general ban is imposed upon them, as stated in seif 10. Shach.
