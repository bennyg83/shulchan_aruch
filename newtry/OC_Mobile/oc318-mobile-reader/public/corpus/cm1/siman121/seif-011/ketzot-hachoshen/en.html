The debtor should have taken a pledge before witnesses. Nimukei Yosef explains that where the creditor directed him to deliver property to a particular person, the debtor's reliance on that person may show trust. If no such trust was expressed, the debtor who takes security or arranges a substitute transaction must protect himself with witnesses. In addition, if he later intends to claim return of the pledge, he must stipulate that it not be returned except before witnesses. Otherwise he retains a migo of return and the evidentiary structure changes. The rule therefore depends not only on taking the pledge before witnesses but on preserving proof of the entire collateral transaction.
