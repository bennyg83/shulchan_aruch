You shall bring. We find a distinction between the terms "bring" and "give" only regarding acquisition for another, where some hold that "bring" is not equivalent to immediate acquisition while "give" is. Regarding an oath there is no distinction. So writes the Rosh in a responsum. See also siman 125. Shach.
Where. The Tur and Shulchan Aruch state a similar rule in siman 291: an unpaid bailee who says, "I do not know where I placed it," is considered negligent. Sema.
To pay. Nimukei Yosef explains that although the lender could ordinarily exempt himself by claiming unavoidable loss, such losses are uncommon. According to this reasoning, since we rule in siman 72 that a lender holding collateral is only an unpaid bailee, and we do not extract money in a legal doubt, the agent should be exempt because the lender could also exempt himself by claiming theft or loss. The Rama's ruling therefore requires study. Perhaps he understands that theft and loss are likewise uncommon. The chapter HaMafkid somewhat supports this, where unavoidable loss, theft, and similar events are treated similarly regarding the bailee's ability to exempt himself. Still, it is difficult to know how the Rama derives the rule, because Nimukei Yosef may follow his own view that a lender holding collateral is a paid bailee. Perhaps the Rama reasons that since our classification of the lender as an unpaid bailee rests only on a legal doubt, one cannot say that he could safely have claimed theft or loss. He might fear that beit din would rule him liable, or that the debtor might seize property even before witnesses, which can be effective in a legal doubt. The matter requires study. Shach.
