For he should have taken a pledge before witnesses. See Tumim. Although we rule that when the pledge is worth more than the debt the creditor is a gratuitous custodian of the excess, and thus even if he had taken the pledge before witnesses the debtor would still have trusted him because he could claim that it was stolen or lost, I wrote there two answers. There is a practical difference where he took the pledge in the presence of one witness and denies the matter in contradiction to that witness. See there.
