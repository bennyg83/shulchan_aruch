To the borrower or to the depositor. See Shach seif katan 40, who reads instead "to the lender or to the owner of the deposit." The principal reason Shach gives in seif katan 15 for combining claims so as to create a definite enough allegation to impose an oath is that the claimant knows his money was certainly in the other party's possession and was then lost or spoiled. Here we do not know whether the money ever came into the messenger's hand. Nevertheless, because the messenger accepted the agency and because the debtor can exempt himself based on the messenger's statement, it is treated as though we know that the creditor's debt came into the messenger's possession and was lost through him. He must therefore swear when the claims combine to make the allegation definite, just as where the claimant's money was certainly in his hands. This differs from Shach's case in seif katan 42 of one who sends money to Levi to purchase merchandise, where we do not know that anything ever came into Levi's hands, so no oath is required. As to a debtor who paid the messenger but did not take the debt instrument from him, see what I wrote in siman 58, Biurim seif katan 8. See also Shach seif katan 42, where there is a textual error. It should read: "unless he exempts him entirely from an oath, since if one of them swears, one of the oaths will certainly be false." The word "between" is erroneous.
