To the woman. Many authorities disagree, and the Mechaber himself cites their view in Even HaEzer 100, seif 3. The Shach wonders why he rules here simply like the Rambam. The author of the Baer Heitev says that in his work on Even HaEzer he gave a sufficient answer. Here the case is where the woman wrote to the second purchaser, "I have no claim or dealings with you," in a manner fully effective so that she cannot say, "I acted only to please my husband." The first purchaser can therefore say to her, "You caused your own loss." In Even HaEzer, by contrast, the case is not one where the wife executed such an effective waiver after her husband, but where she merely consented to his act. Ordinarily she could claim that she consented only to please her husband. However, because she did not consent to the first sale, the law prevents her from collecting from the second purchaser. In that setting it cannot be said that she knowingly caused her own loss, since she may have believed her consent had no legal effect. Therefore the Mechaber records two opinions there.
