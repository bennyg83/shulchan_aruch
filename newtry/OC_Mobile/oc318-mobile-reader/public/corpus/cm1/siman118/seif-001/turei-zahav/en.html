He cannot seize it from the first purchaser. The Tur writes that if the creditor wrote to the first purchaser, “I have no claim against you,” he collects from the second. This teaches that this is so only where he has no exclusive lien upon the first field. If that field was expressly designated as an apotiki, with the stipulation that payment would come only from it, and he then waived his claim against its purchaser, he cannot collect from the second purchaser. Even free property remaining with the debtor would not be collectible, because his lien was confined to the designated field. He caused his own loss by impairing that lien and therefore cannot seize elsewhere.
