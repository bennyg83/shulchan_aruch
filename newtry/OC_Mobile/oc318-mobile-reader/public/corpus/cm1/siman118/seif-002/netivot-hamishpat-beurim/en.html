And the creditor collects it from the first purchaser. See Shach seif katan 2 through his statement that this case is different because the creditor collects by virtue of the debtor. The principal reason for the rule of "what did the first sell to the second" is explained by the Ran in Perek HaCholetz. Since the creditor could not have collected from the first purchaser, he cannot collect from the second purchaser who acquired only the first purchaser's rights. That reasoning applies where the first purchaser voluntarily sold his rights to the second. Here, however, the property was taken from the purchaser against his will by a creditor acting by virtue of the debtor. The purchaser did not transfer anything to that creditor and the creditor does not derive his right from the purchaser, so the principle of "what did the first sell to the second" does not apply. The Shach's challenge to the Sema therefore presents no difficulty. In siman 119, where returning the document changes the result, the purchaser benefits from the return, because at present the creditor would collect superior-quality land from him, whereas once the document is restored the creditor will collect inferior land. Here, by contrast, the purchaser gains nothing from returning the document, because it makes no difference whether one claimant or the other collects from him. Tumim explains the matter similarly.
