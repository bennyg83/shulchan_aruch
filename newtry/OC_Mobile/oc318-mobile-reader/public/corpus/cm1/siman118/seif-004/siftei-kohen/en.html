Likewise, if the field acquired by the first purchaser was, and so forth. Bach explains that the first field was also made a specifically designated lien to the later creditor. This is not convincing. If the earlier creditor seized that field from the first purchaser, the later creditor cannot return against the first purchaser after seizing the second purchaser’s field. Rabbenu Yerucham states that where an earlier creditor seized the designated field, the later creditor has no recourse. It is not reasonable to distinguish between recourse against the debtor and recourse against the purchaser; if anything, the opposite distinction would be more plausible.
