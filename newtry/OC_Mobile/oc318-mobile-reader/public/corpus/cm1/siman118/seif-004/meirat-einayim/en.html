An apotiki to another creditor. This means an explicit apotiki, where he told the creditor, "You shall have no payment except from this." According to Maggid Mishneh's explanation of Rambam, cited by the Mechaber in siman 117, perhaps even an ordinary apotiki is sufficient if, at the time of collection, no unencumbered property remains in the borrower's hands.
To another earlier creditor. The Tur uses this wording. Since it later says that the later creditor forecloses, "earlier" here means earlier relative to the second creditor. This is difficult, for even if the apotiki creditor were chronologically later, he should still collect that field because it was designated to him. I explained that although he may be later in date, he is called "earlier" in practical order of collection, because the other creditor cannot initially take the field: when the apotiki was created the borrower still had other land, and that other creditor weakened himself by writing away his claims to it. His only remedy may be to allow the apotiki creditor to collect first and then recoup from the first creditor's position. In that practical sense the terms earlier and later fit.
