An apotiki. This means an express apotiki, where he told him, 'You shall have payment only from this property,' as stated at the beginning of siman 117. According to what was stated there, however, even with an ordinary apotiki, if at the time of collection there is no unencumbered property in the debtor's possession, then although such property existed when the apotiki was created, since it is no longer unencumbered he may collect from this apotiki. Accordingly, here too the case may be understood even as an ordinary apotiki. Sema.
Earlier. The Sema writes that from this expression, and from the later statement that 'the later creditor seizes,' it is evident that this creditor is earlier than the second creditor. This requires study, for even if he were later than the second creditor, he would still collect from the designated property because it was made an apotiki for him. One must therefore say that he is called 'earlier' only with respect to the order of collection. The other creditor, although chronologically earlier, cannot collect because when the apotiki was created the debtor still owned another field, and he caused his own loss by writing to the second creditor that he had no claim or dispute against him. He has no remedy unless he first allows the other creditor to collect and then collects from that creditor. In the context of a compromise, therefore, the terms 'earlier' and 'later' are apt. The Taz resolves the matter differently and explains that the text specifically means that the holder of the apotiki is earlier. See there.
