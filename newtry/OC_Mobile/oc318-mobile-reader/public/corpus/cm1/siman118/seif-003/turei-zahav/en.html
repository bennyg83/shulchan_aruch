(Seif 4) An apotiki for a creditor. Sema, seif katan 9, asks about the case where the apotiki creditor is later in priority and gives a very strained answer. It appears to me that if the apotiki creditor were later, the earlier creditor could say: all the debtor's assets are subject to my lien, and I choose to seize from the first purchaser. Although that purchaser could say, “I left you a place from which to collect,” the earlier creditor can answer that he will persuade the purchaser not to invoke that protection. The rule of “I left you a place” was enacted for the purchaser's benefit, and the purchaser may waive it, as the Tur writes in siman 119. In any event the apotiki fields would leave the first purchaser's possession. Once the purchaser does not object, the later creditor cannot take the apotiki from the earlier creditor, because all the debtor's fields are subject to the earlier lien and an apotiki creditor remains only a creditor, since the debtor may discharge him with money. Proof appears from the beginning of siman 117, which states that if an expressly designated apotiki was mortgaged afterward, the apotiki creditor collects it even when free property exists. It does not state the greater novelty that even if it was mortgaged before designation the later apotiki creditor collects. Necessarily, the apotiki creditor cannot seize while free property remains available to the earlier creditor, since the land still bears the status of free property and the apotiki holder is not an absolute owner.
