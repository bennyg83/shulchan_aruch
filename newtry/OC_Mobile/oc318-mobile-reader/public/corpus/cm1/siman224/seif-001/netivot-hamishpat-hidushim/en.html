The owner of the donkey must bring proof. The reason is that here it was found and here it is presumed to have arisen. This applies specifically where it stands in the possession of the donkey's owner, but if it stands in a meadow, the owner of the cow must bring proof (Sema).
And there are those who say that the owner of the cow must bring proof. They maintain that since the uncertainty arose after meshichah, when the item was already in the domain of the cow's owner, and the presumption of continued life also supports the donkey's owner, the cow's owner must therefore bring proof. This reasoning applies specifically here, where the uncertainty arose to the detriment of the cow's owner while the item was in his domain. But if the uncertainty arose to his advantage, such as above in siman 223 concerning one who exchanges a cow for a donkey and the cow gives birth, we do not apply the rule that every uncertainty arising in one's domain is charged against him, Tosafos, Ketubot 75. See Beurim: the rule is not limited to death; the same applies if the item was stolen or lost. See there also regarding two partners who divided property and afterward a deficiency was found in the share of one of them.
