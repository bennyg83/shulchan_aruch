One who exchanges a cow for a donkey, or purchases an animal later found perforated in the reticulum. It contains two seifim.
Whenever a doubt arises while property is in a person's possession, that person bears the burden of proof. Thus, if one exchanges a cow for a donkey, the owner of the donkey performs meshichah on the cow, and before the owner of the cow performs meshichah on the donkey the donkey dies, the owner of the donkey must prove that it was alive when he took possession of the cow. The same rule applies in similar cases. Rama: Some say the owner of the cow bears the burden of proof, because upon his acquisition the donkey became his wherever it stood, and the doubt arose in his possession. If he cannot prove his claim, the seller swears that he did not know of the defect and is exempt. Reuven exchanged a horse with Shimon for wine. After Shimon took the horse, a non-Jew seized it, claiming it had been stolen from him. Shimon wishes to retain the wine. If Reuven admits the horse was stolen, the transaction was erroneous and Shimon may retain what he holds. If Reuven denies it, we assume the non-Jew's claim is false, and Shimon must deliver the wine to Reuven.
