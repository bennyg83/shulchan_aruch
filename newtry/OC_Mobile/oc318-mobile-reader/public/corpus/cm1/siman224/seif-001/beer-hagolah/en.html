Alef) This is the language of the Rambam, chapter 20 of Hilkhot Mekhirah, halachah 14. The Maggid Mishneh cites the statement of Rav Yehudah in the name of Shmuel in Ketubot 76a and writes that the Rif inclines to this view. The Kesef Mishneh writes that we have only the Ran's explanation in the name of the Baal HaMaor: Rav Yehudah holds that the person in whose property the uncertainty arose bears the burden of proof, and the donkey is considered the property of the donkey's owner. According to Rami bar Yechezkel on 76b, however, because the doubt did not arise in his domain but in the cow owner's domain, the cow owner must bring proof, based on the principle that the defect is presumed to have arisen where it is presently found. Where the donkey died in the donkey owner's house, all agree that he bears the burden of proof. The Maggid Mishneh cites some commentators who say that this applies specifically where the donkey died in its owner's domain and the owner does not make a definite claim that it was alive at the time the cow was drawn. But if he makes such a definite claim, or if the donkey was in a marsh, the cow owner bears the burden of proof. The Rashba and Ramban write that the Rif's failure to distinguish these cases requires study.
