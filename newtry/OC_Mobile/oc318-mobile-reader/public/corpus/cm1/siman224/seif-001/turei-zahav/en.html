(Seif 1: whoever's domain the doubt arose in, and so forth.) This wording appears in the Gemara, but the Rif and Rambam disagree with Tosafot and the Rosh regarding its interpretation. The Rif and Rambam explain that it refers literally to the physical domain, meaning the place in which the animal was located, so that the loss falls upon the owner of the donkey if it died in his house. The alternative opinion, that of Tosafot and the Rosh, explains that it does not refer to the physical location but to the legal domain of the owner of the property, namely the owner of the cow, into whose ownership the donkey entered from the moment the donkey's owner drew the cow. One should not ask that, according to this, in the preceding siman, seif 1, where there is doubt as to when the cow gave birth, if the calf is in the possession of the cow's owner, the donkey's owner should have to bring proof, since the doubt arose after he drew the donkey and the cow had already been acquired by the donkey's owner, so the doubt arose in his domain and the burden of proof should rest upon him. Tosafot already addressed this in chapter HaMadir: “One must distinguish between a doubt that arose to one's advantage, as there, and a doubt that arose to one's detriment, as here.” The Sema in seif katan 3 raised this question and strained to find an answer, but these words of Tosafot escaped his notice.
