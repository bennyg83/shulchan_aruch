(Seif 5, he later said to us, “Our father told us that he repaid it.”) The Tur brings two opinions. The first holds that if the heirs claim that their father said he repaid the debt after the admission, they are believed in all cases. If they claim that he said he had repaid it before the admission, there is a distinction. If he had said, “Give,” they are not believed, because in his will he definitively instructed them to pay the creditor. Ramah holds that where they say he repaid before the admission, they are never believed when witnesses heard the testator state that he owed one hundred to so-and-so, for we are then witnesses, as it were, that he did not make the admission in error. Although the heirs have a migo that they could have claimed he repaid afterward, that is a migo against witnesses. If there are no witnesses to the testamentary admission, however, this migo is effective. If they say their father stated that he repaid after the admission, there is a further distinction. If he had said, “Give,” they are not believed, because he entrusted them with payment, and presumably he did not himself pay. If he did not say, “Give,” they are believed that he instructed them that he repaid after the admission. As to the halacha, since the Mechaber’s words do not establish a clear ruling, as the Sema notes in seif katan 11, it appears to me that we should rule like Ramah. In siman 127, seif 13, the Rama rules according to this principle, that once a person admitted before witnesses he is no longer believed to say that his admission was mistaken. We must therefore follow Ramah’s explanation here. This appears entirely clear to me.
