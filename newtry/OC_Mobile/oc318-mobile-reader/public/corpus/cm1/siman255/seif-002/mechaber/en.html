If a person on his deathbed says, "I owe one hundred to so-and-so," then if he says, "Give it," the heirs must give it. If he does not say, "Give it," they do not, because he may have said that he owed the money only so that people would not say that his heirs possess great wealth. Therefore, if he spoke in the manner of a genuine admission and there is no reason to suspect such a device, the heirs give it even though he did not say, "Give it." Likewise, if he acknowledged in his own handwriting that all his property belongs to a certain person, it is a valid admission, and we do not say in such a case that a person is merely trying to avoid making his children appear wealthy.
