And we do not say in such a case, etc. See Baer Heitev and Responsa Chatam Sofer, Choshen Mishpat siman 137. He rules concerning his case that all wills found among the deceased’s papers, and even a will written in his notebook, are like a will tied to his thigh and are ineffective, as explained in siman 250, seif 25. The notebook stated, “My wife is entitled to the one thousand gold coins that she brought into the marriage,” although it was known that she had brought nothing because she married him in poverty. The questioner wondered whether this nevertheless creates liability as an admission, or whether the deceased wrote it only because he feared that a secular court might otherwise refuse to award her that amount and therefore falsely described it as property she had brought in. Chatam Sofer agrees that this assessment is persuasive. Once the will itself is invalid, the purported admission may also fail. He adds that if the deceased had orally said, “My wife is entitled to one thousand gold coins,” it would resemble the deathbed statement “I owe a maneh to so-and-so,” where we say that he may have spoken merely to avoid appearing wealthy, as explained at the end of Bava Batra and in siman 255, even where the other party holds a document. The fact that the statement here was in the deceased’s handwriting is subject to major dispute. The Rama in siman 81, seif 17, together with the Sema, Bach, Maharam Alshich, and Divrei Rivot, holds that writing is no stronger and may likewise have been made to avoid appearing wealthy. The Shach alone disagrees, especially regarding a private notebook where that motive seems weak because no one ordinarily sees hidden private writings. Yet it is difficult to extract money from orphans on the basis of the Shach alone. Moreover, here the deceased may indeed have written it for the very purpose of affecting how others would administer the estate after his death. Joined with the strong factual assessment noted above, one should not extract the money from the orphans who possess the inheritance. It is therefore preferable to compromise the dispute.
