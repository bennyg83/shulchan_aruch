And he later told us, etc. The witnesses are believed. This implies that they are believed in every circumstance, in accordance with Rabbi Yeshayah's view cited by the Tur, and not merely according to the Ramah's limitation. The Sema, seif katan 19, rules likewise, and this is the principal view. See what I wrote above in siman 81, seif 23, seif katan 60.
“And I swear to you, and I do not know that your father did not say to them, and I do not swear to him, and that he did not speak to him.”
But if he said, 'Give,' etc., they are not believed. The Sema, seif katan 20, questions this ruling because the Tur writes in the name of Rabbi Yeshayah that the limitation applies only when the witnesses say the debt had been paid before the admission. If they say it was paid afterward, they are believed even though he initially said, 'Give.' The Mechaber's unqualified language appears to follow Rabbi Yeshayah in the first clause and the Ramah in the second. In my view, no inference should be drawn from the Mechaber's wording, for he merely copied the Rambam and the Gemara, and whatever interpretation applies to the Gemara applies to his words. Indeed, the Hagahot Ashri implies that even if the heirs do not claim that their father said he repaid, the court raises the possibility that he repaid afterward, unless they remained with him continuously from the admission until death. This accords with the general rule above at the beginning of siman 108 and elsewhere, that collection from heirs requires an admission made during the final illness close enough to death that later repayment is not a realistic concern. This is the principal ruling.
