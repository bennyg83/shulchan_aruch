The heirs are not believed to say, "We paid," etc. See what I wrote at the end of siman 253, with proofs and sources, that this must concern specifically a document recording an acknowledgment of debt by a shechiv mera. With an ordinary will, the heirs are believed to say "we paid," as Rama writes there and in siman 41:3.
Since the will document is in his hand, etc. Presumably the heirs know that the beneficiary holds the will document, so the principle applies: if they truly paid, why is the document still in his hand? As explained there, we are not concerned for collusion where the heirs themselves admit they did not pay. The beneficiary may therefore collect from purchasers like any creditor collecting through a promissory note. The Tur states the law here because of the latter clause, teaching that where the will document is not in the beneficiary's hand, we are concerned for collusion.
If they say they did not pay, etc. Meaning, even if they themselves admit that they did not pay, they are not believed to defeat the beneficiary's claim.
