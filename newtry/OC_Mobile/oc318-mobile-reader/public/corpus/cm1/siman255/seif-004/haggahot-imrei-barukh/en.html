(Netivot HaMishpat, there, s.v. 'According to what I wrote, etc., these matters are resolved.') Note: One who studies Sefer HaMilchamot will see that the view cited there explains the case as a dying person who consecrated his property to take effect after death and not immediately. In such a case even an oral creditor collects, because hekdesh acquires upon the dying person's death only what remains after payment of what he owes others, whether the debt is written or oral. According to this premise, those authorities had to interpret differently the Gemara's statement that a person does not collude against hekdesh, as explained there in Sefer HaMilchamot.
