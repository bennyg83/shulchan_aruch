One who consecrates, etc.; when does this apply, etc. This is Rav Huna's teaching in Bava Batra there. Shevuot 42b concludes that this applies to a deathly ill person, but a healthy person, etc., and he collects from consecrated property with an oath like every creditor. This is the meaning of 'even if he was, etc.' Arakhin 23a similarly concludes that the case is not of a healthy person, etc., but concerns release by inquiry, etc. See Tosafot there, s.v. 'through inquiry.' Kesef Mishneh erred here, for his remarks there address the beginning of Rambam's ruling that regarding divorce the halacha follows Rabbi Eliezer. Because of Tosafot's question in the cited passage, he ruled like Rabbi Eliezer in accordance with the conclusion in Shevuot, since the Mishna there follows him. He understands that in the conclusion Rabbi Eliezer's reasoning is not that he follows Beit Shammai, for we do not rule like Beit Shammai, but that consecration may be undone through inquiry, as Rambam writes. According to Tosafot, however, the halacha follows Rabbi Yehoshua, and this is the view of all the decisors.
