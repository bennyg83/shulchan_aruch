If he said, "A maneh belonging to so-and-so is in my possession," etc. Meaning, he said this as a complete acknowledgment before that person, in a manner where we cannot advance for the heirs the claim that he spoke merely so as not to appear wealthy. The Tur says in this case, "they demanded payment from him and he acknowledged, saying, etc.," because the Tur follows his position that even a complete acknowledgment is ineffective without "you are my witnesses." The Mechaber follows Rambam that it is effective without those words, as explained above, and therefore omits "they demanded payment and he acknowledged." The next seif likewise need not be limited to a case where they demanded payment; a full acknowledgment even volunteered by him suffices. The Mechaber uses the explicit word "acknowledged" in the next seif but not here only because he copied Rambam. Rambam, chapter 10 of Hilchot Zechiyah, law 8, writes, "A shechiv mera who acknowledged that he has a maneh belonging to so-and-so, and the orphans said, 'Our father later told us that he paid it,' etc." In law 10 he writes the present case, "A maneh belonging to so-and-so is in my possession, and the orphans say, 'We paid,' etc." He did not repeat the word "acknowledged" because he relied on the preceding law. The Tur reversed the order according to the Gemara, while the Mechaber retained Rambam's wording inside the Tur's order. This is clear, unlike the Ir Shushan, whose explanations in these two seifim are strained. In seif 4 he says the case is where no demand was made, and that although the heirs could have claimed he spoke merely so as not to appear wealthy, the claimant brings witnesses to a complete acknowledgment. In seif 5 he says it is where they demanded payment and he acknowledged. The Gemara derives both rulings from one statement of Rava and they concern the same type of acknowledgment, as I explained in the Derishah and as is clear from the Maggid Mishneh and Beit Yosef.
How would they know that they were obligated, etc.? Meaning, although their father made a full acknowledgment, so long as he did not say "give," heirs commonly think perhaps he erred in his acknowledgment. A similar distinction appears above at the end of siman 79:13 between beit din saying "go give him," which makes the obligation clear, and saying merely "you are liable to give him." See there.
But if he said "give," etc. Rashba writes in Responsa 1046 that if a shechiv mera said, "Give a maneh to so-and-so, a minor," then even if the minor is still in a cradle and has a father or guardian, the heir is believed to say, "I paid them." Darkei Moshe cites this.
They are believed in every case. Their reasoning is that once he acknowledged the debt fully and told them to give it, they presumably knew they were obligated to pay and are believed when they say they did so.
