The Tur, however, writes that the view of the Raavad and Rabbi Yeshayah is more reasonable, namely that the witnesses are believed in every form. Rashba, cited by the Maggid Mishneh in chapter 10 of the Laws of Acquisition and Gift, agrees. The Maggid Mishneh states that Rabbi Yosef ibn Migash follows Rashbam, but his words do not prove this. Rabbi Yosef ibn Migash may have been explaining only the Gemara's initial suggestion, comparing it to the distinction between 'Go and give him' and 'You are liable to give him.' Yet the Gemara concludes in Bava Metzia that even after the latter formulation a claim of payment may be believed. The Rif and Rosh omit the Gemara's proposed reversal, which also indicates that in the final ruling the witnesses are believed in every case. This is also the view of Baal HaMaor and Tosafot. The preceding Tosafot passage that implies otherwise should therefore be deleted as following the rejected approach of Mahariach. The principal ruling is that the witnesses are believed in every case.
