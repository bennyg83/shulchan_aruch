Seif 1. See Shach, seif katan 5, citing authorities who hold that retraction from a consecration within the time of immediate speech is not effective. I find this difficult from Shevuot 11, in the sugya of communal daily offerings no longer needed. The Gemara challenges the principle that the intent of beit din conditions them from Rava's statement that one who consecrates a male animal for its value nevertheless confers bodily sanctity. The question assumes that an express limitation attached to the declaration should function like a prior condition. According to the Shach, however, once the consecration was pronounced bodily sanctity would attach immediately and a limitation added a moment later could not help. The Gemara therefore appears to prove that retraction within immediate speech is effective even for consecration. Yet according to Beit Yosef, Even HaEzer siman 38, there is a distinction between true reconsideration within immediate speech and one continuous utterance. In a single utterance, a qualification is effective in every area. The authorities who deny retraction in consecration can therefore refer to a change of mind, while Rava's phrase, "a male for its value," is one continuous statement. This resolves the proof. The Shach further seeks to distinguish terumah from consecration in order to reconcile statements of Rabbi Eliezer. Elsewhere I have written that this is difficult, because Nazir 8 implies no such distinction. In my opinion one can explain the matter through the view of Mabit, cited by Maharit, that the need for a formal opening or regret in release from vows is rabbinic. On the Torah level release may operate more broadly. Thus even according to Beit Shammai, the rule that consecration is not subject to release may be rabbinic, out of concern that the petitioner is falsely presenting grounds for release. This explains why one may still fear collusion in monetary matters while, regarding the Torah prohibition of possessing chametz on Pesach, the Torah-level possibility of release may still be relevant. In a place of a Torah mitzvah the Sages may not have maintained their restriction. There is much more to discuss, but this is not the place.
Regarding the Shach's statement that once property has reached the treasurer, release is ineffective. See my writings on Orach Chaim, laws of Pesach, where I challenged the Shach and brought evidence against his position, and also discussed the underlying rule that release is effective for terumah.
