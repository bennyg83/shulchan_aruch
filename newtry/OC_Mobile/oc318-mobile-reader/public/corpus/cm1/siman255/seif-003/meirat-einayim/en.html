He is not believed to say it after he consecrated the property. Meaning, even immediately afterward, within the time of immediate speech and while they are still engaged in the same matter. Only regarding a sick person does the Mechaber write below that in such a case he is believed.
He cannot say, etc. Even if he seeks to give that same person the money or article and say it already belongs to him, we do not listen, for the reason the Mechaber states: perhaps they are colluding. The lien of hekdesh is no weaker than a private lien. One whose property is already encumbered cannot, through his own statement, impose a loss on others holding liens against his property.
Even if there was a document, etc., but only in the following manner, etc. If the document is not authenticated, he collects nothing through it. If it is authenticated, the holder must swear that the amount written in the document remains unpaid.
At the time he consecrated it. Meaning, after the moment of consecration but still within immediate speech and while they are engaged in the same matter. The Kesef Mishneh explains Rambam this way and thereby answers the Raavad's objection that if he said it literally at the same time as the consecration, even a healthy person should be believed. It is somewhat difficult, however, that on this reading the phrase "after he consecrated" in the first clause and in the latter clause do not mean the same thing. In the first it means immediately afterward while still in the same discussion, while in the latter it means after they had already ceased discussing it. The case must also be one where the consecration was made in a manner from which he could not retract even upon recovery. Otherwise, since we rule that whenever a shechiv mera could retract upon recovery he also has power to retract before recovery and give the property to another, the same should apply to hekdesh, according to the Mechaber above in siman 250:3.
If he did not say "give," they do not give, etc. Although the explanation "he said it merely so as not to appear wealthy" does not relate to his sons, because in any event the property will not belong to them but to hekdesh or to the named person, he may still have said it so as not to make himself appear wealthy during his lifetime, lest people say of him that he makes himself poor while possessing great wealth. This is the Gemara's explanation.
He takes it from hekdesh because of the will. Meaning, without an oath.
He collects from the redeemer and not from hekdesh. See the end of siman 117, where this is explained in the Tur and Mechaber. See the Ir Shushan, who writes several confused points in this seif. First, he says that one who consecrates all his property cannot retract even within immediate speech, and therefore "even if within immediate speech," etc. This is incorrect. We rule that immediate speech is equivalent to speech in every matter except idolatry, kiddushin, and divorce, as stated in chapter Yesh Nochalin and below in siman 279:5. Perhaps he confused kiddushin with hekdesh. Second, he writes that if the man is sick and did not say "give," the named person receives nothing because the money belongs to the heirs and the dying man said it merely so as not to make his sons appear wealthy. I do not understand this, because the property is not going to the heirs at all, since the case concerns one who consecrated all his property. Nor should he have said "so as not to make his sons appear wealthy," when they receive nothing. Rather, it is "so as not to make himself appear wealthy," as stated in the Gemara, Rif, and Rosh. The Tur's phrase "so as not to make his sons appear wealthy" was written regarding a different case, where he instructed his sons, as explained in the Perishah. Third, the Ir Shushan writes that since at the time of consecration he stated the debt "all at once," the hekdesh never took effect on that portion. This too is imprecise. The matter depends not on his statement but on an authenticated document already in the creditor's possession, which he has no power to consecrate away. Even if the document is not authenticated, once he says "give," he thereby validates the document against himself and cannot consecrate the property. The authorities write this, and for this reason even if he said "give" after the time of immediate speech, the property is given to the named person, because authentication of documents is rabbinic. It appears the Ir Shushan wrote as he did because he thought hekdesh removes prior liens. That too is incorrect except where he consecrates bodily sanctity, such as an ox pledged to a creditor that he then consecrates to the altar, where there is no redemption, as the Tur and Mechaber write above in siman 117. Nowadays, where there is no bodily sanctity, ordinary hekdesh is monetary sanctity and does not remove prior liens.
