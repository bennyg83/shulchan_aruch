After he consecrated it. Baer Heitev cites the Shach’s strong objection that the authorities overlooked an explicit passage in Bava Kamma. Birkat HaZevach to Menachot 81 raises the same difficulty. Mishneh LaMelech, Hilkhot Maaseh HaKorbanot chapter 15, resolves the Rambam and concludes that even in consecration and substitution, retraction within tokh kedei dibbur is not equivalent to immediate retraction, and that the Bava Kamma passage is rejected. Machaneh Ephraim, laws of charity, siman 8, offers another answer, which Shaar HaMelech rejects and replaces. Melo HaRo’im, entry “tokh kedei dibbur,” discusses their arguments and suggests a further resolution. Ketzot HaChoshen likewise treats the matter at length and concludes that the Rambam and those who agree with him are correct that retraction within tokh kedei dibbur does not help for temurah or hekdesh. He adds, however, that our contemporary charitable consecrations are neither bodily sanctity nor Temple treasury sanctity but gifts to the poor, as explained in siman 95. With a vow to the poor, one does not apply the rule that a declaration to sacred property is equivalent to delivery to an ordinary person as a formal acquisition. Rather, the obligation is to fulfill the vow because Scripture includes charity within “that which leaves your lips.” It should therefore be no stronger than a nazirite vow, where retraction within tokh kedei dibbur is effective. Responsa VeShav HaKohen, siman 18, nevertheless concludes that according to the Rambam and Tosafot, who disallow retraction within tokh kedei dibbur from hekdesh, the same applies to charity because the declaration itself is treated like delivery.
