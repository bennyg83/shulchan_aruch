The Sema's general rule is that with a healthy person we are concerned for collusion, so he is not believed even while they are still engaged in the same matter, and the claimant may collect only with an authenticated deed and an oath. Within the time required for an immediate retraction, however, the owner is believed because he could still retract. With one on his deathbed, where the consecration was made in a manner that he could retract, as explained above in siman 250 seif 3, he is believed in whatever he says. But if he consecrated it in a manner that he could not retract even if he recovered, he is believed only while they are still engaged in the same matter, and afterward he is not believed. The Shach disagrees with this entire seif and concludes as follows. Within the time required for an immediate retraction, he is believed. After that time, if he says, I hold one maneh belonging to so and so as a deposit, he is believed. Even if he speaks without specifying deposit, we likewise assume that his intent was certainly to a deposited maneh, and for that reason he is not believed merely because he could have sought release from his vow, even where release is possible, since such a migo stands against the chazakah that whatever is in a person's possession belongs to him. If he admits to an oral loan of one maneh, even where he has a migo, the creditor cannot collect from hekdesh, which is treated like encumbered property. But if he admits that the other person holds a deed against him, he is believed through the migo that he could have sought release from the vow. If he consecrated the property with the consent of the public, delivered it to the treasurer, or transferred it to hekdesh through a kinyan so that release is no longer possible, he is not believed. All this concerns a healthy person. One on his deathbed, however, is believed even where the consecration cannot be released, even if the claimant has no deed at all, because we presume that his intent is to a deposit. But if he says it is a loan, the claimant cannot collect unless he also acknowledges a deed. The case is where he said, Give it, or used language of a full admission, or there is an authenticated deed, for in such cases the concern that he spoke merely to avoid appearing wealthy does not apply. He is then believed even to exempt the other person from an oath. All this applies where it is known that the debt was not paid after the will. Otherwise, we plead on behalf of the heirs that perhaps it was paid. Against other creditors and purchasers, even one on his deathbed has the same law as a healthy person. If he wishes to seek release from his vow after saying, I hold one maneh belonging to so and so, release is granted even if he vowed with the consent of the public, because payment of a debt is a mitzvah.
