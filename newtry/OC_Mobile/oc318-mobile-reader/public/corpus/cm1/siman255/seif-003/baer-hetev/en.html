Afterward. The Sema explains this to mean immediately after the period of immediate speech, while they are still engaged in that same matter, contrary to the Levush, who writes that even within the period of immediate speech he cannot retract. We ordinarily rule throughout the Torah that a statement made within the period of immediate speech is treated as part of the prior statement, except in the cases of idolatry, blasphemy, betrothal, and divorce. The Shach writes that he found the Rambam explicitly agreeing with the Levush. The same implication appears in the Baal HaTerumot, and Tosafot in Menachot, Rabbi Ovadiah of Bartenura, and Tosafot Yom Tov write likewise. He nevertheless finds this very difficult and writes that all of these authorities overlooked an explicit Gemara in the chapter Merubah. He concludes that, as a matter of practical law, consecration should be treated like other legal statements. See there.
Collusion. For this reason, even if he now wishes to give the money or object to that person and say that it actually belongs to him, we do not accept his statement. So writes the Sema. The Shach discusses this subject at great length. One who wishes to understand his position should examine his conclusion and practical ruling there. See also Responsa Maharit, simanim 22 and 116.
The will. Meaning, without an oath. Although there is no ordinary concern here that he made the admission merely so that his children should not appear wealthy, since in any event the property will not belong to his children but to sacred use or to the named person, there can still be a concern that he made the statement so that he himself should not appear wealthy. During his lifetime he had conducted himself as a poor man and did not wish people to say of him, 'There is one who pretends to be poor yet has great wealth,' which would be dishonorable to him. Sema.
