Zayin) This is the language of the Rambam, chapter 7 of Hilkhot Arakhin VaCharamin, halachah 18.
Chet) The Kesef Mishneh derives this from Rav Huna's statement: if a deathly ill person consecrated all his property and said, "I owe one hundred to so-and-so," we do not suspect collusion, Arakhin 23a. This implies that the rule was said specifically of a deathly ill person, whereas a healthy person can collude. Rav Huna's ruling is halachah, because no Amora disputes him. The Gemara's later conclusion involving release of consecrated property does not reject the first version that establishes the case according to Rav Huna; it rejects only the proposed version that all agree regarding a healthy person. Although Rabbi Yehoshua was not followed and the ruling accords with Rabbi Chanina, even though he was from Beit Shammai, this can be explained because Rabban Shimon ben Gamliel in the Mishnah supports that position, and we generally follow Rabban Shimon ben Gamliel in our Mishnah.
Tet) The statement of Rav Huna mentioned above.
Yod) Regarding the Raavad's objection that at the very moment of consecration any person should be believed, the Kesef Mishneh answers that this is true only within tokh kedei dibbur. After that time, a healthy person is not believed, but a deathly ill person remains believed so long as he is still discussing the matter of the consecration.
Kaf) Even if he has no deed at all, since he made the statement at the time he consecrated the property.
Lamed) The Kesef Mishneh explains that consecrated property is no stronger than ordinary property with respect to a person's inability to create a loss for others merely through his own admission.
Mem) This is explained at the end of siman 117 in the gloss.
