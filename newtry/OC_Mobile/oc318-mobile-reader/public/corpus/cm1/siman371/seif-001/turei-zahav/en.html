(Seif 4: the king coerced the robber.) One cannot explain that the king coerced only this robber regarding this field alone, because then liability would be obvious. Even if the authorities had said nothing to the robber and simply took the field from him, he would be liable, since they took it from property in his possession rather than from elsewhere, as stated in the opening clause. Necessarily, the case is that the authorities forcibly seized other fields from other people as well, and when they reached this robber they compelled him to identify his own fields, and he also identified this one. He is liable even though the event is a general regional calamity. Maharshal likewise explains the case as applying even in a calamity affecting the entire region. One cannot object from the Gemara's second version, which says that they told him, "Show us your fields," and he showed this one together with them, implying that according to the first version they coerced only him regarding this specific field. Rather, we must say that there are not two separate versions. It is all one continuous account. Tosafos' words also imply this.
