Seif 1. 'Land cannot be stolen.' Note: Suppose Reuven stole land from Shimon and sold it to Levi. Shimon has not yet recovered it from Levi and now comes to demand that Reuven return his land. May Reuven send him to Levi and say, 'Your land is in his possession, go take it from him, and you have no claim against me,' or may he not? I have not found this law stated explicitly. But it is proven from Chullin 134a, where the Gemara says that all agree with Rabbi Chisda and the dispute concerns whether priestly gifts can be stolen. It is explicit there that if priestly gifts cannot be stolen, the owner has no claim at all against the robber, only against the person in whose possession the gifts are found. Since land, according to all opinions, cannot be stolen, there is likewise no claim against the robber, only against whoever presently possesses the land. Even if that possessor is violent and cannot effectively be sued, the robber is exempt, because the claimant's sole claim is against the present possessor. This appears correct and clear to me.
