By gift or forgiveness. See Netivot HaMishpat, who writes that coercion without a prior declaration of protest also voids a gift. This applies, however, only where he was explicitly coerced to give that particular gift. If he was coerced generally and he voluntarily gave a gift in order to free himself from the coercion, the rule depends upon the two answers given by Beit Yosef concerning the law of sikarikon. Maharam Chaviv concludes in accordance with the first answer, and therefore where one was coerced and on his own initiative gave a gift in order to escape the coercion, the gift is valid. In my humble opinion, this is also demonstrated by Rama in Darkei Moshe, letter 1, where he cites an old Mordechai and concludes that since we know of the coercion but do not know whether a declaration of protest was delivered, it is reasonable to assume that the declaration was written before the sale or gift. See there carefully.
