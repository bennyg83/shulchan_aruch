For this purpose it is treated like a sale. Rivash writes that even though a gift in which the donor received only a small benefit remains a gift for the substantive law, and we do not say that coercion caused him to decide fully to transfer as we do in a sale, it is treated like a sale for purposes of the moda'ah requirement. Witnesses must know the coercion. The reason is that where the donor receives some benefit, one cannot infer coercion merely from the fact that he said he was not giving willingly. A person can act for some benefit even without being coerced. Therefore actual knowledge of the coercion is required, as in a sale.
