Seif 12. In the gloss: All this applies where he was compelled to sell, for we say that because of the coercion, etc. Note: See my Torah writings to Parashat VaEtchanan, year 5611, where I concluded that coercion does not validate a purchase for money, but if one is coerced to acquire an item as a gift, the acquisition is valid. The rule reverses depending upon which side is transferring. If one is coerced to give a gift, the transfer is invalid, whereas a coerced sale for money is valid. By contrast, if one is coerced to acquire, a purchase for money is not valid, while receiving a gift is valid. See there, where I explained the reason based upon what I wrote in my novellae to Kiddushin, siman 42, first edition.
