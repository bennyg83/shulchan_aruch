But if he forces him to give it for less, etc., until "it is as though they compelled him," etc. If beit din knows independently that the sale occurred through coercion, then even without a modaah the sale is void, because it is as though he received no meaningful consideration and gave the property under compulsion. But where beit din does not know of the coercion and a modaah is therefore required, Rama writes that for this purpose the case is treated like a sale. Even in a transaction resembling a gift, the modaah witnesses need not themselves know the coercion when some payment was received, because the seller did receive a benefit and it remains possible that he resolved to transfer. The Rivash writes this.
