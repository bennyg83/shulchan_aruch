His sale is valid. See Baer Heitev concerning the distinction where the property is sold back to the robber himself, and Responsa Beit Shmuel Acharon, Even HaEzer, siman 10.
But if they coerced him to buy. See Even HaEzer 42:1, where Chelkat Mechokek and Beit Shmuel question this ruling and infer from Rambam that there is no distinction between buyer and seller. Shaar HaMishpat likewise writes that Rashba at the beginning of Kiddushin indicates that if coercion causes a seller finally to consent to sell, then all the more so it can cause a buyer finally to consent to acquire. Pitchei Teshuva to Even HaEzer there cites Responsa Chatam Sofer, Even HaEzer, siman 112, who offers support for the Rama's ruling here in the name of Baal HaIttur that coercion to purchase is ineffective. See there and Netivot HaMishpat.
