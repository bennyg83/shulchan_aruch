They themselves may sign, etc. The novelty is that although witnesses are not believed orally to say, "Our testimony was subject to a moda'ah," as the Tur and Mechaber write above in siman 46, seif 36, because their oral statement cannot nullify their signatures, nevertheless when they themselves are signed on a written moda'ah executed before the acquisition document, that earlier signed document can invalidate the later acquisition document bearing their signatures. All the more so where other witnesses testify to a moda'ah delivered before them prior to the sale; that is effective.
Since we know of his coercion. Meaning, through this moda'ah document we know of the coercion, because the witnesses testify to it in the document.
Presumably he delivered a moda'ah. This is the correct text. The reason is that we preserve the witnesses' presumption of proper conduct; they would not have signed unless they knew the moda'ah preceded the sale.
