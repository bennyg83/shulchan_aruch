We do not listen to him. The Sema explains that we fear an erroneous beit din might later see this written moda'ah and fail to investigate whether there were witnesses to the coercion. Beit Yosef gives the same explanation, based on the Rosh's discussion of the rule that chalitzah is performed only after the parties' identities are established, due to concern for an erroneous beit din. Levush and Bach agree. Panim Meirot questions this from Bava Batra 138b, where witnesses may write that a person on his deathbed said, “I have one hundred with so-and-so,” even without knowing that it is true, because beit din is expected to investigate witnesses; the Gemara distinguishes between one beit din relying upon another and a beit din relying upon witnesses. Shaar HaMishpat cites Rabbeinu Yonah from Shitah Mekubetzet and gives a different reason. We do not gratuitously write a moda'ah that weakens a sale where the sale is certain and no factual basis for coercion has been established. Such a document would itself depress the property's value, because people would assume witnesses did not sign a moda'ah for nothing and would fear that evidence of coercion may later emerge. According to this explanation there is no concern that beit din will err, but there is concern that ordinary people will misunderstand and the property will lose value. The Sema gives a similar consideration below, siman 238, regarding a deed that could create damaging public rumor even if carefully worded.
