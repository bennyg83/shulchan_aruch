Although an undated modaah document is generally presumed to have preceded the act it attacks, perhaps this applies only where the document is before us and the party asserts that it was delivered beforehand. If he himself says that he does not know whether the modaah preceded or followed the compromise, then if the other party is in possession his uncertainty is no claim. Perhaps even if he is in possession, his position is like “I do not know whether I repaid you,” because the compromise certainly took effect and he is unsure whether it was later displaced. Even though a prior modaah would mean the compromise never took effect at all, perhaps the uncertainty resembles doubt whether repayment occurred before the loan. A distinction may exist between a gift without coercion, where the parties remained in the posture of a completed gift and there was no reason for the recipient to suspect that the donor acted only for appearance’s sake, so the donor’s uncertainty is truly like “I do not know whether I discharged the obligation,” and a transaction under coercion, where the recipient should have been aware of the issue and the act may have been void from the outset. In a sale, however, since it remains effective even under coercion unless properly invalidated, perhaps ordinary uncertainty is still treated as “I do not know whether I repaid.” This requires study. Where it is uncertain whether the modaah came first, the Shulchan Aruch’s language concerning recognized coercion suggests that the timing is inferred from proof of the coercion. Sema’s language, however, implies that with a gift the same presumption may apply even without proof of coercion, because witnesses are presumed to have arranged the modaah so that it would be effective. If the witnesses themselves did not know when the underlying conveyance occurred, Sema’s reason would not apply, and only the recognized coercion mentioned by the Shulchan Aruch could establish the timing. In a commercial transaction, even if it is strongly favorable to one side and unlikely to cause loss, so long as there remains some real possibility of loss, however remote, it is treated as a sale rather than a gift for purposes of modaah, even if no money was exchanged immediately.
