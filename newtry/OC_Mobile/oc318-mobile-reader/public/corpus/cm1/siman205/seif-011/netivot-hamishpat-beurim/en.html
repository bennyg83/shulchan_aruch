Where we know of the coercion. See Sema, seif katan 28, who gives the reason that with a gift a mere disclosure of intent is sufficient. This is very difficult. The original moda'ah was already canceled by the later cancellation. The correct reason is that although the moda'ah itself has been canceled, in a gift coercion alone, even without an effective moda'ah, voids the gift. Sema further writes that where a person acknowledges an obligation that is known not to have existed previously, but now seeks to obligate himself, the acknowledgment resembles a gift. His wording implies that if he acknowledges a preexisting debt and we do not know whether the debt is true, the moda'ah without known coercion does not void the acknowledgment. According to what I wrote in the introduction, under the discussion beginning with Mahara Chashon, even in that latter case a moda'ah can void the acknowledgment without independent proof of coercion. Rashbam's rationale applies: the very fact that he previously delivered a moda'ah against an acknowledgment is evidence that he was pressured; otherwise why would he make the acknowledgment? In addition, once he already disclosed through the moda'ah that his later acknowledgment would be made only because of coercion, the later words cannot be treated as a complete voluntary admission.
