If the witnesses to the sale testify that the coerced seller later canceled his prior notice of coercion, the notice is canceled. But if he told the witnesses to the notice, 'Know that every kinyan I may make to cancel this notice is itself void, and I say otherwise only because of the coercion known to you; I never willingly intend to transfer the property to this coercer,' the sale is void even if a kinyan was later made from him canceling the notice. Rama: If he afterward expressly canceled all earlier cancellations, some say this is effective. It is therefore customary to write in deeds that the party cancels every notice, including any notice arising from another notice, forever. This distinction applies to a sale. With a gift, whenever the coercion is known, canceling the notice does not validate the gift. If the coercion is not known, canceling the notice is effective even for a gift. An acknowledgment of an obligation one does not actually owe is treated like a gift in every respect.
