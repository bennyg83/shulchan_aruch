The modaah is void. Once he expressly nullifies the modaah, it is as though he never delivered one. We say that because of the coercion he ultimately resolves to transfer ownership and likewise resolves to nullify the modaah, unless he also previously delivered a modaah specifically against the later nullification, as the Mechaber concludes. This is the approach of the Rachash. The Rif and Rambam hold, however, that even if he delivered only an ordinary modaah, a later forced nullification of that modaah is ineffective. We do not say that because of the coercion he fully resolves to nullify it. Sema:
That emerges from within a modaah. But the phrase "forever" alone is insufficient, because that language does not include nullification of the separate modaah he delivered against the anticipated nullification. If he expressly writes that he disqualifies the witnesses to the modaah, that is effective according to all opinions, even according to the Mechaber. Sema:
So long as we know of his coercion. The reason is that even if the modaah itself has been nullified, the coercion independently voids a gift. See the Beurim. The same law applies to an admission concerning an obligation known not to exist, where he uses the admission only to obligate himself. Such an admission has the law of a gift. Sema. See the Beurim.
