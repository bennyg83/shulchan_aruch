See Shach, seif katan 4. One who previously disclosed his intention and said, “I am compelled to sell something to my defendant so that I will become the possessor against him,” and only later sold him something without further qualification, has made a meaningful disclosure even though he did not make the statement in the formal presence of witnesses and did not specify what he would sell. This, however, is only according to the initial possibility in Shach, siman 205, concerning one who sells in order to collect a debt. According to Shach’s conclusion, such disclosure does not invalidate the sale, because we require the coercion to be independently known. This is a coercion that arose from the seller’s own situation. Perhaps, though, since he is now the possessor he may say that the coercion came from the borrower who falsely denies the debt, and one suspected regarding money is suspected regarding an oath. The seller himself knows that the borrower is prepared to swear falsely. This is somewhat stronger than the concern for concealment of land discussed by Shach, where the coercion is less certain. This requires further study.
