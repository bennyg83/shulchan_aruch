Seif 5. In the gloss: However, if the modaah had already been written, etc. Note: Above, in siman 39, in explaining the words of Rabbeinu Yonah, I wondered why their testimony can be combined, since each set seems to testify only to half of the matter. According to the view that whenever the witnesses saw everything they were able to see at that time their testimony is considered a complete matter, this is readily understood, as I wrote there. But according to those who require that each testimony itself be effective for some legal purpose, the testimony of each set here accomplishes nothing by itself. We must therefore say that the authorities from whom the Rama derived this ruling follow the view that everything depends upon whether the witnesses saw all that they were capable of seeing. According to this, for us the matter is a doubt in law. At first glance there appears to be a proof for that view from Makkot 6a. Tosafot there, s.v. "or one sees them," ask how the warning witness can be believed that he saw the witnesses, so as to enable execution. They answer that there are witnesses who testify that they saw the person giving the warning see the other witnesses, although those witnesses did not see the actual act. This is very difficult, for it appears to be precisely half a matter: the witnesses to the act do not know whether the warning witness saw them, while the witnesses who saw that he saw them did not witness the act itself. According to the authorities who require only that each set see everything it could have seen at that time, one may answer that the warning preceded the act. Thus, when the second set saw the warning witness looking at the witnesses, the act had not yet occurred. They therefore testified to everything they could have seen at that moment, and they were not obligated to remain longer in order to witness the act itself. But according to the authorities who require each testimony to have some independent legal effect, the difficulty remains. This would seem to prove against their position. Upon closer examination, however, the matter is resolved. Sanhedrin states concerning one who killed a person without testimony sufficient for execution that he may be placed in the kippah, and the Gemara explains that this can be based upon separate testimony. It follows that even testimony insufficient to execute him in beit din can nevertheless have the legal effect of subjecting him to confinement in the kippah. Thus the testimony to the act itself is legally effective, for if there were no witnesses that the warning witness had seen them, it would at least suffice for confinement. Therefore this is no longer half a matter, since only one of the two sets lacks independent effect, and one set alone does not render the entire testimony "half a matter," as the authorities write in several places. This is well understood. There, seif 5: Some say that even if he told the witnesses, "Write a modaah for me," etc. It appears to me that for every modaah he must tell them to write it. If he did not say "write it" and they wrote the modaah on their own, the writing is ineffective until they later testify orally to what occurred, for otherwise it is testimony from their written statement. A proof is the Knesset HaGedolah, siman 28, Hagahot HaTur, letter 12, who writes that in a protest one must write that "so-and-so appointed us as witnesses to write that he protested," otherwise it is testimony from writing. So too the Rama in siman 146, end of seif 3. The Gemara equates these matters when it says that a protest may be written and a modaah may be written. If a protest requires that the protester make them agents to write, the same applies to a modaah. Although the Sema there writes that even without an express instruction, if he designates them as witnesses his intention is that they write it, the Sema gives the reason: a protest is made in order to publicize the matter, so his intention is naturally that they write it. A modaah is the opposite, for he generally wishes it to remain secret. It therefore works only when he expressly tells the witnesses to write it; otherwise it is testimony from writing. Moreover, even the Sema says only that the protester need not expressly say "write it," but the witnesses must still write that he appointed them to record it, as is explicit in Nimukei Yosef and in the Rama. If they omitted that formulation, the Sema would agree that the writing is ineffective. The same is true of a modaah. This is clear and correct in practice. See also my responsa on Even HaEzer, eighth edition, siman 28.
