For he is a chamsan, etc. Ramban frequently uses the word "for" in a way that does not supply a reason. It is as though the text read, "under coercion, because he is a chamsan." Every chamsan pays money; his wrongdoing consists in compelling the seller to sell against his will.
What he writes, "But one who robs and is established as a robber, etc." The Mechaber already wrote this above at the end of siman 151. There Rama writes that some say that if the seller did not deliver a moda'ah, the sale is valid. Here Rama does not note any dissent against the Mechaber. In the Derishah there I reconciled this, and likewise the Tur, who in siman 151 presents the dispute between Rambam and the Rosh but here writes only Rambam's view as though no one disagrees. I distinguished the cases as follows. Here we speak of a field known to have come into the robber's possession by robbery, which the owner later sold to him. In such a case even the Rosh agrees that because he was already established as a robber with respect to that field while it was in his possession, even if he later paid its value we do not say that the owner fully resolved to transfer it. Above at the end of siman 151, however, the robber paid the value of the field before it came into his possession through robbery. Although it also says there that he was established as a robber concerning it, the meaning is that it had once previously left the owner's possession through his robbery. Since it did not now return to his possession through robbery, we say that the owner fully resolved to transfer it. In the Derishah I proved from the wording of Rambam and the Tur that this is the distinction. Examine this carefully.
What he writes, "as will be explained in Rambam." Rambam's text reads, "as we explained in the laws of robbery." The Mechaber changed it to "as will be explained," and the Levush likewise, because in the order of the Tur the laws of robbery appear later, beginning at siman 359, whereas Rambam placed Hilchot Gezeilah before the laws of sale. At first glance, however, it appears more likely that the Mechaber contains a scribal error and should read, "as was explained," because the essence of this law was already written by the Tur and Mechaber above in simanim 149 and 151.
