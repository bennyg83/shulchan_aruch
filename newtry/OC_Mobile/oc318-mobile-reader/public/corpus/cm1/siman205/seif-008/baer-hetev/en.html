Here, however, the Rama says nothing. The same question applies to the Tur: there he records the dispute between the Rambam and the Rosh, whereas here he cites only the Rambam as though no one disagrees. It appears to me that here the case is where it is known that the field came into the robber's possession by robbery, and afterward he purchased it from the owner. In that case the Rosh agrees that, since he was already established as a robber with respect to this field while it was in his possession, even though he later paid its full value we do not say that the owner conclusively resolved to transfer it. There, however, the case is where he paid for the field before it came into his possession through robbery. Although it says there too that he had been established as a robber regarding it, this means that on an earlier occasion it had left the owner's possession through robbery. Since it did not now return to the robber's possession by robbery, we say that the owner conclusively transferred it. Sema. The Taz writes that the Rambam gives no indication of such a distinction. In Hilchot Mechirah he states the rule that in the case of a robber no protest is required and concludes, "as I wrote in Hilchot Gezeilah," namely the law at the end of siman 151. It follows that the two are one and the same case. In my opinion the Tur and Rama here relied on what they wrote at the end of siman 151, and there is no distinction between the cases. This is clear and correct.
