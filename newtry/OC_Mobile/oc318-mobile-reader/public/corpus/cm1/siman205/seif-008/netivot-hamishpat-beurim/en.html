The robber, once he has been established as a robber. See Sema, seif katan 20, and Perishah, who suggests that the Rambam is speaking where a person robbed a particular item and afterward the owner sold that same item to the robber while it was still in the robber's possession. According to this, the well known difficulty on the Rambam from the Gemara's statement that Rav Huna does not accept Rav Bibi's rule is resolved. Rav Bibi speaks of a robber, but according to the Perishah the Rambam's ruling concerns a robber whose only coercive act is possession of this very item. In such a case there is no basis to say that because of coercion together with payment the owner fully resolved to convey, since the sale does not release him from any separate coercion. Ramban in Bava Batra 40 similarly writes that where there is no coercion apart from this item, no prior protest is required. Rav Huna and Rav Bibi, by contrast, must also be discussing a person established as violent in matters involving danger to life. If Rav Bibi meant only one established as a robber regarding this particular field, why would his proof of ownership be entirely ineffective? At least an express warranty should help, and since we rule that ordinary warranty is presumed even if omitted, even a deed should have significance, as Tosafot write there. Moreover, Rav Bibi's statement relates to the Mishnah, which includes a violent person in matters of life. In such a case the principle that coercion together with payment can produce genuine consent does apply. Therefore, when Rav Bibi concludes that the robbed person has no land, it indeed proves that Rav Huna disagrees with him. It follows that even where the property is already in the robber's possession, if he separately coerces the owner through another form of pressure to sell it to him, the sale is valid in the absence of a prior protest. Ramban expressly writes that if the robber subjects the owner to another monetary coercion, even though he had previously robbed this very item and only later bought it from him, the owner's receipt of payment can cause him to resolve to convey. This is so although Ramban agrees with the Rambam that where the only circumstance is that the robber holds the stolen item, a prior protest is unnecessary.
