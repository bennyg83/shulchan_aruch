Threatens. Even according to the view that coercion by mere intimidation is not considered coercion, where he threatens something as to which he gives himself a color of legal justification, such as a dispute among authorities in which the possessor would prevail by saying, "I maintain that the law follows this authority," and the other threatens to take him to a secular court and extract the property from him, this is coercion. Lechem Rav, siman 37. Regarding the rule that a person may threaten and yet not carry out his threat, there is no distinction between a severe prohibition and a minor one. Maharik, shoresh 187; Rashdam, Yoreh Deah, siman 102, and Choshen Mishpat, siman 334; see also there, siman 13. Nevertheless, everything depends on the assessment of the judge. If the person making the threat is violent, established as one who coerces others, has the power to carry out the threat, and is accustomed to doing so, we do not say in such a case that a person commonly threatens without acting. Mekor Baruch, p. 86. Bnei Chayai.
