Document. Meaning, a deed of sale alone. Although he gave him no money at all, he did not conclusively resolve to transfer ownership; concerning this the Gemara concludes that there are some who disagree. This is not comparable to the case of a sicarius, who never gives any money at all. Here, however, the robber wishes to pay and the owner wishes to receive payment, so we say that the owner conclusively resolves to transfer ownership through the deed of sale alone, even though he did not convert the price into a loan obligation recorded in a promissory note. This is explicit in the Beit Yosef, contrary to what the Sema wrote. So it appears to me. Shach.
Protest. The essential requirements of every protest are three: the witnesses must know that what the coercer demands from him is unlawful; they must have heard from his mouth that the coercer threatened to compel him; and they must know that the coercer has the power to carry out the threat. Mabit, vol. 3, siman 181. If a person was held by the government and, in order to rescue himself, was forced to sell his property, and he delivered a protest against the sale, the protest is ineffective. The institution of protest was enacted only against a robber or coercer, not against one who did him a favor by providing him money with which to redeem himself. Thus ruled the Rif in Piskei Ricanti, siman 472. If, at the time of a marriage, they requested that a man not remove his wife from her locale, although by law they could not compel him, and he agreed but delivered a protest that he agreed only so as not to lose his expenditures, there is no valid protest because there was no coercion. Responsa of the Rif, ibid., siman 494. For a detailed explanation of the laws of protest in a sale, compromise, waiver, and gift, see at length Darkei Noam of Maharam HaLevi, Choshen Mishpat, responsum 16. Mahara Sasson, siman 86, agreed with Maharibal, vol. 3, siman 58, that financial coercion is considered coercion, except that with bodily coercion it is unnecessary to say, "today," whereas with financial coercion he must say, "today." Lechem Rav, siman 177, wrote that financial coercion is not considered coercion according to most authorities even if he says in his heart, "today." Although generally, where there is a dispute, the possessor may say, "I maintain that the law follows the authority favorable to me," here there is an oath involved and one must rule stringently. See there, siman 31. All this is cited in Bnei Chayai in his glosses to the Tur on this siman. See there also a lengthy responsum concerning one who owed rent to a non-Jew, who had him imprisoned, and who was thereby forced to sell his established tenancy right to the non-Jew. He delivered a protest that the sale was made under coercion. The responsum discusses whether the sale is valid, as well as many additional details concerning protests.
