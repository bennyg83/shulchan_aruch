The witnesses must, etc. The Gemara in Ketubot 110a, cited by the Beit Yosef above at the beginning of siman 85, appears to show that if a creditor sells land to his debtor and before the sale issues a moda'ah that he sells only because the debtor intends to conceal his assets and avoid payment, so that the creditor will later be able to seize the land, the moda'ah is effective and the sale is void. It may be answered that there the declaration is effective only to preserve the debt, while the sale itself remains valid. Examine the passage carefully. Responsa Divrei Rivot, siman 301, gives a surprising ruling concerning a gentile who paid interest he owed to a Jew, holding that it was not a coerced gift; his words require study. See also Responsa Maharam Alshich, siman 71; Mahar'i Beit Levi, siman 24, page 129; Mahari ibn Lev, part 2, siman 47; Mabit, end of siman 42; and Rama, simanim 22 and 52.
