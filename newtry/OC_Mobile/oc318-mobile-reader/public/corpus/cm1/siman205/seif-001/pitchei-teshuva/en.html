The sale is void. Responsa Chavot Yair, siman 40, in the course of a responsum concerning a firstborn animal, explains that it is not only Reuven, the seller who was coerced and made a valid moda'ah, who may void the sale. Shimon, the purchaser, may also void it after learning that Reuven delivered a moda'ah, because the transaction was made in error. Had Shimon known that the seller retained the ability to rescind later, he too would not have finally agreed to pay. This differs from overreaching, where the party who committed the overreaching cannot retract if the injured party wishes to maintain the sale, because there the wrongdoer would otherwise profit from his wrongdoing. Here, the sale is not void because of the seller's coercive wrongdoing, since a coerced sale for value is ordinarily valid, but because the seller's valid moda'ah independently nullified the transaction. This applies only where the moda'ah itself was halachically valid. The same principle applies to a compromise where compromise is treated like a sale. Ketzot HaChoshen cites Chavot Yair and questions part of his reasoning, but nevertheless concludes that his practical ruling is correct. See above, siman 190, seif 7, seif katan 5.
