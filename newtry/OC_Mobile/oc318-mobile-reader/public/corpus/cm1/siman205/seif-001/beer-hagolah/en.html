Alef) This is the language of the Rambam at the beginning of chapter 10 of Hilkhot Mekhirah. The Maggid Mishneh writes that this is an agreed halachic ruling in Bava Batra 48b concerning one who is coerced to sell: the halachah is that in all such cases his sale is valid.
Bet) There in the Gemara, 48a.
Gimel) The Maggid Mishneh writes that Rambam appears to explain the Gemara's distinction between whether he consented or did not consent as referring to consent before witnesses or without witnesses. The conclusion is that even if he did not express consent before witnesses, the acquisition is valid if he acknowledged it, and we do not say that he acknowledged only because of the coercion, so long as he did not first deliver a moda'ah.
Dalet) This follows from the Gemara's answer there, Bava Batra 40b, and from the incident of Tavi who coerced Pappi, where Rabbah bar bar Chanah signed both the moda'ah and the sale document. Rav Huna said that one who signed the moda'ah signed properly, etc., there on 48b.
Heh) There, halachah 2.
Vav) A statement of the sages of Nehardea there at the beginning of 40b.
