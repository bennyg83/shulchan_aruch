It is like two witnesses. Nimukei Yosef writes that a protest before two witnesses may be written even without an express instruction to write, because writing it benefits the protester. Since he protested before them, it is as though he told them to write and appointed them agents. Later authorities therefore require the protest document to be phrased as agency, such as, "So and so caused us to testify concerning himself and to write that he protested." If they merely write, "We heard him protest," the document is ordinary written testimony and runs into the rule, "from their mouths and not from their writing." This approach follows Baal HaMaor and Rashi, who explain why ordinary loan and acquisition documents are not invalid written testimony: where the person whose legal act is being recorded authorized the document, the writing is treated as his own act. The same can work for protest because the protest depends only upon the protester himself. Rama adopted this agency formulation in siman 146 for protest but did not mention it here regarding moda'ah. Nimukei Yosef appears to require agency language for both. The distinction is that agency language can explain the witnesses' recording that the party delivered a moda'ah, because it is as though he himself wrote, "I do not wish this sale." But a moda'ah document also says, "We recognize his coercion." Agency cannot help with that portion, because the person himself is not believed merely to testify that he was coerced. Therefore, for moda'ah we must rely on Tosafot's separate explanation that the Sages relaxed the written testimony rule in order to save a coerced person from the coercion. Once that special relaxation applies, agency language is no longer necessary at all. This also explains why a moda'ah can be written even after the sale. If the entire basis were agency, writing would help only before the sale, when the party himself could still state his refusal. After the sale he cannot simply assert that he never consented, and agency would no longer help. Ran expressly explains that the statement, "a moda'ah may be written," includes writing after the sale. The practical authorities likewise assume this. Therefore the true basis for written moda'ah is the special dispensation to protect the coerced person, not agency. That is why Rama did not require agency language here.
