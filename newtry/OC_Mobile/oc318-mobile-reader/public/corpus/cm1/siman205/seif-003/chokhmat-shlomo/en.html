Seif 11 in the Rama's gloss: If he later revoked, etc. See Responsa Maharit, part 2, Choshen Mishpat, end of siman 43, where he discusses at length why the revocation is effective. He also concludes as practical law that if, at the time of first issuing the moda'ah, he swore that he would not retract the moda'ah so long as he had not torn it up, then none of the later revocations are effective, because he swore and has not yet torn up the moda'ah. See there carefully. Also see my commentary on the Torah, Parashas Eikev, year 5610, where I proved from Tosafos in chapter 9 of Shabbos, s.v. 'From here there is a great moda'ah concerning the Torah,' from their wording 'perhaps afterward they would retract.' They did not write simply, 'It can be answered that afterward they retracted.' Why did they use 'perhaps,' and why 'would later retract' in the future tense rather than 'later retracted' in the past? This shows that in truth they never retracted. Rather, Hashem feared that perhaps they might retract, and therefore compelled them so that they could not retract. From this we learn a new law. If a person initially agreed to sell an item to another, but before completion of the kinyan the buyer feared that the seller might retract and therefore began coercing him to complete the sale, and the seller then completed it, this is considered a moda'ah and duress even though the seller had not first retracted. In a sale this case will generally not arise, because prior delivery of a moda'ah is required. If he delivered one, he already retracted; if he did not, ordinary coercion does not invalidate the sale. But the practical difference is for a gift, or where he agreed to sell for less than market value, where a prior moda'ah is not required. Even if he first agreed to give and we never saw him retract, if at the time of completion of the gift and kinyan the recipient coerced him out of fear that he might retract, it is considered coercion, the act of giving is ineffective, and the gift is void. In my humble opinion this is a novel rule.
There, seif 11 in the Rama's gloss: When does this apply? In a sale; but regarding a gift, etc. From this, with Hashem's help, I learned another rule. Although compromise has the law of a sale, that is only where one gives a moda'ah against the compromise itself, because the parties have opposing claims and the compromise therefore resembles a sale. But once a legally effective compromise has already been completed and one party refuses to fulfill it, forcing the other litigant to concede something additional beyond the compromise, there is nothing left to dispute after the compromise. The added concession is therefore like a waiver or gift. Consequently a moda'ah is effective even if he previously nullified all nullifications and even if he declared all witnesses invalid, because the entire matter is under duress. Examine this carefully.
