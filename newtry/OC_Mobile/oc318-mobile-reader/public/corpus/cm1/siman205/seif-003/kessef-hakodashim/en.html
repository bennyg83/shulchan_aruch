With a gift. Perhaps delivery of a modaah regarding a gift is effective even before a single witness. At least the donor disclosed his intent, and one supporting witness can exempt him from an oath, while the recipient has no certain claim that there had been no prior disclosure. Nor does the rule of one who is obligated to swear but cannot swear and therefore pays apply, because this is a matter the recipient could not have been expected to know. Hence one cannot say that a single witness is treated as two only when an oath is actually administered against him; here the witness is supporting the donor. Yet it appears that even the donor’s oath would not suffice. See simanim 269 and 183. Perhaps whenever he did not disclose his intent before two witnesses, the ordinary form of the gift itself indicates that it was not given under the condition he mentioned before one witness, for if that were truly his intent he should have disclosed it before two. This appears to be the implication of the authorities and requires further study. If people refuse to permit burial until a person obligates himself to pay money that he does not owe, this may be treated like a gift, for which a formal modaah is unnecessary and recognized coercion is sufficient. Such coercion may affect all members of the family, and perhaps even the deceased’s wife and her family, even if she bore him no children. But if the obligation is a monetary payment reasonably connected to the value of the burial plot and the services, it is more like a sale, and there a modaah may not help because the payment itself may be legally due.
