Compromise. See above, siman 12, seif 11, what I wrote there, and in the Bach, seif 16, what he wrote on this matter. In Responsa Rashdam, siman 44, he was uncertain about one who delivered a protest against a waiver, so that the other party would not have to return a pledge after the debt secured by it had been paid, whether this is comparable to one who is coerced and gives a gift or to one who is coerced and sells. See there, and see simanim 200 and 285. Beit Hillel writes that where a person swore to give another a gift and, at the time of the gift, delivered a protest that he was acting only because of the obligation of his oath, it is very close to saying that the gift is void. This means that at the time of the oath he said, "I am not giving him willingly; whatever I give him I will be compelled to give because of the oath." This is clear to me, but the practical ruling requires study. Ibid.
Liability. People do not ordinarily accept liability for a gift, so this was certainly a sale, as the Tur and Shulchan Aruch write in siman 175, seif 54, regarding the law of a neighboring landowner. The Rivash likewise writes this. Although the Tur and Shulchan Aruch write at the beginning of siman 242 that even if he accepted property liability in the document it is not thereby a gift, there the case is where it is known that the writing of the liability clause was also coerced. Here the case is where it is not known that he was coerced regarding that clause. See what I wrote there further in resolving this issue. Sema. See also what the Taz wrote there to distinguish these cases. If, with respect to a gift, he revealed that he was acting under coercion and the coercion did not cease, and afterward he made a promissory note because of the first document, he does not even require a protest regarding the second document, because this document derives from the first document, which was coerced. Mahari ben Chaviv, siman 34. According to the Radbaz, however, a protest is required. See Responsa Maharit, Choshen Mishpat, siman 44. Bnei Chayai.
