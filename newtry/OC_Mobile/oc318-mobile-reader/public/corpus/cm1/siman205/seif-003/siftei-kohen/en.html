A compromise has the law of a sale, etc. See above, siman 12, seif 11, and what I wrote there, as well as the Bach, seif 15. Responsa Rashdam discusses a case where one delivered a modaah concerning a waiver so that the other party would not have to return collateral after the debt had been paid, and he was uncertain whether this is comparable to one who is coerced into making a gift or to one who is coerced into making a sale. See Responsa Rashdam, simanim 200 and 285. The Bedek HaBayit writes regarding one who swore to give a gift and, at the time of the gift, delivered a modaah that he acted only because of the obligation of his oath, that it is quite possible the gift is void. Meaning, at the time of the oath he made clear that he was not giving willingly and that whatever he would later give would be only under the compulsion of the oath. This appears correct to me, though the practical ruling requires study.
