With a gift after revealing his intent. Nehardea says that any moda'ah that does not state, "We know the coercion of so and so," is not a valid moda'ah. The Gemara asks: what kind of moda'ah? If it concerns a get or gift, mere revelation is enough. Ramban asks why coercion is needed for a get or gift at all. We rule that if a husband tells his agent before delivery, "The get I gave you is void," it is void. Rashbam answers that only a moda'ah accompanied by coercion works before the act. If he simply says, "The get I will make shall not be a get," without any coercion, we presume that at the later moment of execution he decided sincerely to act, unless he cancels the get after writing. Rambam appears to hold that even a broad prospective cancellation can be effective, but Ramban suggests this is where coercion can ordinarily be inferred. If the husband explicitly says he is not coerced and merely cancels in advance, the later get remains valid. Rashba, Rosh, and Beit Yosef appear to understand differently. They say that even where it is known there was no coercion, a moda'ah can work because it is no worse than direct cancellation. They read the Gemara's phrase "mere revelation" as revelation that he does not want the get or gift to stand. Rashbam and Ramban, however, understand "mere revelation" as referring to the coercion itself. That is why Ramban asks why coercion is necessary. In my view Ramban's difficulty can be resolved through Tosafot's rule concerning written moda'ah. Tosafot asks how written moda'ah is valid despite "from their mouths and not from their writing," and answers that the Sages were lenient in order to rescue the coerced person. If there is no coercion and he merely wishes to cancel the get or gift, that special dispensation does not apply. Written testimony alone would therefore be invalid. Accordingly, where it is known that there was no coercion and the only evidence of cancellation is a written moda'ah signed by witnesses rather than oral testimony, the document is invalid because the special rule protecting the coerced person does not apply. This preserves Ramban's concern and gives a principled role to coercion in the validity of the written moda'ah.
