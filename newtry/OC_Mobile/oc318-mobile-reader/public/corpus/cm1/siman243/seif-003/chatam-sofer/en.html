In the continuation, "and also Tosafot." Note: One who examines Tosafot there at the beginning of the chapter Ben Sorer UMoreh will see that the reason Tosafot, the Maggid Mishneh, and the Ran did not rely on the Yerushalmi is that the plain implication of the Gemara in the chapter Eizehu Neshech, 72a, is that a minor has no capacity to acquire by Torah law at any time. Since all of his acquisition is d'rabbanan, it is impossible to say that once he reaches the age of pe'utot, beit din seizes property on the basis of their enactment. That cannot be, for the Gemara in Shevuot 41a implies that according to the first Tanna against Rabbi Yose, there is no case of rabbinic theft for which property is seized, as is clear to one who studies the passage. We must therefore distinguish and say that the teaching of three stages in a minor concerns a case where another person's intent confers the property upon him. In that case he acquires even by Torah law, and therefore his commercial transactions are effective even by Torah law, so that property may be seized from one who wrongs him in his dealings. This also resolves the Mishnah in chapter 4 of Maaser Sheni. By contrast, the rule that taking a minor's find constitutes theft only because of the ways of peace is because there is no other person's intent conferring it upon him. When such a case came before me, I did not rule to extract money contrary to Tosafot, the Ran, and the Maggid Mishneh.
