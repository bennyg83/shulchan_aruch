(Seif 7: on condition that you write a deed.) See the Beit Yosef in seif 8, citing the Rosh, from “some ask” until “if he retracts from the conditions, the act is also void.” It implies that where one says, “on condition that I give you two hundred zuz,” even those who disagree with the Rosh agree that he may retract both the condition and the transaction. From here we learn that a condition made for the recipient's benefit is a valid condition, and need not resemble the conditions of the sons of Gad and Reuven, which were for the grantor's benefit. Here the condition is “that you write for him,” which benefits the recipient, yet it is a condition. The Maggid Mishneh writes that if one says, “my field is sold to you on condition that you give yourself two hundred zuz,” he may retract both. This shows that he disagrees with the Rosh's reasoning regarding a condition made for the other's benefit. According to the Rosh, such a stipulation is not a condition as against the recipient, whereas according to the Maggid Mishneh it is, as I wrote at the beginning of siman 207 regarding the Rambam's view. Nevertheless, concerning the ability to retract both, he agrees with the Rosh, for according to the Rosh it is at least a condition as against the grantor.
