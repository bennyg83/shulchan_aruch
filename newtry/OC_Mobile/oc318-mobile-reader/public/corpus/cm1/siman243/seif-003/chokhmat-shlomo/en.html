Seif 23. A minor girl acquires through her courtyard, etc. Note: The ruling here follows the conclusion of Bava Metzia 10a that one teaching concerns a male minor and the other a female minor, so there is no contradiction. This requires study in light of the beginning of chapter HaZorek in Gittin. There the Gemara derives that a woman's courtyard can receive her get from the verse "and he shall place it in her hand," and cites a parallel derivation regarding a thief from the verse "if the stolen item is found." It then explains why both derivations are needed. According to the conclusion of Bava Metzia that a man's courtyard is included by the law of agency, while a woman's courtyard is included as an extension of her hand, the two subjects are fundamentally different. There should be no need for the Gemara in Gittin to explain why both verses are necessary. Even according to the earlier view that the derivations are disputed, the law of a thief surely requires its own verse because ordinary agency cannot operate for a transgression. A courtyard is an exceptional case, perhaps because the owner is compelled to accept what is placed there or because liability is not immediately recognizable. Without a verse one would have thought that the rule barring agency for a transgression applies. Thus there is no proof from a get, which involves no agency for a transgression. Yet the sugya in Gittin seems to proceed on the initial assumption in Bava Metzia that Rabbi Yochanan treats courtyard as an extension of the hand rather than agency at all. It is difficult to say that the anonymous Gemara there follows a rejected preliminary view, especially when it would not accord with practical halachah. This requires study.
