Even if he said, "Write and acquire." See Sema, seif katan 20, who explains that a person cannot transfer a davar shelo ba la'olam. The view follows Tosafot in Nazir 12, who hold that one cannot appoint an agent to separate challah while the flour is still flour, because agency for something not yet legally in existence cannot take effect merely because the person could later knead it. Since a required act is still missing, it resembles a davar shelo ba la'olam. Agency works there only because he could presently bring an already-kneaded dough. The dissenting authorities follow the opinion in Yoreh Deah siman 327 that because the person himself can knead the flour, the matter is considered within his control and not a davar shelo ba la'olam. Accordingly, here too the witnesses' agency to acquire through the deed can be effective because it is within their power to write and deliver the deed. Why then does agency work in a get where one says, "Write and give," even though the get does not yet exist? There the husband makes them full agents standing in his place to accomplish the divorce. The woman already exists and is his wife, and the agents act as though they were the husband himself. Likewise, where one says "write and give" a gift, they can be made agents in place of the owner to transfer his existing field. Here, however, he did not appoint them as agents over the field itself. He appointed them only to acquire a future deed for the recipient. After the deed is written they must themselves say that they are now acquiring the deed on behalf of the recipient, as Ran explains concerning a writ of emancipation. Since the only agency concerns the deed and the deed did not yet exist, agency cannot take effect according to Tosafot. Similarly, if one says, "When the palm produces fruit, acquire the fruit for so-and-so," the agency does not work because agency cannot be created over a davar shelo ba la'olam. Even according to the opinion that such agency can work, it appears this is only where a separate kinyan sudar was made. If the deed itself is the instrument of acquisition and he merely said, "Write and acquire," the witnesses cannot simply acquire it for the recipient after writing without checking again with the transferor. This is no better than "write and give," where siman 39 rules that the witnesses must consult the transferor before delivery. "Write and acquire" should be treated like "write and give" for this purpose.
