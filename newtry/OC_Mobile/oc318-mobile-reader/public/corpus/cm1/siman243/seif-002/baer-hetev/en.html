Go. Although with a loan or deposit the sender cannot retract even when he merely says 'go,' as stated at the beginning of siman 125, those cases are different because the money in the sender's hands was already owed to the recipient as a loan or deposit. Therefore it readily leaves the sender's ownership and we say the agent acquires it for the recipient. A gift is different. Ibid.
A poor person. See Maharik, shoresh 133, who writes that perhaps where bodily exertion is required we do not say that a promise to a poor person becomes a vow. See Responsa R. Moshe Alshich, siman 121. Shach.
And even. This is an addition by the Mechaber himself, expressing his own intent, as the Sema writes. The phrase 'as has been explained' used by the Mechaber clearly refers to siman 204, seif 8, which does not specifically discuss a poor person but any person with respect to being considered lacking in trustworthiness. See Responsa Raanach, simanim 30, 103, and 108; Responsa Menachem Azariah, siman 104; Responsa Rama, simanim 47 and 48, question 3; Rif at the beginning of chapter 4 and 5; and Yoreh Deah siman 258. Shach.
With a poor person. The Mordechai at the end of the first chapter of Bava Batra writes that a promise to a poor person becomes a vow only if the property was in the promisor's possession when he vowed. If it was not in his possession then, the vow does not take effect. See also the end of siman 212. Gloss of the Sema.
