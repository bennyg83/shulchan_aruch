But if he said, "Take it," etc. Although with a loan or deposit the sender cannot retract even when he says merely "Take it," as explained above at the beginning of siman 125, there is a distinction. In those cases the money in the sender's hand is already owed to the recipient, whether as a loan repayment or as return of a deposit. It therefore leaves the sender's control more easily, and we say that the agent acquires it for the recipient. A gift is different, because the donor did not previously owe anything to the recipient.
And if the recipient is poor, etc. Rama corrects the Mechaber's wording by adding the law concerning a wealthy recipient. His intention is to teach that a scribal error entered the Mechaber's text. A promise of a gift to a poor person is treated as a vow, and with a vow there is no distinction between a small gift and a large one. The obligation is complete, not merely a matter of lacking good faith. The Beit Yosef writes this in the name of the Yerushalmi. Therefore the Mechaber's conclusion, "if it is a small gift," can refer only to a wealthy recipient. There the matter is not a vow but an ordinary promise. Only with a small gift, upon which the recipient reasonably relies, does retraction render the donor one who lacks good faith. This is unlike the Ir Shushan, who first states that with a wealthy recipient retraction constitutes lack of good faith without distinguishing between a large and small gift, and then limits the poor recipient to a small gift. Both points are reversed. With a poor recipient there is no distinction and he may never retract. With a wealthy recipient the distinction between a large and small gift applies.
And what Rama writes in Yoreh Deah siman 258. Meaning, there the law of a poor recipient is explained. The Tur and Mechaber likewise write the law of a poor person in siman 125:5 without distinguishing between a large and small amount. Rama's reference here to siman 207 is a scribal error and should read siman 204:8, where the distinction concerning a wealthy recipient between a large and small gift is stated. The same appears below at the beginning of siman 249 and in Even HaEzer at the end of siman 51. Mordechai writes that the rule that a promise to a poor person becomes a vow applies specifically when the promised property was already in the donor's possession when he vowed. If it was not yet in his possession, the vow does not take effect. See the end of siman 212.
