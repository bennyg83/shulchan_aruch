Specifically when another person's intent transfers title to him, but not regarding a found object. In the latter case there is only a rabbinic protection for peaceful relations, as explained below at the beginning of siman 270. The Ran in the chapter HaMekabel and the Maggid Mishneh in chapter 4 of the Laws of Acquisition and Gift write this, and the same appears from the other poskim, who state without qualification below, siman 270, that a minor's found object belongs to him only by reason of peaceful relations. In my humble opinion, however, this requires study. The poskim derived their distinction from the statement in the chapter HaTikabel that a minor who discards a pebble when given one but takes a nut when given one can acquire for himself, whereas in the chapter HaNizakin the Gemara states that a found object of a deaf mute, imbecile, or minor is protected only for the sake of peaceful relations. They therefore answered that a minor acquires a gift for himself specifically because another person's intent transfers title to him, unlike a found object. In my humble opinion it is strained to create a distinction not mentioned in the Gemara. Although we find at the end of siman 268 that another person's intent to transfer title is legally stronger, no source distinguishes on that basis with respect to a minor's ability to acquire for himself. Nor is such a distinction logically compelling. If he has the legal capacity to acquire a gift for himself, why should he not likewise acquire a found object? Rather, it appears to me that the Mishna's statement that a found object of a deaf mute, imbecile, or minor is protected only for peaceful relations concerns a younger minor who has not yet reached the level of understanding described above. This is not a strained interpretation of the Mishna's unqualified use of the term 'minor.' In the chapter Mi SheMet as well, the Mishna states without qualification that one may acquire on behalf of a minor, yet Tosafot and the Ran in the chapter HaTikabel explain that it refers to a minor younger than the level discussed here. This is even more reasonable here, where the Mishna places a minor together with an imbecile, who has no understanding at all. The Yerushalmi states this explicitly in chapter 4 of Maaser Sheni, halacha 4, in Eruvin, chapter Chalon, and in Gittin, chapter HaNizakin. Whether another person's intent transfers title to him or he finds the object himself, there are three developmental levels of a minor. The Yerushalmi expressly explains that the Mishna in HaNizakin, concerning a found object protected only for peaceful relations, refers to one who has not reached the level at which he can acquire for himself. Rabbi Shimshon of Coucy cites this Yerushalmi briefly in chapter 4 of Maaser Sheni. The Yerushalmi itself explains it more fully. Tosafot at the beginning of the chapter Ben Sorer UMoreh also allude to this Yerushalmi, and Baal HaItur, under the entry Zekhiyah, cites it and appears to accept it as practical law. Furthermore, in my humble opinion there is proof from the Babylonian Talmud that a minor acquires a found object for himself by Torah law. At the end of Bava Metzia 10a the Gemara asks whether anyone holds that a courtyard was not included by reason of one's hand. It cites the baraita: 'Her hand' teaches only her hand. From where do we derive her roof, courtyard, and enclosure? The verse states, 'and he shall place,' in every manner. Regarding a bill of divorce, everyone agrees that a courtyard was included by reason of her hand. They disagree only concerning a found object. One master holds that the law of a found object is derived from divorce, and the other holds that it is not. Alternatively, regarding a minor girl everyone agrees, and they disagree only regarding a minor boy. One master derives a minor boy from a minor girl, and the other does not. This shows that according to everyone, a minor boy and minor girl possess a hand by Torah law. Their disagreement concerns only whether a courtyard is like a hand or like an agent. Tosafot write at the beginning of page 11 that the derivation concerning a found object is only rabbinic, since a minor's found object belongs to him only by rabbinic law, even according to Rabbi Yose who calls interference with it actual robbery by rabbinic law, and that the dispute concerns only the courtyard, not the hand. In my humble opinion their words are strained. First, it is difficult to explain the Gemara's term 'we derive' as referring to a rabbinic derivation when its plain meaning is a scriptural derivation. Second, why should the authority who does not derive the law refrain from doing so? Once the hand is derived, the courtyard should likewise be derived. The language of the Rif and Rosh also clearly indicates a Torah derivation. They write that the baraita derives a woman's roof, courtyard, and enclosure from the verse, and that the law of a found object is derived from divorce, so that just as her courtyard is like her hand regarding divorce, so too her courtyard is like her hand regarding a found object. A man's courtyard, however, is included by reason of agency, and because a minor lacks agency he lacks a courtyard, since a minor boy is not derived from a minor girl. It therefore appears to me that the Gemara should be understood plainly. Regarding a hand there is no issue of deriving a minor boy from a minor girl, because the verse does not speak specifically of a minor girl. Minor girls and minor boys are equal in possessing a hand by Torah law. Their disagreement concerns only a courtyard, because the scriptural inclusion of a courtyard is written regarding a woman in the verse concerning delivery into her hand. This appears to me to be the clear explanation of the Gemara. It follows explicitly that a minor boy has a hand by Torah law even regarding a found object. Necessarily, the Mishna's statement that a found object of a deaf mute, imbecile, or minor is protected only for peaceful relations concerns a minor younger than the stage at which he discards a pebble and takes a nut, who cannot acquire for himself because he lacks legal capacity of hand, precisely as the Yerushalmi explains. One should not object from Bava Metzia 12a, where the Gemara asks whether Rabbi Yose holds that a minor has acquisition, citing the Mishna concerning found objects, and does not answer that Rabbi Yose refers to a minor who has reached the stage of discarding a pebble and taking a nut. That is no difficulty. Had that been Rabbi Yose's case, how could the Sages disagree with him, when the Yerushalmi states that even according to the Sages a minor who has reached the requisite level acquires a found object? Examine carefully.
