(Seif 1: to Shimon's agent.) The glosses of Mordechai there state: since Shimon admitted the obligation to the charity treasurers, and they acted as Leah's agents, Leah herself may collect, because an agent's hand is like that of the principal. Moreover, this is no less than one who seizes property for a creditor, and there is also the rule of Rabbi Natan. This implies that the case concerns a poor woman, and that the treasurers went to collect charity intended for Leah, but did not publicize this out of respect for her. Had they publicized it, it would be obvious that Leah could collect, for what difference would it make whether the pledge was made in her presence? She acquires through the donor's declaration of charity even when absent, as is evident from the case cited by the Beit Yosef in siman 267. Rather, the case is as I explained, and it teaches that even so Leah may collect for the reasons stated. The reference to Rabbi Natan is to the rule stated in siman 86. It appears that the Rama here also speaks of this case. The Sema's words in seif katan 3 are surprising. If the pledge was made in a manner that courts compel him to fulfill, what difference is there whether it was in her presence? I also do not know how he explains the Mordechai's invocation of Rabbi Natan.
