In a guarded courtyard. It must resemble his hand or his agent, from which the law is derived.
And some disagree. Their reason is that since another person's intent is transferring the property to him, whether as a gift or likewise as a sale, the courtyard need not be guarded with the recipient's knowledge. At minimum, however, it must be guarded on behalf of the giver or those acting for him. See the Perishah here, below in siman 268, and above at the beginnings of simanim 200 and 235. This is unlike the Ir Shushan, who explains the dissenters on the ground that one who gives a gift gives generously. If that were the reason, they would disagree only regarding a gift and not regarding a sale, which is not so, as explained at the beginning of siman 200.
