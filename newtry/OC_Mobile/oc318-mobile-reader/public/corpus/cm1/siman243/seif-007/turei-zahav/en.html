(They remove it from him.) I am surprised. In siman 275, seif 9, it is written that if another person came and took possession, the latter acquires, and both rulings derive from the Rosh. This should be an a fortiori case. Here the property is movable, and the second person acquired it through actual possession and seizure, yet we remove it from him because the first has a possible right arising from an unresolved question. All the more so there, with land where physical seizure is less relevant, why should the later possessor acquire? The gloss notes that the Shach too questions this ruling of the Mechaber and asks why seizure should not help according to Rambam. It appears that the Shach overlooked the Nimukei Yosef cited by the Beit Yosef at the end of this siman, who writes that a gift never leaves the donor's possession until it actually enters the recipient's possession. This is therefore unlike ordinary unresolved Talmudic questions. As for the Taz's difficulty, one can answer in the opposite direction. Precisely because seizure does not apply to land, the land remains as it originally stood, as the Rosh writes in Perek Chezkas HaBatim. But with movable property, where seizure does apply, possession in a doubtful case may help where there is no prior owner currently asserting title. Examine carefully.
