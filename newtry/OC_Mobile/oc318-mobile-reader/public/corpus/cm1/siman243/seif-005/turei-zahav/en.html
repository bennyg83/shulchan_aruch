The reason is that since the deed contains an act of acquisition, the recipient acquired during the donor's lifetime, for one may confer a benefit upon a person in his absence, and the deed serves only as evidence. The Tur's words in seif 4 require study. He writes that where a deed is found in the donor's possession, the recipient is believed through a migo that it reached him during the donor's lifetime. The opening clause implies that mere custody by the beit din suffices, because then the donor no longer has a migo. It appears that the precision of the opening clause is primary, while the latter statement was added only for greater force. It is enough that the donor has no migo; we do not then suspect that there was a condition that was not fulfilled. The opening of the responsum likewise makes the matter depend solely upon the act of acquisition, for the recipient acquired from that moment. The Tur states this explicitly in the name of the Rosh at the end of siman 250 regarding a deed found in another person's hands. There the recipient has no migo, yet he acquires because of the act of acquisition. Accordingly, I am astonished at the Sema's statement in seif katan 22 that the case is where the recipient admits the facts and is believed through a migo. At the end of siman 250, cited above, even where the deed is found in another's hands the recipient acquires, provided only that the donor has no migo. The Mechaber's words are therefore correct. This has nothing to do with the concern for forgetfulness in siman 65, for that siman concerns a loan document, as expressly stated at the end of siman 250.
