But if it is not in his possession. See Taz, who writes that even if the property was not yet in the donor's possession, once it later reaches the recipient the gift can be effective, even with a davar shelo ba la'olam, under the rule that later seizure can validate in the manner discussed in siman 209. His language might imply that if Reuven used kinyan sudar through Shimon to transfer future property to Levi, it would suffice if the item later came only into Shimon's hands as the person acquiring on Levi's behalf. In my view that is incorrect. Later seizure helps with a davar shelo ba la'olam because of mistaken waiver: when the recipient himself later takes the object with the transferor's acquiescence, there is present consent and a present transfer. But if the item comes only to an intermediary, what waiver toward Levi exists? The original agency itself could not take hold for the future object, as Tosafot in Nazir 12 write that agency cannot be created for a davar shelo ba la'olam. Once the agency was void, the intermediary is no more than any unrelated person holding the item. His possession cannot serve as Levi's later seizure. Therefore Taz must mean that the property later came into the actual recipient Levi's possession and Levi himself sought to retain it.
