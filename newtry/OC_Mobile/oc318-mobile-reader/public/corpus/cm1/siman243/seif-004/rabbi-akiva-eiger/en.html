[Shulchan Aruch] There is an authority who says. Note: So writes the Tur in the name of Rabbeinu Yonah. See Shitah Mekubetzet in the name of Aliyot deRabbeinu Yonah, where it is explained that this depends on two resolutions. Regarding the rule that if he tells witnesses to acquire the field for the recipient and write him a deed, he may retract from the deed, one explanation is that even though they took possession with the intention that a deed be written, he may nevertheless retract from the deed and say, "If you are content with the sale without a deed, well and good; if not, I will return your money." Alternatively, the case is where he gave the witnesses two separate instructions, "Acquire and write," but did not say that they should acquire on condition that the deed be written. If, however, he told them to acquire on condition that the deed be written, he cannot retract from the deed. Rabbeinu Yonah then writes that if he said to the purchaser, "Acquire this field and I will write you a deed," he cannot retract, because the purchaser certainly took possession in reliance on receiving the deed. According to the first explanation, however, this is difficult, for why did the Gemara speak of witnesses rather than simply say that one told his fellow, "Acquire and I will write"? Perhaps it teaches a greater novelty. See there. It is thus clear that the Tur's citation of Rabbeinu Yonah refers to the latter, alternative explanation. According to that view, if he obligated himself by a kinyan to write a deed, he indeed cannot retract. But according to Rabbeinu Yonah's first explanation, even if he tells his fellow, "Acquire and I will write you a deed," he may still retract from the deed. Accordingly, the words of the Shulchan Aruch are very difficult, for in seif 10 it rules without qualification that even if they made a kinyan concerning the deed, it is merely a kinyan on words and he may retract. How, then, could one think that by saying, "Acquire and I will write," he cannot retract? What difference does it make that the purchaser took possession in reliance on the deed, when even an express kinyan to write is ineffective? This requires great study.
