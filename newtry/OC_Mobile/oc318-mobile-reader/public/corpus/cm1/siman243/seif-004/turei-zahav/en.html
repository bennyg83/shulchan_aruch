(See there, in the gloss: on condition that the deeds be written.) The Sema in seif katan 2 asks that if they married one another, the acquisition does not take effect. This is difficult, for at the beginning of Kiddushin the Gemara explicitly asks why kiddushin should not be effected through exchange, which is a formal act of acquisition, and answers that exchange can be made with an object worth less than a perutah, whereas a woman does not consent to be acquired for less than a perutah. If the acquisition of marriage were merely an acquisition of words, the Gemara should have rejected exchange for that reason. One may answer that there the proposal was that the woman be acquired to the man by that act itself, just as in ordinary kiddushin, and therefore the Gemara had to explain why an object worth less than a perutah is ineffective. Here, however, the transaction merely obligates her to accept kiddushin later, and that is an acquisition of words. Yet daily practice remains difficult, for one person commonly authorizes another through an act of acquisition to arrange a match for him. Perhaps the authorization empowers the agent to obligate him to a penalty. This appears to be the Rama's intent, since he did not mention penalties here. The Ir Shushan challenges this ruling from seif 7, where we do not say that others should fulfill the condition by giving two hundred zuz and thereby force the transaction to stand, because as to the recipient it is not a condition, since it is for his benefit and he should have stipulated it himself; it is therefore mere surplus speech. He says that this does not apply here, since the conditions benefit both parties, each stipulating that he receive a deed against the other for enforcement, and therefore the witnesses should fulfill the condition and the matter should stand. It seems to me that he did not carefully examine the Rosh's responsum, for otherwise the Rosh's own rulings would contradict each other, as both are his. In truth there is no difficulty. In seif 7 the condition depends on others, such as “on condition that they give him two hundred zuz,” and therefore one may ask why another should not fulfill the condition. Here the condition depends upon the grantor himself. He can certainly retract and say, “I do not wish to fulfill the condition that burdens me,” and the transaction consequently falls away. The Sema also disputes the Ir Shushan at length, but there was no need, for the latter's words refute themselves. The Ir Shushan assumed that this was the Rama's independent view and did not realize that it is the Rosh's ruling, and therefore ventured to distinguish between them.
