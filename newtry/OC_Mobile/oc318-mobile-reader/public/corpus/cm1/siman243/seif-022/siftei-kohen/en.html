And likewise four cubits, etc. The Ritva writes at the beginning of Bava Metzia in the name of some early authorities that four cubits effect acquisition only regarding a found object, not a gift. The Maggid Mishneh likewise writes in chapter 17 of the Laws of Robbery in the name of some early authorities. This also appears from Tosafot at the beginning of Bava Metzia 10a, s.v. Arba Amot. Although the Rosh and Ritva there write that the Yerushalmi in Gittin, chapter HaZorek, proves that the rule applies equally to a gift, one may answer that since the Babylonian Talmud states that the reason for the enactment is to prevent quarrels, this implies that it applies specifically to found objects and not to gifts. The Rama himself also cites this law above at the beginning of siman 200 in the name of some authorities. See what I wrote there. Tosafot there, and likewise in Ketubot 31b in the chapter Elu Naarot, write that the Sages did not institute acquisition by four cubits for theft. They instituted it only for found objects, to prevent quarrels, and for bills of divorce, because of the danger of leaving a woman unable to remarry. The Rosh likewise writes there. Although, in my humble opinion, the proof they draw from the passage in Elu Naarot is not conclusive, because according to the view that the Sages instituted four cubits even for theft, the baraita concerning one who drags an object out must still be interpreted according to Rav Sheshet, who holds that four cubits do not acquire in a public domain. The baraita must be explainable according to all amoraim, and according to those who disagree with Rav Sheshet there is indeed no difficulty. Nevertheless, their conclusion is correct in reason. Even according to the view that four cubits acquire a gift, theft differs because the object did not leave the owner's possession willingly. There is no basis for the Sages to assign another person's four cubits the power to remove property from its owner, because wherever the object is located it remains in the owner's domain, and the owner has not relinquished it. By what means should it leave the owner's possession? This appears clear to me. Maadaney Yom Tov at the beginning of Bava Metzia questions the Rosh: Since the Rosh rules that the law does not follow the view that four cubits acquire in a public domain, why did he need to press himself on this point? In my opinion this is not precise. The Rosh sought to prove that the Sages did not institute four cubits for theft, which has practical effect in an alleyway and at the sides of a public domain. Hagahot Maimoniyot and the Agudah, beginning of Bava Metzia, siman 19, likewise write in the name of Ri that for theft the Sages did not institute acquisition by four cubits even in an alleyway. This is straightforward.
