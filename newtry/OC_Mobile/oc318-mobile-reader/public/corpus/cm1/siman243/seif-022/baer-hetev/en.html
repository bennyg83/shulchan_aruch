Cubits. The Shach writes that the Ritva at the beginning of Bava Metzia cites an opinion among the early authorities that a person's four cubits acquire for him only with respect to a found object, but not a gift. The Maggid Mishneh, chapter 17 of Hilchot Gezeilah, likewise cites such an opinion, and this also appears at first glance from Tosafot in Bava Metzia 10a, s.v. "four cubits." Although the Rosh and Ritva there write that the Yerushalmi in the chapter HaZorek of Gittin implies that the same applies to a gift, it may nevertheless be argued that since the Babylonian Gemara explains the enactment as intended to prevent quarrels, it applies specifically to found objects and not gifts. The Rama too records this rule at the beginning of siman 200 only in the name of "some authorities," as explained there. Tosafot there and in the chapter Elu Na'arot also write that the Sages did not enact acquisition through four cubits for theft, but only for a found object, to prevent quarrels, and for a get, because of the concern of agunah. See the Shach's extended discussion.
