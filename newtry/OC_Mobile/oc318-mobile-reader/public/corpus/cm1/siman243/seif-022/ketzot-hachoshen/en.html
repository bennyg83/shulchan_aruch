Likewise a person's four cubits. Tosafot and Rosh write that the rabbinic acquisition of four cubits was not enacted for theft. My grandfather asked why Rosh needed this statement, since he also rules that four cubits do not acquire in the public domain. Later writers answered that it matters in an alley or at the side of the public domain. In my view my grandfather's question is exact. Even an actual private courtyard, which certainly can acquire property, does not make a person liable as a thief merely because an animal entered and he inwardly decided to steal it. Theft and robbery require an act. Rashi therefore speaks of closing the courtyard before the animal or bringing it into his domain. If an animal merely enters one's four cubits in an alley, thought alone cannot create liability. If he actively brings it into his four cubits, that very act is already meshichah in an alley, independently of the four cubit enactment. Therefore the four cubit rule would add nothing in an alley. It could matter only if four cubits acquired in the public domain, where meshichah does not. Since Rosh rejects that, my grandfather's difficulty is well founded.
