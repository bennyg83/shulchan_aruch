Likewise four cubits. See Shach, seif katan 9, who questions Ma'adanei Melech and says the practical difference is a side alley. Ketzot HaChoshen objects that with courtyard acquisition one also needs some act, such as locking before the object. If the animal was drawn into one's four cubits in a side alley, ordinary meshichah would already acquire there. If it entered the four cubits by itself, no act was performed. Ketzot therefore says the novelty makes more sense if four cubits acquire in the public domain, because meshichah does not acquire there. In my view his objection is surprising. One can perform a distinct act establishing the object within the four cubits without ordinary meshichah, such as tying the animal, holding it by hand so it cannot leave, or placing stones and pebbles in its way so it remains within the four-cubit area. For the law of airspace, see Even HaEzer siman 139.
