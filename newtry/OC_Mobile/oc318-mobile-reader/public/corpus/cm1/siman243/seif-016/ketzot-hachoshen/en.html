One who causes property to be acquired for a mentally incompetent person through a competent adult raises a real difficulty. Beit Yosef treated it as obvious that one may acquire for him in his absence, but Bava Metzia says that a minor has acquisition because he will naturally reach the age of agency, whereas a gentile who requires a conversion act does not. A permanently mentally incompetent person is not naturally destined to become capable of agency, and should therefore resemble the latter more than a minor. Maggid Mishneh therefore properly sought a proof. He cited the rule that a competent man who marries a mentally incompetent woman may write her a large ketubah, because he chose to bind his property for her. That proof is not conclusive. The ketubah can be understood as compensation for her service, and wages for labor can belong by Torah law even to a minor or incompetent person. Ramah's language in Rosh suggests the limitation more clearly. He says a person who has lucid and insane periods can receive a gift through another because he is destined to return to legal capacity, just like a minor who will mature. This implies that a person who is permanently incompetent does not acquire through another person. The same concern applies to a deaf person who never enters ordinary agency. Thus Beit Yosef's broad rule requires study.
