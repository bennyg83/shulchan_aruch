[Shach, subparagraph 9] Specifically regarding a found object, but not a gift. Note: In my opinion, there is proof that our Gemara too follows the Yerushalmi that four cubits acquire even a gift. In Gittin 78a, the mishnah says that when the item is closer to her, within four cubits, she acquires, and it explicitly includes a gift. The same is true of kiddushin. Thus, even in kiddushin, where there is no concern of leaving a woman unable to remarry, she acquires the money or document of kiddushin within four cubits, and the mishnah likewise teaches the rule for a gift and for a debt. In the Gemara there, Rabbi Yochanan says that the enactment was stated only for gittin and not for other matters, and the Gemara cites the clauses concerning kiddushin and debt. That discussion follows Rabbi Yochanan’s view that "closer to her" may mean even one hundred cubits. But according to Rav, who explains the mishnah as four cubits, the mishnah is literal: even for kiddushin and a creditor, four cubits acquire. Consider carefully.
