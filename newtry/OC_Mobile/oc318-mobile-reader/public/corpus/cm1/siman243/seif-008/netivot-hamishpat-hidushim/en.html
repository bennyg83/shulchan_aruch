But he does not collect from encumbered property. This is so even though there was a kinyan, and above in siman 39 many authorities rule that where there are witnesses to a kinyan, collection may be made from encumbered property. Likewise, even one who sells a field before witnesses normally creates sufficient publicity to allow collection from encumbered property. Nevertheless, where the obligor protests and tells the witnesses not to write, they are careful to comply and do not publicize the matter. See Beurim: the principal reason is that so long as the deed has not been written and the borrower protests, the borrower knows that no note will be in the lender's possession and therefore commonly pays without taking a receipt. He is consequently believed to say, "I paid." For that reason one may not collect from encumbered property. According to this, if the debt is still within its due date, collection may be made from encumbered property.
