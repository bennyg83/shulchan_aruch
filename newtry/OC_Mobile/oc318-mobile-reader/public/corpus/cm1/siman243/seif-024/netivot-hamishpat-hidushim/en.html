One who throws a purse. This is an unresolved Talmudic question whether airspace that is not destined to allow the object to come to rest, for example where it was thrown forcefully and it is known at the moment of the throw that it will not come to rest there, is legally treated as though the item were resting in that airspace. If it is treated as resting, the homeowner acquires it, whether as a gift or from ownerless property, and the giver or one who abandoned it cannot retract. If it is not treated as resting, then in a gift case we preserve the item under the prior owner's presumption. In an ownerless case, however, the item has already left the original owner's ownership and entered the airspace of the house before anyone else acquired it. We therefore give it to the homeowner, because he acquired it at least out of doubt before another person.
