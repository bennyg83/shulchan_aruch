He returned in the air. He said, “He who has come out of his house, and that he has come out of his house, and that he has been taken out of his house, and that he has not yet returned from his house, and that he is in the air of his house.”
And the owner of the house did not acquire, etc. This is difficult, because that is true specifically according to the Rosh and the Tur, who hold that possession is ineffective in an unresolved question. The Mechaber, however, rules like the Rambam that possession is effective in a monetary doubt, as above, siman 202, seif 3, and below, siman 248, seif 11, as well as simanim 376, 388, 390, and many other places. Accordingly, here too the owner of the house should acquire. It is strained to say that the case concerns an owner who did not take possession, because the wording, 'we leave it in the possession of the original owner,' does not imply this. Moreover, in Bedek HaBayit the Mechaber writes explicitly and without qualification that even if the object is in the possession of the owner of the house, it is removed from him and given to the donor, because it is left in the possession of the original owner. He mentions no dissenting view. Furthermore, in Bedek HaBayit he writes that the Tur's formulation, 'the owner of the house cannot remove it from his possession,' is imprecise, and here in the Shulchan Aruch he omitted the Tur's wording. This clearly implies that he follows what he wrote in Bedek HaBayit, that even if the owner of the house took possession, the object is removed from him. This requires study. See the Sema, seif katan 41, who writes that although the Tur states that it is not removed from the recipient's possession, the Tur nevertheless holds that if the owner of the house subsequently seized it, it is removed from him. This is the principal ruling according to the Rosh and the Tur, as I have written. See above, siman 212, seif 13, and examine carefully. The Bedek HaBayit and the Bach likewise write in accordance with this.
