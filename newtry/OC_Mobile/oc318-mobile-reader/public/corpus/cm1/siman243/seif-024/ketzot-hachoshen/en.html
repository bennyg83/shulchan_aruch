Shach asks why, since the issue is an unresolved Talmudic question, a recipient who seized the gift should not retain it under the Shulchan Aruch's general rule that seizure can help in a legal doubt. Chacham Tzvi answers that a gift never leaves the donor's ownership until it reaches the recipient, unlike ownerless property, which has already left its former owner. But that does not resolve Shach's question. The very uncertainty is whether the disputed act already transferred the gift. In many sales and gifts involving unresolved acquisition, the Shulchan Aruch allows the recipient to retain the object if he seized it, despite the original owner's prior presumption. Examples include land transferred to one person with movables to another and other unresolved acquisitions. Thus the existence of a prior owner does not by itself defeat seizure under Rambam and the Shulchan Aruch. The ruling here remains difficult under that approach. See Kuntres HaSefeikot.
