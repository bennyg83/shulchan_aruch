And he retracted while it was still in the airspace of the house. In the Derishah and Perishah I discussed at length why the Tur and Mechaber add that he retracted while the object was still in the airspace of his house. If airspace is not treated as though the object is resting there, he should be able to retract even after it leaves the house. The Gemara, Rosh, and Ran do not contain this addition. See there.
And the householder did not acquire. The Tur writes: Therefore, if the donor retracts, the householder cannot take it from him. This wording may imply specifically that if the donor first seizes it, it is not removed from him, but if the householder seizes first it is likewise not removed from him. That inference is not compelled. The Tur may simply mean that the property remains in the legal possession of its original owner. Even if the householder seizes after the retraction, it can be removed from him and returned to the original owner. This parallels the later case of ownerless property, where the former owner has already abandoned his legal possession. There, if another person seizes the object we remove it from that person and give it to the householder because the householder's domain had acquired it before the second seizure. See the Perishah for another explanation.
