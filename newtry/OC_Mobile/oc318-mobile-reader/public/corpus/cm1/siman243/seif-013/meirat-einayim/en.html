They return it to the donor's heirs. This concerns a gift deed containing no kinyan, where the recipient does not acquire until the deed reaches his hand. There is only a doubt whether perhaps it already reached the recipient and was later deposited with the person now holding it. Because a doubtful claim does not remove land from its existing ownership, the deed is returned to the donor's heirs. If the deed contained a kinyan, it would ordinarily be returned to the recipient named in it, as explained at the end of siman 250, unless there is evidence that it belongs to the donor, such as its being tied together with other documents unquestionably belonging to him. In such a case we infer that the donor had the deed written and made a kinyan subject to some condition that was never fulfilled. The Tur cites such a responsum of the Rosh. The same general subject is treated in siman 65. With a loan document, even one containing a kinyan, we return it to neither borrower nor lender because perhaps it was written in anticipation of a loan that was never actually made.
