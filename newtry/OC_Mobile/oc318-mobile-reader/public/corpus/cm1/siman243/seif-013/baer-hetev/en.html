Giver. The Sema writes that this concerns a gift document without a kinyan, where the recipient does not acquire until the document reaches his possession. The uncertainty is whether it had already reached the recipient and the recipient then deposited it with this custodian. According to the Sema, because of that uncertainty we do not remove the real property from its present possession, and the document is returned to the giver's heirs. This is incorrect. The Gemara and the authorities explicitly indicate that in such a case, even without a kinyan, the document is returned to neither party, as explained in siman 65, seif 5. Rather, the law here plainly derives from the responsum of the Rosh cited by the Tur, where it was expressly known that the giver had deposited the document, for example, because it was tied together with the giver's other documents. Likewise, the Sema's assertion that where a gift document contains a kinyan and was found tied with the giver's documents it is returned to the giver is also incorrect. The Rosh's reason is that from the moment of the kinyan the giver obligated himself and the document serves only as evidence, as he states explicitly in responsum 66:3. What difference, then, does it make that the giver deposited it? This is also clearly implied at the end of siman 250, where even though it is known that the document came from the giver, the recipient acquires. Even if one were to say that the distinction between documents found tied together and those not tied together concerns a case where it is not known that the giver made an ordinary kinyan before witnesses, still the Tur writes in siman 55 that even when it is known that the giver deposited the document with him, it is not returned to the giver. See the Bach there and the end of siman 250. Necessarily, therefore, the responsum of the Rosh cited here by the Tur, and the Mechaber's ruling in siman 65, concern specifically a document without a kinyan. Shach.
