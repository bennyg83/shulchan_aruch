They shall return it to the donor's heirs, etc. The Sema explains that the case concerns a deed of gift containing no act of acquisition, so the recipient does not acquire until the deed reaches his hand. Since there is room to suspect that it had already reached the recipient and that the recipient then deposited it with the present custodian, the ruling is that property is not removed on the basis of doubt and the deed is returned, etc. This is incorrect. The Gemara and poskim explicitly indicate that in such a case, even without an act of acquisition, the deed is returned neither to this party nor to that party, as I wrote above in siman 65, seif katan 6. Rather, the matter is straightforward. The Mechaber took this law from the responsum of the Rosh cited by the Tur. There the case expressly concerns a situation in which it was known that the donor had deposited the deed with the custodian, such as where it was tied together with another deed belonging to the donor, or a similar circumstance. The Mechaber therefore writes, 'it was found deposited with him,' etc. The Sema further writes that where a deed contains an act of acquisition, if the deeds were tied together it is returned to the donor. This too is incorrect, because the Rosh's reason is that from the time of the act of acquisition the donor obligated himself and the deed serves only as evidence, as he writes explicitly in responsum 66:3. What difference, then, does it make that the donor delivered it to the custodian? This is also explicit at the end of siman 250, where even though it is known that the deed came from the donor, the recipient acquires. Even if one were to say that the distinction between tied and untied deeds concerns a case in which it is unknown whether the donor effected an unspecified act of acquisition before witnesses, the Tur writes in siman 55 that even if it is known that the donor deposited the deed with the custodian, it is not returned to him. See the Bach there in siman 55 and at the end of siman 250. It necessarily follows that the responsum of the Rosh cited by the Tur here in seif 18, and by the Mechaber above in siman 65, concerns specifically a deed lacking an act of acquisition. The Sema also writes, 'It was likewise explained there that regarding a promissory note, even if it contains an act of acquisition,' etc., because perhaps the debtor wrote it intending to borrow but never borrowed. This too is incorrect, because from the time of the act of acquisition he obligated himself, as explained above in siman 39, seif 13. See what I write below at the end of siman 250 in explanation of the words of the Tur and Rama. Nevertheless, there is another reason not to return a promissory note, namely concern that the debt was repaid, as I wrote.
