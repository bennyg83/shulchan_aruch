(Seif 24: while it is still in the air.) Many ask why this condition is necessary. The Gemara itself merely asks: if one threw a purse through one entrance and it left through another, what is the law? Is airspace that is not destined to come to rest treated as though it came to rest, or not? It appears that the question indeed applies even if he retracts after the purse has already exited through the second entrance. Our master uses the formulation "while it is still in the air" because of the following clause concerning an object destined to come to rest. There, retraction is relevant only while it is still in the air. The unresolved question too must be speaking of the same type of situation, because it asks whether airspace not destined to come to rest is treated like airspace that is destined to come to rest. Thus both must concern the same stage.
