In a deed. Meaning, even though the witnesses made a chazakah on the field for the recipient, so that the giver can no longer retract from giving the body of the field. The Tur concludes: 'Even though he transferred the field to him through a kinyan.' This means that although an ordinary kinyan is presumed intended for writing, as stated in siman 39, that is only so long as the transferor has not protested before witnesses. Until then the witnesses may write a deed about the kinyan without obtaining separate permission. But if the transferor later protests before witnesses, his protest is effective even if the deed has already been written, and the witnesses may not deliver it to the recipient, because he can say, 'I do not want many deeds issued against me, which would reduce confidence in my assets.' Responsa Rosh gives the reason that writing the deed itself would amount to a kinyan devarim, as the Mechaber writes in seif 10. Sema.
