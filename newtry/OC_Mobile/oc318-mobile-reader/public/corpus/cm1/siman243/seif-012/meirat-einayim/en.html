Responsum of the Rosh explains that once the deed states that Reuven transferred the courtyard to Shimon by kinyan, the physical deed need not reach Shimon during Reuven's lifetime. Nor must another person have acquired it on Shimon's behalf during Reuven's life. The deed serves merely as evidence, because Shimon already acquired the courtyard at the time of the kinyan, and one may acquire a benefit for a person in his absence. The rule that there is no deed after death does not apply where the deed merely proves an earlier completed kinyan. Even if the document was returned to the donor, the gift itself was not thereby voided. The Sages disagree with Rabban Shimon ben Gamliel and hold that returning the deed does not return the gift until the recipient reconveys the property through a valid kinyan. If the deed is found in the donor's hand, however, and the kinyan witnesses are no longer available, and the donor claims that the kinyan was conditional and the condition was never fulfilled, he may perhaps be believed through a migo that he could have destroyed the deed before it was established in beit din. The same defense is pleaded for his heirs after his death. But where the deed is not found in the donor's possession and the recipient says that the donor told him during life that he had transferred it by kinyan and that the deed remained merely as evidence on deposit, the recipient is believed because he could have claimed that the deed itself came into his possession during the donor's lifetime and no one could contradict him. This is the setting of the Mechaber's ruling. If the deed had been in the hands of a known third person, we would ask that person how he received it. If the third person forgot or died, different rules apply, as explained in siman 65. The Mechaber abbreviated where explanation was needed.
