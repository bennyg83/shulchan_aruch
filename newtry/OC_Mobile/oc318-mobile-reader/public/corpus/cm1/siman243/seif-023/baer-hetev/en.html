Father. This ruling requires study. The Nimukei Yosef writes that a minor girl who has a father has no independent legal hand, as stated in the chapter HaTekabel, and that this is also the Rif's view. It is well known, however, that Rashi, Tosafot, the Baal HaMaor, the Itur, and the Rosh disagree with the Rif there and hold that even a minor girl who has a father has an independent legal hand. The Mechaber himself cites the dissenting view in Even HaEzer, siman 141, seif 4. Moreover, it appears to me that even the Nimukei Yosef spoke only regarding a get, not regarding a found object or gift. Although the laws of a found object are derived from those of a get with respect to a courtyard being equivalent to one's hand, this particular point cannot be derived from a get. The reason a minor girl cannot receive her get when she has a father is not an inherent deficiency in her own legal capacity. Rather, because Scripture compares divorce to betrothal and grants the father authority to betroth his minor daughter from the verse, "I gave my daughter," her own hand has no standing in matters of kiddushin and gittin where her father's hand is operative. The Torah displaced her hand in that sphere and vested the authority in her father. She nevertheless does possess a legal hand, as shown by the fact that when she has no father she may receive her own get. Thus, that limitation applies specifically to gittin. If any person gives her an item as a gift, there is no reason to say that she fails to acquire it merely because she has a father. Since she possesses a legal hand, she likewise possesses a courtyard capable of acquiring on her behalf. Examine this carefully. See Tosafot Rid to Kiddushin 44a and the Ritva there. Shach.
