A female minor who has no father. See Shach, seif katan 11. His view is reasonable. If a gift is given to the minor in a manner where the father has no right in it, as explained in Even HaEzer siman 37, or even according to those who ordinarily give the father rights where the gift is expressly conditioned that the father have no control, then why should the girl's courtyard right be canceled? Her courtyard is modeled after her hand, and for this gift she is legally like a girl whose father has died. Therefore, in my view, Rama speaks only where the acquisition would legally belong to the father. This is clear with a found object, which belongs to the father. The same applies to a gift given to a minor daughter supported at her father's table where the law gives the benefit to the father, as explained in siman 270. Since the courtyard would not be acquiring for the girl but for the father, it cannot be treated as her hand against his superior right. A minor girl's courtyard acquisition for found objects and gifts is learned from gittin. In gittin itself, where she has a father and the Torah vested the relevant hand-right in him, her courtyard does not function independently in the same way. The derived monetary law cannot be stronger than its source. Beit Shmuel in Even HaEzer siman 141 likewise writes that a female minor's courtyard while her father is alive depends on the dispute between Rif and Rosh.
