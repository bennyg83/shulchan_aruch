Likewise, one who takes only the improvement. It appears the meaning is as follows. If his hand is on the upper side and the expense exceeds the improvement, the law ordinarily entitles him to the expense, as explained above. If he says, "I do not wish to take the expense, which exceeds the improvement, through an oath. I prefer to take the lesser improvement without an oath. Let them assess it for me," we listen to him. The case is one where it is visibly clear that the expense exceeded the improvement, though the exact amount is unknown. Thus "he takes only the improvement" means he elects to take the smaller amount. The novelty is that the other party cannot say, "Swear and take according to your ordinary right, or take nothing."
