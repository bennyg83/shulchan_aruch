The person who descended is believed. Mechaber gives the reason that we can tell the owner, "Your liability has not yet been formally assessed." Ketzot HaChoshen writes that the primary reason is that the legal amount due to an unauthorized improver is not a rule known to everyone, as explained in siman 157, whereas where the amount is fixed by clear law the debtor is not believed to say he paid. In my view Mechaber's explanation is necessary. The rule applies whenever formal appraisal is still required, and this can include even one who descended with permission. In an authorized case the owner certainly knows that some payment is due. Nevertheless, until experts appraise the improvement and expenses, the precise debt is not a fixed known amount. Thus "the law is not known" here means not merely that ordinary people do not know the legal doctrine, but that the actual amount of liability cannot be known without a factual valuation. Accordingly, where laborers built for a person even without formal prior authorization, and it is known that he was actively seeking workers, while the daily wage and the prices of timber and bricks are fixed and commonly known, the worker can be believed to say he was paid according to the ordinary rules. The amount can be calculated without a special expert appraisal. The present case concerns agricultural improvement of a field, where the value of the planting, the expenditure, and the resulting appreciation are not ordinarily known without expert assessment. Until that assessment is performed, the improver is believed under Mechaber's rationale.
