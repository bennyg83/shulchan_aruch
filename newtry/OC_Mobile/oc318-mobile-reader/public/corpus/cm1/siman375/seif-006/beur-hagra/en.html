One who enters, etc., specifically, etc., the courtyards, etc. This is learned from the law of a field, which distinguishes land normally planted from land not normally planted. See the Yerushalmi at the end of chapter 3 of Bava Batra, where Rabbi Nasa says that ruins were not given to be built. Nimukei Yosef likewise equates a house with a field, though he holds that an ordinary ruin is normally intended for rebuilding. The Rashba disagrees, as cited in the gloss to seif 7.

(Collected note) The landowner said to him, etc. Bava Kamma 21a, in the incident there. The Rashba explains that the claim concerns rent for use of the land. Although minor heirs must ultimately reimburse necessary expenditures, collection cannot be made from them until adulthood, and even then they may perhaps tell him to remove his wood and stones. He writes that this applies even to a ruin normally intended for rebuilding. Although the Rashba later raises a question against his own explanation, the objection is not conclusive. (End of note.)
