A small, obstetric wife made a regulation and a bamboar in the IDF in the column and in the words of the author in the diaspora:
And his partner in the field was a sign of the column and the author:
Like one who entered with permission. In the preceding seif I wrote in the names of Nimukei Yosef, Ran, and Raah that such a person does not have the complete law of one who entered with permission regarding a case where the expense exceeds the improvement. The same applies to one who incurs expenses on his wife's property, as Terumat HaDeshen writes explicitly, and as I cited above at the end of siman 178 in my comments on Rama. Darkei Moshe further cites Rabbi Yehudah son of the Rosh concerning Reuven, whose house was breached and who went far away. The community told a scribe to rebuild it for himself so that the breach would not endanger the town. He did so. Later another person bought Reuven's house from Reuven's stepdaughter. The ruling was that even though it was not absolutely established that the house belonged to the stepdaughter, the scribe was treated as one who entered another's property without permission. He is removed, and the property is given to the person who purchased from the stepdaughter.
