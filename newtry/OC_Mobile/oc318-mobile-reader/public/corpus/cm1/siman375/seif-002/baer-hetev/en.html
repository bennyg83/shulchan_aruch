We heed him. The Rosh and Ramah hold that even where the field is ordinarily planted, we heed the owner if he says this. The Kesef Mishneh writes that this is also the Rambam's view, unlike the Maggid Mishneh, who understands the Rambam to speak only of land that is not ordinarily planted. Sema.
Which weakens. Rashi explains that the plantings have already weakened the soil by drawing nourishment from it, and since they caused monetary loss to the landowner, for this reason even outside Eretz Yisrael we do not heed the planter's request to remove them. It appears that this is likewise the view of the Rambam and the Mechaber, although they wrote only that the plantings weaken the land. Ibid.
