We listen to him. The Rosh and Ramah hold that even if the field is ordinarily intended for planting, we listen to him when he makes this request. Nimukei Yosef writes that this is also the view of the Mechaber and Rambam, unlike the Maggid Mishneh, who explains Rambam as speaking only of a field not ordinarily intended for planting. See the Derishah.
Because it weakens the land, Rashi. Meaning, the plantings have already weakened the land by drawing nourishment from it. Since they caused monetary loss to the landowner, even outside Eretz Yisrael we do not listen to the person who wishes to uproot them. End of Rashi's explanation. It appears that Rambam and the Mechaber agree, even though they use the phrase "weakens the land," unlike the Ir Shushan, who writes that the weakening occurs specifically through uprooting the trees.
