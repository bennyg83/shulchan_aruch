A name for the genius
Beit Yosef cites Rabbi Yechiel son of Rosh concerning Reuven who traveled far away, whose house collapsed and created a breach between the Jewish and gentile areas. The community told a scribe to rebuild the house for himself so that no damage result from the breach. He did so. Reuven died, and another person later purchased the house from Reuven's stepdaughter, his wife having already died and having no children. The purchaser entered under his deed, while the occupant demanded proof that the seller owned the house. The response was that under the law of the Gemara it is more proper to remove the person who certainly entered without the owner's permission, appraise his claim according to one who descends into another's ruin without permission, and leave the house with the purchaser, who may possibly have bought it from one who inherited it from her mother. Some reliance may also be placed on the presumption that witnesses do not sign unless the matter was done properly.

The land owner
Regarding rent for the period already occupied, Beit Yosef cites Nimukei Yosef on the Mishnah concerning one who sells his olive trees for wood, Bava Metzia chapter 8, 100b. Rashba infers that if the property was initially not for rent at all, the occupant need not pay rent; but if it was initially for rent even at a small rate, he must pay according to the current rental value. Rashba proves this from Bava Kamma chapter Keitzad HaRegel, 20b, as explained in siman 363.
