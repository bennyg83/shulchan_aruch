Courtyards are ordinarily fit for building and adding houses or upper stories. Therefore, one who builds in another's courtyard is like one who plants a field intended for planting. He receives the amount a person would pay for that useful building, provided it suits the courtyard and local custom. Rama: The builder cannot say, 'I will remove my building.' If he rented the building to others and received rent, that is deducted from his expenses even if he normally would not rent. If he lived there and caused even a small loss to the landowner, he must pay the full benefit received. If Reuven left a dilapidated house and Shimon occupied it, spending money necessary to prevent collapse and also plastering and decorating it, Reuven must reimburse only what was necessary to save the house, not the decorative work, because he may say he did not need it. Once Reuven pays what is due, Shimon must leave.
