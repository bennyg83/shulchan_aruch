If a worker performs a repair but violates an express condition that it be completed by a fixed time, and the repair cannot now be removed from an expensive movable, he may resemble one who entered and improved another's property without authorization and may receive only his expenses up to the amount of the benefit conferred. If, however, it was clear that even after the deadline the owner still needed this particular repair and could not obtain it otherwise, the worker may resemble one who returns lost property rather than a wholly unauthorized improver. In that case the agreed price can still be reduced because of his breach, but he may be entitled to the fair value of the work as assessed by experts. Even if no other worker could have performed the repair, valuation according to experts remains appropriate, analogous to the rules governing one who saves another's property at exceptional expense, such as the cases of the ferry and of honey and wine.
