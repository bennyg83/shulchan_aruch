And some say. In truth, all authorities, including the Rambam, agree that if all workers will hire themselves only for four, the employer must pay the agent four, as stated at the beginning of siman 336. The Rama's phrase "and some say" does not indicate a dispute. Since the Beit Yosef implied that the Rambam disagreed, the Rama used that phrase, but in the Darkei Moshe he makes clear that all agree. The Sema and Bach likewise agree, and this is primary. See Responsa Maharam Mintz, end of siman 28. Shach.
Who benefited him. The Ramah writes that the agent does not take more than four from the employer, even if the labor is worth more, so that he not conduct business with another's property. The Tur cites this. Meaning, the agent never receives more than the amount he fixed with the workers, even where they benefited the employer beyond that amount. It is obvious that there is no distinction whether the agent added one-fourth or more, unlike the Beit Yosef. The Ir Shushan also explains this with difficulty. The matter is as stated, and so explain the Sema and Bach. Ibid.
The employer. If he hired them without qualification and the workers do not know the work is not his, the law is as though he said, "Your wages are upon me," as below, siman 339, seif 7. See what I wrote there.
Three. A person's intent follows the lower market rate, and the workers were responsible to inform the employer that they would not hire themselves for less than four. The Gemara teaches that even if the worker himself is the employer, he receives only three. The Tur implies that even if some workers will hire themselves only for four, he receives only three, even if he is the employer. The Gemara implies that where the worker himself is the employer, he has a complaint against the agent even if all workers hire themselves for three, because he can say: had you not told me four, it would have been beneath my dignity to hire myself. See Responsa Rashdam, simanim 335 and 372. Ibid.
A complaint. Since some workers are hired for four, they could have exerted themselves and found someone who would hire them for four. So it is in the Gemara and authorities. It follows that if all workers are hired for three, they do not even have a complaint against him. Ibid.
Recognizable. For example, he hired them to dig a pit and it filled with water, making it impossible to assess the quality of the excavation. Gemara, and similarly in analogous cases. Ibid.
Four. This implies that even if all workers are generally hired for three, if their work is worth four he pays four. This is explicit in the Tur and evident in the Gemara. The Rosh, however, implies that this applies only where some workers are hired for four, and Rabbeinu Yerucham explains so explicitly. It seems that they mean only that some are hired for four to perform workmanship of this higher quality, excluding a case where no one is ever hired for four even for work of this quality; then it is obvious they cannot receive four. The Gemara and Tur concern a case where all are hired for three for ordinary work, but these workers performed superior work. Accordingly, the Tur, Rosh, and Rabbeinu Yerucham do not disagree in practice, and both formulations are true. Perhaps this is what the Beit Yosef meant in Bedek HaBayit when he wrote that the Rosh's words can be reconciled. Ibid.
