And one loses one from his own pocket. This is where there are workers who hire themselves for three, as explained below. See Responsa Maharam Mintz, end of siman 28.
And some say, etc. In truth, all the poskim, including Rambam, agree that if no workers can be hired for less than four, the employer must pay the agent four, as explained below at the beginning of siman 336. Although the Rama phrases this as "some say," he does not intend to record a dispute. Since the Beit Yosef's words imply that Rambam does not hold this way, the Rama used the phrase "some say." In truth, the Rama's position is that everyone agrees, as he writes in Darchei Moshe. The Sema and Bach likewise agree, and this is the primary ruling.
According to the benefit he conferred upon him. Ramah writes that the agent does not receive more than four from the employer, even if the work is worth more, because he may not conduct business with another person's property. The Tur cites this. Meaning, the agent never receives more than the amount he fixed with the workers, even if they benefited the employer beyond that amount, because he cannot profit from the employer's property. He receives the amount of the benefit only up to the amount he fixed with them. Obviously there is no distinction whether the agent added one quarter or more, contrary to the Beit Yosef. The Ir Shushan also explains this with difficulty. The matter is straightforward as I have written, and the Sema and Bach explain it likewise.
And if he told them, your wages are upon the employer, etc. If he hired them without qualification and the workers did not know that the work was not his, the law is as though he said, "your wages are upon me," as explained below, siman 339, seif 7. See there.
He gives them only three. This is because a person is presumed to intend the lower market rate, and it was upon the workers to disclose to the employer that they would not hire themselves for less than four. See Responsa Rashdam, simanim 335 and 372. The Gemara states that even if the worker is a homeowner, he receives only three. The Tur implies that even if there are workers who will hire themselves only for four, he receives only three, even though he is a homeowner. The Gemara implies that a homeowner worker has a grievance against the agent even if all workers are hired for three, because he can say, "Had you not told me four, it would have been beneath my dignity to hire myself out."
And they have a grievance. Since there are people who hire themselves for four, they could have exerted themselves and found someone who would hire them for four. This is the rule in the Gemara and poskim. Accordingly, if all workers are hired for three, they do not even have a grievance against him.
Where the value of their work cannot be recognized. For example, he hired them to dig a ditch and it filled with water, so because of the water it is impossible to determine the value of their digging. This is the case in the Gemara, and the same applies to similar cases.
And if it is worth four, he gives them, etc. This implies that even if all ordinary workers hire themselves for three, once the work they actually performed is worth four, he gives them four. The Tur says this explicitly and it is also proved by the Gemara. The Rosh, however, implies that this applies specifically where there are workers who hire themselves for four, and Rabbeinu Yerucham explicitly explains it this way. In my humble opinion, they mean only that there must be workers who would charge four for this superior quality of work that was actually performed. They exclude a case where no one would charge four even for work of this quality, for then it is obvious that they cannot receive four. The Gemara and Tur discuss a case where all workers take three for ordinary work, but some would take four for work of this superior quality. Accordingly, the Tur, Rosh, and Rabbeinu Yerucham do not disagree as a matter of law, and both formulations are true. Perhaps this is what the Beit Yosef intended when he wrote in Bedek HaBayit that the words of the Rosh can be reconciled with some difficulty. Examine carefully.
