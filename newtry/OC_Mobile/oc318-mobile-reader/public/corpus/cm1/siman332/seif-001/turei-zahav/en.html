(Seif 4: As other workers are hired) If an employer says, “Work for me for four, as others are hired,” and the workers remain silent, why assume they relied on the wage of others rather than accepting the express offer of four? A doubtful inference should not annul an agreed wage and extract additional money. The proof is this same seif: if the employer told an agent to hire for four, the agent hired for three, and the workers said, “As the employer said,” they are deemed to have accepted three, although the employer in fact authorized four. Since their reliance may have been on the agent's express words rather than the undisclosed statement of the employer, the agent's agreement controls. Rav Yerucham's cases, cited by the Beit Yosef, involve workers who expressly asked, “What do you pay the others?” and thus clearly relied on that comparison. Likewise, the Rama's nearby language says that the workers themselves referred to what their colleagues received. Without such an express indication, reliance is on the stated wage. Everyday sales are similar: a seller may say that another buyer offered a certain price and he refused, and the sale is not void merely because no such offer existed, unless the buyer made clear that he relied upon that representation.
