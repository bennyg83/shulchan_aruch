And he loses, etc. Rambam's language. Rif and Rosh likewise write there: the rule we adopt, etc., where he said to them "for three," etc., and afterward he himself takes three from the employer, etc.
Others say, etc. Rashi likewise holds this, s.v. "let us see, etc." Darkei Moshe writes that everyone concedes to this, since Rif and Rosh cite the baraisa, "one who hires a worker, etc.," as stated above at the beginning of siman 336. All the later authorities likewise rule so. See Sema and Shach.
And if, etc. The Gemara there: "it was necessary, etc.; let us see, etc."
If there was, etc. There: "it was necessary where there are, etc."
When does this apply, etc. There: alternatively, always, etc. This also refers to the first clause where the employer said he would give them, etc. In such a case he always gives four, even if all workers ordinarily hire for only three. This is also implied by the Gemara's wording "alternatively." It is likewise implied by Rif and Rosh, who write that this applies where the work is not identifiable, etc., but where they have a grievance, etc. Necessarily they refer to such a case, for otherwise the workers already have a grievance as stated above, and their grievance would be against the agent. The Rosh's statement, "and what Rav Alfasi wrote, etc., that we require superior work only where, etc.," is not exclusive. The same law applies here. He merely excludes a case where everyone hires for four, in which he wrote that even though there is, etc.; and also excludes where the agent said, "your wages are upon me," as stated there.
(Collected note) When does this apply, etc. This refers to the first clause, as stated in seif 3, "therefore they appraise, etc.," which implies every case even where everyone hires for three. But the statement that they have a grievance applies even where their work is identifiable and is worth no more than ordinary work. Nevertheless they can have a grievance because there is, etc. Likewise where their work is not identifiable, even if all workers ordinarily hire for only three, as stated in the sugya there.
