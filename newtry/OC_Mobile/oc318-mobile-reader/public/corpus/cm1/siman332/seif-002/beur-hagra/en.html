Although, etc. Rambam and Tur rule this way, as do Rif and Rosh. The workers knowingly accepted the arrangement. The Gemara's final answer shows that the first explanation is unnecessary and that the rule applies whether the agent said, 'Your wages are upon me,' or 'upon the employer.' Rosh's discussion of the unresolved question also concerns an agent who said that the employer would pay. Tosafot's explanation that the agent said, 'Your wages are upon me,' was needed only for the first clause, not the latter one.
(Supplement) Although, etc.; and there is no distinction, etc. The earlier answers distinguish whether the agent undertook payment and whether the work was superior, while the final answer makes no distinction. (End of supplement.) See 81.
