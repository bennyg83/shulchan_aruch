See Sema, seif katan 8, citing Terumat HaDeshen that even where local custom is for employers to pay travel expenses separately from wages, the stated wage can indicate that expenses were included. Ketzot HaChoshen asks from the rule that in a place where employers customarily provide food, the employer must provide it even if the wage is higher than normal; we do not say the wage reveals that food was included. The answer is that Terumat HaDeshen's case itself departed from the custom even according to the worker's version. Local practice there was to negotiate and settle the expenses explicitly before the mission. Here the worker did not demand that in advance. Since both sides' descriptions already involve a deviation from standard practice, the amount of wages can be used as evidence to interpret the agreement.
They receive only three. Ketzot HaChoshen asks why this differs from the bathhouse case in siman 312, where doubt can require payment because land stands in the owner's chazakah. Here too, a worker is in possession of his own labor. He answers that the worker voluntarily performed the work, which is like placing the result in the employer's hands with permission. In the bathhouse case, by contrast, entering during the disputed thirteenth month can be treated as occupation without permission. On that basis he proposes that if Reuven authorizes an agent to rent a house from Shimon for fifty, the agent tells Shimon forty, and Shimon says, "as the renter said," the renter may owe the higher amount because he entered the property with permission under a doubtful price. This is essentially correct, but only if the agent informed Reuven that the landlord had said "as the renter said," so Reuven knew of the uncertainty before entering. Then the case resembles the bathhouse, where both sides knew of the unresolved term and the tenant entered despite it. If the agent did not tell Reuven the landlord's words and merely reported that he rented the house for forty, Reuven entered on the definite understanding of forty. Even if the landlord subjectively meant to rely on the renter's higher authorization, there was no completed direct price agreement between landlord and renter. The case resembles living in another's courtyard without a fixed price, and the renter pays only the ordinary value.
