Therefore, the work is appraised, etc. This case is treated as though the workers had not said, ‘We accept whatever the employer said.’ It follows the rule in seif 1. If the agent said, ‘Your wages are upon me,’ he pays them four and recovers from the employer the value of the benefit. If he said, ‘Your wages are upon the employer,’ the work is appraised: if it is worth four they receive four from the employer, even if ordinary workers are hired for three, provided that work of this quality would be hired only for four. If their work is not worth four, then even if some workers demand four the employer pays only three, since some workers accept three. If all workers can be hired only for four, he pays four. This is the view of the Maggid Mishneh, Nimukei Yosef, the other authorities, Rabbeinu Yerucham, and the Rosh. The Sema and the Rama in Bedek HaBayit correctly state that the Tur contains a scribal error. The Bach’s lengthy objection rests upon a strained interpretation. The Tur’s own abridgment of the Rosh states expressly that where the employer told the agent three, the agent told the workers four and placed the wages on the employer, and the workers said, ‘As the employer said,’ they receive four only if their work is worth four. Since the agent departed from his instructions, the employer did not hire them at all beyond the value he actually received. The Ateret Zekenim and Maharam Lublin rule accordingly.
