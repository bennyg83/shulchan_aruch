Their intent is only that he give, etc. Do not say that because the workers explicitly said, "As the employer says," the employer need pay only three even if their work is worth four or all workers earn four, since they explicitly accepted whatever the employer had said. The ruling teaches otherwise. Their purpose was not to diminish the four promised by the agent. They meant only that if the employer had authorized more than four they should receive the larger amount. Nevertheless, the law does not simply follow their subjective intent. Their status is the same as in seif 1 where the employer told the agent to hire for three and the agent offered four, and the workers did not respond, "as the employer said." If the agent told them, "Your wages are upon the employer," then because he exceeded his authority it is as though he never hired them on the employer's behalf. They performed the work for the employer on their own initiative and receive the value of their work or the prevailing wage. If he told them, "Your wages are upon me," he must in any case pay the four he promised. The same applies here. This is the Mechaber's and Rama's intent. The Rosh states explicitly that their statement "as the employer says" is treated as though they had not said it at all. This is the view of all the poskim. The text of the Tur must therefore be adjusted accordingly, as I explained in the Derishah. Terumat HaDeshen adds a case. Reuven hired Shimon to travel on his business for ten dinars. Before leaving, Shimon told others that he also expected travel expenses beyond the ten, but Reuven was not present so he could not be informed. Reuven later refused, saying that although other workers commonly earned only four, he had offered ten precisely to include the travel expenses. Terumat HaDeshen ruled for Reuven. Regarding workers, the amount itself can indicate the parties' understanding, even though price alone is not generally decisive regarding houses. See simanim 214 and 220.
They receive only three, etc. Here too their law is as though they had not returned and said, "as the employer says." In the preceding seif the ruling is that in every event they receive only three because they knowingly accepted that amount. The Gemara leaves the issue unresolved, so we rule leniently for the defendant and do not extract more.
