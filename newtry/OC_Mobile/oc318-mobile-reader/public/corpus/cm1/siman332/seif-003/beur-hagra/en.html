Therefore, etc. Rambam's language. So too Rif and Rosh: "it is obvious, etc.; and if the work is worth four, etc."
And if he said, etc. The Rosh likewise writes there. Regarding Rav Alfasi's words, etc., the Rosh initially assumed that the Rif explains the case as Tosafos do there, s.v. "da'ataihu, etc.," where the agent said, "your wages are upon me." He therefore asks that in such a case he must always give four, for only where the agent said, "your wages are upon the employer," and some workers hire for that amount, etc., is there room for the distinction. He answers that the Rif actually explains the case as one where the agent said, "your wages are upon the employer." Tosafos likewise hold this, as does Rashi there, s.v. "da'ataihu," as Maggid Mishneh writes, and so rule all the poskim. The Tur contains a scribal error, as Beit Yosef in Bedek HaBayis notes. Darkei Moshe likewise expresses surprise at the Tur. The rule is that this law is identical to seif 1 where they did not say, "we agree to what the employer said," unlike Bach, who struggles greatly to distinguish them.
