And it was found that they are hired for more. Sema implies that if an employer expressly told workers that most workers are hired for four, and it later turns out that most are hired for more, he must pay the prevailing majority rate rather than the lowest minority rate. Rabbi Akiva Eiger disagrees. Where workers mislead an employer and he nevertheless agrees to pay according to the prevailing majority, he accepted that standard. But where the employer falsely says that most workers receive five, he is plainly offering only five, not agreeing to pay whatever the true majority receives. If no binding rate was established, the workers must accept the lower legally applicable rate. This distinction is straightforward.
To give him an object as his wage. See Panim Meirot I:92 and Ketzot HaChoshen and Netivot HaMishpat concerning a promise to pay a worker with a particular object or debt instrument, and whether the worker may demand its value instead. The cited authorities distinguish between acquisition of the object itself and the employer's monetary obligation for the wage.
He may afterward give him its value. Ketzot HaChoshen writes that even where the promised object itself was not acquired, its value must be paid because employment is binding by verbal agreement. If the employer wishes to deliver the very object promised, the worker generally cannot insist on money instead. Ketzot raises the question what happens if the object burns before delivery. Netivot HaMishpat compares it to an expressly designated lien and inclines that if it was destroyed the employer may be exempt from replacement money, although the matter is debated. The same discussion applies where the promised wage was something not yet in existence, such as future fruit. Even though the future object itself cannot be acquired, its monetary value can become the wage obligation. Ketzot would assess according to the fruit's value after it grows, while other authorities assess at the time the work is completed. This also illuminates Yaakov's arrangement with Lavan concerning future offspring.
