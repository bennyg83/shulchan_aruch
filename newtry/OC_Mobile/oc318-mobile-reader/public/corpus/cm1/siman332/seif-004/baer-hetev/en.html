And they accepted. This is an unresolved question in the Gemara, and the authorities rule leniently for the person in possession. Semag, positive commandment 89, p. 173b, likewise writes that since it was unresolved, possession controls. Accordingly, if the workers seized four, we do not remove it from them according to the Rambam, Semag, and those who hold that seizure is effective in an unresolved Talmudic question. This applies even if their work is not worth four, according to the Rif, Rosh, and Nimukei Yosef, because the Gemara's question applies even when the work is not worth four: since the employer said four and the workers said, "as the employer said," perhaps they accepted four. It is possible that the Gemara's question applies even when all workers hire themselves only for three. So appears from the Rif, Rosh, and Nimukei Yosef, who write: although they did not perform work worth four, they take four because the employer contemplated and accepted paying four, and the question remained unresolved. Shach.
To their fellows. Their later statement, "as our fellows did," did not mean that they trusted the agent's assertion that he gave the others only four and accepted that amount. Rather, they intended to improve their position: if he gave the others more, he should give them more as well, even though some workers are hired for less and their own work is not worth that much. This differs from the Mechaber's case where the employer said four, the agent told them three, and they replied, "as the employer said," in which case they receive only three. Here the agent deceived them by saying that he had hired the others for four; there he merely told them three without tying their hiring to the wages of other workers. This is the Sema's explanation. The Shach objects to the Rama's ruling and writes that it requires study, because the employer explicitly said, "Work for me for four," and did not consent to more. He proves this at length from the Gemara and Yerushalmi.
For more. The Sema explains that all workers are hired for more than four, but at different rates, such as five, six, seven, or eight. The rule that he pays them according to the lowest rate therefore means that the agreement for four is void because all workers are hired for more, but he pays five, the lowest actual rate. The same applies when workers deceive the employer. Rabbeinu Yerucham in the name of the Rashba, cited by the Beit Yosef at the end of this siman, does not state the final clause in the same way. See the Shach, who explains at length that they need not disagree.
An object. This law applies specifically to a tangible object, not to a promissory note, as stated in Piskei Mahari, siman 230, cited briefly in the Darkei Moshe. The Sema explains that regarding a promissory note, if he does not give him the note the condition was not fulfilled and he receives nothing, because the employer obligated himself to give him his wages on that condition. Accordingly, if he wishes, he may give him the market value of the promissory note, as in the law of one who forgives a debt instrument, siman 66, seif 32. So it seems to me. Shach.
