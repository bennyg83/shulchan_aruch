If the employer himself hired them for a sela, the market wage fell, and he showed displeasure, but they appeased him verbally, he cannot claim that he was appeased only on condition that their wages be reduced. They may answer that they appeased him on the understanding that the work would be done well, which they did. Rama: Some distinguish where the employer explicitly said he would pay only a lower amount and could have withdrawn without grievance. Even if they then appeased him, he pays only what he stated. Conversely, if wages rose and the workers showed displeasure but he appeased them verbally, they cannot claim an increase; he may say that he intended only to add food and drink, which he did.
