Above, siman 14, seif 4. This is the correct reference. The printed reference to siman 5 is a typographical error, because siman 5 contains nothing about writing a ruling. Even siman 14 does not concern an ordinary memorandum of the judgment. It concerns a case where one party says, "Write for me on what basis you judged me," intending to take the written decision to a greater beit din and challenge it. In that writing they record the claims and the basis of the ruling. It is somewhat difficult why Rama cross-references specifically that type of ruling. Perhaps he was troubled by the Tur and Mechaber's wording, "one litigant asked, etc." If this referred to an ordinary judgment document, they should have said simply, "when the judges come to write the judgment." Rama therefore understands that precision in the wording is particularly necessary where one litigant requests a written decision for review by a greater beit din. Then they write, "from their words, so-and-so emerged entitled," so that the document not appear false and so that the dissenting judge not be misrepresented before the higher court as having joined the error. In an ordinary judgment record, perhaps it is enough to write simply, "so-and-so owes so-and-so such-and-such." The Gemara in Zeh Borer, however, appears to apply the special wording to every divided beit din, and Rashi and Nimukei Yosef explain it that way. This requires study. Darkei Moshe also cites Responsa at the end of Chazeh HaTenufah: where Reuven and Shimon litigated and one emerged entitled, and the losing party later sues him before another court, the successful party need not litigate again or answer the same claim, and the second court may not hear it at all. Darkei Moshe also cites Responsa Rosh that one beit din does not review another beit din's decision, and Rabbeinu Yerucham that students may not tear up a ruling even if it appears wrong until the rabbi who issued it comes, because he may know more than they do.
And from their words, so-and-so emerged entitled. They do not write, "So-and-so came with his litigant before beit din, and so-and-so emerged entitled while so-and-so was liable," because that wording appears false by implying that all judges shared one opinion. They therefore write "from their words," which indicates that there was disagreement. So states the Gemara and the Rosh. From this I inferred in Perishah that where all judges agreed, they should not use "from their words," because that would falsely imply disagreement.
