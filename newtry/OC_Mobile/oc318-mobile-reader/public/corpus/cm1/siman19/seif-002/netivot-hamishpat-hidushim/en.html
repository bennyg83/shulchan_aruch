As explained. Sema notes some difficulty with Rama's reference to siman 14. There the case concerns a litigant who asks that the judges write the reasons for their ruling because he wishes to appeal to the Great Beit Din, while here the discussion appears to concern an ordinary written judgment. Perhaps Rama holds that only in the special case of siman 14 do the judges carefully write, "From their words there emerged, etc.," so that the document not appear false and so that the judge who dissented not be blamed by the Great Beit Din as though all the judges erred. In an ordinary judgment, it is enough to write simply that one party owes the other such and such. However, the Gemara in Sanhedrin indicates that in every written judgment the judges should write, "From their words there emerged, etc." This requires study. Darkei Moshe cites Responsa Chazeh HaTenufah: one who left beit din acquitted and is then sued by the same opposing party before another beit din need not descend again to litigation, and the second beit din may not hear the claim at all, because one beit din does not ordinarily reexamine another beit din. Likewise students may not tear up a judgment that appears to them incorrect until the rabbi who issued it arrives, for he knows more than they.
