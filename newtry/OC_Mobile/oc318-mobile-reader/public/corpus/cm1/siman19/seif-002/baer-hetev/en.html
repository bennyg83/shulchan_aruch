As explained. The Sema writes that Rama's cross-reference to siman 14 is somewhat difficult. There the case concerns a litigant who says, "Write for me the reasons on which you judged me," because he wishes to go to the Great Beit Din. Here, however, the text appears to discuss an ordinary written judgment. Perhaps Rama holds that only in the case described in siman 14 must the judges carefully write, "from their deliberations the following emerged," so that the document does not appear false and so that the judge who dissented will not be identified as having joined the ruling, lest the Great Beit Din later say that all of them erred without knowing which judge actually erred. In an ordinary judgment, however, perhaps it is sufficient to write simply that one party was obligated to pay the other such-and-such. Yet the Gemara in Sanhedrin, chapter Zeh Borer, implies that in every written judgment the judges must write "from their deliberations," etc. This requires study. Darkei Moshe cites Responsa Chazeh HaTenufah: where two parties litigated, one emerged victorious, and afterward the losing party again sued him before another beit din, the prevailing party is not required to litigate again or answer the renewed claim. The second beit din is not permitted even to hear his arguments, because one beit din does not ordinarily scrutinize the ruling of another. Likewise, students may not tear up a written judgment even if it appears to them incorrect until the rabbi who issued the ruling arrives, for he may understand the matter better than they do.
From their deliberations. If the judges were unanimous, they should not write wording that implies disagreement among them, for that would appear false. Sema.
