If they were established in the city for thirty days. This law is copied from Rambam, but Maggid Mishneh does not identify its source. I found in Hagahot Alfasi: how long is the chazakah? Thirty days, as explained in the Yerushalmi, similar to the rule that one who establishes a name for himself in a city does so for thirty days. He further writes that chazakah follows ordinary social practice and that we do not punish a person solely on his own statement. This wording implies that chazakah of family relationship likewise requires thirty days. Thus, if a man and woman arrive from overseas, he says she is his sister and she says he is her brother, and within thirty days they have relations, they are not punished solely on their own words so long as the relationship had not been established for thirty days. This is also the straightforward implication of the Gemara's case of a man, woman, boy, and girl who grew up together in one household, meaning over a substantial period. Rambam's proof from striking or cursing one's father likewise concerns a chazakah established over thirteen years. Bach, however, does not write this way and brings proof from a woman wearing menstrual garments, which can establish status within a single day. That proof is not compelling because menstrual garments are ordinarily worn only for a few days, so a thirty-day requirement would be impossible. Hagahot Alfasi's formulation is correct: chazakah depends on normal usage. But for kinship chazakah, it is not less than thirty days. The rule that a person establishes his name in a city for thirty days appears in Choshen Mishpat 49 and Bava Batra 8a.
