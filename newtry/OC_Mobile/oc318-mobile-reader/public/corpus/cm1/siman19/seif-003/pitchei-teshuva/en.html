In the manner explained. Baer Heitev cites Darkei Moshe and the principle that one beit din does not ordinarily scrutinize another. Mabit, volume 2, siman 174, quotes Radbaz as saying that although the Gemara teaches that we do not suspect a beit din of error and one court does not review another, that was in earlier generations. Today courts do review one another, because Rabbeinu Yerucham cites Rashba that contemporary judges are not as expert in law and therefore their rulings are scrutinized. If this was true in Rashba's generation, how much more now, especially where the judges acted individually. Urim VeTumim notes that this teaching of Rabbeinu Yerucham in Rashba's name is also codified in Shulchan Aruch 39:11, but Shach there limits it: only where there is some defect or reason to suspect error do we say that contemporary judges may have erred. Ordinarily, even today one beit din does not review another. Avkat Rochel, siman 21, also prints Radbaz's responsum and includes a gloss by Beit Yosef making essentially the same distinction: Rashba spoke only of that unusual case; in ordinary matters even today one court does not scrutinize another. And although the judges there were individuals, once the parties accepted them as a beit din their decision is not routinely reviewed.
