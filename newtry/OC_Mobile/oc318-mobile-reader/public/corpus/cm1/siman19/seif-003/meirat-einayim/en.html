And if he remained thirty days, etc., they place him under a ban. In Derishah I explained why Rambam and Tur write here that after thirty days they immediately place him under a ban and do not write an authorization against his property, while below in siman 100, regarding one who refuses to carry out a judgment, they first write an authorization against his assets and, if none are found, warn him on Monday, Thursday, Monday, then excommunicate him twice for thirty days each, and only afterward place him under a ban. The distinction is as follows. Here he already accepted the judgment, left beit din, and did not ask for time to borrow, sell, or mortgage property. This indicates that he had ready money or movables with which to pay and intended at first to do so, but later changed his mind and refuses. Therefore, they do not write an authorization against real property, because ready money and movables take precedence for payment, as explained below at the beginning of siman 101. In siman 100, by contrast, the person resists carrying out the judgment and beit din does not know whether he has money or movables. Therefore, they first write an authorization against his assets. If none are found, they wait sixty days before placing him under a ban. During the first thirty days perhaps he is trying to borrow funds; during the second thirty perhaps friends willing to lend are trying to mortgage their own property or otherwise obtain money for him. I brought proof for this distinction from the Gemara and from the Tur below in siman 55.
And if he does not wish to accept the ruling, they excommunicate him immediately. Beit Yosef likewise infers this from Rambam and Tur, who write, "after he accepted," implying that if he refuses to accept the judgment he is excommunicated at once. Beit Yosef says this is also somewhat implied by the Gemara in the final chapter of Bava Kamma. Their words are difficult to me, for Baal HaTerumot explicitly writes, in gate 3, part 8, and the Tur and Mechaber themselves cite him below in siman 100, that ordinarily one is not excommunicated immediately without first receiving warnings on Monday, Thursday, and Monday. Immediate excommunication applies only to brazen defiance, such as refusing altogether to appear before the judges, disregarding their summons, or openly violating a Torah prohibition, as the Tur writes in Yoreh Deah at the beginning of siman 334. Rather, I understand the wording in the opposite direction. Rambam and Tur write "after he accepted" to teach that if he refused to carry out their ruling, one does not place him under a ban immediately; rather, the usual waiting process applies. Although Rambam and the Mechaber's wording is somewhat difficult, because they should have said explicitly that they warn him Monday, Thursday, and Monday, their intent is only to exclude immediate excommunication. The Tur therefore altered the wording and wrote that they wait through Monday, Thursday, and Monday. In such a case there is also no need to write an authorization against his property or to wait sixty days, for the reasons explained earlier.
