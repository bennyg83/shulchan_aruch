They place him under ban. The Mechaber writes here that they place him under ban immediately after thirty days, and he does not mention writing an authorization to seize property. Below in siman 100, concerning one who refuses to comply with a judgment, he writes that beit din immediately issues an authorization against his property, and if no property is found they warn him and only later place him under ban. The difference appears to be that here, after he accepted the judgment and did not ask for additional time, it appears that he has available cash or movables, which have priority for collection as explained in siman 101. Therefore an authorization against land is not written. In siman 100 he refuses to comply with the judgment and beit din does not know whether he has cash or movables. They therefore issue an authorization against his property from the outset. If no assets are found, they wait sixty days before placing him under ban: perhaps during the first thirty days he is trying to borrow money, and during the second thirty days perhaps friends willing to lend him are trying to obtain funds. Sema.
Immediately. Sema questions this. The Tur and Mechaber write in siman 100 that ordinarily one is not placed under ban immediately without the standard Monday, Thursday, Monday warnings, except for open contempt, such as ignoring a beit din summons or violating a Torah prohibition, as explained in Yoreh Deah siman 334. Shach answers that here he expressly rejects the judges' ruling, whereas there he merely refuses to pay without openly denying the validity of the judgment.
