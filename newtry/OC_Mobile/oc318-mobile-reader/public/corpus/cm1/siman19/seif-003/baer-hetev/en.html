They place him under cherem. The Sema notes that here the Mechaber says that after thirty days the party is immediately placed under cherem and does not mention issuing an adrachta against his property. Below, in siman 100, regarding one who refuses to comply with a judgment, the Mechaber says that an adrachta is issued immediately against his assets; if none are found, he is warned, and only afterward placed under cherem. The distinction is that here, once the litigant accepted the judgment and did not request an extension, it appears that he has cash or movable property, which must be used first for payment as explained in siman 101. Therefore no adrachta is issued. Siman 100, by contrast, concerns one who refuses to carry out the judgment and the court does not know whether he possesses cash or movables. Accordingly, an adrachta is first issued against his property. If no assets are found, the court waits sixty days before placing him under cherem. We say that during the first thirty days he may be working to borrow money, while during the next thirty his friends who wish to lend him may themselves be working to obtain funds. The Sema notes support for this from the Gemara and Tur in siman 95. Examine this carefully.
Immediately. The Sema writes that this is astonishing, because the Tur and Mechaber in siman 100 rule that one is never placed under nidui immediately without the standard warnings on Monday, Thursday, and Monday, except in a case of open defiance, such as ignoring a beit din summons, or violating a Torah prohibition, as the Tur writes in Yoreh Deah, siman 334. The Shach answers that there is no difficulty. Here the person refuses to accept the judges' ruling itself, while there he accepts the ruling but refuses to pay.
