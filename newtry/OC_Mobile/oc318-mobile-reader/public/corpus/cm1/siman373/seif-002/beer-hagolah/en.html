heh) The language of the Tur, seif 2. The Rambam likewise writes in chapter 9, law 8. The Maggid Mishneh writes that this clause is somewhat difficult, because it appears otherwise from Rashi in Gittin 48b on the Mishnah concerning produce, and also from the first interpretation in Bava Metzia at the end of 14a, since the Gemara did not establish it as a case where one robbed a field, sold it, and the purchaser consumed its produce. Nevertheless, the Rambam's ruling is principal, as is proven in the first chapter of Bava Kamma, where the Gemara discusses whether slaves are treated as land. Tosafot likewise rule this way on 97a, even where one entered the land as a robber, and Scripture says, 'restore to her all the produce of the field from the day she left.' It is also clear in Bava Batra 33b that the land and produce are both returned when the possessor is established as having consumed it through robbery. So too in the case of one who resides in another's courtyard without his knowledge, explained in chapter 2 and above in siman 363, seif 6.
vav) Explicit in Bava Metzia 72b from the Gemara's statement there according to the view that the owner is pleased, Maggid Mishneh.
