Seif 1. "If he knew that it had been stolen, etc." Note: It requires study why the authorities omitted the view of the Rosh in the first chapter of Bava Metzia, in this sugya, that the purchaser cannot demand repayment of his money from the robber so long as the true owner has not actually dispossessed him of the field. See there. Why did the later authorities not cite this view? This requires study. See also my work on Even HaEzer, sixth edition, responsum 43, where I was uncertain in a case where the purchaser knew that the field did not belong to the seller and nevertheless bought it, whether the robber himself may retract and take it away from the purchaser, or whether at least the robber cannot retract because he has removed himself from the property and the purchase is not his to undo, so that only the true owner can come and take it from the purchaser. See also what I asked concerning this law in my work on Bava Kamma. There are practical consequences. In my work on Choshen Mishpat, fourth edition, responsum 303, I wrote that if the seller is a notorious robber, the payment is treated as a gift. See there and examine carefully.
