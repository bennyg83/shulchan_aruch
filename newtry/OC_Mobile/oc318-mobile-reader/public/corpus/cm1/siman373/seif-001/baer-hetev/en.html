And improved. See Responsa Mabit, part I, second group of questions, siman 27, p. 111.
Takes. For even had the item remained with the victim, he would have had to pay this expense from his own pocket. Sema.
They divide. Their reason is that if the property had remained with the robber, this expense would not have been necessary. Thus he has a claim concerning the amount by which the expense exceeded the improvement. Ibid.
The improvement. Even if the seller did not expressly assume responsibility for improvement, liability is presumed, both for principal and improvement. Shach.
Unencumbered property. See above, siman 115, seif 1. Kol Bo, siman 123, letter 12, writes that if Reuven bought a Chumash from Shimon for one zekuk, spent money adding vocalization and Masorah, and it rose in value to three zekukim, and afterward it was found stolen and the true owner took it from Reuven, Reuven may recover all three zekukim from Shimon, based on the rule that one who sells land he does not own must reimburse even the improvement. This is from Raavan, chapter 1 of Bava Metzia. These words do not appear correct to me, because that rule applies specifically to land, not movable property, as is clear in chapter HaGozel Kamma, Tosafot there, and the Rosh, chapter 1 of Bava Metzia. See also simanim 354 and 362, seif 8, in the gloss. Shach.
The principal. The Tur explains that since the buyer knew when he purchased it that it was stolen, the money was not given as a purchase price but as a loan. If he received more than he gave, it would appear like interest. The Shach writes that he nevertheless recovers the principal even though he knew it was stolen, and the same applies to movable property, as the Bach explains at length. I already discussed this above in siman 356.
Amount. See Responsa Rashakh, part II, siman 81, and Responsa Rashdam, siman 264.
The expense. The Shach writes that this law is surprising. He proves at length that many authorities hold simply that the purchaser at least recovers his expenses from the true owner, and concludes that this is primary.
