Together with the rest of the enhancement. This applies even if responsibility for enhancement was not expressly stated, because omission of a standard warranty is treated as a scribal error both regarding principal and enhancement.
And the remaining enhancement from unencumbered property. See above, siman 115, seif 1. Kol Bo writes that where the purchaser knew the land was stolen he has no claim for excess enhancement against the robber, because his payment was in the nature of a deposit or gift, but where he did not know he may recover according to the ordinary rules of warranty. The distinction depends upon whether the enhancement was produced by expense or arose by itself, and whether it can be collected from encumbered or only unencumbered assets. The purchaser may in any event recover his actual expenditure from the true owner to the extent that the land was enhanced, under the law of one who descends into another's field without authorization.

And he loses the remaining enhancement, etc. The Sema writes that regarding enhancement arising by itself all agree that the purchaser does not collect it from the robber when he knew the property was stolen, whereas enhancement produced by expenditure is subject to dispute. In my view, the authorities' language indicates that he at least recovers his expenditure from the true owner, but not excess enhancement from the robber. See the preceding discussion.
He has no claim for the expenditure, etc. See Responsa Maharash Kohen, book 2, siman 81, and Responsa Rashdam, Choshen Mishpat, siman 267.
Some say that since he knew it was not the seller's, he loses even his expenditure. Although the Tur writes this, it is difficult. Even the robber himself receives reimbursement of his expenditure, as the Tur states at the end of the preceding siman. Tosafot and the Rosh only deny the purchaser the enhancement exceeding his expenditure; they disagree with the Rif insofar as the Rif appears to grant even that excess where the enhancement resulted from expenditure. Reimbursement of the expenditure requires no special rabbinic enactment, for it is his own money and follows the law of one who improves another's field without authorization. The Rosh explicitly writes that if the purchaser knew the land was stolen, he receives his expenditure from the true owner but not the remaining enhancement from the robber. Baal HaMaor, Rabbeinu Ephraim, and Baal HaItur state the same. The Beit Yosef's assertion that Rambam follows the Rif is also unnecessary; Rambam can be understood like the Rosh, or as interpreting the Rif to refer only to enhancement equal to the expenditure, as Ramban explains in Milchamot. We therefore obtain the rule that in all events the purchaser recovers from the true owner the expenditure up to the amount of enhancement, contrary to the Tur and Rama. Rabbeinu Yerucham and Mordechai likewise rule so, and this is the correct halacha.
