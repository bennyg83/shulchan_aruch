[Seif 1] Some say that if he expressly accepted. The Gemara in Bava Metzia 15a explains, according to Shmuel, that where one purchased from a robber without knowing the property was stolen, if the robber formally undertook at the time of sale to pay the purchaser for the appreciation, collecting that appreciation later does not appear as interest, because the obligation arose before the money was given. Likewise, if the robber owns land from which the purchaser may collect the appreciation, it does not appear as interest. The Gemara challenges both distinctions from the prohibition against lending a seah for a seah, even where there was a formal undertaking or the borrower owns land, and answers that there the transaction is a loan, whereas here it is a sale. All this follows Shmuel, who holds that an unwitting purchaser from a robber does not receive the appreciation. We rule, however, like Rava, that when the purchaser did not know, he receives the appreciation in every case. The Ramah therefore holds that according to Rava, where the purchaser did know that the field was not the seller's, a formal undertaking or ownership of land helps, just as it helped for Shmuel where the purchaser did not know. The Tur cites the Rosh as disagreeing: because the purchaser knew the property was stolen, there was no genuine sale even temporarily, and he had no intention of acquiring through sale. According to the Rosh, therefore, neither a formal undertaking nor ownership of land helps. When he did not know, neither is needed, because it was a genuine sale; when he did know, neither can cure the problem. The Sema erred in two respects. First, he wrote that the Rosh agrees where the robber owns land but disputes only a formal undertaking, although the Gemara clearly equates the two. Second, according to the Rosh, ownership of land certainly cannot help because the transaction is a pure loan, and the Gemara itself asks whether lending a seah for a seah becomes permitted merely because the borrower owns land. The Gemara's answer, that the case is a sale, is unavailable according to the Rosh once the purchaser knew. The Rama's first opinion, that an express undertaking helps, likewise includes the case where the robber owns land, as the Tur states explicitly. He did not elaborate because he then cites the dissenting opinion of the Rosh, which is the law. See also what I wrote in Yoreh Deah at the end of siman 172 concerning presumed warranty in that context.
