The law when a robber sells a field and the purchaser improves it, and the purchaser's rights against the robber. It contains three seifim:
If one robs a field, sells it, and the purchaser improves it, then if the improvement exceeds the purchaser's expenses, the purchaser recovers his expenses from the true owner. Some say this is so even if the field is no better than when it was originally stolen, such as where the robber had allowed it to deteriorate; the purchaser nevertheless recovers his expenses from the original owner. Nimukei Yosef, first chapter of Bava Metzia, in the name of Ran. Others disagree, citing Rashi. The purchaser recovers the principal purchase price and the remaining improvement from the robber. The principal may be collected even from encumbered property, while the additional improvement is collected only from unencumbered property. If the purchaser knew at the time of purchase that the land was stolen, he recovers only the principal from the robber and loses the remaining improvement beyond his expenses. If the expenses exceeded the improvement, whether or not he knew the property was stolen, he recovers from the true owner only up to the amount of the improvement. Some say that if he knew the land was not the seller's, he loses even his expenses. Tur, seif 1, in the name of Ri and Rosh. Others say that if the robber expressly accepted liability, he must pay according to the liability he accepted. Tur in the name of Ramah; Responsa Rashba, siman 1155; Rivash, siman 250; and Maggid Mishneh, chapter 9 of Hilchot Gezeilah, in the name of some authorities. Others disagree. Tur in the name of Rosh, Maggid Mishneh chapter 8 of Hilchot Gezeilah, Nimukei Yosef first chapter of Bava Metzia, and Ran, who writes that the other view does not appear correct. The purchaser recovers the principal from the robber even from encumbered property.
