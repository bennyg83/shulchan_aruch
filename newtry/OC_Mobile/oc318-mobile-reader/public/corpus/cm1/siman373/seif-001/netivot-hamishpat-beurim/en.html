He loses even the expense. See Shach, seif katan 7, who correctly finds it very difficult to say that the purchaser always loses even his expenses. Rosh's language can be understood consistently with Tur. The purchaser loses his expenses only where the true owner need not reimburse those expenses, such as where the robber himself caused the property to deteriorate in a way described in the gloss to seif 1. The purchaser then seeks to recover both principal and expenses from the seller. If he gave the seller one thousand and receives back one thousand two hundred, the transaction can look like prohibited interest. But where the true owner reimburses the purchaser's improvement expenses, while the purchaser recovers only his purchase principal from the seller, there is no appearance of interest. He receives the expense reimbursement from the owner because that owner's property actually benefited, and receives only his principal from the seller. Therefore Rosh need not disagree with Tur's narrower rule.
