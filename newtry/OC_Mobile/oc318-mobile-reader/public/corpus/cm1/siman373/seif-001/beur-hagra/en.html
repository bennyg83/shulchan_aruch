If the improvement, etc. Bava Metzia 15a, in the alternate baraisa and the Gemara's two explanations there.
And some say, etc. The reason the purchaser receives the improvement is the law of one who descends into another's field without permission, as explained in the preceding siman. See Tosafos there 14b, s.v. "and he has," etc.
And some disagree. Rashi there, s.v. "and he has," etc. Tosafos explains that the purchaser receives even beyond his expenses, and Rashi himself later retracts in s.v. "in the case of a robber and the robbed," and in s.v. "who robbed a field." When Tosafos says "and it deals with," the intention is that even such a case is included. See Tosafos Bava Kamma cited there.
And the principal together with, etc. Bava Metzia 15b, where the Gemara concludes the halacha.
The principal is collected, etc. There 14b and the Mishna at the beginning of chapter 6 of Gittin.
(Collected note) And if he recognized, etc., and the principal is collected, etc. This is the Rambam's language, and Baal HaTerumos writes likewise. The Rambam follows his view regarding Rav's principle, "whatever the first sold to the second," even where the buyer knew that the property was not the seller's, as the Gemara's challenge from one who sells what he expects to inherit indicates. The Rambam and Shulchan Aruch likewise do not distinguish at the beginning of siman 374. According to Tosafos, Nimukei Yosef, and the Rama there, however, that rule applies only where the buyer did not know. If he knew, he does not collect from encumbered property, as indicated by the Gemara. (End of note.)
The excess, etc., whether he recognized, etc. The Rif writes that in this case the purchaser does not receive the excess improvement, proving it from Bava Kamma 96a. Milchamos explains that a buyer who knowingly purchases another's land according to Rav is legally parallel to a buyer who unknowingly purchases according to Shmuel, yet Shmuel still allows recovery of expenses. Baal HaMaor himself agrees to that parallel and therefore discusses Shmuel at length for its application here. Moreover, even a robber himself is entitled to reimbursement of expenses as stated in siman 372 seif 3, and a purchaser who knew should not be worse.
And some say, etc. The Tur cites the Rosh this way. Yet Tosafos and the Rosh discuss only improvement exceeding expenses. Their questions elsewhere likewise concern excess improvement, because the Gemara expressly says that such improvement is collected from the robber. Baal HaMaor also disputes only the excess, but expressly agrees that expenses are recovered from the owner. It is surprising that this distinction was overlooked, as Milchamos and the Shach both note.
And some say that if, etc. Tosafos, Baal HaMaor, the Rosh, the Ramah, the Tur, and other poskim maintain that recovery is possible where there was a formal kinyan or where the seller owns other land, again treating Rav's case of a buyer who knew like Shmuel's ordinary purchaser. See the Gemara's language, "even if he expressly stipulated," etc.
And some disagree. As above. According to Shmuel, even an express stipulation and a formal kinyan do not help where the purchaser knew, because he never intended an actual sale; he is treated only as one who receives compensation for the use of his money.
