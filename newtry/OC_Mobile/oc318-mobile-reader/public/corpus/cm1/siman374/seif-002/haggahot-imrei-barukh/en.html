(Rama, seif 4) And we do not remove it from the robber, etc. Note: According to Rashba and Ramban in their novellae, cited in Shitah Mekubetzet to Bava Metzia 15 and 16, when the robber later buys the land it belongs to the purchaser because we say that the true owner transferred the land to the purchaser through the robber, who is treated as the purchaser's agent. There is therefore reason to say that even with a gift, where the Gemara remains in doubt, we leave the land with the purchaser. The Gemara concludes with a doubt as to where the land should remain. But there is no opposing presumption of the prior owner, because if a gift is treated like a sale and the robber served as the purchaser's agent, the robber never possessed a valid presumption in the land at all. Shitah Mekubetzet there explicitly writes that the Gemara's question, "this document is merely a shard," applies only where the robber collected the land for his debt, because in that case the rationale that the true owner transfers the property to the purchaser does not apply.
