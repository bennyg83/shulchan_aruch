Nor with the property of one who abandoned it voluntarily and whose whereabouts are unknown, etc. The Tur additionally mentions the property of one who abandoned it under compulsion. The Gemara in chapter HaMafkid, near the end of Bava Metzia 38, uses both expressions in the discussion whether a relative is placed in charge of such property. The Tur and Mechaber state those laws below in siman 285. It is evident there that one term refers to a person who leaves his property involuntarily because of some coercion and departs. Scripture likewise says concerning Shemittah, 'you shall release it and abandon it,' using the language of abandonment for a compulsory legal relinquishment. The other term refers to one who leaves voluntarily and we do not know where he has gone. The verse, 'a mother was abandoned concerning her children,' likewise describes one who fled on her own before the enemy because of fear, as Nimukei Yosef explains. The Tur here refers to one whose whereabouts are unknown, meaning that since we do not know where he went, perhaps he is in a place where caravans do not ordinarily arrive, whether that place is geographically near or far. The Mechaber mentions only this latter category here because the law of property left under compulsion had already been explained in the preceding rules. If a person abandoned his property because of danger to life, another person's possession never establishes a presumption. If he left because of monetary danger, possession can establish a presumption unless he fled so far away that news of the possession and of a possible protest could not reach between them. In that situation, as already explained, no person's possession establishes a presumption. See the Perishah for further discussion.
