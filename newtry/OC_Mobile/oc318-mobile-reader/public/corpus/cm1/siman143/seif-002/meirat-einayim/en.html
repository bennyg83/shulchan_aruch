We do not heed him in what he says, etc. The Rosh gives the reason: Since this person maintained uncontested possession for three years, and such possession becomes known even in another province, the challenger must establish that he was in a place from which his protest would not have been heard had he made one, and that this was why he refrained from protesting. The Rosh does not conclude by saying that the challenger is not believed to say that he did not hear of the possession. In my view, his meaning is broader. Even if the challenger attempts to excuse himself by saying, 'It is true that I eventually heard of the possession, but because of the distance I did not hear until the third year. At that point a protest I made would not have become known to the possessor during the third year, so I refrained from protesting altogether,' he is not believed to say this.
