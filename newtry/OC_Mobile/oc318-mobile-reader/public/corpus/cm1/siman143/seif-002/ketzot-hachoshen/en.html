Rosh writes that because three years of peaceful possession is ordinarily known even at a distance, the challenger who says his protest could not be heard must prove that he was in a place where protest truly could not reach. This differs from the inner rooms case, where there is doubt whether the possession itself was legally complete. There the possessor must prove the quality of his possession, just as he must prove the three years themselves. Here the possession is objectively complete even when the owner is far away, because possession itself is publicly known. The only issue is whether the owner's failure to protest has an excuse. That excuse is equivalent to a claim that he was unable to protest. Therefore the burden lies on the challenger to prove the special circumstances excusing his silence. If possession itself were not known at a distance, the doubt would concern the very existence of a valid presumption and the burden would instead fall on the possessor.
