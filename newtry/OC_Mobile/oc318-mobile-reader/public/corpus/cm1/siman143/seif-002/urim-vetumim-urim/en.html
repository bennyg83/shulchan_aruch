We do not listen, etc. The Rosh gives the reason that since the possessor held the property peacefully for three years, and such possession becomes known even in another country, the protester must prove that he was in a place from which his protest would not have been heard had he made one, and that this was why he refrained from protesting. See the Rosh, cited by the Sema. The Sema finds this difficult: why must the Rosh say that the chazakah would have become known to the protester but his protest would not have become known to the possessor? Why not say simply that the protester is not believed to claim that he was in a place where he did not hear of the possession? The Sema gives a very strained answer. See Tumim, where I gave a correct and satisfactory explanation. I also resolved an apparent contradiction in the Mechaber: here he rules without qualification like the Rosh, yet below in siman 146, seif 11, where the protester claims that he was initially outside the country, the Mechaber requires the possessor to prove otherwise. I also explained why the Mechaber rules here like the Rosh although the Maggid Mishneh, Ramban, and Rashba disagree. Their entire proof is from the Gemara, but according to the Rif, Rabbeinu Chananel, Rashbam, Tosafot, and Rosh, that proof does not stand and their law does not follow. The Mechaber therefore properly adopted the Rosh's view. See Tumim there at length.
