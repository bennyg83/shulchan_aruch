We do not listen to him, etc. The Rosh explains that since this person possessed the property peacefully for three years, and possession becomes known even in another country, the owner bears the burden of establishing that he was in a place where his protest could not be heard. The Sema in seif katan 5 is troubled by this. Why was it necessary for the Rosh to say that the possession could be heard but the protest could not? He could have simply said that the owner is not believed to claim he was in a place where he did not hear of the possession. The Sema's answer is very strained. In my view the Rosh's words are precise. Ordinarily, it is more reasonable to place the burden of proof on the possessor, because he can easily bring proof that the owner was somewhere within the country during the three years. It is difficult for the owner to bring proof that he was not in that country. "We did not see" is not proof, and who can testify that he was never there unless someone can say, "my hand never left his hand," which is practically impossible. The owner could, however, bring proof from a distant country that he remained there continuously for three years, by arranging witnesses to observe his arrival, departure, and continuous residence. But that is realistic only if the owner knows that another is possessing his property. Then, in order to establish that the possession is ineffective, he has reason to trouble himself and designate witnesses who will observe his continuous residence in the distant country for three years. If he does not know at all that anyone is possessing his property, however, there is no reason for him to designate witnesses while he is away. Why would he do so? When he later returns and discovers another in possession, how can he establish through witnesses that he had continuously remained in another country for three straight years? People do not ordinarily pay attention to such a matter. Thus, in that case it is plainly more proper to require the possessor to bring some witness who saw the owner in the country during the three years. The Rosh therefore writes, to reject the contrary view, that the possession itself is indeed heard from afar, whereas the owner is merely unable to protest. Since he knew of the possession, he should have designated witnesses in the distant country who could testify to his continuous residence there. It is therefore reasonable to place the burden of proof upon him. The Rosh's words are clear and exact. This also resolves the Mechaber's rulings. Here he rules simply like the Rosh, although Ramban and Rashba disagree. They explain the case of "the inner residences" as one in which the owner claims that during the years of possession he was in southern lands far from the property, and according to the Ran the possessor must prove otherwise. Ramban in his novellae and the Maggid Mishneh in chapter 15 of Hilchot To'en cite this in the name of Ramban and Rashba. Yet below, in siman 146 seif 11, where the owner claims that at the outset of the possession he was not in the country and therefore it was impossible for the possessor to have purchased from him, the Mechaber rules that the possessor must bring proof. What is the difference? According to what I have written, the distinction is clear. In the latter case, how could the owner have known at the beginning of the three years that this person would later possess his property and claim to have bought it from him, so that he should arrange witnesses at his place of residence to prove that he was not in the country and could not have sold it? Such a possibility never entered his mind. Therefore the possessor bears the burden of proof, because the owner cannot reasonably establish the negative. Here, however, the owner heard of the possession but was unable to protest. He therefore should have designated witnesses establishing that he was in a distant country. Since he could conveniently prove it, the burden is on him, and if he failed to do so he caused his own loss. This resolves the apparent inconsistency. The reason the Mechaber rules like the Rosh rather than Ramban and Rashba is that their entire ruling is founded on their interpretation of "the inner residences" as meaning that the owner spent all three years in a distant country. The Rif, Rabbenu Chananel, Rashbam, Tosafot, the Rosh, and the other authorities did not interpret the passage that way. There is therefore no basis to adopt their derivative ruling, and the Mechaber correctly does not rule like them.
