In villages. Because people have more open space and gather more frequently with many others. Also, towns commonly have market days throughout the year, so people there are less preoccupied. Sema:
From this one can learn. Since the law requires the reason that he was preoccupied in the market, it follows that without that reason possession would establish chazakah even though the owner was not with him until the end of the third year. The reason is that he had no other opportunity to protest and therefore should have protested immediately. The converse also follows: if the owner was with him at the beginning but not at the end, he may explain that he intended to return before the three years ended and protest then. Sema. See the Beurim. If he knew that he would not return before the end of the third year, he had to protest, and failure to do so establishes chazakah. If at the beginning he knew that the possessor was occupying his property, but later claims that because he was occupied with the market or similar matters he did not know whether the possessor remained there through the end of the three years and assumed that he had already moved away, this is not a valid claim.
