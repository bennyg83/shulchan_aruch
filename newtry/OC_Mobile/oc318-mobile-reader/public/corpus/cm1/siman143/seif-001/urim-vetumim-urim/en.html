He had a different house for a generation where I brought in the name of Rashi, and I did not know that you were in the house, and that he had one house that was covered by his name, and that he had no time to protest, and that he was not seen in my eyes, and that the author took up the first place of the time, that he had to be a different house, and so that he agreed upon himself Sign up in the pools:
If at first he was in a place where he could protest and then went where he could not protest, there is no presumption. Nimukei Yosef states this and derives it from the case, 'I was in the outer markets.' Beit Yosef says it follows one reading of Rashbam. Darkei Moshe, Derishah, and Bach say the law follows simply from logic. See Tumim. I showed that logic alone can be challenged and even some Talmudic material points the other way. I then explained Nimukei Yosef through a deeper reading and concluded not only that his rule is correct, but that Maggid Mishneh cites the same principle in the name of Ramban and Rashba. Rama could therefore have cited those authorities as well. In practical law Rama is correct.
And he went to a place where he could not protest. The Shach says that if the claimant initially knew the possessor was using his property and later wishes to say, 'I was preoccupied and did not know you continued using it,' that does not help; the possession is valid. He understands Beit Yosef this way. See Tumim. I do not agree. I proved from Rosh that the opposite is true, otherwise Rosh contradicts himself. Since Rosh and Baal HaTerumot agree and no contrary authority is known, the Shach's ruling requires study.
