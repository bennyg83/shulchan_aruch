Even if he established possession in the claimant's presence. The reason is that for up to three years a person is careful to preserve the deed of purchase or gift that was in his possession, but after that he is no longer careful. Therefore, where there is a present weakness in his case because he has no deed within the first three years, we say he is lying. This is so even if the claimant is a person who ordinarily objects to others eating produce from his field. We may say that with this person he did not object, or that he placed him there merely to take the produce, or any of the claimant's other explanations.

In villages, where the people are occupied, etc. See the Perishah, where I cited Kesef Mishneh, who wondered why villages should differ from cities. He suggested that it refers to fairs, called fairs in the vernacular, where exceptionally large crowds gather in villages because there is much open space. It appears to me that the reason is that cities ordinarily have a market day every week, so people are not so preoccupied by it.
From this one may learn, etc. Since the ruling requires the explanation that he was occupied at the market, it follows that without that reason the possession would count as chazakah even though he was not with him throughout the three years but only at the end of the third year. The reason is that since this was his only opportunity to protest, he was required to protest immediately. Conversely, if he was with him at the beginning but not at the end, he can excuse himself by saying, 'I thought I still had time.' See the Perishah.
