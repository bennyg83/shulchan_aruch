The utensil was lost by itself. See Baer Heitev, who explains that it is known to have been lost without negligence. Ketzot HaChoshen and Netivot HaMishpat dispute Sema and write that even if it was lost through negligence, the robber does not become liable on that basis merely as a custodian. A robber is not a custodian at all, not even an unpaid one, as Tosafos, Bava Kamma 56, s.v. peshita, and Rosh in chapter Merubah state explicitly. Therefore, when Shulchan Aruch says that if another person caused the loss that person pays fourfold, it must mean that the second person destroyed the item directly, making him an ordinary tortfeasor. Shaar Mishpat defends Sema and writes that Sema's words are correct and should be accepted. Divrei Mishpat below, siman 362, seif 10, also answers Ketzot's objection and cites Responsa Maharit, Choshen Mishpat, siman 116, who likewise rules like Sema. See there at length.
