The stolen object, etc. This follows the phrase, 'the stolen object returns as it is.' Rosh differs in seif 1 and therefore limits the later inquiry to Rabbi Shimon, whereas Rambam applies the principle also according to Rabbi Yehuda.
It changed, etc. No authority disputes that a complete change effects acquisition, as is explicit in Bava Kamma 66a-b and 68a.
And he acquired its increase. This follows the opening Mishna of chapter 9 and the discussion on 95a according to Rabbi Meir. The case is before despair, as shown by the proof from improvement to land.
