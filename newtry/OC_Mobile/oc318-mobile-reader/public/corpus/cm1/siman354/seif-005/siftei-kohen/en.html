Specifically where he has money, etc. At first sight the Rama's words are difficult. They resemble the Ramah's view, cited by the Nimukei Yosef, that the rule 'we do not appraise for a thief or robber' means only that the broken pieces are not credited at their former value; the thief may nevertheless tender them at their present value. Tosafot cite a similar view in the name of Rabbeinu Shmuel. Rashi, Tosafot, the Rosh, and Tur, however, hold that the thief cannot tender the fragments at all, but must return a whole article or its value. Moreover, if the Rama followed the Ramah, he should permit tender of the fragments even when the thief has money. The Ramah's distinction between one who has money and one who does not follows his separate view that every tortfeasor who has money must pay money, whereas we do not follow that rule and permit payment even with bran. The Rama must therefore mean only this: where the thief lacks money and cannot provide a whole replacement, and the owner wishes to collect movable property, even Rashi and those who agree with him concede that the fragments may be included among the movable assets at their present value. They are no worse than any other movable property.
"And thou shalt listen to them," he said to them, "And thou shalt be thrust down to hell."
