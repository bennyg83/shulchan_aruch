If the owners wish. Maggid Mishneh questions this ruling because change ordinarily acquires for a thief or robber. Bach answers that Rambam may distinguish change that occurs by itself from change caused by the thief's own act, but that cannot be the accepted rule. Rambam himself says that an animal that aged, or a coin that cracked, is acquired through change even though the change occurred on its own. The Talmud likewise treats natural change as acquisition. Tosafot's distinction between natural and active change was suggested only to reconcile a particular opinion of Rabbi Elazar, not as the accepted halachah. Rashi's language that "change acquires where he changes it by hand" is therefore difficult, because the Mishnah clearly uses aging as a case of acquisitive change. The precise scope of Rashi's wording requires study.
