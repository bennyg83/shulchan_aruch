The Sema writes that the Rambam holds that giving birth and shearing are not considered a change that acquires either the body of the animal or the enhancement, because this is a reversible change, since the animal can again become pregnant and again bear wool. Therefore, even if it became pregnant in the thief's possession and also gave birth there, the Rambam holds that he did not acquire it. The Tur expressly writes so in his name. The Shach likewise writes this and challenges the Maggid Mishneh and the Mechaber, stating that their words require study; see there. The statement that 'he pays for it' is not literal, as though he merely gives money for it. Rather, he takes the ewe itself. The alternative view cited by the Rama holds that this is not called a reversible change, because when it becomes pregnant again or bears new wool it is a new state. It is therefore a complete change, the enhancement belongs to him, and the ewe itself remains in his possession, but he pays its value to the owners. See Maharshal, chapter HaGozel Kamma, simanim 4 and 7.
