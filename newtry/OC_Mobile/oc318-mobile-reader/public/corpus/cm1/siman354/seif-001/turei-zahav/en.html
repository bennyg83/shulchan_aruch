(Seif 1: he pays for it and its shearings.) Rambam's reasoning is that there is a legally significant change only when the body itself becomes a different body, such as a lamb becoming a ram, where a small body became a large one. Here the change is only in appearance. At first, while full of wool, the animal looked robust, and afterward it looked thin. This is not a substantive change. The Sema's explanation is strained. He writes that it is a reversible change because the animal can become pregnant again, but a renewed pregnancy would certainly be a new circumstance, like the case in siman 360, seif 6. There is clear proof from Perek Eizehu Neshech 64 concerning the prohibition against selling "whatever wool this ewe will shear." The Gemara concludes that the later wool is not growing from the earlier wool. Rashi explains that after the existing wool is entirely removed, new wool later grows. Thus the later product is a new creation, not the restoration of the prior state.
