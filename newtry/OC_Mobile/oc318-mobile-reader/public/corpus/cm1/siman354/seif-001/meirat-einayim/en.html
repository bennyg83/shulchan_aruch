He pays it and, etc. Rambam holds that giving birth and shearing do not constitute a change sufficient to transfer ownership of the animal itself or its enhancement, because they are changes that can recur: the animal can again become pregnant or grow wool. Therefore, even if the animal became pregnant in the thief's possession and gave birth there, Rambam holds that he does not acquire it. The Tur writes this explicitly in Rambam's name. The phrase "he pays it" is not literal if understood to mean that he pays its value and keeps the animal. Since he did not acquire it by change, he returns the ewe itself. The dissenting view cited by Rama holds that when it becomes pregnant again or regrows wool, this is a new state and the original birth or shearing is not called a reversible change. It is therefore a complete change. The enhancement belongs to the thief, and even the ewe itself remains with him because he acquired it through the change, while he pays the owner its value.
He pays according to its value at the time of the theft. Meaning, where it was already carrying some wool or fetal development when stolen and remained with the thief until the wool or offspring increased. One must say that Rambam distinguishes as follows. So long as it has not yet been shorn or given birth, the animal together with the wool or offspring is returned to the owner, and the owner must pay the thief the value of the enhancement that occurred in the thief's possession. If all the wool grew only after the theft, the owner must pay the thief the full value of that enhancement in money. If the animal already gave birth in the thief's possession, the thief retains even the mother by virtue of the offspring and pays the victim the value it had at the time of the theft. See the Perishah and Derishah for further distinctions according to Rambam where the property improved after the owner's despair.
All the more so if he stole a ewe and it became pregnant, etc. This requires study, because the Mechaber introduces the case as "all the more so," whereas the Tur says, "and furthermore," presenting it as an additional novelty. See the Tur in seif 1.
