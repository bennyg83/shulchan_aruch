Even an increase that came by itself. See Shach, seif katan 4, through his statement that after despair the thief acquires even without change. He refers to one who stole an animal while it was not pregnant, and it later became pregnant and gave birth. The animal itself is not considered changed merely because it later became pregnant. The Shach therefore holds that even Ra'avad and Rosh agree that without despair the thief does not acquire. I do not understand this. Even if the body of the animal is not regarded as changed because it was originally empty, the offspring and added value certainly constitute an increase produced through change. According to Ra'avad and Rosh, that increase should be acquired even without despair. Perhaps the Shach refers to the type of increase described by the Sema in siman 362, seif katan 17, where there is no qualifying change in the increase itself. That ruling, however, is difficult and will be explained there.
A ewe that became pregnant. Although Rama rules in siman 360, seif 6, that so long as the name of the stolen item has not changed because of the physical alteration, it is not legally considered a change, here it is nevertheless treated as a change because of the enactment encouraging wrongdoers to repent. This is evident from Tosafos in Bava Kamma 95, s.v. "regarding the increase attached to the stolen property."
