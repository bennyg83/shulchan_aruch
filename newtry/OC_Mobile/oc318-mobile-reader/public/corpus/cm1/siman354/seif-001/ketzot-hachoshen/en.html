It was pregnant. Rambam holds that birth and shearing do not constitute a legal change even as to the offspring or wool themselves. Raavad says there is no greater change than birth and therefore the thief acquires even before despair. Ramban says the birth or shearing changes the offspring or wool but does not change the mother animal itself. Baal HaMaor and Rosh go further and treat the mother's own body as changed because it was previously full and is now emptied. There is strong proof for Rambam from Avodah Zarah. The Gemara distinguishes grain ground into flour from an animal born from its mother. Concerning the newborn animal it says that it was an animal before and remains an animal now; the womb merely held it closed. Rashi explains that even in the womb it was already called an animal. This demonstrates that birth is not treated as a legal change even for the offspring itself. Rambam and the Shulchan Aruch are therefore well supported.
