Or it was lost. Sema interprets "lost" as lost through negligence. In my view the phrase means that the robber himself caused its destruction, so that he is a direct tortfeasor. A robber is not a custodian of the stolen property. He never accepted responsibility to guard for the owner. Therefore negligent loss after the theft does not create a new custodial liability measured at the later value. Rosh states this expressly. If the object was worth four at the time of theft and later only one when it was accidentally or negligently broken, the robber pays four because the liability was fixed at the theft. The later negligence does not create a new custodial debt. Conversely, if it was worth one when stolen and later rose to four, mere negligent loss should obligate only one, the original theft value. Only if the robber himself destroys or consumes it at the later time does a new direct damage act fix liability according to the later value. Therefore the Shulchan Aruch's phrase, "he broke it or lost it, he pays four," should be read as an active destruction or active causing of loss, not ordinary custodial negligence.
