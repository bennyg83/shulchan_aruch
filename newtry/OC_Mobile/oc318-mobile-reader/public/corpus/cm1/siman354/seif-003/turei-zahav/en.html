In the words of the Tur. The Tur writes that if an animal was worth one zuz when stolen and four when brought to court, and the thief slaughtered or sold it, principal and double payment are assessed at the time of judgment, while fourfold or fivefold payment is assessed at the time of slaughter or sale. At first glance this implies that the animal's value may differ between slaughter and judgment. But if so, even principal should be assessed at the time of slaughter, as the Beit Yosef explicitly cites from the Rosh: if the animal rose in value and then broke on its own, principal is paid according to the earlier value because the thief did nothing at the later stage. The same should be true between slaughter and judgment. Moreover, the Tur does not mention here a price change between those times, though he discusses such a change later. It is therefore clear that in this ruling the value at slaughter and at judgment was the same. The Tur first says generally that he pays all amounts, indicating one valuation. His separate references to the time of slaughter for fourfold and fivefold payment and the time of judgment for principal and double payment identify the legal measuring points, while an actual price difference is addressed later.
