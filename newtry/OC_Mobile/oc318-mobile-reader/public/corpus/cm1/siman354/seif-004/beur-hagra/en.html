(Likkut) 'But I fattened it,' etc., implies specifically that he fattened it. Although double payment and four- or fivefold payment are involved, the same would appear to apply to principal, as stated there concerning appreciation in price. Tosafos, however, do not hold this way, as seen there, s.v. 'I,' etc., and likewise where the animal fattened on its own. Although Tosafos there, s.v. 'What difference is there,' are uncertain and write, 'According to this, the same applies if it became emaciated,' etc., and 'according to this reason,' etc., that is only for the approach of the present sugya. According to the final law, even in this respect all payments follow the value at the time of theft, as they write s.v. 'I,' etc. Maharsha likewise explains this, and Tosafos on daf 66a, s.v. 'lambs,' etc., write 'or the reverse,' etc. According to the Rif, however, natural fattening is also like a change in market price: principal is paid according to the earlier value, while double and four- or fivefold payment follow the present value. Rama did not write anything about principal here because no one disagrees on that aspect. The Rosh explains that the Rif's reason is that we do not rule like Rav Chanina, who says, 'They stole an ox,' etc., but like R' Ila, etc. His words require great study, for Rav Chanina was necessarily needed to explain the Baraisa according to the view that change does not acquire, precisely through the principle 'they stole an ox,' etc., as stated there: 'They disagree only,' etc., R' Ila said, 'lambs,' etc. Regarding a lean animal that fattened, or a fat one that became lean, all agree that everything follows the value at the time of theft. Regarding rise and fall in market price: if it depreciated, principal follows the time of theft and double and four- or fivefold payment follow the time of judgment; if it appreciated and he slaughtered it, all payments follow the time of judgment. If it died on its own, Rambam and the Tur hold that everything follows the time of theft, whereas Tosafos hold that double payment follows the time of judgment. See Tosafos, s.v. 'lambs.' Rabbeinu Tam's approach is that Rav's statement that double and four- or fivefold payment follow the time of judgment applies where the object depreciated, but where it appreciated or fattened, double and four- or fivefold payment follow the value at the time of theft. See Tosafos there. The Rosh and Tur write that when the Gemara says double and four- or fivefold payment follow the time of judgment, double payment literally follows judgment, whereas four- or fivefold payment follows the time of slaughter. The Tur writes that principal follows the same rule as double payment. Rambam, however, implies that even four- or fivefold payment follows the time of judgment, and Yam Shel Shlomo, siman 3, rules likewise. End.
(Likkut) The words of Rambam and the Tur, who write that where the stolen animal appreciated and then died on its own even double payment follows the value at the time of theft, are exceedingly difficult. This does not depend upon Rabbah's law. The Gemara says, 'Shall we say that Rav disagrees with Rabbah,' etc., implying that if he did disagree and held that slaughter is like natural death, the matter would be straightforward, which shows that double and four- or fivefold payment follow the time of judgment. Likewise on daf 66a, 'They disagree only,' etc., is explained according to Rav. Rashi on daf 65b writes that the question is not against Rabbah because Rabbah spoke only about principal. Tosafos and Semag are therefore exceedingly clear: there is no distinction in double and four- or fivefold payment; with rise or fall in market price they always follow the time of judgment. The Rosh likewise writes that the verse 'he shall pay life' is not teaching the rule for principal, but for double and four- or fivefold payment at the time of judgment. Rabbeinu Tam's approach is that double and four- or fivefold payment always follow the more lenient valuation. Although the Gemara asks, 'Shall we say Rav disagrees with Rabbah,' implying that the law of double and four- or fivefold is otherwise clear, that is only according to the initial assumption. The same applies to the question on daf 66a, 'They disagree only,' etc.; the Gemara ultimately answers there, R' Ila said, 'lambs,' etc. The general rule in these laws is as follows. If the thief intentionally fattened or emaciated the animal, all payments follow the value at the time of theft. Regarding market appreciation or depreciation, double and four- or fivefold payment always follow the time of judgment, while principal follows whichever valuation is more stringent against the thief. If the animal naturally became lean or fat, according to the Rif this is like market appreciation or depreciation, whereas according to Ri it is like an intentional physical change. Rabbeinu Tam agrees with the Rif on the classification, but holds that both natural change and market change cause double and four- or fivefold payment to follow whichever valuation is more lenient. The Rosh writes that even according to Ri, who treats natural fattening as an intentional change for double and four- or fivefold payment, regarding principal he agrees that it is like market appreciation or depreciation. According to this, Rama's words here would accord with everyone. Tosafos there, s.v. 'lambs,' however, do not imply this, for they write, 'It appears to Ri,' etc., 'like principal.' As practical law, the Rosh and Tur rule like the Rif, and Yam Shel Shlomo likewise rules so. End.
