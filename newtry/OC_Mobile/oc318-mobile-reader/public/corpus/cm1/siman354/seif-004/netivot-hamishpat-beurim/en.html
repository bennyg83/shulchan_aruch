See Sema, seif katan 9, who explains that once the thief slaughters or sells, the animal has changed from its prior form and therefore any later rise in value is a rise in the thief's property. This is very difficult. Everyone agrees that double payment is assessed according to the value at the time of standing in judgment, even if that occurs after slaughter, sale, or another change. Thus the fact that the thief already acquired through the change does not by itself freeze the statutory payment at the earlier value. The better explanation is that Torah itself fixed the measure of the statutory payments according to the relevant valuation point. Even if the thief has already acquired the body through change, the special obligation can still be measured by the later value specified by the law. The question is therefore not ordinary ownership of appreciation but the statutory measure of payment.
