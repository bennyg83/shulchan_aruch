He stole or robbed, etc. Ketzot HaChoshen cites Tosafos Rid, Kiddushin, chapter HaIsh Mekadesh, concerning the Gemara's rule that if one mistakenly used maaser sheni money to buy a nonkosher animal and the seller is no longer present, the purchaser must eat other food corresponding to that value in Yerushalayim. Tosafos asked how one can redeem money that is not in his possession. Tosafos Rid answers that the seller did not receive the money with intent to steal it, but only through mistake, and if he knew the sale was void he would return it and obey beit din. Therefore the money is like a deposit held by another. Tosafos elsewhere do not give this answer, and Ketzot infers that they disagree. Ketzot argues that even where the holder is unaware and would obey beit din, the property is still considered outside the owner's possession if the holder currently has some claim to retain it. Only a pure deposit, where the bailee has no defense and must return it whenever demanded, is considered in the owner's possession for purposes of consecration and transfer. Chatam Sofer, Choshen Mishpat, siman 169, discusses one whose item was stolen and then sold to an innocent purchaser. While it was in the purchaser's possession, the original owner consecrated it to charity. The questioner wished to validate the consecration based on Tosafos Rid, since had the purchaser known it was stolen he would never have bought it, and therefore perhaps it remained legally in the owner's possession. Chatam Sofer defends Tosafos Rid against Ketzot's proof and suggests that Tosafos may simply have avoided that answer because the Gemara's wording "the seller fled" sounds as though the seller was intentionally refusing to comply. He nevertheless distinguishes the present case. Tosafos Rid spoke about maaser sheni where, had the seller been present, he would have been pleased to unwind the mistaken sale and return the purchaser's money, taking other money for the animal. That situation is treated as though the money remained available to its owner. By contrast, property in the hands of an innocent buyer from a thief is not considered in the original owner's possession. The owner therefore cannot consecrate it while it remains there. See there.
