But if the sender left property, the claimant collects from that property and the sender's heirs do not claim against the agent. It is as though their father were alive, and they recover the money from him.
There, in the name of Ramah: since he said 'take it,' it is as though he said 'acquire it,' or as though he said 'take it on condition that you give it to so-and-so.' If the agent fulfills the condition, he acquires; if not, he does not acquire, and it is as though the money never left the possession of the borrower or depositary. Bach agrees, because the settled halachah is that whenever an agent violates the instructions of the principal, the agency is void from the outset.
