If the messenger comes to retain the money for a debt owed to him by the creditor. The Tur writes in the name of Rabbi Meir HaLevi Abulafia: if the messenger wishes to retain the money for a debt that the lender or depositor owes him, he cannot. Since the sender said, "Give it to him," the expression "bring" is treated like "acquire for him," or, more precisely, like saying, "Take it on condition that you give the money to so and so." If the messenger fulfills that condition, the acquisition is effective; if he does not, it is not. It is therefore as though the money never left the hands of the borrower or custodian. It appears, however, that this is specifically where the sender says, "Bring" or "Give." Those formulations expressly require that the messenger actually deliver the property to the lender or depositor. Although they are treated like "acquire" for purposes of the sender's inability to retract, the delivery instruction still remains. It is therefore as though the sender said: acquire it for so and so on condition that you place it into his hand. But if the sender says simply, "Acquire it for him," there is no such condition. The recipient acquires immediately. The messenger should then be able to retain the property against the lender or depositor for a debt that they owe him, because the property has already become theirs and is now in the messenger's possession.
