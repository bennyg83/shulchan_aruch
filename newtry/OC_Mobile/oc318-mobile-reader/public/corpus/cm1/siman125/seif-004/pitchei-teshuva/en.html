As to the further statement that in the present case he cannot petition for release, Tumim, seif katan 8, is uncertain. Yoreh Deah, siman 258, speaks of property that has reached the charity treasurer, who is treated as the hand of the poor. Perhaps if it was given merely to another individual, the donor can still petition for release, because the donor said only "give" and not "acquire on behalf of," making the recipient merely the donor's agent. See Responsa Noda BiYehudah, second edition, siman 50, and what is cited from it in Pitchei Teshuva to Yoreh Deah, siman 258, seif katan 8. See also Responsa Beit Ephraim, Choshen Mishpat, siman 70, concerning Reuven who gave Shimon one hundred to invest on iska with a suitable borrower, directing that principal and profit remain with Shimon until a certain minor relative reached marriage age, at which time the fund would be given to her or her representative upon her marriage. Reuven expressly stipulated that before that time the girl should have no present right whatever in either principal or profits, and that the funds should not yet bear her name. A loss later occurred, and the girl's father sued Shimon. Shimon answered that the father was not his litigant: even if appointed guardian for his daughter, she had no present right in the money. When Reuven, the donor, sued him, he would answer Reuven. Beit Ephraim rules clearly for Shimon. The funds remain legally within the donor's domain, and the girl's father is not yet a party to the dispute. He derives this from the Mordechai in the chapter Mi Shemet in the name of Maharam and from the Rosh in the chapter Metziat HaIshah, cited by Tur and Shulchan Aruch, siman 53, seif 3. He adds that even if the donor had expressly told the stakeholder to acquire the fund on the girl's behalf so that she would acquire it upon reaching adulthood, she still would not acquire now, as appears from Nedarim 48. Nor can one treat this simply as a gift to a poor person, where a promise has the force of a vow and cannot be withdrawn. That law applies where the donor says "take" without fixing a future time. Where he fixes a future time, he may retract before that time, as Rashba writes regarding one who says, "This sela shall be charity after thirty days." Thus even if the arrangement were made in a form from which the donor could not retract and acquisition would necessarily occur at the specified future time, the girl presently owns nothing in principal or profits and therefore has no standing to litigate with the stakeholder. Beit Ephraim further maintains that if the stakeholder negligently loses the money, he is not presently liable to the girl merely because she may obtain a right later. Her future interest is not present property but only something that may cause monetary consequences, and we rule that such a contingent cause is not itself money. This differs from damaging another's lien, where a present lien already exists. Here she presently owns nothing in body or produce, similar to the rule of "my property to you and after you to Ploni." All the more so where the donor can still retract, because then even the future monetary consequence is uncertain. Therefore the stakeholder would be exempt even for negligence as against her future claim, and certainly need not litigate now with her representative. See there.
