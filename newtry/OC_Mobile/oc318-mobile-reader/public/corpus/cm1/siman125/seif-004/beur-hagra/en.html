And even if he received them, etc. As stated in Bava Batra 45a: "If so, even with witnesses as well, since," etc.
And there is an authority who says, etc. See Beer HaGolah. This requires study as to which case it addresses. If the sender died, why can the agent not retain the funds? Even if they were held by others and he seized them from there, why should the seizure not be effective, as explained above in siman 58? All the more so here, where the creditor has no claim at all against the heirs. If it refers to the first case, where the sender is alive but has no other property, why should the seizure not be effective? Even if the property were still in the sender's hands, the creditor could take it under Rabbi Natan's rule. It therefore appears that the Ramah's ruling likewise concerns a case in which the sender has other property. This is the meaning of "as though it had not left the hand of," etc. We do not take it from the sender because he has other property, as above. Without that reason, although the sender remains responsible, the Ramah holds that the agent may retain the funds. This is a sound position, for what practical difference is there, since in any event the sender must pay him? As stated there, "In the end," etc. One might answer that collection cannot be compelled under Rabbi Natan's rule because the sender has other property, but once the creditor has already acquired these funds for the lender, it is as though they are in the lender's own hand and they do not have the status of encumbered property in a purchaser's possession. Regarding a deposit, the Rashba writes that the creditor may collect it, as explained above in siman 104, seif 4 in the gloss, showing that it is not treated as encumbered property in a purchaser's possession. Certainly, then, the creditor himself should be able to retain it. Rather, the reason he cannot is that the lender never acquired it, since the agent acted contrary to the sender's instructions, so it remains as though it were in the sender's hand, and collection cannot be compelled because the sender has other property. This is clearly the Ramah's position. The language of the Tur and Shulchan Aruch requires study.
