And some say, etc. It appears that the latter view disputes the first and maintains that even if the sender died and the agent has a migo, he cannot retain the money for himself, for the reason stated below. The Bach understood that this view disputes the opening case, where the sender has no assets and the creditor admits that he owes the agent. He therefore asks what practical difference exists. Since the creditor admits that he owes the agent, and the sender would have been obligated to return the money to the agent because the creditor has no other assets, why send the money around in a circle? He answers that the difference arises where the creditor also owes other creditors. According to the latter view, since the agent did not acquire the money and it is as though it never left the debtor or custodian, all creditors share in this debt. According to the first view, which is that of the Baal HaTerumot, where the creditor has no other assets the agent has already acquired the money and the other creditors have no further right in it. I do not understand his reasoning. The basis of the first view is that the sender says to the agent: “Even had the money remained in my hands, it would have been extracted from me under Rabbi Natan’s lien.” If other creditors exist, however, this particular agent would not have extracted all of it from the sender, because the other creditors too would have collected from the sender under Rabbi Natan’s lien. Why, then, should the other creditors have no right in the money? The correct explanation is therefore as I have written.
He cannot. Since the sender said, “Take it,” this is equivalent either to saying, “Acquire it,” or to saying, “Take it on condition that you give it to so-and-so.” If the agent fulfills the condition, he acquires it for the recipient; if he does not, he does not acquire it, and it is as though the money never left the debtor or custodian. This is the Ramah’s language as quoted by the Tur. The Bach writes that this view is compelling, because it is a settled rule that whenever an agent departs from the sender’s instructions, the agency is void from its inception and the property is treated as though it never left the sender’s domain. In my humble opinion, this is no proof, because here the agent does not violate the sender’s instructions at all. He merely wishes to retain the money for his own debt. Examine this carefully.
