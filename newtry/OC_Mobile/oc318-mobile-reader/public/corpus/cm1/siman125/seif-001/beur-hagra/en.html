Therefore, whether, etc. This follows from the Gemara's statements there on 14a, "No, all agree that 'take' is," etc., and on 14b, "All agree that 'take' is not," etc.
The same applies, etc. The Yerushalmi there states: if one says, "Carry this maneh to so-and-so," "Take this maneh to so-and-so," or "Let this maneh be for so-and-so in your hand," if he wishes to retract, he may not retract.
But if he said, etc. Since the Gemara says that the reason is that "take" is equivalent to "acquire," meaning that although regarding a get the Gemara disputes whether "take" is equivalent to "acquire," and likewise regarding a gift here all agree that an expression of acquisition is required, this indicates that some language of acquisition is nevertheless necessary. See Shach.
And if he returned it, etc. The Yerushalmi there, in the incident of Rabbi Dostai, records Rabbi Chaggai as saying: this applies where he could not withstand them; but if he could withstand them, he must take the money back from them and give it to the intended recipient. In other words, if the agent could withstand their threats, he is liable. See Maggid Mishneh and Rashba there. This is the meaning of "And if because of," etc., and the Gemara likewise indicates this.
Who has in his power, etc. As above, in the case where he could not withstand them. This is also what the Gemara means when it says, "These people," etc., "have horses," etc. It is identical with the Yerushalmi's distinction.
And some say that when he returned, etc. The agent bears responsibility as above, while the sender bears responsibility as stated in the Yerushalmi: one who says, "Give this maneh to so-and-so whom I owe," or "Take this maneh to so-and-so," etc., remains responsible for it until that person receives what is his. See Sema and Shach.
Reuven, etc. See Tosafot there, s.v. Lesof, etc., and see Sema and Shach. The statement that matters held only in the heart, etc., is in chapter 2 of Kiddushin. See there.
