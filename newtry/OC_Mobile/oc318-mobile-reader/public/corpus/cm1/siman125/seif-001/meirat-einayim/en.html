But if he said, 'Return it,' etc. This is somewhat difficult. One who examines the Ritva's responsum from which this law comes, cited by the Beit Yosef in this siman, will see that the case there concerns a gift, as shown by the proof from the case where the agent went and sought the recipient but did not find him, which concerns a gift. If so, perhaps only with a gift, where even the word 'take' does not effect acquisition, did the Ritva also write that 'return' is ineffective. With a debt or deposit, however, 'return' may be effective just as 'take' is effective. The responsum does say that the expression 'return' is weaker than 'take,' but it appears that this was written only to teach that 'return' is not more effective than 'take' for a gift. With a debt or deposit, it is highly likely that the Ritva agrees that 'return' is effective. This requires study.

And if he returned it because of coercion, etc., he is exempt. This is not comparable to Rama's nearby gloss, based on the Mordechai, where the law is in favor of the depositor. It is strained to distinguish merely by saying that there the agent is told not to return the money initially, whereas here if he returned it after the fact he is exempt. Rather, there Reuven appointed Levi as his agent to receive the money. The moment Levi received it, it became Reuven's money. If Levi then returns it to Shimon because Shimon threatens him, Levi is saving himself with another person's property.
