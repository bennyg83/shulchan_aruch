Sending a debt or deposit through an agent. It contains 10 se'ifim.
If Reuven owes Shimon one hundred or holds a deposit for him, and tells Levi, 'Bring this one hundred to Shimon,' he cannot retract and take it back from Levi. The word 'bring' is treated like 'acquire,' and as soon as the money reaches Levi, Levi acquires it on Shimon's behalf. Therefore, whether the borrower or lender dies, the money is given to Shimon's heirs. Nevertheless, Reuven remains responsible for it until it reaches Shimon. {Rama: The same applies if he says, 'Carry this one hundred to so-and-so,' or, 'Let this one hundred belong to so-and-so.' Each expression is treated like 'bring' and like 'acquire.' But if he says, 'Return it to so-and-so,' it is not treated like 'bring' or 'acquire.' If Levi returned the one hundred to Reuven, Reuven later became impoverished and could not pay, and Shimon thereby lost his money, Levi must pay because he was negligent in returning it to Reuven. If Levi returned it only under duress, because Reuven threatened him with something within Reuven's power to do and thereby compelled him to return it, Levi is exempt. Some say that when Levi returns the one hundred to Reuven, both remain responsible for it until Shimon receives his entire debt. If Reuven had money in Shimon's possession and told Levi to receive it from Shimon, Levi received it, and Shimon then asked Levi to return the money because he had paid it by mistake and threatened that otherwise he would deny a separate debt he owed Levi, Levi cannot say to Reuven, 'I did not receive it for you in order to lose my own money,' and return it. Reuven may say that Levi already completed his agency and acquired the money for him. The law follows Reuven. See note 4.}
