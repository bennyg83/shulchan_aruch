When he came to him, we were when he was in the middle of his apartment, but if he had come to his office, he would not be able to give him a call to him, and he did not return to him
He did not say, 'Acquire.' Even if the deposit is in the agent's courtyard, he does not acquire it merely for the recipient. Sema holds that this is only where the principal did not say, 'Acquire.' If he did say, 'Acquire,' then if the deposit is in the agent's courtyard, he acquires it. Mechaber's language likewise inclines that way. See Tumim, where I wrote that Mechaber's phrase, 'if he told him to acquire it while the object was in his hand,' is not literal; possession within his domain is enough. The Shach holds that an unqualified 'Acquire it for him' does not help unless the principal actually transfers the courtyard to him. See Tumim, seif katan 11, where I discussed this and brought proof from Tosafot and Ramban in Bava Batra that there is no distinction, unlike the Shach. I also discussed several unresolved points there. The Shach further holds that even Rabbeinu Tam agrees that with a debt or deposit, the item must pass from hand to hand, as Rambam and Rabbi Meir HaLevi Abulafia require. He cites Tosafot, Rosh, and Mordechai. See Tumim. I showed that those sources speak only according to Rabbeinu Tam's first answer. Under his final answer, that 'give' is not like 'acquire' in a gift, the proof falls away. Tosafot in Gittin likewise indicates this. Nevertheless, I agreed with the Shach as practical law, and Tur in Yoreh Deah 267 clearly supports him.
