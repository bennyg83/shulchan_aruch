And some say that "give" is like "acquire." See Shach seif katan 29. There is a textual error at the end; it should refer to siman 126 seif 2 below. Ketzot HaChoshen writes that according to the alternative opinion in seif 4, although "give" is like "acquire," it remains conditioned upon actual delivery. The messenger therefore has the choice not to deliver and not to acquire for the recipient, as Rif and Rambam write regarding a get. Ketzot consequently asks on seif 1: if the messenger returned the money to the sender and the sender later became impoverished, why is the messenger liable, since the condition of actual delivery was never fulfilled and the recipient never acquired? This is no difficulty. Monetary conditions differ. Any condition inserted solely for the recipient's benefit can be waived by the recipient, who may say, "I regard it as though I received it." This is the principle stated in Nedarim concerning one who vows if the other does not give his son a kor of wheat. By contrast, in matters of prohibition, such as a get or deed of emancipation, the object must actually reach the recipient. See also Shach seif katan 32, where there is a textual error. It should read: "when the seller said to the custodian, 'acquire it for so and so,' and the custodian says, 'let my courtyard acquire it for me.'"
