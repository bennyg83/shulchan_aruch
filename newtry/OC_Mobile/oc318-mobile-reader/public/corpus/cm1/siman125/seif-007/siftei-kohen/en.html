But if he did not say “Acquire it” in one of the recognized manners, even if the deposit is in the agent’s courtyard, etc. This is the Ramah’s wording in the Tur, and the Aruch HaShulchan likewise quotes it. The Sema explains that the sender did not say “Acquire,” but only “Give” or “Take.” If, however, he said “Acquire,” and the object later entered the agent’s courtyard, the recipient acquires it according to all opinions, even though the agent did not lift it for that purpose, since he originally accepted the task of acquiring it and the sender did not retract. At first glance, the Mechaber appears to have understood it this way, because he omitted the phrase “in one of the recognized manners” and apparently held that all agree that the agent’s courtyard is equivalent to his hand. In truth, however, the phrase “but if he did not say ‘Acquire it’ in one of the recognized manners” does not bear that meaning. Moreover, it should then have said: “But if he did not say ‘Acquire it,’ even if it is in his courtyard,” and the greater novelty would have been that if he did not say “Acquire,” even when it is actually in his hand the recipient does not acquire, as stated immediately before. Furthermore, such a ruling would be unnecessary, because it was already stated that if the object did not initially come into the agent’s hand for the purpose of acquisition, then even if he holds it when told “Give,” the recipient does not acquire. All the more so where it merely lies in his courtyard and did not originally enter the courtyard for acquisition. Rather, the correct explanation is as follows. Even when the sender says “Acquire,” the recipient does not acquire unless the instruction is joined with one of the recognized modes, namely that the object is then in the agent’s hand or that he subsequently lifts it on the recipient’s behalf. When the sender tells him “Acquire,” the agent becomes his agent; if the object is in the agent’s hand, or he later lifts it for the recipient, he thereby acquires it for him. But if the sender did not say “Acquire” together with one of those recognized modes, then even if the deposit is in the agent’s courtyard when he is told “Acquire,” the recipient does not acquire. Although the agent became his agent, by what act does he acquire for the recipient? He did not lift it for the recipient; it merely lay in his courtyard. The Bach likewise appears to understand the Ramah this way. Nevertheless, it appears that Rabbeinu Tam and the Ramah do not truly disagree here, for another reason. Below, siman 200, seif 2, it is ruled that where produce belonging to the seller is deposited with another person, the buyer’s courtyard may acquire it through verbal instruction alone. This means that the seller tells the custodian, “Acquire it for so-and-so,” and the custodian says, “Let my courtyard acquire it for him,” as explained there. The Ramah therefore teaches only that the courtyard by itself does not acquire for the recipient. If the agent subsequently says, “Let my courtyard acquire it for him,” it does acquire. Rabbeinu Tam’s case is one in which the agent makes such a declaration that his domain should acquire for the recipient. This also explains why the Tur cites the Ramah’s words last here, yet below in siman 243 records only the first formulation. Examine this carefully.
