(Seif 3) But if he owns property. Many ask from the end of siman 160, where a depositary who delivered the deposit to the depositor's creditor is exempt because he can say, “The creditor may collect from you or from your other property.” There is no difficulty. Here the creditor's complaint is specifically against the sender: “I would have discharged the creditor with something else, not with the money in your hands,” and that complaint is valid. In siman 160, by contrast, the depositor seeks to evade his own creditor entirely and has no legitimate interest in the particular property used for payment. The depositary can say that the deposit still stands for him, since the creditor will return it when the depositor pays from other assets. Therefore the phrase “or from your other property” is effective there. The Tur's statement that the agent returns and collects “that amount” is not literal. He collects his debt from whatever property he finds.
