Give one hundred to so-and-so as a gift, etc. At first glance, this implies that regarding a debt or deposit, all agree that “give” is equivalent to “acquire,” even where the object is not presently handed to the agent, but enters his domain only afterward, before the sender retracts. In truth, however, Tosafot in the first chapter of Gittin 13a, the Rosh there in the name of Rabbeinu Tam, the Mordechai there, Tosafot in Bava Batra 85a in the name of Rabbeinu Tam, and Rabbeinu Yerucham, netiv 15, beginning of part 2, all state that even with a debt or deposit, “take” or “give” is equivalent to “acquire” only when the sender now hands it to the agent and says “take” or “give.” It may be that the Tur and Mechaber agree. Their point here is only that according to Rabbeinu Tam, with a gift, “give” is not equivalent to “acquire” even when the donor presently hands the object to the agent, whereas according to the Ramah, “give” is equivalent to “acquire” in such a case even with a gift. Examine this carefully. See Even HaEzer, siman 140, seif 6, concerning “take” and “give” with respect to a bill of divorce.
So long as it has not reached the recipient. See Responsa Mahari Yaakov of the House of Levi, siman 55. The Mabit writes in responsa, volume 2, siman 195, that even Rabbeinu Tam, who holds that “give” with a gift is not equivalent to “acquire,” agrees that once the agent has brought it to the recipient’s location, it is equivalent to acquisition, because the recipient could then take it. See there. This does not appear correct. The authorities, the Tur, and the Mechaber indicate that the right to retract depends simply on whether the property has reached the recipient. Perhaps the Mabit also relied on the fact that in his case part of the gift had already been delivered, as his language somewhat suggests. Yet even that does not suffice, because one may retract the portion not yet delivered even after part has been given, as stated below in siman 226, seif 2.
He said, “And he shall be blessed with him, and he shall be given to him, and he shall be given to him.”
That was in his domain, etc. The Bach writes that this follows Rabbeinu Tam, whereas according to the Ramah cited by the Tur, the object must actually be in the agent’s hand. He adds that below in siman 243 the Tur rules without qualification like Rabbeinu Tam, and that this is the practical ruling, contrary to the Shulchan Aruch here, which did not decide. This is no objection to the Mechaber. On the contrary, the Mechaber understood that all agree that an agent’s domain is equivalent to his hand, as I wrote above in seif katan 32, and he states this without qualification below in siman 243, seif 4, in presenting the Tur’s words.
