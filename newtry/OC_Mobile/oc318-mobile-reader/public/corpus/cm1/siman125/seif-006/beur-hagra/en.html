If he said, etc. This is derived from the expressions there, "Take a maneh to so-and-so" and "Give a maneh to so-and-so." If "give" were equivalent to "acquire," why would it be necessary to state "give" regarding a debt, where even "take" is effective? The Tosefta at the end of chapter 1 states: one who says, "Give a maneh to so-and-so that I owe him," etc., or "a deposit of his that is in my possession," etc., may not retract, and he remains responsible until the recipient receives what is his. If one says, "Take this maneh to so-and-so," "Give this maneh to so-and-so," "Take this gift document," or "Give this gift document to so-and-so," he may retract, etc. But if he says, "Receive this maneh for so-and-so," "Acquire this gift for so-and-so," "Receive this gift document for so-and-so," or "Acquire this gift document for so-and-so," he may not retract. The Yerushalmi there states similarly, though its text is somewhat corrupt. At the beginning of halachah 6 it asks: "One who says," etc.; "Everywhere you say that 'give' is like 'take,' yet here you say that 'give' is like 'acquire'?" It answers that the Mishnah should be read: "Acquire this get for my wife; acquire this writ of emancipation for my slave," etc. Although the Babylonian Gemara disagrees there, saying, "And if you would say, our Mishnah," etc., that is because it regards emancipation as analogous to a debt, as Tosafot explain there. Nevertheless, we learn from the Yerushalmi that wherever "take" is not equivalent to "acquire," the same is true of "give."
Unless, etc. As above in the Tosefta, and likewise from the Gemara as above, for "take" is not equivalent to "acquire," implying that an explicit expression of acquisition is effective.

Nevertheless, etc. This is implied there, where it says that after death they should not give it. This implies that during his lifetime they should give it, necessarily for the reason stated there on 11b, "And if you would say, our Mishnah," etc.
Unless, etc. As stated in Bava Metzia 10a, "And if you would say, our Mishnah," etc. The Gemara initially thought that the earlier appointment was equivalent to acquisition, and answered that the Mishnah concerns a case where he had said so from the outset.
