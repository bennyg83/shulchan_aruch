Give. At first glance it appears that regarding a debt or deposit, everyone agrees that give is equivalent to acquire even where the object is not presently handed to the agent, provided it later comes into the agent's possession before the sender retracts. In fact, Tosafot in the first chapter of Gittin and Rosh there write in the name of Rabbeinu Tam that even with a debt or deposit, bring or give is equivalent to acquire only when the object is presently handed to the agent. So too Tosafot, Bava Batra 85, Mordechai, and Rabbeinu Yerucham. One may say that Tur and Shulchan Aruch also agree, and teach here only that according to Rabbeinu Tam, for a gift give is not equivalent to acquire even when the object is handed over immediately, whereas according to Ramah, in such a case give is equivalent to acquire even for a gift. See Even HaEzer siman 140 seif 6 regarding bring and give with respect to a bill of divorce. Shach.
Into his hand. Meaning, into the recipient's hand. See Responsa Mahari Beit Levi, siman 55. Mabit II, siman 195, writes that even according to Rabbeinu Tam, who holds that with a gift give is not equivalent to acquire, he agrees that where the agent brought it to the recipient's place it is as though he acquired it, since the recipient could then take it. This does not appear correct, and the authorities and Tur and Shulchan Aruch indicate that the donor's right of retraction continues until the gift actually reaches the recipient's hand. Perhaps Mabit combined this with the additional fact in his case that part had already been given to the recipient. Yet even that does not suffice, because the donor may retract from whatever portion has not yet been given, as stated in siman 226 seif 2. Shach.
In his possession. It appears that even with a debt or deposit, and even where he said acquire, the property must subsequently enter the agent's possession; otherwise the sender may retract. Ramban writes so in his novellae, and it is explicit in Tosafot and Rosh cited above. Bach writes that according to Ramah, cited by Tur, the property must literally be in the agent's hand. In siman 243 Tur rules without qualification like Rabbeinu Tam, and this is the rule, unlike Shulchan Aruch here, which did not decide the issue. This is not an objection to the Mechaber, however, because he holds that all agree that one's domain is legally equivalent to one's hand, as he rules without qualification in siman 243 seif 4 in accordance with Tur. Shach.
