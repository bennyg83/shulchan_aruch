Ibid., Mechaber, end of seif 2, "he came to retract." Note: if the agent accepted full liability like the original custodian, the ruling is clear. But if he did not accept liability and the case is one of a paid custodian who entrusted the item to an unpaid custodian, thereby reducing the level of care, this requires study. Such conduct is deemed negligence, as below, siman 291, seif 26, and as the Kesef Mishneh wrote at the end of chapter 1 of Hilchot Sechirut. If so, the transfer is a liability rather than a benefit to the depositor, who can say, "I do not want my property in the hands of an unpaid custodian," and the sender should be able to retract. This also appears explicitly from the Gemara, which answers by establishing the case where the recipient had already demonstrated himself to be a denier, rather than answering that the depositor regularly entrusted property to that agent. It follows that the baraita concerns a case where the agent bears no liability. See there. This requires study, and so it appears to me. See my Responsa Chatam Sofer, Choshen Mishpat, siman 94.
