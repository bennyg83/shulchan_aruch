I wish to retain them on account of the debt. See above, siman 83 at the end of seif 2, where Rama discusses an agent who retains money because the sender owes him. The Mechaber discusses this at the end of siman 58. In siman 99, the Tur and Mechaber discuss one who seizes property placed in his hands by an agent and claims that the agent or broker owes him. See there, seifim 3 and 1.
And he has no other property as chairman of Israel to settle for a low-level settlement from here to the M.C.:
He did not die from the messenger who was sent to the messenger who did not come to the hand of Isaac:
Even if he has no property, etc. The Tur writes that although the agent is believed against the lender to say, I am retaining this toward my debt, through a migo that he could have said, I paid you, nevertheless, until the money reaches the lender the agency has not ended. The agent therefore remains the litigant of the sender, and against the sender he has no migo, because he is not believed to say that he returned the money, since the expression 'take it' is treated like 'acquire it for him.' Therefore, if the sender dies, etc. This means that if the agent did have a migo even against the sender, such as where the money was delivered to him without witnesses so that he could have denied ever receiving it, he could say: I carried out your agency by collecting the money for the lender toward the debt that the lender owed me. His agency would then terminate upon his taking an oath of hesset concerning that debt, through the migo that he could have denied receiving the money. See the Derishah, where I wrote further that in such a case the sender too may be exempt from the person to whom he sent the money.
