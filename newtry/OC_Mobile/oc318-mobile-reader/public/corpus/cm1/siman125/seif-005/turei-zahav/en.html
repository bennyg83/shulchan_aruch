(Seif 2). “But he has not become established as a denier.” We must say that it is not known that the second depositary is trusted by the depositor. Otherwise the first may not retract, as stated at the end of this seif. Since his trustworthiness is unknown, the first depositary remains liable even before he retrieves the deposit. Therefore, if the depositor comes to the first depositary, the latter cannot simply refer him to the second; he must recover it from the second and return it to the owner. The text says that he “may” retract because it is addressing only whether he has the power to do so, but once he has transferred it he remains the depositor's litigant. Sema, seif katan 13, writes otherwise, but in my view it is astonishing. How can a depositary be discharged merely by giving the property to another person whose trustworthiness to the owner is unknown?
