Regarding a gift, etc. The reason is that with a deposit or debt the property already belongs to the person to whom it is being sent, and therefore relatively simple language suffices to acquire it on his behalf. With a gift, by contrast, he is only now acquiring it, and therefore stronger language is required. See Sema and Shach; this is straightforward.
It becomes a vow, etc. The Sema holds that if he did not yet hand it to the messenger, he may seek release from his vow. The Shach agrees and supports this from a responsum of the Rashba. This is correct. However, I remain uncertain as a matter of practical law whether he may seek release from the vow even after the gift has reached the messenger. The Rashba provides no proof on this point. I discussed the reasoning somewhat at length in Tumim; see there.
