It becomes a vow. The Tur's language is: A declaration to sacred purposes is like delivery to an ordinary person. He writes the same below in siman 243. The Beit Yosef objects that this principle does not apply here, and therefore gives another reason, namely that the statement becomes a vow, as the Mechaber writes here. His reason is that the rule of a declaration to sacred purposes applies only to consecrated property, while a poor person is an ordinary person. The Ir Shushan writes similarly. The Beit Yosef further objects in siman 243 that if the Tur meant a literal equivalent of transfer to an ordinary person, why require him to say to another person, Take it? It would have been enough merely to say, I will give such and such to a poor person, which itself cannot be retracted. In my view there is no difficulty. Charity to the poor is also included in a declaration to sacred purposes, because one who says, I will give such and such to the poor, is as though he said, I will give it to sacred purposes, for the poor receive from the table of Heaven. Proof appears from the verse, 'That which has gone from your lips you shall keep and perform, as you have vowed to Hashem your God, a freewill offering that you spoke with your mouth.' The Gemara derives from this both consecrated offerings and charity. Scripture includes them in one verse and refers to all of them as vows to Hashem your God. It is already explained in the Gemara, and in the Tur and Mechaber in Yoreh Deah siman 238, that even one who merely says, I will give such and such to a poor person, may seek release from his vow through an opening and regret so long as the property remains in his possession. Once he has given it to another person to deliver to the poor person, however, release is no longer available. Thus the phrase, a declaration to sacred purposes is like delivery to an ordinary person, means that he cannot simply retract, although while the property remains in his possession he may still obtain release through regret. Once it has left his possession, even that option is gone. See the Derishah.
