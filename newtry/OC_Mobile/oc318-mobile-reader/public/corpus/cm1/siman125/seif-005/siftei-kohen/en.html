But with a gift, etc. A deposit or debt already belongs to the person to whom it is being sent, and another can therefore acquire it for him with relatively simple language. A gift, by contrast, presently grants him property that was not previously his, and therefore clearer and more effective language is required. See Responsa Mahari ibn Lev, volume 3, siman 11.

It becomes a vow, etc. Therefore, once he has delivered the property to the agent, he can no longer retract even by obtaining release from the vow. Had he not delivered it, although he would have been obligated to fulfill his vow, he could nevertheless have petitioned for release and had it annulled, as stated in Yoreh Deah, siman 258. The Sema so writes. The Rashba likewise writes in responsum 656 that in the present case one cannot petition for release. His language is: Anything already placed in the hands of the person acquiring it, or in the hands of another who acquires it for the poor, cannot be retracted, for it is then like terumah in the hands of a kohen, regarding which one can no longer petition for release.
