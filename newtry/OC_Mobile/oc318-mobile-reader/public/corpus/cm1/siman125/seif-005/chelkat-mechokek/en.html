Only where the entire letter was formed in this manner. Since he does not perform an act upon the body of the remaining letter, but merely scrapes around it and thereby the letter comes into being, this is considered engraving the surroundings. But where, after scraping and erasing, he still has to write and add in order to make it into a proper letter, this is not engraving the surroundings. Terumat HaDeshen implies that he was not entirely certain about this ruling, because part of the letter is nevertheless formed automatically. Beit Yosef, however, writes that whenever the letter does not come into being automatically, it is not called engraving the surroundings. See Responsa Mahari Weil, siman 107, who rules so plainly in practice, and Orach Chaim 32:18.
