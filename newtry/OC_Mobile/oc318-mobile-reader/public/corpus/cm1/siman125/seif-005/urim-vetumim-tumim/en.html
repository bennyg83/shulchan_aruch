It becomes a vow and he cannot retract. See Sema, who writes that he may seek annulment of his vow so long as he has not delivered the money to the messenger. The Shach in seif katan 27 similarly cites a responsum of the Rashba. I remain uncertain. In the Rashba's responsum the donor gave property to people so that they should acquire it on behalf of the poor. They therefore acquired for the poor, and how could the donor afterward annul the vow? Here, however, where he merely says, "Give it to the poor," and we do not rule that "give" means "acquire" for a gift, the messengers' hands remain like the donor's own hand and the property remains in his domain in every respect. Perhaps he can therefore seek annulment. What causes my doubt is the Tur and Shulchan Aruch, who write that once the property reaches the hand of the charity treasurer, he can no longer seek annulment. This is also the ruling of the Tur and Shulchan Aruch in siman 258. Why specify the treasurer? If delivery to any person barred annulment, there would be no need to mention him. Evidently, only delivery to the treasurer has that effect because his hand is like the hand of the poor. Delivery to another person would have that effect only if the donor said "acquire," but where he merely said "give," which is not equivalent to "acquire," perhaps he can still seek annulment. The matter requires study. See also the Rosh in Nedarim in the name of Maharam, who holds that even terumah already in a Kohen's hand may still be annulled by the Israelite. This requires study.
