Shall return. Since the intended recipient was already dead, he was incapable of acquiring. So writes the Tur. The Shach adds that even if the giver said, "Acquire for so-and-so," the acquisition fails when that person was already dead at the time of the instruction. Rashi's wording implies that this is an acquisition made in error. This is also proven by the rule that "bring" stated by a person on his deathbed is like immediate acquisition, since his words are treated as written and delivered. Shach.
During the giver's lifetime. Although a gift of a person on his deathbed ordinarily takes effect only after the completion of death, as stated in siman 248, seif 4, here the giver removed the money from his own possession, indicating that he intended the recipient to acquire it immediately. Beit Yosef. The same appears in Responsa Rivash 346 in the questioner's presentation.
Were born. Although one who attempts to transfer property to a fetus does not normally effect an acquisition, as stated in siman 210, here the acquisition works because the words of a person on his deathbed are treated as written and delivered. The recipient is regarded as having acquired retroactively from the time the money was handed over, and it then passes to his heirs. Tur.
Retracts. This follows the law of a gift made by a person on his deathbed, where we assess his presumed intent, as stated in simanim 246 and 250. Sema. The Shach cites the Rivash that the Rashba appears to hold that if the recipient was alive when the money was delivered, the recipient or his heirs acquire even if the giver later recovers from his illness. The Mordechai and Hagahot Ashri cite Ri HaLevi similarly. The Rivash appears to distinguish a full deathbed gift, such as one of all the giver's property or one expressly made because of impending death. In such a case, even according to the Rashba, if the giver recovers he may retract. See there. See the Taz.
