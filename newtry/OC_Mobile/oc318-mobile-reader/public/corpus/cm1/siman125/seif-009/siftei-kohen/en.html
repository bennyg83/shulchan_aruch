He shall return it to the heirs of the sender. Since the recipient had already died, he was not capable of acquiring when the sender gave the instruction. In my view, even if the sender said, “Acquire it for so and so,” there is no acquisition where the intended recipient was already dead at that time. This is also indicated by Rashi, who calls it an acquisition made in error. It is likewise clear because the instruction “take” of a person on his deathbed has the force of “acquire,” since his words are treated as written and delivered.
Even if the recipient died during the donor’s lifetime. Although we rule that a deathbed gift does not take effect until completion of the donor’s death, as explained below in siman 248, seif 4, here the donor removed the money from his own possession. His intent is therefore that the recipient acquire it immediately. So writes Beit Yosef, and this also appears in Responsa Rivash, siman 346.
And even if the recipient’s heirs were born afterward, etc. Although one who grants property to a fetus ordinarily effects no acquisition, as explained below in siman 210, here the acquisition is effective because the words of a person on his deathbed are treated as written and delivered. The recipient acquires retroactively from the time of delivery and transmits the property to his heirs. So writes the Tur.
And the donor cannot retract. This differs from an ordinary deathbed gift, where the donor has not made physical delivery and has not removed the money from his control. Here, because he removed the money from his possession, his intent is clearly that the recipient acquire immediately if the donor dies. So writes the Tur in the name of the Ramah.
And if he recovers, he may retract, etc. The Rivash, siman 346, writes that the Rashba appears to hold that if the recipient was alive when the money was delivered, the recipient or his heirs acquire even if the donor later recovers. The Mordechai and Hagahot Ashri cite the same view in the name of Rabbi Yosef HaLevi. The Rivash indicates, however, that where the donor made a complete deathbed gift, such as a gift of all his property or an explicit instruction because of impending death, even the Rashba agrees that if the donor recovers he may retract.
