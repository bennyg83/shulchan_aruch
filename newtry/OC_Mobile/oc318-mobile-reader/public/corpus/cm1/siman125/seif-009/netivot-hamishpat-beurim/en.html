And if the giver was a shechiv mera. Rivash, responsum 346, makes clear that according to all opinions we require a shechiv mera who gives because of impending death, or one who gives all his property. Rashba, however, holds that even a partial gift is effective when a shechiv mera says "bring," and he cannot retract. The Tur does not follow Rashba. See Rivash, whose words indicate that the principal halachah is not like Rashba. In the case of a healthy person, it appears that according to all opinions the giver must say "give" at the very time he removes the money from his own possession, because the central reason Sema gives here and in siman 250 for treating "give" as "acquire" is precisely that the giver has already handed the money out of his own possession.
And if he recovered, he may retract. See Shach seif katan 39 through his words concerning a shechiv mera who gives a complete gift, such as all his property. His intent is that the Mechaber here is speaking of a gift of all the giver's property, as explained above, because the Mechaber would certainly not disagree with the Tur and with the consensus cited by Rivash. Therefore Shach writes that in the case addressed by the Mechaber even Rashba agrees that if the giver recovers, he may retract. See Taz, who explains that only recovery permits retraction, but while the giver remains ill he cannot retract. Although in general a gift that is revocable upon recovery is revocable because its validity rests on the special power of a shechiv mera, here the strengthening of the gift comes from the fact that the giver actually handed the money to the person acquiring for the recipient. See Sema in siman 250 seif katan 74, who writes that even if the messenger already delivered the money to the recipient at the shechiv mera's instruction, if the giver later recovers he may retract, but while he remains ill he cannot. The Perishah there adds that even if the shechiv mera himself delivered it, he may retract upon recovery.
