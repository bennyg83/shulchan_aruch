Even if, etc. Since the Gemara states that both cases concern a healthy person, it follows that under the first resolution there is no distinction.
And even if they were born, etc. Tosafot explains that although the law is that one who transfers property to a fetus does not effect acquisition, the words of a person on his deathbed are different. An inheritance that comes automatically is also effective. Rava does not dispute Abaye, as Rosh explains in chapter Almanah LeKohen Gadol. See there.
And the giver may not, etc.; and if he recovered, etc. Ran asks why the recipient's heirs acquire when the recipient died during the giver's lifetime, since a deathbed gift takes effect only after death and the recipient was then no longer alive. Rashba answers that with a person on his deathbed, 'take it' is equivalent to 'acquire it.' Ramah rejects this because the Gemara states that all agree 'take it' is not equivalent to 'acquire it,' and distinguishes only between a healthy person and one on his deathbed. Rather, it is as though he said, 'Acquire it from now if I die.' His urgency supplies the omitted wording, just as an instruction to write is treated as though he said, 'Write and deliver.' It cannot mean immediate acquisition, for then the Gemara should have said that 'take it' itself is equivalent to 'acquire it.' See Rivash 346.
