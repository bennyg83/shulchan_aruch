(Seif 9) As long as he has not recovered. The reason a deathly ill donor cannot retract while ill but may retract if he recovers is that the case concerns a deathbed disposition, which may be revoked upon recovery even if it was only a partial gift, as stated in siman 250, and even if accompanied by a formal acquisition. Therefore he cannot retract while still ill. It remains difficult, because in Mi SheMet, page 151, the rule is that any gift revocable upon recovery is also revocable during the illness, implying exact equivalence. The answer is that the general rule concerns the special force of a deathbed gift, whose words are treated as delivered. Here, however, the gift's added force arises from delivery of the money to the recipient, as the Tur expressly writes in the name of Ramah: regarding a deathly ill person, “take” is like “acquire” even for a gift, unlike a healthy person. Once he recovers, the special law of a deathbed donor no longer applies, and “take” is no longer like “acquire” for a gift. I have discussed this further in siman 249.
