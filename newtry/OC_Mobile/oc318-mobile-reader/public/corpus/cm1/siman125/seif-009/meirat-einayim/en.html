Return to the sender. Derry did not say but to give him permission to accept himself and as such he did not say any of his fees:
Even if the heirs were born afterward, etc. The Tur writes that this applies even if they were born only afterward, so that at the giver's death they were not yet capable of acquiring according to the opinion that one who grants property to a fetus does not effect an acquisition, a subject explained below in siman 210. Nor did the original recipient acquire during his lifetime, since he died during the giver's lifetime. Nevertheless, the heirs acquire, because the words of a person on his deathbed are treated as written and delivered retroactively from the time the property was handed over. Since the recipient was alive when the money was first delivered, it is clarified retroactively that he acquired it from the time of the gift and transmitted it to his heirs.
And the giver cannot retract. The Tur writes that this differs from other gifts of a person on his deathbed, where the giver may retract during his illness and thereby cancel the will even if he later dies, as explained below in siman 250. There the donor does not remove the property from his own possession at the time of the directive, and therefore the recipient does not acquire until after death. Here, by contrast, since the donor delivered the property into the agent's hand, his intention was clearly that the recipient acquire immediately through the agent's act of taking possession. We therefore say that this was his intention. Although he used the expression 'take it' rather than 'acquire it for him,' that is because a dying person is agitated by the approach of death. This is comparable to the rule concerning the get of a person on his deathbed, where if he says, Write a get for my wife, without expressly adding, Give it to her, the get is nevertheless written and delivered, because his failure to say 'give' is attributed to his agitation.
