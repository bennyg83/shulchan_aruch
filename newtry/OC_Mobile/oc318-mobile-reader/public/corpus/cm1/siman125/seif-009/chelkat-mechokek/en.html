And some are lenient in pressing circumstances to repair before, etc. The reason is that if witnesses signed an invalid get, what benefit is there in repairing it after it was signed invalidly? This applies specifically where the husband instructs that it be repaired. Without the husband's instruction, who has authority to repair a get other than the scribe, who is the husband's agent? The Rama's language here implies that after signing, or where the husband did not instruct repair, the get is invalid even in a case of agunah. But the Rosh writes in a responsum, rule 45, that touching letters are invalid in our lands, etc.; nevertheless, in a case of agunah, where the get came from a distant country and caravans are not available to bring another, he would not invalidate it. This sounds as though he would validate it even after signing and even where the husband is absent. His wording may imply that he would direct that it be delivered without repair, or perhaps he would order repair merely for added certainty. In truth, since all the other authorities validate such a get, Tosafot write plainly that no proof is even required to validate it, the plain Gemara supports that view, Rivash says so in responsum 56, and no authority is found invalidating it other than the Raavyah, whose words can be understood as applying outside a pressing case of agunah, it is clear that one should validate the get in pressing circumstances and in a case of agunah. This is subject to the condition that the letter remains recognizable to an ordinary child who is neither exceptionally clever nor foolish. If not, the get is certainly invalid, and repair does not help even by the husband's instruction because that would constitute engraving the surroundings. The Bach distinguishes that if the letters are joined through their entire height, scraping is forbidden because it is engraving the surroundings. In my view, as stated, everything depends on whether the letter can still be read by an ordinary child. There is no distinction between a slight touching and contact along the full height.
