This rule applies with a deposit, etc. But with a loan, since money lent is given for expenditure, and from the outset the lender lent to Reuven in order to accommodate him in every respect, it is a benefit for Shimon that the money leave Reuven's possession and come intact into Shimon's hands. This is unlike a deposit, which remains intact even while in Reuven's possession, and Shimon deposited it with him for his own benefit. Therefore, so long as Reuven has not subsequently denied the deposit, Shimon gains no particular benefit merely from having it returned to his own hand, and the depositor may therefore retract. See the Perishah.
He can go back and play. According to Dr. Delet, I wrote in his hand that he would return to Totlan or not, and that he would not be held to the priest, and that he would not be entrusted with his hand, and that he would not be obliged to return to his hand, and that he would be taken away from the messenger, and we would be a trusted messenger:
And even where he merely indicated an agent to him. Meaning that he said: I have money in the hands of so and so. Go tell him that you intend to come here, and if he gives it to you, bring it to me. The Tur and Mechaber explain this formulation at the beginning of siman 121, seif 2. See there. See also Hagahot Ashri at the beginning of Gittin concerning Reuven, who sent a maneh to Shimon for safekeeping, and Shimon returned it through that same messenger, after which it was lost through unavoidable circumstances on the way.
If he seeks to retract, he cannot retract. In the Perishah I cited the Maggid Mishneh's reason: Since the owner is accustomed to entrust property to this second person, the first custodian can no longer retract, because he has already been discharged from responsibility. It appears that even if the first custodian wishes to take the item back from the second person and assume responsibility anew, Shimon has no claim against Reuven, since Reuven returned the deposit to a person with whom Shimon is accustomed to deposit property. This also explains the continuation, which says that even if the owner did not presently appoint him as an agent, but the messenger is trusted by him, the first custodian still cannot retract, even though the messenger was not actually appointed this time and the first custodian is prepared to accept responsibility anew. See the Perishah.
