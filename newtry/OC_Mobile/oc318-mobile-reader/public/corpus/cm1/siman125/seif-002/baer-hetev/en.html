Regarding a deposit. A loan is given for expenditure, and the lender initially lent to Reuven only to satisfy his request. Therefore it is always a benefit to Shimon that the money leave Reuven's hands and come into Shimon's hands as actual money. A deposit differs. Even while in Reuven's hands the deposited property remains intact, and Shimon entrusted it to him for his own benefit. Therefore, so long as Reuven has not subsequently become a known denier, Shimon gains no legal benefit merely from having it returned through another, and the depositor may retract. Sema.
To retract. The bailee may choose whether to retract and take the deposit back from the agent. We do not say that because the bailee was not established as a denier, the depositor prefers the deposit to remain with him rather than with another, forcing the bailee to take it back. Presumably the depositor never intended that the bailee keep it forever, but that when the time came he would return it through a trustworthy agent. So writes the Sema. The Shach says he does not see the practical difference, because in any event the property remains the bailee's responsibility until it reaches the depositor, as stated in seif 4. Perhaps the Sema only means that using the agent is not regarded as acting contrary to the owner's instructions. See the Taz.
Is exempt. As above in siman 121, seif 2. See Hagahot Ashri at the beginning of Gittin concerning Reuven who sent one maneh to Shimon for safekeeping and Shimon returned it through the same messenger, after which it was lost on the way. Sema.
Ordinarily. Although the depositor did not formally appoint him as agent, if this person is ordinarily trusted by the depositor in the circumstances explained below in siman 291, seif 26, the bailee may send through him. This remains so even if the bailee later wishes to retake the property and accept responsibility himself. So write the Sema and Bach. See also the end of siman 293. Shach. The Taz explains that the novelty is that the depositor need not presently demonstrate trust in him. It is enough that on other occasions he was regarded as trustworthy.
