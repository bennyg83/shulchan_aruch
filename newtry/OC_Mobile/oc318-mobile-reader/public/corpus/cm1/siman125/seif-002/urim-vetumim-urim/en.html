Regarding a deposit, etc. With a loan, which is given to be spent, the lender originally lent the money to Reuven only so that Reuven could satisfy his own needs in any manner, and therefore it is a benefit to Shimon for the actual money eventually to come into his possession. By contrast, with a deposit, the object remains intact even while in Reuven's possession, and Shimon deposited it with him for his own benefit. Therefore, so long as Reuven did not later become an established denier, there is no particular benefit in returning it, and the sender may retract. See Perishah and Sema; the Shach writes likewise, correctly.
He may retract. The choice lies with the custodian. We do not say that he is obligated to take the deposit back, because presumably at the outset he never contemplated guarding it forever. See Sema. The Shach writes that by doing so he is not considered to have violated the owner's instructions.
To retract and take it back, etc. This implies that even if the messenger accepts all responsibility, nevertheless, so long as the custodian has not become an established denier, the original custodian may retract and take it back. The Bach writes that the Rambam implies that if the messenger expressly accepts all responsibility, even for unavoidable accidents, the original custodian cannot retract. The Shach, seif katan 15, disagrees and writes that there is no proof of this from the Rambam. See what I wrote below in siman 291, which is the proper place for this law.
Even where he merely provided him with a messenger. Meaning, the owner said, 'I have money in the possession of so and so. Go and tell him that you intend to come here, and if he gives it to you, bring it to me.' This is the language of the Tur and Mechaber above in siman 121, seif 2. See Sema and what I wrote above. See also Hagahot Ashri, first chapter of Gittin, concerning Reuven who sent a maneh to Shimon for safekeeping, and Shimon returned it through that same messenger, and it was lost by unavoidable accident on the road.
If he comes to retract, etc. The Maggid Mishneh writes that where the depositor ordinarily entrusts deposits to this messenger, the original custodian cannot retract, because he has already been released from responsibility. See Derishah, Perishah, and Sema, seif katan 15. See Tumim, seif katan 5, where I discussed at length the difficulty on the Maggid Mishneh from the first chapter of Gittin, where Rav Dositai and Rabbi Yose were told to bring a deposit. I explained that the Maggid Mishneh's case may be where the deposit was handed to the messenger to bring to the depositor within that same city, without a journey, whereas the Gemara deals with carriage along a road. Nevertheless, I concluded that the Rambam must in fact be speaking even of a messenger carrying it along the road. See there.
