Because the agent is believed. See Sema seif katan 15 through his statement that Shimon nevertheless has no further claim or litigation with Reuven. It seems to me that Maggid Mishneh follows his own position in siman 291 seif 24, that where the first guardian transfers the item to a person with whom the owner regularly deposits, the first guardian is exempt even if the second guardian was negligent and cannot pay. It therefore makes sense for him to say that Shimon has no further claim against Reuven, since the transfer counts as a complete return and can no longer be retracted. But according to the opinion there that the first guardian remains liable, the owner still has a claim against the first guardian, and the first guardian may certainly retract. The depositor has gained no greater security by the item being in the second guardian's hands than in the first's; indeed, it may be somewhat worse, because the first guardian had an obligation to guard it. It therefore cannot be said that the depositor has already acquired an irrevocable right in the transfer.
