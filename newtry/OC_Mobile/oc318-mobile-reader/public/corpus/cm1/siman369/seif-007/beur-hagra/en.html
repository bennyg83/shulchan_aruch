Likewise a king, etc. This is unlike the Ramah, who writes that a king cannot institute a new law even if it applies generally. See Tur. Ramban and Terumas HaDeshen follow the Ramah, based on the passage in Bava Batra concerning arbitrary seizure; see Hagahos Ashri and the responsa and Hagahos Maimoniyos cited there.
Whether it was, etc. Although the Gemara elsewhere qualifies the rule where a partner is involved, and Mordechai and other poskim cite that limitation, Rambam understands the Gemara to describe the royal law that existed in those times. See seif 9 regarding tax, unless it was, etc.; Lechem Mishneh.
And a Jew, etc. Rashi explains it this way as well.
Provided that, etc. As above, because a customs officer who has no fixed rate is treated differently, and similarly an ordinary non-Jewish customs officer according to Rambam.
And he shall not deviate. There, where the Gemara says, “But they do not do so,” etc.
And not, etc. As above concerning a non-Jewish customs officer.
