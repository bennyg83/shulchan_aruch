[Seif 11] One who marries a woman. The Sema's question from the beginning of siman 248, and his answer, are very difficult. According to his reasoning, even where there is a special Jewish custom in the city where the marriage took place, different from other places, such as the difference between the currency of Eretz Yisrael and other countries concerning payment of the ketubah, we should not follow the place of marriage because at the time of marriage he did not contemplate the time of payment. Yet the Mishnah explicitly teaches that one who married in Cappadocia pays her in Cappadocian currency. His explanation of siman 248 is also difficult. He says that the words “and after him to so-and-so” are interpreted literally even if the first recipient is a lawful heir. Yet in Bava Batra 133b Rava asked Reish Lakish that the donor had interrupted the inheritance by conditioning that the second person inherit after the first, and the Gemara answers that although the donor thought inheritance could be interrupted, the Torah says it cannot, so the condition contradicts Torah law and is void. Thus, whenever one says “and after him,” his intent is indeed to terminate the first recipient's right, but the condition may be legally ineffective. What, then, is accomplished here by the fact that the command was made in a place where the community judged under gentile law? The Rivash's ruling is otherwise, as I copied in siman 248: he did not remove this case from Jewish law merely because of local practice. It appears that the Sema did not examine the responsum carefully. Nor can support be drawn from the Rashba's responsum cited by the Beit Yosef at the end of siman 26, which is the source of the Rama's gloss here. There the Rashba says that one must distance oneself from gentile courts and that Jews may not apply gentile law in such a case, for this is not called law of the kingdom. I have already noted in siman 248 what is difficult in the Rama's presentation of the Rivash.
