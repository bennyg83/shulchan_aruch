Because the king decrees that they may not live in his land. The reason is that the land is regarded as specifically belonging to the king, and every person may regulate use of his own property as he wishes. The Rosh, in Ramah's name, concludes similarly: even a private person who owns land may say that no one may benefit from his land without his consent and according to the terms he fixes. Darkei Moshe cites this. See there.
He may sell it after one year, etc. This was also written above in siman 73, seif 14, in the gloss, based on the Mishnah in chapter HaGozel. It was necessary to explain that under dina d'malchusa he may sell it after one year. One might otherwise think that since the collateral belongs to a non-Jew and the lender receives interest from him, it would be prohibited to sell the pledge and expose the pledgor to possible harm from the non-Jewish authorities, who may lodge a claim against him. This differs from collateral belonging to a Jew, which may be sold after thirty days following warning, unless local practice does not permit sale of non-Jewish pledges until one year, as Rama explains above in siman 73, seif 14. For that reason he wrote there that it may not be sold within the year, implying that one might otherwise have thought sale within the year permitted, while here he needed to teach positively that after a year it may be sold. This is straightforward.
