If he legislated, etc. This requires study in light of seif 6 in the Rama, “even if he commanded,” etc. Some say that he may not, etc. Rashbam in Bava Batra and other poskim write this. Their proof is from Sanhedrin, where the authority described in the section of the king is limited to the powers written there, and from Bava Batra, where “dina d'malchusa” functions as a general rule followed by examples of Persian royal law. Thus it applies to comparable established laws, not every personal decree. Because the king, etc. For this reason this doctrine did not operate in the same way for the kings of Israel, since Eretz Yisrael belongs collectively to the Jewish people. Others disagree, etc.; therefore, etc. Mordechai brings a proof from the first chapter of Gittin. Their disagreement appears connected to whether the latter version of that sugya disputes the first. Ran also indicates this. Maharik, however, construes the first view narrowly so that it can agree with the first version in Gittin, explaining it as dealing with land documents. The Rosh in Nedarim also follows the first view while accepting the first version in Gittin, so the matter still requires study.
