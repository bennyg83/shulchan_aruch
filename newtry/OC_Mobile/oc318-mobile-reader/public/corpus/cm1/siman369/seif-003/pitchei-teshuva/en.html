One who marries a woman in a place, etc. See Baer Heitev and Chatam Sofer, Choshen Mishpat, siman 142. A husband gave a house to his wife and registered it in her name in the secular courts. When she died, she had made a secular will excluding her husband from inheritance and distributing her property to her own heirs. The question was whether the will was valid. The questioner advanced three reasons to uphold it: first, the husband himself had registered the house in her name in the secular system and therefore arguably relied upon that legal system; second, the woman's heirs were already in possession because the secular registrar had entered the assets in their names; third, local law gave the sovereign a share where a person died without children, which might give the sovereign a direct interest and make the local inheritance rules binding under dina demalchuta, reducing the husband's rights to those of an ordinary heir. Chatam Sofer rejects all three arguments at length. None is sufficient to displace Torah inheritance law, and the husband retains his proper share under halachah.
