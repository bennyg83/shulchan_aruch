The field shall belong to the one who pays the tax. In their time the body of the fields belonged to the king, and Jews and gentiles would customarily take possession of them, plow and sow them, and pay the king a fixed annual tax. It is about such a case that the ruling says: if the king's law is that one who does not pay the tax loses the field and another who pays it may take it, this is not robbery. Certainly, if the body of the field actually belonged privately to a Jew, then even if he owes a fixed tax to the king, the king cannot confiscate all of its produce merely because the tax was not paid unless that broader power is itself part of the established royal law.
