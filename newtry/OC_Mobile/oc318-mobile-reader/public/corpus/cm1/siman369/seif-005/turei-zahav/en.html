[Seif 7] Whether or not he owns the field. The Sema explains that he must nevertheless be among those who own fields in that valley. It appears to me, however, that the Rambam speaks where the king had already proclaimed that anyone passing through the threshing floor would be required to pay everything due to the king there. This is valid law of the kingdom, because the person knew of the decree and disregarded it, thereby accepting it willingly. The Tur's distinction between a sharecropper and another person applies where the king gave no prior notice, but at the time of collection exacts the entire amount from whoever is found at the threshing floor. There one distinguishes between a partner and a sharecropper. Maharshal writes that the sharecropper may not complain to the king against the others, since he thereby causes them loss, and even if he did so and collected from them, he must return the money, because his own misfortune caused the unlawful collection from him. Nevertheless, it appears to me that if he saved himself by directing the royal collector to one of the people of the valley, and was thereby released, he need not pay. So long as his intention was to save himself and not to cause harm, he is not treated as an informer. Regarding payment, he may truthfully say: I pointed out persons who were legally liable. Although one may evade a gentile creditor's claim, the money was not taken from you unlawfully, as I wrote in siman 28 concerning a Jewish witness testifying for a gentile.
