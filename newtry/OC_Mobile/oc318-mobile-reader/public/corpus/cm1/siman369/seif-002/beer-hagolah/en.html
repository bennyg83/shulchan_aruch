gimel) Ibid., law 2. The Maggid Mishneh writes that this is straightforward, because despair alone does not acquire, as written in chapter 1 of Hilkhot Geneivah and above in siman 353. In Sukkah 30b, and in Bava Kamma 113b regarding the law of the kingdom, the Gemara says that perhaps the owners despaired. There, however, there is both despair and a change of possession, because the property was transferred to the public. Whenever there is despair together with a change of possession, one may benefit from the property.
dalet) The Maggid Mishneh writes that it is straightforward that land cannot be stolen, as stated there on 117b, following the Sages.
heh) Explicit there in the second chapter, 51a, and explained above in siman 363, seif 6.
vav) The Maggid Mishneh writes that this is explained in the latter chapter HaGozel, 113a. Although a change in the object itself can acquire, this is a reversible change, as written in chapter 2 and above in siman 360, seif 8.
zayin) Ibid., law 16, explained in the latter chapter HaGozel.
chet) Tur, seif 9, from the Gemara there, which explains that the king's agent is like the king, and that the owners caused their own loss because the agent need not trouble himself; they should have collected proportionately from all the fields and taken payment. So appears in the Rambam.
tet) Ibid., law 18. The Maggid Mishneh writes that this is explained.
