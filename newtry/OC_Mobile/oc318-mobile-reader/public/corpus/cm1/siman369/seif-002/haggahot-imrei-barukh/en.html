(Mechaber, seif 6) But if a gentile acquired it, it is permitted. Note: Beit Yosef writes that the Tur derived this rule from Nedarim 28, where Rav Ashi says that the case concerns a gentile tax collector. The Beit Yosef's words are surprising, because Rav Ashi's answer does not appear in Nedarim but in Bava Kamma 113. Raavad there, as cited in Shitah Mekubetzet, understands Rav Ashi's answer to resolve the mishnah in Nedarim as well. According to Rabbeinu Tam, one may vow falsely to a gentile tax collector even when he rents the tax right from the king and has a fixed rate. Rashba, however, in his novellae cited there, disagrees. Although evading a debt owed to a gentile may be permitted, one cannot permit a false vow merely on the basis of unspoken intent where the gentile is not actually trying to rob him. Rashba supports this by noting that Nedarim does not cite Rav Ashi's answer, and therefore Rav Ashi's statement may refer only to the baraita concerning kilayim. Ran appears to agree with Rashba. Ran, and Beit Yosef citing him later, hold that evading a debt may be permitted even against a king, yet Ran still rules explicitly that one may not vow falsely against a Jew who stands to collect the king's tax. This likewise appears to be the Shulchan Aruch's view in Yoreh Deah 232, where the permission to vow against coercive tax collectors is mentioned only when the collector takes more than the fixed rate or acts on his own authority. Know that Yam Shel Shlomo, Bava Kamma, chapter 10, ruling 18, first explains simply that Rav Ashi's answer applies also to the mishnah in Nedarim, but then cites the Ran and agrees with him. At first glance these are contradictory approaches and require study.
