It is forbidden, etc. See Beer HaGolah and Bava Kamma 114a, where the baraisa says, etc.; this is the basis of the statement that it is forbidden to assist him, etc. There 119a the Gemara discusses a robber, etc. See what I wrote at the beginning of siman 356. And one who purchases from him, etc. This concerns the market enactment and a notorious thief. It rejects the view that an ordinary robber is automatically treated as notorious. The proof is from Rabbi Chiya's statement that if one robbed and the owners did not despair and another person came, etc.; if every robber were deemed notorious, the market enactment would never apply. Rashba cites an alternative explanation: there can still be practical differences for a gift, where the market enactment was not made, for a sale far below value, where the excess is returned, or for transactions such as payment of a debt in which the enactment does not apply.
