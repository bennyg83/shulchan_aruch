From the thief. If he is a notorious robber, the purchaser must return the object to the owners and then seek reimbursement from the robber. If he is not notorious, the market regulation applies: the purchaser receives the money he paid from the robbed owner and returns the stolen object, and the robbed owner then seeks recovery from the robber. So writes the Tur. The Rambam writes likewise in chapter 8 of Hilchot Gezeilah, the Maggid Mishneh rules so there, Rabbeinu Yerucham, netiv 31, part 4, writes likewise, as does the Raah in Sefer HaChinuch, mitzvah 236. This also appears expressly from a responsum of Maharam in Mordechai at the end of HaSefinah. Baal HaTerumot, gate 49, part 13, likewise rules this way and writes that the Raavad did so as well. This is the primary ruling, unlike the Beit Yosef in siman 356 in the name of the Rashba, and the Rivash at the end of siman 393 in the name of some authorities, who hold that the market regulation does not apply to a robber even if he is not notorious, because the robbery becomes publicly known and thus resembles a notorious thief. Shach.
