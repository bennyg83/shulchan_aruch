There, law 16, in Bava Metzia and Yevamot chapter 4, 46a.
Kesef Mishneh writes that the reason is as follows. We rule that one may not employ a Hebrew servant in tasks specifically associated with slaves, such as carrying his master's garments after him to the bathhouse or removing his shoes, because Scripture says, 'You shall not work him with slave labor.' That applies to a Hebrew servant whose spirit is humbled because he was sold. But an ordinary Jew who was not sold may be served in the manner of a slave, as explained in chapter 5 of Hilkhot Avadim and Yoreh Deah siman 267. Rambam teaches here that these people, since by the law of the kingdom they are subjected to service and the king pays their head tax, are considered as though sold and have the law of Hebrew servants. See Maharik, roots 1, 66, 195, and 188, Mordechai to HaGozel U'Ma'akhil for many details of dina d'malkhuta dina, and Rivash siman 179.
