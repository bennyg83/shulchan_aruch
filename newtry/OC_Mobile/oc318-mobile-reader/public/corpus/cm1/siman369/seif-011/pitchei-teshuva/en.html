One who marries a woman in a place, etc. See Baer Heitev. See also Responsa Chatam Sofer, Choshen Mishpat, siman 142, concerning a husband who gave a house to his wife and registered it in her name in the non-Jewish courts. When she died, she made a will in those courts excluding her husband from her inheritance and dividing her property among her heirs. The question was whether the will is valid. The questioner sought to uphold the will and distinguished it from the Rashba cited by the Beit Yosef at the end of siman 26 and the Rama at the end of siman 369 on three grounds. First, since the husband himself registered the house in her name in the non-Jewish courts, he thereby relied upon their legal system in all its rules. Second, the wife's heirs were already in possession, since the governmental official had entered the property in his registry in the names of her heirs. Third, under the law of that country, when a person dies without children the sovereign takes a share equal to that of one heir. Thus the sovereign has an interest in the matter, and dina de-malkhuta dina establishes that the husband does not inherit his wife but takes only a share like the other heirs. Here, since she made a will excluding him entirely, her will should therefore stand under the law of the kingdom. Chatam Sofer discusses the matter at length and rejects all three arguments, holding that none has substance and that the husband's rightful share must remain intact in accordance with the laws and judgments of the Torah.
