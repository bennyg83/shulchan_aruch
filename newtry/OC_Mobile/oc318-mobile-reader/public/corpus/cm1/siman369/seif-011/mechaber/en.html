If a king decrees that one who pays another person's fixed poll tax may compel the nonpayer to work for him, a Jew who pays the tax of an impoverished Jew may employ him beyond ordinary measure, because the law of the kingdom is law, but may not treat him as a slave. Rama: If a Jew owes a non-Jew and the non-Jew sells the note to another Jew, Jewish judges award the purchaser whatever the non-Jew could have recovered in the royal courts, because he derives his right from the non-Jew under the law of the kingdom. But if one marries in a place governed by non-Jewish law and his wife dies, her father or heirs cannot demand that inheritance follow local non-Jewish law on the claim that marriage follows custom. The law of the kingdom applies to matters benefiting the king or regulating society, not to replacing Torah law with non-Jewish law, for that would nullify all Jewish civil law.
