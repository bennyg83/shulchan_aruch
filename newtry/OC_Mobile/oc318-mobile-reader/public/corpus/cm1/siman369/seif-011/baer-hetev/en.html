The topic. The Sema challenges this from what is written at the beginning of siman 248, that if a person made a testament in a place where the custom is to adjudicate in secular courts, and so forth. See there for his resolution, distinguishing that case from the present one.
That they adjudicate. It appears that even if the ruler expressly decreed that people must follow the rules of the secular courts, Jewish judges should not decide the case on that basis through the principle that the law of the kingdom is law. That principle was not stated regarding such matters, but regarding laws that are part of the governance of the kingdom. Likewise, when the Rama writes in seif 8 that some disagree and hold that the principle applies 'in every matter,' he means even matters other than taxes and royal levies, but the matter must at least concern the governmental laws of kings. This is also the position of Maharik; see there. Sema. See also what I wrote in the Shach's name at the end of siman 74.
