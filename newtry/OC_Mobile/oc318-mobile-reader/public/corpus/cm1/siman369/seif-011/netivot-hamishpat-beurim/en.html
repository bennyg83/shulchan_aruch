And the non Jew sold the document. See Shach, seif katan 9, who cites Maharam that the same applies if the non Jew gave it as a gift and there are witnesses that he gave it. I find this surprising. Why is evidence required? We rule in siman 66, seif 25, that a non Jew who sells a Jewish promissory note does not require a written assignment and the note is acquired by delivery alone. See what I wrote there in siman 66: because this is the law of the kingdom, the note is treated as though written payable to whoever presents it. We rule like Rava in Bava Batra that according to the opinion that documentary rights are acquired by delivery, the holder need not bring proof of transfer because we do not suspect that the document reached him through loss or deposit. If so, even without witnesses the recipient should be able to collect.
For if she dies, her husband does not inherit her. See Sema, seif katan 6, who gives a reason for this. His reason is needed only according to those who hold that a stipulation can validly prevent a husband from inheriting his wife. Under that view, an established custom has the effect of an implied stipulation, so the Sema must explain why the custom controls. According to the authority cited by Beis Shmuel there that such a stipulation is ineffective, there is no difficulty in the first place.
