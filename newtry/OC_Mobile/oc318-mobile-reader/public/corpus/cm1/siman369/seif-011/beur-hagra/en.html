More than, etc. Bava Metzia 73b; see Tosafos there and Beer HaGolah. The passage implies that the debtor's body is treated like that of a servant, as also suggested in Yevamos, which speaks of bodies being acquired and requiring a writ of emancipation.
A Jew, etc. This view applies the relevant rule concerning a non-Jew also where it benefits the Jewish litigant. See siman 66. The rationale is that the possessor can say, “If you remain silent,” etc.; the opposing party cannot insist that the property first be formally returned, because we view it as though it had already been returned, as explained in Bava Kamma and the final chapter of Kesubos. See Tosafos in Kesubos there; this is also the Rosh's position.
