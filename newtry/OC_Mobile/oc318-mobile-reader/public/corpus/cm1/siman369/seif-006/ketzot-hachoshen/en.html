It is forbidden to evade even the fixed tax. Beit Yosef writes that although the tax collector unlawfully takes more than the fixed rate, one may evade only the excess and must still pay the lawful fixed amount. In my view the Tur in Yoreh Deah indicates otherwise. He permits one to swear falsely to escape a tax collector who takes more than the king's fixed rate. Rosh explains that a collector who exceeds the royal rate is acting wholly outside the law of the kingdom. Since the collector's authority exists only by the king's law, once he exceeds that law his entire collection is treated as robbery. Therefore, where the collector comes to take more than the lawful fixed amount, the person may evade him entirely. The collector is like one who appointed himself unlawfully. This is the logic behind Tur's wording and the Shulchan Aruch in Yoreh Deah, which permits evasion where the collector seeks more than his fixed amount.
