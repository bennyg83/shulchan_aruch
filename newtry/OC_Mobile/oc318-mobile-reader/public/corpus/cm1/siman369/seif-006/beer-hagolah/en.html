tzadi) Ibid., law 11. The Maggid Mishneh writes that in the Rambam's view, where the tax collector is a gentile, even if appointed by the king, he is presumed to be like a tax collector who has no fixed assessment, because such a collector ordinarily takes more than the lawful amount. The Rambam explains the baraita and Rabbi Yoshiyah's interpretation on Bava Kamma 113a as referring to a gentile tax collector, and he concludes that this applies even to the Mishnah and does not conflict with the other explanations. Other commentators do not explain it this way.
kuf) The Gemara there asks: is it permitted to evade a tax, when Shmuel said that the law of the kingdom is law?
reish) So appears in the Rambam and elsewhere.
shin) Tur, seif 8. The Rosh writes this there, and the Ran in the third chapter of Nedarim 28a likewise writes it in the name of Tosafot.
tav) Ibid., as the Gemara in Bava Kamma 113b concludes. Rashi explains that evading a gentile's loan, where there is no actual robbery, is permitted when there is no desecration of Hashem's Name, such as where one says, 'I paid your father,' and the father has died, so the gentile does not know that he is lying. The same principle applies to tax. The Maggid Mishneh writes that since the king does not lose through this, and if he knew he would not object, and since danger to life may be involved, evading the tax is permitted as an evasion of a debt.
a) See there, where he writes that one may not swear falsely that the goods are terumah, because there is no coercion when the law of the kingdom is lawful.
