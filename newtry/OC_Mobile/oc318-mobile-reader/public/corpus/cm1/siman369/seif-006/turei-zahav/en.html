[Seif 10] The field belongs to the one who pays the tax. The Sema limits this to former times, but I do not know his source. The language of the Gemara does not imply this, for it says that land is pledged for the land tax, which indicates all land, even privately owned land. Reason also supports this, since all land belongs ultimately to the king, who may take it from one who does not pay its tax. Moreover, if the rule applied only to royal land, the Gemara and seif 9 should have distinguished within land tax itself between royal land and privately owned land, rather than distinguishing between a tax on fields and a personal tax. This requires study.
