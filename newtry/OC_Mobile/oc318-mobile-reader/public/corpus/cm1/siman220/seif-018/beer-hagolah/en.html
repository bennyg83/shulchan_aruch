John Spiech, who sold everything, also sold your return
The Mishnah there at the beginning of 80a, as taught there, and so writes Rambam, chapter 23, law 13.
The language of Rambam there, law 11. The Maggid Mishneh writes that his words follow his teacher Ibn Migash in explaining the baraita. The Gemara asks why he leaves two broods from those born to the daughters and one brood from the mothers. It answers that each brood needs two of its descendants for companionship, otherwise it will leave. The mothers already have daughters, and the single brood that he leaves provides two descendants for companionship. The Beit Yosef writes that this appears to be the Ran's explanation and also seems to be the Rif's meaning. The Maggid Mishneh writes that according to this explanation, if at the time of purchase there was only one brood in the dovecote, he leaves two broods from its descendants, and that suffices because it then has companionship.
Rambam writes in law 13 that one who purchases honeycombs leaves two combs in the hive so that the bees will not fly away. The Maggid Mishneh writes that this too is in the Mishnah there. He explains that bees build perforated combs in the hive, reproduce in those cells, and when the young mature and fly out the bees fill the cells with honey; they also consume the honey and raise bees through it. I likewise do not know why the Mechaber and Rama omitted this law.
