Some say the converse also applies: one who sells the pigeons sells the dovecote, and one who sells the bees sells the hive. This applies only if he expressly sells all the produce of the dovecote or hive without retaining anything. If he merely sells the produce of the hive, not only does he not sell the hive, he does not even sell all the bees. Rather, the purchaser takes the first three swarms meaning the first three groups born, since at the beginning of summer a swarm of young bees leaves the hive, a new hive is brought and they are placed in it, and similarly again after nine or ten days, and so forth. Rashbam. Thereafter he takes one swarm and leaves the next, so that the hive will remain populated. If he merely sells the produce of a dovecote, he may not take every young pigeon born from then onward, because the mothers would flee and the dovecote would be ruined. He must leave enough to maintain the dovecote. How much must he leave? If the dovecote already contained mothers and daughters when the produce was sold, he leaves the first brood pigeons normally produce two young each month, one male and one female, and each pair of young is called a brood. born to the mothers, so that the mothers will remain attached to that first brood and to the daughters already with them. From the daughters' offspring he leaves two broods so that the daughters will remain attached to those two broods. Everything born thereafter, beyond the daughters' two broods and the mothers' first brood, belongs to the purchaser. Rama: In all these matters there is no distinction between a sale and a gift. Although there is a distinction regarding things attached to the ground, as explained above in siman 215, seif 6, with movable property there is no distinction. Tur in the name of Rashbam.
