The mast. The Tur explains: the tall pole upon which the sail is spread.
And the nes. This is the sail spread upon the mast.
And the anchors. These are the iron implements used to hold the ship in place, called anchors in the vernacular. They are called ognin from the verse "would you remain bound," meaning tied or anchored.
And the gangway and the ladder by which they ascend, etc. This is Rambam's wording in chapter 26 of Hilchot Mechirah. The Tur, however, mentions only "the gangway by which they ascend" and does not mention a separate ladder. Maggid Mishneh writes that Rashbam's text has "ladder" rather than "gangway," and that the ladder is the gangway. This is also how Rashbam's commentary appears in our Gemara. In the Perishah I wrote that from these differences in wording, and from the Tur's statement that they ascend "to the land" whereas Rambam writes that they ascend "to the ship," we can infer their meaning. The ship's entrance is located high on its side so that water does not enter. Entering from land therefore involves ascending from the land to the opening and then descending from the opening into the floor of the ship where people sit. Exiting involves the reverse, first ascending inside the ship to the opening and then descending to land. The Tur describes the time of departure from the ship, when one first ascends to the side opening before going down to land. Rambam describes entry, when one first ascends from land in order to enter the ship. This may also explain why Rambam writes both "gangway" and "ladder": the external ramp used to ascend from land to the ship is called a gangway, while the fixed structure inside the ship near the opening, used to descend and ascend within it, is called a ladder in his terminology. Perhaps it had a different name because the interior one was fixed and attached whereas the exterior one was movable, or for some other reason. Although Maggid Mishneh cites Ibn Migash as explaining the "ladder" as the lavatory inside the ship, Rambam and the Mechaber evidently did not explain it that way. Otherwise the grammar and order would need to be reversed. Rambam also explicitly writes "by which they ascend," in the plural, showing that both items are for ascent. See there.
And the water compartment. Meaning, the ship contains a place where fresh drinking water is stored, since the sea water through which they travel is salty and undrinkable.
But he did not sell, etc. Although all these items are made for the ship's use, they are not themselves included under the name "ship" and can also be used for other purposes.
