(Seif 7; Tur, seif 9.) One who sells a donkey, etc. The Tur cites the Ramah's view here, and the matter requires explanation, for the Beit Yosef did not clarify it sufficiently. He also wrote that the distinction concerning an express statement “for riding” was mentioned unnecessarily. This does not appear correct to me, as I shall explain. I have also seen an interpretation of the Ramah's repeated phrase, “stringency for the claimant,” as meaning that his own uncertainty was whether superfluous wording, namely “for carrying,” has legal effect. This is wholly unacceptable. No decisor, when personally uncertain about a law, characterizes it as a stringency for the claimant, since what is uncertain to him may be clear to others. Moreover, “for carrying” is not superfluous wording at all, because we rule like the Sages that an unspecified donkey is presumed sold for riding. The correct explanation of the Ramah's words appears clear to me. The Mishna in the chapter HaSefina teaches: One who sells a donkey does not sell its implements; Nahum the Mede says that he does sell its implements. Ulla explained that the dispute concerns the sack, saddlebag, and female riding-seat. The first tanna holds that an unspecified donkey is intended for riding, while Nahum the Mede holds that it is intended for carrying. Regarding the saddle and pack-saddle, however, all agree that they are sold. The Gemara asks whether they disagree only while the implements are on the donkey, whereas when they are not on it Nahum agrees with the Sages, or whether they disagree only when they are not on it, whereas when they are on it the Sages agree with Nahum. The question was not resolved. The Ramah therefore rules that, because the question was unresolved, we follow the principle that the burden of proof rests upon one who seeks to extract. We treat Ulla's statement as saying that even while the sack and related implements are on the donkey, the first tanna holds they are not sold. It follows that Ulla's concluding statement, that all agree concerning the saddle, refers specifically to a saddle presently on the donkey. The Ramah therefore says that even according to the first tanna, a saddle is sold only when it is on the donkey, whereas a sack is not sold merely because it is on the donkey, since the Gemara's question was unresolved. The Ramah did not need to write there “a leniency for the possessor” or “a stringency for the claimant,” because that is the direct subject of the Gemara's unresolved question, and the burden of proof plainly rests on the claimant. He used that expression only in cases not expressly identified in the Gemara as part of the question, thereby teaching that they too are included in its uncertainty. Thus, when the Ramah writes that if the seller expressly said “for riding,” his statement teaches a significant novelty. Even if he also expressly said, “it and everything upon it,” that phrase does not include carrying implements, because by specifying “for riding” he revealed that “everything upon it” means everything connected with riding. The Beit Yosef was therefore incorrect to say that this distinction was unnecessary. The Ramah's next ruling, that if the seller expressly said “for carrying,” the sack is sold when it is on the donkey, is obvious, because the sack serves carrying and he expressly said “for carrying.” When it is not on the donkey, however, the matter falls within the Gemara's uncertainty. If Ulla's statement concerns implements presently on the donkey, we learn that according to the first tanna an unspecified donkey is intended for riding, which is equivalent to expressly saying “for riding,” yet even the saddle, which serves riding, is sold only while on the donkey. So too, one who says “for carrying” may not transfer a sack that is not on the donkey. This is therefore a stringency for the claimant. The Ramah then rules concerning a saddle that if it is not on the donkey it is certainly not sold, for two adverse factors are present: the seller expressly said “for carrying,” and the saddle is not on the donkey. If it is on the donkey, however, it enters the Gemara's uncertainty. If Ulla's statement concerns implements not on the donkey, while those on it are sold even according to the first tanna, then although a saddle is not associated with carrying, its presence on the donkey may cause it to be sold. This too is a stringency for the claimant. Finally, if the seller expressly said “both for riding and for carrying,” then while all the implements are on the donkey they are certainly sold, since two favorable factors are present. When they are not on it, however, the matter again falls within the unresolved question. Even according to the first tanna, an unspecified donkey is intended for riding, yet under the view that Ulla speaks specifically of implements on the donkey, a saddle is not sold when absent. The same applies to a sack even where the seller also said “for carrying.” This explanation is exceedingly clear, and only one who stubbornly denies evident truth could reject it.
