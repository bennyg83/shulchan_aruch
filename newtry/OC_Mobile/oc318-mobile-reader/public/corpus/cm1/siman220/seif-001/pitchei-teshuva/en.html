And an unspecified cow. See Baer Heitev, who explains this as a case where he said "this cow," and see Drishah, Bach, and Taz below, siman 223. Their words indicate that according to this explanation there is actually no dispute, and Rif and Rambam also agree with the Rosh. Taz proves this, because otherwise their position would contradict the Mishnah in Bava Metzia 100a concerning one who exchanges a cow for a donkey, where the parties dispute whether the calf was born before or after the sale. If the seller merely said "a cow," the fetus would not be included even if it was born later. If he said "a pregnant cow," the fetus would be included even if it had already been born. Pilpula Charifta to the Rosh, chapter HaSefinah, siman 9, likewise regards that Mishnah as a conclusive proof for the Rosh. Responsa Chatam Sofer, Choshen Mishpat, siman 105, however, writes that this is not correct. Rambam certainly disagrees, and presumably Rif does as well. Shitah Mekubetzet to Bava Metzia 100a, in the name of Ramach, cited also by Ketzot HaChoshen, explicitly disagrees and holds that even if the seller says "this cow," the fetus is not included unless he expressly says "a pregnant cow." The case of the Mishnah is then explained as follows: he did say "a pregnant cow," but the seller claims, "I sold you a cow that was pregnant, with the risk of childbirth resting upon you. Now that it had already given birth before meshichah, both the cow and calf remained mine, and I am prepared to deliver another pregnant cow to you so that you bear the risk of birth." See there. As for Ketzot HaChoshen's proof from Temurah 28 that according to the Rosh, even without saying "pregnant," once one says "this cow" or "this maidservant" the offspring is sold, Chatam Sofer writes that although it might seem forced to distinguish between consecration of the body and ordinary transactions, that distinction is correct and was already made by Pnei Yehoshua in Kiddushin 63 and by his teacher in Haflaah, Ketubot 11. He further writes that all of this applies where both parties knew that the cow was pregnant. But where neither intended anything other than a barren cow and it turned out to be pregnant, it is obvious that the offspring is not included. Even the Rosh would agree that the seller can say, "Had I known this, I would not have sold on that understanding."
