He sold the pair. The burdens that give the two bulls when preparing them together for a whisper or the rest of a burden are called a pair.
Where they call the yoke “cattle.” Maimonides in chapter 27 of Hilchot Mekhirah and Rama below in seif 8 have this wording. If it is not a scribal error, it must refer to the preceding clause, “If he sold the cattle, he did not sell the yoke,” meaning even in a place where the yoke itself is also called “cattle.” But in seif 8 Rama writes that if everyone calls the yoke “cattle” and when they mean only the yoke they specifically say “the yoke alone,” then everything is sold. That clearly treats the question as whether cattle are included when one sells the yoke. It therefore appears that the text here should be corrected to “where they call the yoke ‘for cattle,’” or, in the Tur, “where they call cattle ‘yoke.’” This then comments on “he sold the yoke but did not sell the cattle.” The gloss in seif 8 properly belongs here, for it explains that the Mechaber's statement, even where one calls cattle by the term yoke or conversely, applies only where not everyone uses that terminology. If everyone calls cattle “yoke,” then the price can indicate that cattle were included, and sometimes even without relying on price the terminology itself includes both. For example, if everyone calls cattle “yoke” but calls the implement itself “the yoke alone,” an unqualified sale of a “yoke” includes the cattle as well. I have found no dispute regarding this. On the contrary, it follows from the Gemara at the beginning of HaMokher Et HaBayit and from the Tur and Mechaber in siman 214:7 concerning one who sells a house in a large residence. The Tur there also clearly organizes the various linguistic distinctions. Rama merely arranged his glosses following the Mechaber's later general rule that price does not ordinarily prove intent.
