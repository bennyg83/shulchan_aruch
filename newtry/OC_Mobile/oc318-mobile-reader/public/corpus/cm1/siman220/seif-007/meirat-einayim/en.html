Even if they were upon it at the time of sale. The Tur likewise rules this according to the Rif, Ri, and Rosh. It is clear from the Gemara and from the Rif and Rosh that the reason a sack and a woman's riding seat are not sold even while resting on the animal is that the Gemara leaves unresolved whether the Sages still hold they are excluded when actually upon it. In monetary doubt the burden of proof lies upon the claimant. Therefore, if those accessories have already come into the purchaser's possession and the seller seeks to remove them, the seller bears the burden of proof. The Ramah states this explicitly according to his approach. See the Perishah.
