And an unspecified cow. Baer Heitev explains this as referring to one who says, "this cow." The Derishah, Bach, and Taz in siman 223 indicate that according to this understanding there is no dispute and that Rif and Rambam also agree with the Rosh. Taz proves it from the Mishna in Bava Metzia 100 concerning one who exchanges a cow for a donkey: if "a cow" never includes the calf, even one born later, and "a pregnant cow" always includes it, the Mishna's dispute cannot be explained. Pilpela Charifta likewise regards that Mishna as a complete proof for the Rosh. Responsa Chatam Sofer, Choshen Mishpat, siman 105, disagrees and maintains that Rambam certainly disputes the Rosh, apparently Rif as well. Shitah Mekubetzet cites Ramach expressly that even "this cow" does not include the fetus unless pregnancy was mentioned. He explains the Mishna as a case where the seller promised a pregnant cow and now claims that the cow already calved before delivery, so he may provide another pregnant cow and the buyer bears the risk of that birth. Chatam Sofer also rejects the proof brought from Temurah 28, explaining that consecration of the body differs from ordinary transactions. He further writes that even according to the Rosh, if neither party knew the cow was pregnant and both intended an ordinary nonpregnant cow, the calf is certainly not included, for the seller may say that he never sold on that understanding.
