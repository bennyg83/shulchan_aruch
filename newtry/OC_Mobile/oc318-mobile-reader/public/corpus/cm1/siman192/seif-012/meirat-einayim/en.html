Once he took possession of one of them, etc. The general principle of his words is as follows. Even in a place where money does not acquire without a deed, chazakah can acquire without a deed, because chazakah is stronger, since the purchaser performed an act upon the land itself. To acquire that one field alone, chazakah is effective even if he paid no money. But where he comes to acquire several fields, taking possession of one does not acquire the others, even if they adjoin one another, unless he paid the price of all of them. In that case he acquires all of them through one act of chazakah, even in a place where money alone does not acquire and even if the fields do not adjoin one another. This is unlike the Levush, who writes that the Mechaber's words imply that when the fields adjoin one another, one chazakah acquires all of them even if the purchaser did not pay the price of all of them. That is incorrect, as I proved in the Derishah from the words of the Rosh, and the Tur writes this explicitly; see there.
For the use of this one is not, etc. Meaning, even though the use of this one is not, etc.
Because he paid the price of all of them. In that case we presume that the seller transferred all of them to him through one act of chazakah.
Only corresponding to the money he paid. Nevertheless, corresponding to the money he paid he acquires even if the seller is repeatedly demanding payment. See Rivash, siman 476.
Therefore, if all of it was a gift, etc. With a gift, it is as though the giver has already received the price of all of them.
And some disagree regarding a gift. Their reason is that since the giver received nothing from the recipient, we presume that he intended to retain the option to retract as long as the recipient had not acquired each property. In the Perishah I wrote that even those who disagree regarding a gift agree concerning rental, addressed in the following seif, that if the renter takes possession of one property he acquires all of them even though he has not yet paid the landlord anything. Rental differs because rent is payable only at the end; from the beginning of the rental the landlord agreed that the rent should remain as a debt and loan obligation upon the renter until the end of the rental period. See there.
The same applies to ten houses. Meaning, do not say that the rule applies only where he sold ten fields, because there is not such a substantial interruption between them and all land is regarded as one continuous base, whereas houses are separate structures. The ruling teaches that the land upon which the houses stand joins them, even if they are in ten different countries.
