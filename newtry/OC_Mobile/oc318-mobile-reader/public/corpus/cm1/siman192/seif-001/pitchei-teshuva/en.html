He sold or gave. Machaneh Efrayim, Hilchot Sechirut 13, cites Radbaz IV:122 concerning one who sold his house to the tenant already living there. Radbaz rules that even if the seller said, "make a chazakah and acquire," the tenant does not acquire unless he performs some new act that he had not previously done as tenant. Even locking and opening the property do not suffice if those were acts he regularly performed before. Machaneh Efrayim disagrees with this conclusion. See there, and see Nachalat Tzvi on this matter.
Once he locked or fenced. Responsa Maharshadam, Choshen Mishpat 376, writes regarding his case that payment by the purchaser to workers who dug on the property constitutes a complete chazakah. It makes no difference whether the purchaser dug personally or others dug for him. Even though a non-Jew is not legally capable of agency, the ruling is not based on agency. Machaneh Efrayim, Hilchot Mechirah 15, raises a similar doubt regarding hagbahah where a buyer causes the purchased object to be lifted by non-Jewish workers. Even with Jewish workers there is room for doubt because the workers do not themselves intend to acquire on behalf of the buyer. This resembles the rule in siman 275:21 that one who builds through workers does not acquire ownerless property because the workers do not intend acquisition for the employer. Machaneh Efrayim considers both sides and leans toward the view that no acquisition occurs, because even though the hand of a worker is like the employer's hand, the worker is analogous to a moving courtyard. Netivot HaMishpat at the beginning of siman 188 also analyzes this and concludes that hagbahah, meshichah, chazakah, and other acts of acquisition performed by non-Jewish hired workers are effective as though the employer performed them himself. They are not effective when done by non-Jewish agents who are not workers. A worker's act works if the employer himself intends to acquire through the act the worker performs, even if the worker has no such intention. The rule in siman 275 concerns a case where the owner too did not intend to acquire the materials through the workers' lifting, but merely told them to build. However, where the worker would acquire only by virtue of his "hand," such as when he does not lift the object at all but merely holds it near the ground, the employer does not acquire, because a worker's hand is like a moving courtyard. Likewise, meshichah by workers is effective only when they pull the object into the employer's domain. If they pull it into their own domain or into an alley, it does not work unless the worker is legally capable of acting as an agent. See there.
