He acquires by walking. See Taz, who writes that the same applies to a synagogue seat: since it is intended only for sitting, it is acquired by sitting. Although Sema, seif katan 17, says that Raavad disagrees regarding barren rock even though its only use may be spreading fruit, he agrees concerning walking and sitting because the person's body itself derives the intended benefit in that manner. It resembles spreading bedding, except that there an important form of use normally requires bedding, whereas walking and sitting are themselves the ordinary forms of use. Therefore a path is acquired by walking and a synagogue seat by sitting. Responsa Masat Binyamin rules likewise.
