And what is the measure of the width of the path, etc. In the Perishah and Derishah I explained that the language of the Rambam and Tur means as follows. Even if the seller intended to sell a broad area to serve as a path, the purchaser does not acquire the whole width through one act of walking. If the path is defined by walls and he walks between them, then even if the space between them is no wider than needed for walking, that act acquires nothing beyond the walls. To acquire outside them he needs another act of possession, whether by walking there or otherwise. If there are no defining walls, one act of walking acquires the width needed by one carrying a small load. The Maggid Mishneh also explains the Ramban this way, and the Ramban's language is like that of the Mechaber. The Maggid Mishneh gives another interpretation, namely that if one sells a path without specifying its width, and the path is defined by walls, then even if it is only wide enough for passage we say that the purchaser accepted it and the seller need not give more. He nevertheless writes that the first interpretation is primary. The Beit Yosef cites the Maggid Mishneh and questions him, but in the Perishah I explained that there is no difficulty.
