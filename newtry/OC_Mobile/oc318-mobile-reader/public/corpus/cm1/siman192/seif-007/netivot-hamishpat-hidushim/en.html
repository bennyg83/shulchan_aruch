He bought the gear, and in the place where he was sitting on it, he was given the place to it, and even on the back of a rock beard, he did not buy fruit from the mountain to the knowledge of the Aravad in the section, although he did not enjoy what the oppressor does not enjoy:
What is the required width of the path? The Sema gives two explanations. First: where the seller expressly specified how much land was being sold, then if there are partitions the purchaser acquires by walking only the area between them, not beyond them. The Mechaber means that even if the space between the partitions is only wide enough for the foot, and when carrying a load the load would extend beyond the partitions, he may not carry there. If there are no partitions, one walk acquires only the width needed for carrying a small load, and the remainder is not acquired until he makes additional acts of chazakah by walking. Second: where the sale was unspecified, then if there are partitions, even if the path is only wide enough for walking, we say the purchaser accepted that width and the seller need give no more. The Sema rules that the first explanation is primary.
Some say the reverse. Their rationale is that one always needs enough space to carry a bundle of branches on his head. Therefore, where partitions obstruct the load, the space between them must be wide enough for that. Where there are no obstructing partitions, only enough ground for walking need be transferred. This follows the Sema's second explanation above. According to the Sema's first explanation, which he treats as primary, the dissent means as follows: where there are no partitions, each act of walking acquires only the width of one footstep, because that is sufficient for walking with a load. He cannot acquire a wider path with one walk and must make repeated walks side by side according to the width he wishes to acquire. But where partitions exist, one walk acquires the full width needed for walking while loaded. See Perishah. Rama's statement that the width suffices means that it is enough for each walk to acquire only the width of a single step.
