One who sells a field, etc. Bava Batra 57a states that any act which would acquire the property of a convert who died without heirs, etc., and mentions plowing. Rashbam explains that this is not limited to an ownerless convert's property, but applies equally to acquisition of a field. Thus plowing acquires the field, and certainly sowing, gathering produce from a tree, and similar acts do so. Ramban writes that plowing is not even credited against the required measure, whereas other forms of labor are credited, as explained above in siman 141 according to Ramban. The Yerushalmi there states that Rav regarded bringing in the produce as the principal act establishing possession. It asks whether Rav does not concede regarding weeding and hoeing, and answers that he does concede. Rav's statement means that even if witnesses saw him plow, reap, bind, thresh, and winnow, but did not see him bring in the produce, this does not establish a presumptive right. Rambam's reference to gathering produce does not mean consuming produce, which the Gemara says does not acquire ownerless property, and likewise does not acquire a field. It means gathering the produce from the tree, comparable to reaping, which is labor performed upon the land or tree. Rambam maintains that only the expressly excluded act of consuming produce is outside the rule; any act sufficient for a three-year presumption likewise effects acquisition. This is why he writes, 'Likewise, if he gathered,' etc. Rav Hai Gaon writes similarly regarding lifting water, etc.
