Not through eating its produce, etc. This law is stated by the Raavad and applies also to the Rambam's rule that where land consists of dry rock it may be acquired by spreading produce upon it or by standing an animal there. The Raavad says so explicitly in his glosses to the first chapter of Hilchot Mekhirah. Although such land is not fit for acquisition through fencing or locking, there remain other methods of acquisition, such as money, a deed, and exchange. It appears, however, that all agree that spreading bedding there and personally reclining upon it, as mentioned above in seif 9, effects acquisition, because his body derives benefit from the body of the land. This is comparable to acquisition of slaves by personal service, as the Tur and Mechaber write below in siman 196. It differs from merely eating produce, where one's body does not derive benefit from the land itself. The Rosh writes this explicitly, and I cited his words in the Derishah, contrary to the implication of Darkei Moshe.
