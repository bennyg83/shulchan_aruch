And some disagree. Shaar Mishpat writes that this dissent applies specifically where the purchaser has not paid money. If he has paid, then even in a place where deeds are customarily written, so that payment alone does not acquire, a chazakah consisting of eating the produce or a similar act should nevertheless be effective. We see that chazakah alone, without money and without a deed, acquires even in a place where deeds are written, as stated at the end of this siman. The early authorities explain that once the thing itself has entered the purchaser's control, his mind relies on the transaction. Shaar Mishpat concludes that this is logically correct, though he did not find an explicit statement of it in the earlier authorities.
