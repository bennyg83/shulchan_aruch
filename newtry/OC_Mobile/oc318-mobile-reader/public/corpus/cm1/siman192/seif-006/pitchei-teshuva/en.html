Because removing the damage. See Baer Heitev, which cites Rashbam's explanation that this is comparable to returning a lost object. Shaar Mishpat cites Ramban's novellae, which say that this is obvious where one repairs another person's property in the owner's absence, because he is performing hashavat aveidah, a mitzvah incumbent upon him. But even if the owner is present, or even with ownerless property where there is no mitzvah, people often act quickly to remove a defect so as not to be called destructive, and such conduct is not ordinarily intended as an act of acquisition.
