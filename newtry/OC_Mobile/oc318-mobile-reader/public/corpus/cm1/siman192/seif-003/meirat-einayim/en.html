And he opened it again. The Tur cites this from the Rambam and says: I do not understand why he must reopen it afterward. Ramban likewise questions the Rambam, because the Mishnah merely says that locking alone suffices. The Beit Yosef suggests that the Rambam holds that locking by itself may not look like an act of acquisition. Perhaps the purchaser locked at the owner's request merely to protect the contents of the house. When he later opens it again, it becomes clear that the locking was performed as an act of possession. The Mishnah therefore says only "locked," because that act itself constitutes the possession once he later reopens and thereby reveals its purpose retroactively. The Beit Yosef gives a second explanation: the Rambam writes that he reopened it to teach a greater novelty. One might think acquisition works only if he locked it and left it locked, because his act continues to protect the contents. The Rambam teaches that even if he immediately reopens it and restores the house to its prior state, he nevertheless acquires. The Mechaber's unqualified wording appears to adopt the first explanation as primary.
He must lock it with a key. Meaning to close the door with its lock or another fastening so that people cannot freely enter and leave the house. It is possible that when the Rambam requires one to lock and then reopen, he speaks where the purchaser did not lock it with an actual fastening. He therefore requires a second act that demonstrates that the initial closing was intended as possession. He might agree that if the purchaser closes it with the fixed lock, locking alone suffices, and the Mishnah's statement that he "locked" would refer to that case. If so, however, the Rambam should not have omitted this distinction.
