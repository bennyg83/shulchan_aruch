The same applies if, etc. Rashbam explains 'he locked' to mean that he made a lock for the door, even if he did not actually lock it. Merely locking with a key is not an act of possession, because it only drives away a lion from another's property, as stated there and in seif 6. Tosafot, Rosh, and the other commentators challenge him from the cited passage in Gittin. Tosafot and Rosh further challenge him from Bava Batra concerning one who builds a palace, etc. Necessarily, merely installing doors does not acquire, just as building the palace does not, because one has only rearranged loose bricks. Tosafot in Bava Kamma and Rosh in Bava Batra answer that the Gemara describes the ordinary case: the person who installs the doors also locks them. Had the first builder installed them, he too would have locked them and acquired. Rosh then writes that Rashbam's explanation implies that fashioning a lock alone acquires, and the same applies to installing doors without locking. Tur cites Rashbam's view here in the name of Rosh, understanding that Rosh agreed because he recorded it last. This is the meaning of, 'The same applies,' etc. Shulchan Aruch and Rama omit the view of Tosafot, Rosh, and the other authorities that locking a door alone, without reopening it, is effective. Below, siman 275, Shulchan Aruch follows Tosafot and rules that installing doors does not acquire: if the second person installed the door and locked it, he acquires; likewise, if the first person installed the doors but did not lock them, and the second came and locked them, the second acquires. Rama writes similarly there in seif 21. This contradicts the ruling here. Although Sema and Bach distinguish between an ordinary owner's property and the property of a convert who died without heirs, their distinction requires great study, because Tosafot and Rosh explicitly challenge the ruling here from the case of a convert's property, as Taz already observed. Rama's own wording there is also imprecise, because it is appended to Rambam and Shulchan Aruch's ruling. According to Rambam and Shulchan Aruch there, locking is unnecessary; installing the doors alone acquires, following Rambam's view that many acts stated expressly in the Gemara apply specifically to a convert's property. See Rambam and Shulchan Aruch there, seif 13. Thus there are three views regarding installing doors: Rambam, Rosh, and Rashbam. Regarding locking, the other authorities require locking alone, while Rambam requires locking and reopening. Tosafot, Avodah Zarah 71a, s.v. (Supplement) The same applies, etc. Rashbam holds that installing doors, making a lock for the door, or delivering the key constitutes possession, whereas locking the door with its lock does not. He interprets 'he locked' as 'he made a lock for the door.' Installing doors is also possession, based on Bava Batra 53b concerning one who builds a palace, etc.; delivery of the key follows the first chapter of Pesachim. Locking the door does not acquire, based on Bava Batra 53a concerning one who merely drives away a lion, etc. See Rashbam at 53a, s.v. vehashata ka'aylei, and 57a, s.v. beneikhsei chaveiro nami kanah. Tosafot at 53a, s.v. na'al, Tosafot in the fifth chapter of Bava Kamma, and Rosh in both places disagree and rule conversely: locking acquires, while making a door or lock and delivering a key do not. See their extended discussions. (End of supplement.)
