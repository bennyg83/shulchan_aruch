Seif 3: They have no right of way. The Sema writes in seif katan 7 that this concerns a division without appraisal or additional payment. This is difficult, for the Rosh writes in the first chapter of Bava Batra, as cited by the Beit Yosef in siman 172, that where brothers divide houses by appraisal and additional payment, four cubits are allotted to every entrance, because this is included in the appraisal. He distinguishes a window, for which the owner does not acquire a four-cubit setback, because the area opposite the window is not as integral to the use of the house, and because the house has other light. Thus, the Rosh compares the rule of a window in a division by appraisal to the four cubits of an entrance. The Gemara in Bava Batra 7 likewise clearly speaks of a division by appraisal and additional payment, and cites the teaching of Nehardea that brothers who divide are regarded as purchasers. It is therefore clear that the ruling concerning brothers who divide also applies where there was appraisal and additional payment. Nor does the present case concern one who blocks the light entirely. Although Rabbeinu writes that he may remove it, he means that the other has authority to compel him to remove the obstruction, contrary to the Ramban's view in siman 154. It does not mean that he may darken the house completely.
