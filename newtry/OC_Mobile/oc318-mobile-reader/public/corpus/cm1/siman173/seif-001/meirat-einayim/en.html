Because it is degrading to the books to divide them from one bound volume. Meaning, cutting a single bound volume into two for the purpose of partition is degrading. Cutting and dividing pages or sections for one's own legitimate use is not necessarily the same degradation.
The rule of buy or sell does not apply. Meaning, neither can one say, 'You buy this one and I will take the other,' with an equalizing payment, because the other can say, 'I need both.' Nor can one say, 'You buy both and pay me half their value, or I will buy both and pay you your share,' because the other can say, 'One of them is enough for my study,' since neither book depends on the other for its use. The Tur and Mechaber explain all this in siman 171:13. There they also write that one can sometimes invoke buy-or-sell regarding one item while leaving the other in partnership. If they wish, he may present the choice regarding both sequentially, or even simultaneously say, 'The choice is yours to select both or whichever one you want, or I will do so.' See the Perishah and Sema there, where I explained the reasons.
