They have against one another neither a right of way nor ladders, etc. The Tur explains: no right of way means that if their father used to pass through the field now allotted to Reuven in order to reach the field allotted to Shimon, Reuven may prevent Shimon from using that route. No ladders means not only that if the upper story fell to Reuven and the house and courtyard to Shimon, Reuven cannot place a ladder in Shimon's courtyard to reach his upper story. Even if the upper story and courtyard fell to Reuven while the house fell to Shimon, Reuven cannot rest his ladder against Shimon's house. No water channel means that if a channel had run through one brother's field to the other's field, the first may block it. No windows means that he may remove the light entirely and close the window, as explained above. They are treated like two people who bought a field from one owner and then divided it, so neither retains rights over the other's portion. In the Perishah and Derishah I demonstrated that this case concerns a division without appraisal and equalizing payments. Therefore one may build directly opposite the other's window even though he entirely blocks the light. This is why the Tur and Mechaber state the rule without mentioning appraisal or a limitation against total darkening, unlike siman 154. The later rule in seif 7, giving the vineyard owner four cubits in the grain field, concerns specifically a division made through appraisal and equalization, where the owner of the vineyard compensated the owner of the field for the vineyard's greater value. We then say that the four cubits were included in that valuation. The Tur and Mechaber teach a novelty in each context: here, without appraisal, one may even block the other's light completely; later, with appraisal, the vineyard owner receives four cubits even though he could theoretically leave four cubits from his own vineyard in which to turn his plow.
