And if it fell, the right is gone. Beit Yosef at the end of the siman cites Rashba explicitly concerning a jointly owned well for which a right of way had been acquired. Rashba says that if the walls of the well collapse, even if the well is rebuilt, the right of way is lost because the servitude was granted for that well and not another. This is very difficult. Sema, citing Rashba in siman 154, seif katan 25, states that as long as the underlying thing to which the servitude attached remains in existence, the servitude is not lost, such as where the wall containing a window falls and is rebuilt, or a gutter breaks and is repaired. The same should apply to the rules of siman 214 concerning an inner room and its right of passage. Even if the room collapses and is rebuilt, the passage should remain because the site to which the servitude attached still exists. I do not understand Rashba's proof from Rashbam at the end of HaMokher Et HaBayit. Rashbam says only that if a pit or cistern is destroyed and can no longer be repaired, its path right ends. That is certainly true, because the path was granted only for the pit and not for a different purpose. But if the pit is rebuilt and used again as a pit, why should the way be lost so long as the object of the servitude continues? No posek states generally that a pit or cistern loses its way permanently merely because its walls fell and were rebuilt. Perhaps Rashba's case involved an express condition when the way was acquired that the right existed only for that particular physical pit. Then, when its walls collapsed and were rebuilt, it is no longer legally the same pit. Rashba's novelty would be that ordinary debris falling into the pit and later being removed does not make it a new pit, because that is merely like removing a cover, whereas collapse of the walls and reconstruction does.
