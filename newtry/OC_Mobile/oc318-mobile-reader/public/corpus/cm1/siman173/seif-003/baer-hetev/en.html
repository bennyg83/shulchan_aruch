Windows. See the Sema, who cites the Tur's wording explaining this matter. He writes that here the division was made without appraisal and equalizing payment, and therefore one may build opposite his fellow's window even if he completely blocks its light. See there. The Taz disagrees and writes that it is clear that brothers who divide are also discussing a division by appraisal and equalization, and that this passage does not permit completely darkening the other's window. Previously. The reason is that originally everything belonged to their father, and passing through one area or placing ladders from one part to another did not constitute one owner damaging another. Now, however, one enters or places a ladder in the other's portion and thereby causes damage. Therefore the ruling concludes that if two purchasers bought from two prior owners who had mutually burdened one another, the purchasers acquire those rights and stand in the place of the former owners. Sema.
