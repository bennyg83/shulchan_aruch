(Mechaber, seif 4) And if he says, "I repaid you before so-and-so and so-and-so." Note: This requires study where those witnesses died and the borrower claims that he repaid, but not in their presence. Is he believed by a migo according to the Rosh cited by the Sema, seif katan 13, who explains that he is not believed by a migo to say, "I paid before so-and-so and so-and-so and they died," because people would say, "one who wishes to lie distances his witnesses"? That reasoning does not apply here, because here he was obligated to pay before those very witnesses. Why should he appear dishonest if he claimed that he fulfilled the condition? Consequently, even if he says that he repaid outside their presence, perhaps he should be believed by the migo that he could have said he paid before them. One may answer that while the witnesses were still alive he would not have been believed by such a migo. His claim that he paid outside their presence was therefore defective from the outset, because had the lender sued him while the witnesses were alive he would have been required to pay again. For this reason he remains not believed even by a migo after the witnesses' death.
