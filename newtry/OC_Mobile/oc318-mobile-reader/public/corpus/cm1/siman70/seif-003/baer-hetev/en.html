Rather. From here until the end of the siman, the case is where the condition was stated before witnesses. Otherwise the borrower would be believed through a migo that he could have denied that such a condition was ever made. Sema.
Must. The Shach writes that if the borrower protested and said he did not agree to repay specifically before witnesses, he is believed to say that he later repaid without witnesses, contrary to the Ran. If, however, the lender warned him before beit din not to repay except before witnesses, the Rosh, Tur, and Rabbeinu Yeruham indicate that even if the borrower protested he must repay before witnesses, since the lender had already been forced to bring him before beit din. The Shach nevertheless questions how one party can impose such a condition against the other's will and leaves the matter requiring study.
Oath. The Shach understands this as referring only to an oath before payment. After payment, the borrower can require the lender to take a shevuat heset, as follows all the more so from siman 71, seif 5. The Sema asks why the borrower is not believed through a migo that he could have claimed, "I paid you before so-and-so and so-and-so, who have gone overseas or died." He answers that this is not a proper migo because one who wishes to lie can distance his witnesses, and further, since the parties expressly stipulated repayment before witnesses, there is a strong presumption that he did not repay privately. The Shach disputes this reasoning. See also his comments to siman 133, seif 2.
