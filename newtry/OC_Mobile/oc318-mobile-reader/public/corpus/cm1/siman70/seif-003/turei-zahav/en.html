(Seif 4) “For he said that he fulfilled his condition.” It appears obvious that even if he says, “I paid you privately,” he is believed once fulfillment of the stipulated form became impossible because the named witnesses died. The text mentions payment before them to teach that one should not argue that, had payment truly been made in their presence, he would have obtained a written receipt. The instruction that he must pay before beit din is difficult: why do ordinary witnesses not suffice, when the preceding ruling states that testimony by witnesses to payment discharges him? Perhaps he must obtain a written and signed record, and ordinary witnesses may not later submit written testimony because that would be testimony from writing; they are permitted to sign only in the form of a formal document, as explained in siman 21.
