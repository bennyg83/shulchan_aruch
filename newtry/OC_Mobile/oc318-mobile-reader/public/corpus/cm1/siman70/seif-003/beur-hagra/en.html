(Collected note) And if he said... This applies even to a deposit. All the poskim say so, unlike the Rosh in chapter 3 of Bava Batra, siman 46, who holds that the custodian is believed with a Torah oath through a migo that it was lost through unavoidable circumstances, just as with a document. The Rosh follows his own reasoning in HaDayanim, siman 13, where he raises and answers a difficulty, but that reasoning does not apply to a migo of unavoidable loss. When the Gemara there says he is not believed, it means only that he is not believed to exempt himself from an oath, as Tosafos there 45b, s.v. b'shtar, explains. Maggid Mishneh, however, answers the Rosh's difficulty by saying there is no migo at all: the defendant himself admits he did not fulfill the stated condition, so liability follows from his own admission. Beit Yose gives another answer, that it is a migo against witnesses. The Ran similarly writes that "we are witnesses," etc. See Beer HaGolah, seif katan 10. (End of note.)
