[At the time of the loan. And he said to him, “I am not afraid of Hashem’s mercy, and I am not afraid of Hashem’s mercy, and I will not be able to do it with him.”
After the loan. Rosh and Tur rule that if, after the loan, the creditor warns the borrower before beit din, "Do not repay me except before witnesses," the condition binds even if the borrower protests and says he does not accept it. The Shach asks how one litigant can force such a condition on the other against his will. It appears that the answer lies in the power of beit din. If the creditor asks beit din to write the judgment, they may prepare and give him a written ruling even against the debtor's will. Once a judgment is written, the debtor can no longer ordinarily claim payment without witnesses, as explained in siman 79. While the creditor has not requested a written judgment, beit din does not write it automatically, unlike a kinyan that ordinarily stands ready for writing. Mahari of Trani explains this in siman 79. Therefore, once the debt has actually been litigated and established in beit din, the creditor has the power to transform the oral debt into a written judicial debt by requesting the written ruling. Since he could impose that stronger documentary form, he may likewise impose the lesser procedural condition that repayment be made only before witnesses. This applies specifically where the debt was actually claimed and established in beit din. If the creditor merely appears before beit din and announces the condition concerning an ordinary oral loan that has not been adjudicated, he cannot unilaterally convert it into the equivalent of a written debt. In that case the borrower's protest remains effective.
