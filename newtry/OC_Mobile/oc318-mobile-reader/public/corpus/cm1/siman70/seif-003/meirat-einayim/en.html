He told him, 'Do not repay me, etc.' From here until the end of the siman the discussion concerns a case where he said this before witnesses. Otherwise the borrower would be believed through a migo that he could say no such condition was made. See Perishah.
Whether he said it after lending him, etc. Ran in chapter Shevuot HaDayanim writes that according to Rambam, even if the borrower says, 'I do not wish to repay you except not before witnesses,' he must nevertheless repay before witnesses. The reason is that once the lender imposed this condition, it is not the borrower's practice to repay privately, because he wishes to avoid suspicion and dispute. Therefore, when witnesses testify that the lender made this condition, we regard it as though witnesses establish that the borrower did not repay privately. The lender is consequently believed without an oath. Maggid Mishneh at the beginning of chapter 15 of Hilchot Malveh writes similarly and concludes with the principle, 'the borrower is servant to the lender.' See Perishah.
He is not believed. We do not believe him through a migo that he could have said, 'I fulfilled your condition and repaid you before So-and-so and So-and-so, and they went overseas or died,' a claim on which the Tur and Mechaber later say he would be exempt. This is not a valid migo, because one who wishes to lie can distance his witnesses. Moreover, the presumption just explained weakens his present claim, for we regard it as virtually witnessed that he would not have repaid privately.
