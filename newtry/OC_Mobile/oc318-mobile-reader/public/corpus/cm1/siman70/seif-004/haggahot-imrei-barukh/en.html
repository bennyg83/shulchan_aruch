(Taz, seif 4) For he said, etc.; it appears simple, etc. Note: This requires great study. The Shulchan Aruch discusses a case where the borrower claims that repayment occurred while the specified witnesses were still alive, when he could still have fulfilled the condition. One may further say that even now, after they have died, and he could exempt himself by claiming that repayment occurred after their death when the condition had become impossible, he is nevertheless not believed by such a migo if he voluntarily admits that repayment occurred before they died. This is a migo against a presumption, as the Maggid Mishneh explains. Even according to the Rosh cited by the Sema, seif katan 13, who does not rely on the Maggid Mishneh's presumption, the Rosh may agree here that the claim is impaired because he says that he repaid outside the witnesses' presence while, so long as they lived, he had no migo and would have had to pay again if the lender sued him.
