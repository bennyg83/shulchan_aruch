If he repaid before them, he is exempt. This follows the Rambam and the Mechaber, who hold that a borrower may say, "I repaid you before witnesses and they died." We therefore explain that the lender designated particular witnesses only to prevent that uncheckable claim, not because he intended to invalidate all other witnesses who might actually come to beit din and testify to payment. According to the Rif and those who follow him, however, since even without a designation the borrower is not believed to say that he repaid before witnesses who died, the lender's designation must have been intended to disqualify all other witnesses. Thus even if other witnesses testify that payment was made, they are not believed. The Shach writes, however, that this applies where the lender says that the payment those witnesses saw was for a different debt. Even though they say that it was for this debt, we can divide their testimony and accept that money was paid without accepting their characterization of which debt it satisfied. But if the lender admits that there is no other debt and merely says, "These are false witnesses," then even according to the Rif he is not believed to establish witnesses as liars.
He is exempt. If the specifically designated witnesses went overseas or died, and the borrower claims, "I paid you privately," he is not believed by virtue of a migo that he could have said, "I paid before the witnesses you specified." This depends upon the dispute between the Rosh and Ran that I cited above in seif katan 10, and the same rule applies here. If, however, he claims that he paid privately only after those witnesses had already died, when it was impossible to fulfill the condition, he is believed. See Shach. The Shach further concludes that if the lender designated a class of people, such as Torah scholars or physicians, the rule is the same as where he specified ordinary individuals. Other witnesses who actually testify to payment are nevertheless believed.
That it was for another debt, etc. See Tumim. This applies only when the witnesses testify generally that money was paid, so the lender may say he received it for a different debt. If they expressly testify that this very debt was paid, the lender is not believed to say that he took the money for another debt, because he cannot contradict witnesses.
He is believed with an oath. According to the Rif he is not believed. The Shach writes that if he brings other witnesses who testify that he paid in the presence of the witnesses designated by the lender, those witnesses are believed according to all opinions.
He pays through beit din. See Tumim for the reason he cannot simply pay before witnesses and obtain a receipt or handwritten acknowledgment from the lender.
