He said to him, etc. A condition that the debtor not repay except in the presence of specified persons is effective whether made at the time of the loan or afterward, even without a kinyan, just as explained in the preceding seif. The Mordechai, citing Rashbat, requires a kinyan, but that discussion follows the view of the Rif and Rashi that all other witnesses are rejected and therefore treats the condition like granting the creditor credibility against two witnesses. Rabbi Yoel disagrees and follows Raavyah that even such enhanced credibility does not require a kinyan. According to the Rif and Rashi, however, if the stipulation was made after the loan, a kinyan is indeed required, just as in siman 71, seif 2.
If those other witnesses came, etc. This law requires study. In my view the position of the Rif, Rabbi Yosef Halevi, Rashi, and those who follow them is primary. Baal HaMaor, the Ramban in Milchamos, and Hagahos Ashri also rule in this manner. The Raavad's position can likewise be defended against the objection of the Maggid Mishneh. When the debtor stipulates that repayment must be made before specified witnesses, he effectively disqualifies all others from establishing repayment of this debt, much as one who grants the creditor credibility equivalent to two witnesses. If other witnesses testify that money was paid specifically toward this debt, the creditor may nevertheless say, "That money was applied to another debt," because their testimony was disqualified only for purposes of proving repayment of this obligation. If, however, the creditor says, "I never received the money at all," he ultimately cannot retain it. The other witnesses at least establish that he received the money, even if they cannot establish its legal application to this debt, and the debtor could immediately sue to recover that same sum. There is no reason to require such a circular transfer. Accordingly, only a claim that the payment was applied elsewhere can preserve the creditor's position. The same applies where the creditor was expressly granted credibility equivalent to two witnesses. The Raavad therefore advises that where there is a document, it should be torn upon repayment, and where there is no document a receipt should be written, thereby preventing repeated collection. The language of the Gemara also supports the Rif. Thus, if the creditor has seized property, even before the other witnesses appeared, he may say that he follows the Rif, Rashi, Ri Migash, Raavad, Baal HaMaor, Ramban, and Hagahos Ashri, but only where he claims that the payment was applied elsewhere. If he denies receiving any money and witnesses saw him receive it, the debtor is exempt according to all views. If the witnesses designated by the condition went overseas and other witnesses testify that the debtor paid in the presence of those designated witnesses, the condition was fulfilled and the debtor is believed even according to the Rif, as the Ramban writes. Baal HaMaor would apparently agree in such a case as well. Baal HaTerumos, at the end of Shaar 25, may treat testimony that the condition was fulfilled in the same manner as testimony that the debtor acknowledged payment before others, within the dispute of Baal HaMaor and the Ramban. The words of Rabbeinu Yerucham on this point are difficult. As a practical matter, the Ramban's view is primary: if the debtor acknowledged before two other persons that he had paid, those witnesses are believed, and the creditor cannot say that he follows Baal HaMaor. This is certainly so where they testify that the debtor paid before the specifically named persons. The Mechaber in siman 71, seif 3, rules that even when the creditor has credibility equivalent to two witnesses, witnesses may testify to the debtor's acknowledgment. All the more so here.
And they testified that he paid in their presence, etc. According to the Rambam, Rosh, Ran, and those who follow their approach, naming particular witnesses was not intended to disqualify all other witnesses. Its purpose was only to prevent the debtor from evading payment by falsely claiming that he paid before witnesses who subsequently died or went overseas.
He is exempt, etc. Shiltei Giborim distinguishes between a condition naming ordinary individuals and one requiring payment specifically before learned halachic scholars. In his view, other witnesses may prove payment in the first case because the named persons were not uniquely credible, while unlearned witnesses are disqualified in the second. This distinction does not appear correct. All the authorities indicate that there is no such difference. The Rif derives his position from a case involving two persons who study halachot, while the Rambam, Rosh, and others reject the Rif because of their textual reading, not because the designated persons were of a special category. The Tur explicitly presents the same dispute whether the debtor named particular witnesses or required Torah scholars or physicians. Thus, according to the Rambam and Rosh, other witnesses are effective in either case.
Nevertheless, the creditor, etc. The Rama rules that the creditor may claim that the payment was applied elsewhere even when the debtor paid before other witnesses. This requires study. Under the view of the Rambam and the Mechaber, those witnesses were never disqualified. If they testify expressly that the money was paid toward this conditioned debt, the creditor should therefore be unable to claim that it was applied elsewhere. Even if they testify only to a general payment, the source in the Mordechai may be following the position of the Rif and Rashi. Perhaps the Rama inferred that the Mordechai rejects complete disqualification yet still allows the creditor to claim that the payment was applied elsewhere, but this is difficult. The ruling should at most have been introduced as a dissenting opinion.
If they died or went overseas. If the other witnesses died or went overseas and therefore cannot testify in beit din, the debtor is not believed to claim payment.
"I will not repay you until so and so and so and so come," etc. The Sema, Ir Shushan, and Bach explain that the debtor should repay in beit din so that the creditor cannot later claim that the money was applied to another debt. This explanation is strained. The Tur and Mechaber do not mention such a concern, and the debtor could simply repay before witnesses while expressly designating the payment for this debt, which is effective even against a creditor who has credibility equivalent to two witnesses according to the Tur and Mechaber in siman 71, seif 3. Rather, the concern is that the witnesses may later die or go overseas, in which case the debtor will no longer be believed. The witnesses themselves cannot simply write a proof of payment without the creditor's authorization, because testimony must ordinarily be given orally and witnesses may not write a document unless authorized. Beit din, however, may compel the matter and issue a written receipt. This is why the Tur specifically says to repay in beit din.
