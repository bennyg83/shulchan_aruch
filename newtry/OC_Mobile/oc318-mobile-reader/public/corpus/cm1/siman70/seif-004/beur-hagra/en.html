(Collected note) He said to him... The Rif has another reading: Rav Pappa in the name of Rava, etc., and if the witnesses went overseas he is not believed. In the incident where one stipulated, "Pay me before Reuven and Shimon," the Rif explains like Rashi that the witnesses are present and the debtor contradicts them. This is compelled by his approach, because if they are absent he could not make the claim at all. He further proves it from the incident where a ruling was taught, reading that he went and paid before witnesses, and the creditor said, "Since you admit..." The decisive factor is his admission. Our text differs. See Tosafos there, s.v. azal, who answer by reference to the later passage at 42a, "You trusted me..." See the Rosh, Ran, and Rambam, who investigated the variants and accepted our reading. (End of note.)
If they came. Rambam and Tosafos there, "he went..." This accords with the accepted version rather than the rejected alternative; in such a case there is no basis for rejection.
Nevertheless. This follows Rashi and the Rif there, that even if they came he is not believed, but only in this particular type of case, not for their broader reason.
And if they died. The Gemara there according to Rava and their explanation above.
And if he said. So it appears from the Gemara's expression, "so that he will not push me off." This is the meaning of, "for he said..."
And if he admits. As Tosafos there writes, "But here I..."
