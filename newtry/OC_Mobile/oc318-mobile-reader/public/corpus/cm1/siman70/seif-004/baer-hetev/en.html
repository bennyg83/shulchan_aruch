Before them. The Sema explains that payment should be made before beit din so the lender cannot say, 'I received the money for another debt.' The Shach writes that this is strained. If that were the reason, why require beit din? He could pay before witnesses and state that payment is for this debt, which is effective even where the lender has been made credible like two witnesses, as stated in siman 71, seif 3, and certainly here. Rather, the concern is that ordinary witnesses may later die or travel overseas. If it later becomes known that he paid before them, but they have died or departed, he may have to pay again. The witnesses cannot give him a written and signed receipt without the lender's permission because testimony must be oral, but beit din can write over the lender's objection. The law therefore states the definitive solution. The Taz likewise writes this.
