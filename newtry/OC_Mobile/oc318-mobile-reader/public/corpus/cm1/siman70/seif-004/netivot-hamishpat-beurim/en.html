For he said that he fulfilled his condition. See Taz, who writes that if the two specified persons have already died and the borrower says that he repaid privately after their deaths, he is believed because it was no longer possible to fulfill the condition. In my opinion this ruling requires substantial study. He could still fulfill the condition by paying before beit din. The entire point of the original stipulation was to prevent the borrower from evading payment by later saying, “I paid you,” until he pays before the specified persons, before beit din, or obtains a handwritten receipt from the lender. If so, what difference does it make that the specified persons died?
He paid him before beit din. See Taz and Shach, who discuss whether he should take a receipt, or whether a written record of beit din might be disqualified as testimony from writing. Although for a receipt the witnesses do not need an express instruction to write, as explained in siman 81, seif 29, it is nevertheless possible that the lender can protest against their writing. Still, the Tumim properly questions why this matters. Since the lender is presently demanding payment, the borrower need not pay until the lender instructs the witnesses to write a receipt, which would then be valid according to all opinions. The essential answer is as the Tumim writes. According to the Rif, other witnesses are not believed in the face of the stipulation, and if the lender has already seized property we do not remove it from him, as the Shach writes. Likewise, a judge who rules in accordance with the Rif is not reversed. Therefore the borrower need not make payment in a manner that leaves him exposed to that view. The Tumim nevertheless asks that the borrower could obtain a receipt in the lender's own handwriting. In my opinion, a handwritten receipt certainly is effective. The Mechaber is teaching a further rule, namely that a receipt issued by beit din is effective even according to the Rif, because the stipulation invalidated only witnesses, not the formal record of beit din.
