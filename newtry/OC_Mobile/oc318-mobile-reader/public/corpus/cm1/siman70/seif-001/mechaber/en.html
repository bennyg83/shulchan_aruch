An Oral Loan, and a Lender's Instruction, 'Repay Me Only Before Witnesses.' Six seifim. It is forbidden to lend without witnesses, even to a Torah scholar, unless the loan is secured by collateral. A documented loan is preferable. One who lends without witnesses violates, 'Do not place a stumbling block before the blind,' and brings a curse upon himself. If a person lends before witnesses but without a formal act of acquisition or document, the borrower need not repay before witnesses and is believed upon a heses oath to say, 'I paid you.' {Rama: If a formal act of acquisition was made, even without a document, some say the borrower cannot claim payment, because it is like a document, as every acquisition is intended for writing. Others disagree. See above, siman 39, seif 5. Wherever one is believed to say, 'I paid,' he is also believed to say, 'You waived the debt,' although waiver is a weak defense. See below, siman 82.}
