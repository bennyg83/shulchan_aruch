(Shulchan Aruch, seif 1) “Even to a Torah scholar.” At the end of Eizehu Neshekh, Ravina refused to lend Rav Ashi one hundred zuz without a document and witnesses. Rav Ashi asked, “Even to me?” and Ravina replied that this was all the more necessary for one immersed in study, who may forget and thereby cause a curse upon himself. This sounds like a Torah scholar is an a fortiori case, whereas the Tur says “even a Torah scholar.” In my opinion, according to the explanation that an undocumented lender causes a curse upon himself, a Torah scholar is indeed more likely than others to forget. But according to the explanation that the lender violates “Do not place a stumbling block before the blind,” one might have thought that the prohibition does not apply to lending to a Torah scholar. The Tur therefore writes “even a Torah scholar” and follows the latter reason. The practical difference is that under the curse rationale, one who accepts the risk might not be restrained; under the stumbling-block rationale, an actual prohibition remains. The Gemara’s expression “all the more so a Torah scholar” relates to the likelihood of forgetfulness, but the legal prohibition applies even in his case. Hence the Tur and Mechaber use “even.”
