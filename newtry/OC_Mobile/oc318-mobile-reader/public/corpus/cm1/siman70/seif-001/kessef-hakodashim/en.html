Seif 1. A loan documented by a note is preferable. The admonition not to lend without a document logically applies to the borrower as well. The authorities mention the lender because safeguarding proof is primarily his responsibility. Even after the loan, if the lender asks the borrower for a handwritten acknowledgment and this creates no danger for the borrower, it is proper for the borrower to provide one, especially where at the outset he expected to repay immediately but payment was delayed, and all the more where he does not know when he will be able to pay. One should not rely on the hope that the borrower will become wealthy, for that is not sufficiently common. This is especially true where the lender is willing to wait and ultimately collect from the borrower's land in the hands of heirs. The fact that the borrower records the debt in his private ledger is not sufficient proof for the lender. If the borrower prefers to place an acknowledgment with a third party, he need not necessarily do more than that, particularly after the loan. Where a handwritten instrument could expose him to improper proceedings in secular court, but depositing the writing with a third party avoids that concern, he should do so. At minimum he should acknowledge the debt before witnesses. They can prepare an instrument expressly excluding collection from encumbered property and drafted so that it cannot be abused in secular court. It may also be kept discreetly so as not to multiply documents against the borrower, with notice that when he pays he should do so before the witnesses and demand return or cancellation of the document.
