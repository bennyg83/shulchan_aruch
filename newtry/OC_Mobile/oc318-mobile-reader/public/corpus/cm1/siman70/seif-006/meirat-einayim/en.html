And he cannot collect from encumbered property by virtue of, etc. In the Perishah I explained that this means as follows. If a creditor of the seller comes and seizes the land from this purchaser, the purchaser cannot in turn collect from property that the original seller acquired and sold after the coerced sale to this purchaser. Certainly, however, the seller himself cannot remove the land from this purchaser, who acquired it from him under coercion, without returning the money. The case assumes that it is known that the purchaser paid money for the field, even though the acquisition was compelled. See the Perishah and Derishah.
