One who sold, etc. The Tur states that the debtor is also believed to say that he paid. The Beit Yosef makes no independent comment, merely identifying the source as the case of the orchard in Bava Metzia. The Gemara there does not expressly state that payment may be pleaded. On the contrary, it appears to ask only whether collection may be made from encumbered property, implying that collection from unencumbered property is certain and that payment cannot be pleaded. Tosafot, however, state that it was obvious that payment may be pleaded, and the Rashba’s responsum cited by the Beit Yosef likewise indicates this. Baal HaTerumot, by contrast, writes that collection may at least be made from unencumbered property and that if the debtor pleads payment the creditor answers, “Why is your note in my hand?” Rivash also writes this. Since Baal HaTerumot and Rivash compare this law to an antedated note, and most authorities, including the Mechaber in siman 43, rule that an antedated note collects from unencumbered property and defeats a payment claim, the practical law requires study.
