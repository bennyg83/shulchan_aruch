If a person's document was erased and the beit din wrote him another, it has the status of a documented loan and the borrower cannot claim payment. Some say the same applies if an oral claim was adjudicated, the defendant was held liable but could not pay, and the beit din wrote an official record: he is not later believed to claim payment, and collection may be made from purchasers. If a person was forced to sell a field and made a prior declaration that voided the sale, the coercer's purchase money remaining with the seller has the status of an oral loan. The coercer cannot seize encumbered property by virtue of his document, because it was not lawfully authorized to be written, and the seller is believed to say he repaid it. The same applies to every document not authorized to be written. {Rama: See above, siman 61, seif 2.}
