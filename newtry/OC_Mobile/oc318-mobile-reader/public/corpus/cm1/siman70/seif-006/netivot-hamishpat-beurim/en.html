And the same applies to any instrument that was not permitted to be written. See Tumim, who asks that whenever a person sells a field in the presence of witnesses, the transaction has publicity and the purchaser may collect from encumbered property. If so, why does it matter that the instrument was not authorized to be written? Even without an instrument, the purchaser should collect from encumbered property like any purchaser of a field in the presence of witnesses. In my opinion there is no difficulty. Only a valid sale gives rise to collection from encumbered property in that manner. Here, because the sale is legally ineffective, the money paid has the status of a loan. With a loan, even if it becomes publicly known, the creditor cannot collect from encumbered property unless the debtor created that right through a valid instrument. Whenever the instrument is invalid, collection from encumbered property is unavailable, as is evident from an instrument susceptible to forgery. The Tumim himself writes this in siman 49, seif katan 8.
