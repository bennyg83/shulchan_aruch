That he must clarify his words. Rambam did not write in this unqualified language. Rather, he writes: 'We say to him, bring them and you will be exempt. If they died or went overseas, he takes a hesset oath that he repaid, for we require him to bring them only in order to clarify his words and thereby be exempt even from an oath, since one who borrows before witnesses need not repay before witnesses.' Thus Rambam did not say without qualification that he must clarify his words, but immediately explained that this is only in order to exempt him from an oath. From this the Tur carefully infers that according to Rambam, even if the witnesses come and contradict him, he is still exempt upon taking an oath, as I wrote in Perishah. But according to one who simply states that he must clarify his words, an oath obviously does not help once the witnesses contradict him. Rama therefore should not have written on the Mechaber's words, which according to Beit Yosef are Rambam's words, that he must 'clarify his words' without qualification, since the Mechaber concludes that he is believed with an oath even if the witnesses come and contradict him. See Perishah and Darkei Moshe.
And they contradicted him and said, 'It never happened,' etc. The reason is that since he was not obligated to repay before witnesses, we apply the principle that a matter which a person was not required to keep in mind is not necessarily remembered accurately. He may think that he repaid before these witnesses when in fact he repaid before others or privately.
There is no distinction whether he specifically designated them, etc. In Derishah I proved that Rif, Rash, Rashba, Ran, Rosh, and Tur hold that even if the witnesses did not die or go overseas, whenever bringing them involves substantial trouble he need not bring them, but may swear and be exempt. If they later come and contradict him, he is likewise exempt for the reason just explained. This is also Rambam's view according to the Tur's interpretation. Rambam holds only that initially we tell him to bring them so that, if they confirm his statement, he will not need to swear, because whenever there is a practical way to resolve a matter without requiring an oath, we do so.
