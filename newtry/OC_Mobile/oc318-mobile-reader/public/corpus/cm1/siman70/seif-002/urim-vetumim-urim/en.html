That he must clarify his statement. The purpose is to exempt him from an oath if his explanation is established. It may also happen that the witnesses explicitly testify to the opposite and say they know he did not repay, as Baal HaTerumot writes in the name of Ramban. This is unlike Sema's narrower explanation.
And they said, "It never happened." Sema explains that the borrower himself mistakenly thought he had repaid privately. Because the fact was not one he was focused on preserving, he may have erred. The Shach objects: if that were the explanation, why would it matter whether the borrower had summoned those witnesses or not? He therefore says that because the witnesses were not expressly summoned to observe repayment, they themselves may not have paid close attention. Perhaps they saw the repayment and later forgot it. The borrower's claim can therefore remain true.
But if he says he specifically summoned them. The Shach holds that even then, if after the witnesses contradict him he retracts and says, "I was mistaken," he can remain exempt. His position is not compelling. In any event, according to Rama he is liable in all such cases once his asserted specifically designated witnesses contradict him.
