They did not come. This means that even if the witnesses are available but he does not bring them, he is still believed. Rambam’s wording, “they did not come, or they died, or went abroad,” shows that the first clause includes witnesses who are locally available. Their production is required only to clarify his words and exempt him even from an oath. This follows Tosafot and the Yerushalmi, which says that Rabbi required clarification only to establish the truth. If they are not produced, he is nevertheless believed upon a rabbinic oath. The stricter Geonic view, that failure to clarify invalidates the claim, cannot be reconciled with the standard reading of Sanhedrin 23b.
