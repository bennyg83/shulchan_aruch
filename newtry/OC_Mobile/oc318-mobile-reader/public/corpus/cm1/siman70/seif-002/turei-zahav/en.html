(Seif 2) “Bring them and be discharged.” The Tur cites this from the Rambam, who also holds that if the witnesses contradict the claimant, their contradiction does not destroy his right to swear. The Gemara’s requirement that he clarify his words means that whenever clarification is possible and would avoid an unnecessary oath, beit din first requires it. If clarification is impossible, or if the witnesses say that the transaction never occurred, he may nevertheless take the oath. The Rambam mentions the witnesses’ death or departure overseas, rather than the greater novelty of their contradiction, because even where they merely went overseas and clarification may later become possible, the oath is not delayed. This also answers the Sema’s objection to the Rama’s gloss “he must clarify his words” upon the Rambam. The Rambam agrees that clarification is initially required; the court simply does not deny an oath when clarification fails.
