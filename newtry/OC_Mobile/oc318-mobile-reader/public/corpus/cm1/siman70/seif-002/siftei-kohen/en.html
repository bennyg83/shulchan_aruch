He said, “I paid you,” etc. I discussed this at length in my work and concluded that the primary ruling follows Rashbam, the Rif, the Tur, and the Ramban: the debtor is not automatically established as a denier merely because the witnesses he identified contradict him. This accords with the Gemara in Shevuot. The Rosh and Tur must be understood as distinguishing between an ordinary oral loan and a case where possession or a presumption rests upon a particular note, as in the case discussed at the beginning of siman 140, where the possessor must substantiate the document upon which the presumption depends. The Rama’s unqualified statement that whenever the witnesses contradict the debtor he must pay is therefore difficult, because no authority can reject the Gemara’s rule altogether. Some distinction must be made. Apparently the Rama follows the Maggid Mishneh’s interpretation of the Rambam concerning witnesses to the loan, but that is a minority view. In practical law, the various explanations of the Gemara may both be true. If the debtor retracts and says that he mistakenly thought he paid before the named people, he is not established as a denier, even if he initially said that he summoned them as witnesses. Payment is not ordinarily a matter he was required to prove, so he may have confused the identities or circumstances. If, even after the witnesses contradict him, he insists that he certainly paid before them, then a distinction applies. If he says that he specifically designated them as witnesses, he is established as a denier. If not, he is not, because the matter may not have been fixed in the witnesses’ minds. This reconciles the interpretations that the matter was not fixed in the debtor’s mind and that it was not fixed in the witnesses’ minds. If the debtor immediately retracts after naming the witnesses, he need not bring them, because clarification is required only for his benefit, to exempt him even from an oath. This is the conclusion reached in my work from the views of many authorities.
He must clarify his words, etc. The Sema challenges the Rama, arguing that anyone who requires clarification plainly holds that an oath cannot help once the named witnesses contradict the debtor. This is not correct. The Tur first states without qualification that the debtor must clarify his words and later concludes that he remains believed even if the witnesses contradict him. Tosafot and other authorities write likewise.
