That he must clarify. All the poskim agree that where clarification is impossible, such as where the named witnesses have gone away or died, the litigant does not lose merely because he cannot produce them. Rashi in chapter Zeh Borer alone appears to hold that even where clarification has become impossible, for example because the witnesses died, he loses. See Beit Yosef and Knesset HaGedolah. Nevertheless, even according to Rashi, this applies only where he merely says, 'I repaid you before witnesses,' without adding that those witnesses died or left. He must then clarify his assertion by producing them. But if he expressly says, 'I repaid you before so-and-so and so-and-so, and they have died or gone away,' he is certainly believed and need not clarify further, as the Gemara in Shevuot says that if he states, 'I repaid you before so-and-so and so-and-so, and they went overseas,' he is believed.
