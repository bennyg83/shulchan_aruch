[Shulchan Aruch] Before specifically designated witnesses, and they died. Note: If the specifically designated witnesses died and the debtor claims, “I repaid you before two other witnesses, and they also died,” he is not believed by means of the migo that he could have said, “I repaid you before the witnesses whom you designated.” This follows the Ran’s reason, that there is a presumption equivalent to witnesses, and that applies here as well. According to the Rosh’s reason, however, that one who wishes to lie will distance his testimony, here he should be believed, because according to his present claim too he has distanced his testimony. I later saw that Knesset HaGedolah inclines to say that even according to the Rosh he is not believed. See there. If, however, he claims, “I repaid you privately after the designated witnesses died,” he is certainly believed, because it was no longer possible for him to fulfill the condition, as the Taz writes. As to the Taz’s statement that the Beit Yosef chose a different case merely for greater novelty, see there, one can simply say that the Mechaber is speaking where we know that from the time the designated witnesses died he did not repay. This is straightforward.
