See Responsa Zikhron Yosef, Choshen Mishpat, at the end of siman 1, who writes that this is not limited to an ordinary deposit. Even if he expressly says that he is placing the money with the creditor as a deposit so that the creditor will be secured for his debt and may acquire it only after the due date, if the money is lost during the term the loss remains the debtor's and he must pay again. On this basis Zikhron Yosef discusses Reuven, who had to place his daughter's dowry in escrow. Reuven and the intended groom together deposited with Shimon a sealed bundle bearing both their signatures. The money was later lost through unavoidable accident while in the escrow holder's possession. The groom demanded that Reuven provide a new dowry. Reuven answered that he had undertaken only one dowry, not two, that the groom had agreed to the escrow arrangement, and perhaps the groom's fortune caused the loss. The ruling was for the groom. The groom acquires no legal hold in the dowry until after the wedding, so any loss occurring beforehand falls solely upon the father-in-law. Indeed, even if the groom were entirely trustworthy in the father-in-law's eyes and the father-in-law had placed the dowry with the groom himself as a deposit until after the wedding, when he would acquire it, an unavoidable loss before the wedding would not cause the groom to lose his entitlement. This follows from Bach, Sema, and Shach in siman 70, seif 5, regarding a debtor who gives the amount of the debt to the creditor as a deposit. Likewise, where a father-in-law places the dowry with the groom before the wedding, we are witnesses to the fact that he did not give it as payment but only as a deposit to be acquired after the wedding. What sensible person would pay a dowry to his prospective son-in-law thirty days before the wedding when no one knows what the next day may bring? If, even when the money was deposited with the groom himself, the loss falls on the father-in-law, then certainly where the dowry was deposited with a third party, even in the groom's presence and with his signature, the entire loss falls on the father-in-law, who must provide a replacement dowry before the wedding in the amount stated in their original conditions.
