The Sema writes that if the lender explicitly said at the time he received the money that he accepted it only as a deposit, that declaration would be effective. Although ordinarily we rule that repayment made against the creditor's will is still valid repayment, this case is different because the lender had stipulated that payment be made specifically before named witnesses. The Taz already disagreed, and Chacham Tzvi agreed with him as a matter of law. This is correct. The purpose of the condition was only to prevent the borrower from later claiming that he had paid. If the borrower is willing to trust the lender not to collect twice, why should the lender care? Nevertheless, if at the moment of receipt the lender says that he accepts the money as a deposit and the borrower remains silent, his silence constitutes agreement. According to all opinions the money then remains only a deposit. In this manner the words of Rashi in Shevuot 41b, s.v. keivan, are resolved. See there.
