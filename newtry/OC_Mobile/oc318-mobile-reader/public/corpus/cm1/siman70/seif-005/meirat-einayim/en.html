And the matters that this person says, etc. Even if the creditor says that when he received the money he intended to receive it only as a deposit, this is merely an unexpressed intention, and unexpressed intentions have no legal effect. So writes Rashi. If, however, when receiving the money he expressly said that he was accepting it as a deposit and it was then lost through unavoidable accident, the debt was not repaid. This is not comparable to the law in siman 120, where even if the creditor expressly says, 'I will not accept this as repayment,' and the debtor throws the money before him as repayment, the debtor is discharged. Here the creditor can say that so long as the witnesses have not appeared, it is as though the time for repayment has not yet arrived.
