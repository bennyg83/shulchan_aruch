And the words this one says. If at the time he receives the money the lender expressly says, "I receive this only as a deposit," that declaration can be effective. Although forced repayment ordinarily counts as repayment, this case differs because the borrower had previously accepted the condition that he would repay only before particular witnesses. Sema. The Taz objects that the lender's intent in the condition was surely only to prevent a later false plea of payment. If the borrower trusts the lender not to sue him twice, why should the lender object to receiving payment? Nevertheless, if the lender says at the time he receives it that he accepts it only as a deposit and the borrower remains silent, the silence is treated as consent, and everyone agrees it remains merely a deposit.
