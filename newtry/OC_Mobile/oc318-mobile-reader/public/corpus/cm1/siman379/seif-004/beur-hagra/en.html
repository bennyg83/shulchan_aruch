Even, etc. This follows the Rambam's position in the preceding siman that even where entry was permitted, liability requires intent. The Raavad disagrees and limits the exemption to one who entered without permission, following Rashi's approach. The Tur appears inconsistent because in the previous siman he follows the Rambam, while here he agrees with the Raavad. I already explained that the Tur's text there is corrupt and that he in fact follows Rashi. According to that approach, even when entry was unauthorized the exemption applies specifically where the person did not know of the other's presence. The baraisa concerning one filling his courtyard is different because the entrant is lawfully enforcing his own right.
(Collected note) He may enter, etc., and therefore, etc. The wording "he may break and enter" indicates that this specific case is intended. It follows the Rambam's view that even one acting with permission is liable only for intentional damage, as explained in the preceding siman. The other authorities disagree, but here the entrant is exercising self-help lawfully. (End of note.)
