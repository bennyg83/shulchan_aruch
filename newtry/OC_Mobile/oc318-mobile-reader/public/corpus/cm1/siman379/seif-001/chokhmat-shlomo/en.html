Seif 1. "Two people who were walking in the public domain, etc." Note: See Shach, seif katan 1, on the case "if he stopped in order to adjust his load." This comment was marked here by mistake and belongs under seif 3. The Shach's challenge to the Yam Shel Shlomo is, in my view, difficult. In seif 2, where the beam-carrier was walking first and stopped to adjust the beam, how can we hold him liable merely because the barrel-carrier did not know that he had stopped for that purpose? What difference does the second person's lack of knowledge make to the beam-carrier? The beam-carrier was not negligent, because he was compelled to stop in order to adjust his load. Even if he was preoccupied and gave no warning, that need not make him negligent, and he is exempt. Here, however, where the barrel-carrier was first, liability of the beam-carrier would rest on the claim that he was negligent and should have stopped. In that setting he can properly defend himself by saying that he did not know the barrel-carrier had stopped to adjust his load and thought that he had stopped to rest. He was therefore not negligent and is exempt. The two cases are not comparable at all, and the Shach's words are difficult in my view. Examine carefully.
