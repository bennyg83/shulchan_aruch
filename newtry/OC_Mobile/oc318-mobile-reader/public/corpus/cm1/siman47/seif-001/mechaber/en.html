One Who Claims That a Document Is a Document of Trust or Has Been Paid. Two seifim. If a creditor says that a document he holds is a document of trust or has been paid, but he owes others and has no property from which to pay except that document, he is not believed if the document grants him credibility and was authenticated in beit din, or if it is held by a third party. Even if his debt to others was unknown when he made the admission and became known only later, he is not believed, because his admission harms others. This remains so even if he also harms himself, as where the document is for two hundred and he owes others only one hundred. If after calling the document a document of trust he pays his creditor and then seeks to collect from the borrower, we examine the circumstances. If he made that claim only when his creditor sought collection from this document, he clearly intended merely to delay his creditor and may afterward collect upon it. If he admitted spontaneously, without any creditor demanding payment, that it was a document of trust, he may not later collect. See also siman 4.
