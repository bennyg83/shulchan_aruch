Or paid. Even one who sold a promissory note is not believed to say that it was paid, and all the more so here, where the obligation to the other party takes effect by Torah law. The matter is straightforward, since his statement causes a loss to others. A proof concerning a sale is from Bava Metzia 19b: "The Rabbis taught: one who found a receipt," etc., and the Gemara asks that perhaps, etc. If she could make the claim independently, she could in any event claim that it was paid. Raavad. One cannot answer that at that stage the Gemara assumed that she cannot waive the debt, whereas after concluding that she can waive it she can likewise claim payment, for there remains a proof for our case, since here he cannot waive it.
(Collected note) And he has no, etc. See siman 86, where it is explained that he cannot pay him if he has assets.
And it states in it. For otherwise the creditor would have to take an oath, while the debtor claims that it was paid.
(Collected note) And a clause of trust is written in it. The Taz and Shach question this, since if he says, "I paid," the trust clause has thereby been nullified. Rather, there is no distinction: he is obligated to the first creditor, and we are not concerned with his oath. See the Shach, who writes that this applies even if one witness testifies that it was paid.
(Collected note) That it was established, etc. Otherwise he is believed through a migo, as stated at the end of the third chapter of Sanhedrin. Although there we rule that even when it was established he is believed, that is because the other party granted him credibility, as explained there.
(Collected note) And it was established, etc. The Ramban and Maggid Mishneh write that this is unnecessary, because we do not apply a migo against an established presumption, as stated at the beginning of siman 99. The Rashba likewise writes this in responsum 1047 and proves it from Tosafot's difficulty there, since he could have waived the debt. See siman 86, seif 5. The Rosh, Tur, and Shulchan Aruch, however, hold that this is not as strong a presumption as one concerning property, and this is also the view of the other poskim.
Even if he thereby causes a loss. This is because of concern for collusion.
