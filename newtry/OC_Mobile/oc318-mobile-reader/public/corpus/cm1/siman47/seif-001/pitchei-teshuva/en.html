See Baer Heitev, who cites the Shach as holding that even if the document contains no credibility clause, etc. Responsa Shevut Yaakov, part I, siman 154, disagrees with the Shach and writes that no one should presume to rule as he does in order to extract money against the great early authorities, Baal HaTerumos and the Tur, especially when their view is codified in Shulchan Aruch. The Shach's proofs are not compelling. No proof can be brought from the unqualified wording of the Rambam, Semag, or the Gemara in Kesubos. He concludes that the Shach's words are not sufficient to displace the rulings of the early authorities, particularly to extract from the party in possession. However, one may require the lender to take a severe oath, as where he claims, 'I have nothing,' as explained in Beit Yosef siman 47 and the Sema there. The borrower, however, perhaps cannot require the lender to swear that he was repaid, because the lender supports the borrower's claim and exempts him from an oath. The lender is not considered financially interested, since ultimately the document remains collectible from him. The borrower, by contrast, cannot exempt the lender because the borrower certainly is interested, since he seeks to free himself from payment. All of this appears clear to me. End of quote.
