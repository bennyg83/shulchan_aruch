Seif 1. 'A lender who says regarding the document in his possession that it is a trust document or has been paid,' etc. Note: I was asked by the distinguished Rabbi Baruch of Maklinetz, while he was in Barzan, why, where there is one witness that the document was paid, the lender is not believed to say that it was paid. Since the debt is subject to Rabbi Natan's lien, why, if the lender has no other assets, should he not be believed with the force of one witness, just as in the laws of a guarantor the borrower may testify for the guarantor? I answered that with every witness there is technically room to suspect falsehood, but we rely upon his presumption of fitness. Here, however, by saying that he retained a paid document in his possession, he admits violating 'Do not let injustice dwell in your tent.' His presumption of fitness is therefore weakened, and we suspect that he is lying. It is thus a case of conflicting presumptions, since the principle 'Why is your document in my possession?' contradicts his words, and such a presumption is comparable to one witness. He therefore does not have the status of a valid witness, because the presumption arising from the document in his possession contradicts his statement. Analyze this carefully.
