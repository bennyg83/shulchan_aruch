He is not believed. The Rosh, rule 79, siman 11, writes that if a person says concerning debt documents in his possession, 'These documents are designated for Levi, who lent me money,' his designation is ineffective. Likewise, if money is found in his possession, he cannot say, 'It belongs to Levi, who lent it to me, and it remains intact,' because a loan is given for expenditure. Even if the identical coins remain, for legal purposes they are treated as though expended, for example regarding acquisition of land, as explained in simanim 204 and 60:16. They are considered his. But if merchandise or business capital is found in his possession, he is believed to say, 'It belongs to so-and-so from business funds he entrusted to me,' where it is known he received business capital from others, because he has a migo that he could have handed it to that person. The Rosh says so and Rama cites it below in siman 59. Beit Yosef and Darkei Moshe cite this here. It appears specifically where he says the money in his possession belongs to so-and-so because that person lent it to him do we reject the claim, for the reason above. But if he says, 'This money belongs to so-and-so, who deposited it with me,' and it is known that he had deposits from others, he is believed by the migo that he could have handed it over, just like the merchandise case. A proof appears from Even HaEzer 75, where the Tur and Mechaber rule that if a woman says of money found under her control that it belongs to so-and-so, she is believed even while her husband protests that it is his, because she could have given it away. There the money is known to be under her control. This differs from the rule below in siman 99:3 that a debtor cannot remove property from a creditor's possession merely by claiming it belongs to another, because the creditor is already in possession. Another proof appears from Mordechai at the end of Na'arah Shenitpetah, where a husband is believed to say he purchased certain clothes for his wife and thereby defeats his creditor. One could distinguish that clothing is specially associated with the wife and therefore the claim has some support. Nevertheless, the migo itself appears sufficient in all these cases.
