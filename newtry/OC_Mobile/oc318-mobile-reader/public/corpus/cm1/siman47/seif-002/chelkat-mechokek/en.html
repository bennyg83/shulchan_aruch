One witness says she was betrothed and one witness says she was not betrothed. The same applies if the woman herself alone contradicts the witness who says she was betrothed; she is believed, because a single witness who is contradicted has no legal force. Here the case can even be one where she herself is silent, such as where one witness says her father accepted kiddushin for her and the other says he did not. We set one witness against the other and maintain her prior presumption of being unmarried, so she may marry initially. Rambam, Hilchot Ishut 9:31, however, holds that where a witness supports her, the case can in one respect be worse than where she has no supporting witness. The distinction drawn here, between a witness who says there never was any possibility of kiddushin and a witness who says the kiddushin were closer to him, so that she should not marry initially, is Tosafot's view and is cited by the Tur. Raavad, Ramban, and Rashba hold that even where the witness says there were no kiddushin at all, she should not marry initially if she herself knows nothing. The Rosh too states the law without citing Tosafot's leniency. It is surprising that the Mechaber did not cite their stringent view here.
