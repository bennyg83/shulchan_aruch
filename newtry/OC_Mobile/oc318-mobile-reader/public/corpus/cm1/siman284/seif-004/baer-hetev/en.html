It was heard. The Kesef Mishneh, in chapter 6 of Hilchot Nachalot, asks that here it appears that two elements are required: witnesses must testify to the circumstances and they must afterward hear that he died. Yet regarding witnesses who testified that he drowned before them in water, the Rambam did not write that they must afterward hear that he died. He leaves the matter requiring study. See there. It is possible, with some difficulty, to say that the Rambam holds that whenever all trace of a person is lost, a report that he died ordinarily spreads, and therefore he did not need to state this in the first case. Here he states it as a greater novelty: even if they did not hear that he died until after they testified to circumstances of this kind, where one might have thought that the report spread only because of their testimony, nevertheless the heirs may take possession of the estate. Certainly this is so if the report was heard before they testified to those circumstances. Ibid.
