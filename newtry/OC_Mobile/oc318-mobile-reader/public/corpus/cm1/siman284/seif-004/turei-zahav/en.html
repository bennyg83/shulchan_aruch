(And afterward it was heard that he died.) Kesef Mishneh asks that the language implies that two elements are required, whereas immediately before Rambam states the same law based solely on disappearance without any report of death, and he leaves the matter unresolved. The Sema answers that even initially, where all trace of him was lost, there was presumably also a report that he had died. The latter clause teaches that even though one could say the report spread only because of these witnesses’ testimony, the heirs nevertheless descend to the estate. According to this explanation, a report of death is still required. This is not indicated by Rambam’s language. If it were essential, he should have mentioned it in the first clause. In my view Rambam’s word “and afterward” means “or afterward,” as we frequently find in the language of the authorities and Gemara. His intent is that if witnesses testify concerning circumstances that carry a presumption of death and also say that afterward all trace of him was lost, or if they do not testify about disappearance but a report was heard that he died, either is effective.
