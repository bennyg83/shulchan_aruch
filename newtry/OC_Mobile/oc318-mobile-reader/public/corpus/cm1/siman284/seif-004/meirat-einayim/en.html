And afterward it was heard that he died. This too is the language of the Rambam and Tur. The Kesef Mishneh questions it, because the wording implies that two things are required: witnesses must testify to circumstances that ordinarily lead to death, and afterward it must also be heard that he died. Yet immediately before, where witnesses testified that he drowned before them in water, the Rambam did not require that it later be heard that he died. He leaves the matter in difficulty. In the Perishah I wrote that one can answer with some difficulty that whenever all trace of the person was lost, a report of death ordinarily follows, and therefore the Rambam did not need to state it in the first case. Here he stated it as a greater novelty: even if the report of death arose only after the witnesses testified to circumstances that ordinarily result in death, and one might have thought the report spread merely because of their testimony, nevertheless the heirs may descend to the estate. Certainly this is so if the report of death had already circulated before the testimony about the dangerous circumstances.
