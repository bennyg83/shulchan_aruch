[Shulchan Aruch] But if they heard that he died. Note: Beit Yosef alludes to the discussion in Tashbetz from the end of siman 70 through siman 83. There, in simanim 77 and 83, he writes that where one witness testifies that the person died, we place the heirs into the estate because this is a matter that is likely to become known. The case in Ketubot 107a, however, is where the single witness said that he heard that the person died, which is testimony from one witness through another. This is also the case discussed by Rambam, whose wording is the same as the Mechaber here. Since such a witness can evade responsibility by saying he merely repeated what he heard, his testimony is not accepted by Torah law, and is accepted only for testimony concerning a woman because of the concern for an agunah, but not for inheritance. The same applies to a non-Jew speaking incidentally. If two non-Jews each speak incidentally, independently of one another, it may be said that the heirs are placed into the estate. See there. Nevertheless, as a matter of practical halachah, all the authorities appear to hold that in every case a single witness is insufficient to place heirs into an estate. See the gloss to Shulchan Aruch above, at the end of siman 50.
