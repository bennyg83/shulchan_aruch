Although they permit his wife to marry on the basis of such testimony, etc. The reason is that only regarding a woman's remarriage did the Sages initially act leniently because of the severe consequences imposed if she later proves to have remarried wrongly. She therefore will not remarry until she investigates carefully whether the report is true. For the same reason the Sages permitted remarriage on the testimony of a single witness, a slave, or a maidservant, as explained in Even HaEzer siman 17. That reasoning does not justify removing money from its current possession and giving it to heirs. Conversely, in the next law, concerning one who fell into water from which one cannot ordinarily escape and similar cases, the Sages allow the heirs to take possession but do not permit the wife to remarry. In those cases there is a strong presumption of death, so the Sages were not stringent for monetary law. With remarriage, however, that very presumption creates a concern that the wife will rely on it and no longer investigate thoroughly. Because the possible prohibition is a severe one carrying karet, the Sages required fuller investigation before allowing remarriage. See the Perishah.
