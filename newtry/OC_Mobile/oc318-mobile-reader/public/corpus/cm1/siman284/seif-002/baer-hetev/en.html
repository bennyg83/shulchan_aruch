She certainly will not remarry until she carefully investigates whether the report is true, as stated in Even HaEzer, siman 17; see there. This does not apply to removing property from the possession of the missing person and giving it to his heirs. As for the nearby ruling that they were lenient where one fell into water without a visible end, and in similar cases, permitting heirs to take possession of the estate on such evidence although a woman may not remarry on it, the reason is that in all such cases there is a presumption of death, and the Sages were not stringent regarding monetary law. Regarding a woman's remarriage, however, they were more stringent precisely because the circumstances create a presumption of death, lest she rely upon that presumption and cease investigating. Because the prohibition involved carries the severe penalty of karet, the Sages required more investigation, inquiry, and examination before she remarries. These are the words of the Sema. See Responsa R. Yosef of the House of Levi, end of siman 11, Responsa Rashach, part II, siman 65, and part III, siman 116.
