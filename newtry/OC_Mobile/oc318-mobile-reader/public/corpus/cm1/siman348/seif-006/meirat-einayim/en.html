And the thief lifted the stolen object. Meaning, by lifting it the thief acquired it into his legal responsibility, and he could have removed it even without the person who assisted him. The theft was already complete without the helper. The Rosh concludes, and the Tur cites him, that when we say regarding one who robbed and the owner did not despair, and another then robbed from the first robber, that the owner may collect from whichever he chooses, that applies specifically where the second robber took the object from the first. Since the owner had not despaired, it is as though the second robbed directly from the owner. But where the second did not come to rob, and merely assisted after the first had already acquired the theft by lifting it, he is not liable as a robber. The Rosh gives another reason for exemption where the helper did not know it was stolen.
