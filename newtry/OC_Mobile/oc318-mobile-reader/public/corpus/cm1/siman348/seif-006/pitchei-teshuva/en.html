Payment for theft. Shiltei HaGibborim, at the end of the first chapter of Bava Batra, derives from Bava Kamma 78b that where one vowed to bring an olah, designated an ox, and another stole it, the thief may discharge his liability by supplying a sheep. He infers that if one steals an item from a person who himself had borrowed that item, and the borrower then settles with the lender for a small amount, the thief need pay only the amount of that settlement. He raises a further possibility where the borrowed item belonged to a non-Jew. Mishneh LaMelech initially sought to derive from that Gemara that if one steals another's expensive etrog before the festival, the thief might discharge himself by giving another kosher etrog sufficient for the mitzvah even though it is less valuable. He then rejects the proof, because the designated sacrificial ox has no ordinary market value to the owner, since sale of an olah or shelamim is ineffective. The thief's obligation there is therefore only to replace what the person needs to fulfill his vow, and a sheep suffices. An etrog, by contrast, has monetary value, and its owner can say that he could have sold it for its full price. Chacham Tzvi, siman 120, writes similarly, as does Beit Shmuel Acharon, Choshen Mishpat siman 5. Mishneh LaMelech concludes that the words of Shiltei HaGibborim regarding the borrowed item are difficult. Shaar Mishpat, above siman 72 seif katan 12, likewise cites and rejects Shiltei HaGibborim, ruling that even if the item belonged to a Jew, the thief must pay its full value. See there.
