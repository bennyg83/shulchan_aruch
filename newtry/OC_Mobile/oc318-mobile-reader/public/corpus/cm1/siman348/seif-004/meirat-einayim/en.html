When is a thief liable for double payment, etc. See the Tur, who writes that liability for double payment and fourfold or fivefold payment applies where witnesses testify that he stole and slaughtered or sold. If he admits on his own, he pays only the principal, because Scripture excludes one who condemns himself. Even if he admits to the penalty and witnesses later arrive, he is exempt, provided his admission first imposed some principal liability upon himself. Thus, if he admitted that he stole, slaughtered, and sold, he obligated himself in the principal before the witnesses came. But if he never admitted the theft before witnesses established it, and after they testified to the theft he quickly admitted the slaughter or sale, and witnesses later testified to the slaughter or sale, he is liable for fourfold or fivefold payment. An admission to a penalty exempts only if made before a beit din of three expert judges sitting as a beit din. Admission before two experts, even while sitting in judgment, or before three outside a beit din setting, does not exempt. Therefore nowadays, when we lack a beit din of experts authorized to judge penalties, an admission does not exempt from the penalty. The Tur records many additional details of double payment and fourfold or fivefold liability in simanim 350 through 352. The Mechaber omitted them because such penalty law is not ordinarily adjudicated today.
Nevertheless there is a practical difference if he seized, etc. The Tur likewise writes at the end of siman 349 that this is why he records some details of these penalty laws. Perhaps the Mechaber follows the Ramah, cited there by the Tur, who holds that seizure of a penalty is ineffective, not even up to the amount of the damage and certainly not for the additional double, fourfold, or fivefold amount. The Sages made an enactment for the injured party to recover his damage, but not to profit beyond it. Therefore the Mechaber omitted the details. Rama follows the Rosh, who disagrees. The Rosh holds that the liability exists by Torah law, but we lack judges empowered to compel payment. In such a case a person may take the law into his own hands and seize up to the amount of the double, fourfold, or fivefold obligation, and we do not remove it from him.
