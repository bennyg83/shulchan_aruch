If it is guarded, etc. This requires study, because if he stands beside his field that should suffice, as stated on Bava Metzia 11a.
(Supplement) If it is guarded. The rule is derived from lost property, where a garden or ruin acquires only if guarded. But the analogy is incomplete: there the owner does not know of the lost item, whereas here his courtyard acquires for him when he stands beside it. (End of supplement.)
Nevertheless, etc. This follows Rosh and Rif, who permit seizure in such circumstances. Ramah disagrees, which likely explains the Shulchan Aruch's omission. The Gemara appears to support Ramah, because seizure is mentioned only for half-damages and comparable penalties, and Babylonian courts enforced such claims only through seizure.
(Supplement) Nevertheless, there are those who, etc. Rif and Rosh rule that if the claimant seized first, the seizure is effective, unlike Ramah. (End of supplement.)
