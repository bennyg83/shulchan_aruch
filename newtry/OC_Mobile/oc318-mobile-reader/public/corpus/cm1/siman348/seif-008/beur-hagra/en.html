(Supplement) It is as though he removed it himself. He is treated as the thief's agent and is liable, as stated in Bava Metzia 8a. Although the agent did not intend to acquire the stolen object for himself but for the principal, the same rule applies. Rashi's statement that he is exempt from double payment means only from the penalty; the principal amount remains payable because the object entered his control. This is the meaning of, 'Reuven may collect from whichever one, etc.' (End of supplement.)

(Supplement) Nevertheless, if the agent, etc. According to Terumat HaDeshen, one who merely points out property is exempt because the damage is indirect. This differs from showing Jewish property to a non-Jew or an informer, where the damage is certain. Here the thief may not steal. Ri likewise holds that liability for indirect damage requires certain or immediate damage, and according to Ritzba it must at least be a common occurrence. This case is not sufficiently common. Accordingly, even if the agent is not personally subject to the prohibition, he is exempt in such a case. (End of supplement.)
