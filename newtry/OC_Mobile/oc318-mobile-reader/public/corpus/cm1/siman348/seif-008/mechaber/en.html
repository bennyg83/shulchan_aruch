Reuven claimed that Shimon entered his room, stole his books, and removed them. Shimon admitted removing them but claimed that Reuven's daughter-in-law asked him because she could not carry them, that he did not know whose they were, and that she lifted and handed them to him. Shimon must return them. He had no right to enter the room, and it is evident he intended to assist in stealing from her father-in-law. Even if she lifted them, he admits they were too heavy for her to carry, and without his help they could not have been removed, so it is as though he removed them. Reuven may collect from whichever he chooses, either the daughter-in-law who admits possession or Shimon. Rama: If a thief hid stolen property and had to leave town so he could not remove it, then sent another person who knowingly brought it, the messenger must pay as the principal thief. One who merely points out property to steal or sends another to steal is not liable because there is no agency for a transgression, though some impose liability if the agent is not legally accountable.
