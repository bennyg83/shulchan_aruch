To become involved. Meaning, even if his relative had some claim explaining why she took the item, since he himself did not know whether her claim was true, he should not have become involved in the matter. This applies even though there were no witnesses to the removal, as implied by Shimon's response, "It is true that I removed it," which indicates that without his admission Reuven could not have proven the matter through witnesses. In the preceding ruling, however, Shimon did not become involved in assisting the theft; Reuven merely sued him for returning to the thief an item that had come into Shimon's possession. Although the Tur implies that in that incident too he entered and assisted in removing it, the Tur gives other reasons to exempt him: the thief could have removed it without him, and Shimon claimed that he knew the thief's claim was true. See there. Sema.
He is liable. We cannot say here that the sender assumed the agent would not carry out his request because "the words of the Master and the words of the student, whose words should be obeyed?" since the agent himself is not personally subject to the prohibition. The Darkei Moshe cites Mordechai, first chapter of Bava Metzia, in the name of Maharam: if one says, "Take for me an ox in a certain house; it is mine," and it is later found that the ox was not his and that he intended to steal it, the sender is liable for the ox once the agent performs meshichah, because the agent did not know that it was stolen. The Darkei Moshe further cites Nimukei Yosef in chapter Merubah as holding that the agent is liable, because there is no agency for a transgression even where the agent did not know he was committing a prohibition. These are the words of the Sema. The Shach writes: I already explained in siman 182 that the Rama's inference from Hagahot Maimoniyot and Mordechai is not conclusive. I concluded there as practical law that there is no distinction whether the agent is personally subject to the prohibition or not. There is never agency for a transgression, except in the special case of a courtyard acting as one's agent. See there. As to the Darkei Moshe's statement here in the name of Nimukei Yosef that the agent is liable, this too is incorrect. Nimukei Yosef states only that the sender is nevertheless exempt; he gives no basis for saying that the agent himself is liable. Maharshal, chapter Merubah, siman 33, explicitly states that according to all opinions the agent is exempt where he did not know. See there. He criticizes Nimukei Yosef, though in my view that criticism is unwarranted. See there at length. See also Responsa Maharam of Lublin, siman 81.
