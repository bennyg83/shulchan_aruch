Or in order to cause him distress. The prohibition against theft includes taking an object merely to distress the owner, even if the taker intends later to return it. Shitah Mekubetzet cites an opinion limiting this to one who actually intends to retain the stolen property, but Rambam's Sefer HaMitzvot states explicitly that even one who takes merely to pain the owner and afterward intends to restore it violates the prohibition. Accordingly, the common practice of taking another's property as a prank requires care. I was uncertain whether such a person also acquires the full liability of a thief for unavoidable accidents. Shitah Mekubetzet distinguishes a robber who takes in order not to repay from a custodian who misappropriates intending to repay. Its language implies that one who takes only temporarily, intending to restore the very object, may violate the prohibition without assuming all the thief's accident liability. The matter therefore requires study.
