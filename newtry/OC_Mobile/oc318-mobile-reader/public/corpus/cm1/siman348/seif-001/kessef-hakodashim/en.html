To pay double payment. The Tur at the beginning of the laws of theft writes that one may not steal even with the intention of later paying double, and explains that the reason is so that one should not accustom himself to theft. According to the view that the case is where he did not first confer ownership upon the other through a third party, there is no need to make the prohibition depend solely on self-discipline. If he effectively transfers an equivalent item to the owner through another person, the concern of loss may disappear, since equal fungible items can be allocated in partnership without the other partner's knowledge. If the taker already has a debt claim against the owner, perhaps formal transfer through a third party is unnecessary where there is no realistic concern of loss, especially under the principle that each creditor may collect from the other and that we compel against the trait of Sodom where one benefits and the other loses nothing. Still, in matters of interest and similar prohibitions, formal characterization can matter and requires study. In any event, where the owner has not acquired an equivalent, it is not certain that the transaction is to his benefit, because there remains some risk of loss, and the Torah therefore prohibited the act categorically. Since we generally do not derive operative exceptions from the stated reason of a verse, even one who is personally certain that the owner would benefit may not permit himself to steal on that basis. And even if the sole reason were to prevent habituation, one could not say, 'I know myself and will do it only once.' The prohibition applies even once.
