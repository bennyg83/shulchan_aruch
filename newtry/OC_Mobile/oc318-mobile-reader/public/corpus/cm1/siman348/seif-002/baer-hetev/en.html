Non-Jews. This implies that theft from a non-Jew is prohibited by Torah law. Maharshal, chapter HaGozel, likewise writes that the Rambam, Tur, and Semag, who use this language, hold that it is a Torah prohibition. This creates difficulty with the Rama in Even HaEzer, siman 28, who rules that if a man betroths a woman with property stolen from a non-Jew, the betrothal is valid because he need return it only due to sanctification of Hashem's Name. The responsum of Mahari Weil, siman 139, from which the Rama derived this, does not appear to say so upon examination. This requires study. See what I wrote at the beginning of siman 359. Shach.
To deceive him. The Sema writes that this is learned from the prohibition against deceiving even a non-Jew. Maharshal, chapter 10 of Bava Kamma, siman 20, writes that evasion of a debt is permitted only where the obligation is a tax or debt, but where it arises from a sale it is forbidden. See there, and Responsa Rashakh, part I, siman 190.
