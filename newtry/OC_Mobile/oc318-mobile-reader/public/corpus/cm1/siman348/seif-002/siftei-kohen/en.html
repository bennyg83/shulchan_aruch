Or one who steals money from a non-Jew. This implies that the prohibition is of Torah law. Maharshal likewise writes in Perek HaGozel that Rambam, Tur, and Semag, who use this formulation, hold that it is prohibited by Torah law. This is difficult in light of the Rama's gloss at the beginning of Even HaEzer, siman 28, that if a man betroths a woman with property robbed or stolen from a non-Jew, the betrothal is valid because she need not return it except because of sanctification of Hashem's Name. The Rama derives this there from Responsa Mahari Weil, siman 138, but if one studies Mahari Weil's responsum, it is apparent that this is not what he says. This requires study.
