Above, seif 2, concerning “You shall not steal”. The singular prohibition “You shall not steal” refers to kidnapping, as Rashi explains in Yitro. Yet in Mishpatim Rashi says that the ear which heard at Sinai “You shall not steal” and then went and stole is pierced. This is difficult, since the singular verse concerns kidnapping. One may answer that we interpret it that way only because the Torah also writes the plural “You shall not steal,” and the Gemara derives a matter from its context. Since the singular verse appears among “You shall not murder” and the other capital offenses, it is taken to concern kidnapping. Once one of the two verses must refer to kidnapping, it is more reasonable to assign the singular verse to that offense and the plural verse to theft of money. Had the Torah written only the singular verse, however, it would have been more natural to interpret it as monetary theft. Therefore, a thief who heard only the singular prohibition should have understood it as monetary theft, and if he heard both verses he certainly should have understood that one of them forbids monetary theft.
