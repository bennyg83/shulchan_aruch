But if there is no, etc., for it is considered, etc. Bava Metzia 30b states that one who takes hold of the animal becomes obligated. Although Bava Batra 88a limits a similar rule concerning living creatures, here returning the property to the wrong person's hand is direct destruction. This case is not deliberate abandonment by the owner. Anyone who receives another's property while it is being lost becomes a custodian of lost property and is liable for negligent return. The same principle appears in simanim 72 and 267. Market-overt protection does not apply where the seller is a known thief, and all the more so here. See simanim 72, 267, and 349.
