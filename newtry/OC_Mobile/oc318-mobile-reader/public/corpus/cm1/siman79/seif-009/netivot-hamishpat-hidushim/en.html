But if one says, "I never borrowed," and one witness testifies that he did borrow, according to the Rambam and, in his view, most authorities, with the Rashba almost alone in disagreement, he is not believed to say that he repaid, because he is one who cannot swear against the witness and therefore must pay. This does not apply to matters for which a Torah oath is not imposed, such as documents and rental of houses. If, however, he claims that he paid afterward, he is believed and, according to all opinions, is not established as a denier, as stated in Bava Metzia 71:
Since both claims come to exempt him. But if he first said, "I am liable," and later said, "I paid," he is not believed, because the two claims do not both seek to exempt him. An exception is where he first said, "I do not know," and later says, "I remembered." In that case there need be no contradiction, because forgetfulness is common:

However, if he first said, "I never borrowed," witnesses then came and testified that he borrowed, and he responded, "I paid," but those witnesses were subsequently disqualified and only afterward other witnesses arrived, his admission is nevertheless deemed one made after the arrival of witnesses, and he is established as a denier:

If he said, "I repaid you." Since he was not established as a denier, he may claim that he repaid afterward. But if he denied in beit din and witnesses then came, he is established as a denier and is no longer believed. See the Beurim. This applies specifically when he now claims that he paid later, after he had been sued in beit din.
