And two witnesses came and contradicted him. A single witness who contradicts him does not make him an established denier according to any opinion. Nevertheless, according to Rambam, one witness may render him obligated to swear and unable to swear, so that he must pay. Rashba disagrees. The Tur writes here that even if a single witness contradicts him, he may swear and be exempt. The Sema says this follows Rashba. My revered teacher correctly noted that this would make the Tur self-contradictory, and the Tur nowhere expressly cites Rashba’s view. The better explanation is that here the witness arrived only after the defendant had already changed to a claim of payment, so he moved from one exempting claim to another. The Bach also writes this. Alternatively, even if the witness arrived before the second claim, the Tur is not discussing whether the defendant must pay as one unable to swear. He is teaching only that one witness does not make him an established denier. Thus, if he later claims payment, he is believed. Baal HaTerumot expressly says that although one witness may create the rule of one obligated to swear who cannot swear, he still does not establish the defendant as a denier. The Tur’s later phrase that he swears heset regarding payment or ignorance refers to ordinary cases where he does not contradict the witness. This also answers the Bach’s objection concerning a claim of ignorance.

Because he saw witnesses approaching, etc. The Sema notes that the Tur likewise records this rule at the end of siman 80 in the name of Ri Migash and Rambam, and wonders why the Beit Yosef and Darkei Moshe present it as a newly found ruling. There is no difficulty. The Tur does not expressly say that the witnesses had not yet arrived before the second claim. Moreover, the source quoted by the Beit Yosef and Darkei Moshe explicitly says that even if he merely saw witnesses preparing to enter, his retraction is attributed to fear of them. This is also the Rama’s intent. My revered father similarly wrote in the margin of his Sema. I noted above at the end of seif katan 14 that a responsum of the Rosh and Rabbeinu Yerucham disagree with this source, so the Rama’s ruling requires study.
Unless the rumor was widely known, etc. Darkei Moshe does not distinguish between a widely known rumor and any other rumor. It cites in the name of Tosafot Shitah that if one first says, “I never borrowed,” and later says, “I repaid,” because there was a rumor in the city that he had borrowed, his retraction is accepted, since he changed his claim on his own initiative. Nor is a distinction based on the publicity of the rumor inherently persuasive. This is not comparable to siman 72, seif 18. There the defendant relies upon a migo that he returned the property. If there is a widespread rumor that he did not return it, he is afraid to make that claim and the migo is weakened. Here, however, he already advanced the original denial. Perhaps the Rama can be defended because changing from one exempting claim to another is itself weak, as Ri Migash implies, and is accepted only through the migo that he could have maintained his original claim. If the rumor was widespread, he had reason not to maintain that first claim, and the migo disappears. Still, the matter requires study. Since he in fact made the original claim despite the rumor and would have been exempt had he maintained it, one may say that the migo remains. A rumor alone does not extract money.
