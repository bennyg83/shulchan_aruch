And two witnesses came and contradicted him. If only one witness contradicted him, everyone agrees that he does not become established as a false denier and may say that he later repaid. Nevertheless, the Rambam and Rashba disagree about the law where one witness contradicts him. According to the Rambam, although one witness does not make him a false denier, if he first claimed, "There was never any such transaction," and one witness then says that he borrowed, even if the witness does not know whether he repaid, when the defendant changes his claim to, "I borrowed and repaid," he is one who is obligated to swear but cannot swear, and therefore pays. The Tur writes this in siman 75:14, and the Mechaber likewise rules there in seif 13. The Rashba, however, holds that we do not say in such a case that he is obligated to swear and cannot swear. With two witnesses, the monetary liability arises because he became established as a false denier after saying, "I did not borrow," while witnesses say that he did. Once established as a false denier, he is no longer believed to say that he repaid even though the witnesses do not know whether he did. One witness, however, cannot establish him as a false denier. Since that witness does not know that he failed to repay, the defendant is believed upon an oath of hesset to retract and say, "Yes, I borrowed and repaid," just as though the witness had never testified, because without testimony he could retract from "I never borrowed" to "I borrowed and repaid." The Tur here likewise writes that even if one witness contradicted him, he may swear and be exempt, following the Rashba, as I explained in the Derishah. Darkei Moshe also appears to understand it this way. The Mechaber omitted the Tur's statement here concerning one witness because he rules like the Rambam, as stated in siman 75:13. See there.

If the denial was not made in beit din, etc. A person may withhold his true claim until he appears in beit din, as the Tur and Mechaber state in siman 80.
But if he said, "I repaid you afterward," etc. In this respect there is a difference between one who said before witnesses, "You are my witnesses," and one whose denial was contradicted in beit din. Where his denial was contradicted in beit din, even if he says, "I repaid afterward," he is not believed, as stated in seif 5. The reason is that his initial claim in beit din established him as a false denier, and once a person is established as a false denier concerning particular money, he is no longer believed regarding that money. Here, by contrast, he is not called an established false denier. Rather, he is merely unable to retract his original assertion and say that he had already repaid before then, because he accepted in the presence of witnesses that at that time he had not repaid. This is clear.
