He is established as a denier. In the glosses of Rabbi Akiva Eiger, of blessed memory: If one claimed, 'You owe me one hundred that I lent you,' and the defendant said, 'I never borrowed from any person,' and witnesses then testified that he borrowed but they do not know whether it was from this claimant or another person, he is considered established as a denier and is liable to pay this claimant. So it appears to me from the Yerushalmi, fourth chapter of Shevuot, halachah 5. Apparently the same should follow if the witnesses clearly testify that he borrowed from another person, and this requires study.
