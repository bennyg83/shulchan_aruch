(Seif 12: When does this apply? When he did not accept.) I wrote in siman 39 that where a debtor remains able to claim payment, the creditor cannot collect from purchasers because they may say the parties colluded. Where it is clear that payment did not occur, however, an admission before beit din can support collection even from purchasers. Here the rule concerns uncertainty about payment. If nonpayment is established by witnesses or by an admission made while the debtor still had unencumbered property, without concern for collusion, the creditor may collect from purchasers because the judicial act becomes public. The Sema says that where judges imposed a ban on one who failed to attend a fair and pay a handwritten obligation, or arranged installments and later imposed a ban after default, this is not a judicial act sufficient to bar a later payment claim, especially against heirs or purchasers. It appears to me that a judicial act is public. The debtor presumably knew of the ban and of the signed decision given to the creditor. Had he then paid, he should have taken back the writing. As the Rama says in siman 69, the judge must rule according to what his eyes see; the same applies here.
