And the lender cannot. This is obvious, because he himself admits it.
For behold. This reason is unnecessary, as stated in the Rama, for as explained above, whoever says, etc. See the Raavad's glosses. One must construe the clause as meaning that even if he later claims, "I paid," he is not believed. So too writes the Shach. This is why the Shulchan Aruch above says that he denied in beit din, because if he denied before witnesses, even if he said, "you are my witnesses," he is not established as a denier, as stated at the end of seif 9. But if he said, etc. The words of Maggid Mishneh here require study.
