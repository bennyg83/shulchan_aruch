In beit din. Rivash 354 writes that even if the witnesses testified in a different beit din, the litigant becomes established as a false denier. Sema.
Liable. Responsa Mabit II, 16 rules that one who first says, "I never borrowed," and is contradicted by witnesses who prove the loan, may later claim in beit din that he had paid before the first denial if he produces witnesses to that payment. The Shach disagrees and says the authorities do not imply this, rejecting the Mabit's proofs.
More. The Sema discusses why the Rama uses the word "more" although Rambam and Tur do not. The Taz says the point is simple. If the defendant's own admission were only equal in weight to the witnesses, one might balance his admission, which is like two witnesses, against the two actual witnesses and then leave him with his presumption of honesty rather than establish him as a liar. The Rama therefore says his own admission is stronger than witnesses, so it governs and makes his first denial decisive against him.
Oath. This differs from a generally suspect person, whose opponent still cannot collect without an oath under siman 92. There the suspicion arose from another matter. Here the defendant has been established as a false denier concerning this very money. Sema.
Shevuat heset. The Ir Shushan says no heset is imposed because he has been established as a denier regarding this money. The Sema says that rationale is inaccurate. Even without the special status of a false denier, he would not be permitted to swear because his original statement itself admitted that he had not paid: one who says "I never borrowed" is treated as saying "I never paid." The Tur states this explicitly. The Shach adds that if the rationale were merely that he is an established denier, one would still have to explain why a heset cannot be imposed after payment. The Bach writes that if he seeks to impose a general cherem upon whoever unlawfully took his money, beit din should stop him as well. The Shach does not accept that extension. See siman 82, seif 4.
