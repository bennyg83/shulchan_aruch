He must pay, etc. Mabit rules that if the defendant first says, “This never happened,” witnesses then testify that he borrowed, and he subsequently says in beit din, “I had already repaid,” he is believed if he brings witnesses. This does not appear correct. The language of the authorities does not support his interpretation. The Maggid Mishneh’s phrase “until he establishes it by witnesses” means witnesses to a later payment, not witnesses to payment before the denial. The proof from Ramah in siman 80 is likewise no proof, because there the witnesses contradicting the first claim arrived only after the defendant had already advanced his second claim. The plain rule is that once he said, “I never borrowed,” he admitted that he had not paid. An admission by a litigant is equivalent to one hundred witnesses and defeats later testimony that he previously paid. Nimukei Yosef likewise says that one who called a document forged and it was later authenticated cannot then bring witnesses that he had paid, because saying “forged” and “I never borrowed” is equivalent to saying “I did not pay.” The Tur’s rule that if the creditor later says, “You paid me twice,” the defendant is not even required to take a heset oath also presupposes that witnesses proved an earlier payment, yet the defendant remains bound by his first admission and must pay. Therefore, even if he later says he paid and brings witnesses to that effect, he is liable.
For he has been established as a denier. This is Rambam’s wording. Raavad objects that this reason is unnecessary. The Rama therefore adds that anyone who says, “I did not borrow,” is deemed to have said, “I did not repay.” Rambam may have intended to teach that even if the defendant later claims payment, he is not believed.
We do not require him to take a heset oath. The Ir Shushan says this is because he was established as a denier regarding this money. That is imprecise. Even if he were not formally established as a denier, no heset oath would be imposed because he already admitted that he had not paid initially. The Sema could have objected more strongly: if the reason were merely that he became a denier, why would no heset oath be required after he pays, as discussed below in seif katan 18?
