The law of one who says, 'I did not borrow,' and the distinction between, 'Go, give it to him,' and, 'You are obligated to give it to him.' It contains 14 se'ifim.
If one claims, 'I lent you one hundred,' and the defendant denies it in beit din, saying, 'This never occurred,' and two witnesses then testify that he borrowed one hundred and repaid it, while the lender says he was not repaid, the defendant must pay. {Rama: Anyone who says, 'I did not borrow,' is regarded as saying, 'I did not repay.' His admission concerning himself is more credible than one hundred witnesses; his statement that he did not borrow was contradicted by the witnesses.} The lender need not swear, because the defendant has been established as a denier. If, after paying, the defendant is sued again and says, 'I paid you twice,' he is not required to take a heses oath on this claim. Similarly, if the claimant produces the defendant's handwritten acknowledgment of a debt, and the defendant says, 'This never occurred; this is not my handwriting,' then if his handwriting is authenticated in beit din or witnesses testify that it is his handwriting, he is established as a denier and must pay.
