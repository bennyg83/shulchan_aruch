More than one hundred witnesses. For this reason he cannot later be subjected even to a hesset oath. If his own admission were merely equivalent to ordinary witnesses, he could at least demand a hesset oath, because we could set one body of testimony against another and leave an ordinary monetary claim requiring hesset. But because a litigant's own admission is stronger than witnesses regarding his own liability, no such oath can be imposed. Sema.
For he has been established as one who falsely denied. See Tumim. I wrote that he need not even swear that the original loan occurred, and certainly need not swear that it remained unpaid, since he himself admitted that he did not pay by denying the loan. This is unlike a person generally suspected of false oaths, where the opposing party must swear before collecting, because there the person was not established as a liar concerning this very transaction. Here he was established as a liar concerning this debt itself. If he later claims payment, he is likewise not believed, because he has already been established as a false denier regarding this obligation. Shach.
