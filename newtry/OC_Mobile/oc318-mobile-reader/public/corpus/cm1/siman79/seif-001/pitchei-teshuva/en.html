Who borrowed one hundred from him and repaid it. See Responsa Rabbi Akiva Eiger, siman 149. He writes that if witnesses say only, “We saw him give money as payment of a debt,” but did not witness the original loan, one does not say that their testimony to payment itself proves that a loan occurred and therefore should be treated as though they testified both that he borrowed and that he repaid. Since knowledge of the loan derives only from the very act of repayment, if the debtor is believed against the repayment component and that component is disregarded, no independent testimony of the loan remains. The rule that a person’s own admission can nullify the payment testimony while leaving the loan testimony intact applies only where the witnesses independently testify, “He borrowed and later repaid.” Rabbi Akiva Eiger further considers, and ultimately questions, whether the rule that saying “I never borrowed” is treated as saying “I never repaid” applies only where the defendant could otherwise have exempted himself by claiming payment.
