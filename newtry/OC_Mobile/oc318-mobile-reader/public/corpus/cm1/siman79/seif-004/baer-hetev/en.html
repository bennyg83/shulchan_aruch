And so-and-so. The Sema cites Maharshal and finds his distinction reasonable, but the Bach disagrees and holds that naming particular witnesses makes no difference. One becomes an established false denier only when he says, for example, "I counted out one maneh to you," and is contradicted. The Shach agrees with the Bach and notes that Rabbeinu Yeruham likewise uses the formulation "I counted out one maneh to you."
Witnesses. Beit Yosef cites Baal HaTerumot that even "special testimony," where one witness saw the act from one window and another from another window, can establish him as a false denier. Sema.
That he gave them. This differs from witnesses who merely heard him admit that he owed the money. In that situation he may say that he made the admission in jest, as the Tur explains in siman 32. See the beginning of siman 81. Sema.
I was not concerned. This defense is available only because the witnesses did not see the actual loan but merely heard that the money had been given, so liability ultimately rests on his own statement. He can therefore say that he was not concerned about making such an admission. But if witnesses saw the actual loan, he cannot escape through such explanations. The Mechaber in siman 81, seif 12 rules that anyone who saw the loan, even while concealed, may testify. Rivash explains that a loan differs from an admission: an admission may have been playful, while a completed loan is an objective act, and witnesses exist only because of the possibility of later falsehood. Accordingly, witnesses who saw the loan may testify simply that one party owes the other based on what they saw, without concern that he will say, "I was not concerned about my words." Shach.
