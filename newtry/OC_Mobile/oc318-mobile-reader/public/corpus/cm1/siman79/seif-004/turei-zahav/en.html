(Ibid.: “I paid afterward” is believed.) The Tur cites Rabbi Yonah that even a denial outside beit din prevents a later claim of payment. At first glance this is difficult because a person ordinarily need not reveal his legal position outside court. One answer is that the statement was made with “You are my witnesses,” which is effective even outside beit din. More likely, Rabbi Yonah follows his view in siman 146 that an admission outside beit din, where one intentionally states facts to his own advantage but thereby necessarily admits a disadvantage, is as strong as one hundred witnesses. Here he intends to benefit himself by saying he never borrowed, but thereby admits that he did not repay. Thus even without “You are my witnesses,” the implication binds him. Since the Shulchan Aruch accepts Rabbi Yonah’s principle there, it should apply here too, although the ruling was omitted.
