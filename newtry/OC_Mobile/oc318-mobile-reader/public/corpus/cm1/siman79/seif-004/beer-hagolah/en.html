The wording of the Tur, from the incident where Rav Hamnuna sat before Rav Yehudah and Rav Yehudah raised a question, etc.; Rav Hamnuna said to him, etc., and Rav Yehudah told him, "Hamnuna, enter and come," Shevuot 34a and 34b. The Rambam likewise rules so in chapter 6, law 2.
The Rambam explains that this applies where the defendant makes the statement in beit din and the witnesses contradict him, as explained in seif 9. Maharshal writes that this is specifically where the claimant said, "in the presence of so-and-so and so-and-so," because then the defendant should have taken the matter to heart and answered in beit din that the money was a gift, etc. But if the claimant says in beit din, "I lent you a maneh," and the defendant answers, "There never was such a matter," and the claimant then brings witnesses who testify that he counted out a maneh to him in their presence, we listen to the defendant if he returns and says, "That maneh was a gift; when I originally said there never was such a matter, I meant there was no loan." The Sema writes that this reasoning is sound.
