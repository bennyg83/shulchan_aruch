In the presence of so and so. It need not literally be in the presence of a named person. It is enough that the claimant said, "in the presence of witnesses," for then the defendant should have paid attention. But when witnesses were not mentioned at all, a person does not pay close attention to the matter, particularly where there was no loan at all. So writes the Sema:
He is established as a denier. Although below, siman 81, even one who claims that the event never occurred is not thereby established as a denier, there the event consisted only of an admission, and people do not remember idle words. Here, however, even if he did not hear that money was given, he should remember the underlying event itself. Sema and Shach:
But if he later claims. The Bach writes that this applies specifically where the claimant said, "I counted one hundred to you." If he said, "I lent you one hundred," the defendant may respond, "Why should I have paid attention? You described it as a loan, whereas that money was a gift." Shach:
But if he claimed. The Shach holds that this applies specifically where they did not see the loan. If they saw it, even through concealment, an explanation does not help.
