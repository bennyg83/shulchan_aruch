I lent you one hundred in the presence of so and so, etc. Maharshal writes that this applies specifically when the claimant says that the loan occurred before named witnesses. The defendant should then remember what occurred in their presence and explain in beit din that the money was a gift or repayment of another debt. But if the claimant merely says, I lent you one hundred, and the defendant denies any such loan, then witnesses later testify only that they saw one hundred counted out to him, he may explain that those funds were a gift and that his original denial referred only to a loan. The Sema finds this reasoning sound. The Bach disagrees and holds that no distinction depends on whether the witnesses were named; a defendant becomes an established denier only where he specifically denies that any money was counted out to him. I agree with the Bach, and Rabbeinu Yeruham likewise uses the formulation, I counted out one hundred to you.
He is certainly established as a denier. Although in siman 81 an ordinary denial does not necessarily establish him as a liar after a mere prior admission, because people do not always remember casual words, here witnesses actually heard or saw the transfer of money. He should certainly remember such a payment.
But if he claims, etc., his claim is valid. This applies only where the witnesses did not actually see a loan with their own eyes, but merely heard the money being given as a loan. Since his liability then rests on his own statement, he can say that he did not take care in speaking because he did not see witnesses present. But where witnesses actually saw the loan, he cannot escape by such a claim. The Rivash and Mechaber rule in siman 81 that anyone who actually saw the loan may testify even if concealed, because a loan is unlike an admission: an admission can be explained as jesting, but the loan transaction itself was completed immediately, and witnesses are needed only against false denial. Thus witnesses who saw the actual loan may testify directly that the debt exists, without concern that the borrower will say he did not pay attention because he saw no witnesses.
