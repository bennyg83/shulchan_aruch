"I lent you one hundred in the presence of so and so and so and so," etc. Maharshal writes that this applies specifically where the claimant said, "I lent you in the presence of these named witnesses." The defendant should then have paid attention to the transaction that occurred in their presence and responded in beit din that the money was a gift or repayment of another debt. But if the claimant merely says in beit din, "I lent you one hundred," and the defendant responds, "There was never any such transaction," and then the claimant brings witnesses who testify only that they saw him count out one hundred to the defendant, the defendant is heard if he then explains that the money was a gift or repayment of a debt, and that his original statement, "There was never any such transaction," referred only to the asserted loan. Maharshal's words are well reasoned. The Beit Yosef also cites, from the Baal HaTerumot, that even special testimony in which one witness saw from one window and another from another window can establish him as a false denier.
