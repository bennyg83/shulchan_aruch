See Shach, seif katan 13, who writes that the stated excuse works only where the witnesses did not actually see the loan, but if they saw the loan even while concealed, an excuse does not help. Tumim proves from Baal HaTerumot that even if they saw the loan while concealed, an excuse may still be effective. He is correct, for that is indeed implied by Baal HaTerumot. Nevertheless, where the defendant has no migo that he repaid, he certainly cannot claim, "You owe me an offsetting amount." Remove his admission from the case and he is still no more than one who seized property in the presence of witnesses without a migo, and such a person is not believed to say that the other party owes him. Accordingly, the Shach's statement that even if the witnesses saw the loan while concealed the excuse does not help can be understood as referring to a case where he has no migo. The distinction between witnesses who saw while concealed and those who did not applies where the defendant does have a migo that he repaid. If the witnesses heard the admission openly, so that the borrower and witnesses could see one another when he admitted the loan, he is not believed to say that he accepted the money as repayment of a prior debt and admitted the loan only because of some excuse. He cannot use a migo to claim an offsetting monetary debt in cash when he expressly borrowed in the presence of witnesses and acknowledged before those witnesses that it was a loan. Such an open admission is a full admission, and an excuse does not undo it. This differs from a case where the witnesses heard the admission only while concealed and he has a migo. There he may say, "I received it as payment of my debt, and because I could not otherwise collect what he owed me I described it as a loan." An admission overheard by concealed witnesses is not a valid formal admission, because witnesses may be concealed only in the exceptional contexts of an inciter and one who attempts to lead others astray, as Baal HaTerumot writes. In that form of case, the Shach would also agree, and there is no dispute between the Shach and Tumim. This appears clear.
