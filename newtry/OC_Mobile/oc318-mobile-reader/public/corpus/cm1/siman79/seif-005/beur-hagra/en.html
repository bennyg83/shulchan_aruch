And the lender... See Tosafos Bava Metzia 5a, s.v. "the opposing party," who ask, etc., and answer, etc. So too Tosafos Shevuos 47a, s.v. "because," write that this applies specifically to one who is generally suspect, etc. That differs from here, where he is established as a denier only concerning this particular money. Tosafos take it as obvious that in a case where one is obligated to swear but cannot swear, the claimant need not swear. This also appears in Shevuos 32b: "all agree concerning the opposing party..." The Gemara asks, "Let him say, who says..." and answers, "rather, where..." It then says, "all agree in the case of one witness, according to Rabbi Abba..."
And specifically concerning that. As stated there: only concerning that money and that garment. Regarding other money, one who denies a loan is nevertheless fit to testify. So writes Rav Hai Gaon, cited by Hagahos Maimoniyos, chapter 6. In the Gemara there, 16b: "not all judgments of beit din are alike..." because, etc. See Tosafos there, s.v. "and because..." Raavad explains that he had been established as a denier in another matter. If it concerned this same matter, why specifically a judgment of beit din? Even ordinary witnesses would suffice. This is the meaning of "and because..." In a different matter, even denial in beit din does not establish him as a denier. Sefer HaTerumos, section 12, part 1. There on 3a Rabbi Chiya taught: "You have a maneh of mine..." and "he shall swear concerning the remainder..."
(Collected note) And specifically... This follows from Rabbi Chiya's teaching. Mordechai there, siman 292. (End of note.)
And he is believed concerning it with his oath. He does not become generally suspect, as explained above, because one who denies a loan remains fit, as stated there on 5b, and on 3a, "and he shall swear concerning the remainder," as above.
