Rashba, responsum 905, discusses Reuven who produced documents against Shimon, where it was established that Reuven had stolen some of Shimon's documents from his house. Rashba says Reuven loses credibility regarding all documents he produces against Shimon. The Sema cites this, while the Shach asks why theft of some documents should invalidate the rest. He suggests that Rashba follows his view that a person suspected of wrongdoing who cannot take the required oath loses the claim, whereas according to our ruling a suspect can still collect on a valid document. In the responsum itself, however, Shimon had died and his widow proved that Reuven stole documents. Where one seeks collection from orphans, everyone agrees that a claimant who cannot take the necessary oath cannot collect. This would resolve the case and would mean Rashba's rule does not apply while Shimon is alive. But a careful reading shows that Rashba's actual reason is not general disqualification as a suspect. Once Reuven was proved to have stolen documents from Shimon's house, the ordinary evidentiary chazakah, "why is your document in my hand if the debt was paid," disappears. The documents may in fact have remained in the borrower's possession, and Reuven may simply have stolen them. The theft therefore weakens the very evidentiary basis of every similar document he produces against Shimon. This reasoning applies independently of the rules governing a suspect's oath and explains Rashba directly.
