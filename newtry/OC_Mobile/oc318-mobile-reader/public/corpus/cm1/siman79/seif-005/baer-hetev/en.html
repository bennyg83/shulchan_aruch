Another. Even if he denied one hundred and was established as a false denier regarding fifty, he must swear concerning the other fifty, as written in siman 75, seif 4, and siman 87, seif 5. See there. The Sema writes: The Rashba ruled in a responsum concerning one who was proven to have stolen promissory notes from Reuven's house that, by this, he was established as a false denier regarding every note he produced against Reuven. The Beit Yosef likewise cites this in the Rashba's name at the end of siman 66. In truth, this ruling requires study, for why should he be established as a false denier regarding all of them? Perhaps because once he stole some of them, he becomes suspected of having stolen all of them, and the Rashba follows the first view in siman 92, seif 9, that when a suspected person produces a note, the opposing party takes a heset oath and is exempt. There, the responsum explicitly concerns Reuven who had died and whose widow established the theft, and the widow was exempt from an oath. According to our ruling, however, where we follow the latter view in siman 92, he should not be established as a false denier regarding the other notes. Moreover, that responsum itself concludes that the matter requires consideration. The Rivash, siman 354, writes: 'There is no doubt that Reuven has been established as a false denier and can no longer advance any claim concerning those articles. Shimon is also believed as to the value of the articles, provided he assesses them at an amount appropriate to Reuven's means, and no oath is required.' The Beit Yosef cites this. It requires study why he should be believed regarding their value without an oath, since he was not established as a false denier on that point. See below, simanim 75, 87, and 90. Thus concludes the Shach.
