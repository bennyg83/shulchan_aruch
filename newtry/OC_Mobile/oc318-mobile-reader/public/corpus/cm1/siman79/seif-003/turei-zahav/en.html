(Seif 9: Two witnesses came and contradicted him.) One witness does not make him a proven denier, and he may later say that he paid. This seems inconsistent with siman 75, where one witness can make a defendant one obligated to swear who cannot swear and therefore liable. The distinction is as follows. If the defendant immediately admits the witness’s underlying account but presents another defense, the single witness is treated for that purpose like two, and the new defense may leave him unable to swear. But here the defendant initially said, “There never was such a matter,” and directly contradicted the single witness. The witness then creates only an oath obligation. When the defendant comes to swear, he may refine his oath by saying, “I borrowed but later paid.” He still swears to be exempt; it makes no difference whether the exemption arises from denying the witness or from another valid defense. Indeed, refining the oath to the more precise truth supports his credibility. The Tur’s wording, “when he came to swear, he retracted and claimed,” signals this distinction. In siman 75 the defendant’s immediate response to the witness is an admission of the loan coupled with payment; there the witness’s testimony is not denied and functions like two witnesses. The Tur’s reference there to “I do not know” is separate, since uncertainty is not a contradiction of the witness.
