More than one hundred witnesses. See Baer Heitev and the Sema. The Sema explains why the phrase “more than one hundred witnesses” is exact. Where witnesses contradict one another, one may still impose a hesset oath upon the defendant, whereas where the contradiction arises from the party’s own admission, no oath can be imposed upon him after payment. Responsa Chatam Sofer, siman 98, adds that there is also a Torah-law consequence. With contradictory witnesses, the defendant may later be subjected to an oath through gilgul when another oath obligation arises. When the bar comes from his own admission, however, no further oath can be rolled onto him. Chatam Sofer uses this to explain the Gemara’s objection in the first case of Rabbi Chiya, “What is distinctive about one’s own mouth, which cannot be contradicted?” and thereby resolves a difficulty raised by Nimukei Yosef against the Rambam, who holds that a litigant can say, “I remembered later.”
