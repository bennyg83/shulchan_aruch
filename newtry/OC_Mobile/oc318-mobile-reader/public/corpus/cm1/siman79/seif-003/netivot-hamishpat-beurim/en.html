The witnesses came first. Ketzot HaChoshen challenges Nimukei Yosef at the beginning of Bava Metzia, who distinguishes between one who claims, "I never borrowed," and one who admits liability, along the lines of the distinction made here by the Shulchan Aruch. Ketzot argues that if he seeks to retract only after witnesses have already come, then even where he had admitted that he owes the money he cannot retract according to Nimukei Yosef, whereas if he retracts before the witnesses arrive everyone agrees that he may do so because he is moving from one exempting claim to another. This misunderstands Nimukei Yosef. His distinction is as follows. If a person admitted in beit din that he owes money, witnesses then came and testified that he borrowed but did not say that he repaid, and he afterward says, "I now remember that I repaid," and still later witnesses come and testify that he in fact repaid, their testimony is effective. See Sema, seif katan 9. By contrast, if he originally claimed, "I never borrowed," witnesses came and contradicted him by testifying that he did borrow, though they did not testify about repayment, and only afterward he himself claimed that he repaid, then even if additional witnesses later come and testify that he had already repaid, their testimony does not help him because his earlier denial established him as a liar regarding repayment. This is straightforward.
