And they placed him under ban. As stated in Bava Basra 174b, where they placed him under ban, etc. It then has the status of a documented loan, which is collectible from orphans, and he is not believed to say, "I paid." This is the source of the Rif's distinction according to the Raavad's explanation.
(Collected note) And they placed him under ban. The Raavad writes this, but the Rif does not mention it, nor does the Rosh. See siman 157 there. (End of note.)
And he collects even... As stated there on 15a, as above.
(Collected note) Even from encumbered property. Meaning, even if it was not established that he failed to pay. Otherwise he could collect from encumbered property in any event, as the Rif and Rosh write there and as stated in siman 372. This is contrary to the Sema in siman 39, seif katan 24. The Taz and Shach already challenge him there. (End of note.)

One who accepted... against a strong chazakah... Meaning, although we accept a migo against an ordinary chazakah, as stated above in siman 78, seif 5, see there, we do not accept it against a particularly strong chazakah. This resolves Tosafos' question in the first chapter of Bava Basra 5b, s.v. "mi." Nevertheless, their further difficulty remains, etc.
