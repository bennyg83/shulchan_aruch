And they placed him under ban. The Sema writes that the text specifically means that they placed him under ban. The Bach writes that this is not precise, but merely describes the usual circumstance. See there. His view appears correct to me from what the Tur and Shulchan Aruch write in siman 157, at the end of seif 8, 'and he refused to carry out the directive of beit din,' without mentioning a ban. The same appears from the Rosh and Rabbeinu Yerucham. Baal HaTerumot likewise implies that the Raavad agrees with the Rif, and the Rif at the beginning of Bava Metzia does not mention a ban. Rather, once he refuses to obey the judgment, that alone is sufficient and he is no longer believed to say, 'I paid.' The same appears from Nimukei Yosef at the beginning of Bava Kamma, cited by the Beit Yosef in siman 119, seif 4. This is the principal ruling. Thus concludes the Shach.
