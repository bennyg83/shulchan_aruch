If the debtor says, 'I paid you one hundred with specified merchandise whose market price was such-and-such,' and the creditor brings witnesses that it was not worth that amount and claims the balance, and the debtor then changes his claim and says that he paid either with that merchandise or with something else, he is established as a denier. People pay attention to and remember the amount of a market price. The lender therefore collects the balance of the debt without an oath. An admission in beit din or testimony in beit din is treated like a loan recorded in a document, and it is therefore written and given to the opposing party. When does this apply? When the defendant did not accept the judgment until a panel of three judges, sitting on their own in their fixed place, summoned and brought him, as explained in siman 39. But if two litigants came for judgment, one claimed, 'You have one hundred of mine,' and the other answered, 'Yes,' then whether the judges said, 'You are obligated to give it to him,' or, 'Go, give it to him,' if he left and later said, 'I paid,' he is believed upon taking a heses oath that he paid. Therefore, if the claimant returns to the judges and says, 'Write his admission for me,' they do not write it, lest the defendant already have paid and the claimant then collect a second time.
