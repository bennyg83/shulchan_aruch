An admission in beit din, etc. When does this apply? When he did not accept, etc. All this was explained at the beginning of siman 39. The qualification concerns whether beit din writes a document of admission or a formal judgment. But regarding collection from purchasers, an admission in beit din is treated like a documented loan even if he came to beit din voluntarily, no summons was sent, and nothing was written for the claimant, so long as he admits that he did not pay. The Tur states this in the name of Tosafos. When the Mechaber later writes that if he defied the court and was placed under a ban, he is not believed to say 'I paid' and collection may be made even from encumbered property, the meaning is not that encumbered property may be collected only in a case of defiance and ban.
Rather, in such a case even a claim of payment is not believed, and therefore collection proceeds even from encumbered property. If he was not placed under a ban and says he paid, the claimant cannot collect even from free property. This interpretation is also necessary to reconcile the Tur's words here with siman 39, where he rules that every admission in beit din can support collection from encumbered property even without defiance. The distinction about whether beit din writes the ruling concerns when the debtor came voluntarily, when they had to summon him, whether he obeyed the ruling, and whether the parties were still present. If both parties are still in beit din, beit din may write the admission and give it to the claimant, because the debtor knows that a written admission exists and will not pay without taking it back. If he first defied the court, or later refused to obey and had to be placed under a ban, they may write even without his consent. The Mechaber further rules at the end of siman 70 that even without defiance, where he lacked the ability to pay and beit din had to record the matter so it would not be forgotten, he is later not believed to say that he paid, because he should have taken back the court record upon payment.
This applies to a formal court record that functions like a full debt instrument. But where beit din merely imposed a ban because he failed to appear at a fair, or established installments and he missed one, and he later says that he paid, that is not necessarily a court instrument that deprives him of a payment claim unless he had earlier defied the substantive judgment.Whether they said, 'You are obligated to give him.' Even though this language is not as absolute as 'Go give him,' and could sound like the judges were saying only that it appears he is obligated, people do not ordinarily distinguish so finely between these formulas. Therefore the claimant cannot recover merely because the wording sounded less final. In the following seif, where witnesses testify that he did not pay, we distinguish between 'Go give him' and 'You are obligated to give him' in order to avoid declaring the defendant a false denier where possible. In monetary doubts we seek a construction favorable to the possessor.Write me his admission. Maharshal holds that specifically an admission document should not be written, but a formal judgment may be written because the debtor is believed to say he paid against it according to some opinions. The Tur here, however, says not to write even a judgment. The reason is that initially one should not write it, lest after time passes the debtor forget that he borrowed and paid, deny the loan entirely, and then be established as a false denier because of the document.
