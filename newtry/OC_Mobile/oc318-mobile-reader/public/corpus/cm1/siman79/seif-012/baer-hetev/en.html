Merchandise. Meaning, he retracted and said, 'I forgot, and I now know that I repaid you with that merchandise and with money.' Otherwise, the ruling would be obvious. Thus writes Rabbeinu Yerucham. See the responsum of Mabit, volume 1, siman 48, and the responsum of Maharashdam, siman 349. Shach.
Received. See the Sema and Shach, who discuss these laws at length. The Shach concludes that this applies specifically where the lender asked, in the borrower's presence, that the document be written for him. See there. See also what the Taz writes on this matter.
His admission. The Sema writes in the name of Maharshal that only a written record of an admission may not be prepared for him, but a written judgment may be prepared, because the debtor is believed to say concerning a judgment, 'I paid,' as discussed among the opinions in siman 39. The Tur, however, writes here that they should not prepare a written judgment for him. It appears that the reason is that all agree that, initially, they should not write one, lest he produce it after some time, the debtor forget that he borrowed and repaid, claim, 'I never borrowed,' and thereby become established as a false denier through this written judgment. Baal HaTerumot gives such a rationale, as I cite his language in siman 69. Thus concludes the Sema. The Shach writes that these words are not correct. Rather, the principal view is that the Tur holds that in every case the debtor is not believed to say, 'I paid,' so long as the written judgment remains in the lender's possession. In siman 39 I concluded that this is the principal practical ruling. The Sema's proof from Baal HaTerumot is entirely unlike our case. There, the case concerns one who signed as a witness on a document. Since he never signed in connection with a loan, we are concerned that he may forget the circumstances of his signature, because he would not anticipate the forgery, and therefore truthfully claim, 'I never borrowed.' Once his signature is authenticated he would then be required to pay. Here, however, he actually did borrow. Why should he claim, 'I never borrowed'? If he does make such a claim, he has caused his own loss. Thus concludes the Shach.
