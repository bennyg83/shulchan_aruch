An admission in beit din or... Bava Metzia 17a: "He said to him, go and give it to him..." and then, "the lender came to have it written..." The Rif writes there that although an admission or testimony in beit din has the status of a documented loan, as stated there on 15a, "whether according to Rava..." nevertheless it may still be treated as an oral loan, etc., where the case is, etc. This applies where he did not obey the judgment, etc. See Beer HaGolah, seif katan 60. Rambam explains the Rif's words, "he did not obey the judgment," to mean that he did not accept the ruling until they sent for him, etc., as stated in Sanhedrin 29b: if he admitted before two, etc.; before three, etc.; until they establish a fixed place, etc. Rambam holds there is no distinction between whether the litigants assembled the judges or not, contrary to the Gemara's initial assumption. Rather, the judges must be sitting on their own in a fixed place, etc. Rambam's language implies specifically that they are sitting there on their own, contrary to the initial assumption that it is stronger if the litigant assembled them. The earlier discussion concerns judges who are not sitting in an established place, and the Gemara's phrase "they established a place" indicates that they themselves established it.
