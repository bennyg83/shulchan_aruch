(Seif 2: “Yet he denied.”) The language need not be forced to mean a total denial merely because the defendant says, “You are claiming from me.” Perhaps he meant, “You owe me the same amount.” Had that been his meaning, however, he should have said, “Just as I owe you,” not “that you claim from me.” His words imply that the plaintiff’s demand is a false accusation and that, on the contrary, the plaintiff owes him. Still, the ruling requires great study. It treats the words as “There never was such a matter,” so that witnesses proving an earlier loan make him a denier. Why can the words “I owe you nothing” not mean, “I already paid,” as explicitly recognized later in this siman, seif 10? Anyone seeking to extract money under this ruling must resolve this major difficulty. I presented it to my father-in-law, who agreed.
