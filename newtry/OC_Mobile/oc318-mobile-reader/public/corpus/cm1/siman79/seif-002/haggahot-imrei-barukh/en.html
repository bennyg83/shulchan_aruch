(Same Rama, at the end) He is not believed. Note: It requires study whether the borrower can require the lender to take a shevuat heset that he was not repaid. There is reason to obligate the lender to swear in such a case. The borrower was not established as a false denier, for at first he admitted that he borrowed and now claims that he remembered that he repaid. He cannot exempt himself with that claim because, as the Sema writes, we can say that he retracted only because he heard the witnesses. But why should we therefore assume with certainty that he is lying and that the witnesses are mistaken? If the law is that the lender must swear in such a case, the Rambam's statement concerning one who says "I never borrowed" is readily understood: we do not impose a shevuat heset on the lender because the borrower has been established as a false denier concerning this money. Meaning, even if the borrower now claims that he remembered paying, the lender need not swear, because the borrower was established as a false denier by his initial assertion that he never borrowed. The Rambam's wording, however, implies that the borrower still maintains his first claim and does not now claim payment. This is why the Raavad objects. The Shach's answer in seif 8, seif katan 3, does not fit the plain sense of the Rambam's wording. I have offered another answer to the Rambam and noted it in my margin to the Tumim.
