"And I do not owe you," etc. Taz asks: what follows from his saying, "I do not owe you"? The statement can be understood to mean that he no longer owes because he already paid, not that he never borrowed. Shach in seif katan 7 appears to address this objection by explaining the plea as: "I do not owe you because I have an offsetting claim against you." The witnesses then testify that he has no such claim, and he is established as a liar. But this does not fit Rashba's or the Mechaber's words, which never mention witnesses testifying that he has no claim against the creditor. It would also add no novelty: if witnesses directly contradict the basis of his plea, he is obviously discredited. Nor does it fit the comparison to one who says, "I did not pay."
It appears more correct that the case concerns the very same day. The claimant says, "On the first of Nisan you owed me 100," and the defendant answers, "It may be that I owed you then, but you likewise owed me an equivalent sum." Thus he is effectively saying: on that date you owed me, and I did not owe you net. If witnesses then testify that on that very date he borrowed from the claimant, he is established as a liar. When could repayment have occurred? If before the first of Nisan, the loan came later than the supposed repayment. If afterward, then he admitted that on the first of Nisan he did not owe the claimant anything and he further admits that he did not repay afterward. Consider this carefully, for it is correct.
