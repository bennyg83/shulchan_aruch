And the lender says, “I was not repaid.” See Responsa Chatam Sofer, Choshen Mishpat siman 98. This rule applies specifically where the lender says, “You did not repay me.” Then the lender may say: “I know that you owe me, for I claimed the debt from you, and I rely upon your own statement more than upon a hundred witnesses.” But if the lender himself says, “I lent you and you repaid me,” while the borrower says, “I never borrowed from you,” and the lender is holding property of the borrower, the lender certainly cannot retain it by saying, “You admitted that you never repaid me, and I know that I lent you.” We answer him: just as the borrower admits that he never repaid you, you yourself admit that no debt remains outstanding. A still stronger application of this principle appears in siman 75 seif 11. Chatam Sofer uses this distinction to reconcile Tosafot in Bava Metzia 110a.
