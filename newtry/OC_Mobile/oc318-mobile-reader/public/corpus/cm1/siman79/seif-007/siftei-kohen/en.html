One who admitted in beit din, etc. This rule comes from the Tur, which discusses one who admitted the validity of an instrument or his handwriting and acknowledged that he truly owed the money. The Rama’s placement of the gloss here, after the case of producing the defendant’s handwriting, also shows this. This resolves the Sema’s question what new point the Rama adds beyond the Mechaber’s rule in seif 3. The novelty is that one might have thought an admission concerning a written instrument is worse, as the Maggid Mishneh distinguishes and the Beit Yosef cites at the end of siman 82. One might therefore have said that after admitting a note he cannot later say, “I remembered,” even with witnesses. The Rama teaches that there is no distinction, following the Tur.
