[Shulchan Aruch] This was explained in siman 70. Note: There, seif 2, the Mechaber writes that even if witnesses came and contradicted him, he is believed, because in any event he was obligated to pay. As for the sugya in Shevuot where we say that a person does not necessarily place the matter upon his mind, one must explain either in accordance with the Ramah’s distinction, that he spoke rhetorically, “Did I not repay you before so-and-so and so-and-so?”, in which case we say that he did not place the matter upon his mind, or in accordance with the distinction that where there were no witnesses to the loan we say that he did not place the matter upon his mind. Either way, it is astonishing that the Rama did not cite the Gemara’s law that in some cases we say that a person did not place the matter upon his mind. This requires great study.
