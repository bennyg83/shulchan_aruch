Maharshal writes that where the representative appears with a signed promissory note and the defendant says, 'Let the principal swear to me that I did not repay him,' the defendant may not withhold the money because of Rav Papa's enactment. Rather, he pays the representative, and the beit din in the defendant's location writes to the beit din in the principal's location, where the principal will swear. Alternatively, whenever the defendant later encounters the principal, he can compel him to swear. The Shach does not accept this ruling. Nevertheless, it appears from Maharshal that his rule applies only where the oath is required because the defendant expressly demands, 'Swear to me,' as in siman 82 under Rav Papa's enactment. Where the document is partially impaired, or one witness testifies that it was paid, or in a similar case where an oath is required even without the defendant's demand under the law of the Mishnah, as stated in siman 84 and elsewhere, the defendant may retain the money and leave it with his own beit din until the principal swears. This is the primary ruling. See Responsa Mahara Sasson, siman 50. This applies particularly where there is concern that the defendant's arguments are evasive or contrived. If beit din sees that his claims are straightforward and free of deceit, he need not release the money from his possession. Responsa Rashdam, Choshen Mishpat, siman 63. See also Responsa Rashach, part II, siman 180. Knesset HaGedolah.
