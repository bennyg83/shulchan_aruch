Maharshal rules that where the agent presents a signed promissory note and the defendant demands that the principal swear that he was not paid, the defendant may not withhold the money merely because of Rav Pappa's enactment. He must pay the agent, and the local beit din should write to the beit din where the principal resides so that the principal swears there. Alternatively, whenever the defendant later encounters the principal, he may require the oath. I do not accept this ruling. Even according to Maharshal, it applies only where the oath is imposed solely because the defendant expressly demanded it, under Rav Pappa's enactment. Where the creditor impaired his note, one witness testifies that it was paid, or another circumstance imposes an oath under the law of the Mishna even without a demand, the defendant may withhold the money and deposit it with his beit din until the principal swears. This is the correct ruling.
