Authorization to another. See Baer Heitev in the name of the Shach. The Shach further cites Responsa Mabit concerning a power of attorney stating that the principal authorizes two named persons to sell his house, and that if an unavoidable circumstance prevents one of them from handling the sale, the other may sell it alone. No unavoidable circumstance occurred, but one of them said that he did not wish to deal with the sale. Mabit ruled that the other may sell alone. The condition requiring both to sell, with an exception for an unavoidable circumstance, was intended only to exclude a case where one sells alone without any unavoidable circumstance and without informing the other. It was not intended to invalidate a sale where one simply refuses to participate. The Shach writes that this is not compelling. Shaar Mishpat comments that in his view the Mabit is correct. Since the principal revealed that if an unavoidable circumstance affects one agent, the other may sell alone, the same should apply where one refuses to deal with the sale, for there can be no greater unavoidable circumstance than that. The principal did not expressly stipulate this case only because it did not occur to him, but the meaning follows naturally from the condition. Shaar Mishpat brings a proof from the words of the Tur and Shulchan Aruch at the end of siman 225. He further writes that where a power of attorney simply authorizes "so and so and so and so" to sell the house, the matter depends on the dispute discussed in Yoreh Deah, siman 216, seif 7. According to the Rama there and the Mechaber in siman 228, seif 40, who rule that one who made his son swear not to lend money to others without the permission of Reuven and Shimon may lend with the permission of either one, because whenever two names are stated it can mean either of them unless both are expressly required, the same applies here and either agent may sell alone. According to the Re'em, however, cited by the Shach, who follows the ordinary language of people and understands such wording to require both together, the same would apply to documents, as explained at the end of siman 61. See the Taz there in siman 228, Responsa Chacham Tzvi, siman 4, and Responsa Rabbi Akiva Eiger, siman 46.
