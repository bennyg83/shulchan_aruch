Under the law of the Gemara. The first opinion, Rambam's, holds that under talmudic law an authorization requires a complete acquisition of the very object concerning which authority is granted, in whatever manner that object would be acquired for oneself. If the object cannot be transferred, authorization cannot be given under talmudic law. The alternative opinion holds that even something not transferable by an act of acquisition may be made the subject of authorization, because the Sages enacted that for purposes of authorization the agent is treated as though he acquired it fully. I do not understand Beit Yosef's statement that this is unlike Rashba's responsum. Under the second version of the Gemara, authorization is written even concerning stolen property. Tosafot and the Rosh explain that although the law follows Rabbi Yochanan, this operates through the enactment of agency. Tur himself writes this in seif 5 in the name of the Rosh. Rashba merely writes that we follow the second version and that although the principal could not consecrate the property, he may nevertheless appoint an agent. Maharshal discusses this correctly at length. Since we rule that authorization may be written in such cases, as Mechaber cites in the alternative opinion and Rama confirms, and this is the accepted custom, I have been brief.
