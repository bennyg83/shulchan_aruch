According to the law of the Gemara, etc. This is the Rambam's view. See chapter 3, halachah 7. The Tur likewise records it in his name, following the approach of the Geonim.
One does not write, etc. This follows the latter version there in the discussion in Shevuot 32b and the latter version in Bekhorot 49a.
My deposit. This excludes a loan, as explained above.
And if it was a deposit, etc. As stated in Bava Kamma 104b, Bava Metzia 46a, and Bava Batra 77b. This excludes a loan, which cannot be acquired together with land, as explained below.
Likewise, he may authorize, etc. The Gemara's statement regarding movable property that was denied applies specifically to movable property. Regarding land, however, even according to the first version an authorization may be written whenever the claimant can recover it through beit din, as stated at the beginning of Bava Metzia 7a, and certainly according to the latter version, since land cannot be stolen in the halachic sense, as the Kesef Mishneh explains there.
Likewise, regarding a loan evidenced by a document, etc. They asked from the passage in Shevuot there, which implies that one may authorize another, yet the Gemara establishes it as a case of a deposit. See Tosafot there, s.v. Heikha, etc., and Bekhorot there, s.v. Hanei Milei, etc. Likewise, regarding the passage in Bekhorot, they said that Shmuel follows his own position, for he says there that if the money was consumed within thirty days, the son is not redeemed. It necessarily follows that the money was not given with the power to reclaim it, as Tosafot in Bava Kamma and the Rashba there explain. The Baal HaTerumot asks from Ketubot 94b, regarding two deeds, etc., which he explains as a loan, and the Gemara says that they should write authorizations to one another. It must therefore refer to a loan evidenced by a document. The reason is that the lien upon land is involved, and it is treated like land.
For just as he can, etc. Without writing and delivery of the document he cannot grant an authorization, because he must convey the right to the representative and say, "Go, litigate and acquire." Certainly according to the Rambam and the Shulchan Aruch in siman 122, seifim 1 and 4, who require an act of acquisition, the document itself cannot be acquired by an ordinary kinyan without writing and delivery, as explained above in siman 66; see there, seifim 2 and 4. All this follows Tosafot and the authorities who hold that a document cannot be acquired together with land, as noted above in siman 66, seif 10 in the gloss. According to the Ri Migash, the Rambam, and the Shulchan Aruch there, who hold that it can be acquired together with land, the same would seemingly apply here. Yet the Rambam writes here that even for a loan evidenced by a document no authorization is written. The Raavad comments, "Unless he delivered the document to him." The Rambam, however, does not hold so, for he writes that one who has money held as a deposit by another gives the representative a parcel of land and conveys the money together with it, but one who has a loan in another's hands cannot write an authorization for it, even if the debt is evidenced by a document. This implies that, in his view, even acquisition together with land cannot create an authorization over a loan evidenced by a document.
And he must, etc. See there.
Likewise, one does not write, etc. An oral loan cannot be transferred by a kinyan, even together with land. This follows Bava Batra 148a, "Although this is not possible for a healthy person," etc.; see Rashbam there, s.v. Haloato, etc.; Bava Batra 152a; and Ketubot 55b, "He mounted it on two steeds," etc. See Tosafot in Ketubot there, s.v. She'im, etc., and above, siman 203, seif 9. Anything that cannot be acquired through a kinyan cannot be made the subject of an authorization, as above. The text in the incident of Rav Pappa should not read that he was owed a loan; rather, it concerned a deposit, as Tosafot and the commentators explain.
But the Geonim, etc.; but an oral loan, etc.; nevertheless, it is no worse, etc. The Rambam writes all of this. His statement, "Even for a loan," etc., and his statement, "although," etc., address two separate matters: first, according to strict law, even when the debtor has not denied the debt, an authorization is not written for a loan; second, even where the defendant has denied the claim, in which case authorization is not written even for a deposit.
So that he should not take, etc. This is like the rule at the end of the chapter HaKotev regarding a creditor who collects in the debtor's absence. See the Rashba.
But regarding, etc. The Rambam writes there that for a loan evidenced by a document the Geonim instituted authorization even when the debtor denied the debt, because his denial concerns a lien upon land. There is no distinction whether the claimant delivered the document to the representative. By contrast, regarding an oral loan that the debtor denied, no authorization is written. It appears from the Rambam's words that an oral loan that was not denied, and a deposit even if denied, may be made the subject of authorization, for he excluded only the combination of an oral loan and denial, three disadvantages together.
Nevertheless, it is no worse, etc. An agent appointed before witnesses requires no kinyan; verbal appointment suffices, as stated in the chapter HaShoel regarding one who says, "Send," and in the chapter HaGozel. See there and see Sema. All this is the Rambam's approach, except the ruling that a loan evidenced by a document may be authorized by writing and delivery, which is the position of the Baal HaTerumot, Tur, Raavad, Rashba, and other authorities.
And some say that regarding, etc. The first version of the Gemara is not accepted as law, and neither is the latter version, because we hold in Ketubot 85 and Gittin 26 that we are not concerned that a document may appear false. Although we do find places where such an appearance is a concern, as in Bava Batra 172a, there it is because the witnesses might later appear invalid when it is discovered that they were not in the place named in the document; see the Rosh there. Although the Gemara challenges from the latter version of the Nehardeans in Shevuot and Bekhorot, it is common for the Gemara to raise a challenge from a view not ultimately accepted, just as it challenges from Rav Pappi's concern for an appearance of falsehood even though the law does not follow him. Thus, according to the law as we rule it, the Nehardeans' statement likewise is not accepted. The first opinion maintains that in this particular kind of case we do remain concerned that it appear false, like the case in Bava Batra, because in the other cases the appearance of falsehood eventually disappears, whereas in Bava Batra it remains permanently; the same applies here. See Rashba there.
Whether, etc. The order is from the less novel to the more novel. A deposit that the holder did not deny may be made the subject of authorization even according to the first version, unlike stolen property. See Tosafot there, s.v. Lo Katvinan, etc. The same applies to a loan evidenced by a document and an oral loan, as above. The reason, as Tosafot there, s.v. Ametaltelin, explain, is that since we do not follow the first version, authorization may be written even concerning stolen property although the owner cannot convey it, just as he cannot consecrate it. The same therefore applies to an oral loan. The reason is that he merely makes the representative his agent, and a complete transfer is unnecessary. The Sages enacted that it should operate as though a complete acquisition had occurred even though the representative does not acquire it as his own. See there.
(Collected note) And some say, etc., and an oral loan, etc., for he merely makes him an agent, etc. Although he cannot convey it, there is proof from Bava Batra 127a, which states that an authorization may be written, etc., even though before division the claimant owns nothing definite. See Tosafot there. This also resolves the passages in Bekhorot and Shevuot without resorting to a strained explanation. (End of collected note.)
