For which claims a power of attorney may be written and how it is written. It contains 15 se'ifim.
Under Talmudic law, a power of attorney is written only to claim a deposit held by another who has not denied it. If the deposit consists of money, it must be conveyed to the agent together with land. A person may likewise authorize an agent to claim land even if the holder denied the claim, and to collect a documented loan even if the borrower denied it. Just as he can convey the document and its lien through writing and delivery, he may grant authority over it and deliver the document to the agent for collection. He must write, 'Acquire this document and every lien contained in it.' But a power of attorney is not written for a deposit the custodian denied, nor for an oral loan. The Geonim instituted that a power of attorney may be written even for a loan, whether documented or established by witnesses to a kinyan, although it was denied in beit din, so that a person should not take another's money and depart for a different country. They did not institute a power of attorney for a denied oral loan. Nevertheless, an agent appointed before witnesses is no less effective than the agent described in siman 122. See also the end of seif 4 there. Some say a power of attorney may be written for every claim, whether a deposit, robbery, a documented loan, or an oral loan, even when it was denied. This is the accepted practice. See note 205.
