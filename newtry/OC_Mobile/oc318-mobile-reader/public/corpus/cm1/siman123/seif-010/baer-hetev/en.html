Known. All the more so where the representative acts without compensation. Terumat HaDeshen, siman 354, goes further and writes that even if the principal says, 'Your compensation depends upon my discretion, whether I choose to pay you or not,' the representative is still not considered financially interested and may testify, because his compensation is not certain. He brings proof and concludes that the matter ultimately depends on the judge's assessment. Darkei Moshe cites this, and notes that Beit Yosef writes that the ruling does not appear correct to him, though he does not explain why. The Sema records this. The Shach agrees with Beit Yosef. Terumat HaDeshen's proof is from Tosafot's statement that a person is not disqualified as a witness merely because he might later become wealthy. That is not comparable. In Tosafot's case there is presently not even a doubt, because he definitely has no financial interest now, and only might become wealthy later. Here there is an immediate possibility that the representative will profit, and there is reason to fear that he will testify in a manner that induces the principal to pay him, since payment is more likely if the case is won than if it is lost. See Responsa Rama, siman 104.
