(Seif 13) And the husband does not consume the produce. Sema, seif katan 20, writes that if the husband did consume the produce, he could not appoint his wife as representative because no benefit to her would be apparent after she recovered from the defendant. This is very surprising and entirely implausible. Since the body of the property is hers, no authorization should be needed for that reason. Here we are not speaking of a genuine transfer of the money to her so that she litigates as owner, for the entire discussion concerns authorization, and we hold that the representative is merely an agent and owns nothing after recovery. Rather, because a wife can possess property independently of her husband, such as a gift expressly conditioned that he not consume its produce, it is meaningful to tell her, “recover it for yourself,” even though she has not in fact acquired the present claim. The wording itself proves this, for “if he gave her” means that he did not actually give it now, only that if such a gift occurred the husband would not consume its produce. The glossator notes that Sema's words can also be explained this way.
