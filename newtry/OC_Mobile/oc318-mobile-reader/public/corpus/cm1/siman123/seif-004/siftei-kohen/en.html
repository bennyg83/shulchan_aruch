An authorized agent may not. If the first agent appointed another, the defendant who paid the second agent in reliance upon the first authorization remains responsible, as Maharshal rules. The first agent is exempt, because the defendant should not have paid someone whom the first agent lacked power to appoint. Mabit discusses an authorization appointing two persons to sell a house, providing that if one is prevented by unavoidable circumstances the other may sell alone. He rules that if one merely refuses to participate, the other may nonetheless sell alone, reasoning that the condition requiring both was meant only to invalidate a unilateral sale made without notice and without any obstacle. He further infers from the authorization's forty day announcement requirement that this was the principal condition. His reasoning is not compelling.
For the defendant can say. Even if the second agent says that the defendant should answer the claim but that any money recovered will not be delivered to him, the defendant need not answer. The defendant may say that the principal never appointed the second agent at all, because the principal can object that he does not want his deposit or money entrusted to another. If, however, the circumstances are such that the principal cannot make that objection, as explained below in siman 291, and the first agent appointed another, payment to the second agent exempts the defendant.
