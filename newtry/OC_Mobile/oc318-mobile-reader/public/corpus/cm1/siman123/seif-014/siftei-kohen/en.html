They may send an authorization through a non-Jew. Maharshal disputes this. For a loan or anything else not transferable by symbolic exchange, authorization cannot be given to a non-Jew, because it operates only as agency and a non-Jew cannot serve as a halachic agent. Land or a deposit, however, can be transferred to a non-Jew by symbolic exchange, though the authorization should not use language of agency. This may also be Rabbeinu Tam's intent, since the authorities who cite him immediately give his reason that symbolic exchange is effective for a non-Jew. On that view, even if the document uses the customary formula, the non-Jew acquires fully and the additional language merely imposes responsibility. Mechaber, however, implies that authorization is effective through a non-Jew in every case. I remain doubtful of Rabbeinu Tam's basic premise that symbolic exchange acquires for a non-Jew. Neither the Gemara nor the authorities discussing acquisition by non-Jews commonly mention it. The Gemara concerning wine sold to non-Jews could simply have advised acquisition by symbolic exchange, yet it instead requires payment or another recognized mode. The authorities concerning transfer of an animal to a non-Jew to avoid firstborn sanctity, and concerning sale of chametz, likewise labor to use money or pulling and do not suggest symbolic exchange. Rabbeinu Tam's proof from “the money of his purchase” is not conclusive. The verse may teach that even a Jewish slave is not acquired by symbolic exchange, or may reveal specifically that a Hebrew slave sold to a non-Jew is not acquired by that method because symbolic exchange is ineffective for a non-Jew. Tosafot's question in Kiddushin is better understood according to its initial assumption that symbolic exchange does not work for a non-Jew. A woman might still have been compared to a field because a field itself is acquired by symbolic exchange when both parties are Jews, even though Ephron personally was a non-Jew. Maharam Mintz and Maharshdam also write that no acquisition by symbolic exchange is found for a non-Jew. Rabbeinu Tam's view therefore appears to be an individual opinion.
