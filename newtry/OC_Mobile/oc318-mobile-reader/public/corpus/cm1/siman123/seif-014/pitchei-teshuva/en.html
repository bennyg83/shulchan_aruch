In the possession of a non Jew. See Baer Heitev through its expression of doubt about Rabbeinu Tam's view that exchange acquisition is effective with a non Jew. The Tumim argues at length against the Shach and concludes that acquisition by means of land does not operate with a non Jew. Otherwise, why did Rabbeinu Tam not instruct that an authorization against a non Jew be made by acquisition together with land? Likewise, in the sale of chametz and firstborn animals, the halachic authorities did not originally direct use of acquisition together with land. He maintains that no source clearly establishes that such an acquisition works with a non Jew. Ketzot HaChoshen challenges him and writes that this was stated too hastily, since Rabbeinu Yerucham, Netiv 20, part 2, expressly mentions acquisition together with land. Moreover, the Jewish communities had already widely adopted acquisition together with land in the sale of chametz, following the responsum of Maharash Kaidanover printed in Nachalat Shivah, part 2, siman 30, and cited by Chok Yaakov, siman 448, seif katan 14. Maharash Kaidanover questioned acquisition by courtyard because a courtyard acquires by the law of agency, and there is no agency for a non Jew, and therefore preferred acquisition together with land. Ketzot explains the matter there. Responsa Chatam Sofer, Choshen Mishpat, siman 66, writes that an explicit source supporting Ketzot is found in Ramban's Milchamot to the second chapter of Pesachim, in the discussion of one who pledged chametz with the creditor.
