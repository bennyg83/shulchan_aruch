They may send an authorization through a gentile. The Tur explains that Scripture teaches that a gentile can acquire through exchange. Therefore, when the gentile himself gives his kerchief to the Jewish principal, and the principal takes it and transfers in exchange the debt or deposit he has against another person, the gentile acquires the claim with the status of an authorized representative. This works only where the gentile personally supplies the object used for the exchange and thereby acquires the monetary claim. Witnesses cannot make the acquisition on his behalf in the usual manner, where the witnesses give their own kerchief to the transferor and acquire on behalf of the recipient under the rule that one may acquire a benefit for a person in his absence. The Tur writes in the name of the Rosh in siman 195 that most acquisitions were already conducted in that manner in their time, because parties normally summon witnesses to avoid later denial, and once witnesses are designated they also conduct the kinyan with their own kerchief on behalf of the recipient. The witnesses then write that they acquired from the transferor on behalf of the recipient with a utensil fit for acquisition, as is customary in our documents. Therefore the Tur concludes that where the claim is transferred to a gentile in this manner, the authorization should not say, 'and we acquired from him,' because that would imply that the witnesses acquired from the principal on behalf of the gentile, and witnesses cannot serve as agents to acquire for a gentile in that way. Rather, the document should state that 'the gentile acquired,' meaning that the gentile personally performed the exchange acquisition, and the witnesses attest to that fact. This appears to me the straightforward meaning of the Tur and Mechaber's warning not to write, 'and we acquired from him.' The Ir Shushan explains that phrase differently, as though it implied that the gentile transferred something to another person and that the problem is that a gentile cannot appoint an agent to acquire for someone else. He therefore says to write instead that the gentile acquired what is stated in the authorization. In my view, he was not precise either in the legal concept or in the wording.
