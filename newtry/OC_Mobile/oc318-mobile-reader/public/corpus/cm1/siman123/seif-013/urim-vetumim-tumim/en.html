The Shach in seif katan 31 cites Maharshadam as holding that where a non-Jew gives authorization against a Jew, even Rabbenu Tam agrees that the authorization cannot be used. His words are incorrect as a matter of law. Certainly, with respect to the prohibition of doing what is improper toward one's fellow, Maharshadam spoke well. Why should one assist in extracting money from a Jew and giving it to a non-Jew, unless there is concern of desecration of Hashem's name or the like, so that accusations not be raised against us in exile that we assist wrongdoing and regard a non-Jew's money as ownerless? Unfortunately, such conduct provokes strife and hatred against us among the nations and causes much harm to Jews who walk innocently and uprightly. Therefore in our times people have at times acted leniently in such situations, and the sages of the generation did not protest. But regarding the law of acquisition itself, Maharshadam's words are not understandable. Wherever a kinyan is effective when a non-Jew acquires from a Jew, the same kinyan is effective when a Jew acquires from a non-Jew. Why should the laws of acquisition differ in that respect? Tosafot explain that chalipin worked in the field of Ephron, so that kiddushin can be derived from it, and there the non-Jew transferred property to a Jew. Likewise the Rashbam in Bava Batra, regarding property of non-Jews, writes that the non-Jew withdraws from the property upon receiving money because all of his acquisition is with money. There he is discussing a non-Jew transferring to a Jew. How could these rules be learned from one another unless the acquisition laws are symmetrical in this respect? Tosafot likewise equate the two directions in many places. If anything, it is more readily accepted that the property of a non-Jew can be removed from his possession than that Jewish property can be removed from a Jew. Maharshadam's distinction therefore has no basis in Shas or poskim. This is clear.
