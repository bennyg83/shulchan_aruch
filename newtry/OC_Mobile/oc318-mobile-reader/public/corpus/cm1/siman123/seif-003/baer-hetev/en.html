To the second. Even if the principal did not expressly revoke the first power of attorney, we presume that it was revoked when he subsequently authorized another person. The exception is where it is known that the first authorization was not revoked, and the principal intended payment to be made to whichever representative arrived first. If both then arrive together, payment may be made to whichever one the defendant chooses. Baal HaTerumot further implies that if the defendant paid the first representative after knowing that his authority had been revoked, and the money was then lost, the defendant must pay again to the second representative or to the principal. Sema. The Shach adds that if the defendant litigated with the first representative and was found liable, he cannot overturn the judgment by arguing that the representative had been revoked and therefore was not his proper adversary. Since one may acquire a benefit for another person even in his absence, and the judgment in the principal's favor is unquestionably a benefit, the representative was a valid litigant for that purpose. The Shach proves this from Terumat HaDeshen, siman 217, cited by Rama at the beginning of siman 122, that one who wishes to intervene under an authorization may compel litigation because one may acquire a benefit for another. All the more so here, where it is evident that the principal will approve the result once judgment has already been won in his favor.
