(Seif 9) Only a minor safeguard. It appears that if the defendant wishes to deposit the amount claimed with beit din until a ban is first proclaimed in the principal's presence, he should be heard, since his conduct shows that he does not seek delay. This is included in the rule stated in this seif, and Maharshal's words in the chapter Merubah incline the same way. Maharshal also writes that even if the principal transferred a share of the debt to the representative, the principal may afterward release the defendant from the entire debt, just as one who sells a promissory note may later release the debtor. The principal must reimburse the representative only for his actual expenses, not for lost time. If the subject is land or a deposit, however, where the representative acquired the body of the property in the depositary's possession, the principal cannot release it. Nevertheless, if the depositor admits the defendant's claim, he is believed in all respects, since the representative derives his authority from him. The representative may impose a ban upon the principal that his admission is not collusive.
