To authorize. The Shach writes that since we rule that authorization was enacted as a form of agency, one can appoint a representative even regarding an oath. The Tur and Mordechai imply this, and common practice is to issue authorizations even for oath claims.
Claim. Darkei Moshe inferred from the Mordechai that one cannot authorize another regarding a mere claim. The Sema, Bach, and other later authorities object that the Mordechai means the opposite: authorization can operate even regarding a claim because it is a form of agency. What cannot be done is to transfer a bare claim by sale or gift. There is no difficulty in the Rama's gloss here, because he may be writing according to the Mechaber's view that one cannot authorize another regarding an oath. According to that view, an oath and a bare claim are alike because neither can be transferred through ordinary kinyan, and Darkei Moshe expressly compares them. According to our ruling, however, that authorization does not depend upon an actual property acquisition, one may authorize another regarding a claim. Shach.
