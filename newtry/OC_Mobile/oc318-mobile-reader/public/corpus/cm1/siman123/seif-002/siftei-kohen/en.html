One who had, etc. This is the Rambam’s ruling, and it appears to follow his particular approach. According to what we rule, however, that the Geonim instituted authorization as a form of agency, the principal may authorize another even regarding an oath. This is also indicated by the Tur, which presents this as the Rambam’s view, and by the Mordechai in Mi Shemet according to the explanations of the Sema and Bach. This is the accepted practice, to grant authorization even regarding an oath.
One who had a claim, etc. The Darkei Moshe derived from the Mordechai in Mi Shemet that an authorization cannot be written over the bare claim itself. The Sema, Bach, and other later authorities correctly objected that this is not the Mordechai’s meaning. The Mordechai means that authorization works even regarding a claim because it is merely agency, whereas an outright sale or gift cannot transfer a bare claim that is not itself subject to acquisition. Here, however, the Rama’s gloss is understandable according to the Rambam and Mechaber, who hold that authorization cannot be given over an oath, since an oath and a bare claim are both matters that cannot be acquired by kinyan. This comparison is stated explicitly in Darkei Moshe. The phrase that one must instead write authorization over the underlying money means that even the Rambam accepts authorization over the monetary right, including an oral loan not denied by the debtor, after the enactment of the Geonim. According to our ruling, however, authorization does not depend on ordinary kinyan, and therefore it may be given even over the claim itself, as the Sema, Bach, and later authorities explain the Mordechai. The Sema cites the Ran in Shevuat HaEdut as saying that authorization is not written over money that never entered the claimant’s possession, such as payments for humiliation, depreciation, or damages, in opposition to Rashi. This is a scribal error. It should read that the Ran says authorization is written even over such money. So it appears in Darkei Moshe and in the Ran. Still, even Rashi may agree that nowadays authorization can be written over money that never came into the claimant’s possession. Rashi explained the Mishnah according to the earlier legal framework, before the enactment of Nehardea. Rabbeinu Eliezer ben Natan explains that originally authorization required acquisition by agav, which cannot transfer a right that has not yet come into existence; Nehardea later enacted the formula “go litigate and recover” as though the right had been acquired by agav. Tosafot, the Rosh, and the other authorities therefore agree that after the enactment, authorization may be written even for a right not transferable by ordinary kinyan.
