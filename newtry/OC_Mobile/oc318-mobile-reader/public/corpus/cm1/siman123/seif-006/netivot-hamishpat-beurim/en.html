A debt owed by a non-Jew. Ketzot HaChoshen comments on Beit Yosef, who cites a responsum concerning Reuven, who held a note against a non-Jew and gave it to Shimon to collect. Shimon collected the money, but told Reuven that he had merely granted the non-Jew an additional two months, offering instead to give Reuven another note of his own in payment. Beit Yosef writes that this is astonishing: once Shimon collected the money from the non-Jew, Reuven acquired those funds and they were his, and Shimon's deception cannot destroy Reuven's right. Ketzot attempts to prove otherwise from Tosafot in Bava Metzia 71, s.v. Bishlama Seifa LeChumra, who hold that a Jew cannot acquire money from a non-Jew on behalf of another Jew even where the second Jew was appointed as agent and received the money from the non-Jew. This proof is very difficult. Even if it is true that the money does not become the Jew's property immediately, the non-Jew is nevertheless discharged from the loan, as is clear in siman 171 where the creditor says "send it through a non-Jew." Although a non-Jew cannot serve as a halachic agent, the borrower is discharged. This resembles Ritva in Kiddushin, who writes that if the creditor says "place it on the ground and be discharged," the borrower is discharged from the debt even though the money does not thereby become the creditor's and the borrower may still reclaim it. That rule is correct. But even if the money remains technically the non-Jew's, why should the creditor not be able to collect the amount of his debt from those very funds, especially where he has no other source from which to collect? Those funds have effectively become an expressly designated apotiki for the debt. The creditor has acquired the right that those funds stand for his collection, and no one else may take them. So too, if the creditor said "place the money on the ground and be discharged," no third party may take it for use. The creditor collects his debt from it, and if another person takes it, the creditor may recover it from him through beit din. This is clear.
