Reuven held a debt instrument against a gentile and gave it to Shimon to collect. Shimon collected immediately but told Reuven that he had granted the gentile two months and offered instead to give Reuven one of Shimon's own debt instruments. Reuven later claimed that he accepted Shimon's document only conditionally, if the gentile debt was not collected within two months. Shimon said the exchange was unconditional. Rosh ruled that Shimon's document could be transferred only by proper writing and delivery, and if the witnesses to the exchange did not testify to a condition, the exchange stands as unconditional. One might object that because Shimon had already collected the gentile's money, those funds immediately became Reuven's through Shimon's agency, and Shimon's deception should not deprive Reuven of them. In my view Rosh is correct. Tosafot teaches that when one Jew receives money directly from a gentile debtor, he cannot automatically acquire it for another Jew merely as the recipient Jew's agent. The gentile is the owner who is effecting the payment, and a gentile has no halachic agency. The receiving Jew cannot simultaneously function as the gentile's agent to transfer the gentile's money to the Jewish creditor. Therefore Reuven did not automatically acquire the money at the instant Shimon collected it from the gentile. The later exchange of instruments must be analyzed on its own terms.
