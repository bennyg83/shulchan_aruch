For this reason it appears to me. Sema asks why Mechaber says “it appears to me,” since Tur likewise writes that one who has witnesses should not appoint one of the witnesses as agent. There is no difficulty. Some editions of Tur read “one of the witnesses' relatives,” and Maharshal adopts that reading, treating “one of the witnesses” as a scribal error or abbreviation. As a matter of law, Mechaber's ruling is primary, and Mahari Weil also appears to agree. Mahari Weil further implies that if compensation was not fixed initially and the opposing party seeks to disqualify the agent, the principal may later fix or remove the compensation and the agent may testify, because in monetary matters removal of an interest is effective. Nevertheless, having an agent testify is unseemly even where he serves without pay.
To litigate with a guarantor liable as a principal debtor. This is specifically such a guarantor, because the creditor may collect first from whichever party he chooses. The same applies to an ordinary guarantor when the borrower presently lacks assets. When the borrower has assets, however, the creditor must collect from him first, and the borrower cannot serve as agent to collect first from the guarantor. Where the borrower presently cannot pay, his testimony against either type of guarantor may generally be accepted. He gains nothing, because even if the guarantor pays now, the guarantor may later recover from him. Nor is he disqualified merely because he may later become wealthy. The Gemara and authorities teach that a merely possible future benefit does not create present interest. Still, another concern exists: the borrower may wish to avoid being called “a wicked borrower who does not pay.” That concern may render him interested, especially where he borrowed nothing from the guarantor and people would not ordinarily call him wicked for failing to reimburse the guarantor. Even according to Sema's lenient approach, the borrower is qualified only where he asked the guarantor to guarantee or assume principal liability for him. If the guarantor acted without the borrower's request and therefore has no right of reimbursement, the borrower is plainly interested when he lacks assets, because the guarantor's payment releases him from the creditor. If the borrower has assets, he may testify regarding an ordinary guarantor because the creditor must collect from him immediately in any event. He may not testify regarding a guarantor liable as principal debtor, because his testimony causes collection from that guarantor, who cannot then recover from him absent a request. If the borrower's assets are in another country, even testimony regarding an ordinary guarantor is disqualified, because the creditor may then collect first from the guarantor, who again has no recourse absent the borrower's request.
