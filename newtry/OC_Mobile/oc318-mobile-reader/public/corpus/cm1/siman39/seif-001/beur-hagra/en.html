(Collected note) See Shach, seif katan 2. The main dispute is between the Rif and Tosafot in explaining the Gemara at the end of Bava Batra. Rav Pappa says, "the halacha is, etc.," because otherwise lenders would be deterred from lending, while Kiddushin 13b gives the reason that lien liability is Torah law. The Rif answers that Bava Batra means that because of concern for locking the door before borrowers, the Sages restored the Torah rule. Tosafot in Kiddushin answer that the passage there concerns a monetary obligation written in the Torah, such as damages and valuations, etc. See Tosafot, Bava Kamma 8a, s.v. "Kulan, etc." They require these answers because in their view Rav Pappa also speaks about obligations written in the Torah. According to the Rif, however, even according to the view that such an obligation is not like a written document, it may still collect from encumbered property because lien liability is Torah law. Rav Pappa's limitation applies only to an ordinary loan, which has no publicity, unlike damages. So Tosafot write on 14b, s.v. "Shema Mina, etc.," in the name of Rabbeinu Tam. Their difficulty there concerns Rav and Shmuel, etc., but not Rav Pappa according to Tosafot's earlier explanation. The Rashba similarly answers Tosafot's difficulty on 8a that the case can be where judgment was entered, or alternatively that damages collect even from encumbered property according to the view that liens are Torah law, which is our ruling. I further see proof from HaManiach, Bava Kamma 33b, "since it has no publicity, etc.," meaning as Tosafot explain on 14b in the name of Rabbeinu Tam. The Rashba therefore follows the Rif. The Maggid Mishneh, chapter 21 of Hilchot Malveh, cites some commentators similarly. So it appears from Shulchan Aruch, siman 119, seif 3: "the injured party collects, etc." See Sema and Shach there. Although we rule that an obligation written in the Torah is not treated as a written document, as stated in Yoreh Deah 305, seif 16, nevertheless an injured party collects, apparently even before a formal judgment. One must therefore use the Rashba and Maggid Mishneh's answer that lien liability is Torah law. So too it is ruled in siman 97, seif 24, and siman 104, seif 13; see Beer HaGolah there and the Rashba, responsum 914. The rule in siman 64, seif 35, concerning one who waives, etc., is because dina d'garmi is rabbinic according to siman 386 and the Shach there. Even according to the Ramban that it is Torah law, it lacks publicity, unlike damages, which can generate publicity, as Rav Pappa's rule regarding an ordinary loan indicates according to the Rif. See Shach, siman 66, and Tosafot, Bava Kamma 12a, s.v. "Ana, etc.," who follow their own approach, and likewise the Rosh there and in HaGozel. At the end of Bava Batra, however, the Rosh copies the Rif's language, as is his practice. See Shach. In all the cited places the Shulchan Aruch rules that the lien is Torah law.
