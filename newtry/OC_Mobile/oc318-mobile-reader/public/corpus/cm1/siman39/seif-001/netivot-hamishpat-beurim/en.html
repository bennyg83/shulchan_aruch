Or he admitted before witnesses, etc. Sema wondered what a kinyan adds where one merely admits an existing oral debt. The answer is that the ordinary rule that responsibility is treated as a scribal omission rests on the lender's reliance at the time he gives money. If years later the borrower merely admits the old oral debt and makes a kinyan without mentioning responsibility, there is no reason to assume the borrower mistakenly omitted a clause favorable to the lender. The lender did not now part with money. Therefore the kinyan can formalize the admission without automatically creating full encumbrance on later purchasers. The kinyan has substance only where it changes some aspect of the obligation, such as shortening or extending the payment term. In such a case it resembles a new transaction, and ordinary responsibility can again be presumed.
One who lends to his fellow by document, etc. The authorities differ over whether property liens are Torah law. According to some, 'the lien is Torah law' means that the creditor acquires a property-like interest and the debtor cannot fully transfer the encumbered asset away from him. According to the contrary view, the debtor's own property can still be taken from him because payment of a debt is a mitzvah and beit din coerces performance, but there is no Torah lien that follows property into the hands of heirs or purchasers. A practical difference can arise where the borrower later becomes mentally incompetent. Rabbinic law certainly allows collection from the estate, but according to the view that even collection from the borrower rests only on coercing him to fulfill a mitzvah, a person who is not legally obligated in mitzvot cannot be coerced on that Torah basis. I also explain how Rav Papa's statements concerning a debt written in the Torah should be read and why they do not necessarily mean every such debt has the legal force of a written deed. The classic question about collection from heirs under the verse concerning an oath can be resolved through a pledge. If a pledged object has been temporarily returned to the borrower and he has a migo, a partial admission can generate a Torah oath even though ordinary heir collection would not exist under the no-Torah-lien view.
