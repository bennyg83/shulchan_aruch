a) Mishnah, Bava Batra 175a and 175b.
b) Ulla explains there in the Gemara that the debtor's property is subject to the lender by Torah law. The reason an oral loan is not collected from encumbered property, meaning property the debtor sold, is to avoid loss to purchasers. But with a loan documented by a written instrument, there are two publicity factors, the document and the witnesses, which generate public notice. The purchasers therefore caused their own loss, since they knew he was indebted and nevertheless bought from him.
c) Conclusion of the Gemara, Bava Metzia 14a.
d) Statement of Rava, etc., Bava Batra 40a. Rava explains there that an ordinary kinyan is intended to be written. The Rashbam explains that once the debtor made a kinyan, there is no stronger empowerment than that, and presumably he wants the witnesses to write a document for the creditor. The Sema writes in the name of Hagahot Ashri, chapter Zeh Borer, that if one says, "I hereby admit to you by kinyan before witnesses that I owe you," he does not know upon what the kinyan can take effect, since he is not presently transferring anything to him. The Beit Yosef cites this, from siman 66.
