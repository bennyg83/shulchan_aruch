(See there: when does this rule that they do not write apply, etc., until: and these are the words of Rambam in chapter 7 of Hilchot To'en VeNitan, etc.) This is not compelled by their words. Since movable property still requires actual collection and the creditor could sue the borrower again and force him to pay a second time if he held a document, Rambam's reason applies to a loan. By contrast, in the case of a deposit, even if the depositor has a document, he cannot necessarily sue a second time, because the custodian is believed to say that he returned it through a migo that it was lost through unavoidable circumstances, even against a document. Rambam explicitly gives as the reason regarding movable property that the creditor could sue a second time, and the Maggid Mishneh also somewhat implies that Rambam did not write this merely to exclude authorities who hold that even with a loan involving identifiable movable property a document may be written. He did not necessarily intend to exclude a deposit, where the concern of a second claim does not apply. Examine carefully. Rashba, however, in responsum 111, agrees with the Nimukei Yosef's position, and one must therefore interpret the Gemara somewhat forcefully according to that view.
