He obligated himself, etc. This is the Tur's text. The Beit Yosef says that the Baal HaTerumot writes a similar ruling in gate 52. Careful study shows otherwise. The Baal HaTerumot discusses one who obligated himself generally to two people, gave no instruction to the witnesses, and the witnesses delivered the document to one of them. Rabbi Yitzchak disputes that case, the Baal HaTerumot remains uncertain, and Rabbi Natan ben Meir disagrees with Rabbi Yitzchak. But where the obligor told the witnesses to write and place the document in his own hand, they did so, and he himself then delivered it to one recipient, Rabbi Natan does not dispute the result. Indeed, Rabbi Natan writes that when the obligor says, "Make a kinyan from me, write, and give the document into my hand," the creditor does not acquire until it reaches the recipient. Thus, even if the obligor delivered it to one creditor, the other did not acquire immediately. We have only what the obligor actually did: the person to whom he delivered it acquired, but what basis is there for the other to acquire? Perhaps the Tur speaks of a case in which delivery to the first was expressly made so that he should also acquire his colleague's share. This would be acquisition through another person, which Rabbi Natan mentions. The Tur would then teach that even if the second creditor never knew of the delivery, and even if he died, his heirs acquired his share. The matter requires study.
He acquired his share, etc. The Bach explains that because the obligor became liable to both creditors in one document, they are partners and each may collect the entire debt, as stated below in siman 77. In my opinion the cases are not comparable. There both became partners and acquired together from the outset, and the document was written accordingly. Here, who says that the creditor to whom the document was never delivered acquired anything? As I explained above, the Baal HaTerumot does not give the Bach's reason. His case is one in which the obligor did not say that the document should first be placed in his own hand, so both creditors acquired immediately through the kinyan. See there carefully.
