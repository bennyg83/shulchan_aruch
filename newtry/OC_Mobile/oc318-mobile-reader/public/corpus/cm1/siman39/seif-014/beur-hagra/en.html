He obligated. Because one may acquire on behalf of a person even without his knowledge.
(Collected note) He obligated himself, etc. Mishnah in Bava Batra there: "he transferred it to him through another, etc." It concerns a kinyan, as above, and also where the beneficiary did not know, because the opening clause says "and it was found, etc." (End of collected note.)
(Collected note) And even if he died, etc. Gittin at the end of chapter 1, 14b: "one baraita teaches, etc.; let us say the dispute is, etc.; shall we say that 'take' is like 'acquire' according to Tannaim, etc.; some say 'take' is like 'acquire,' etc." See the entire sugya. We learn that where there is an actual acquisition on his behalf, all agree that it is given to his heirs. (End of collected note.)
