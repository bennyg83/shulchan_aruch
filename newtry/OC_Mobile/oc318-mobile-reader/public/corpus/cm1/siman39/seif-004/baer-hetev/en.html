They are permitted. This implies that they may write even a memorandum. The Beit Yosef's statement that they may write a memorandum does not mean that the writing itself will be effective if the borrower later denies the matter while the witnesses do not testify orally before beit din. That cannot be so, because the Responsa of the Rosh make clear that the borrower there did not deny what they had written; he merely said that he had not instructed them to write it. If he denies their written account, the writing plainly has no effect until they testify orally, because testimony must be "from their mouths and not from their writing." The Beit Yosef further writes that where beit din received testimony from witnesses that a person borrowed in their presence, but there was no kinyan and the borrower did not say "write," beit din may not record the testimony in a document, because that would have the force of an act of beit din and the borrower could no longer claim that he paid. It therefore appears that this applies only where no judgment was rendered on the testimony. If they adjudicated the matter and imposed liability based on the testimony, it is an act of beit din and they may issue it as a complete judicial document. Shach.
They disagree. The Nimukei Yosef writes that although an ordinary kinyan is presumed to be intended for writing, it is not presumed to authorize insertion of liability provisions and other enhancements. His statement that liability is a scribal presumption refers to a case where liability was stipulated. However, in Bava Metzia the Nimukei Yosef did not write this. It must therefore be explained that his statement here concerns a guarantor, who receives no benefit from the loan. A borrower or seller, by contrast, receives a benefit, and we therefore presume that he accepted liability in every respect. See siman 61, seif 5. Sema. The Shach writes that as a matter of law the authorities imply that in every case liability is a scribal presumption, and an ordinary kinyan is intended for writing in every respect, so that the resulting document has the full law of a formal instrument. See also siman 238 and Responsa Rashdam, siman 18.
