And there is. See seif 13, "and even if the lender says, etc."
(Collected note) There is one who says, etc. The Mishnah necessarily concerns a kinyan, as stated at the beginning of Bava Metzia. Therefore the latter clause, "and we do not write, etc.," also concerns a kinyan, for otherwise we could not even write for the borrower. The Maggid Mishneh avoids this difficulty, as written in seif 13, "and if the borrower comes to make a kinyan," meaning an actual kinyan. Nevertheless the Ramban's explanation is more natural. The reason we may write for the borrower is because he told the witnesses to write. A kinyan not in the beneficiary's presence certainly operates for all matters, except an ordinary kinyan where, etc. See seif 13 in the gloss. (End of collected note.)
