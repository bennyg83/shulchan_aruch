They write notwithstanding his objection. Sema limits this to a loan document or sale, not a gift. Shach correctly notes that Rashba initially distinguished gift but later retracted and held that after a kinyan the giver can no longer prevent writing. Without kinyan, however, Rashba allows even a seller to object to the deed. Shiltei Gibborim saw tension between two Rashba responsa, but there is none: one lacks kinyan; the other has it. One should not object that after the principal revokes the witnesses' agency their writing becomes invalid written testimony. Just as in a protest document the Sages prevent him from retracting, so too here the kinyan gives the witnesses continuing authority by rabbinic enactment. The same is necessary where the debtor dies before they write, since death ordinarily terminates agency; nevertheless the deed may still be written because the Sages gave the kinyan this force.
An unspecified kinyan. Sema cites Nimukei Yosef that the witnesses should not include responsibility unless specifically instructed, even though omitted responsibility is normally supplied by law. Shach notes that most authorities disagree and hold that an ordinary kinyan is presumed to include responsibility, because one does not give money for nothing. This is also implied by many Shulchan Aruch rulings and is the principal law. Shach cites Rabbeinu Yerucham that if the debtor admitted before one witness and told him to write, then later admitted before another witness and told him to write, the two may combine to write one deed even though he did not tell them jointly. They date it from the second admission, because an earlier date would make the deed antedated.
