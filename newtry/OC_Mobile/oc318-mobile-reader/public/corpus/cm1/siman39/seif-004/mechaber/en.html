If the borrower protests to the witnesses that they must not write a document, some say they may not write it, even if a formal act of acquisition was made. A stipulation that he cannot protest their writing is ineffective. {Rama: See below, siman 243, seif 7.} If the lender says, 'Since you do not want them to write a document for me, return my money,' he is heeded. If the witnesses intend to travel overseas, some say that the lender may go before a beit din, which will write for him, but the witnesses themselves may not write. Others say that even if the borrower stands and cries, 'Do not write a document for him,' they write it against his will, even when the witnesses do not intend to travel. Some say that an ordinary act of acquisition is presumed intended for writing only when made in the lender's presence; if made outside his presence, they do not write. {Rama: Others disagree (Nimukei Yosef, chapter Get Pashut, in the name of the Rashba and Ra'ah).}
