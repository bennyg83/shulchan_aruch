But the witnesses are not permitted to write. Even when they remember the admission, they may not write a document unless the debtor authorized them or a kinyan gave them authority. Writing is not a neutral memorandum, because it converts an oral obligation into documentary evidence with stronger consequences. The rule therefore applies even where the witnesses are trustworthy and the debt itself is clear. 3
They write it for him against his will. Where the legal act itself authorizes a document, the witnesses may write for the creditor even if the debtor now objects. Some disagree with this. The disagreement concerns whether a general kinyan automatically authorizes writing or whether the witnesses must still be expressly instructed. In practice, every place follows the established halachic rule governing documents of acquisition. The decisive distinction is whether the writing merely records a completed transaction or creates new liability. Where it merely records what was already completed, many authorities permit it even without renewed consent; where it adds a lien or other consequence, express authorization is required. 1030100230380
