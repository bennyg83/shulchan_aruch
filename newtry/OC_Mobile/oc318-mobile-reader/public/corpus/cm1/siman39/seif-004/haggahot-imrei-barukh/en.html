(Shach, seif katan 21) An admission before beit din immediately, etc. Note: This requires study. If we are concerned that the actual claimant never appeared before beit din and that another person appeared fraudulently, then it is as though no valid claim was presented. The debtor's admission should therefore not have the status of a judicial admission, which requires a claim by the claimant. I later found this point in the Tumim. One can add that the debtor admitted before beit din that he owed the particular person standing opposite him. Even if he addressed that man as "Shimon" when his true name was not Shimon, an admission directed to that person does not vest a right in some other man actually named Shimon. The court dealt only with the individual standing before it.
