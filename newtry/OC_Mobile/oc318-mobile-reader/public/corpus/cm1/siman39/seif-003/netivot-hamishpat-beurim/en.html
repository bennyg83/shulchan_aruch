If it was authenticated, we adjudicate on its basis. Darkei Moshe and Rama require that the writing have already been authenticated. The reason is that even according to Rabbeinu Tam, who validates written testimony from a witness capable of oral testimony, the testimony is not judicially complete until the writing reaches beit din. At the time it reaches court the witness must still be capable of giving oral testimony. If the writing had already been authenticated in beit din while the witness still remembered, then the written statement had already become judicial testimony and may later be relied upon even if he forgets. But if the document comes before beit din for the first time only after he no longer remembers, he is then like one incapable of oral testimony, whose writing is invalid. A properly drafted formal deed is different because the testimony is treated as judicially complete from the time of signing. This also explains why one witness's written memorandum can differ from a full two-witness deed drafted in ordinary documentary form.
