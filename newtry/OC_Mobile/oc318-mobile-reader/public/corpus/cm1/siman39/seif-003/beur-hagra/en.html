And there are those who disagree. They disagree with Rav Hai and return the case to the ordinary law of an oral loan, where only within the due date do we not suspect repayment. Proof is from Bava Metzia 17a, the distinction between "go out and give," etc., where if he says "I repaid" he is believed even though collection could otherwise be from encumbered property. See Rif there. Ramban in the name of some Geonim.
(Collected note) And there are those who disagree, etc. Their proof is from a found promissory note, where we suspect repayment, and from Bava Metzia 17, "between 'go out and give,' etc.," where we suspect repayment even though collection is from encumbered property, as the Rif writes there. The first opinion responds that a found document is different, for even within its due date we are concerned because the document's condition has been weakened, as written at the end of siman 41. The case of "go out and give" is also no proof, because there the debtor did not anticipate needing a receipt and ordinarily follows beit din's instruction, unlike a kinyan, since an ordinary kinyan is intended for writing and he caused his own loss, as stated in Bava Batra 171b. (End of collected note.)
