We postdated it. The Tur explains the concern. Suppose one borrowed in Nisan, purchased land in Iyar, and the witnesses postdated the loan document to Sivan. He then sold the land purchased in Iyar. The lender could wrongfully collect from the purchaser, because the document appears to show that the loan occurred in Sivan, after the borrower had already acquired the land. Had the true date in Nisan been written, it would be clear that the land was acquired after the loan, and under the rule that where one borrowed, later acquired property, and then sold it, the lender cannot collect from the purchasers, collection would be barred. Therefore, if the document contains an explicit clause obligating after-acquired property, there is no need to state that it was postdated, as explained in siman 43, seif 13. Even according to the opinion that such an after-acquired-property clause is automatically implied as a scribal omission, as discussed in siman 112, one can say that the present case concerns a document that states only partial warranty, where such a clause is not automatically implied, as Rama writes there. Thus the Sema.

They place it with a third party. The Shach finds this difficult. These dissenters necessarily hold that with an ordinary unstated loan term the borrower is not believed to say "I paid" within thirty days, and the Ran expressly says this is their view. Yet we rule at the end of siman 78 that even within thirty days a borrower is believed to say he paid an ordinary loan. The Mechaber therefore seemingly should have written that according to the dissenting opinion, even with an ordinary loan one may not write the document during those thirty days. The Shach says the matter can be reconciled only with difficulty and requires study. One might answer that the Mechaber holds that where two favorable factors combine, namely that the claim is still within thirty days and that a kinyan was made, according to all opinions the borrower cannot claim payment.
