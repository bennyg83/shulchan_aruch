The foregoing applies when no formal act of acquisition was made. If such an act established that the debtor owes one hundred zuz, the witnesses may write and deliver a document even though he did not instruct them to write, because an ordinary act of acquisition is presumed intended for writing. Even if much time passes and the creditor later asks them to write, they may do so without concern that the debt was paid. They either date the document as of the acquisition or write that the document was deliberately written later. Even if the debtor died before the document was written, and even if the payment date had been fixed and passed, they may write it after his death. Likewise, if the creditor died and his heirs ask the witnesses to write the document for them, they may do so. Others disagree and say that if the matter continued more than thirty days, it may not be written; and if a payment date was fixed, it may not be written after that date, because the debt may have been paid. {Rama: If the debtor did not say, 'Write and sign,' then even if they wrote the matter in a document, it is merely a memorandum (Responsa of the Rashba, siman 1111; compare siman 111). Some say that if it is authenticated, judgment may be based upon it. See above, siman 46, seif 10. The requirement to say, 'Write and sign,' applies where a person obligates himself. If he waives a right or releases another from liability, no such instruction is needed. See below, siman 81, seif 29.}
