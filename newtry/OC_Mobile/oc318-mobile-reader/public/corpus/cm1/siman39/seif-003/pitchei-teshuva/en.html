To be concerned that perhaps he repaid. See Baer Heitev. See Responsa Chavos Yair, end of siman 230, who writes that were he not afraid to say something novel, he would say that even if, after the kinyan, the borrower told the witnesses not to write a document, and the lender requested return of his money, but the matter remained unresolved until after the due date and the borrower then claimed that he had paid on time, the borrower should not be believed. Had he paid, he should have informed the witnesses to the kinyan. Although he was not concerned about a written document, he nevertheless should have been concerned that the kinyan itself has the legal force of a document, and therefore at the time of payment he should have notified the witnesses. Nevertheless, it is difficult to extract money on the strength of this reasoning in a case where he protested the writing of a document, for after he felt secure against a document one cannot readily infer from his failure to notify the witnesses, unlike a case where the kinyan included an after acquired property clause.
