(Seif 7: if they were not a fixed court, etc., and did not summon him.) The Sema explains “and did not summon him” as though it meant “or did not summon him.” This is unnecessary. An admission must occur before a body possessing the status of a beit din from two perspectives. From their own side, they must sit as a regularly constituted court in their fixed place. From the litigant’s side, he must submit to their authority by appearing when summoned. The Rambam therefore first states that if they were neither fixed nor had summoned him, both deficiencies exist and they certainly lack the status of a beit din. He then adds that even if the litigant himself assembled them and treated them as a court, they may not write the admission, because their constitution derived from the litigant rather than from themselves. The second category, where they sit as a fixed court but he appears voluntarily without summons, is addressed later in seif 4.
