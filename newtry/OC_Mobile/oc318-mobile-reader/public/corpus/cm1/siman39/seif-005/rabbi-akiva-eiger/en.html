[Shulchan Aruch] And even if they made a kinyan from him, etc., they do not heed him; see below, siman 243. Note: This is not mentioned here by the Tur. Rather, below in siman 243 the Tur writes it in the name of Responsa HaRosh, and the Mechaber wrote it here by comparing the cases through reasoning. It appears that the Tur deliberately omitted it in this siman. Below, Responsa HaRosh explains that making a kinyan from him is ineffective because it is merely a kinyan on words, and no kinyan can take effect upon it, since there is no lien upon property to which the kinyan could attach. In a deed of gift he does not encumber his assets, while in a deed of sale the assets were already encumbered from the time of the sale. This is cited by the Sema, siman 243, seif katan 19. Here, however, in the case of a loan, the document is effective to encumber his property, because one who lends to another before witnesses cannot collect from encumbered property. Therefore, a kinyan by which he obligates himself to provide a document is a kinyan concerning property, not merely words. Still, it appears to me that the Mechaber can be defended. Even though the deed creates a lien on property, this is a kinyan of "I will give," which according to several authorities is ineffective. The Tur, in siman 243, gives the reason stated above because he wished to state a rule accepted without dispute, whereas the effectiveness of a kinyan of "I will give" depends upon a dispute among the authorities; see Tur, siman 157. Although the Mechaber, siman 245:1, also cites the dispute, it appears there that he regards the first view, that it is ineffective, as primary. According to the other authorities, however, it appears to me that if the borrower received a kinyan obligating him to provide a deed, he must provide it. All this applies where he borrowed and then made a kinyan to provide a deed. But if they first made a general kinyan from him upon the debt, and afterward again made a kinyan from him to provide a deed as evidence, then according to all opinions he may object, because by the first kinyan his property was already encumbered and the creditor may collect from encumbered property. The second kinyan is then merely a kinyan on words, comparable to a deed of gift or sale.
