(Seif 5: where he did not produce a document.) At first glance this implies that even if the witnesses to the kinyan appear, the absence of a document weakens the claimant’s case, like the dissent cited by the Rama in siman 70, seif 1. This cannot be correct. Rashba explicitly writes that even though witnesses made a kinyan, and even testified that the debt was still within its term, the borrower is believed to say he paid because the claimant’s failure to hold a document weakens his claim. This shows Rashba spoke specifically of ordinary eyewitnesses, not kinyan witnesses. The reason is that kinyan witnesses may write the document even over the borrower’s protest, according to Rashba, responsum 1034, so it is as though they had already written it and the claim is not weakened. This also resolves the Mechaber’s statement in seif 1 that if witnesses made a kinyan the borrower cannot claim payment, since the creditor may collect from encumbered property. In seif 3, however, after the due date, some disagree and suspect payment, and the Rama’s dissent in siman 70 concerns a debt after its term. Even then, if the creditor requested that the document be written but delay resulted from the scribe or witnesses, his claim is not weakened, as follows from the Ran cited by the Beit Yosef.
