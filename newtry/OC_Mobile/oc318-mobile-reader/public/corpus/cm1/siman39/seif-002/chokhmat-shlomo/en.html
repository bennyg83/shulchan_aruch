Seif 10. In the gloss, “and if they did not say to him.” See Shach. In my marginal notes there I indicated a difficulty with the Shach from siman 46, seif 24. In my sixth edition to Even HaEzer, siman 39, I resolved it; see there. Seif 13. “When is this said? With a document containing a kinyan.” See Shach, seif katan 39, who writes in the name of the Rosh that because the Rif distinguishes waiver from a loan, he must likewise distinguish a loan from a sale. I was asked what connection there is between these matters. The explanation is as follows. The Rosh was troubled by the Rif's statement in the sugya of one who finds a promissory note that even though the document never reached the creditor, waiver is different. From the Rif's conclusion the Rosh proves that the Rif must hold that, with a sale, we do not say that the witnesses acquire retroactively for the recipient. The reason is that the witnesses do not acquire immediately on his behalf. Rather, if the document ultimately reaches him, their act is treated as acquiring retroactively. That may suffice where the property itself had not already been sold and only a lien intervened, because the witnesses can uproot a mere lien retroactively. But where a purchaser already acquired the actual property, they cannot retroactively uproot an actual completed sale. The Rosh therefore asks: even if the document reaches the creditor now, perhaps the debtor had already sold the property, and with a sale we cannot say that the witnesses acquired retroactively on the creditor's behalf. The necessary answer is that waiver is stronger. There it is unnecessary for the document ultimately to reach the recipient; the witnesses acquire immediately from the outset, before any later sale. Once the lien has already been acquired for him, a subsequent sale is ineffective against it. But where the acquisition can operate only if the document later reaches him and then relate back retroactively, the witnesses do not have power to divest property that was actually sold in the interim. This explains the Rif's words. Because I have seen many great scholars raise this difficulty, I have explained it here.
