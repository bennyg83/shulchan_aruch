So that they should not convert an oral loan into a written loan. To avoid the defect of testimony from writing, it is enough that the borrower tell the witnesses, 'Write and sign,' even if he does not yet say, 'Give it.' The proof is Rama's rule that without 'write and sign' the writing is merely a memorandum. But to create the full collection rights of a written loan against purchasers, the borrower must also authorize delivery. Thus if he said, 'Write, sign, and give,' and later retracts before delivery, the writing was still originally made under his instruction and is not disqualified as testimony from writing, even though the lender may lose the purchaser-enforcement effect.
They must consult him. Rosh asks why, if a person says, 'Give this object to so-and-so,' the agents need not return for confirmation, while here the witnesses must consult him again before delivery. The answer is that here he appointed them to two successive tasks, first writing and then giving, and no kinyan was made. The absence of a kinyan suggests that he may wish to reconsider after the writing is complete. Therefore, once the writing mission is finished, they must consult him before performing the second mission of delivery. This is different from an ordinary instruction simply to deliver an existing gift document.
