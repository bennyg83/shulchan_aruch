One who lends money to another, etc. When a loan is made in the presence of witnesses, the witnesses may not write a note without the borrower's authorization unless the transaction itself included a kinyan that empowered them to do so. An oral loan remains collectible from free property only, because writing creates a lien against purchasers and may expose the borrower to greater liability than he accepted. The same distinction applies to an admission before witnesses.
Some say they need not consult him. This refers to a case involving a kinyan, where the witnesses may write the instrument without returning to obtain fresh authorization. The opposing view requires consultation where the original language did not clearly direct them to write. The practical ruling depends on the nature of the kinyan and the wording used before the witnesses.
