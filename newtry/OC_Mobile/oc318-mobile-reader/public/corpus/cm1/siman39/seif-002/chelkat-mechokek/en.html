Because we are concerned that she may go to a Sage. This "some say" is one opinion in the Yerushalmi, cited by the Ran in chapter HaMadir. The Yerushalmi teaches that if vows are found upon her, she is not betrothed. One tanna says she may marry another without a get; another says she may not marry without a get, lest she go to a Sage who annuls the vow and the kiddushin then take effect retroactively, rendering her children from the second union mamzerim. The Rashba and Ran, however, rule like the view that we are not concerned that she will deliberately cause her own ruin. The same rule is stated below in siman 143: where fulfillment of a condition is in her hands and consists of passive nonperformance, we do not fear that she will violate it. How, then, can the ruling here require a get because she might undo the vow? One could have interpreted the passage not as regulating her ability to remarry, but only to teach that because both Yerushalmi opinions agree that later annulment of the vow can validate the kiddushin, the man is prohibited to her relatives. But the Rama's gloss, "therefore she may not marry without a get," remains difficult. The Ran did not say that we should be concerned for the restrictive opinion of the Yerushalmi; he only shows that annulment can help even after the husband learned of the vow. It would have been preferable to place the "some say" in the following seif, after the rule that annulment before discovery helps but afterward does not, which is Tosafot's view. There it would be appropriate to cite the Yerushalmi that annulment helps even after discovery. If she actually goes to a Sage she is betrothed, but if she has already married another man we do not suspect that she will later do so, because she will certainly not destroy herself, as explained.
