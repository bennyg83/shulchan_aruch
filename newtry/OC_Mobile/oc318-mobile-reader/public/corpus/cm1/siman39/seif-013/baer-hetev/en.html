They do not. The Shach writes that the primary reason is as the Bach explains: because writing such a document would be false, we are concerned that the lender is scheming. Perhaps the document will fall from the witnesses' hands or the lender will collect on it before it ever reaches the borrower. The Sema writes that even if the borrower comes before witnesses without the lender and they make a kinyan recording that he owes the lender a certain amount, if the lender later comes and asks them to write a document based on that kinyan they do not comply. A kinyan made outside the lender's presence is not ordinarily intended to be written. All of this applies where he asks them to write and sign a formal document. Mere writing without signature presents no problem, as is evident from the Beit Yosef.
When does this apply. This refers back to the beginning of the seif: even for the borrower, the witnesses do not write a document unless they made a kinyan from him. This is the view of the Rif and Rambam. Tosafot, the Rosh, and Tur disagree and hold that the witnesses' signatures acquire the right for the lender from the time of signing even if the document does not reach him until later. Rabbeinu Yerucham distinguishes between something not presently under the recipient's control, such as a loan document, where the witnesses' signatures do not acquire for him, and something already in his control, where he acquires from the time of signing. See below, siman 65, seif 13. The Sema expresses surprise that Rama omits this distinction entirely. Perhaps Rama relied on Nimukei Yosef, who writes that many great authorities rule like the Rif. See also siman 43, seif 15; siman 49, seif 7; and siman 65, seif 13. The Shach likewise discusses Rama at length and writes that certainly regarding a receipt or similar document, the person in possession may say, "I rely on the authorities who disagree with the Rif." If, however, the receipt never later reached his hand, such as where it was lost after being found, it appears that according to all opinions we do not say that the witnesses' signatures acquire it for him. The Ramban in Sefer HaMilchamot likewise rules that the document must eventually reach his hand.
And he shall give. Rambam writes this. Tosafot, however, state that according to all opinions, if the lender is present together with the borrower before the witnesses, there is no concern, because the borrower will certainly deliver the document to him immediately. Thus the Sema. The Shach says he does not know where Tosafot say this. The Perishah cites Tosafot, Gittin 17b, s.v. "until the time of delivery." In my humble opinion that passage is not proof. Tosafot say only that the lender need not later prove when the document reached him, because witnesses do not write a loan document unless the lender is present, so we assume the matter was conducted properly and the document was delivered immediately. It may nevertheless be that the witnesses themselves must know it was delivered in their presence. See Tosafot there.
And they wrote. The Rashba writes that there is no objection to writing in the document, "We wrote and delivered the document into his hand," even if it was actually delivered to his agent, because a person's agent's possession is legally his own domain. Sema.
