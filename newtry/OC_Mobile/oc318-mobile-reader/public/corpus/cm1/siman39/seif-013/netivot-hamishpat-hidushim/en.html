In a document that contains a kinyan. Whether such a document must actually reach the lender is disputed among the decisors. This issue concerns specifically a kinyan made outside the lender's presence. If the kinyan occurred in the lender's presence, then certainly even if no document was written at all he may collect from encumbered property, and all the more so if a document was written but never physically reached him. Many decisors disagree with the Mechaber and are cited by the Sema and Shach. They hold that even a document without a kinyan, if it later reaches the lender, is treated as though the witnesses' agency operated retroactively, and the possessor may assert kim li. The same applies to a gift of land: if the donor later gives the same land to a second person and the first deed afterward reaches the first recipient, the first recipient acquires retroactively. With a receipt, the Rif holds that physical delivery is unnecessary. Nevertheless, actual payment is certainly required. Once payment occurred, however, the debt is considered remitted retroactively from the time the receipt was signed. This retroactive remission applies only where he said, "Give the receipt into my hand." If he merely said, "Write a receipt," the remission is immediate, as explained in siman 81 by the Shach, seif katan 74. See Beurim. Regarding the Shach's statement that a standard kinyan applies whether or not the loan is ultimately made, see Beurim for the explanation.
