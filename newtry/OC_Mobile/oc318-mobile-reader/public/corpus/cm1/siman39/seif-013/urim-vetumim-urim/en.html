They write a document, etc. When the wording makes clear that the borrower alone appeared, the witnesses need identify only the borrower, not the lender. Sema.
They do not write it against his will. A lender may object because he does not want a reputation of wealth to spread about him and thereby expose him to demands. This is obvious.
They wrote and signed the document, etc. The concern is that it appears false. Mere writing does not create that appearance, as explained below in siman 48. See Tumim concerning the Shach's difficulty. Sema writes that even if a kinyan was actually made from the borrower, but it occurred in the lender's absence, since a kinyan made outside the lender's presence does not automatically stand for writing, as explained above in seif 4, the witnesses should not comply even if the lender says, "Write it and keep it with you until the borrower instructs you to deliver it." In Tumim I wrote that in such a case there is no reason for concern, especially because there is no appearance of falsehood since the underlying transaction is true and the kinyan already occurred. See there, where I noted that the positions of Ramban and Maggid Mishneh almost contradict each other.
To make a kinyan from him. Meaning, to make the kinyan today. Otherwise the document would be antedated, since the lien begins only at the time of the kinyan. Sema. The words "to make a kinyan from him" are really unnecessary, since the text could simply have said, "If he agrees, give it to him." It is merely conventional phrasing.
In a document containing a kinyan, etc. Sema and Shach both question why the Mechaber and Rama did not mention the view of Tosafos and Rosh that no kinyan is required because we rule like Abaye that a witness's signature effects acquisition. They conclude that the party in possession may say that he relies upon the opinion that a witness's signature effects acquisition, and they are correct. As to the underlying rule, whether it applies also to a sale document and a receipt, and whether in every context the document must actually reach the recipient's hand, see what I wrote below in siman 65, where I discussed these matters at length in Tumim and clarified them with God's help.
Or if it is written in the document, etc. See Tumim, where I resolved the Shach's difficulty with the Ran. Nevertheless, I concluded for practical halachah that this rule remains a legal doubt dependent upon a dispute among the authorities. See there.
Until the lender is with him. The same applies if the lender instructs the witnesses to acquire the document on his behalf and to hold it for him until he wishes to receive it. They then serve as the lender's agents and it is as though the lender were present. Nimukei Yosef, cited by Sema and Shach.
And he gives the document into the lender's hand, etc. This excludes Tosafos in Gittin 17b, s.v. "until the time," who write that if lender and borrower are together, the witnesses need not supervise actual delivery because it may be presumed that the document will be handed over. Other authorities require actual delivery into the lender's hand. Sema.
Into the lender's hand, etc. Even if the witnesses did not see the money being given, once the document was delivered to the lender there is no further concern. This is unlike Rashi, who also requires the witnesses to see the money delivered.
Or he will lend, etc. Meaning that even where they saw the money being given, so there is no concern that the loan might not occur, there remains a concern that the borrower may not deliver the document. In that case the creditor may seize purchasers only from the date of delivery, not from the earlier date. All the more so where the witnesses did not see the money, for there are two concerns, that the loan may never occur and that the document may never be delivered. In truth, however, everything depends upon delivery. If the document was delivered, it makes no difference that the money had not yet been lent, as explained above.
And they shall write for him. From this Sema and Bach infer that even where a kinyan occurred, if it was made outside the lender's presence it does not automatically stand for writing, in accordance with Ramban and the Mechaber above at the end of seif 4. Therefore the borrower must expressly say, "Write." See Tumim concerning the Rosh's view.
And he shall not acquire, etc. See Tumim, where I left this law requiring study. It appears clear that if there was a kinyan, then even if the document was subsequently returned to the borrower, he nevertheless remains liable to pay, and only the document itself reverts to him. Once he made a kinyan, he ordinarily undertook liability whether or not the loan ultimately occurred. What then is achieved by delivering the document back to the borrower? See there.
Until it leaves the possession of, etc. At that point the lender acquires the rights retroactively from the time of the kinyan according to all opinions. Shach.
