A promissory note may be written for the borrower even when the lender is not present. However, if the lender protests that the document not be written for the borrower, it may not be written against his will. A promissory note may not be written for the lender unless the borrower is present, as stated by the Maggid Mishneh in chapter 23 of Hilkhot Malveh. Even if the lender tells the witnesses, "Write and sign the document, keep it in your possession, and if the borrower comes to make a kinyan, give it to me, but if not, tear it up," they do not heed him. This rule applies where the document contains a kinyan, because from the moment the kinyan is made the borrower's property becomes encumbered, or where the document states explicitly that the property becomes encumbered from now. But with a document that contains no kinyan, even if the money is handed to the borrower in our presence, the document may not be written even for the borrower unless the lender is present and the document is delivered to the lender in our presence. Otherwise, perhaps the document will be written now for a loan dated in Nisan, but the lender will not actually lend until Tishrei, or perhaps he will lend but the document will not be delivered until Tishrei, and the lender would then wrongfully collect from purchasers on the basis of a lien dating from Nisan even though the document did not come into his possession until Tishrei. Rama: If the borrower says, "Make a kinyan from me that I owe so and so such and such, and write it for him," the witnesses write it and give it to the creditor. They do not give it to the borrower. If instead he says, "Write it and give it into my hand," they may not give it to the creditor, and the creditor acquires no rights until the document leaves the borrower's possession.
