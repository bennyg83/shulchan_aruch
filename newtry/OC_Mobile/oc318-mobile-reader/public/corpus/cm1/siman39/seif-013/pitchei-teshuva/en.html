He may retract. See Baer Heitev, who writes that it appears he must reimburse him for the scribe’s fee. See Rabbi Akiva Eiger, end of responsum 134, who brings a proof from the responsum of the Rosh cited below in Shulchan Aruch, siman 333, seif 8: if one tells a craftsman, “Make this particular article and I will buy it from you,” and the craftsman makes it, but afterward the customer refuses to take it, he is liable under the law of garmi. See there further. A portion of this will be cited below in siman 209, seif katan 11. His words there indicate that this is specifically where the lender told him to have the document written. As to Baer Heitev’s further statement that this law appears to remain a doubt in law, see Mishkenot Yaakov, responsum 19, and Beit Ephraim, Choshen Mishpat, responsum 20. See also what I write below in siman 190, seif 2, under the words “with money.” As to Baer Heitev’s further statement that leading authorities disputed the Mabit concerning one who sold wool to another, see what I write below in siman 96, seif 6, seif katan 2.
