(Collected note) And if the borrower comes to make a kinyan, etc. The case necessarily concerns an acquisition document, as stated in Bava Metzia 13a. (End of collected note.)
When does this apply? According to the Rif's explanation.
(Collected note) When does this apply, etc. Unlike Abaye, because Rava disagrees with him there 21a, and the halacha follows Rava. If the text reads Rabbah, a student's view does not prevail against his teacher. Likewise on 35b Rabbah and Rava disagree with him, according to the Rif. The Rosh, however, rejects these proofs: from Abaye and Rava onward the halacha follows the later authorities; on 35 there is no proof that Rava disagrees on this point. He writes that Tosafot rule like Abaye as above. Tosafot in Ketubot 94b, s.v. "Katav, etc.," write likewise. The Rosh there writes that according to the Rif, Abaye's rule that a signed document takes effect from its signing applies only when it has reached the beneficiary's hand with respect to an item not already in his possession. But with a receipt and the like, delivery is unnecessary. Further, with a sale or gift we do not say that the witnesses' signatures effect acquisition, and if the property was sold to another before delivery, the second purchaser acquires. This resolves Tosafot's difficulty in Ketubot. In the name of Rashi, however, the Rosh writes that he disagrees with all these distinctions: there is no distinction between receipt, loan, sale, and gift. The Shach rules accordingly. See the Rosh at the end of the chapter. (End of collected note.)
(Collected note) When does this apply, etc. Unlike Abaye, because Rava disagrees with him there 21a and the halacha follows Rava. So too Rav Asi there 13a, Rabbi Abba bar Mamal there 19, see Tosafot there, s.v. "Beraita, etc.," and Rabbah there 35b according to our textual reading. See Tosafot there and Ketubot 104b. Or he wrote. According to Rashi's explanation.
(Collected note) Or he wrote, etc. See Rashi there, s.v. "beShtarei Hakna'ah, etc." Rivash, siman 161, explains Rashi's words according to the Rif: Rashi's statement "he transfers, etc." gives the reason why it is permitted with an acquisition document. Otherwise Rashi is difficult, because what do lender and borrower have to do here if this is simply a document creating an obligation? The Rosh, however, explains Rashi literally, that he stipulates, etc. The implication would then be specifically that no loan occurs at all. The Ran in chapter 2 of Ketubot asks: if so, why did they say there that a trust document is invalid because it is an injustice, etc., meaning perhaps he will claim it from the borrower? Why not say it is antedated? Rather, even if he says "from now, if I later lend, etc." The Rav's words here follow this and also resolve Rivash's difficulty. (End of collected note.)
Even if he delivered. Bava Metzia 19: "when did the promissory note reach, etc.?" Until the document is written and delivered, giving the money creates only an oral loan.
(Collected note) Even if he delivered, etc. Bava Metzia 19a and Bava Batra 167b, "we signed and delivered it, etc." See Rashbam there. One might distinguish those cases because the document was lost and thereby weakened; see Sema, seif katan 41. (End of collected note.)
He said, make a kinyan. Ketubot 55a: "write and sign and give it to him; make a kinyan from him, etc."
(Collected note) He said, make a kinyan from me, etc. Bava Batra 135b, one who died and a will was found, etc. Rabbeinu Chananel, Rif, Rambam, and all the Geonim explain that it concerns a kinyan, and there he transferred it through another person. These are the two distinctions stated here. So too Rabbeinu Chananel explains Bava Metzia 13a: this applies where the document reached his hand, even in acquisition documents according to Rav Asi. See siman 65, seif 14, and Beer HaGolah there. Rashi there disagrees; see s.v. "Hanicha, etc." This requires study. It also appears from the Gemara there, which says, "but then this, etc.; this is understandable according to Rav Asi, etc.," and it does not raise the same question from the Mishnah of a found will. The Rif similarly rules in chapter 4 of Yevamot regarding "leave it with witnesses, etc.," that so long as the document remains in the witnesses' hands the recipient has not acquired, and the rule that written testimony is treated as examined testimony does not change this. See there and Baal HaTerumot, sha'ar 52, chelek 1 and chelek 4. (End of collected note.)
(Collected note) And they wrote. Specifically so, as stated by the Shulchan Aruch in seif 4; Sema and Shach. (End of collected note.)
And they do not give. Bava Metzia 13b: when the borrower admits, etc.; we never give the document to the borrower.
But if. Bava Batra 152: perhaps he did not finally intend, etc.; the same applies here.
