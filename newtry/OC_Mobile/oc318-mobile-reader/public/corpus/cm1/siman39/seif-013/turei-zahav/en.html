Since the borrower could have claimed repayment, we ordinarily advance for purchasers the claim that perhaps he repaid and the present parties are colluding. I found, however, in Responsa Maharit an explanation as follows. The case is where, after the borrower left beit din, the lender came to have a document written. Since we know that he did not repay, and the borrower himself admits this, a document may be written and the lender may collect from encumbered property from that point onward because the matter has publicity and resembles a written loan. This applies specifically where the borrower originally refused to obey the judgment, left in defiance, and was placed under ban. There is then a presumption that he did not pay until beit din was notified, in accordance with the Rif's view. Thus, in siman 79 the Tur writes according to the Rif, while here he writes according to the Rosh, and there is no actual disagreement between them. See there in part 3, siman 46, contrary to the Beit Yosef. This also resolves Mahari ibn Lev's difficulty in part 1, page 109. See also the cited work.
