They write a document for the borrower, etc. I already explained the reason above, nearby, in Sema seif katan 20: in such a case they write it when they recognize the borrower alone, even though they do not recognize the lender, because they do not write it in direct-address language and a person does not ordinarily sin for no benefit to himself; see there.
They do not write it against his will. Because he does not wish to create a public impression that he is wealthy, especially concerning something that is not his.
Even if the lender tells the witnesses, 'Write,' etc. See Perishah there, where I explained the novelty: even if the borrower came before witnesses without the lender and they made a kinyan from him that he owes a certain person such and such, nevertheless, if the lender later comes and tells the witnesses, 'Write me a document against that borrower who made a kinyan before you and admitted owing me such and such,' they do not listen to the lender. Since the kinyan occurred outside the lender's presence, such a kinyan is not presumed to have been made for the purpose of writing a document. This is the view of the Tur and Rambam, and of Ramban, cited above in Sema seif katan 28.
They wrote the document and signed it. Mere writing is not objectionable so long as they did not sign it. This is also evident from the Beit Yosef.
If the borrower comes to make a kinyan, etc. Meaning, if he comes today. Otherwise the document would be antedated.
When does this apply? To a document containing a kinyan. This refers back to the beginning of the seif and teaches that even for the borrower they do not later write a document merely because a kinyan had been made from him. This is the view of Rif and Rambam. Tosafot, Rosh, and Tur disagree and hold that the witnesses' signatures confer the right upon the lender from the time of signing even if the document does not reach him until later. The Tur writes this in the name of Ri. Rabbeinu Yerucham gives a third view, distinguishing between something not in one's possession, such as a loan document, where we do not say that the witnesses' signatures confer the right until the document reaches him, and something that is already under his control, where he acquires from the time of signing. See below siman 65, seif 13. It is surprising that Rama omitted their view entirely. Perhaps his reason is that Nimukei Yosef in the first chapter of Bava Metzia and in chapter Get Pashut writes that many great authorities rule like Rif. See also below siman 43, seif 15, siman 49, seif 7, and siman 65, seif 13, where I discuss this further. See Darkei Moshe, seif 23, where he cites Rivash, siman 161, and many other authorities who hold that with a kinyan document it need not reach the lender's hand at all, unlike Rif. See also below siman 65, seif 16, where Darkei Moshe cites Rif and Rabbeinu Chananel as holding this view.
But a document without a kinyan, etc., they do not write, etc. Nimukei Yosef in the first chapter of Bava Metzia writes that even according to Rif and Rambam this applies specifically if they will give the document to the borrower or it will remain for some time in the witnesses' hands. But if they give it immediately to the lender, or the witnesses immediately acquire it on the lender's behalf, it is as though the lender himself acquired it and it is in his possession, for one may acquire on another's behalf in his absence. So writes Darkei Moshe, seif 23.
And he shall place the document in the lender's hand in our presence. So writes Rambam. Tosafot, however, write that according to all opinions, if the lender is present together with the borrower before the witnesses, we are no longer concerned, for he will certainly hand it to him immediately. See Perishah.
Lest he write, etc. Meaning, when he did not hand the money to the borrower in our presence, we are concerned lest he write, etc. With respect to when he did hand over the money, the reason given is, 'or he will not deliver,' etc.
The correct reading is: 'And he will not borrow from him until Tishrei,' or 'he will borrow, but he will not deliver,' etc.
And they wrote for him, etc. From this too I inferred in Perishah that the Tur follows Ramban, that a kinyan made by the borrower outside the lender's presence is not presumed to have been made for writing. Therefore the Tur requires him to tell the witnesses, 'Write.' See there.
They write and give it to him. Rashba, siman 10154, writes that there is no objection to writing in the document, 'we wrote and gave the document into his hand,' even if they gave it to his agent, for 'his hand' means his legal domain. See Darkei Moshe, siman 42, seif 12.
