(Shach, same passage) Although with a borrowed item everyone agrees that the borrower becomes obligated, etc. Note: In my humble opinion one can distinguish. With an axe, the borrower's obligation is consideration for the right placed in his hand to use it. When the lender retracts and terminates that right, the corresponding obligation likewise terminates. But where a borrower writes a debt document encumbering his assets before the loan money has reached his hand, if the lender is not required to give him the money, the borrower received nothing in exchange for the lien he granted. With the axe, however, before retracting the lender delivered it for the borrower's use, and the borrower thereby acquired a present right to use it for the period of the loan. At the very least, because the power of use was in his hand, he bore the status of borrower throughout that period until the lender retracted. His property lien was the consideration for that right. See Shitah Mekubetzet to Bava Metzia 99, passage beginning "Rav Huna said."
