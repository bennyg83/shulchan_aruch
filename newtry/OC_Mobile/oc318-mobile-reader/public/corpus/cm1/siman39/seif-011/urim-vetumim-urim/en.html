An admission document. The same applies to a loan document. In both cases the witnesses must have been told to write it. Otherwise it does not have the status of a document. Shach.
In this time, etc. Because people are not sufficiently expert in these laws and may not know that they cannot write unless the admitting party explicitly told them to do so.
Regarding collection from purchasers, etc. From the debtor himself one may collect, because the witnesses are not suspected of lying. See Tumim concerning why this is not disqualified as testimony from writing. From purchasers, however, one may not collect even where there is no concern of payment, such as where the document contains a clause of credibility, because unless the debtor said, "Write," no lien took effect against purchasers. If the witnesses are asked and say, "He told us to write," they are believed. This appears from a responsum of Rashba cited by Beit Yosef in the additional rulings, item 21. See there.
