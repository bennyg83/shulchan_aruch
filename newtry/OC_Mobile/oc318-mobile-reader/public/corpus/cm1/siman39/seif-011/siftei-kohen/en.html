A document of admission, etc. The same applies to a loan document. In a loan as well, the witnesses may not write until the borrower tells them, "Write and sign," as stated above, seif 2.
We are concerned with respect to collection from purchasers. This means that even where there is no concern that the borrower repaid, for example because he granted the lender special credibility and similar cases, we still are concerned about improper collection from purchasers. See Gidulei Terumah, gate 42, end of part 5, whose difficulty on this point is readily resolved. See the next seif katan carefully.
With respect to collection from purchasers. But against the borrower himself, so that he cannot deny the obligation, the writing is valid, because witnesses do not write and sign a falsehood. So states Hagahot Ashri there. This is difficult to me, however, because we hold that testimony must be "from their mouths and not from their writing," as I explained at length above in seif katan 8. If so, the writing is not testimony at all. Hagahot Ashri can perhaps be understood as following Rabbeinu Tam, but the Mechaber above, siman 28, seifim 11 and 12, ruled like Rashi and Rambam. His ruling here therefore requires study.
