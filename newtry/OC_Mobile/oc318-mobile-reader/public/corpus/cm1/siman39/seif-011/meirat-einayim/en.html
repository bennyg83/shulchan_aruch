An acknowledgment document that was produced, etc. The Tur's wording is: 'An acknowledgment document signed by two,' etc., and later: 'When does this apply? Where it was written in a form showing that they were not acting as a beit din, such as a memorandum of testimony, etc. But if it was written in the language of a beit din,' etc. There it is explained that the reason is that, since it is known that they were not a beit din, they would not have written the acknowledgment unless he had told them, 'Write it,' etc. The Mechaber adopts the language of the Gemara and Rambam, and it appears that his intent is likewise what the Tur wrote. What is written here in seif 11 concerning an acknowledgment document also refers to a document signed by only two. In seif 12 immediately afterward he writes that if it was drafted in the language of a beit din but only two signed it, since there is a defect before us we are concerned that they erred and thought that two judges have the status of a beit din, following Shmuel's view that two who adjudicated have rendered a valid judgment. The halacha, however, does not follow him, but Rabbi Abbahu, as explained above in siman 3. Therefore, even if they summoned him to appear and he admitted before them, they do not have the status of a beit din for purposes of writing his acknowledgment unless he instructed them to write it. Consequently, it is not treated as a valid document. The general rule is that any acknowledgment document presented to a beit din is, as a matter of law, maintained in its presumption of validity unless there is a defect in the document. Understand this well.
That the scribes nowadays, etc. The reason is that our scribes are not expert in the laws. See Perishah.
With respect to collecting from purchasers. But one may collect from the person who made the acknowledgment himself, for we do not suspect them of fabricating the basic substance of the matter.
