Even if they are still intact. If the entrusted movables were already consumed or used, it is obvious that the recipient does not want a deed standing over him and pressing him harshly. Even where the movables remain intact and must simply be returned, writing can still harm him. The same is true of an ordinary deposit that he may not use at all: perhaps he will return it and forget to obtain his deed, and then he would be unable to plead payment. Although in some cases he has a migo of unavoidable loss, a Torah oath may still be required. Sema and Nimukei Yosef. In Tumim I explain that regarding an admission concerning deposited movables where there is no responsibility and no laws of custodianship, one can write because the foregoing concern does not apply. This appears to be Tur's intent.
