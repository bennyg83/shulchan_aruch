It is not only where he admitted owing money, which is given for expenditure, or borrowed movables that were lost, where he may not want a document written because without one he can delay and say, "I do not presently have money to pay." Even if the movables are still extant, the rule applies. With borrowed movables, so long as he still needs them for use he may prefer not to have a document written against him, so that he can delay until he no longer needs them. And even with deposited movables still intact, where this reasoning does not apply, they still may not write a document on the admission, because perhaps he will later return the items and the document holder will demand them again on the basis of the document. This differs from an admission concerning land, as the Mechaber concludes.
