For in every case he is believed. See Baer Heitev and Avodat HaGershuni, end of responsum 51. See also Chacham Tzvi, seif katan 9, concerning one who possesses a written judgment against another granting him the right to choose one of two rooms belonging to the defendant. One of those rooms later collapsed. The defendant claims, “Before it collapsed you had already chosen that room, and your loss is your own misfortune,” while the claimant denies this and says that he had not yet chosen either room. Who is believed? Chacham Tzvi ruled in favor of the claimant. Certainly, according to the opinion that where there is a written judgment the opposing party is not believed to say, “I paid,” here too the defendant is not believed to say, “You already chose.” Even according to the opinion that he is believed to say, “I paid,” that applies to claims involving movable property or money, matters which ordinarily are not effected by a written instrument. A claim concerning land, however, is one for which a document is ordinarily written, and its legal acts are not completed except through a document. So long as no document was made recording this selection, the defendant is not believed. Moreover, who says that the claimant acquired the room merely by verbal declaration? Perhaps even if he expressly said, “I choose this room for myself,” nothing was yet effected and he did not acquire the room until he selected it and took possession through one of the recognized modes of acquiring land. Until he made such an acquisition, he could retract and choose the other room. From the defendant’s own words it appears clear that no kinyan or act of possession occurred, only a verbal statement.
