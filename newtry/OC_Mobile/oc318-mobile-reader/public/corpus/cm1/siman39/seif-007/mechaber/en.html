When no formal act of acquisition was made, even if a person admitted before three judges, they may not write the admission if they were not a regularly constituted beit din and did not summon him. This remains so even if he assembled and seated them and said, 'Act as my judges.' If the claimant later asks them to write the admission, they do not, because the debtor may pay and then face a claim based on the document. If, however, three judges were sitting on their own in their regular location, the claimant presented his claim, they sent an officer for the defendant, and he appeared and admitted the debt, they write the admission and give it to the opposing party, even if he did not say, 'You are my witnesses.' The beit din must recognize both parties so that they cannot collude to impose liability upon someone else.
