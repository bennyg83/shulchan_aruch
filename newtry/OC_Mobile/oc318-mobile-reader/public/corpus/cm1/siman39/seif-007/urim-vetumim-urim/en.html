They do not write, etc. Shach cites Nimukei Yosef that this applies only where there had been no actual litigation and dispute before beit din. If the parties argued and the court decided between them, the court members are judges and can always write the ruling. Shach says he found no dissent. In Tumim I show that Rambam and Baal HaTerumot disagree, and Mechaber follows them, not Nimukei Yosef.
They write. Baal HaTerumot writes that if beit din merely received testimony that Reuven lent Shimon one hundred or that Shimon admitted it, but Shimon never told them to write and no kinyan was made, the court may not create a written record of the testimony that would deprive Shimon of the plea of payment and convert an oral loan into a written one. Shach says that if the court issued a formal judgment on the testimony, it may write, following Nimukei Yosef. That follows Shach's view that Nimukei Yosef is undisputed. But since I have shown that Mechaber does not follow Nimukei Yosef, even after issuing a judgment the court should not write unless the other requirements are met.
