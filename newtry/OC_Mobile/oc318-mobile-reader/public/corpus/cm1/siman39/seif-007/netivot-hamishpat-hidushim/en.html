They were not sitting as a fixed beit din, or they did not summon him. Meaning, either condition was lacking. This applies specifically where he admitted voluntarily. But if the case was litigated on claims and he became liable through a judicial ruling, they have the status of judges and may always write a judgment record when the borrower is informed. Until it is written, however, he may still be believed to say he paid. See Netivot, seif katan 8, and the Shach in the name of Nimukei Yosef. The reason that an admission is not written where they neither sat formally nor summoned him is that in such circumstances he does not expect them to create a written obligation and may pay without taking a receipt. If they formally sat and summoned him, he expects that they may write and will not pay without a receipt. But if they notify the borrower, they may write even without those formalities. The Shach has a different approach, explained below.
So that they should not engage in deception. Specifically where both parties appeared before beit din or witnesses, those writing the document must identify the lender as well. Otherwise the lender may owe money to someone else and change his own name in order to keep the debt he is about to collect away from his own creditor. In addition, a person does not want false publicity that someone owes him money. If only the borrower appears, however, we do not fear that he will change the lender's name, because a person does not sin for the benefit of another. Moreover, the document is not written in direct address and does not create the same public impression, because an admission made outside the creditor's presence can be explained as made to avoid appearing wealthy. Therefore the lender need not be identified. The borrower, however, must always be identified, lest he falsely use another person's name to shift the financial effect of the admission. Sema. The Shach writes that the primary law follows what the Mechaber writes below in siman 69.
