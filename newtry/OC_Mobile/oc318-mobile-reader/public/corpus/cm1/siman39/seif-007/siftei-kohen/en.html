If they were not a permanent court and did not summon him, etc. A ruling issued without the defendant's presence has the force of a written judicial act only where the judges were an established beit din or properly summoned him and he refused to appear. If they were merely three individuals and did not summon him, their writing lacks the publicity and authority of a formal judgment. The Rambam's language indicates that where the defendant voluntarily appeared and accepted their adjudication, or where an established court summoned him, the resulting ruling may be written and can have the legal force of a court document. The authorities debate how much notification is required and whether failure to appear must be formally recorded, but all agree that an informal tribunal cannot create a powerful instrument against an absent person without due process. 1270120
