[Rama] But if he said to lend to him upon collateral. Note: Urim writes that the responsum refers to a mortgage of land, and that the Rama stated the rule without qualification because he holds that it applies also to movable property. See there. In my humble opinion, the law requires study with respect to movable property. By what act did the prospective lender become obligated to lend? The other party did not acquire a lien in the movable property according to the authorities who hold, in the case of "there is no maneh here," that meshichah does not acquire the object for the purpose of a lien, because such pulling is not considered a true acquisition when the object must return to its owner. See the Ran at the beginning of the sixth chapter of Shevuot concerning whether collateral is considered "here it is." This requires study.
