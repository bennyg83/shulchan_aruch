If he holds a written judgment, etc. See Tumim. Even according to Rambam, who requires that the defendant have been summoned before the court may write, if the judges themselves do not testify that they summoned him and only other witnesses say so, then if the written judgment is not presently in the creditor's possession the debtor is believed to say, “I paid,” just as in the earlier rule concerning witnesses to a kinyan. Likewise, even if a written judgment had once been given but is no longer in his possession, although it is technically a judicial record according to Rambam, its absence weakens the creditor and the debtor is believed to claim payment, similar to a ketubah no longer held by the claimant.

There is a presumption that they acted properly. If the judges themselves say they did not tell him the relevant condition, they are believed. This is not necessarily barred by the rule that a witness cannot retract after testifying, because perhaps that rule is different for judges, and because the substantive judgment itself is not being undone; only the defendant gains a possible plea of payment. Shach. This may depend on the dispute whether witnesses are believed to say there was a condition where the body of the testimony remains intact. Maharam allows such testimony. Here too the core ruling, that one party is liable and the other entitled, remains intact, though the ruling's collection force is diminished. According to those who do not allow witnesses even to add a condition, the judges should likewise not be believed. Rama perhaps omitted this rule because it depends on that dispute. The matter requires study.
Some disagree. See Tumim. In a mere admission without a written judgment, even where there had been a dispute between the parties, all agree that the debtor may plead payment.
I paid. See Tumim. One can argue that this applies only where the debtor himself presently pleads payment. If he died, we may not necessarily plead payment for the heirs according to the opinion that we do not plead an uncommon defense for heirs. See siman 108. As practical law, where the written judgment resulted from a formal summons, the debtor is not believed to claim payment, in accordance with Shach and accepted practice. If there was no summons and he merely came voluntarily, the possessor can say, “I am certain like the opinion supported by Rambam and Mechaber,” and retain the money.
