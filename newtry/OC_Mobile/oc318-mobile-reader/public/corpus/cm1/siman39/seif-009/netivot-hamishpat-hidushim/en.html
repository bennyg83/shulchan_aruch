When is this said? When he admitted before beit din, etc. The rules may be summarized as follows. According to the Sema, an admission made before a formally seated beit din that summoned the defendant immediately has the force of a written loan even before anything is written, and the judges may write it even without notifying the borrower. According to the Shach, writing is permitted specifically after notifying the borrower, and only then does it obtain the law of a written obligation. Without formal sitting and summons, the Sema allows writing after notifying the borrower, and then it has the law of a written document. Without notice it may not be written, and the borrower is believed to say he paid. The Shach, following the majority of authorities, holds that even with notice the admission may not be written unless the borrower told them to write, because those present have only the status of witnesses. A judicial ruling in which the judges obligated him through litigation differs. Rama allows the judgment to be written in every case. According to the Sema, the distinction between an admission and a judicial ruling concerns mainly whether a plea of payment is accepted. Against a written admission, everyone agrees that he is not believed to say he paid, even without formal sitting and summons. For a judicial ruling, there is a dispute. If beit din formally sat and summoned him, Rama and the Sema hold that even an unwritten ruling bars a plea of payment. According to the Shach, so long as it was not written with the borrower's knowledge, even after formal sitting and summons he may claim payment. Nevertheless, when the borrower is notified, the ruling may be written, and once written it has the law of a document and he is no longer believed to say he paid.
And he said, "I paid," he is believed. According to this opinion, he likewise cannot collect from encumbered property even if the borrower admits the obligation, because of concern for collusion. Shach, unlike the Sema. The Shach writes that judges who merely receive testimony but do not render a ruling may not hand the parties a written record when there was no formal sitting and summons. Where testimony may lawfully be accepted outside the defendant's presence, they may give a written record, but must state that the testimony was received outside his presence. See Netivot.
Perhaps he already paid him. Even if it is definitely known that he has not yet paid, they still may not write it according to the Sema, because perhaps he will pay later and when sued again will deny the original debt. The creditor could then produce the judgment record and establish him as one who falsely denied a debt. The Shach disagrees and holds that where it is known he has not paid, they may write after notifying the borrower.
