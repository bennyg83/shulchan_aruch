(The Tur further wrote in seif 8.) Regarding an admission concerning movable property, Rashi explains that movables are ordinarily given for consumption, so the matter becomes a debt, and an oral loan is weaker than a documented loan; the debtor does not wish to strengthen it by authorizing a document. With land, the writing merely records an existing fact. The Rambam instead explains that with movables there is concern the claimant may collect and then collect again, whereas with land that concern does not exist. Neither explanation readily fits the Tur’s later statement that if the admitting party accepted responsibility, the writing is permitted, for how does responsibility cure either concern? Moreover, the Gemara asks, “What if the movables remain in existence?” implying that its earlier rule not to write concerns movables no longer in existence, that is, a loan, and every loan already entails personal responsibility, yet the Gemara still says not to write. The Tur must therefore mean responsibility of property, allowing collection from purchasers, expressly accepted when the movables were received. If so, the parties clearly intended a document, since otherwise the claimant could not collect from purchasers. The Tur’s phrase “from the time of the admission” means that collection from purchasers begins at that time.
