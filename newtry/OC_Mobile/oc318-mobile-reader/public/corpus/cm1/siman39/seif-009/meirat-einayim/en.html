When does this apply, that an admission in beit din is like a documented loan? See Rambam, chapter 7, halachah 5. Immediately before this he writes of three judges who sat, summoned a person, and the person came and admitted before them. That case is treated like a documented loan. The present phrase "when does this apply" refers back to that case.
But two people who came for judgment, etc. Meaning that even an admission made in beit din can be treated like a documented loan for purposes of collecting from purchasers because a judicial act has publicity. Yet this applies only where it is known that the debt was not paid, or the borrower admits he did not pay. If he claims, "I paid," he is believed, whether the judges told him, "You are obligated to give him," or used similar language. Tosafot say this, and the Tur cites it in seif 10. When the Tur later writes that an admission in beit din is like a documented loan, it refers not only to the preceding case where the court summoned him, but to the general rule stated by Tosafot. Rambam and the Mechaber likewise intend this.
Whether the judges said, "You are obligated to give him," etc. Even this wording does not necessarily mean the judges regarded the obligation as finally and unquestionably established. It can mean, "It appears to us that you will be obligated to pay, but we will still examine the law further." One might therefore have thought he should not be believed to say that he paid. See above, siman 22, and below, siman 79. See Perishah.
And he says, "I paid," he is believed. In Derishah I proved from Rambam that even if he comes to have a document written while it is still certain that he has not yet paid, they nevertheless do not write one for him when the admission was made without the court having summoned him. The reason is that in such a case he is later believed to say, "I paid," whereas if he possessed a court-written admission he would lose that defense. Rama's gloss appears to equate a written admission with a judicial ruling. I discussed this at length in Perishah. The Ir Shushan writes, "he obeyed the judgment and it was established that he had not paid," but that is not precise.
