When is an admission in beit din treated like a loan recorded in a document, so that the admission is written and given to the opposing party? When the defendant did not voluntarily accept judgment and had to be summoned and brought, as explained above. If two people came voluntarily to litigate, one claimed, 'You owe me one hundred zuz,' and the defendant answered, 'Yes,' then whether the judges said, 'You are obligated to give it to him,' or, 'Go and give it to him,' if the defendant later left and said, 'I paid,' he is believed upon taking a heses oath. Therefore, if the claimant later asks the judges to write the admission, they do not, because payment may already have been made. {Rama: Even according to the view that a person is believed to say, 'I paid,' against a written ruling, as explained shortly, the judges nevertheless do not give him an admission document, because the debtor may already have paid.}
