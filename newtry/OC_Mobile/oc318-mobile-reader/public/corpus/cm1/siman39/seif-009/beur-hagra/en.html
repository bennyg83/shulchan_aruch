(Anthology) When does this apply, etc. See Shach, seif katan 18, 23, and 28. He writes that the other decisors disagree with Rambam, but his words are not correct. According to his view that a judgment, even if unwritten, has the force of a written loan, Shulchan Aruch's ruling in seif 10, 'if there is,' would contradict Rambam here. This is also what the gloss here means by 'even according to the view,' etc. What relevance does a judgment have here, since this is an acknowledgment document? Moreover, Rambam explains the passage in Bava Metzia concerning 'go out and give him' as an acknowledgment, while his distinction regarding one who denied the claim concerns liability imposed by beit din, as stated in siman 79, seif 13. It is especially difficult why Rambam would then write that he is believed to say, 'I repaid,' in one case and not the other, for an act of beit din is involved. Rambam also writes explicitly that acknowledgment and testimony before beit din are equivalent, as stated there in seif 12: 'an acknowledgment before beit din or,' etc., and this is an act of beit din. Maggid Mishneh writes that Rambam's words derive from the Rif in Bava Metzia, but this does not appear correct, since the Rif writes that he did not obey the judgment, as Raavad explains and as stated below in seif 14. Rambam also omits the Rif's first resolution distinguishing between collection from encumbered property and the plea of repayment. Rather, Lechem Mishneh is correct that Rambam's words derive from Sanhedrin 29b, where an acknowledgment may be written, while Bava Metzia states that it may not be written. The distinction is as stated in Sanhedrin, namely, only where the court summoned him. Rambam holds that an acknowledgment before beit din and a liability imposed by beit din are equivalent. Where the court summoned him, it is treated like a written loan even if no document was written, as stated here and in siman 79, seif 12. Therefore they may write an acknowledgment or judgment for him even after he left beit din and returned. If the court did not summon him, however, it is not treated like a written loan and they may not write after he left and returned. While they remain before beit din, however, they may always write, and there is no concern that he may pay, for the document is written in the debtor's presence and he should take it when he pays. This is the meaning of 'he left and said,' here and there, and of 'therefore, if he returned,' and likewise in seif 7, 'if he later came,' etc. While they remain before beit din, they may always write, and he is not believed to say, 'I repaid,' while the judgment remains in the creditor's possession. This is also the case in seif 10, 'if there is,' namely, where the court did not summon him. Nimukei Yosef, cited by Shach in seif katan 18, disagrees with Rambam concerning an acknowledgment where the court did not summon him, holding that even while they remain before beit din it may not be written, because it is like testimony and they may not write unless instructed. In other respects he agrees with Rambam. This resolves Shulchan Aruch's ruling in seif 10 and Rama's gloss here that even according to the view, etc., there is no distinction between a judgment and an acknowledgment. Rambam's words, 'when does this apply,' and 'therefore,' derive from Bava Metzia there, while Sanhedrin states that an acknowledgment before three may be written. For Rambam there is no distinction between a judgment and an acknowledgment, and the resolution is as explained there in Sanhedrin. His statement that it is like a written loan follows from Bava Batra 41a, 'if it is like an act of beit din,' and Gittin 18a, 'as an act of beit din,' etc. His statement, 'if he returned,' and likewise seif 7, 'if he later came,' show that while still before beit din they may always write, from Bava Metzia's wording, 'he came and said,' etc. Nimukei Yosef, however, limits this to a judgment. The Rif's difficulty is resolved in Rambam by the Rif's first answer, as stated in siman 372, seif 1. The Rosh likewise cites only the first answer, although in practice he agrees, as stated at the end of the first chapter of Bava Batra, 'or alternatively, where he stood in judgment,' and as stated at the end of siman 79.
