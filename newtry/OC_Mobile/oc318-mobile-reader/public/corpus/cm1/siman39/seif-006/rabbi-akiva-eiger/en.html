[Shulchan Aruch] But if they made a kinyan. Note: It is not clear to me whether, nevertheless, collection may be made from encumbered property because the kinyan creates publicity, or whether the publicity depends primarily upon the writing, and since the witnesses have no authority to write they do not generate public notice. The same doubt arises where witnesses were not specifically summoned for the kinyan, in which case they may not write: may one nevertheless collect from encumbered property? From the law of a kinyan made outside the creditor's presence, where a deed may be written for the borrower even without the creditor, as the Ramban explains in Bava Batra by interpreting the Mishnah as involving a kinyan outside his presence, and as the Sema, seif katan 33, explains, no proof can be drawn. There, the borrower says, "Write," and although the writing alone would not create publicity until delivery, since even when money has been given a deed may not be written without the creditor, it is possible that in such a case the kinyan suffices to generate publicity. But where he did not say, "Write," the practical law requires analysis.
