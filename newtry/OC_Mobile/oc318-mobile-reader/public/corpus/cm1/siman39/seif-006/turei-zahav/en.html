(Seif 9: therefore they write and deliver, etc.) The Tur, seif 10, cites Tosafot that even where the borrower is believed to say he paid, the creditor may collect from purchasers. The Beit Yosef in siman 79 likewise cites the Raavad that litigation creates publicity, so if the debtor admits the debt the creditor may collect from purchasers. How can purchasers be charged? They can say perhaps the borrower paid and the creditor now conspires with him by claiming nonpayment, as the Tur cites from the Rambam in siman 79 concerning a document in which the lender trusted the borrower to say it was paid. Tosafot also imply in Eizehu Neshekh that even without a formal document, based only on the borrower’s admission, collection may be made from purchasers. It is difficult to say they dispute the Rambam, and in general a person’s admission cannot harm third parties. The case must be where the borrower admitted while he still had unencumbered assets sufficient to pay, and at that time the creditor told him, “Do not repay me except before witnesses.” No conspiracy concern then applies.
