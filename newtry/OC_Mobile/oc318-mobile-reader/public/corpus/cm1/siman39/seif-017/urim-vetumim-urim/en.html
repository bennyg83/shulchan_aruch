He may retract. Even if the deed has already reached the lender, the borrower may still retract under this opinion. Ramban disagrees. In Tumim I conclude that this is an unresolved monetary question. According to Ramban, if one sold land or movables and then converted the purchase price into a loan, all the more if he made a deed for it, an acquisition has occurred and he must supply property in payment. Shach says he may choose which property to give. According to my analysis, if specific land or specific movables were designated, Ramban would treat that property itself as acquired through a complete exchange mechanism because the buyer's corresponding obligation became a lien. This requires study. If the borrower has no land, everyone agrees he can retract. Likewise, if circumstances materially changed, such as one party dying, or certainly if the borrower became impoverished or was discovered to have been poor from the outset, retraction is permitted. Levush writes that if the lender retracts after knowingly causing the deed to be prepared, he must pay the scribe's fee as direct consequential damage. If the retraction results from a genuine changed circumstance, perhaps he is exempt because there was no intent to cause loss, especially according to the view that *dina de-garmi* is a penalty.
