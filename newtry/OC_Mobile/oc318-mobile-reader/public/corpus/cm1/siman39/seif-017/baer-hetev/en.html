To retract. The Ir Shushan writes that it appears he must reimburse the other party for the scribe's fee that was paid, under the laws of causative damages. The Sema says that this reasoning is sound. The Shach writes that Ri Migash and the Ramban disagree with this law, and the Mechaber cites their views at the end of siman 238. It is therefore difficult that the Mechaber rules categorically here. It appears that the matter is a doubt in law, and the possessor may say, "I rely on the Ramban and those who agree with him." Responsa Mabit, part 1, simanim 200 and 213, states likewise. Great authorities also disputed with the Mabit concerning a person who sold wool to another and wrote him a note for the price. The purchaser died, and a creditor of the purchaser seized the wool from the seller's possession. The Mabit wrote that even the Rashba would agree with regard to movable property, while those who disagreed held that even the Ramban would agree with regard to movable property. See there at length. The Shach concludes that the dissenters are correct, and therefore the purchaser's creditor seized unlawfully. He further writes that if the borrower dies, the lender may retract even according to the Ramban, and likewise if in the interim it becomes known that the lender will suffer a loss, such as that his money will no longer be secure. Reason dictates this, because the lender plainly did not consent on that understanding. The Shach further cites the Mabit that even if the document has reached the lender, as long as he has not yet delivered the money he may retract. See the Tur and Beit Yosef.
An iska. Even though half of the profit is to be given to the lender, the recipient must nevertheless pay the entire writing fee. Sema.
He pays. The Darchei Moshe writes this in the name of the Nimukei Yosef and says that it is also implied by the Maggid Mishneh. In fact, neither source says so, for their discussion concerns a receipt. Nevertheless, the law itself is correct by reason. See siman 54, seif 1. Shach.
