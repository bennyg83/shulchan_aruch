This is what Hashem’s command is that He will not have any time, and any condition in which he exists:
That even for this one time is granted. Maharshal rules that if the debtor possesses movables, no time is granted. It is true that the authority who gives time here applies his view even where movables exist. But this makes little practical difference, because Mechaber and Rama elsewhere state that ordinarily no time is granted where movables are available. Nevertheless, the universal practice in every beit din I have seen is to grant some time even where the debtor has movables in his house, so long as cash itself is not readily available. Therefore, when a specific time was already fixed, we should not add yet another period. See Sema. There is no distinction whether the fixed term was oral or written.
And even if he came, etc. See Tumim. The same applies to an ordinary unspecified loan, whose standard term is thirty days. If the lender sues on the second day, the borrower still receives the ordinary loan period and afterward the additional time of beit din. But if the claim concerns a deposit or another item that must be returned in its existing form, beit din grants no additional time at all. See Tumim.
