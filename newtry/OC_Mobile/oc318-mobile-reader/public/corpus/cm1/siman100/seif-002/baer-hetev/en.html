This is also implied by the Tur at the beginning of siman 24: even if a payment date was fixed in the document, he is nevertheless granted thirty days, unless the document expressly states that there shall be no delay. In that event, the rule that monetary stipulations are binding applies, as Rama writes in siman 16, seif 2. Thus writes the Sema. The Shach writes that Maharshal limits this to a debtor who owns only land, but where he has movable property, all agree that no additional time is granted. Terumat HaDeshen writes that the rule of an additional period applies when the date was fixed at the beginning of the loan. If, however, a dispute had already arisen and the parties then fixed a payment date, we say that the debtor was merely seeking delay. If he later comes to beit din, they nevertheless grant him thirty days, provided he did not accept the deadline under oath or ban.
