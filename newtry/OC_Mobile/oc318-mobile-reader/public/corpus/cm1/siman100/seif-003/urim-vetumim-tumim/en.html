They place him under nidui again, etc. In the Gemara in Moed Katan it is stated that they place him under nidui for thirty days, repeat it, and after sixty days place him under cherem. Rav Chisda said that they warn him on Monday, Thursday, and Monday. This applies to monetary matters, but for contemptuous conduct they place him under cherem immediately. The Rambam, in Hilchot Talmud Torah chapter 7, writes that one who remains under nidui for thirty days and does not come to seek release is placed under nidui for another thirty days, and if he still does not come he is placed under cherem. These are the words stated here in the Shulchan Aruch. Yet in Hilchot Sanhedrin chapter 25 the Rambam writes that one who accepts the judgment, is told to pay, and then fails to pay is warned on Monday, Thursday, and Monday, then placed under nidui, and if he remains thirty days without seeking release he is placed under cherem. These are the words of the Mechaber above in siman 19. The formulations appear contradictory, as the Kesef Mishneh already noted. His explanation that the Monday, Thursday, Monday warning substitutes for the first thirty days is strained and does not fit the form of the Gemara. The same contradiction remains in the Shulchan Aruch and the Tur. The primary explanation appears to be this. Where there was no dispute over the underlying claim, but the defendant admitted the debt and merely requested time to pay, and beit din granted thirty days but he thereafter failed to pay, there is no contempt of the judgment itself. He is not claiming that beit din ruled improperly, since there was never a substantive dispute. He merely refuses to pay his creditor. Since repayment of a debt is a mitzvah that beit din compels, he remains under nidui for sixty days before cherem. By contrast, where the parties disputed whether he owed anything at all, came before beit din, and beit din ruled against him, yet he refuses to comply because he believes he is right, this is defiance of the ruling of beit din itself, as though their judgment were of no force. That is contemptuous conduct, and after the first thirty days he is placed under cherem. This is the Gemara's meaning. Monetary matters require the Monday, Thursday, Monday warning, unlike matters of prohibition. But the rule that the nidui is repeated for another thirty days applies specifically where the matter is merely monetary, meaning that there was no defiance of a judicial ruling, only withholding another's money. Therefore they are lenient and wait sixty days. In contemptuous conduct, meaning that there had been a dispute, beit din ruled, and he refuses the ruling, after thirty days he is placed under cherem. This is the intent of the Shulchan Aruch in siman 19, which says that he accepted the judgment. That language itself indicates a prior dispute and an acceptance that beit din would decide between them. Therefore cherem follows after thirty days. Here, however, where there is no dispute and he merely seeks additional time, there is no contempt, and he has sixty days. In a matter of prohibition, if there is no contempt of beit din, the same sixty days apply, except that the Monday, Thursday, Monday warning is unnecessary, since that warning concerns monetary proceedings on the regular days of judicial sitting. This also explains why in the laws of nidui the Mechaber omitted that warning and simply wrote that after sixty days he is placed under cherem. This is straightforward and clear, although the later authorities labored over the passage. However. There remains a difficulty with the Tur. The Rosh cites the Raavad's question on the statement that ordinary nidui is no less than thirty days. What practical difference does that make? If he appeases his litigant even within thirty days, they release him, and if not, he remains under nidui even after thirty days. From this question the Raavad derived a separate rule, cited by the Tur in the laws of nidui, siman 334. At first glance the question has no place, since the thirty day measure matters for the rule that the nidui is repeated after thirty days. Were ordinary nidui not thirty days, one might have thought it is repeated after seven days. See the Shach at the beginning of the laws of nidui. Rather, the Raavad's question must be why the Gemara speaks of repeating the nidui instead of simply imposing sixty days at once. If he seeks release and agrees to obey, they release him at any time. If not, he remains under nidui for sixty days in any event, after which he is placed under cherem. According to the Rambam, however, there are cases of contemptuous conduct in which cherem follows after the ordinary thirty day nidui, rather than immediately in the literal sense assumed by the Raavad. Thus the Gemara indeed had to teach that ordinary nidui lasts thirty days, because otherwise one might have thought that in a case of contemptuous conduct cherem follows after seven days. It teaches that he must first remain under nidui for the ordinary thirty day term. Accordingly, since the Tur here adopted the Rambam's ruling, he should not also have adopted the Raavad's rule derived from that question. This requires study, as I have discussed at length in my novellae to Yoreh Deah. In any event, it is clear that the Rambam understands "immediately" there to mean after thirty days, not literally at once.
