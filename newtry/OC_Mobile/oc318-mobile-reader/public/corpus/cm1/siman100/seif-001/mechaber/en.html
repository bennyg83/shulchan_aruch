How much time is granted to one who requests time to pay, and the law if he does not return at the appointed time or refuses an order of beit din. It contains 3 se'ifim.
If a borrower says, 'I will pay; set me a time so that I can borrow from another person, pledge property, or sell something and bring the money,' he is granted thirty days. {Rama: If it appears to the judge that thirty days will not suffice, he adds time; if the borrower does not require that much time, he reduces it. If the claimant says he must travel and cannot wait through the period, he is told to appoint an agent who will wait until the time expires, as stated in Responsa of the Rosh, rule 81, responsum 3. The borrower is not obligated to provide collateral, because if movable property were available, beit din would collect it immediately. If the lender wishes to impose an unspecified ban upon anyone who has money or movable property but puts him off with words, he may do so. The borrower is not required to provide a guarantor pending payment unless the judge sees a concern that he may conceal himself or evade payment. Time is granted only for payment. No time is granted for an oath; he must swear on the first court day. Nor is time granted for other matters a person obligated himself to perform by a specified date under a penalty. Once the date arrives, he incurs the penalty or must perform his obligation. See note 80.}
