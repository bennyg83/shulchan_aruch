A borrower who says, etc. Whether the loan is oral or documented, and even where the debtor has known assets, beit din grants him time to arrange payment. See Sema.
For if there were movable property, etc. With land we can tell the creditor, 'Why hurry? The debtor cannot conceal the land and a later creditor cannot remove your priority.' With movable property there is a concern that it may be concealed or seized by a later creditor, and therefore no additional time is granted. See Sema. From this I wrote in Tumim that if the movable property is delivered into the creditor's possession as collection, so that a later creditor cannot preempt him, the debtor should likewise receive thirty days, just as with land. Certainly this is so where the creditor already holds collateral. This resolves the apparent contradiction with siman 73. See there.
Lest he conceal them, etc. This means that after the due date the property may be sold immediately. Above in siman 73, where in a case of visible risk of concealment we seize the debtor's property even before the due date, the initial act is only a protective attachment. We do not permanently sell the property before the due date. After the due date, however, it may be sold outright. This resolves Taz's question from siman 73.
No time is granted for an oath. Mordechai explains that the reason time is granted for payment is that the Gemara recognizes the need to search for money. That reason does not apply to an oath, and therefore one must swear immediately. It follows that if beit din sees a genuine reason for delay, such as the expected arrival of witnesses who were present during the transaction and whose testimony may clarify the matter so that no oath will be needed, time should certainly be granted. We do not withhold time merely out of concern that the litigant may die, for Mordechai did not give that reason, and the Sages were greatly concerned to avoid needless oaths, as is evident from the concern that one party may swear while the other later produces a deposit.
When the time arrives he becomes liable for the penalty. This means that he stipulated in a legally effective manner, without asmachta, 'If I do not come by such and such a date,' or, 'If I do not pay by such and such a date, I will owe this penalty.' Once the date arrives, one might have thought that he still receives the ordinary thirty day period, and that if he performs within beit din's period he avoids the penalty. The ruling teaches that this is not so, and the penalty becomes due at the stated date. Certainly, however, he receives the ordinary period from the time the penalty itself is demanded in which to pay that penalty. This is simple.
