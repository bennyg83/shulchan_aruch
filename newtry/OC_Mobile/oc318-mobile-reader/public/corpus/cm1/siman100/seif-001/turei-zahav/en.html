(Shulchan Aruch, seif 1) “For if he had movables.” The wording is unclear, for how does this reason relate to the preceding law? It appears to mean as follows. Had we known that he possessed movables, we would not have ordered him to give them as security, but would have transferred them immediately to the creditor as actual collection. It is therefore impossible to say that beit din’s enactment granting time was conditioned upon his providing movable security. If movables existed, no security stage would be needed at all. Necessarily, the Sages did not require him to provide security. This does not conflict with siman 73, seif 14, which requires waiting thirty days from the demand before selling a pledge. There the pledge originally entered the creditor’s hand as security, not as collection. He may therefore not sell it until thirty days after demand, during which the debtor may redeem it. See Sema in siman 72, seif 14.
