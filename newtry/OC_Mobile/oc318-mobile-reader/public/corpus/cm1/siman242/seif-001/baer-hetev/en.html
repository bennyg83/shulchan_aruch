To give. Only where one was coerced to sell do we say that because of the coercion together with receiving money he fully resolves to transfer ownership, unless he delivered a modaah, as stated in siman 205. With a gift, however, since he receives no money, any indication that he did not wish to give is enough to invalidate the gift. The phrase here 'because of compulsion' does not mean only severe coercion such as beating or torture. Rather, whenever he does not wish to give and nevertheless gives because of some pressure, that pressure is considered the compulsion and the gift is not wholehearted. This explains the conclusion that one who delivers a modaah invalidates the gift even when we do not independently know of the coercion. Thus far the Sema. See Responsa R. Moshe Alshich, siman 64, regarding these laws.
Responsibility. In siman 205, seif 3, the Rama writes that a gift deed containing responsibility is treated like a sale. That case must be where the giver says he gave under coercion while the recipient claims that it actually came to him through sale, that the giver fully transferred it for money but used gift language because of the law of adjoining landowners or because a giver gives more generously than a seller, as stated in simanim 214 and 240. In such a case, if the judge sees that the gift deed includes property responsibility, we presume it was truly a sale because gifts do not ordinarily contain responsibility. The Tur and Mechaber state this explicitly in siman 175, seif 54. Here, however, even the recipient admits that it did not come to him by sale. Thus far the Sema. The Bach writes that here the case is one where we know of the coercion, as the Sema also writes in siman 205. His words require study as practical halachah. Shach.
