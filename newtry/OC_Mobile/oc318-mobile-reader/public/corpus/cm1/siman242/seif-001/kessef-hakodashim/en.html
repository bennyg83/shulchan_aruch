Open and public. If a gift was publicly announced in the synagogue according to custom, it is rescued from the concern of a hidden gift even if the written instrument itself was executed before non-Jewish authorities and would not by itself remove that concern. Perhaps the very purpose of the customary announcement is to ensure that the gift not be hidden. Although the legal form of the document may ordinarily contain language sufficient to avoid the problem, in practice a supposedly valid document may still conceal a secret gift. Perhaps the custom established that without announcement a claim of hidden gift remains available. We do not presently know this with certainty, and a doubt concerning a communal enactment is treated as though there were no enactment. If one gives property to one of two sons and the announcement says merely that he gave it 'to his son,' without identifying which son, perhaps this does not remove the hidden-gift concern, all the more so where the gift to one son operates to defeat the other son's claim.
