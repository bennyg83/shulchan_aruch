Even if the possessor claims that the donor truly gave it to him as a complete gift and told him to write it publicly, his possession does not help, since we rule that an ordinary concealed gift is removed from the recipient. Even if the matter is treated as doubtful, the Rosh writes that it is a doubt of law and the property is removed from him. The Tur cites the Ramah as holding that if the recipient seized the property and claims that it was given as a complete gift, his possession is effective, since in a doubtful case we leave the money in the possessor's hands. My father the Rosh, however, holds that possession is ineffective. This implies that the Ramah holds possession effective in a doubt of law, as I have demonstrated elsewhere with sound reasoning. The Beit Yosef writes that the Ramah did not speak of a doubt of law, but where the possessor claims that the gift was in fact made publicly, although the witnesses did not hear the instruction. In that case, the Beit Yosef says, he is believed. I am astonished, for this is a claim of migo against witnesses. Moreover, the Ramah expressly writes that “it is doubtful,” which implies that possession helps because the law itself is doubtful.
