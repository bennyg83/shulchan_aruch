A person dangerously ill. During the first three days of illness, for this purpose he is treated like a healthy person. After three days he is considered one who gives because of impending death, and a different law applies, as stated below in seif 6 and by the Tur in siman 253, seif 7. Ibid.
Public. See Responsa Maharashdam, siman 500; Responsa R. Moshe Alshich, siman 99; and Responsa Mahari ben Lev, part 1, siman 59. The Sema explains that publicity is required so that it appear he gave generously and willingly.
Hide yourselves. Rashbam explains that the reason is the concern that he may already have given it to another person. Tosafot in chapter HaSefinah, folio 77, write that a person does not want a rumor to spread that he gave his land to others, lest people then refuse to lend him money. Why, then, is this not a valid concealed gift? The Beit Yosef at the end of this siman cites the Ran that whenever there is a plausible reason why the gift was made secretly, it is not considered a matanah temirta. This means where the explanation is intrinsically reasonable and the giver volunteered it without being pressed. The Mechaber did not combine the rules into one clause saying, 'Write and sign in the marketplace,' requiring even the writing itself to be public, because although public writing is preferable, the next ruling says the writing need not itself be public. Furthermore, we rule that delivery witnesses effect the transfer, so publicity can arise from writing alone. Taz.
And he took possession. The Tur concludes that according to the Rosh, even if the recipient took possession of movable property, beit din removes it from him. Even if the possessor claims that the giver actually gave it to him as a full gift and told the witnesses to write publicly, that does not help, because with an ordinary unexplained gift we remove it from him. Even if this is regarded as a legal doubt, the Rosh writes that it is a doubt of law and we still remove it. The Tur writes in the name of the Ramah that if the recipient seized and claims that it was a complete gift, the seizure is effective because in a doubt we leave the money with the current possessor. This implies that the Ramah holds seizure is effective in a doubt of law, as I have proved elsewhere. The Beit Yosef writes that the Ramah is not discussing a doubt of law but a factual claim that the giver expressly ordered publicity and the witnesses simply did not hear it. This is difficult, because it would be a migo against witnesses, and moreover the Ramah explicitly says it is a doubt, implying that possession helps because of the uncertainty. Taz.
