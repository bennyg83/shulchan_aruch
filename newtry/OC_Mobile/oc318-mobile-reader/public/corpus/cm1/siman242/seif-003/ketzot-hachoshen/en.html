It must be open and public. Rambam, chapter 5 of Hilchot Zechiyah, writes that one who gives a gift, whether healthy or ill, must make it open and public. If he says to the witnesses, 'Write it secretly and give it to him,' the gift is ineffective, because he is scheming to cause others monetary loss by later selling property that he has already given. Raavad comments, 'Meaning, the benefit he received from this one.' Maggid Mishneh says that Raavad apparently did not see the conclusion of Rambam's clause as it appears in our texts: 'to cause others monetary loss, because he will later sell after he has given.' Maggid Mishneh explains that if a hidden gift were effective, it would cause loss when the donor later sold the same property and purchasers paid him without knowing of the gift. Therefore the Sages invalidated a hidden gift to prevent collusion. Rambam's reason is the primary one.
Therefore, every gift document that does not contain. Rambam there writes, 'Therefore every document gifting land that does not state, "he said to us," etc., is suspect.' Maggid Mishneh says Rambam's use of 'land' does not mean that a hidden gift of movable property is valid, because the Gemara states the rule generally and the commentators imply the same. Were it not for Maggid Mishneh, however, it would appear that Rambam intentionally limited the rule to land in accordance with his reason, namely preventing monetary loss through a later sale after the gift, where subsequent purchasers pay without knowing of the prior gift. With movable property, the later purchaser must be reimbursed the money he paid because of the market enactment. Thus there is no ultimate monetary loss, only removal of the particular object from him. Perhaps the Sages did not invalidate hidden gifts for that lesser concern. This could explain Rambam's precision in writing, 'Therefore every document gifting land,' because regarding land the market enactment does not protect the purchaser.
