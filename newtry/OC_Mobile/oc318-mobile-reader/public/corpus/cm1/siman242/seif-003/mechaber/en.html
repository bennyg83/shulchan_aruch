Every gift, whether made by a healthy person or a deathly ill person, must be open and public. It is certainly invalid if the donor tells the witnesses to hide and write it. Even if he merely says, "Write it for him," without directing publicity, it is ineffective unless he says, "Write it in the marketplace and sign it publicly," or equivalent words. Therefore, any deed of gift that does not recite that the donor instructed the witnesses to sit in the marketplaces and streets and write an open and public gift, or similar language, is suspected of being a concealed gift, and the recipient does not acquire, even if a kinyan was made. Rama: Even if the recipient seized and took possession, the gift is removed from him. If the donor placed him in possession immediately, however, concealment is not relevant. Thus, language such as, "It is given to you, and take possession now," is equivalent to an instruction that it be written publicly.
