There, law 4, from the incident of the man who went to betroth a woman, etc., before Rava. The Gemara concludes: that case is different because the circumstances themselves demonstrate coercion. The Maggid Mishneh explains that according to Rambam, the son's hidden gift serves as a moda'ah because the incident itself demonstrates pressure. Were it not for the gift to the son, however, we would say that the pressure was not complete coercion, because he could choose not to marry her and not to give her his property, so we would assume he resolved to give it. But once he wrote a concealed gift to his son, the later gift is void by virtue of the moda'ah. So explain the authorities, and this is primary, as the Rama writes in his gloss.
