Which was found, etc. This follows Ri Migash's first explanation, that the prior gift alone creates the coercive disclosure. As explained in seif 10, the phrase means that the earlier gift serves as a declaration of coercion. Although ordinary coercion does not require a prior declaration, that is where another person imposes the coercion. Pressure arising from one's own circumstances is not complete coercion. This is why seif 10 says: and like coercion, etc. See Baer HaGolah.
