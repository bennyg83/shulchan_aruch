And he is like one coerced in the matter, since, etc. The wording carefully says "like one coerced," not that this is complete coercion. Therefore the earlier gift, serving as a modaah, is also required. The Tur explains that this is not complete coercion because the pressure arose from himself. He could have controlled his inclination and refrained from marrying that woman. True coercion is pressure that comes from outside and forces a person into the act. Therefore the second gift is not void solely because of coercion, but because the first gift disclosed his true intention and functions as a modaah together with the partial coercion surrounding the second transaction.
