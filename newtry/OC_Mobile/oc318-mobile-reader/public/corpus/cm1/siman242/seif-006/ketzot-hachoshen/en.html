A shechiv mera who instructed. This entire seif is Rambam's language in chapter 9 of Hilchot Zechiyah. Since he writes that at the time the gift takes effect, namely after death, the donor has already said to reveal it, the case must concern a shechiv mera who gives all his property. If he gave only part, a kinyan would be required and it would have the law of an ordinary gift, completed during his lifetime. But if it concerns a shechiv mera who gives everything, that is the same as one who gives because of impending death, and regarding such a person Rambam wrote that he need not say to publicize the gift. Sema distinguishes between a shechiv mera who gives all his property and one who expressly gives because of impending death. In my opinion they are one category. Rather, even a deathbed instruction is valid without an affirmative statement to publicize only when the donor is silent on the matter. Kesef Mishneh explains that because of his distress he cannot be expected to elaborate and say, 'Write it publicly.' But if he expressly says, 'Do not reveal it,' even a deathbed gift is invalid as a hidden gift. Therefore, where the shechiv mera says, 'Do not reveal it until after my death,' the gift is valid because at the time the transfer takes effect after death he has instructed that it be revealed. One might argue, according to Kesef Mishneh's first reason, that a person giving because of death has no reason to flatter others, and love and hatred end with death, so even an express instruction to conceal should not invalidate. Yet Rambam's language, that one giving because of death 'need not say, reveal the gift,' indicates that an express instruction not to reveal it does invalidate. There is still a difficulty. Rambam's reason for invalidating a hidden gift is to prevent loss to others when the donor later sells what he previously gave. A shechiv mera who gives all his property may retract his gift, so why should an instruction never to reveal it be invalid? Perhaps once the Sages enacted that a hidden gift is invalid for an ordinary healthy donor, they did not distinguish in the enactment between a shechiv mera and a healthy person.
