Even if the first gift was unstated. Meaning, he did not tell the witnesses, "Hide yourselves and write it." It is somewhat difficult why the Mechaber repeats this, since he began this seif by saying that the first gift was concealed or unstated, and he already wrote in seif 3 that there is no difference whether he explicitly told them to hide or merely spoke without qualification. Rambam's wording, however, is orderly. At the beginning of chapter 5 of Hilchot Zechiyah he writes that a hidden gift is invalid and that the document must state that he told them to write it publicly, because otherwise we are concerned that it was a hidden gift. At that point he had not yet written that an unstated instruction to write is also invalid. He then concludes: "One who writes two gifts concerning one field, the first concealed and the latter open and public, the latter recipient acquires; even if the first was unstated, with neither concealment nor publicity mentioned, the latter acquires." The Mechaber follows Rambam's wording here in seif 8, and therefore concludes "even if the first was unstated." He also added at the beginning of the seif "or unstated" to Rambam's wording, lest one think that Rambam's phrase "the first was concealed" means specifically concealed.
And we do not say that the first gift constitutes a protest. The Tur writes that we do not say that through the first gift he revealed that he did not desire the second, so long as we know of no defect in the second gift itself indicating that he did not desire it. This is why the Mechaber continues, "one concerning whom the circumstances prove, etc." Meaning, only where there is also an indication in the second gift itself that he did not desire it do we say that it is no gift, together with the additional factor that he had already given it to another, as he concludes, "and this gift had already preceded it." The explanation of why both factors are needed is set out in the following seif, in the incident involving a certain person. The same is stated in Rambam and the Tur.
