The first document is not treated as a protest against the later one. At the end of siman 242 we rule that a concealed gift is not itself a moda'ah invalidating a later gift. This applies whether both gifts are to the same person or to different persons, because absent an independent presumption that the later gift was involuntary, the first concealed gift is legally disregarded. If, however, the later transaction itself carries an evident presumption that it was not made willingly, perhaps a transaction designed to evade creditors, as discussed in siman 246, can function as evidence against it. Thus, where both transactions are evasive and the creditors' claims remain unresolved, the first may support treating the second as evasive as well, even if the second was a deathbed gift and at that point the donor had less reason to conceal his assets. The later act may still be understood as continuing the purpose of the first.
