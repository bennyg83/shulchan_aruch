We do not concern ourselves with an unspecified formulation. Beit Yosef gives two explanations of Rosh's reference to an unspecified document. One is that the deed itself was written without the usual publicizing language. The second is that the donor merely told the witnesses, without further qualification, to write the deed and did not explicitly instruct them to write it publicly; under that reading the witnesses may nevertheless use the publicizing formula. Beit Yosef prefers the second explanation, and this appears also from Shulchan Aruch here and above siman 61 seif 5. Sema likewise explains that the issue is whether the donor explicitly told the witnesses to write the deed in public form. It follows that if the actual deed was written in an unspecified private form, that deed is ineffective even nowadays. Rabbi Akiva Eiger, responsum 139, discusses this at length, including why Shulchan Aruch presents the rule as “there is one who says.” In his case he concludes that it is difficult to remove land from the possession of the heirs. Even if one follows Rosh, according to Beit Yosef's preferred reading a deed written without the relevant public form may fail to acquire. Maharit, III, siman 54, and Mishpetei Shmuel, siman 25, likewise treat this as the primary view. Since the question is at least doubtful, land remains where it is presently held. Rabbi Akiva Eiger therefore recommends compromise, with the compromise weighted more heavily toward the heirs.
