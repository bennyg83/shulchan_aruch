(Shach, seif katan 3) Not specifically land, for follow the reason, etc. Note: According to the Sema's reason that this would be a migo used to extract property, one can distinguish. Although we rule like Rabbah regarding land, there it may not be considered extracting, even though the prior owner's presumption is stronger. The Rosh explains that once the possessor says the document was valid, he acquired the land, and although afterward the document was lost, we apply the migo to keep the land in his possession. See Responsa Maharit, Choshen Mishpat, siman 82, which interprets Rabbeinu Yonah in accordance with the Sema. Shitah Mekubetzet to Bava Batra 33 cites Rabbeinu Yonah at length. His essential distinction is that movable property can be seized as a pledge, so one who holds it may claim up to its value and retain it as security by a migo that he could have claimed purchase. The migo therefore concerns the same property. Land, by contrast, remains in the owner's presumption and cannot be seized as a pledge. Using a migo there would be using a claim about one asset to extract another.
