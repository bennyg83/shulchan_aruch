The mortgagor conveyed it, etc. “They are only like an oral loan.” Sema in seif katan 3 writes that if this arrangement was not made under duress in order to escape an oppressor, but rather the owner himself devised the stratagem of first giving the property to another person and then having it conveyed to this purchaser, it is treated like a loan evidenced by a deed. Sema supports this from Responsa Rashba, siman 70. See there. Taz challenges Sema and holds that even in such a case it remains like an oral loan, because the deed was not authorized to be written. I am surprised that either view requires extended discussion. The matter is clear. If the owner first conveyed the property to another and that recipient then conveyed it to the purchaser, then if the original owner later repurchased it from the first recipient, the purchaser would acquire it, because the seller wishes to uphold his trustworthiness and does not want the purchaser to complain that he sold him property that was not his. The Gemara explicitly says this regarding one who sold property that was not his and later purchased it from the true owner: the purchaser acquires it because the seller wishes to maintain his credibility. This differs from one who sells because of coercion, where he has no such desire, since his entire purpose was to frustrate the forced sale and escape the coercion. It follows that in the present voluntary stratagem the document is plainly a valid deed. The Gemara itself asks concerning the baraita that when a deed for the sale of stolen land emerges from the seller’s possession, the principal may be collected from encumbered property: why do we not say that the deed was not authorized to be written? The Gemara answers that because the seller wishes to maintain his credibility, he will make the effort to purchase the property, and therefore the deed is authorized to be written. By contrast, in the case of the orchard the owner’s entire intention was to evade the coercive sale. This is also ruled below in siman 373, and for that reason collection may be made from encumbered property. It is therefore obvious that in the present case collection may be made from encumbered property. I do not understand why great authorities found this matter difficult when it is an explicit Gemara.
