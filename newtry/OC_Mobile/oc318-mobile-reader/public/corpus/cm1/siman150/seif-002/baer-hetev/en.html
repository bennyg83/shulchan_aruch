To his son. The Sema and Taz write that this refers back to the preceding seif. This is also the case discussed in the Gemara concerning one who pledged an orchard and protected himself by transferring it to his son. There the money is treated like an oral loan. But one who voluntarily devises a scheme, gives property to his son, and then sells it to others creates an obligation that has the status of a documented loan, even though there was no valid sale. This is also evident from the end of siman 70, where only when the acquisition itself is prohibited does the money receive the status of an oral loan. This is obvious. I write it only because I have seen people uncertain about the matter due to the Mechaber's unqualified wording. See also siman 242 concerning a concealed gift.
