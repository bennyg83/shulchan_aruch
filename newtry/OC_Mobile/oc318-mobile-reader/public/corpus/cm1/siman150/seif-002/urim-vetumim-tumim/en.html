He can never establish chazakah over it, etc. See Sema, seif katan 4, who writes that the Rosh agrees with Ramban specifically regarding this rule of a mortgage, but not regarding a craftsman and the other analogous cases. His conclusion concerning the Rosh is correct, but the distinction he gives is strained. There is no need for difficulty, because the reason is simple. Only where it is known that the property originally came into his possession as a mortgage can he never establish chazakah by later saying, “I purchased the land,” because this is equivalent to witnesses together with seeing the property. The Rosh does not accept Rashba’s distinction between land and movable property in this respect. But regarding a craftsman, sharecropper, and the like, do we actually know with certainty that the property originally entered their possession by way of craftsmanship or sharecropping? We merely presume so because owners commonly deliver property to such persons for repair or management. There are no actual witnesses. Therefore, if they later possess the property for three years without protest, the circumstances reveal retroactively that they did not enter as craftsmen but that the property came to them by way of sale. And if the possessor admits that it originally came to him as a craftsman but says that he later purchased it, he is believed by a migo, as Ramban himself writes in Sefer HaMilchamot. See there. In Chiddushei Ramban to Chazakat HaBatim, page 26, Ramban writes this rationale in the name of “some say.” It is plainly the view of the Rosh, and it is correct.
