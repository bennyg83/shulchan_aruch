(Seif 2) The pledgor gave it to his son, and so forth. It appears that the text should read, “The Sema wrote.” This refers to the preceding seif, and the same appears in the Gemara. He was compelled under duress to sell the field because the other party wished to suppress the mortgage document, and he saved himself by giving the field to his son. Concerning this case it was ruled that the money has the status of an oral loan. However, one who voluntarily employs a device, gives the property to his son, and afterward sells it to others, has money with the status of a loan evidenced by a document, even though there was no valid subject for the sale. This is also demonstrated at the end of siman 70, where Beit Yosef cites a responsum of Rashba: “This document was written unlawfully, since the transaction involves a prohibition of interest,” and so forth. Meaning, for that reason it has the status of an oral loan. This indicates that only where the acquisition involves a prohibition does it have the law of an oral loan. The wording of the Mechaber there likewise implies this, for he writes, “The same applies to every document that was not permitted to be written.” Meaning, it was not permitted to be written because of a prohibition of interest or because the sale was made under duress. Where, however, he voluntarily employed a device, it has the status of a loan evidenced by a document, since a written instrument generates publicity. This is obvious to me. I wrote it only because I saw that some were confused by the matter because the Mechaber wrote without qualification. See siman 242 regarding a concealed gift.
