They have only the law of an oral loan. A similar rule was stated by the Tur and Mechaber at the end of siman 70 concerning one who sold a field under duress after first delivering a protest. There they write that the buyer's money has the status of an oral loan: he cannot collect from encumbered property by virtue of the sale document, because the document was not legally authorized to be written, and the seller is believed to say that he repaid. The same applies here. The Gemara's case is one where the mortgage holder told the owner, "Sell me the mortgaged property, and if not I will suppress your mortgage document." Thus the present ruling refers back to the preceding seif. If the owner later sells voluntarily, however, it appears that at least the money paid has the law of a documented loan. This is evident from the Rashba's responsum cited by the Beit Yosef and Darkei Moshe in siman 70 concerning a sale document involving interest. There the Rashba says that the buyer is believed to say he was repaid because the sale document was improperly written due to the prohibition of interest. The Mechaber therefore concludes there that the same applies to every document that was not lawfully authorized to be written. That means because of a legal defect such as interest or coercion. But where the purchaser himself committed no impropriety and the sale is later voided because of something done by the seller, for example the seller had already sold or gifted the property elsewhere, the money recited in the deed has the law of a documented debt and may be collected even from encumbered property. The document creates public notice, and later purchasers should have known that the seller's property was subject to responsibility for the money received. This is clear. See also siman 242 concerning a hidden gift.
