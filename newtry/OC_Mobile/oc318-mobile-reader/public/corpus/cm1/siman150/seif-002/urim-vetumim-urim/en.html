They are only like an oral loan. Sema writes that if the arrangement was not made under coercion to escape an oppressor, but the owner himself created the device by first transferring to one person and then arranging a sale to another, it should have the force of a written loan. He cites Rashba. Taz disagrees and says even then it remains only an oral obligation because the document was not lawfully authorized to be written. See Tumim. The matter is clear: where the arrangement was not designed merely to escape coercion, the document can be properly written and collection from encumbered property follows. The Gemara itself supports this.
