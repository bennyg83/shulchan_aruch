With respect to land, even after the possessor has held it for three years, seizure does not apply as it does to a pledge of movable property, where the holder is believed up to the value of the article by virtue of a migo that he could have claimed that he purchased it. Therefore, regarding land, once he admits that he did not purchase it, it remains in the established possession of its owner. He has only a migo, and such a migo is not accepted to extract money. If he has already consumed the produce, however, the migo is effective to exempt him from repayment. This is the intent of the Tur in the name of Rabbeinu Yonah, who writes that we find a migo only in one who comes to acquire money, and so forth. By the expression “to acquire,” he alludes to movable property in the claimant's possession, which he could acquire through a claim of purchase, unlike a field. Proof is found at the end of the siman, where the Tur cites another ruling in the name of Rabbeinu Yonah and writes that he is believed by virtue of a migo and that this is not a migo used to extract money. This demonstrates that Rabbeinu Yonah's reason above is that the migo would be used to extract money.
