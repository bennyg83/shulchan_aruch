The Sema explains that this excludes the opinion of Rabbeinu Yonah cited by the Tur, who holds that where the creditor once had a deed, his claim is weakened, because a person normally safeguards his deed as proof lest the debtor later claim that the term of the pledge has already expired. The Shach, however, discusses the matter at length and proves the opinion of Rabbeinu Yonah from many authorities. He concludes: Although all this appears clear to me, I do not have the confidence to rule against the Rif, Rambam, Rosh, and those who follow them. Nevertheless, because many authorities disagree with them, including major authorities, and their position also appears primary in the Gemara, it appears to me at least that the matter is one of unresolved monetary law. Therefore, in a pledge with deduction of produce, where the creditor has not yet consumed the produce of the additional two years, we remove the land from him and return it to the debtor, because land remains in the presumptive ownership of its owner and the burden of proof rests on the claimant. But with a Sura type pledge, or with a rental, the creditor or tenant is believed by virtue of a migo to retain the land for another two years. If, after the creditor has consumed produce for five years, the debtor claims that he was entitled to consume only three, the creditor is considered in possession of the produce already consumed. This is so even where he has no migo, such as where he consumed it in the presence of witnesses, and even if the witnesses to the deed have died and beit din sees that the creditor cannot clarify his claim, so there is no concern that requiring proof would unduly burden beit din. Although in such circumstances we ordinarily remove property from one who lacks a migo, as stated in siman 317, seif 3, here, since according to the Rif, Rambam, Rosh, and their followers he does have the migo of purchase, we do not extract the produce he already consumed, even in a pledge with deduction of produce. Shach.
