And he wishes to retain the land until he consumes it. This is difficult because Mechaber rules that the migo does not help regarding future produce. Shach, seif katan 3, struggles to explain Tur. In my view there is a straightforward case in which even Mechaber would agree that he is believed regarding produce he will consume. Suppose the possessor says that the produce he already consumed was applied first to a different debt, while the mortgage principal still remains unpaid. This resembles one who claims that a payment should be applied to a separate debt, as explained in siman 58, where he is believed if he has a migo. Here, because he has a migo that he purchased the land, he is believed to say that the earlier produce was applied to the other debt and that the mortgage debt remains. When he says that the mortgage principal is still outstanding, his migo concerns the same property and is effective. One cannot object that he had no right to apply the produce to an earlier loan because the produce itself was mortgaged. A creditor holding a written debt can collect even from other encumbered property. Produce that did not yet exist when the lien was created is comparable to property acquired after successive loans. The case can therefore involve a written loan where the creditor seeks to avoid the more stringent oath ordinarily required for collection under a document. By relying on the migo of purchase he would take only a heset oath. It is possible that all the authorities who interpret the Rabbah bar Sharshom incident as concerning future produce agree that a migo does not ordinarily help to establish a right to future produce, and understand his case in precisely such a manner.
