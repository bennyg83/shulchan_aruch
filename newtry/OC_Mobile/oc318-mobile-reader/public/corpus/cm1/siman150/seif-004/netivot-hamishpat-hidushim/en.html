Before he consumes. The reason is that land differs from a pledge of movable property. Since movables are physically in his possession, the migo operates to retain what he holds. Land remains legally in the owner's domain, so using the migo would be to extract. The Shach disagrees. He writes that the authorities who say he is not believed before consuming the produce do so because they reject a migo from one item of property to another. According to them, even with movable collateral he would not be believed for this reason. Since we rule concerning movable collateral that such a migo is accepted, the same applies to land. Nor is it a migo used to extract, because he is actually in possession of the land, and there is no distinction between movable property and land. See the Beurim. According to the Mechaber, who understands the case as produce already consumed, we must say that the possessor had previously declared before witnesses that he was consuming the produce because the land was his, such as through purchase.
