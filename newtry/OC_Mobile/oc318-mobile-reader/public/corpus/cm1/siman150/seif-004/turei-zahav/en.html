(Seif 3) He has no prescriptive right in it. In Derishah I proved that this applies specifically to a pledge and similar cases, where the name and nature of his possession have not changed. By contrast, in the case of the son of an artisan, a sharecropper, and similar persons, the name and nature of the possession later changed. Although they could not establish prescriptive possession when they first entered the property, if it later came into their possession, remained with them for three years, and they claim, “In my presence you acknowledged the artisan's or sharecropper's ownership, and I know that you sold it to him,” they are believed by virtue of a migo that they could have claimed, “I purchased it from you.”
