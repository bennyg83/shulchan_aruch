Before he consumes the produce he is not believed. The reason is that with land, even after he has possessed it for three years following expiration of the mortgage term, physical seizure does not operate as it does with movable collateral. With movable collateral the holder may claim up to its value through the migo that he could have claimed purchase. Land, however, remains legally in the owner's possession once the holder admits that he did not purchase it and that the mortgage term has expired. He has only a migo, and we do not use such a migo to extract money. This differs once he has already consumed produce equal to his claim and the other party seeks to recover what he has already taken. This is the meaning of Rabbeinu Yonah's statement cited by the Tur: a migo is found where a person seeks to retain money through a claim by which he could have retained that same money under another claim. With movable property already in his hand, he might retain it by saying, "It was purchased by me." But with land, once he admits that the mortgage ended, the land stands in the owner's possession and the migo would be used to extract. Proof is found in the law at the end of this siman, where the Tur cites Rabbeinu Yonah that one who originally entered the field under a claim that immediately gave him legal possession may later rely upon a migo, because he already acquired legal standing in the land from the first moment. Thus Rabbeinu Yonah's distinction turns on whether the migo is being used to extract. See the Perishah.
