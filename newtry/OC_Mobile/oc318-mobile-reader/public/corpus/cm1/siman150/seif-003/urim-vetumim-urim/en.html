There is never a presumption, etc. Sema writes that Rosh said this only concerning a mortgage and similar situations where the legal character of possession never changes. This differs from a craftsman's son, a sharecropper, one who entered property of a married woman, a convert, or one who acquired from a gentile. In those cases, after the original disabling status ended and the person then possessed for three years, he can establish title by claiming that the owner later sold to him. See Tumim. Sema's legal distinction is correct, but it need not be framed exactly as he did. The key is that where it is known the land originally came into his hands as a mortgage, that original basis functions like sight witnesses showing the nature of his holding; mere continuation therefore does not convert it into ownership. By contrast, in the other cases the original disabling circumstance can end, and later possession may take on a new character. Ramban explicitly records this distinction in the name of early authorities, and it accords with Rosh.
And they do not know for how many years. Tur first cites Ramban that where it is known he entered as mortgagee, he never acquires merely through length of possession. Rabbi Meir HaLevi Abulafia disagrees, which is the second view here. Tur then cites Rabbi Yonah, which is the third view. Perishah wondered why Tur did not place Rabbi Yonah immediately after Rabbi Meir HaLevi Abulafia and introduce him with 'Rabbi Yonah wrote.' See Tumim. I analyzed the structure and concluded that Tur's ordering and language are exact. See there for the explanation.
