At first glance there is proof for his explanation from the chapter Eizehu Neshekh, page 72, where Rashi explains the case in which he first gave the property to his son: “Since he had already given it to another as a gift, and they compelled him to write the document for this person.” This implies that had the matter been voluntary, the document would have been valid. Upon careful examination, however, the opposite emerges. If so, what is the Gemara's challenge there from an antedated document? We should say that it too was not permitted to be written. Nimukei Yosef there likewise mentions only that he had already given it as a gift, and nothing more. It therefore appears that Rashi also intended only to say that he had already given it as a gift. There is also a clear proof from siman 200, that a lien upon property is ineffective where one sells something not yet in existence or something not presently in his possession. Certainly, therefore, one cannot collect from purchasers by means of a sale document covering property that was not in the seller's possession, unless the document states that he undertakes a personal obligation, which is effective even regarding something not yet in existence. This is also the intent of Rashba cited by Beit Yosef at the end of seif 1, and it accords with the Mechaber's wording, “Whenever it was not permitted to be written,” implying for whatever reason. Rashba mentioned the prohibition of interest because that was the principal reason the document in his case was deemed impermissible to write. I am further astonished at the distinction itself. A transaction performed in violation of a prohibition nevertheless remains legally effective, as stated in siman 208. Why, then, should we distinguish here between a document that could not be written because of a prohibition and one defective in the substance of the transaction? Moreover, if the matter depends upon prohibition, even one who acts voluntarily violates a prohibition by taking another's money under the guise of an invalid sale. It therefore appears to me that every invalid document that was not permitted to be written is not considered a document at all.
