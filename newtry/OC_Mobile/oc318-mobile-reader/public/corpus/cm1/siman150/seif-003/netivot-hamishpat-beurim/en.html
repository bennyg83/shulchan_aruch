Tumim cites Ramban's distinction: with a craftsman or sharecropper, we do not actually know that the property entered his hands as property entrusted for craftsmanship or as a sharecropping holding. He lacks chazakah merely because it is possible that this was how he received it. Once he then possesses it for three years after the disqualifying relationship ends, it is revealed retroactively that it may originally have entered his hands by sale. Here, however, witnesses know that the property originally came into his hands as a mortgage, so no later possession can reinterpret the original entry. A similar distinction can explain one who possessed property of a married woman during her husband's lifetime and continued for three years after the husband's death. The authorities imply that even where witnesses know the possession began during the husband's lifetime, the later three years can establish chazakah, because even during the marriage it remains possible that husband and wife jointly sold it to him. Yet siman 149, seif 29, concerning an administrator appointed over this particular field, states that after administrators cease their appointment they can establish chazakah, and Tumim explains that this means where the field remained continuously in the administrator's hands, for if he first returned it the rule would be obvious. That case cannot be explained by Ramban's distinction, because his original entry was known to be as administrator. We are therefore forced to retain the Sema's distinction.
