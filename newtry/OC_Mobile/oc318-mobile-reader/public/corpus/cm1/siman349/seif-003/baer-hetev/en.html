To swear. Although the claim is uncertain, the Rosh writes in his responsum concerning this incident: "They also suspect Shimon himself of knowing what happened to them, and perhaps they are still in his possession." According to that wording, one might say that this is why they required him to swear. But the Mechaber did not copy that detail and therefore clearly holds that an oath is imposed even without it. The reason appears to be that since the items entered his possession and are now no longer there, there is an adverse circumstance sufficient to impose an oath even on an uncertain claim. This resembles all oaths of bailees, which are imposed on an uncertain claim. For the same reason he must swear that he was not negligent with them, although he never expressly accepted responsibility as a bailee. At the beginning of siman 291, where one says, "Place it before you," he is not even an unpaid bailee; that case is different because he revealed that the owner should not rely upon him. Here there was no such indication. See also the end of siman 96. Shach.
