Beit Yosef deliberately omitted that oath, explaining that if the husband never became a custodian, negligence cannot obligate him. That is correct. Where the husband has no custodial status, the ordinary Torah custodial oath does not apply. If he appropriated the property for himself, he can be liable as an ordinary thief or robber, but not through the special custodian's law of unauthorized use. Any oath that the property is not presently in his possession is therefore only a hesset oath arising from the suspicious circumstances, not a custodial Torah oath. The same applies to the statement that he did not appropriate it. Rama and Sema's insistence on an oath for negligence is additionally difficult because a husband who acquires his wife's rights as a purchaser is elsewhere exempt even from negligence. Whether the wife borrowed before or during the marriage should not change that basic purchaser status unless the husband knowingly assumed the borrowing after being informed. The more consistent ruling is Beit Yosef's: he is not a custodian, and the oaths are only rabbinic hesset due to the circumstances.
