He must swear that they are not in his possession. This is imposed even though the claim is uncertain. In the responsum of the Rosh and the Tur's version of the incident, the townspeople suspected Shimon himself of knowing what happened and perhaps still holding the items. That could explain the oath in the original case. But the Mechaber omits that circumstance, showing that in his view the oath applies even without it. The reason is that the property definitely entered his possession and is now absent. That creates a concrete deficiency, and we impose an oath on a doubtful claim where such a deficiency exists, just as with the oaths of custodians. For the same reason he must swear that he was not negligent, even though he never expressly accepted custody. This differs from one who says, "Place it before you," where the Tur and Mechaber rule at the beginning of siman 291 that he is not even an unpaid custodian. There he expressly reveals that the owner should not rely upon him, unlike the present case.
