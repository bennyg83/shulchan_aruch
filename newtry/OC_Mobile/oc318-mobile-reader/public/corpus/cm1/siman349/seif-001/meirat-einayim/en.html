The law is the same for a man and a woman. Meaning, although the verse in Parashat Mishpatim says, "If a man steals an ox or sheep and slaughters or sells it, he shall pay five cattle for the ox, etc.," the word "man" is not exclusive. A woman is equated to a man for all punishments in the Torah. The word excludes only a minor, as the Mechaber concludes, and the same applies to others lacking legal competence, namely a deaf-mute and an incompetent person.
Except that a married woman who stole, etc. The law specifies a married woman to exclude a widow, divorcee, or adult unmarried woman, who ordinarily has property from which to pay immediately. A married woman, by contrast, generally brought whatever she owns into her husband's domain. At the end of siman 346 it was explained that if she has nichsei melog she must immediately sell her beneficial interest and pay the principal. See the Derishah there and siman 424, seif 10.
Until she is widowed or divorced. Meaning, the victim should presently obtain a judgment against her establishing her liability for the principal and double payment, to be collected when she later has property. This is as the Tur and Mechaber write in the following seif. Although the Tur here says, "until she becomes widowed or divorced and he sues her," it does not mean that only then does he initiate the claim and obtain judgment. Rather, it means that he can then collect from her under the judgment already obtained now. The Mechaber therefore deliberately omitted the words "and he sues her," to avoid this misunderstanding. The Ir Shushan restored those words even though the Shulchan Aruch deliberately left them out, and thereby made the wording less precise.
