These rules apply when the donor says, "After you to so-and-so," without saying "from today." If he says, "After you, from today, to so-and-so," and the first sells, the second may recover from the purchasers. Rama: Some say that in this formulation we do not apply the rule that inheritance cannot be interrupted. Others disagree and make no distinction. The first view is primary. If the second dies during the first's lifetime, the property passes to the second's heirs. If there are three recipients and the donor says, "to A, after you from today to B, and after B from today to C," A and B acquire only the produce, while C acquires the principal immediately and the produce after B's death, provided the property first came into B's possession. Therefore, if B dies during A's lifetime, the property returns to the donor's heirs. Rama: These rules apply when he says, "My property to you and after you to so-and-so." If he says, "after you to me or my heirs," and the first sells, the donor or his heirs may recover from the purchaser after the donor's death. Even if he says only, "My property is given to you for your lifetime," the recipient has only the produce, and the donor or heirs may recover the principal from purchasers. If he says, "after you to my heirs," and his daughter dies during the recipient's lifetime leaving descendants, her descendants stand in her place. Without descendants, other heirs do not stand in her place unless he said, "from now to my heirs," in which case the daughter acquired during life. If he says, "My property to so-and-so and after him to my heirs," while having one daughter, and sons are later born, some say the daughter acquires because he referred only to those who were his heirs at the time. If he says, "My property to you and after you to your heirs," some treat it as "after you to so-and-so," so that if those heirs die during the first recipient's lifetime, their descendants receive nothing unless he added "from now." Language in ordinary deeds, "to you and your heirs," is merely standard deed language. Others disagree. If Reuven gives property to Levi subject to a condition that upon breach it shall belong to Shimon from now, and Shimon later assigns all his rights to Levi from now and dies, then even if Levi breaches the condition he acquires through Shimon.
