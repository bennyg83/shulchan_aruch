After you, to my heirs. The Sema reads, After you, to your heirs, and understands this as the very law stated in seif 9. The rule is that when he says, to your heirs, his intent is to the heirs who existed at the time of the gift, not to whoever would be the legal heirs at the time of death. Thus, where the heir who existed at the time of the gift was a son, daughter, or brother, even if that heir died, the descendants of that heir who died during the giver's lifetime stand in that heir's place and take. But where the heir died during the first recipient's lifetime and left no descendants, only other heirs through his father, it is treated like a case where the second recipient died during the first recipient's lifetime, and the property returns to the heirs of the first recipient where the heirs of the first are not heirs of the second. An example is where the first recipient and her heirs were a mother and daughter, since the daughter's paternal heirs do not inherit the mother. The Rama then cites a dispute among the poskim regarding one who says, My property is yours and after you to your heirs. His wording implies that they dispute even whether the descendants of a daughter stand in her place. The Sema objects that there is no opinion disputing this. The primary rule is that her descendants are included in the gift and stand in her place, but other heirs do not. The Shach reads the opening phrase as, and after you to his heirs.
