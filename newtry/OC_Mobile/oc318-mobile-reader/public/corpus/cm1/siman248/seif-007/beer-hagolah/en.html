He explains that it is as though he said, 'and after you, to me or to my heirs.' The Beit Yosef, siman 241, seif 6, cites a responsum of the Rashba concerning Reuven, who lent one hundred to his sons Shimon, Levi, and Yehudah and took houses as security. Reuven then died, and before the estate was divided Shimon wrote Levi a deed of gift of everything he had inherited from his father. Levi then demanded this debt from Shimon. The Rashba ruled for Shimon, because at the instant of the father's death Shimon acquired the portion of the debt that he himself owed the father, and the lien was extinguished before the gift. The Sema cites this in the name of the Darkhei Moshe and discusses it at length.
