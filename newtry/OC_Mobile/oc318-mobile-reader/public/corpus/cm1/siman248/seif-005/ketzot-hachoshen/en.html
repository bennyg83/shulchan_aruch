Beit Yosef understands Rambam to mean that when the assets were deposited with a shalish for this purpose, even inheritance language does not allow the first heir to defeat the later recipient. As explained above, the ordinary deathbed gift cannot interrupt inheritance, but the heirs are still obligated by the mitzvah to fulfill the deceased's instructions. Maharit argues that this would use a mitzvah to nullify the Torah rule that inheritance has no interruption. In my view there is no nullification. The heirs remain heirs and owners. The mitzvah does not create title in the later recipient. It simply obligates the heirs, as owners, to carry out the deceased's direction. Rabbeinu Yerucham's rule that a purchaser from heirs is protected proves that the mitzvah is not a kinyan. Therefore the title is never interrupted, while the heirs must nevertheless deliver the property when the requirements for the mitzvah are present.
