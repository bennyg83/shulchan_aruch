Beit din does not collect from the corpus of the property, etc. The Mechaber rules the same above at the end of siman 104. The Maggid Mishneh explains that when the first recipient actually sells or gives the property, the later beneficiary cannot remove it from the purchaser or donee, so the transferee acquires it completely. But where the first merely encumbers the property to a creditor at the time of the loan or to a wife for her ketubah, the creditor has not acquired the corpus absolutely. If the debtor later has money, he can discharge the lien with cash. Therefore, after the first recipient dies, the second may remove the property from those lienholders. During the first recipient's life, however, the creditor may collect the produce, because there is no practical difference whether the first recipient himself consumes the produce or his creditor does. Beit din will not commit the prohibited act of transferring the corpus, since the first recipient himself is forbidden to sell it in violation of the donor's intent. It appears clear, however, that if the first recipient himself voluntarily transfers the property to his creditor in satisfaction of the debt, or to his wife in satisfaction of the ketubah, the transfer is effective just as an unauthorized sale is effective. One cannot say that judicial appraisal must later return and that the creditor merely has a lien, because when the debtor himself voluntarily conveys property to his creditor, the law explained in siman 103 is that the transfer does not automatically return.
