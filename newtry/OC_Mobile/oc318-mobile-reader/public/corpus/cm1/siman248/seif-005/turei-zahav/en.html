(The heirs of his heirs do not, etc.) The Sema writes that the sugya cannot be reconciled according to this view. Apparently his difficulty is that the Gemara first says, "to my heirs," even including the heirs of my heirs, yet later concludes that if the grandmother had a son, he would inherit. According to the Beit Yosef and Rama here, however, even the son would not inherit, since the daughter died during the grandmother's lifetime and never acquired the property. This is indeed a valid difficulty.
