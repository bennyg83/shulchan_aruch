Therefore, if he placed the money with a trustee, etc. This is based on the baraita in Bava Batra. One who says, "Give my sons one shekel each week," when they actually need a sela, receives a sela for them, because we assume he did not intend to deprive them of their needs but only hoped they would economize. If he says, "Do not give them more than one shekel," they receive only a shekel. If he also says that when they die other heirs shall take after them, then whether he used the language "give" or "do not give more than," they receive only a shekel, because the later beneficiaries have already acquired an interest after them. This creates a difficulty, because it seems to show that inheritance can be terminated, although ordinarily inheritance has no interruption. The Rambam resolves the baraita by saying that at the outset the father expressly declared that he was not giving the sons by way of inheritance. The Ran gives another answer, that the property was placed with a trustee and never entered the sons' possession as inheritance. He also gives a further answer that the sons were never given the corpus at all, but only a weekly shekel, and only each shekel as it was distributed became theirs. The Rambam's wording, copied by the Mechaber, shows that he does not rely upon the Ran's first answer, because he writes that even where the money is placed with a trustee the father must say that it is not given by way of inheritance. The word "therefore" in the Rambam and Mechaber means: Since a condition made at the outset excluding the law of inheritance is effective, even if he merely said "give" rather than "do not give more than," once he also said that the sons take not by inheritance and that after them the remainder belongs to another person, we do not apply the principle that inheritance cannot be interrupted. Therefore the trustee gives the sons only the stated shekel even if they need more. The Ir Shushan writes that they receive only a shekel simply because that is what he expressly commanded. That misses the point of the baraita, whose first clause says that despite the identical instruction to give a shekel, they receive all their needs unless later beneficiaries are given an enforceable interest.
