Ketzot HaChoshen writes that this ruling is from Rivash, while Responsa Tashbetz, part 1, siman 61, disputes Rivash and writes that Heaven forbid one should thereby extinguish and destroy a Jew's legal right. Maharit, Choshen Mishpat, siman 6, agrees with Tashbetz. See Responsa Chatam Sofer, Choshen Mishpat, siman 142, who also cites Tashbetz and writes that had the Rama seen his words, he plainly would not have disagreed, for they are persuasive. Chatam Sofer further suggests that Rivash himself spoke only in the circumstances of his own case. The expression "gift" does not itself mean inheritance. It is only Torah terminology that treats a gift to one who is entitled to inherit as also an inheritance. If it is clear that the deathly ill donor did not intend Torah terminology, then even under Torah law the disposition is a gift rather than inheritance and can be interrupted. Since the matter turns on intention and language, and those people were unfamiliar with Torah terminology because they conducted their affairs through gentile courts, Torah law itself construes their words as a gift rather than an inheritance. But Heaven forbid that one imagine uprooting Torah statutes and laws even by a hairbreadth. He adds that this is close to the Sema's intent in siman 369, seif katan 20.
