(Seif 4: he did nothing.) It appears to me that the reason is this. Had he given the property to the first recipient without qualification, that recipient's heirs would inherit it. When the donor says, "and after you to so-and-so," his intent is specifically to exclude the first recipient's heirs. Although the first recipient may still be able to transfer the property to others, the donor does not suspect that he will actively violate the donor's stated intention by selling or gifting the land. Rather, he fears only that the property will automatically pass by inheritance. He therefore removes the heirs' power by saying, "and after you." Once he came to exclude them, he excluded them entirely, such that even if the first recipient performs some act such as sale or gift, it is also void. This appears to me the reason.
