Likewise if the first gave them as a deathbed gift. Bava Batra says that if the first recipient gives the property by a deathbed gift, he has accomplished nothing against the second recipient, because a deathbed gift acquires only after death and the second has already preceded him. Rashbam explains one form as a healthy person's gift phrased "from today and after death," treating it like a deathbed gift. This is difficult according to Rashbam's own explanation elsewhere that such a gift transfers the body from today and only leaves the fruits until death. If the first recipient already owned the body and could have sold it, then by giving the body from today he has left nothing for the second. The purchaser should precede the "after you" recipient. Tosafot gives a different reading. "From today and after death" means from today if I do not retract before death. In that form the first donor retains a power of retraction. Since he has not finally removed himself from the property, the later "after you" right precedes the attempted gift even if he ultimately never retracts. This resembles Maggid Mishneh's explanation that a creditor of the first recipient cannot collect the property where the lien can be discharged with money. An outright sale or gift, which cannot be removed by paying money, finally removes the first recipient. A lien does not. Likewise a gift that remains revocable until death does not finally remove him, so the later recipient's right remains. This also fits Tosafot and Rosh's theory that a deathbed gift, once completed by death, can relate back to the original statement. Even so, before death the donor retained the right to retract, so the "after you" recipient is treated as having precedence over an attempted disposition that was never final during life. Accordingly, Tosafot's interpretation is clear. Under Rashbam's interpretation, where the body truly passes from today without any right of retraction, the first recipient seems to have made a complete transfer and the second should take only what was left. This remains difficult.
