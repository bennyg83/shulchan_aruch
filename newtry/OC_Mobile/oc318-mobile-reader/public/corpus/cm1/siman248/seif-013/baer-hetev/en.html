Acquired. The Mechaber's words are astonishing. Since the giver reserved five gold coins for his heirs, this is not a gift of all the property by a person dangerously ill, as stated below in siman 250, seif 4. If so, the debt instrument should not be acquired, because writing and delivery are required. The Beit Yosef at the end of siman 66 likewise cites Responsa Rashba: A debt instrument can be transferred by the mere statement of a person dangerously ill, provided the gift includes all his property. The Rashba's responsum cited by the Tur is speaking generally where nothing was reserved. It might be possible to reconcile this according to the Sema's explanation in siman 250, seif 12 in the gloss. This requires study. See also there, seif 17 in the gloss. Shach.
A healthy person. See what I wrote above, siman 66, seif 3, that some disagree. Ibid.
Wrote. Meaning, because he did not write it to him. Mere speech is effective only through agav land, as stated in siman 66. The Responsa Rosh cited by the Tur must likewise be understood this way. As practical halachah, however, I concluded there that writing and delivery are required. See there. Shach. See Responsa Shevut Yaakov, part 1, siman 171, concerning one who gave a deathbed instruction that all his garments and clothing be given to a relative. See there.
The heirs. We do not say that the property becomes ownerless. The donor certainly did not intend to abandon it. He merely removed his heirs from it on the assumption that his fellow would acquire it. Since the intended recipient did not acquire, the property remains in its former ownership and the heirs succeed to it. Tur in the name of Responsa Rosh. Sema.
