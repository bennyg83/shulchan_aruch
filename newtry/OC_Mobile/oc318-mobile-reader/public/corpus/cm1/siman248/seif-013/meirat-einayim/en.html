If it was a gift of a shechiv mera, he acquired. The Tur writes at the end of this siman in the name of a Responsum Rosh: promissory notes are certainly included in the term "my property." This applies, however, to a shechiv mera, who requires no kinyan because his words are as though written and delivered. It is then like a healthy person who wrote, "Acquire the notes themselves and all their liens," and delivered them to the recipient. With a healthy person it does not work. Even though he transferred all his property through movable property together with land and the notes were in the house, so that there is writing and delivery through his courtyard, he did not write, "Acquire them and all their liens." The Rosh concludes that the notes remain with the heirs. We do not say that they became ownerless, because the giver certainly did not intend to abandon them. He intended only to remove the heirs from them on the assumption that his fellow would acquire them. Since the fellow did not acquire them, they remain in their prior status and the heirs acquire them.
