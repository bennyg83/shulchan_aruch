The first. The Tur writes that the third recipient acquires only through the second. Since the property never came to the second, the third cannot acquire it. As long as it remains with the first, the body and produce are entirely his, for he may even sell it. See there. See Responsa Mabit, part 2, siman 319; Responsa R. Moshe Alshich, simanim 89, 90, and 114.
It is prohibited. In order that the giver's words be fulfilled, because his intent was that the property reach the second after the first recipient's death. Sema.
Advice. Where the person is the heir by law, one who advises him to sell is considered completely righteous, because his advice restores the inheritance according to Torah law. Maharit Tzahalon, siman 86. Bnei Chayei.
For the deceased. Nimukei Yosef explains that he threw more property onto the deceased than was needed for burial, so there was no mitzvah aspect. On the contrary, there is a transgression, as the Tur and Mechaber write in siman 349 that one violates bal tashchit and increases decay and disgrace to the body. One might therefore have thought the first recipient's act should not take effect. The ruling teaches that it does. Sema.
