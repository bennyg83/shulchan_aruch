If one says, "My property to A, after him to B, and after B to C," then upon A's death B acquires, and upon B's death C acquires. If B dies during A's lifetime, the property passes to A's heirs. Rama: The same principle governs C's relationship to B. Although the second receives only what the first leaves, the first may not sell or give away the principal. He may consume the produce until death, when the second acquires. If the first nevertheless sells or gives the property, the second cannot recover it from the purchasers, because the second has no present right in either principal or produce beyond what remains. Anyone who advises the first to sell is called wicked. If the property includes slaves whom the first frees, or utensils he turns into burial shrouds, his acts are valid. Rama: If the donor gave the first the property for ten years and afterward to another, and the first sells it, the second may recover from the purchasers, because the first received only a time-limited interest.
