Kaf) This is the wording of the baraita. The Tur likewise writes so. Rambam states the law using two recipients; the Maggid Mishneh explains that two are enough to clarify the rule.
Meaning, if the third recipient dies, the property belongs to the heirs of the second recipient once the first recipient dies. Maggid Mishneh there.
Lamed) There, halachah 4. The Maggid Mishneh writes that this is explicit on Bava Batra 137a according to Rabban Shimon ben Gamliel.
Mem) There, halachah 9, from Abaye's statement concerning "what kind of crafty wicked person," etc. Rashbam explains that the first recipient, whose legal right is to sell or give the property for his own benefit, is not called wicked. One who advises him to do so, however, has no personal benefit and is therefore called wicked.
Nun) A statement of Rabbi Zeira in the name of Rabbi Yochanan there. The Maggid Mishneh explains that even though one who frees his slave violates a positive commandment, we do not say that the donor would not have given property for the purpose of enabling a prohibition. This is explicit in the Gemara.
Samekh) A statement of Rav Yosef in the name of Rabbi Yochanan there: one might have thought that the donor would not give the property if the recipient could render it forbidden for benefit; the Gemara therefore teaches otherwise.
