(Rama, seif 1) If he made the will in a place, etc. Note: The Rivash writes that even regarding their testimony they should have followed the law of the gentiles, because the community of Majorca had always willingly followed that practice. If so, how could he be required to leave his place and travel to distant lands so that he could be judged under Jewish law? See there. At first glance this language conflicts with the Sema at the end of siman 369. As for the apparent contradiction between the Rama here and there, one can answer that according to the Rivash the rule applies only where the community voluntarily accepted this practice. See Knesset HaGedolah, siman 26. See also Tashbetz, volume 1, siman 61, who disagrees with the Rivash. From his refutation it appears that the Rivash's rationale is as stated, not the reason offered by the Sema in siman 369. Maharit, Choshen Mishpat, siman 16, likewise understands the Rivash this way, though he disagrees and accepts the Tashbetz.
