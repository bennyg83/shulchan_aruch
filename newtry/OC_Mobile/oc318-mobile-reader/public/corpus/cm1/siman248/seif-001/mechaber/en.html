One who gives property to another and says, "After you, to so-and-so." It contains thirteen seifim.
If a deathly ill person says, "My property to so-and-so, and after him to so-and-so," the second receives only what the first leaves. If the first is fit to inherit, such as one son among several, the second receives nothing, Rama: even if the second is also an heir, and even if the donor designated consecrated property after the first. Language of gift to an heir is treated as inheritance, and inheritance cannot be interrupted, despite the words "after him to so-and-so." Rama: If the will was made in a place where the custom was to adjudicate in non-Jewish courts, and the parties later litigate elsewhere under Jewish law, we do not apply the rule that inheritance cannot be interrupted. A healthy person who makes such a gift, writing, "My property to you, and after you to so-and-so," leaves the second only what the first retains, even when the first is an heir.
