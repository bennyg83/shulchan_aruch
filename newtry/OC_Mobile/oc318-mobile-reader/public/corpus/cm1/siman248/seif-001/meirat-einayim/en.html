"My property to so-and-so, and after him to so-and-so": the second recipient has, etc. The Tur writes in the name of Rabbeinu Yonah: the matter does not depend on the word "after you" as though it meant only what you leave unsold. Rather, the phrase "my property to you" connotes a complete gift. The same law therefore applies if he says, "My property is yours, and if you die it shall go to so-and-so." See the Mechaber below in seif 2.
Who was a son among the sons. Meaning, if the giver has sons and only they are fit to inherit him, we say that inheritance cannot be interrupted where the recipient is one of those sons. Likewise, if the giver has no sons but has daughters or brothers, where he gives to a daughter or brother we say that inheritance cannot be interrupted. The Mechaber does not discuss here whether the later recipient is also fit to inherit him. Rama adds that rule immediately afterward in his gloss, from the Maggid Mishneh.
If he consecrated it after the first recipient, etc. Meaning, the first recipient is fit to inherit him. Although a shechiv mera can retract a gift he already made and give it to another, here he has not retracted from the first recipient, since he still wants the first recipient to enjoy it throughout his lifetime. He merely directs that after the first recipient's death it go to another or to hekdesh. Since he wants the first recipient to acquire it during his lifetime, the inheritance can no longer be interrupted. All this concerns a case where we cannot say that he intended merely to appoint him as an administrator, such as where he retained some property, explicitly stated that he intended a full gift, or one of the other forms explained in siman 246 applies.
Hekdesh receives nothing. This applies specifically where he gave without qualification. If he stipulated that when his son leaves the house the poor should acquire the property, then as soon as the son leaves the house the poor acquire it. So the Beit Yosef and Darkei Moshe cite in the name of a responsum of the Geonim; see there at length.
In non-Jewish courts. Meaning, their courts do not apply the principle that inheritance cannot be interrupted. See the Rama's gloss at the end of siman 349 so that this not be misunderstood.
But a healthy person who gave a gift of a healthy person, etc. He carefully writes "gift," because a healthy person may transfer property with language of gift, but cannot transfer it with language of inheritance.
