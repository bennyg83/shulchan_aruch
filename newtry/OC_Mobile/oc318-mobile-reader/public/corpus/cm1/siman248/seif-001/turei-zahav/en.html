(Seif 1: If the will was made in a place, etc.) The Rama's words here require study. The Rivash writes in responsum 52 that the claim that the provision, "after you, to the woman," in place of the daughters is void because the daughters are fit to inherit him is correct only if the matter occurred in a place governed by Jewish law. The testator, the woman, and the daughters, however, were in Majorca under gentile rule, and even among the Jewish community they had always adjudicated according to gentile law by communal choice. Why, then, should the claimant travel a great distance to obtain judgment under Jewish law? Let them come to Majorca and litigate before the official who remains their judge. His meaning is clear: were they to appear before a Jewish judge, he would certainly adjudicate according to Jewish law. The Rivash merely did not require them to travel far to a Jewish court, permitting them instead to litigate before the gentile tribunal they had previously accepted. The Beit Yosef likewise writes at the end of siman 26, citing a responsum of the Rashba concerning one who assaulted a woman in a place governed by gentile law, that Heaven forbid Torah law should be uprooted and the matter adjudicated by gentile law. It is therefore astonishing that the Rama writes that if they come to litigate under Jewish law, the Jewish court should rule for them according to gentile law. This is the opposite of the Rivash's intent.
