Seif 7, gloss. However, if he said, "After you, it shall return to me," or "to my heirs." Note: Ran, in his novellae to Gittin, cited by Avnei Miluim in siman 28, holds that a gift for a fixed period is a complete gift once the donor's ownership has ceased. Ran asks from the rule of "my property to you and after you to so-and-so." In my opinion the distinction is that every acquisition requires two distinct intentions, that of the transferor and that of the recipient. This is shown by the rule that an agent appointed to sell land or an object cannot buy it for himself. Thus, where one says, "After you, to so-and-so," there are two parties, the donor transferring and the later recipient acquiring. But where he says, "After you, back to me," the same person is both transferor and recipient, and one cannot transfer to oneself through the same act. The property therefore remains with the first recipient. This explains the position of the Raah.
