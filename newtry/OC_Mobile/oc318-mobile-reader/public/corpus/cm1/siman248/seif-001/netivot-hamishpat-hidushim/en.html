And after you, to so and so. The same applies if he said, And if you die, to so and so. We likewise apply the principle that an inheritance cannot be interrupted. This applies specifically where he first said, My property is yours, thereby placing the recipient into the position of inheritance. But if he merely said, After my son dies, or after he leaves the house, it shall go to so and so or to hekdesh, we do not apply the principle that inheritance cannot be interrupted, because one on his deathbed may direct that property be given, even long after his death, to whomever he wishes:

But a healthy person. The reason is that a healthy person cannot transfer property through language of inheritance, so his words must operate as a gift. But regarding one on his deathbed, even if he has a firstborn and an ordinary son, so that he cannot validly allocate through language of inheritance alone, we nevertheless apply the principle that an inheritance cannot be interrupted. His words function through two legal mechanisms so long as he has not uprooted the status of inheritance: they operate as a gift against the firstborn's special share, and as inheritance against the later recipient. See Beurim. The later recipient acquires only by force of the original giver. Therefore, in a gift made by a healthy person, both the deed and the property subject to the transfer must still exist at the death of the first recipient. Likewise, if the giver did not say from now, the giver may retract before the first recipient dies.
