[Shach, letter 3] Rama, seif 7, in the gloss. Note: I do not understand this. In the gloss to seif 7 the case is where he says, "and after you, from today, to so and so," and the phrase "from today" is effective to permit interruption because the second recipient acquires retroactively. Here, however, where the first recipient himself received the gift from today, it may still be said that an inheritance cannot be interrupted. See Maharit, part 2, Choshen Mishpat, siman 74, who infers from the wording "but a healthy person" that where a deathly ill person gives only part of his estate with a kinyan, or gives the whole estate from today with a kinyan, we say with respect to an inheritance that it cannot be interrupted. He further writes there that even regarding a healthy person, this applies specifically where he gives to one son among several sons. But if he gives to his only son and says, "and after you to so and so," the latter does not acquire, for even regarding a healthy person it remained unresolved whether one can designate one son among several as heir, and because of the doubt the property cannot be removed from his possession.
