Everything is included in property. See Responsa Mishkenot Yaakov, siman 68, concerning a deathly ill person who said, "All my property to my minor sons from my second wife." His estate included an oral loan secured by pledged synagogue seats, and the question arose whether that loan was included in "all my property." He answers that in his view it is not. The Gemara, Bava Batra 150b, separately asks whether money is called property and whether a document is called property. If "money is called property" included even an oral loan, there would be no need to ask whether a document is called property, because every debt instrument embodies a loan. It follows that an oral loan by itself is not called property at all, and "money" there means actual money in hand. The same follows from Bava Batra 152a concerning a deathbed gift accompanied by a kinyan, where the language implies that if the donor wishes to transfer a loan he must expressly say, "my loan to so and so," in addition to the general expression "all my property" at the beginning of the gift. This indicates that a loan is not included in "all my property." There is some room to consider whether the pledge held by the lender changes the result, but even that does not clearly establish that the loan is included. At the least, if the matter is disputed, it is difficult to extract property from the heirs, particularly because a deathbed gift is rabbinic. See there.
