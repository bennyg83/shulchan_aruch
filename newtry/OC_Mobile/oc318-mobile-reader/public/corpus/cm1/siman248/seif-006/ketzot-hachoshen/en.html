The same law applies between the third and the second. Ramban in Milchamot limits this to language, "after you to so and so, and after him to so and so." There the third takes specifically through the second, so if the second dies during the first's lifetime the property returns to the first's heirs. But if the donor said, "My property is yours all your life, after your death to so and so, and after the second's death to the third," without using the dependent phrase "after him," then the third gift can be independently designated. If the second dies during the first's lifetime, the third can still receive after the first. See Ramban's discussion of the grandmother case.
