Seif 11. "My property." Note: Bikkurim 2:11 states that one who writes his wild animals or his domesticated animals to his son does not thereby include a koy. Rambam explains the case as one who wrote either category, so doubt prevents recovery, but if he wrote both categories the koy is included in any event. The same follows from the Rash's approach and the ruling in Nedarim 19. It requires study why the codifiers omitted this Mishna. See above siman 218, seif 24, and Shach there.
Shach, letter 11: It is included in the term "movable property." Note: This is unlike the Rama in siman 2, for according to Mabit, possession does not help when the doubt concerns the term "movable property"; the uncertainty exists only with the broader term "property." The Gemara says, "Once it has ceased, it has ceased." Although the Gemara distinguishes "after you to another" from "after you to myself," that case concerned a gift for the recipient's entire lifetime. The Raah can agree that once the recipient dies the gift necessarily leaves his possession and would otherwise pass to his heirs; allowing it to return to the donor does not require a new acquisition in the same sense. But if the donor stipulated that it return to him during the recipient's life, the Raah holds that this cannot work, because once the donor's right has ceased it has ceased. The Rosh, however, appears not to follow the Raah, for he rules that if one says, "It shall be yours until you use it and afterward it shall be mine as before," the recipient does not fulfill the mitzvah with it. This requires study.
