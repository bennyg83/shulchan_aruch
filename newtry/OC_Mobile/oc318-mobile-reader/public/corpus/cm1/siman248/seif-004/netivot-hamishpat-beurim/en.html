A shechiv mera gift. Ketzot HaChoshen cites Rashbam as holding that even a healthy-person gift phrased "from today and after death" has the same law in this respect as a shechiv mera gift. Taken literally this is difficult. But Ketzot correctly explains Rashbam where the language was "from today if I do not retract." Since the donor retains the power to retract, we can still say that the later recipient has already preceded the first recipient in the relevant manner, just as Tosafot and Rosh hold that a shechiv mera gift can acquire retroactively yet we nevertheless apply the rule that the later recipient has preceded him. This is the correct way to understand Rashbam.
And when he dies the second precedes and acquires. See Sema, seif katan 16, through his words concerning the first recipient becoming a goses. This is based on Bava Batra 137. The Gemara asks whether Abaye indeed holds that a shechiv mera gift takes effect only after death, because elsewhere Abaye says it takes effect with completion of death. It answers that Abaye retracted from the latter view. Rashbam proves from the Mishnah, "This is your get if I die," which is not a get, that the intention of a shechiv mera is not to transfer until after completion of death. Otherwise the get should take effect with completion of death as well. This appears to show that if one gives a gift or get on condition that it take effect once he becomes a goses, the act can take effect even though at that moment he is no longer capable of making a new acquisition. But Bava Batra 127 says that Scripture needed the verse of recognition for property that falls to a person while he is a goses, and Rashbam explains that because at the moment the acquisition would take effect he is no longer capable of transferring, the acquisition fails. Similar disputes exist regarding a get scheduled to take effect the next day when the husband is then seized by mental incapacity. Machaneh Efrayim addresses this and writes that the rule is different where the property was already within the person's power to transfer at the time he created the transaction. In that case the later vesting can occur even if he is a goses. But property not yet in existence or not yet in his power cannot be acquired in this manner, because he could not transfer it when making the original act. Machaneh Efrayim also questions Rashba, who writes that one who transfers one thousand zuz "with his death" can acquire even when the recipient is a goses. He argues that the recipient too should need legal capacity at the moment of vesting. According to what I wrote above in siman 243, however, one can acquire for even a mentally incompetent person through another person. Therefore Rashba can be speaking where the acquisition was made through others for the recipient. More fundamentally, I am not convinced that the recipient's incapacity should be treated more severely than the transferor's in every case.
