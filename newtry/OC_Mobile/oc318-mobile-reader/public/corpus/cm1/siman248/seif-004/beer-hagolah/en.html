Ayin) There, halachah 10. The Maggid Mishneh writes that Rambam derives this from the incident of Rav Bibi, Bava Batra 137a, who transferred the property to his son. Even Rabban Shimon ben Gamliel permits the first recipient to transfer only to another person, etc. The reason appears to be that when the giver said, "and after you to so-and-so," he did not intend that the first recipient could transfer the property to his own heirs, for then the reservation would accomplish nothing, since a person ordinarily gives everything he has to his children. Although the first recipient may transfer it to outsiders, a person does not ordinarily sin against the giver's intent merely to benefit outsiders. This is Rambam's view, and Rabbenu Chananel explains similarly. Thus he may not transfer it to himself, and his son is considered like himself. Rambam's version apparently did not read "his minor son," but simply "his son."
Peh) From Rabbi Yochanan's statement, as Abaye explains its reason there on 137a.
