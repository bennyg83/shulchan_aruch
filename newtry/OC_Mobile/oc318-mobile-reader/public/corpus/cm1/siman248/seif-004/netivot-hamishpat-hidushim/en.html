To one of his heirs. The reason is that otherwise the giver accomplished nothing by granting rights to the second person. He knows that people ordinarily give property to their children. A sale to an outsider is different, because the giver does not ordinarily anticipate that the first recipient will commit the wrongdoing of selling away the property.
And when the first dies, the second acquires first. The reason is that when he says, "and after you to so and so," his intent is that as soon as the first person is dying and no longer needs the property, the second should acquire. This differs from a dying person who gives away his property, whose ordinary intent is that the gift take effect only after his soul departs. See Beurim: the same law applies where a healthy person gives from today on condition that he does not retract. There too the second recipient may acquire when the first is dying.
