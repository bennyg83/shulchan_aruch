Some disagree. Sema explains that these authorities hold that inheritance has no interruption even when the later recipient was designated from today, because the first at least received the fruits for his entire life and therefore also receives the body. Yet in my view their reason is different. They hold that even when one says "after you, from now, to so and so," the first recipient still owns the body and can sell it. The present language functions like a condition: whatever the first leaves at death shall belong to the second from now. This is the approach of Baal HaMaor and several other authorities, and it is consistent with Rav Huna's view that "after you" can be treated like "from now" while nevertheless a sale by the first is valid. If the first can sell, he clearly possesses the body. Therefore, when he is an heir, he entered the body by inheritance and the rule that inheritance has no interruption applies. The disagreement thus depends on the nature of "from now." If it deprives the first of the body and leaves him only fruits, the inheritance can be interrupted. If it merely creates a conditional future right for the second while the first retains transferable title, the first has inherited the body and it cannot be interrupted. Rivash cites Rashba to the same effect. Rashba found a way to interrupt inheritance only where a healthy donor used a kinyan and gave the later recipient a true present right that prevented the first from selling. But if even a present "after you" leaves the first with power to sell, a deathbed heir still takes full inheritance and the later recipient cannot interrupt it.
