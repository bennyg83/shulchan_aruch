The lender... Bava Kamma 118a.
If there is. Even in an inhabited place, arrangements are made for a debtor's basic needs before collection, as stated at the end of HaMekabel.
And if the lender claims. This follows from the statement there: "A loan is given to be demanded..." and "a lost item..." If the money is present in his possession, both are subject to demand; if it is not present, neither is subject to demand. So too the Rif writes there regarding a loan when the money is present and regarding a deposit when it is not present. The case, however, is of this type. Regarding a deposit he cannot compel him to swear, because ordinarily he did not bring it with him.
Nevertheless, in every place... Since the Mishnah says "in the wilderness" and does not say merely "in its place," this implies that only in the wilderness may he not compel him.
A caravan. Tosefta, chapter 10 of Bava Kamma: "but he may return it to me with a caravan." This likewise proves the preceding point, that in any inhabited place he may compel him.
If he lent him in the wilderness... The Mishnah there says, "in an inhabited place..." implying that if the loan was made in the wilderness, he may repay in the wilderness.
Even if he lent him... The Gemara there. See the Rosh there, contrary to Rashi's explanation.
