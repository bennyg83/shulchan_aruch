Place. Rabbeinu Yeruham and Maharshal say the same rule applies to stolen property. The Shach understands this only where the robber has acquired the object and now owes its value, such as after despair plus transfer of possession. If he must return the actual stolen item, it has the status of a deposit and is claimable only at the place where it was entrusted, as in siman 293. Shiltei Giborim questions whether, when a lender demands repayment outside the place of the loan and the borrower has no money there, the borrower must bring funds to the place of demand. The Shach considers it clear that he need not.
In the wilderness. Shiltei Giborim questions whether a debt for wages or purchase price is treated like a loan or a deposit. The Shach considers it clearly like a loan, because the distinction is that loan money is given for expenditure while a deposit may not be used and is expected to remain where deposited. Wages and sale proceeds therefore follow the law of a loan.
To compel him. Shiltei Giborim writes that if the borrower encounters the lender outside the lender's city in an inhabited place and wishes to repay with currency that is not usable in the lender's city but is usable in the current place, where the loan was simply denominated in currency without further condition, the lender must accept it because the currency is spendable at the place of lawful payment. The same applies in similar cases. Shach.
As much. The Sema appears to hold that before the due date the borrower cannot compel the lender to accept repayment in such circumstances. He also says that a later journey through a wilderness counts as a manifest danger. The Taz finds this very difficult, because the lender can simply remain in an inhabited place until a caravan becomes available. Even if the delay causes loss, the fixed term was instituted for the borrower's benefit, as stated in siman 100. This differs from a future currency change where the loss cannot later be avoided.
To accept. The Bach likewise says that before the due date the lender cannot be forced to accept payment in the wilderness. The present rule concerns an ordinary loan whose default term is thirty days, which is not fully equivalent to an expressly fixed term, as discussed at the end of siman 78. The Shach refers to Shiltei Giborim.
Wants. Maharshal rules that the borrower must also say, "If you wish, I will return it to you there." Shach.
