The lender gives, etc. See Tumim. I concluded as practical law that if the lender demands payment from the borrower at a place other than where the loan was made, and the borrower has no money there, but the lender asks him to return home and bring the money himself or send it through an agent, then if the borrower would not otherwise be traveling home and back he cannot be compelled to make the trip merely for this purpose. If he ordinarily travels that route anyway, however, the lender may compel him to bring the money, and the borrower cannot say that he refuses the burden, because a borrower is subject to his lender. With a deposit, by contrast, the depositor cannot compel the custodian in this manner. The custodian may say, "Why should I bear this trouble? Return to my home and collect your deposit there."
