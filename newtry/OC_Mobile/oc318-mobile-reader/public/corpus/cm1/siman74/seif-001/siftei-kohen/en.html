One who lends, etc. The Shiltei Giborim in the first chapter of Makkot is uncertain whether, when the lender demands payment in a place other than the place of the loan and the borrower has no money there, the borrower must bring the money to that place. In my humble opinion it is obvious that he is not obligated to bring it there.
To demand payment in any place. Rabbeinu Yerucham, netiv 3, and Maharshal, chapter 10 of Bava Kamma, siman 53, write that the same applies to stolen property. It appears to me that they speak where the thief acquired the object and must pay its value, such as through despair of recovery followed by a change of possession. If he must return the actual stolen object, it has the law of a deposit and may be demanded only in its proper place, as stated below in siman 293.
Even if he lent him, etc. The Shiltei Giborim there is also uncertain whether money owed for rent or for an item purchased has the law of a loan or of a deposit. Here too it appears obvious to me that it has the law of a loan. The distinction between a loan and a deposit necessarily rests on the fact that a loan is given to be spent, whereas the custodian may not use a deposit and it is expected to remain where it was deposited. Payment arising from rent or sale is therefore plainly governed by the law of a loan.
He shall take a heset oath. This applies only when the lender asserts his claim with certainty. Otherwise the borrower need not take a heset oath; the lender merely pronounces a ban against anyone withholding his money, as stated below in siman 223.

He may compel him, etc. The Bach, seif 2, writes that during the fixed term the borrower cannot compel the lender to accept payment in the wilderness, as is stated in the next seif. The present ruling that he may compel him to accept there concerns an ordinary loan. Although its term is thirty days, this is not equivalent to an expressly fixed term, as stated at the end of siman 78. See the Bach there and the Shiltei Giborim.
