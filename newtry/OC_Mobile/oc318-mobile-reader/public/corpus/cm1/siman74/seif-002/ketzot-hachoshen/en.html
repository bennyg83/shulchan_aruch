The concerns. Beit Yosef rules that where the parties fixed a term because of a currency disturbance for the lender's benefit, and that concern later disappeared, the borrower still cannot force early repayment. Since the time was established for the lender's benefit, the borrower cannot retract from it. The Shiltei Gibborim gives a parallel rule concerning merchants who receive capital to lend onward at interest and share profits with the investor: because the agreed term protects the investor, they cannot withdraw during the term. Tumim challenges this from an iska, where the active party can withdraw like a worker who may leave in the middle of the day. The answer is that labor and custodial responsibility are distinct. A worker can withdraw from personal labor because "the children of Israel are My servants," but one who accepted custodial responsibility for property until a fixed time cannot simply throw off that liability before the term. This is clear from the law of a deposit made for a fixed term. Therefore the active party in an iska may stop the labor aspect, but the custodial obligation over the deposit portion remains until the agreed time. The same explains the Shiltei Gibborim.
