If a lender fixed a term for the borrower, and the borrower wishes to repay so that the money will no longer remain at his risk until the due date, but the lender refuses to accept it, then if the due date has arrived, the law follows the borrower. This is so even when he hastens to repay in order to save himself from future losses, such as a change in the currency, a tax decree, or compulsory service for the king. Since the loss is not presently evident, even if it is expected the next day or later that same day after payment, he need not concern himself with the loss to the other party. If the due date has not arrived, and an immediate currency change, tax decree, or royal service is common and plainly evident, he cannot compel the lender to accept early payment. But if none of these or similar concerns exists, the lender is not heard when he refuses early payment merely so the money will not be at his risk until the due date, because fixing the term was for the borrower's benefit.
