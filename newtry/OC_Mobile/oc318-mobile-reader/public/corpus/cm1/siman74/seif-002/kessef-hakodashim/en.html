See Shach, seif katan 9. Although the Shach in siman 74 writes that payment made in the creditor's absence is effective specifically when deposited with beit din and not merely by granting possession through another person, it appears that in the creditor's presence such a transfer through another person can be effective. Urim VeTumim also somewhat implies this. This avoids creating an unnecessary dispute between siman 74 and siman 120 concerning the formula "here the money is deposited." Presumably the stakeholder will not use the money. Perhaps even in the creditor's absence a full beit din is unnecessary and deposit before the recognized head of the local beit din is sufficient, just as his authentication can have the force of beit din for certain purposes. It is also possible that prior formal warning through beit din alone may suffice here. The matter requires further study.
