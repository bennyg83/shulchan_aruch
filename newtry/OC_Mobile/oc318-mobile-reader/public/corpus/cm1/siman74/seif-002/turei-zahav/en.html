(Seif 3) “And it is at the time.” The Sema explains this as within the loan period, making the borrower liable, unlike siman 104 where the borrower is exempt after maturity. This is difficult. The reason there is that payment may be forced upon the creditor. If the deterioration is not apparent, forced payment is valid even within the period. It is also difficult that the Rama concludes that a depositary is exempt, for within the agreed deposit period he is certainly not exempt. The Rama’s words follow his Darkei Moshe. The Beit Yosef cited an anonymous gloss questioning why a depositary of loose money permitted for use should not remain liable after the owner demanded it. The Rama answers that once the period of custody ends, the depositary cannot be forced to continue. The owner’s refusal to take the money does not renew permission to use it, because the depositary’s refusal to continue guarding controls. Thus “at the time” means when the agreed time has arrived, a common Talmudic usage. The depositary is then exempt. The contrary opinion in siman 120 follows the Mordechai’s view that a borrower who may still use the money remains liable until it leaves his hand. This is the true basis of the dispute.
