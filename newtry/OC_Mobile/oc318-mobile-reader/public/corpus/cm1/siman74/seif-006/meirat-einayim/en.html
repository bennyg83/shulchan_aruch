Take it in satisfaction of your debt. Meaning: Take this property for your debt and return to me the promissory note that you hold against me. One may ask what the Mechaber adds by this law, since he already wrote it in seif 5. The Tur likewise states both laws, but there is no difficulty there because the Tur states the second law first and brings it as a proof for the first law, which the Rosh wrote in a responsum. See there in seifim 7 and 8. Nor can we say that seif 5 deals with a case before the due date and that the Mechaber proceeds from the lesser to the greater, teaching that not only before the due date but even after the due date, where the borrower has no money with which to pay, he still cannot compel the lender to accept the property. For if so, he should have stated it briefly in the reverse order, saying that not only within the term but even when the term has arrived the borrower cannot say this. Rather, the novelty is as follows: In the first law, in seif 5, the lender made the loan upon a pledge of land or movable property. The lender can therefore say, "I prefer to retain both the promissory note and the pledge, so that you will have reason to redeem your pledge quickly." But even in the present law, where the lender holds only the promissory note, one might have thought that the borrower can compel him by saying, "It is surely better for you to hold the land itself than merely to hold a note." The Mechaber therefore teaches that he cannot.
