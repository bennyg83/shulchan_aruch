Take it, etc. The law is with the lender. The Sema, seif katan 13, asks what this ruling adds beyond seif 5; see his answer. It appears to me that seif 5 concerns a borrower who does not say that he lacks other assets. He admits that he can pay from elsewhere, but refuses, arguing that because the loan was made upon the pledge the lender accepted it as payment. The ruling rejects that argument. Here, by contrast, the loan was not made upon this land, and the borrower says, “I presently have neither money nor movable property; take this land for your debt.” The ruling teaches that the lender may refuse because he is willing to wait. The two cases are entirely distinct.
