The law is with the creditor. See Baer Heitev. Responsa Beit Ephraim, Choshen Mishpat, siman 7, writes that the plain implication of the Rosh, Tur, and Shulchan Aruch is that this rule applies even where the borrower’s property will be sold at a depressed price because of it. However, Responsa Rashba, volume 3, Livorno edition, siman 43, records a dispute among the Rashba’s teachers in such a case, and the Rashba himself inclines to those who require the creditor to accept land where forced payment would cause the debtor’s assets to be sold cheaply. He concludes that here a judge must rule according to the circumstances he sees. At the end of that responsum it is clear that if the document expressly requires payment in cash, even the Rashba agrees that no consideration is given to the depreciation of the debtor’s property, and the debtor cannot compel the creditor to take land in payment. See there, and what I write below, siman 82, seif 3, seif katan 4.
