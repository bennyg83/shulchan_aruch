The creditor. One might ask what the Mechaber adds here, since he already wrote this in seif 5. The Sema answers that the novelty is that not only in seif 5, where the loan was made against a pledge of land or movable property, may the creditor say, 'I prefer to retain both my debt instrument and the pledge so that you will hurry to redeem your pledge.' Even here, where the creditor holds only a document, one might have thought the borrower could say, 'Surely it is better for you to hold land than merely a document.' The Mechaber therefore teaches otherwise. The Shach answers that in seif 5 the borrower does not claim that he has no other property; he merely wants the creditor to accept the pledge as payment. Here the borrower says he owns nothing except land, and the Mechaber teaches that the creditor prevails because he is willing to wait for payment. Thus the two cases are unrelated.
