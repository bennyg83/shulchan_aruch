One who lends, etc. See what I wrote in siman 73:15. Raavan likewise writes that even according to the view that one who takes collateral at the time of the loan is a paid guardian, this is only because he performs a mitzvah, not because he acquires the collateral. Rabbi Eliezer says he holds it merely as a memorandum; Rabbi Akiva disagrees only because he performs a mitzvah. The same is evident from the dispute between Rabban Shimon ben Gamliel and Rabbi Yehudah HaNasi at the end of chapter 6 of Shevuot concerning cancellation by the Sabbatical year. See Gittin 37a, 'If so, one who lends,' etc. The case must concern a place where the debtor may redeem the collateral, for otherwise the Sabbatical year would not cancel the debt, as stated in chapter 5 of Bava Metzia. See Tosafot to Gittin there, s.v. 'If so.' It follows that even with collateral the debtor may redeem it. Conversely, the creditor cannot be compelled to accept the item against his will, for otherwise the Gemara's question, 'What is the reason?' would fail, since 'he shall not press' would not apply, as stated in Makkot 3b, even according to the first version, specifically because in the end, etc.
