m) The wording of the Rambam, chapter 4 of Hilkhot Malveh, law 12, following Shmuel's statement as Rav Nachman explains it, Bava Kamma 96a. We maintain that the halachah follows Shmuel and Rav Nachman in monetary law, in accordance with the consensus of all poskim except Rashi.
The Maggid Mishneh explains there: every loan, whether produce whose monetary value was fixed or money, is made on the basis of the circulating coinage. That is, the lender's intention is directed to the value represented by the coin form. This excludes a loan of produce to be repaid in produce rather than money. This is what is meant by a loan "on the coin," rather than an unspecified loan. The Tur explains differently, as the Rama cites in the gloss. The Maggid Mishneh there likewise cites this in the name of Rashi, Tosafot, and the Rashba.
n) The same applies if he expressly stipulated a particular coin. Maggid Mishneh there, and so writes the Bach.
s) The Bach writes in the name of Maharshal that where he lent for a fixed term and the coin was invalidated after the due date, and the borrower failed to pay on time, it is obvious that he must pay in the new currency. If the exact coins that were lent remain intact, there is a dispute. The Maggid Mishneh in chapter 4 of Hilkhot Malveh holds that he must pay in the current currency, while the Mordechai in the chapter HaGozel holds that he may say, "Here is your property before you." Sema in the name of Darkei Moshe.
