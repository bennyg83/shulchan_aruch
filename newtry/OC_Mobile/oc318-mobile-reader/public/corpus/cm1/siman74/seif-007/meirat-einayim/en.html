One who lends his fellow on the basis of the currency, etc. This is the language of the Gemara in chapter HaGozel Etzim, Bava Kamma 97a, following Rav Nachman according to Shmuel, whose opinion we accept. The Rambam and the Mechaber follow the language of the Gemara. The Maggid Mishneh writes there that, in his view, the meaning is that whenever one lends another either produce whose value was fixed in money or actual money, he lends with reference to the currency currently in circulation. Meaning, his intention is directed to the value represented by the coin's form, so that the borrower must repay him money. This excludes only a case where the loan consisted of produce to be repaid with produce. This is why the Gemara says "on the basis of the currency" rather than simply "one who lends." The Maggid Mishneh further writes that this also appears from the Rif, and that the rule concerns anyone who is owed money by another. If the old currency can no longer be spent at all, the debtor must give him currency currently spendable. The Maggid Mishneh then writes that Tosafot disagree and hold that the borrower ordinarily repays with the invalidated coin itself unless he expressly stipulated that payment be made in currency. The Rosh and Tur likewise rule this way according to all opinions. The Gemara's phrase "he lent him on the basis of the currency" does not mean that he explicitly stipulated that the borrower give him a particular coin. Rather, the same rule applies if he stipulated simply that he would give him money. This is also evident from the Tur, who begins by speaking of one who lent on the basis of the currency and then concludes: All of this applies only where he stipulated that payment be made in money. The same appears in Tosafot. Rama follows them and therefore writes in his gloss upon the Mechaber's words: All of this is where he stipulated that payment be made in money, etc. See the Perishah. Still, there is some difficulty. Rama should not have written without qualification that all this refers only to a stipulation, because the Mechaber is following the Rambam's wording, and the Rambam's unqualified language means, according to the Maggid Mishneh's explanation, that even without an express stipulation, once the debt is denominated as a certain amount of money the parties intend the value represented by the coin's form, and the debtor must pay money that can actually be spent.
And likewise with a ketubah. The Maggid Mishneh writes there: This means where he wrote for her a specified currency, and this was already explained in chapter 17 of Hilchot Ishut. End quote. According to Rama's position, following Tosafot, that the law applies only where there was an express stipulation, it appears that a ketubah likewise requires such a stipulation.

Currency that circulates in every respect. The Mordechai concludes there that this applies provided the new coinage was not increased beyond the measure stated in the Talmud. End quote. He means the measure of one fifth, as explained in Yoreh Deah siman 165. It is specifically when the authorities increased the coinage that there may be a prohibition, because the debtor is giving him a different coin of greater value. If, however, the same coin itself increased in market value, it is permitted, since he is returning the very type of coin that was lent to him. This is stated by the Tur here in seif 10 in the name of a responsum of the Rosh. The Tur and Mechaber similarly rule in Yoreh Deah siman 162 that although lending a seah of produce to be repaid by a seah is prohibited in certain circumstances, lending currency in kind is permitted.
