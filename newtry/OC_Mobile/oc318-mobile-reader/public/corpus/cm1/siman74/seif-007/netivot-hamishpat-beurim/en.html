See Yoreh Deah, siman 165, and see there in my work Chavat Da'at, where I wrote that the rule applies specifically where the borrower stipulated to repay "currency," and the original currency was completely invalidated so that it no longer has the legal name of currency. Since he stipulated to pay currency, he must pay currently valid currency. But if the king merely reduced the value of the original currency, for example a dinar was formerly worth twenty five smaller coins and was then set at twenty, the borrower certainly repays only the same type of currency that he borrowed. It still bears the legal name of currency and has merely fallen in value, like one se'ah borrowed for one se'ah, where if grain falls in price he repays only the se'ah he borrowed even though its value is now lower. So too is stated in Responsa Lechem Rav. See there. The same is evident in a responsum of the Rosh, rule 150, end of section 1: "Moreover, in this country when the king issues a new coin he does not invalidate the old one, but merely reduces its value, and all circulate according to their relative values. Kondrish and sholdish all circulate in this country. If he lent him kondrish, why should he not repay him in kondrish, since they were not invalidated?" The same appears from several other poskim and responsa to one who studies them. Tumim likewise writes at the end of siman 42 that appreciation and depreciation of currency fall upon the lender. However, if he lent red coins but did not insist upon repayment in that particular coin and instead calculated the obligation in gold coins, such as where he lent one hundred red coins and wrote in the note that he lent one thousand eight hundred gold coins, or they orally calculated the obligation as so many gold coins, he must pay the stated sum of one thousand eight hundred gold coins. In that case appreciation and depreciation of the original coins fall upon the borrower, like the case of lending one se'ah for one se'ah with a fixed monetary amount, as explained in Bava Metzia 75 and in Rashi there, s.v.
