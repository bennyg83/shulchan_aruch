Beyond that, he must obtain annulment of his vow, etc. Why do we not say that after one year she should adorn herself, thereby become prohibited to him through the vow, and then wait another seven days under Beit Hillel's rule concerning a husband who vows to refrain from marital relations? One may answer that Beit Hillel's seven-day period exists only to avoid immediate divorce, in the hope that within seven days he will find a way to annul the vow. Here, after he failed to find an opening for annulment during an entire year, we do not give him an additional period. Tosafot at the beginning of chapter HaMadir, Ketubot 70, passage beginning "so too, since he is obligated to her," indicate otherwise. They write that according to Shmuel, who says even an unspecified vow is allowed a waiting period lest he find a way to annul it, the mishnah should not have said, "beyond that he must divorce and pay the ketubah," but rather after thirty days she should derive benefit, become prohibited, and under Beit Hillel wait one additional week. See Rambam, Hilchot Ishut 13:12-13, concerning one who vows that his wife may not visit her father's house. She has one month if they live in the same city, or one festival if they live in different cities, and no additional period is stated. Likewise, in halachah 13, one who vows that she may not go to a house of mourning or feast must either annul his vow or divorce and pay the ketubah. Maggid Mishneh explains that this means within the known thirty-day period discussed in chapter 12. One can reject the proof by saying that even one who prohibits her from her father's house receives the entire following month as the additional waiting period. Likewise, when Rambam writes in chapter 13, halachah 8, that for a poor woman he may maintain a vow against adornment for one year, and beyond that must annul it, perhaps his intent is that after the year he receives another thirty days to obtain annulment. Maggid Mishneh explains similarly regarding the house of mourning, because Rambam does not interpret those cases as vows tied to marital relations but as ordinary prohibitions of benefit from his property, for which the standard period is thirty days.
