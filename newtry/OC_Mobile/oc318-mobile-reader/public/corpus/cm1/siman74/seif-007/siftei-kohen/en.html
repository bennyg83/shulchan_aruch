And he has a route to that country, etc. All the more so when merchants from that country are presently found here. So write the Semag and other authorities, and Maharshal in Bava Kamma.
And he tells him, etc. This applies even where the governments object when they discover the currency, so long as they do not actively search for it. So follows from the Gemara and the Rosh.
And the governments object, etc. Even if the borrower offers to indemnify him against every loss, the lender need not accept it, because no one knows how far a king’s anger may reach. So writes Maharshal.
He gives him currency, etc. Where the actual coins originally lent remain intact, the Darkei Moshe and Sema cite two opinions. The Bach treats it as an unresolved question of law, placing the burden of proof on the claimant. Tosafos in HaGozel appears to make no distinction, and the other authorities likewise do not distinguish. In every case he pays with currency valid at the time of payment. The Semag states this and records that Rashi so ruled in a responsum. This appears correct.
Current at that time. If the governments do not object at all even when they find the old currency, then even where the lender has no route to that country the borrower may repay with the currency originally lent, as stated by the Gemara, Rosh, and Tur. Maharshal limits this to a case in which merchants of that country are present locally.
He stipulated that he would give money, etc. The same applies if he sold merchandise on condition that the buyer pay him money. So writes Tosafos and Maharshal.
He gives him currency, etc. Maharshal writes that if a loan had a fixed due date, the currency was still valid then, and the lender demanded payment but the borrower repeatedly postponed him until the currency was invalidated, the borrower pays only the currency current at the later time. Maharam Alshich writes similarly. In my view this is not correct, and Maharam’s ruling does not prove it. According to the principal reading in Maharam, cited by the Mordechai, he would have had to give the original money had he paid on the eve of Chanukah, which if anything suggests the opposite. See also Shiltei Giborim.
And some disagree, etc. The Darkei Moshe attributes the dissent to Responsa Rosh and Rivash, and the Sema records this, but the Rivash actually writes the opposite and the Rosh is not conclusive. I have explained at length elsewhere that dina demalchuta dina applies here, contrary to Maharshal and the Bach. I later found that Maharam Alshich rules this plainly and indicates that the Rosh also agrees. Shiltei Giborim likewise rules so. See the additional responsa cited there for further cases of these currency laws.
