If one lends another currency that is subsequently invalidated, and the borrower can spend it in another country and has occasion to travel there, he may repay with the currency he borrowed and tell the lender, 'Go and spend it in such-and-such place.' If he has no occasion to travel there, or he does but the governments object and search anyone transporting invalidated currency, he must pay with the currency currently in circulation. The same applies to a ketubah. {Rama: All this applies where he stipulated that he would give him money. If he made no stipulation, he may in every case repay with the currency he borrowed. If he stipulated that he would give currency current in commerce, he must in every case give spendable currency. If the king enacted how debtors must pay, the law of the kingdom is law, and payment follows the enactment. Others disagree, as stated in Responsa of the Rosh, rule 103, responsa 1 and 2. See below, siman 369, regarding the law of the kingdom, and Yoreh De'ah, siman 165, regarding these laws.}
