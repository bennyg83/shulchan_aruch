By contrast, in a place where the borrower may redeem during the term, the parties are understood to have regarded the term as a benefit granted by the lender to the borrower. If the borrower wishes to pay early, he may do so. The Baal HaTerumot writes this in the name of the Ramban, and the Ran in HaIsha Shenaflu cites a responsum of the Ramban accordingly. This resolves Rabbeinu Yerucham’s ruling, cited by the Beit Yosef in seif 8, concerning one who mortgaged two fields for one hundred and before the term wished to pay fifty and recover one field. The lender may say, “I will continue eating the produce until you pay all one hundred.” One might ask why the ruling speaks only of paying fifty, since even payment in full could seemingly be refused until the term. The case must concern a place where early redemption is allowed. See what I write below in seif 4, seif katan 18.

But a deposit in such a case is exempt. The reason is that his period of custody has ended. The Sema, seif katan 9, asks why a custodian who ordinarily deals with money should not remain liable, since siman 292 explains that such a person may use deposited money and is liable as a paid custodian. There is no difficulty. That rule concerns an ordinary deposit, not one whose owner has been told, “Take what is yours,” for at that point the custody has ended. My teacher wrote this in the margin of his Sema, and this also appears to be the Bach’s view. The Mordechai in Mi SheAchazo writes that a custodian is plainly exempt once he says, “I no longer wish to guard it,” even from negligence. Maharshal, chapter 10 of Bava Kamma, siman 53, rules likewise. See also Ritva to Kiddushin 12 on “Take this sela as a deposit,” and what I write at the end of siman 120.
