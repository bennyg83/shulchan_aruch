At the time. The Sema writes that it appears this means while still within the term. The Rama added this phrase so that it should not contradict what the Tur and Shulchan Aruch write at the end of siman 120 concerning a borrower who tells the creditor, 'Take your money, bundled,' and so forth. The Bach explains that it means at the end of the term, and that is the principal explanation. The Sema himself explains it this way there in siman 120. Shach. The Taz likewise explains it this way and writes that there is no difficulty from siman 120, because the Rama actually cites a dissenting opinion there. See there.
Exempt. The reason is that his custodial obligation has ended. The Sema wonders why the custodian should not remain liable when he regularly conducts business with money, since siman 292 explains that he may use deposited money and is liable for it as a paid custodian. There is no difficulty. There the case is an ordinary deposit, not one where he has told the owner, 'Take what is yours,' at which point his custodial obligation has ended. My teacher, the Gaon, wrote likewise; this also appears to be the Bach's view, and Maharshal ruled accordingly. All this applies when the due date has arrived. Before the due date he remains liable, since he accepted responsibility for guarding the deposit until that time, as the Mechaber writes at the beginning of siman 293. Shach.
