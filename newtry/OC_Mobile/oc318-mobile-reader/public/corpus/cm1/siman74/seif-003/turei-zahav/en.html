(Seif 4) “If he mortgaged it.” This concerns land mortgaged so the creditor may consume its produce while deducting its value, which is the ordinary form of such a loan. Sefer HaTerumot asks whether one who mortgaged property for one hundred may force the creditor to accept a portion of the property worth one hundred and relinquish the rest. The creditor can say that he prefers cultivating and consuming all the produce, not merely part. Therefore, if movable property was pledged and the debtor wishes to redeem half while leaving a proportionate half, the creditor should be compelled to accept, because refusing is the trait of Sodom when he suffers no loss. The Beit Yosef wonders why the Tur compares the Rosh’s ruling to the Ramban’s. The comparison is sound. The Rosh considered a house worth two hundred mortgaged for one hundred while the creditor occupied the whole house through deductions. The debtor sought to convey half the house in satisfaction of the debt. The Rosh ruled that ordinarily a debtor cannot force a creditor to accept excess property or a partition, and all the more so where the creditor would lose the benefit of occupying the whole house. Even if the whole house remained physically intact but the debtor sought to force a conveyance as a sale, he lacked that power. This supports the Ramban where partial redemption would deprive the creditor of full use. Where no loss exists, however, movable property should be divided and the creditor compelled to avoid the trait of Sodom.
