In a place where they do not permit early repayment. See Sema, seif katan 7, regarding loans made by shopkeepers in which a fixed time is set during which the borrower gives them profit, and the lender may prevent the borrower from terminating the arrangement early. At first glance this is difficult, because one may receive profit only through an iska arrangement, and below, siman 176 seif 23, it is explained that a recipient in an iska may withdraw just like a worker. I found that Tumim raises this question. It appears that the case is an iska of the sort commonly used in our time, where the parties settle on a fixed return, for example ten per hundred for a year. If the recipient withdraws, although he can cease conducting the business, he remains obligated to pay the entire agreed amount for the year. If he does not wish to pay that amount, he must instead account for the actual iska profits, swear how much he earned during that period, and pay one half. He cannot simply offer a proportional part of the fixed settlement, because the fixed term was established for the lender's benefit. The lender can say, "I agreed to this fixed amount only because of the benefit of the fixed term that was established in my favor." This appears correct.
