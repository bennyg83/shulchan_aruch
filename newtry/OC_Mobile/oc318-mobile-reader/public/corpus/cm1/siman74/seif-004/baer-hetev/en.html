Payment. The Shach proves at length from the authorities that the Gemara's discussion concerns the effect after the fact rather than an initial right to insist upon installment payment. The lender cannot say, "Take back each coin you paid me one by one and keep responsibility for it until the entire amount is paid; if you refuse, I will hold it at your risk and it will not count as payment." The law teaches that after the fact each partial payment does count as payment. See the Shach's full discussion.
Half. According to the Tur, even where the lender does not consume the produce of mortgaged land, the borrower cannot force him to return half the mortgage upon paying half the debt during the term. The lender may say, "Since you seek to redeem before the due date, I am not obligated to release any portion of the collateral. I want to retain strong security in full so that you will be pressured to pay the whole debt at maturity." The Shach says the same rules apply to land and movable pledges. The Taz disagrees and limits the case to where the lender consumes the produce. According to him, with movable collateral the borrower may redeem half and leave proportionate collateral for the remainder, and refusal would be an improper use of one's legal right.
