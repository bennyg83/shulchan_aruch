Or after thirty days, it is not consecrated at all. See Responsa Rabbi Akiva Eiger, siman 142, who strongly questions this ruling. The Rashba explains that with consecrated property one cannot say, “the corpus from today and the produce after thirty days,” because during the interim the produce would draw nourishment from consecrated property. The phrase is therefore analogous to a get that says “from today and after death,” which is uncertain whether the latter words are a condition or a retraction. In a case of doubtful consecration, property remains in the original owner’s possession. Rabbi Akiva Eiger asks: even if the words “after thirty days” retract “from today,” the statement should then be treated simply as “after thirty days,” which can itself be effective for consecration. He cites the sugya in Nedarim and the Ran. The difficulty is understandable with “from today and after death,” because when the later time arrives the consecration can no longer take effect after death. But with “from today and after thirty days,” why is it worse than saying only “after thirty days”? One may explain the Rashba as meaning merely that the owner may retract during the thirty days. Had we interpreted the later words as a condition, retraction would be ineffective; because they may instead be a retraction, we leave the property in the owner’s possession. Yet the Rama’s categorical wording that it “is not consecrated at all” remains difficult. Even as practical law after the owner retracts, Rabbi Akiva Eiger is uncertain whether one may be lenient, since the Ran holds that retraction is ineffective and the Rashba himself expresses doubt in his novellae. The problem is even greater because the Rashba’s responsum dealt only with Temple treasury consecration, where drawing nourishment from consecrated property is prohibited. Our charitable hekdesh, meaning funds of the poor, creates no such problem. There one can uphold the words exactly like a private gift, with the corpus acquired from today and the produce after thirty days. Thus, the Rama’s gloss requires great study.
