One who writes his property to his son or another from today and after death, and a healthy person's gift written from today and after death. It contains seven seifim.
If one writes his property to his son to take effect after the father's death, the son's ownership of the principal begins from the date of the deed, while the father retains the produce until death. Therefore, the father cannot sell the principal because it belongs to the son, and the son cannot sell unrestricted possession because it remains under the father's control. Rama: If one consecrates land "from today and after my death," or "after thirty days," the consecration is entirely ineffective, because principal-now and produce-later cannot be applied to consecrated property while the donor continues consuming sacred produce. One who writes all his property to another from today and after death must sell the assets, purchase land with the proceeds, and consume its produce like a husband in his wife's property, unless he stipulated that he may deal with the assets throughout life as he wishes.
