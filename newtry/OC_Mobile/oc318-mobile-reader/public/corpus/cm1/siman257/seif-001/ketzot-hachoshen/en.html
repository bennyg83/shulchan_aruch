Or after thirty days it is not consecrated at all. Rashba rules that one who consecrates a field to Temple maintenance "from now and after death" or "from now and after thirty days" effects no consecration. In an ordinary private gift, "from now and after death" can mean the body from now and fruits after death. But Temple consecration cannot be structured so that a private person's tree continues drawing nourishment from sacred land unless the place of nourishment was expressly retained. If one of the two components must fail, we leave the land in the prior owner's possession. Rashba compares the Jerusalem Talmud's treatment of ownerless designation: "My field is ownerless from now and after thirty days." According to the Sages, who are uncertain whether the latter phrase is a condition or a retraction, the ownerless designation fails because one cannot easily divide the body and fruits in that way. Still, this ruling requires qualification. Nedarim teaches that one who says, "This ox is an olah after thirty days," can create consecration at the later date. Ran explains that according to one view he cannot retract because speech to Heaven is like actual delivery; Rashba says he may retract, but if he does not, the consecration nevertheless takes effect. Therefore even if "from now and after thirty days" cannot be divided into body and fruits, it should at least be doubtful whether the speaker meant a condition or withdrew from the word "from now" and intended consecration after thirty days. Rashba himself rules elsewhere like Rav that "from now and after thirty days" is doubtful whether condition or retraction. Accordingly, the present responsum should be understood where the consecrator retracted within the thirty days. If he never retracted, the later consecration should at least remain subject to the ordinary doubt. Rama's unqualified wording, "it is not consecrated at all," is therefore difficult. See siman 258.
The limitation stated by Rashba applies only to true Temple maintenance consecration, where sacred ownership makes it impossible for a private tree to continue drawing nourishment from the consecrated field unless a place was retained. Our contemporary charitable dedications are not Temple consecration. They have the monetary law of ordinary private property for the poor. Therefore one can validly transfer the body from today and retain fruits until after thirty days, exactly as in an ordinary gift. Rabbeinu Yerucham states this explicitly: the rule invalidating consecration with reserved fruits applies to sacred Temple property, but with dedication to the poor the donor's words stand. Since we no longer have Temple maintenance consecration in ordinary practice, Rama's citation of Rashba's rule is not generally applicable to our contemporary dedications.
