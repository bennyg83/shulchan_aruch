One who consecrates, etc., for indeed, etc. As stated there: "and when it is written, etc., but here, etc."
For indeed, etc. As stated there 71b, Rabbi Shimon said: "what is the reason, etc.?" The same applies here. If you ask why one should nullify the consecration rather than nullify the retention of the fruits, that is not so, because once we are in doubt the law is necessarily like that of a get, where we rule stringently that she must perform chalitzah, etc. So too here the stringency operates against the claimant, as I wrote above siman 250:3. This is also stated in the Yerushalmi concerning the dispute between Rebbe and the Sages in Gittin 72b, and Kiddushin 59b, regarding "from today and after my death": if one declares property ownerless from now and after thirty days, according to Rebbe it is ownerless, while according to the Sages it is not ownerless, because one cannot say the field is ownerless while the fruits remain the owner's. Since they hold the matter is doubtful, the money remains in its owner's possession.
One who writes, etc. As stated there 137a: "the first one has no, etc.," and certainly so here.
