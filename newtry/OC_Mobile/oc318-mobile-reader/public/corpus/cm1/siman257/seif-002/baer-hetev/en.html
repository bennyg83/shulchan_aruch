The Rashbam, however, explains that only produce already detached belongs to the recipient; produce still attached to the ground belongs to the son even if it is ready to be harvested. Tosafot Yom Tov in the chapter Yesh Nochalin questions the Rashbam from the discussions in the chapters HaKones and Naarah. He did not examine the poskim I cited in siman 95 closely enough, for according to what I wrote there there is no difficulty. On the contrary, the plain meaning of the Mishnah supports the Rashbam. There is also a difficulty for the Rambam and those who agree with him. The Gemara gives as the reason that a person's mind is close to his son, but if the produce remains attached and still requires the land, everyone treats it as land. One may answer that without the Gemara's reason we would have said that presumably the donor intended to give only the property that existed at the time and would be treated as though he had expressly excluded produce that would arise later. The Gemara therefore needs the reason that a person's mind is close to his son. According to the Rashbam, however, the Gemara's reasoning fits straightforwardly. What the Bach writes on the Rambam and Rashbam is incorrect; his question is unfounded from the outset, and perhaps there is a textual error in his words. See there. Shach.
