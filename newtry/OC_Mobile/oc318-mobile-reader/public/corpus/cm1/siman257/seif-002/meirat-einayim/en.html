Or the produce had reached the time for harvesting, etc. See what I wrote in the Perishah in siman 95, siman 193, and siman 215:5 regarding the dispute between Rambam and the Tur.
The recipient has no right even to the produce, etc. Since the produce grew while the property was still in the giver's possession. Specifically where the recipient is the giver's son do we say that the father's intention was to give generously to his son. We do not say so when the son sells his right to someone else, and all the more so when the recipient was an unrelated person from the outset.
And this recipient gives money. This must mean that the recipient gives the heirs the assessed value of the produce that had grown before the giver died. The Tur likewise writes this.
Or he leaves the produce. Meaning, once it ripens, the heirs take it for themselves, and they need not pay the recipient anything for the growth that occurred after the land entered his possession. See the Perishah.
But if he wrote the gift to his grandson, it is as though he wrote it to his son. Although the Sages said that a grandson is not included in the ordinary expression "my sons," as the Tur and Mechaber write above in siman 247, if he explicitly writes a gift to his grandson, we assume he gave that gift generously, just as we say concerning a gift to his son, because a person's mind is close to his son. Terumat HaDeshen, siman 350, says this concerning a granddaughter, and the same, or all the more so, applies to a grandson.
