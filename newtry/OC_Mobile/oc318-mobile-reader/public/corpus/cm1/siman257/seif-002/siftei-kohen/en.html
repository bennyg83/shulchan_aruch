Or when they became ready to be harvested. This is the view of the Rambam, Rosh, and Tur. Rashbam, however, explains that only detached produce is included; attached produce, even when ready to be harvested, belongs to the son. Tosafot Yom Tov, in Perek Yesh Nochalin, questions how Rashbam can explain the passages in HaKones and in Perek Na'arah, but he did not examine closely the depth of the authorities' words that I cited above in siman 95. According to what I wrote there, there is no difficulty. On the contrary, the plain sense of the Mishna supports Rashbam, while the Rambam and those who agree with him face the difficulty that the Gemara explains the rule by saying that a person's intent is especially favorable toward his son. If attached produce that still needs the ground is legally treated as land for all purposes, why is that reason needed? One may answer that without this reason we would assume that he intended to give only the assets then in existence, as though he had expressly excluded future produce. Therefore the Gemara requires the principle that a person's intent is favorable toward his son. According to Rashbam, however, the passage is straightforward. Examine this carefully.
