And he purchased it. Meaning, the father later purchased back from the son the corpus of the land that he had given him. When it says that the father's sale was ineffective, this means after the father's death. During his lifetime, however, the purchaser eats the fruits. It teaches that we do not say the situation is as though the father had never given the land to his son, such that the land would remain with the purchaser from the father. Rather, since at the outset, when the father sold it to this purchaser, he had power to sell only the fruits for his lifetime, we do not later treat that sale retroactively as a complete sale. Sema.
And some say. They hold that the father sold the purchaser only the right that the father himself possessed in it. Therefore, the son need not give the purchaser anything for the fruits that grew while the property was in the son's possession, just as he would not have given anything to the father's heirs had the land remained with the father until his death.
