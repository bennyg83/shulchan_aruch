The Maggid Mishneh writes concerning Ulla's answer, 'there is no difficulty; here regarding his son, here regarding another,' at the beginning of 139a. The Rif explains the second case as one who purchased from the father. In the case of a son, because a person's mind is close to his son, the father waives the attached fruits to him at the time of death, so the other heirs do not inherit them. A purchaser, however, is an outsider, and the seller does not waive them in favor of the seller's son, so they are appraised. The Rosh likewise explains so, and this is Rambam's view. The same understanding follows from Baal HaMaor's reading of the Tosefta, that the father's waiver to the son occurs at death.
