The son removes them from the purchasers. See Responsa Avodat HaGershuni, siman 89. The questioner wished to say that this applies only where the father sold the property not for his own food and support, but that if he sold it for his food and support the son cannot recover it from the purchasers after the father's death, because there is a compelling presumption that the father gave it to his son only if he would have a surplus beyond all his needs throughout his lifetime, but not that he intended to leave himself destitute if he needed it for sustenance. The author rejects this. Rashba explicitly writes in a responsum, cited in Beit Yosef, siman 250, section 15, that even if the father sold it for his sustenance, the sale is effective only until the father's death. See there at length.
