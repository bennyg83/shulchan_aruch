Property that is not then in his possession, etc. The Beit Yosef, siman 250, cites the Rashba. Noda BiYehuda, siman 29, records a question: the Rama seems to imply that a kinyan fails only for property not then in the giver’s possession, whereas the Rashba appears to say that even presently owned property cannot be effectively arranged in the contemplated manner. The Rashba writes that if a person has business merchandise or unspecified movable assets and wants to divide them, giving one person more and another less, he knows no way to convey them through a healthy person’s gift “from today and after death,” because the assets he owns today may not be the assets he owns at death. Thus, his only reliable solution is through personal obligations. The questioner found this puzzling. Noda BiYehuda answers that the Rashba and Rama are entirely consistent. The Rashba’s statement concerns his second proposed method, in which the giver expressly reserves a power of retraction or writes that the gift shall stand only if he does not retract. If the giver retains that power, there is no secure way to identify current business assets as the subject of the future gift, because he may sell or consume them before death. The Rashba does not mean that the initial acquisition is ineffective. The recipients do acquire at the time of the kinyan, but if the giver later sells the items, that sale itself constitutes a retraction from the gift as to those items. By contrast, under the Rashba’s first method, where the gift is made now in a manner that leaves no power of retraction, then even if the specific property is no longer in existence at death the heirs may be liable for its value. The Rama’s words are therefore also precise. He is discussing the Mechaber’s case, “from today if I do not retract,” and says that even there a kinyan is needed, and that a kinyan cannot help as to property not in the giver’s possession. Property presently in his possession can certainly be given, but because he retained the power to retract, the heirs may respond that he later sold or consumed it and thereby retracted. To prevent that argument he would have to purport to give whatever assets he will own at death, but that fails because such future property is not presently in his possession. The Beit Yosef’s citation of the Rashba regarding the use of an acknowledgment refers to the Rashba’s explicit alternative that such an arrangement can be accomplished through an obligation or acknowledgment. Noda BiYehuda further discusses a healthy man who divided all his property by a formal kinyan between his wife and his niece, thereby disinheriting his brothers. The brothers later sought to invalidate the gifts because the merchandise then in the estate was not the merchandise owned at the time of the kinyan; the earlier stock had been sold and replaced. They argued that the entire gift should fail as a single transfer combining property in the giver’s possession with property not in his possession, under the dispute in siman 203. Noda BiYehuda rules that if the giver did not reserve the power to retract, the heirs’ claim fails entirely, because even what he later sold gives rise to liability for its value. If he did reserve a power of retraction, the heirs are correct only as to merchandise that he sold, because the sale was a retraction as to those items and the beneficiaries do not receive their sale proceeds. All other objects, movable property, and land that remain in existence are still acquired by the beneficiaries. The heirs’ attempt to invalidate the entire gift as a mixed acquisition works only on the mistaken premise that the Rashba meant that the initial kinyan never took effect. Once we understand that it did take effect and was later retracted only as to items sold, retraction as to some property does not constitute retraction as to property never sold.
