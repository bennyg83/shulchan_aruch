With the permission of the courtyard owner. Meaning, the courtyard owner gave Reuven permission to bring his ox into the courtyard, and Reuven's ox then damaged the courtyard owner's ox. In that case everyone agrees that Reuven is exempt. If the reverse occurred and the courtyard owner's ox damaged Reuven's ox that had entered, the authorities disagree. The Mechaber rules like Rebbi, who holds that even though the owner gave Reuven permission to bring in his ox, he did not thereby accept responsibility to guard Reuven's ox. Rama cites the authorities who rule like the Sages, who hold that by default the courtyard owner accepted responsibility for guarding Reuven's ox. The reason is that the courtyard belongs to him and ordinarily he and his household are present there, unlike the person who brought in his ox, who does not remain there to guard it from injuring the courtyard owner. See Perishah and siman 291, where this dispute is explained. See also siman 393, where I wrote that Rama's gloss there is difficult.
If the courtyard owner's ox caused damage, etc., he is exempt. The wording specifically says the courtyard owner's ox. If the courtyard owner himself injured the entering ox, he would be liable, as the Mechaber writes in seif 1, that if he damaged it knowingly he is liable. "Knowingly" means he knew the animal had entered, even if it entered without permission and even if his act of damage was inadvertent, because a person is always forewarned, as explained in siman 378. All the more so here, where the animal entered with permission.
