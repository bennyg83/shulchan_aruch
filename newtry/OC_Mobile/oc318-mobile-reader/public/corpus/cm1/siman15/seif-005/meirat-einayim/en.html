A judge may adjudicate, etc. Rambam and the Tur set out the details more fully; see there.
After courts multiplied, etc. Maharik writes that even nowadays a judge must decide according to the truth that has become clear to him rather than follow pleadings that contradict established reality. Rashba, Responsa siman 1146, writes likewise.
Likewise, we do not extract from orphans, etc. Rambam discusses an older practice: if someone claimed that a certain object was deposited with a deceased person, gave identifying marks, was not ordinarily present in the deceased's house, and the judge knew the deceased was unlikely to own such an object, the judge could rely on the strong estimate and remove it from the heirs. Rambam says this is not done nowadays. Rif and the Rosh omit this limitation, however, and the Tur states without qualification below at the end of siman 297 that property may be taken from the heirs on this basis, implying even nowadays. Their reason is that the heirs do not make a definite claim that the object belonged to their father and was not deposited with him. The Beit Yosef acknowledges this view, yet the Shulchan Aruch here rules simply like Rambam. It is surprising that Rama does not mention the dissent.
