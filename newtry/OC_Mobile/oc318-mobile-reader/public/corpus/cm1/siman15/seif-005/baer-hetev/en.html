From the orphans. This excludes the practice described in earlier times: if a person came and claimed, "I have a deposit in the possession of so-and-so who died without leaving instructions," and he gave identifying marks, and the claimant was not accustomed to entering the deceased's house, then if the judge knew that the deceased was not of sufficient means to own such an object, the item was removed from the heirs and given to the person who supplied the identifying marks. Know that the Rif and Rosh omit this qualification, and the Tur states generally that the item is removed from the heirs. This implies that even today one should act this way. The reason is that the heirs do not make a certain claim that the item belonged to their father, and therefore it may be removed from them even today. It is surprising that the Rama did not mention their view. Sema. See Responsa Maharam Alshich, siman 40, and Responsa Maharam Alashkar, siman 119.
