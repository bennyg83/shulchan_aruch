But if his relative, etc. Urim VeTumim asks from Shevuot, where Rabbi Yitzchak sent word concerning Ulla son of Rav Ilai, and apparently his father was a Torah scholar, so why was lineage through a scholar not enough for precedence? There is no difficulty. The special honor applies while the scholarly relative is alive, when honoring the litigant also honors the relative. After the relative's death, there is no basis to investigate everyone's ancestry merely to determine precedence. Urim VeTumim further seeks proof that a Torah scholar who is the defendant has no priority from the same sugya, where Tosafot say the opposing party was also a Torah scholar. He infers that the other scholar must have been the defendant, otherwise there would already have been priority. This proof is not decisive. The opposing scholar may have litigated through an authorized representative after the case had already begun, in which situation no precedence would arise. Moreover, the rationale for giving precedence to a Torah scholar who is the defendant can rest on avoiding disrespect to beit din or unnecessary waste of his learning time, and those considerations do not apply in the same way to a representative.
