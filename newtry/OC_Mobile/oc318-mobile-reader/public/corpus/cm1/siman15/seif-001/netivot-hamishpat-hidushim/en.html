Before him first. Meaning, he appeared first before the judge and the judge prepared himself to hear his claims, Sema:
In his favor. The Sema writes that in the present time there is no special law of precedence for a Torah scholar in this regard. Many disagree, and their view is primary:
They already began. By Torah law, precedence applies only once the court has actually begun the case. If they have not begun and one litigant merely arrived first, his precedence is only rabbinic. Therefore, only the Torah scholar himself, regarding whom there is a positive commandment to honor Torah, displaces even a case that has already begun, because the positive commandment to honor Torah takes precedence over the Torah law of judicial precedence. A relative of a Torah scholar, however, takes precedence only where the earlier case has not yet begun, because that precedence is merely rabbinic, but not where the earlier case has already begun, where the precedence is from Torah law. See Beurim, seif katan 1, that this applies to the relative of a Torah scholar only while the Torah scholar is still alive, not after his death.
