But if he is his relative. The Bach and Knesset HaGedolah challenge Rama’s ruling here. Responsa Shevut Yaakov, part I, siman 144, writes that although their textual criticism of Rama is not compelling, since Rama followed the Krakow printing of Beit Yosef, their practical conclusion is correct. We find no source in the Gemara or poskim imposing a duty to honor the relative of a talmid chacham, and Rif, Rambam, and other authorities do not mention priority for a scholar’s relative. Rav Nachman’s wish to give priority to Rav Anan’s relative in Ketubot 106 occurred because Rav Anan’s message revealed that he desired his relative to be advanced, and Rav Nachman sought to honor Rav Anan by fulfilling his wish. But if the talmid chacham himself does not request priority for the relative, there is certainly no basis to treat such priority as obligatory. Even if the scholar wants it, one should not do so, because it may inhibit the opposing litigant’s arguments; indeed Rav Anan was punished in connection with that incident. A son of a talmid chacham may possibly be different and may have a right of priority, as we find regarding Torah reading and seating at a meal. Birkei Yosef analyzes Shevut Yaakov and Responsa Chavot Yair, simanim 21 and 23, and concludes that the primary halachah follows Shevut Yaakov: a relative of a talmid chacham receives no priority merely by virtue of that kinship.
