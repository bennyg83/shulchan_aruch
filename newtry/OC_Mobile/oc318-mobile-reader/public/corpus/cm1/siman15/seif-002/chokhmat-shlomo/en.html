Seif 2. "If many cases were before him," etc. Note: The Tur cites in the name of the Rambam that a widow takes precedence over a Torah scholar. They wondered at him, for in the Gemara, chapter 2 of Ketubot, it is proven regarding an orphan that a Torah scholar takes precedence; and Darkei Moshe already raised this difficulty — see there — and among the commentators. And in my view it appears that it is resolved simply: for necessarily there is no obligation to prioritize an orphan's case except when the claimant is the orphan; for then, since he lacks the capacity to look after himself, if he sees that they push him off his spirit will be anguished lest he lose his money, thinking that the court does not wish to attend to him; therefore one is obligated to prioritize an orphan's case so that he not think the court is unwilling to attend to his claim. But when the orphan is the defendant, there is no obligation to prioritize him. For in that case there is no anguish for the orphan; on the contrary, if they do not attend to him he will certainly be exempt, since so long as the court does not extract from him he is exempt — and what anguish has he if they do not attend to him? Therefore there is no obligation to prioritize him. And accordingly it is well that the Rambam deals with a case where the claimant is a Torah scholar; and likewise the orphan and the widow who are the claimants — therefore they take precedence over the case of a Torah scholar. But the Gemara deals with a case of orphans' law, namely where the orphans were the defendants; therefore in that case the law of a Torah scholar takes precedence. And that the Gemara spoke of orphans' law and mentioned the defendants rather than the claimants is itself for this novelty: that with defendants a Torah scholar takes precedence over an orphan's case, and one is not obligated in that case to prioritize the orphan — and this is clear and very correct. And afterward they showed him that Urim VeTumim cites in the name of responsa Chavat Yair and responsa Shevut Yaakov who distinguish thus between claimant and defendant. And thank God that I intended their view in this. In that same seif: "If many cases were before him," etc. Note: At first glance it is difficult to me why all the later and earlier authorities were silent and did not explain whether, if one is a kohen, one must prioritize him first and judge his case first, as they prioritize the case of a Torah scholar; and all the more regarding a kohen, since the Sages said, "And you shall sanctify him" — for every matter of sanctity, to open first, etc.; and if so, to begin with his case first is included in "to open first." And it seems to me that I saw so in some book that explains thus in truth: that which the Gemara said "to open first" means regarding judgment, that one must prioritize a kohen's case first. And even without this interpretation it appears proven explicitly from the Gemara, Nedarim 62: it says there that a Torah student is permitted to say, "I am a Torah student; settle my dispute first," as it is said, "And the sons of David were kohanim" — just as a kohen takes a share first, so a Torah scholar, etc. If so, it is proven explicitly that we only know that a Torah scholar's case should be prioritized because they are called kohanim and a kohen takes his share first; therefore all the more with an actual kohen there is an obligation to prioritize him over another's case. And if so, since all ruled that a Torah scholar's case should be prioritized, all the more a kohen's case, as proven explicitly in the Gemara — and this requires further study regarding all of them, why they did not explain that such is the law between a kohen and a Yisrael, that one must prioritize the kohen's case; and for practical law it appears as I wrote. And especially since the Gemara there asks, "And a kohen — from where do we know?" — implying that also for a kohen one is obligated to settle the dispute first. And on the contrary, the essence of the Torah-scholar law grew from the fact that he is called a kohen, as the Gemara wrote — and this requires further study. And see what I wrote in my novellae on Yoreh De'ah, laws of milah, regarding milah of a kohen — which takes precedence. And one may say that they omitted this because we are not expert in priestly lineage, as Magen Avraham wrote in Orach Chayim siman 510; but if so, behold the law of a Torah scholar also does not apply nowadays — and it still requires further study.
