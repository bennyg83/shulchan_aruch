But when the defendant is deceitful. Maharam and Rosh state this rule in resolving the Tosafot difficulty. Sanhedrin says that a suspicious case requires investigation, while Shevuot derives from "distance yourself from falsehood" that one should withdraw from a deceitful case. They distinguish: if the plaintiff is deceitful, withdrawal prevents his fraud from succeeding; if the defendant is deceitful, withdrawal would reward the wrongdoer, so the judge must pursue the truth. Rambam appears to resolve the sources differently. Initially the judge must investigate carefully. If, after investigation, the judge concludes that the case remains fraudulent, he withdraws. Mechaber in seif 3 adopts Rambam's wording, yet here in seif 4 also cites Maharam and Rosh. Sema therefore explains that Mechaber considered both principles independently compelling even if neither one alone is the exclusive resolution of the textual difficulty.
By a compelling assessment. If the defendant refuses to answer the judge's substantive questions and the judge can see that this refusal is designed to conceal falsehood, the judge may treat the conduct as evidence of deceit and rule as though the evasive answer had exposed the falsehood. Rosh in responsum, cited by Sema and Shach.
