We give priority, etc. Responsa Chavot Yair, simanim 21 and 23, discusses a suggestion that all these priorities apply only when an orphan, widow, or other protected party is the plaintiff, but not when such a person is defendant, since delay may benefit a defendant. He rejects this. Although the verse “judge the orphan, plead the widow’s cause” may appear to speak of plaintiffs, once a person is in court seeking justice, defendant and plaintiff are alike in this respect; delay of judgment is an affliction to both sides. Shevut Yaakov, part I, siman 144, however, writes simply that priority for a talmid chacham applies only when the scholar is plaintiff. If he is defendant, delay in extracting money from him is actually to his advantage. He adduces support from Rashi and uses this to explain the Talmudic rule that the plaintiff’s claim is generally heard first. Birkei Yosef challenges Shevut Yaakov and agrees with Chavot Yair. See Tumim and Netivot HaMishpat. Responsa Rabbi Moshe Rothenburg, Choshen Mishpat, siman 1, asks whether a case in which both litigants are talmidei chachamim takes priority over one in which only one litigant is a talmid chacham. He explains that this depends on Chavot Yair’s question. If defendant status also carries the special priority, then two scholars are certainly superior to one. If a scholarly defendant has no special priority, there may be no difference between one scholar and two because only the plaintiff’s status matters. He offers some support for applying priority to defendants as well. In particular, according to Urim VeTumim’s explanation of Rambam that a scholar is advanced to prevent bitul Torah, there is plainly no distinction between plaintiff and defendant. See there, and see Tumim and Netivot HaMishpat. Chavot Yair further rejects a distinction suggested by the questioner between an orphan who personally appears in beit din and an orphan represented by a guardian. Ordinary discussions of an orphan’s case assume that a guardian argues for him, yet the priority still applies.
