When a judge recognizes that a case before him is fraudulent, he may not say, 'I will decide it, and the chain of sin will hang around the witnesses' necks.' What should he do? He must examine and investigate the matter extensively, as in the examination of a capital case. {Rama: Nevertheless, its law is not actually that of a capital case, in which testimony is invalidated if a witness answers, 'I do not know,' to one of the formal inquiries. Rather, the judge must investigate a fraudulent case as thoroughly as possible (Rivash, siman 266).} If in the judge's estimation there is deceit, if he cannot rely on the witnesses' words although he cannot formally disqualify them, if he believes that a litigant is deceitful and cunning and prompted otherwise valid witnesses who testified innocently, or if the total circumstances indicate concealed facts that the parties refuse to reveal, then in all such cases and the like he may not decide the case. He must withdraw, and the case should be judged by someone whose heart is confident in the matter. These matters are entrusted to the judge's conscience. When the Rosh saw a compelling indication that a case was fraudulent, he would write and give the defendant a document stating that no judge should involve himself in that case.
