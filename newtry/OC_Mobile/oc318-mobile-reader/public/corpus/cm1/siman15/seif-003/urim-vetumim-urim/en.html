Likewise, if a document comes before the judge and appears suspicious, he must investigate even though witnesses signed on a document are ordinarily treated as though their testimony had already been examined in beit din. In a potentially fraudulent case, searching inquiry is required to uncover the truth. Maharik and Sema.

These are matters entrusted to the heart. If the judge senses inwardly that fraud may be present, he may not excuse himself by saying, "I do not sense any fraud and I assume the facts are as presented." The One who examines hearts will judge every hidden matter that the judge himself perceived as suspicious. Nevertheless, the judge can act only according to what his eyes discern. Sema.
When the Rosh would see, etc. Sema asks that above the ruling says that a judge whose heart is whole should adjudicate the matter, yet here the Rosh says that no judge should involve himself. Tur's text reads, "The King of the world will judge them," but the Shulchan Aruch's text is difficult. It appears that if an expert judge, outstanding in his generation, withdraws from the case, who would dare say, "My heart is purer and more capable of separating truth from falsehood and deception"? Such a claim would be arrogance and presumption. Therefore no lesser judge may take the case. This was the situation of the Rosh, who was the great authority of his generation and teacher of the rabbis, so he properly wrote that no other judge should undertake it. But where an ordinary judge who is not renowned as the foremost authority withdraws, and another judge greater than he carefully investigates and says that his own heart is clear enough to decide, there is no reason to forbid him. Hence the matter is "entrusted to the heart": the person whose heart and judgment are more refined may decide, depending on the purity of his motives and clarity of intellect.
