(Collected note) The judge, etc., up to matters entrusted to the heart. All of this is the language of the Rambam. He reconciles the passages in Sanhedrin and Shevuot as written here: initially the judge must investigate, and afterward, if necessary, withdraw. The Rosh distinguishes between claimant and defendant, as written in seif 4. The Rambam begins with the Shevuot passage, "he should not say, etc.," and concludes, "rather, he should investigate, etc.," and afterward, "rather, he should withdraw, etc., if it appears, etc." This is a general statement, after which he details the cases: "or if his mind is not, etc.," from the Shevuot passage; "and the responsibility will hang, etc."; "or if his mind is, etc.," from the Sanhedrin passage, and see Rashi there, s.v. "Merumah, etc."; "or if it appears to him," from the passage at the end of Gittin about the need to clarify, and see the end of siman 75. (End of collected note.)
