[Seif 4] The guard at the threshing floors, and so forth. It appears that the Rambam interprets Rav Pappa's statement, “Here we deal with a guard of the threshing floor,” as follows. A valley contains fields belonging to many owners, who appoint one guard, himself one of the field owners. No individual owner may authorize an outsider to stack produce in a field of the valley without the agreement of the appointed guard. The Sema's question, that no guard was mentioned at the beginning, is therefore unfounded. The conclusion properly says “the guard,” because he is the principal person empowered to grant permission. The Sema attempts to explain why the Tur did not cite the Rosh's view that even according to Ravin, Rabbi grants the threshing-floor guard special authority so that entry without permission is treated like ordinary silent acquiescence. Maharshal writes that the Tur properly omitted this, because no dispute is found concerning entry without permission. Why should unauthorized entry into a field differ from any other unauthorized entry? The Rosh himself offered that suggestion only tentatively.
