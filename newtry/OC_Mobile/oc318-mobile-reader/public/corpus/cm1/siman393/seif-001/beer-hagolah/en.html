a) The language of the Rambam, chapter 3 of Hilkhot Nizkei Mamon, law 14, from the Mishnah in the fifth chapter of Bava Kamma 47a.
bet) From the baraita concerning a woman who entered to grind there on 47b. The Rashba likewise writes that if she slipped and the homeowner's animal was injured, she is exempt, and likewise if the homeowner's animal ate the produce she is exempt. In my humble opinion, this gloss refers to the second clause, 'if he accepted responsibility,' and belongs at the end of the seif. Its dispute is explained at the end of siman 358.
gimel) Following Rabbi Yehudah HaNasi in the Mishnah and Shmuel, who rules accordingly on 48b. The Maggid Mishneh writes that even Rabbi Yehudah HaNasi required explicit acceptance only in order to make the homeowner liable for damage to the entrant's produce. But to exempt the owner of the produce from damage caused to the homeowner's property, once he had permission to enter he is exempt. This is correct, and Tosafot agree; even the Raah conceded this in his glosses.
