One who stacks grain in his fellow's field, etc. The Mechaber's wording is the Rambam's language at the end of chapter 3 of Hilchot Nizkei Mamon. It requires explanation. He concludes that the guardian of the threshing floor, since he told him, "Stack your grain," accepted responsibility, yet earlier did not mention a separate field guard at all. On the contrary, he said that if one stacked with the field owner's permission, which suggests that the field owner himself gave permission. To reconcile the language we must say that the Rambam is speaking where the field owner himself also serves as its guard. Perhaps that was the usual practice, and therefore he did not need to state it. Once the owner himself guards the field and gives permission to stack, the Rif and Rambam agree that even though ordinary permission does not mean, "Enter and I will guard for you," here the owner is already sitting there guarding the field. His permission therefore ordinarily includes acceptance of guarding the stack. It appears that the Rosh too, who writes that even according to the Sages there is a practical distinction regarding a guardian because the law places him one degree higher, speaks only where the field owner himself is the guardian. Otherwise it would be unreasonable that a person could stack grain in another's field without any permission at all and thereby obligate the owner to guard it. The Tur, however, interprets the Gemara as speaking of a separate field guard, for he writes, "If the guardian of the field told him, Enter and stack, even though the field owner did not tell him to enter." This resolves a major difficulty in the Tur. At first glance he contradicts his father the Rosh, who wrote that according to the Sages even without permission it is treated as though permission had been given, while the Tur requires at least permission from the guardian. On the explanation I have given, there is no contradiction. It remains difficult why the Tur omitted the Rosh's particular rule, but according to another explanation I gave in the Perishah this too is resolved.
