If one stacks grain in another person's field without permission and the field owner's animal eats it, the field owner is exempt. If the animal slips on the grain and is injured, the produce owner is liable. If the animal eats the grain and is harmed by eating it, the produce owner is exempt. If he stacked the grain with permission, the field owner is liable even without expressly accepting responsibility to guard it, because at the threshing floors the guard's statement, 'Stack it here,' is understood as, 'Stack it here and I will guard it for you.'
