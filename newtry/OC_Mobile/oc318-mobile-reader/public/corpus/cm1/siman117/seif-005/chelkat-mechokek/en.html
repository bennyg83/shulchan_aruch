He must divorce her and give the entire ketubah. This implies that he must also give the additional ketubah, and that Rambam agrees with Ramban. But Responsa Maharam printed within Responsa Rashba rules that she receives only the one hundred or two hundred of the principal ketubah, not the addition. Whenever she is compelled to leave him against his will, he compares it to a woman who demands divorce on account of a claim, where we rule that he did not add to the ketubah with the intention that she would leave him. This is difficult. Why should this differ from a woman prohibited by a negative commandment whom he married without knowing the prohibition? She deceived him, yet she still receives the additional ketubah. Where the defect does not arise from her own misconduct, he must pay the addition. Why should the present woman be worse, when she is compelled by circumstances? The bleeding may even be caused by contact from him. Why should she lose the addition, especially if she still receives the principal ketubah? One can explain that Maharam speaks where the husband does not wish to divorce her and is prepared to support her through a third party, while she demands divorce because everyone knows, etc. Then he can say, "I did not add to your ketubah with the intention that you would leave me. I am still prepared to wait in hope that a cure may be found." Ran writes that beit din does not forcibly compel him to divorce, although he is obligated to do so. Therefore, if she is the one demanding the divorce rather than he, her case resembles one who comes with a claim and she does not receive the additional ketubah. By contrast, with a secondary forbidden marriage or a widow married to a Kohen Gadol, beit din compels the divorce and she is not the party seeking it. There he must pay the addition because he knowingly exposed himself to that financial liability and should have investigated before marriage whether she was prohibited to him, by Torah or rabbinic law. But where she has defects or vows, which she herself should disclose and the man is not expected to discover, she receives no additional ketubah.
