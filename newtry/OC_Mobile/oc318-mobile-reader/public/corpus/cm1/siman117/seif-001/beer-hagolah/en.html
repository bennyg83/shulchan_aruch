The language of Rambam there, law 4. Maggid Mishneh cites the baraita: one who makes his field an apotiki and then sells it, the creditor collects from it, etc. He writes that Rambam rules like the first Tanna, as does Ramban, and that Rif explains the baraita as referring to an ordinary apotiki. But with an express apotiki, even if the debtor has other property, the creditor certainly collects from it, because his lien rests exclusively on that property. Sema cites Responsa Rashba, siman 117, that where an express apotiki was written, even if the debtor also liened all his other property in the document, it still has the law of an express apotiki and the creditor cannot be forced to collect from other property.
Maggid Mishneh, and likewise Ran, explains Rambam as follows: although if there is other unencumbered property the creditor collects from it rather than from the apotiki, nevertheless if the debtor sold the other property after selling this apotiki, the creditor may foreclose specifically upon the apotiki. This is the special strength of an apotiki. Rabbeinu Ephraim writes likewise, and this is the meaning of the Yerushalmi's statement that "the purchaser should be concerned for himself," implying that a purchaser of an apotiki has greater cause for concern than other purchasers.
