["One who designated his field as an apotiki. See Responsa Mabit, part 1, second collection, siman 141."]
["Only with money. The same applies to movable property, which is considered the debtor's best property. Even where the apotiki was expressly designated, the borrower may remove the creditor from it by paying with such movables. This is the wording of the Sema, and he wrote likewise above in siman 103, seif 6; see there. The Bach also writes so. The Bach writes concerning the Tur's statement that the borrower may remove the creditor with money that he found no explicit source for it and wondered from where the Tur derived it. The Beit Yosef wrote that this appears from Baal HaTerumot, gate 4, siman 4. The Bach sought to derive it from the Mishnah in HaSholeach. In my view nothing is proved from that Mishnah; see there and Tosafos there. Rather, it appears that the Tur derived this from the Rosh in Eizehu Neshekh, regarding Rav Pappa's statement that although an asmachta does not acquire, an apotiki may nevertheless be collected from. The Gemara concludes that this refers to where the debtor said, 'You shall have payment only from this.' The Rosh writes there that it is an apotiki if he says, 'You shall have payment only from this,' even though the debtor can remove the creditor with money. Otherwise it would not be called an apotiki, because the property would already be sold, in whole or in part. Nevertheless, it is not treated as an asmachta. The Rosh likewise explains in the first chapter of Bava Metzia, in the case where Reuven sold a field to Shimon and Reuven's creditor came and seized it, that Reuven may litigate with the creditor. One practical difference is that if Reuven made the field an apotiki, Reuven may remove the creditor with money whereas Shimon cannot. The Tur writes this below in siman 226. That case necessarily concerns an express apotiki, because with an ordinary apotiki the purchaser too may remove the creditor with money, as is evident from the Gemara in HaGozel Kama, the first chapter of Bava Metzia, and HaMekabel, and from the Tur above in siman 115, seif 6, the Beit Yosef there, and all the authorities. Tosafos in HaSholeach also appear to agree. The Maggid Mishneh at the end of chapter 21 of Hilchot Malveh writes in the name of the Ramban and Rashba that with an apotiki even heirs cannot remove the creditor with money, because it is considered as though sold once the father did not redeem it and did not direct that it be redeemed. The Beit Yosef cites this above in siman 115, seif 6. This itself shows that the father could redeem it, and the Maggid Mishneh there explicitly concerns an express apotiki. See Responsa Maharshal, simanim 273 and 275."]
