He collects from other property. Shaar Mishpat was asked about the trustee of holders of a lease of a weighing concession. A certain person was owed brokerage fees by the leaseholders, and they instructed the trustee to give him a debt document, a bill of exchange, signed by the trustee for a certain sum, payable from the funds of the weighing fund. Eventually the trustee ceased serving, and the leaseholders refused to pay the debt. The holder of the bill seized the former trustee's personal funds. The question was whether this seizure was effective.
Shaar Mishpat ruled that the trustee was exempt from paying this debt out of his own pocket, since the debt document stated that payment was to be made from the weighing fund. This is not comparable to an ordinary apotiki, where if the designated property is swept away by a river the creditor collects from other assets unless the debtor expressly stipulated that collection would be only from this property. There is a distinction. In an ordinary apotiki, the debtor did not designate the source of the payment itself, but only a specific field from which the creditor may collect if the debt is not paid. We therefore say the loan was not made solely in reliance on that field. Here, however, the source of payment itself was designated, namely that the debt would be paid from that fund. The borrower therefore undertook no personal obligation beyond that method of payment, even if he did not explicitly say, "you shall have payment only from this." This resembles the rule at the end of the chapter Kol HaGet concerning one who lends money to a poor person on condition that he continually separates tithes for him. Even though he did not explicitly say that he will have repayment only from this, if the poor person later becomes wealthy he is exempt from paying from his own pocket. This is likewise stated in Yoreh Deah, siman 257, seif 5, in the Rama. Nevertheless, Shaar Mishpat distinguishes further: if the trustee voluntarily resigned after the bill had already reached its due date, then even if the holder had not yet demanded payment he is personally liable. If he resigned before the due date, however, then even if he resigned voluntarily he is exempt from paying from his own pocket. See there at length.
