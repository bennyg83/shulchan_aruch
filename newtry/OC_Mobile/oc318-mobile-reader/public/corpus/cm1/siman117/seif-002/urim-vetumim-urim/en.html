A mortgage. Sema explains this as a mortgage with deduction of produce from the debt. This is also the language of Tur and Baal HaTerumos. See Tumim. I concluded that this refers to a case where the mortgage term has ended. Before the term ends, a subsequent sale cannot take effect so long as the creditor has not been removed. See there. According to the Mechaber in Yoreh Deah, who prohibits ordinary deduction arrangements and permits only a Sura type mortgage, which is plainly an explicit apotiki, one must say that the case concerns a full valuation of all the produce so that no interest prohibition remains.
Its law is like an explicit apotiki. One might have thought that the phrase, 'You shall have no payment except from this,' refers only to the produce being credited against the debt. The ruling teaches that for all purposes it has the status of an explicit apotiki. See Sema. Sema cites Rashba that even if the debtor additionally encumbered all his other property, this land nevertheless remains an explicit apotiki and the debtor cannot remove the creditor by offering other property. Responsa Rashba siman 942 further states that if a river washes away the apotiki, the creditor does not collect from the other property. One must therefore explain what purpose the general lien on the other property serves if the creditor can never substitute it and loses even if the apotiki is destroyed. The practical difference is where the designated property turns out not to belong to the debtor, but to have been stolen and held by him unlawfully. In that case the debt remains, as Rama writes in seif 1, and the general lien applies to the debtor's other assets. Otherwise, however, his lien rests solely upon the apotiki, with no effective lien on the remaining property.
