An ordinary mortgage, which is like an ordinary apotiki. This comparison applies only with respect to the right to remove the lien by payment of money. Certainly, however, if the mortgagor sold the property, although with an ordinary apotiki the creditor cannot collect from the apotiki when there is unencumbered property available, as explained above, nevertheless with a mortgage he certainly cannot sell the portion corresponding to the debt, as explained in Yoreh Deah, siman 258, in the gloss. Tosafot likewise write this in Bava Metzia 72, s.v. hashta. It is also evident from Ketubot 59, concerning one who says, "This field that I mortgaged to you, when I redeem it from you it shall become consecrated," which implies that before redemption he cannot consecrate it, and certainly cannot sell it. Tumim also proves this, and it is clear. See Sema, seif katan 11, where there is a textual error. It should read: alternatively, a mortgage is stronger and he cannot remove the creditor by another means, even with movable property. Likewise, in seif katan 19 there is a textual error, and it should read: alternatively, even without this, since a slave has an element of significance. In Shach, seif katan 4, there is also a textual error: the notation was mistakenly placed on seif 6, and it should be placed on seif 5, concerning one who made his slave an apotiki.
