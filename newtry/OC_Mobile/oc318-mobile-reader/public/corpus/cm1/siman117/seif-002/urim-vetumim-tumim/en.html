A mortgage, etc. It appears that this concerns a mortgage given for a fixed term, for example three years, under a deduction arrangement whereby a set amount is deducted annually in exchange for the fruits during those years, and the term has already ended. Otherwise, how could the debtor sell the property while it remains mortgaged? All the poskim derive from the rule, "This field that I mortgaged to you shall become consecrated after I redeem it," that consecration can take effect only after redemption. While the field remains mortgaged, the debtor cannot consecrate the portion corresponding to the debt. Many authorities discuss whether sacred prohibitions created by a vow differ from ordinary consecration; see Yoreh Deah siman 221, seif 42, the Tur, Shulchan Aruch, and later authorities. Tosafot in Bava Metzia 73b, s.v. "now," likewise infer expressly that while the property is in the mortgagee's hands the owner cannot consecrate it, and certainly cannot sell it. Only the excess value above the debt remains available to him. The Ritva in Ketubot, in the discussion of a mortgaged field and consecration, likewise clearly implies that the value corresponding to the debt cannot be consecrated and all the more cannot be sold. See also the Shach in the cited Yoreh Deah passage. Accordingly, our law must concern a mortgage whose term of deduction has already expired. Once the fixed period has ended, it is no longer treated as a lease-like right that prevents a sale, and the discussion of sale becomes meaningful. This still requires study.
