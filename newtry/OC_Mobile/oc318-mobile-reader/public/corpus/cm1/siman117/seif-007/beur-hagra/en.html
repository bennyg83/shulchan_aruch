On condition that he be buried... Sanhedrin 48b, according to Ulla's explanation.
But sanctity of... Erchin 23b, Shevuos 42b concerning collection from consecrated property, and Bava Basra 174b show that ordinary consecration differs. Rava's statement must concern sanctity of the body, comparable to a konam, as stated in Kesubos 59b.
Rather, he redeems it... Erchin there.
And the value... Rashi there, and this is implied by the language that he "adds," meaning beyond the debt itself.
And even with sanctity of... Otherwise any debtor could prohibit the property to his creditor through a konam. Kesubos says that the Sages strengthened certain liens against such unilateral prohibitions. This strengthening is not limited to a husband's rights; the Gemara applies comparable reasoning to a wife's rights. But where one prohibits an item to everyone, rather than only to the creditor, the lien is not always strengthened to override the prohibition.
And some disagree. They hold that the debtor is placed under a ban until he seeks release from his vow. We do find that where the Sages strengthened a lien they sometimes did so even against an act resembling consecration, such as emancipation. Bava Kamma discusses this expressly. See Tosafos there.
