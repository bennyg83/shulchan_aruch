He need not divorce her. At first glance this refers to a case where he has already fulfilled the mitzvah of procreation. Nevertheless it is difficult, because a man may not live without a wife, as explained above in siman 1:8. Moreover, below in siman 154:10, if a man says, "I will live with her in the presence of witnesses," we do not listen to him. All the more so here, where she is not fit for relations at all. One can answer that this very distinction explains the rule. There, because she is fit for relations, there is concern that he will not marry another woman, and therefore he must divorce her or immediately marry another. As long as he remains attached to the first, we fear he will not marry again. Here, however, because she is not fit for relations, he certainly will not remain without another wife. Until he finds a suitable woman, he need not divorce the first and may support her through a third party. This also implies that if he actually marries another woman, he certainly need not divorce the first. That resembles the rule below that he may marry another woman fit to bear children. Yet Responsa Maharam rules that if he wishes to marry another, he must divorce the first lest people say, "This one is his wife and that one is his paramour." I do not know the source for that. When a man marries a woman and remains childless for ten years, we permit him to marry another and do not fear that people will call the second a paramour, even though the first remains fit for relations. All the more so here, where the first woman is not fit for relations. The Aggadah in chapter Af Al Pi uses such language concerning the daughter-in-law of Rabbi who became barren, but it is not stated as practical halachah.
