A prohibition of benefit, etc. This resembles intrinsic sanctity. The Mechaber abbreviated and did not state the general law that hekdesh can uproot a creditor's lien, because intrinsic Temple sanctity does not practically apply nowadays. The Tur explains: if the borrower consecrated property with intrinsic sanctity, such as an ox among movables encumbered through agav and dedicated it for the altar, the creditor's lien is displaced. But monetary sanctity does not uproot the lien; the creditor redeems it for a nominal amount. Rama's gloss states the same distinction.

Once it was spread over the dead person and designated for him, it has the status of something connected to the dead and becomes prohibited in benefit, as explained in Yoreh Deah siman 349. Mere designation without actual use does not prohibit it, in accordance with Rava's rule concerning a tefillin case: designation without use, or use without designation, does not create sanctity.
Some disagree. They hold that even if the borrower prohibited the property only to this creditor, the prohibition takes effect. Nevertheless, the borrower is placed under a ban until he seeks release from his vow.
