A prohibition of benefit removes property from an existing lien, just as bodily sanctity does. Thus, in the case of the cloak that was spread over a corpse with the intention that it be buried with him, a creditor cannot collect from it. Rama: Sanctity that attaches only to monetary value does not remove an existing lien. Rather, the creditor redeems it for a small amount, so that people should not say that consecrated property leaves its sacred status without redemption; he then adds the redemption money to the debt and collects it from the debtor. Tur, seif 7. Even bodily sanctity cannot be imposed solely against the creditor by saying that the debtor's property is forbidden to him by konam, unless he forbids it to the entire world. Mordechai, chapters HaMadir and Get Pashut, in the name of Maharam. Others disagree. Ran, chapter Af Al Pi, and Nimukei Yosef, chapter Almanah, present both views.
