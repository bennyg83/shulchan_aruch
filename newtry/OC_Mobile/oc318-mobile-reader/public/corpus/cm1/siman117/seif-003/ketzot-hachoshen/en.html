If a debtor made his ox an express apotiki and sold it, the creditor does not collect from the purchaser. Rashba extends this to a gift recipient. Tosafot in Bava Kamma, however, expressly distinguishes: sale is protected because the lack of publicity would unfairly harm purchasers, but a donee receives property without paying value and cannot be preferred to the prior creditor. Tosafot therefore says that if the ox was given as a gift, the creditor can collect it. There is an apparent contradiction from Tosafot elsewhere, which suggests that even heirs are not subject to collection from an ox made an apotiki. If heirs are protected, certainly a donee should be protected. The two Tosafot passages therefore require reconciliation. The practical rule remains disputed: Rashba treats gift like sale and protects the donee, while Tosafot in the robbery sugya gives the creditor stronger rights against a donee.
