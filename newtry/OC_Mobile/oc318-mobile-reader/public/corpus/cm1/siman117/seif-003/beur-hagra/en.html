Even if he knew, etc. See Baal HaTerumot, who derives this from the rule that a creditor does not collect movable property from orphans because he did not rely upon it, even though with orphans one cannot say that they were unaware of the lien. There is a further proof: even an apotiki is sometimes not collected from orphans. See Tosafot, Bava Kamma 11b, who asks why the creditor should now collect, even though he does not collect from orphans. If knowledge in a case without publicity were sufficient to permit collection from purchasers, then certainly collection should be permitted from orphans. According to Tosafot's explanation of an ox made an apotiki, however, collection is not made even from orphans.
