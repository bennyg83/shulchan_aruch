And even if it was lost. There. So too it appears from Ketubot 16b, "ultimately, etc., in fire," which necessarily concerns witnesses. We learn that even if it was lost in the presence of witnesses, no replacement is written. Rashi there says so explicitly.
(Collected note) Rashbam, and even if it was lost in the presence of witnesses, etc. Responsa of the Rosh disagrees with Rashbam, Bava Batra there, s.v. "Avad, etc." He writes that although the phrase "one who comes, etc." implies specifically without witnesses, it may refer to loss in a fire, as at the beginning of chapter 2 of Ketubot. See Rashbam there, s.v. "beShtarei, etc.," "or alternatively, etc.," which at first glance implies Rashbam himself retracted. See Maharsha, who explains it as specifically without witnesses. (End of collected note.)
