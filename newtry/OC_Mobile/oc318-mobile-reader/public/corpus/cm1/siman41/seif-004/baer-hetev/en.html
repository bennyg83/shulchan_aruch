The Shach explains that the Raavad's objection is this: since the witnesses are present and say that the claim is still within the due period, set the lost document aside. It should be no worse than an oral loan made before witnesses, where the debtor is not believed to say that he paid within the due period, as all the authorities rule in siman 78. One must answer that although without a document a debtor is not believed to claim payment within the due period, here the case is worse because the witnesses originally made a document. The loan was therefore fundamentally transacted on the strength of that instrument, and once the document fell and was lost, the loan itself was impaired. Consequently, even if the witnesses are still present, the debtor is believed to say that he paid. A comparable rule appears in siman 39, seif 5. This explanation is not compelled by the Gemara and authorities, however, and the practical law requires study. The possessor may perhaps say, "I rely on the Raavad." Shach.
