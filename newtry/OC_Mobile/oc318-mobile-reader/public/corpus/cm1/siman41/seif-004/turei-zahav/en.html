(There: and this is known through witnesses.) It appears to me that a certified copy still has practical value. If the original is later lost without witnesses, the creditor can sue the borrower orally. If the borrower says, "I repaid," the original document had existed, and the creditor can obtain from the beit din in the borrower's city a written and signed record of the borrower's claim. All the more so if the borrower says, "There never was such a debt." One cannot simply tell the creditor to take the original with him and leave the copy here, because we fear that the original document may be lost on the road without witnesses. The borrower could then say, "I repaid you and you returned the original document to me," and the creditor would no longer be able to sue later on the strength of the copy.
