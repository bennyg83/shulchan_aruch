And some say that even the unrelated women are not betrothed. Although we rule that one who says, "Acquire, you and the donkey," effects acquisition of half for the party capable of acquiring, a distinction can be drawn between a formulation naming the parties separately, "you and a donkey," and the inclusive formulation "all of you," which joins everyone in one statement. According to this, if he says, "The sisters and the unrelated women are betrothed to me," the unrelated women are definitely betrothed. The Ran, however, gives a different distinction. In the case of "you and the donkey," there is no reason to say that because one party lacks capacity to acquire, the competent party should also fail. But a purchaser commonly seeks to acquire good and inferior property together, and his intent here is to acquire them all. If he cannot acquire the sisters, perhaps even the unrelated women are not betrothed, although he separately identified them.
