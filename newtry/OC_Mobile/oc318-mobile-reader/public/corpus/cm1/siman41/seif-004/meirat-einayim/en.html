And therefore he tore the document. Beit Yosef, seif 3, cites Ritva that this applies specifically to a loan document, where payment is a concern. With a gift document stating that a gift will take effect at a future time, if it is torn before that time the witnesses may testify that the document was torn and beit din may prepare a judicial record. There is no concern for payment or waiver, because even if the donor had physically taken back the document the gift itself would not thereby be canceled.
The borrower is not believed, etc. Although the custodian here is not literally a formal third-party stakeholder, since only the lender deposited the document with him, he is nevertheless believed through a migo that he could have privately returned it to the lender.
