(Collected note) One whose document became worn, etc. This is the Rambam's language, and the Rosh and Tur write similarly in the name of Ri. Rashbam explains that it was completely erased, as written in the gloss. There is no practical difference once it was brought to beit din, for according to both explanations it is already erased. The difference is when he first shows it to witnesses. Then, according to the first explanation, some trace must remain so they know what was written. According to Rashbam, he appoints witnesses who had previously read it. This is the meaning of "after, etc." (End of collected note.)
And this testimony. Bava Kamma 112b.
(Collected note) And this testimony, etc. From the statement "they validate the document," implying even through the original witnesses, following the Sages who say they testify concerning the maneh in the document. All the more so here, where they testify only regarding the writing. If they testified regarding the debt itself, it would be hearsay. The Mishnah and Gemara there likewise say "they make validation for him," implying this is merely ordinary validation. (End of collected note.)
(Collected note) And this testimony that they testify, etc. The rule that testimony is not accepted outside the litigant's presence applies only in a case like Rabbi Yirmiyah's, where one comes to remove property. Here the purchaser is in possession of his purchase and no challenger is present, so we do not presume an unknown challenger. In the case of a lost promissory note, acceptance of testimony in the litigant's presence is not mentioned. On the contrary, the passage implies it is unnecessary, since on 170a Rav Safra says, "because there is no, etc.; perhaps, etc." If the seller were present and made no claim, why should we ourselves be concerned? So throughout the sugya. Torat HaBayit, siman 362. (End of collected note.)

(Collected note) And if they wrote, "and we examined, etc." Meaning, they examined the testimony of those who testified concerning the erasure. There is no need to specify that they examined every aspect of their testimony, but only their testimony concerning the original document's witnesses. This is the meaning of "testimony of testimony." (End of collected note.)
(Collected note) And if they did not, etc. Meaning, the original document's witnesses must be authenticated, either by witnesses who saw their signatures on the original document and recognize them, or by the original witnesses themselves testifying to their signatures. They need not testify anew to the entire document, unlike the Sema. This is what the Maggid Mishneh and the Rav mean by "and if they are not, etc.": the novelty is that only validation of the signatures is required. Baal HaTerumot writes this explicitly in the name of Ri Migash, unlike Rashbam. The Tur's words incline to the Ra'avad's explanation that "and we examined, etc." refers to the testimony of the witnesses to the erasure concerning the entire document. The Ra'avad was compelled to explain this because he understands that the original signatories are still present before beit din. (End of collected note.)
