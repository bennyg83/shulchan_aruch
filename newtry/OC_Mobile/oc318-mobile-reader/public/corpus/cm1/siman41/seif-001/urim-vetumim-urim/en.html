He presents witnesses concerning it, etc. These may be the witnesses who were originally signed on the document or other witnesses who saw it become erased. Sema.
Some say even if it was completely erased. Sema writes that there is no real dispute here and that all agree with this law, differing only in their interpretation of the Mishnah. See Tumim, where I explained that this is in fact a major dispute among the poskim. According to the first view, testimony is effective only if some trace of the writing remains visible, or if no trace remains but the document had already been formally authenticated. In the latter case it has the force of an act of beit din and is not testimony from one witness's report to another. Certainly where traces remain it is a matter readily subject to verification. But if the document was entirely erased and had not been authenticated, later testimony about it is ineffective because it is testimony based upon other testimony. The Rama holds that the witnesses may testify in every case. The Tur rules likewise, and the Rama also states this below in siman 386 regarding a burned document.
And this testimony, etc. The Shach wished to limit this to a case where some trace remains, but not where the writing was entirely erased, because one may not impose liability upon a person in his absence. See Tumim, where I proved from the Rosh and Gemara that the testimony is accepted in every case. It is comparable to witnesses who are urgently leaving for overseas, whose testimony may be accepted in the other party's absence. This case is the same.
But the witnesses to the document, etc. Sema writes that the Tur says this because witnesses who have completed their agency cannot perform it again. See Tumim, where I explain that this does not fit the Tur's own view. According to the Tur, the original witnesses may write a replacement document even without a new instruction from the borrower, provided it is dated from the later date, and where there was a kinyan they may even use the original date. The Mechaber, however, follows Rambam: the witnesses may not write any replacement document, even with a later date, without the borrower's instruction. If the borrower instructs them, they may write with the later date, but not with the original date because that would be antedated. Their original agency ended once they had written the first document, so they cannot use its date again even where a kinyan had occurred. If the document was erased in Nisan and the borrower instructs them in Iyar to write another, or if he instructed them in Nisan but they delayed until Iyar, they must date it in Iyar. Otherwise it is antedated. If, however, he instructs them and a new kinyan is made from him, they may date the replacement from the date of that second kinyan, but not from the date of the erasure. This is obvious.
They do not write, and if they erred, etc. See below in siman 49, where this is explained. Shach writes in seif katan 3 that if only one witness signed a loan or sale document and it was then erased or lost, the witnesses may write another document because their agency was not yet completed, since the second witness had not yet signed. The replacement must nevertheless bear the later date, or there must have been a kinyan, for otherwise it would be antedated. This is obvious.
And its date was on such and such a day, etc. The reason is that the creditor collects from purchasers from that original date. Beit din has authority to create a replacement document that preserves the right to seize from the first date.
And so and so were its witnesses. See Sema, who notes that the Tur also says to identify the witnesses who testified about the erasure, because the borrower may later bring proof disqualifying them. Sema adds that Mordechai writes that they need not be identified. I am surprised by this, because Mordechai merely reproduces Tosafos in Bava Batra 168b, s.v. "so and so," word for word, and Tosafos leave the matter unresolved. Their doubt cannot displace the definite ruling of the other authorities.
He must bring proof. See Tumim. Sema holds that if the document's original witnesses testify concerning the erasure, they require searching examination to verify that their accounts correspond, as explained in siman 28. If other witnesses say that they recognize the original signatures, they too must be examined concerning all details of the document. But if the replacement states, "we investigated," none of this is required, because we presume beit din acted properly and carefully examined everything necessary. I wrote that where the original document witnesses themselves testify concerning the erasure, they must in any event remember the substance of the entire testimony and testify to the debt recorded in the document, as explained above. No separate searching examination is needed, because their substantive testimony is already required. Where other witnesses testify concerning the erasure and say that the signatures were those of the original witnesses, the original witnesses must still testify that they wrote a document for the stated amount, even though they know nothing about the erasure. By combining these two groups, the debt has the status of a written loan. This is the view of Rashbam, Tosafos in Bava Batra, and Rambam. Many other authorities, including Tosafos in Bava Kamma and the Tur, hold that testimony by other witnesses authenticating the document suffices. One need only clarify that the witnesses did not merely see an ambiguous expression in the document and interpret it according to their own understanding. See Tumim, where I explained this thoroughly. This is the meaning of the phrase "we investigated," namely that beit din established the matter after proper inquiry.
Otherwise it is invalid, etc. Meaning that this replacement document is invalid if it does not state that the original document was torn up. One cannot rely upon the presumption that beit din acted properly. Although we generally do not suspect beit din of error, we do remain concerned about forgetfulness. Sema writes this correctly. See Tumim, where I rejected those who explain that the phrase means only that if the original was actually not torn up the replacement is invalid, and therefore beit din should initially record that it was torn. According to them, omission of that notation would not invalidate the replacement because we could presume beit din did in fact tear the original. That explanation is incorrect, as I wrote in Tumim. See there for my reconciliation of the Bach and other doubts in this law. It is possible that according to Rambam and Rashbam, if the original witnesses testify that no other witnesses were present at the loan, the original document need not be torn. See there.
But a gift document, etc. A replacement may be written even by the original witnesses themselves. So it appears from the Gemara. Shach. One should not ask, according to Ramban's explanation that witnesses who completed their agency may perform it again, that the problem should be testimony from writing because the donor never told them to write a second document. In a gift as well, the donor did not explicitly tell them to write again. Rather, since no adverse consequence is possible, we assume that when the donor originally said, "Write," he intended to authorize even ten documents. See Tumim.
And not from the date on which they now write, etc. The reason is that after the gift was made in Nisan, the donor may have repurchased the property from the recipient in Iyar. If beit din now writes a replacement dated Tishrei, the recipient could later claim, "I bought it from you again," relying on the later date. See below in siman 239. Even if the document states that it may not be used for collection and similar limitations, some authorities nevertheless hold that it should not be written with the later date. See there. If the donor himself instructs them to write, there is no concern and they may do so. Shach. This is obvious, since he can simply order an entirely new gift document.
In a gift with warranty. A gift requires an express warranty clause. In an ordinary sale, warranty is treated as though expressly written because we rule that omission of warranty in a sale is a scribal error.
The first document must be torn. The reason is that if the recipient holds two documents and the field is later discovered to have been stolen, the true owner may recover it. The recipient could then use one document to collect from the donor's unencumbered or encumbered property. After collection, beit din would tear that document. He could then collude with the true owner, return the field to his possession, allow the owner to bring witnesses of theft again and recover it, and then use the second document to collect a second time. Even if they write the donor a receipt stating that no other documents are valid except one, the recipient might collect from purchasers, against whom the donor's private receipt is ineffective. Gemara.
And not from unencumbered property. For perhaps he will collect from unencumbered property with this replacement and then use the original valid document, which records that the first document was torn, to collect from purchasers. A receipt is ineffective against purchasers, as explained above. Sema and Gemara.
