(Shulchan Aruch, seif 1: “and we examined the testimony of the witnesses.”) I have not been satisfied by the explanations I have seen. The Sema says this refers to witnesses who authenticate the signatures, but the Tur cites Rabbenu Yonah that the witnesses to the fading document had already recognized the signatures, and the beit din had already recorded that fact. Why would a second authentication be needed? The Tur also writes that the court examined the words of those who testified that the document had been erased. Others explain that the Tur follows the Raavad, cited by the Beit Yosef, that the signatures remain visible. This too is incorrect, for the Tur says the witnesses saw in a person’s possession a document beginning to fade and recognized the signatures, language indicating prior recognition. Rashbam, cited by the Beit Yosef, likewise says that named witnesses “had been signed on it,” implying no signatures now remain before the court. The proper explanation is that according to both Rabbenu Yonah and Rashbam, the document now brought before the beit din is completely erased. Rabbenu Yonah requires that when witnesses were first appointed over it, it had not yet been completely erased; Rashbam permits even complete erasure at that earlier stage. The Tur then teaches from the baraita that if the court examined the actual signatories to the erased document and they confirmed their signatures, no further proof is required. Mere witnesses to the erasure are insufficient to create a document collectible from encumbered property; actual loan witnesses are needed. In an ordinary unverified document, authentication makes it as though the signatories stand before us. Here the document is entirely gone, while the signatories alone are insufficient because perhaps it was paid. Thus if the substitute document does not state that the court examined the testimony, additional evidence is needed that everything in the original was true. Those witnesses stand in place of the original signatories. The Tur therefore says, “If the beit din wrote that they examined, etc.” The Rambam, however, understands “we examined the testimony” as referring to witnesses to the erasure, serving as authentication. The Rama’s conclusion that without this phrase the replacement has the status of an unverified document teaches that this is the very purpose of the wording.
