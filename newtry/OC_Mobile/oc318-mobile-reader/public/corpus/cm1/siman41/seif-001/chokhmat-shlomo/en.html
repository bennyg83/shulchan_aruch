Seif 1. “And we required the testimony of witnesses, and it was found to be consistent.” See Sema's explanation. According to his words, where the original document was burned the witnesses must likewise know the underlying testimony, and otherwise collection cannot be made, since the document itself is no longer before us. This is difficult from Bava Kamma, chapter HaGozel Kama. The Gemara says that one who burns another person's promissory note brings us to the dispute between Rabbi Shimon and the Sages. It asks: What is the case? If witnesses know what was written in it, let them write him another document. If there are no witnesses, how do we know what was written in it? According to the Sema, why not establish the case where witnesses know the contents and recognize the document but do not know whether the underlying loan was true? They could not write a replacement document because they do not know the substance of the testimony. Yet the person who burned the document clearly caused a loss, because had the original remained in existence the creditor could have collected with it, just as Sema says that when the document itself is before us collection may be made even though the witnesses who authenticate it do not know the underlying transaction. Why, then, must the Gemara resort to the strained case in which the debtor trusts the creditor? It is difficult to distinguish between a burned document and an erased one. This presently requires substantial study.
