He appoints witnesses over it. Meaning, either the witnesses signed on the original document or other witnesses, as will be explained.
And he comes to beit din, etc. This refers to a case where the document has already been erased. This is why the Mechaber later writes, 'So-and-so produced before us an erased document.' Otherwise, why would witnesses be needed, since the beit din itself could inspect the document while it was still legible?
Some say even if it was completely erased. In Perishah I explained that the words of the Rosh and Tur imply that Ri agrees to this as well, and that they merely speak in the usual manner of a document that is in the process of being erased, because where it has been completely erased it is uncommon to find witnesses who know what had been written in it. The same explanation can be given for the Mechaber's unqualified wording below in seif 2, 'one whose promissory note was erased appoints witnesses,' etc. Yet one could say that the Mechaber simply adopted the wording of the Gemara and Tur, while Ri comments on the Gemara's phrase 'was erased' that it is not literal but means its traces are still recognizable. Therefore Rama writes that some say even if it was completely erased. See what I write further at the beginning of seif 2.
But the witnesses to the document themselves, etc. Meaning, even if the original witnesses are before us and recognize their signatures and the document that was erased, they themselves do not write a replacement. Rather, they come before a beit din. This is why the text repeats, 'they come before a beit din': at first it was speaking of other witnesses.
They do not write for him. The reason is that they have already performed their agency. For the same reason, the Rosh and Tur, cited by the Mechaber in siman 49, seif 6, write that if the witnesses erred in the first writing and the document was invalidated, since they have not yet performed their agency they may return and write and sign it again.
But they come before a beit din, etc. Because property may be rendered ownerless by beit din.
And what it says, 'and So-and-so and So-and-so were its witnesses.' This means the witnesses whose signatures appeared on the original document. The Tur writes that one must also mention in the certification the witnesses who testified before them regarding what had been written in the erased document. The reason is so that the borrower can challenge and disqualify them if they testified falsely. So write Tosafot and Rambam. The Mechaber recorded only what absolutely must be written in the certification, namely the original document witnesses, whereas the witnesses to the erasure need not be listed when the original witnesses themselves appear before the beit din, for then they are both the document witnesses and the witnesses to the erasure. Mordechai in chapter Get Pashut, however, rules that one need not write who the witnesses to the erasure were. See Darkei Moshe at the beginning of this siman and Perishah.
And we examined the testimony of the witnesses, etc. Meaning, whether the original witnesses themselves testified to their signatures or other witnesses testified to them, each according to the case.
He must bring proof, etc. Nevertheless, the first certification is still needed for this purpose: after he brings proof concerning them, he may collect even from encumbered property and the borrower cannot say, 'I paid.' By contrast, if all he had was the later proof, namely that the loan or sale occurred before witnesses and that they wrote and signed a document, the borrower could later say, 'I paid,' because the creditor no longer has the document, as the Tur and Mechaber write below in seif 4. All the more so he could not collect from encumbered property.
Regarding the first witnesses, until their testimony is established. This exact wording also appears in Rambam, chapter 23 of Hilchot Malveh, halacha 12. Note carefully that they did not write, 'until their signatures are authenticated,' but 'until their testimony is established.' This means that the entire substance of the testimony written in the original document must be established. Even if the original witnesses themselves come and say that they signed this document and it contained such-and-such, although ordinarily with a document it is enough for them to say, 'This is our signature,' as stated in siman 46, here the document itself has disappeared and this is like new oral testimony before beit din. The beit din must question each witness separately and determine whether their accounts correspond, as the Tur writes in siman 28 from seif 11 onward. Therefore the Mechaber writes that if the certification does not state that they investigated and established their words, the certification is treated as though nonexistent until the creditor brings proof regarding the first witnesses, meaning proof that the testimony attributed to those first witnesses is true, and their testimony is established as describing the event accurately. The certification then enables collection from encumbered property and prevents a claim of repayment, as explained. All the more so when the certification was made through other witnesses: even if they say they recognize the original signatures and saw what was written in the document, inquiry is still required regarding everything written there, because the original document is no longer before us. This differs from ordinary document authentication, where recognition of the witnesses' signatures suffices, because when the document itself is before us and the signatures are authenticated, every document presented to beit din is treated as though its testimony had been investigated. The Tur likewise writes explicitly that if the certification does not say that they examined the testimony of the witnesses, proof must be brought concerning what was written in the document. This is also the meaning of Baal HaTerumot, who writes that if they did not record that they examined the witnesses' testimony, etc., the lender must bring proof that the first witnesses' testimony was true and that they signed it. Meaning, proof is required both of the signature and of the substance of the testimony, and then it has the status of a complete document.
For we are concerned lest he go, etc. Meaning, he might appoint two separate sets of witnesses over the document, each knowing what was written in it, and go with each set before a different beit din, causing two replacement documents to be written for him. This concern is removed when the original document must be torn before a replacement is written, because another beit din will not write another certification when he cannot show them the original document. They must write in the new document that they tore the first one. Although we do not suspect a beit din of error, we do suspect forgetfulness: they might intend to tear it and forget to do so. But they certainly will not write that they tore it until it has actually been torn.
But a deed of gift, etc. There is no concern that he will collect twice with it, since the gift concerns a specific field, unless the deed includes a warranty, as the conclusion states.
From the original date, etc. The reason is that we are concerned that after receiving the gift the recipient later sold or gave the property back to the donor through a valid kinyan, and afterward he will produce this replacement document and claim that the donor gave it to him again later. The Tur and Mechaber explicitly give this reason below in siman 239. I also discussed this at length here in Derishah, with God's help.
A gift with warranty, and likewise deeds of sale, etc. In Derishah I cited the Gemara's wording: we are concerned that this field may actually have been stolen in the hands of the seller or donor, and that the recipient or purchaser will collude with the true owner to collect twice. How? First the true owner comes with witnesses proving ancestral title and removes the field from the purchaser. The purchaser then uses the purchase document in his hand to collect from purchasers who bought from the seller afterward, or from unencumbered property still held by the seller. Then the purchaser tells the true owner, 'Let me remain on this field for some time, and afterward come again and remove me through your proof of ancestral title.' The purchaser will then collect a second time from the purchasers or seller using the replacement document in his hand.
This document was not written for collection, etc. Such a notation cannot be used in a loan document, because a loan document is written precisely for collection. These documents, however, are written also in order to establish the field in his possession.
Nor from unencumbered property. This too must be written, so that he not collect with the replacement document from unencumbered property of the seller and afterward return and collect with the original instrument from purchasers, for purchasers do not possess the receipt.
