And this testimony that they give, etc. The Rama's words are astonishing. How could one entertain accepting testimony outside the litigant's presence in order to impose liability upon him? If the document has not been entirely erased and its traces remain visible to the beit din, while the witnesses testify only to the signatures, they may testify outside the litigant's presence, just as a document may be authenticated outside his presence under siman 46. But if the document was wholly erased and the witnesses testify to what it contained, it is inconceivable to accept such testimony outside the debtor's presence. If they say it recorded a large sum, shall we obligate him solely on testimony received in his absence? The Rashba's responsum cited by the Beit Yosef proves the opposite. He permits testimony outside the opposing party's presence concerning a deed of sale because the purchaser is already in possession of the land and we do not presume an unknown challenger. This accords with siman 110, or with the rule that testimony may be accepted in absence to exempt rather than impose liability. It does not permit witnesses to reconstruct a debt instrument that will remove money from the debtor. When the Rashba says that the sugya of one who lost his document proves that the opposing party need not be present, he is discussing a deed of sale and means that the sugya shows that for a deed of sale the opposing party's presence is unnecessary. Tosafot's statement in Get Pashut that an erased document may be rewritten outside the borrower's presence, despite the resulting harm, means that the testimony was first received in the borrower's presence and only the writing is done later without him. Likewise, Tosafot require the replacement to identify the witnesses who testified concerning the erased document so that the borrower can later disqualify them. Maharik's ruling that even if the borrower protests the court disregards him likewise concerns the writing after testimony was properly received, or a case where the writing remains partly visible and testimony relates only to signatures. The Beit Yosef cites the Rashba in siman 46 that a document may be authenticated without the opposing party because the authenticating witnesses do not testify to the debt recorded in it, but only to the signatures. Here, by contrast, the witnesses testify directly to the sum written in the document. Their testimony may not be accepted outside the debtor's presence. The Rama's ruling therefore requires study.
But the witnesses to the document themselves, etc. Certainly the same applies to other witnesses. The Tur says that witnesses acting without a beit din may not create a replacement document, even dated from the time of erasure. He means without the borrower's consent. With the borrower's consent they may write a new document dated from the present, as the Nimukei Yosef says and as appears from the Tur at the beginning of siman 54. If time elapsed between the erasure and the borrower's instruction, they may not date the new document from the time of erasure even with his consent. The Nimukei Yosef writes that if a debt document or deed of sale with warranty was signed by only one of the witnesses and was then lost or erased, both witnesses may return and write and sign it. Until both have completed the deed, even the first witness has not completed his agency. Rabbi Yitzchak HaLevi says the same in Torat HaBayit. The Beit Yosef and Darkei Moshe cite this briefly in siman 49.
They do not write another document for him. The Sema writes that for this reason the Rosh and Tur rule in siman 49 that when the witnesses erred in their first writing and the deed was invalidated, they had not completed their agency and may write and sign again. The Rashba goes further in responsum 951: if the document omitted an express clause of trust regarding payment, they tear it and write another containing the clause, because the witnesses had not fulfilled their agency. The Beit Yosef and Darkei Moshe cite this in siman 71. A similar ruling appears in the Rama's responsum 49. Once the witnesses have completed their original agency, however, they were authorized only to make that first document. Even if the creditor later instructs them to write another document for the unpaid balance after partial payment, they may not date it from the original date. Their first agency has ended, and the creditor cannot now empower them retroactively to create a prior lien that is no longer within his control. Certainly they may not create another document without his consent, even dated from the present. So writes the Nimukei Yosef. See the lengthy discussion in the responsum of Mahara ibn Sasson, no. 66.

For it must state that they tore it, etc., for otherwise it is invalid. The Sema explains that although we ordinarily do not suspect a beit din of error, we do fear forgetfulness: they may intend to tear the original and forget, leaving it in the creditor's hand. By requiring the replacement to state that the original was torn, they will not write the statement until the tearing has actually occurred. Maharam di Modena suggests that "otherwise it is invalid" may refer not to omission of the recital, but to failure to tear the original. If we know it was not torn, the replacement is invalid. The recital is required to remove doubt, but absent contrary evidence we do not presume that the beit din erred. See there and below, seif katan 12.
But a deed of gift, etc. Even the original witnesses may write another. The Beit Yosef cites the Rashba that they need not testify before a beit din; they themselves may write a replacement dated from the original date. He cites the same in siman 239. The Gemara in Get Pashut states expressly that witnesses themselves may write multiple deeds of gift, or deeds of sale without warranty, for the same field, dated from the first date, even after loss. See the Gemara and Tosafot there.
