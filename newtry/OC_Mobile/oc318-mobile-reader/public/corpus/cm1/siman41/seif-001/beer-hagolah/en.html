The language of the Rambam, chapter 26 of Hilkhot Malveh VeLoveh, from the Mishnah, Bava Batra 168a, 'One whose promissory note was erased,' etc. According to his explanation, the document is close to being erased and he fears it may be erased entirely. He therefore establishes witnesses over it so they will see it before it disappears, and afterward he comes before beit din with the witnesses who saw it or with the document itself if its imprint remains recognizable. So explained his teacher and most commentators. This is also evident from the Tosefta. The Tur writes likewise in the name of the Ri, as do the Rosh and Rabbenu Yerucham, Netiv 4 part 4.
So it appears in the Tur. The Beit Yosef writes that they disagree only in explaining the Mishnah, not in the substantive law.
From a baraita there, 31b.
Tosafot there write that the names of the witnesses who testified about the erasure of the document must also be mentioned, so that if the lender wishes he can disqualify them. The Rosh writes that perhaps the Mishnah did not mention them because all the witnesses needed for the matter are included in the phrase, 'So-and-so and so-and-so are its witnesses.'
Meaning, the witnesses to the document themselves appeared before them, were examined, and their testimony was found properly directed, or the document had been validated.
