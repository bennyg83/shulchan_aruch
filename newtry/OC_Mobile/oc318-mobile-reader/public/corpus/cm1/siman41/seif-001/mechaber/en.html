The Law of One Whose Debt Document Was Erased or Lost. Four seifim. If a person's debt document has worn out and is about to become illegible, he has witnesses inspect it and comes to beit din, which creates an authenticated replacement. {Rama: Some say this may be done even if it was completely erased, provided the witnesses know what it contained. Testimony about the document may be given outside the opposing party's presence.} The original signatory witnesses may not write a new document even if the first was erased before them; they instead come to beit din, which creates an authenticated replacement. How is this done? They write another document stating: 'We, the undersigned beit din, were presented by so-and-so son of so-and-so with an erased document dated on a particular day, whose witnesses were so-and-so and so-and-so.' If they add, 'We examined the witnesses' testimony and found it accurate,' the creditor collects with this replacement and no further authentication is needed. If they do not write this, proof concerning the original witnesses is required until their testimony is authenticated. {Rama: If the judges do not authenticate it, it is as though an unauthenticated document were produced.} Some say the authentication must state that the first document was torn up; otherwise it is invalid, lest the creditor go to another beit din, obtain another authenticated replacement, and collect repeatedly. A replacement may be written for an erased gift document even if the first was not torn up, and it is dated with the original date rather than the date of replacement. If the gift included a guarantee, or if it was a sale document, the first must be torn up, or the second must state that it was not written to collect from encumbered or unencumbered property, but only to establish the field in the recipient's or buyer's possession against removal by the donor or seller.
