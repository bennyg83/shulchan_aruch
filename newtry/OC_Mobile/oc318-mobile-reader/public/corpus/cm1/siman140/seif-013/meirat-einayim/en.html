If he lived in the inner room, etc. The Mechaber omitted this ruling because the Rashbam and the Rosh derived it from the incident in chapter Chezkat HaBatim, near the end of Bava Batra 29, where one person asked another, 'What are you doing with this land?' He answered, 'I purchased it from you and have consumed its produce for the years of presumptive possession.' The first responded, 'I was in the inner neighborhood,' etc. The Rif and Rambam explain 'the inner neighborhood' differently, as the Tur and Mechaber write below in siman 146:11. According to them, the seller answered: How can you say that you purchased it from me, when at that time I was in the inner neighborhood, meaning in a distant country? Rama records both laws according to the two interpretations, one here and the other in siman 146. See there and what I write further on this matter.
He would pass through the outer house. Meaning that he also used the outer house together with the possessor, and says: You lived there with my permission, and that is why I did not protest. The Rashbam's language implies that he claims both that he passed through it and that he used it together with him. The Mordechai, in the name of Maharam, holds that even if he claims, 'I used the outer house together with you,' this is insufficient to defeat the presumption unless the challenger claims that whenever he wished he could use the entire house, and that only now was he pushed aside because of the possessor, as Rama concludes. From the language of the Rosh and Nimukei Yosef, however, it is clear that they understood the Rashbam's explanation to mean that merely passing through the outer house constitutes use together with him. This is also evident from the Tur and from Rama in Darkei Moshe. See there.
