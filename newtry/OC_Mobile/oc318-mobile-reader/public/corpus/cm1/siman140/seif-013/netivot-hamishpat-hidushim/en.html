Even if the two did not both live there. We rule like Rabbi Yehoshua ben Korcha that separate monetary witnesses may join, even regarding land. Thus, if one tenant lived there in years one, three, and five and another in years two, four, and six, even where they rented without a written lease it can establish chazakah because each lived there for three years and their testimony combines as though they lived there together. This is subject to the Raavad's contrary view cited above. But according to the first opinion, where even six separate tenants may combine, a written lease is required. Sema and Shach.
That he passed through. The language implies that merely passing through is enough to defeat the chazakah. Rashbam's language suggests that the challenger must claim that he also used the property jointly. The dissent cited by Rama holds that he must claim specifically that the possessor had to move aside for him while the challenger was using it. The Taz asks that ordinarily he surely did not pass through every room, so why should there be no chazakah in rooms through which he did not pass, unlike the specific area in seif 15? See Beurim: there the claimant asserts a purchase only of the specified place. Here he claims to have bought the entire house, and use of part of it functions as protest against the whole, because protest against part is protest against all. See Beurim further: where a house is known to belong to Reuven and afterward Shimon lived there with him for three uninterrupted years and claims that he bought a partnership share, this does not create chazakah. Since they used it together, whenever one used a particular place the other was not using it, and the possessor's use was not continuous. This is even stronger than merely passing through. It differs from cases of gutters and fixed drainage rights, because there a permanent fixture itself can establish chazakah.
