Land known to belong to Reuven. See Sema, seif katan 1, who adds that the same applies where there is a well established public reputation. Darkei Moshe uses this distinction to reconcile a responsum of the Rosh with a responsum of Rashba, explaining that Rashba deals with an uncertain reputation while the Rosh deals with a firmly established one. The Rosh's responsum indeed concerns an exceptionally strong public reputation, comparing it to imposing capital punishment based on established status, meaning that the entire community regarded the property as his. A mere rumor is insufficient, as Rashba proves from the case of Rabbah bar Sharshom. Sema, seif katan 2, cites Rashba that when the possessor makes a claim against orphans he must take an oath while holding a sacred object. He omits that Rashba was speaking where the possessor claimed, "It is held by me as collateral." If he claims purchase, he takes only a heset oath.
