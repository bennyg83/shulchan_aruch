Land known through witnesses to belong to Reuven, etc. The Sema in seif katan 1 cites a responsum of the Rosh that even a well established public reputation that the land was his is sufficient. The Beit Yosef, in his novellae, cites a responsum of the Rashba indicating that we do not remove land merely on the basis of public reputation, and Darkei Moshe cites the same. In truth, the Rosh's responsum proves no such general rule. There it was already definitely known that the land had belonged to Reuven and that he sold it; the only uncertainty was when the sale occurred, before or after he borrowed through a document. The public reputation established only that he continued to own it until after the documented loan. In that setting the Rosh relied on public reputation to determine the timing of a known transfer. But where it is not otherwise known that the land ever belonged to him and the entire ownership claim rests only on reputation, we do not rely upon it. The Rashba himself makes this distinction in the cited responsum. The Sema's broad ruling therefore requires study.
