Land known to belong to Reuven through witnesses who know this. The Rashba likewise writes in a responsum that even if the witnesses do not know with absolute certainty that the land belonged to Reuven, but merely saw Reuven or his father exercising dominion over it, we presume it is his and remove it from the second possessor until that possessor establishes three years of possession or brings witnesses to an acquisition. The same applies if there is a well established public reputation that it belongs to him, as the Rosh writes in responsum, klal 99, siman 8. Darkei Moshe cites this. See also below siman 146.
He takes an oath of hesset and remains with his property. The Rashba writes that if the possessor claims against orphans that he acquired property belonging to their father, so that he must take an oath while holding a sacred object, and the possessor then dies before taking the oath, we do not apply the rule that a person cannot bequeath to his children a monetary claim dependent upon an oath. Here the oath is required only in order to retain property already in his possession. See Hagahot Darkei Moshe, where I discussed this.
