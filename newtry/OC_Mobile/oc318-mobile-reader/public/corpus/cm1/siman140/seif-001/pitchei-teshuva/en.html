See Sema, who cites the Rashba as follows: if a possessor claims against orphans that he established possession in property of their father, he must take an oath while holding a sacred object. If the possessor died before taking that oath, we do not apply the rule that a person cannot bequeath an oath to his children, because here the oath is required in order to retain possession rather than to extract. See above, siman 72, Sema seif katan 50, cited in Baer Heitev there, seif katan 42. Dagul MeRevavah comments on the Sema's statement that the possessor must take an oath while holding a sacred object: see Beit Yosef, who cites this responsum and explains that the possessor claimed the property was held by him as collateral for a specified sum.
