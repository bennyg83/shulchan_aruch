According to Sema's explanation this is difficult, because regarding an oath of hesset there is no distinction between land and movables, and with movables the possessor swears in every case. It appears that the Mechaber understands the passage differently. Witnesses that he purchased the land do not suffice; he would also ordinarily need to swear that his possession was legally complete, since perhaps the former owner protested before witnesses who have died or some other unprovable circumstance defeats the prescriptive possession. The statement that “the one who possessed,” etc., means that he is not given an oath concerning the validity of the possession itself. He certainly must still swear that he purchased it from the claimant.
