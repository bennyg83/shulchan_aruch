Land remains in the possession of... Bava Metzia 110 and 102. The Yerushalmi to Bava Basra 3:4 likewise states that where Reuven eats a field as his own and Shimon later brings witnesses, the field remains in the possession of its established owner, and from then onward the burden of proof is on the claimant.
Land known to belong... unless he possessed it... and if there are no witnesses... These three cases are explained by that Yerushalmi and by Kesubos 17b. See Rif and Rosh at the beginning of the third chapter of Bava Basra and Tosafos Kesubos 15b.
Even one day. Bava Basra 41b implies that evidence that the transferor lived there even one day is enough, for the Gemara requires proof of residence and not a longer period.
And he now... This is the Yerushalmi's language.
He swears a heses oath. As above, by analogy to one who claims, "You owe me one hundred."
Collected note: He swears a heses oath. The Rosh, chapter 3, siman 17, rejects Rashbam's arguments. Rabbinic oaths apply even to land, and a chazakah of land is comparable to possession of movable property for this purpose. Heses therefore applies by Rav Nachman's enactment.
Unless... properly. Meaning a full three-year chazakah.
And with a claim. Bava Basra 41a: every chazakah requires a supporting claim.
He swears heses. As above. Tosafos 33a and the Rosh rule so.
