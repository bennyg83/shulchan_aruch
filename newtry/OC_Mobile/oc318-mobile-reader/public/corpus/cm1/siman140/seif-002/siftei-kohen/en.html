This is another opinion, etc. The principal ruling follows the first opinion, as is evident in many places. However, Hagahot Ashiri in chapter Chezkas HaBatim cites Rabbi Avraham Chadlik that if one purchased land from another and has a deed, and within three years the seller claims, 'You later sold it back to me,' while the buyer remains in possession, the buyer need not take a rabbinic oath of denial. The same applies even without a deed where one claims, 'You sold me land,' although he is not in possession and has no deed, provided the defendant responds, 'This never occurred.' The same appears in Mordechai to chapter Shevuat HaDayanim from the rulings of Rabbi Chizkiyah. This requires study.
