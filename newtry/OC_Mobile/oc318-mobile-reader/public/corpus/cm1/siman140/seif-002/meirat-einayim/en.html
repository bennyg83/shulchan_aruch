This is another and conflicting view, etc. This language is difficult. How could the Mechaber state two contradictory rules one immediately after the other without introducing the second as "some say," as is his normal practice? It appears that the Mechaber, Maharik, resolved the two opinions. Where the challenger has witnesses that the property was his, the possessor must swear in response to those witnesses. That is the case of seif 1. The same applies where the challenger has no witnesses and the possessor has not established the years of possession, for then each side either has some advantage or neither has any advantage, and the possessor swears and retains what is in his hand. But where the possessor has the advantage of three years of possession and the challenger has no corresponding advantage because he has no witnesses, the possessor keeps the property without an oath. This is the case of seif 2. Still, the Mechaber should have explained the distinction explicitly, because the dissenting opinions themselves do not appear to draw it. See the Rosh's language cited in the Perishah, the Maggid Mishneh in chapter 14 of Hilchot To'en, and what I wrote in siman 135. Alternatively, the statement in seif 1 that the possessor swears and keeps the property may not have been intended as a final ruling on the oath at all. Its purpose there was only to teach that if Shimon has possessed properly, the property is not removed from him. At most he may still be subject to an oath according to the view requiring one. Then in seif 2 the Mechaber rules according to the view that no oath is required where his possession itself is sufficient. However, because he initially claimed to have a deed and now cannot authenticate it, at least an oath may be required. Examine this carefully.
