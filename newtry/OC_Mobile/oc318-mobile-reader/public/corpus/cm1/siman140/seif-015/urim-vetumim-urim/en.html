Whether he placed, etc. Through the words, 'if he used it for three years.' The Mechaber abbreviated. He cited the Rambam in chapter 12 of Hilchot To'en, but did not cite the Rambam in chapter 5 of Hilchot Shechenim, where the Rambam distinguishes between using another's courtyard and use by partners. Regarding partners, he makes the law depend on construction of a partition. From the Rambam there and the Maggid Mishneh's explanation, it appears that in another's courtyard, merely erecting a partition does not establish a right unless he then uses the area for three years. The reason is that we assume the owner merely lent him the use for a time. With partners, the reverse applies: partners are particular about a partition, and if one remains silent when the other erects it, his silence shows complete waiver.
And even, etc. The Sema writes that the Rama's unqualified statement, 'even if he had a share, etc., it is a chazakah,' is difficult, because it implies that a partner's chazakah in something partners ordinarily object to requires the same period as chazakah against an unrelated owner. That is not correct according to the Rambam and Ri Migash, who hold that among partners, where the type of use is one they ordinarily object to, silence creates an immediate chazakah, because failure to object shows complete waiver. Perhaps the Rama ruled as he did because the Rivash writes that even among partners there is no immediate chazakah. Since the Mechaber did not expressly state the Rambam's position, the Rama did not introduce his ruling as 'some say.' See Sema; this is straightforward.
