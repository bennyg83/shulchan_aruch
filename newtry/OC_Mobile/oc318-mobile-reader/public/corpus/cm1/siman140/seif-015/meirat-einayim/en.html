Whether he erected a partition there, etc., until if he used it for three years and made a claim, etc. The Mechaber abbreviated and copied the Rambam's words in chapter 12 of Hilchot To'en, but did not copy what the Rambam writes in chapter 5 of Hilchot Shekhenim, law 5, where he distinguishes between another person's courtyard and a jointly owned courtyard. From the Rambam's words there and the Maggid Mishneh's explanation, the general rule according to him and his teacher Ri Migash is as follows. In another person's courtyard there is no distinction between erecting a partition and merely placing the object there. If one maintained the use for three years and comes with a claim, it is presumptive possession even without a partition, as the Rambam writes here in chapter 12 of Hilchot To'en. But if he did not maintain it for three years, there is no presumption even if he erected a partition, as the Rambam writes explicitly in chapter 5 of Hilchot Shekhenim, because we say he erected the partition only temporarily and by way of permission. In a jointly owned courtyard the reverse applies. The matter does not depend at all upon three years, but upon whether a partition was erected. If he erected a partition, the presumption arises immediately, because even a partner objects to such a partition, and if he remained silent we infer that he fully waived his objection. But if no partition was erected, then even after three years and with a claim there is no presumptive possession among partners. See there, where the Maggid Mishneh explains this clearly. Rama writes in his gloss that even if the possessor himself had a share in the courtyard as a partner, etc., there is presumptive possession, but immediately qualifies this as applying only to a matter over which people ordinarily object. The difficulty is that Rama should not have written without qualification, 'even if he had a share,' followed by 'he has presumptive possession,' because this sounds as though a partner's possession in a matter over which people ordinarily object is like the three year possession described above regarding another person's courtyard. That is not so, because according to the Rambam and Ri Migash, with a partner the presumption arises immediately when the matter is one over which people ordinarily object and the partner did not object. It is possible that Rama deliberately wrote without qualification because he himself rules, based on Rivash responsum 248, that even among partners the presumption does not arise immediately. Since the Mechaber had not explicitly stated the Rambam's contrary view immediately before, Rama did not introduce his position as 'some say.' Examine this carefully.
