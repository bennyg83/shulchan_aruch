See Sema, seif katan 22, who concludes that when one partner erects a partition we say that the other partner waives his objection immediately, unlike a stranger, whom an owner does not ordinarily forgive. Although with respect to a stranger's chazakah in matters requiring distancing many poskim also hold that silence effects immediate waiver, the present case differs. A stranger seeks to acquire a right in the body of the land, intending to use it permanently and exclude the owner entirely from that location. Such a claim requires three-year chazakah together with a valid claim of purchase. A partner, however, is discussed where he seeks only the right to place animals or similar items there. Since a shared courtyard is intended for ingress and egress, partners ordinarily object to placing animals there, as Tosafot explain in Bava Batra 57. Silence therefore constitutes immediate waiver, like waiver of servitude rights. But if the partner claims actual purchase of the physical location so that it will permanently belong to him, his law is like that of a stranger and he requires three years and a valid claim. Sema concludes that Rama holds that even a partner requires three years and a valid claim. This does not appear correct. If so, Rama should have cited the rule whether there is a distinction among partners between possession of all or half of the property, as explained in siman 149, seif 2, because that question depends on the two answers of Tosafot there. According to Tosafot's first answer, where partners ordinarily object, chazakah exists only when one enters all or half, according to each view. According to their second answer, which speaks of houses where partners do object, if they are particular then chazakah exists even in circumstances where partners ordinarily would not acquire against each other. Rama's omission of this distinction indicates that he too holds that among partners immediate waiver suffices merely for the right to make the same type of use, such as keeping animals in the jointly owned place, so that the other partner can no longer object to that use. In such a case the rule that partners do not acquire chazakah against one another is irrelevant. However, according to the view that three-year chazakah is required when partners are particular, while also ruling that partners generally are not particular, then even according to Tosafot's second answer one must say that if they are not particular the use is treated as entirely ownerless for this limited purpose and no ownership chazakah arises even in a location where one partner might otherwise acquire, such as all or half the property as described in siman 149. Otherwise, if the Gemara merely meant that where they are particular one partner cannot claim a rotating division after three years because they object to that use, whereas if they are not particular he has the usual law of partners and acquires only as stated in siman 149, the Gemara's question from partners who vowed not to benefit from one another would have no force. Evidently, when they are not particular, the use is treated as freely permitted and creates no ownership chazakah at all.
