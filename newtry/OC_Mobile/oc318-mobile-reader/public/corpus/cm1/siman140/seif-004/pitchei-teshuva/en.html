What constitutes chazakah. See Sema, seif katan 6, cited in Be'er HaGolah, letter 30, that Responsa Maimoniyot at the end of Hilchot Ishut, siman 5, states that three year chazakah is effective only against one who asserts ownership of the land itself. It is not effective against a creditor's lien or a ketubah, because the creditor can say that he remained silent thinking that perhaps he would find unencumbered property from which to collect. Netivot HaMishpat writes that according to this, in our contemporary documents of kinyan sudar that record a kinyan from the wife and then from her husband, the purchaser must preserve his document forever so that the woman cannot later collect her ketubah from the property, because chazakah does not defeat a ketubah lien. See there. See Responsa Shevut Yaakov, part 3, siman 159, concerning an insolvent debtor whose later creditor possessed one of the debtor's synagogue seats for three years. Earlier creditors later sought to seize it. The later creditor claimed that since he had possessed it for three years and the earlier creditors remained silent and failed to protest, they too had waived their liens. The questioner asked the law. Shevut Yaakov answered that Maharam already ruled in Responsa Maimoniyot, cited by the Sema in siman 140, that chazakah does not apply against a debt lien or ketubah. The later authorities copied and ruled this without hesitation. Although one might question the application to a completely insolvent person who owes thousands and has neither field nor vineyard, so that it seems unrealistic for the earlier creditors to have remained silent because they hoped to find unencumbered property, nevertheless the extended responsum of Maharam indicates that chazakah simply does not defeat a debt or ketubah lien. Shevut Yaakov adds a reason: the entire institution of three year chazakah rests on the fact that after that period a purchaser is no longer careful to preserve his deed. In the case of a debt or ketubah lien, however, the debt document or ketubah remains in the hands of the creditor or wife. That rationale therefore does not apply. See there.
