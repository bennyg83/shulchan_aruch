It too is nullified, etc. Meaning, for example, where the witnesses to the document were relatives or otherwise disqualified, and certainly where the document was forged. See Sema. The Bach holds that if the possessor swears that the deed was lost and there is no falsehood in his mouth, he is believed. See Tumim, where I wrote that this is not implied by the authorities, especially the Rosh. The Bach's practical concern, 'What is one whose deed was lost supposed to do?', is not such a strong difficulty. Therefore, as practical law, he is not believed, contrary to the Bach. See there.
