If the document was lost, he is not believed. The Bach asks: what is a person whose deed was lost supposed to do? Should he lose merely because he no longer has the deed despite having possessed the property for three years? He therefore explains that 'he is not believed' means only that we do not accept his bare assertion that the deed was lost and then rely on his chazakah. Rather, he must swear that the deed was lost, after which the matter is judged under the laws of chazakah. This view is cited by Baer HaGolah, Ateret Tzvi, Maamar Kaddishin, and Responsa Shevut Yaakov. Tumim disagrees and maintains that the language of the authorities implies that he is not believed at all. The Bach's difficulty is answered because one who initially comes to beit din and says that his deed was lost is indeed believed, which is the basis of the three year chazakah, since after three years a purchaser is not expected to guard his deed. The present case is different: he first came to beit din asserting that the deed was in his possession, and only after being told to produce it did he claim that it had been lost. That creates a defect in his claim. Knesset HaGedolah similarly writes that the Bach applies where the person went to search for the deed and returned saying he could not find it, or where circumstances indicate that it was lost. But if he first litigated on the basis of both deed and chazakah and then, when told to produce the deed, said that it was lost, he is not believed even with an oath.
