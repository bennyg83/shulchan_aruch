If he later claimed that the document was lost, etc. His chazakah is likewise void. The Bach holds that if he swears the document was genuinely lost and there was no deceit, he is believed. The decisors and the Rosh do not indicate this. The Rosh writes simply that he is not believed, implying complete rejection. Moreover, according to the Rosh and Tur, even a person with a three year chazakah may have to swear that he truly purchased the land, and certainly the opponent can impose gilgul shevuah concerning related matters. If the claimant were merely required to swear that the document was lost, why would the Rosh say he is not believed at all? The Bach asks what a person whose document truly was lost is supposed to do. There is no difficulty. If from the outset he came to beit din and said the document had been lost, he would indeed be believed, for that is the entire reason a three year chazakah works: after three years a person no longer guards his document. Here, however, he came to beit din and admitted that the document still existed, and only after beit din ordered him to produce it did he say it had been lost. That is an evident defect in his claim. The rationale that a person guards his document for three years but not longer no longer applies. He himself admitted he in fact kept it until the litigation began. Once litigation started, he had even greater reason to guard it, especially after beit din expressly told him to bring it. He is therefore not believed to say it was then lost. The basis of the chazakah has disappeared. This is clear.
