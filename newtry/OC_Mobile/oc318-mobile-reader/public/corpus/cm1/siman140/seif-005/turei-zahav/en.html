The Tur rejects this because the tenants come to establish the possessor's ownership, and they cannot do so without testifying that they themselves lived there. Beit Yosef objects that they could testify that the possessor rented the house to someone else. On that understanding, Rabbenu Chananel's migo of “we did not live there” means not silence but the claim that another person lived there. Yet this is not a valid migo, because they fear that the supposed other tenant may contradict them, and it is implausible that they could avoid identifying him without exposing the falsehood. Still, the Tur is difficult. We do not suspect witnesses of lying merely to benefit the possessor, only for their own financial interest. Silence would also serve that interest. One may answer that they fear other witnesses will later testify that they lived in the house, forcing them to pay rent. Since people likely saw them there, they seek to establish the possessor's title so that such testimony will not harm them. Rabbenu Chananel nevertheless reasons that they could make the still better claim that the possessor or someone deriving from him lived there, which would establish possession without exposing themselves to a rent claim. The Tur responds that this alternative cannot establish nighttime residence, because the Gemara concludes that prescriptive possession of houses is proved only by neighbors or tenants. They were not neighbors. Thus they cannot escape suspicion except by saying that they were the tenants. Since their exemption from rent depends on the possessor's success, they remain interested witnesses in every formulation. I later saw that Ramban also disputes Rabbenu Tam, though for another reason, and strongly supports the Tur's ruling.
