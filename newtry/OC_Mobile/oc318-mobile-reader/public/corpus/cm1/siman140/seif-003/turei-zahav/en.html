(Seif 10) Even if they already paid him. This is difficult in light of siman 28, seif 15, where testimony is not received in a sick defendant's absence even though the witnesses may later become unavailable. One cannot answer that there a remedy remains when the minors mature, because here too the rent can be returned, as Rabbenu Yonah himself writes. It appears that this was enacted as a practical safeguard even though it is not the strict law, much as the language in siman 28 implies that the Sages sometimes allowed testimony that would otherwise improperly prejudice orphans. Alternatively, here there was no identifiable opposing party at the time, because no protester was yet known, so it is not literally testimony in a party's absence. One might ask why the Tur needed this reason, since the possessor is the defendant and siman 28 permits receiving a defendant's evidence in the claimant's absence. The answer is that land remains presumptively with its original owner once evidence shows that it was his; the possessor is therefore functionally the claimant and must establish his prescriptive possession. I remain troubled by the Tur's statement that because no protester is presently before us the tenants are not interested witnesses. In siman 37, concerning a first purchaser testifying for a second, Beit Yosef cites Rabbi Yonah that the first purchaser fears that the land is stolen and that the dispossessed owner may later appear. Here too the tenants see the possessor gathering evidence, and therefore understand that a challenge exists. They may testify falsely to avoid paying rent a second time. The matter requires study.
