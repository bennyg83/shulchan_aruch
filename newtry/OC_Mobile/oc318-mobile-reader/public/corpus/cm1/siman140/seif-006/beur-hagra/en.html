One who gives land. Kesubos 109 concerning one who contests a field after acting in a manner indicating recognition of the other party's right. If he claims that he later repurchased it, he may be believed with the proper migo, as Raavad and Tosafos explain. Without such a claim, however, he loses his right because a migo cannot overcome the evidentiary force of his own signature by merely asserting that he signed in error.
