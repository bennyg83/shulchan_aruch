(Seif 13, gloss: A path passed through the house). One cannot object that perhaps the seller reserved the path at the time of sale, since the possessor bears the burden of establishing a possession not vulnerable even to a plausible doubt. Nor may one say that the path presumably did not pass through every room, so the possessor should at least acquire the remaining portions, as with possession of a defined place in seif 15. Here the original owner indisputably owned the entire house, and the newcomer claims it only through three years of possession and the owner's silence. Any satisfactory reason for that silence defeats the presumption. Continued passage through the house, or even retention of a defined place in the courtyard, adequately explains why the owner did not protest. In seif 15, by contrast, a stranger possesses only a defined location; nothing supports a claim to the remainder.
