We do not say a migo against witnesses. See Tumim. I wrote that the Rashba follows his own position that we do say migo to extract property, and therefore he needs the reason that this is a migo against witnesses. According to our ruling that one does not use a migo to extract property, perhaps this additional reason is unnecessary. Nevertheless, I concluded that one should not infer from the Mechaber's citation of the Rashba that the Mechaber holds that a migo can extract property. The Mechaber copied the language in order to teach the broader principle for cases where the objection of extracting property does not apply. See there. I also concluded that there is no contradiction from above, siman 43, seif 9, where the Rashba believes the witnesses can say, 'We erred,' through a migo. There, in any event, they were forced to say that there had been an error or something similar. See Tumim there at length.
