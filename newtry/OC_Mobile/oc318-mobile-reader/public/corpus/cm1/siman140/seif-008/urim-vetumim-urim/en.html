He definitely did not live, etc. The Shach holds that if the protester says, 'I know with certainty that he did not live there,' then there is no chazakah. He cites many authorities who hold so and questions where the Tur derived this, since Tosafot and the Rosh, when presenting Rabbeinu Tam's view, do not mention that a definite claim by the protester defeats chazakah. See Tumim, where I explained the Tur's source in Tosafot and the Rosh.
The residents there are traveling merchants. See Sema, who questions at length why the Rama omitted Rabbeinu Tam's ruling that if the witnesses themselves are traveling merchants, they can testify that they saw the possessor living in the house whenever they were in town, even though they were not permanent residents. See Tumim, where I wrote that Rabbeinu Tam has no proof from the Gemara, and that the law follows Rabbeinu Chananel, whose words are received tradition. It also appears that the Rosh and Tur agree with Rabbeinu Chananel, as do the other authorities, and even those who rule like Rashbam agree with Rabbeinu Chananel, as the Shach writes in seif katan 11. The Rama therefore acted properly in omitting Rabbeinu Tam's ruling, and there is no difficulty.
Where the custom is that each person writes his name, etc. The source is a responsum of the Rosh. See Tumim, where I wrote that the Rama abbreviated excessively. In my opinion, where the custom is strict that no one may sit in another's place except the owner or his agent, and a stranger may not sit in his seat, there is room to question whether merely writing one's name is sufficient proof. The matter requires study.
