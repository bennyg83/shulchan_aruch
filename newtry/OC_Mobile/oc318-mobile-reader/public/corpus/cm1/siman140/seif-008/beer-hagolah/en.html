Rambam, chapter 12 of Hilkhot To'en, law 2. Maggid Mishneh understands Mar Zutra's statement, "and if he claims," as meaning that such a claim is accepted even when made only as a doubtful assertion. He writes that some disagree with Rambam and hold that it is accepted only when asserted with certainty, but Rambam's view appears correct.

According to Rabbeinu Chananel, who explains Mar Zutra's admission concerning peddlers as a case where the possessor or those occupying through him were peddlers: even though they did not live there continuously for three years, if the house remained in their established possession for three years this constitutes chazakah, as Rama writes below. Tur cites this in seif 16, and Tosafot likewise write in Rabbeinu Chananel's name. According to Rabbeinu Chananel's alternative explanation, Mar Zutra refers to the witnesses: they need not testify to actual continuous residence because they travel through towns; it is enough for them to testify that the house was regarded as being in his possession for three years, as Tosafot write in his name.
