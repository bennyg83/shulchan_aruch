Unless they rented it by written lease, etc. The Tur writes this in the Raavad's name and explains that rental possession is like the case of successive purchasers who purchased from one another and each held for one year, as stated in siman 144:2. The alternative opinion holds that rental is different because the landlord's name remains upon the property throughout all three years, since each tenant rented from that same possessor. With successive purchasers, by contrast, each earlier purchaser's name disappears after his year, and the challenger can say: I did not protest because I saw that no single possessor held the property for more than one year and each disappeared like a robber. The Tur cites a third view, that of the Rashba, who distinguishes that if two tenants jointly occupied the property for all three years, this establishes possession even without a written lease. In the Perishah I explained that this indicates that the first view cited by the Mechaber, namely the Raavad, holds that even if the two tenants lived there together throughout all three years, there is no presumptive possession without a written lease from the landlord. This is difficult. If the tenants lived there continuously for three years, the challenger should have protested. Furthermore, the Raavad proves his rule from purchasers, and with purchasers three years of possession suffices even without a deed of purchase. It may be that he distinguishes purchasers because they themselves claim ownership and say they bought the property from the challenger. Their three years without protest therefore establish possession. These tenants, however, do not themselves claim title. The landlord seeks to establish his title through their occupancy. The challenger can say: I relied on the occupants not to make the false claim that they purchased from me, so I had no need to protest against them. I did not know of the landlord's claim because there was no written lease and therefore no publicity. The comparison to purchasers concerns only the need for a document to create public notice.
