If the protester does not know. If the protester knows, they are not believed through a migo that they could have said, 'We never lived there,' because he would contradict them. Tosafot implies this even where the protester has no witnesses that they lived there. Although elsewhere such a migo is accepted despite the other party's contradiction, as explained above in siman 81, seif 23, and siman 110, seif 2, here they are witnesses. Had they denied residence, they would have been required to swear. Their testimony is therefore interested testimony, for any witness who requires an oath is not a valid witness, as explained above in siman 121. This appears correct.
Since they could have said, 'We did not reside there,' etc. The Tur disagrees, but Tosafot records Rabbeinu Chananel's position, Rambam agrees in practice, and Mordechai also rules so. Nevertheless, one may say this is not a valid migo, because they fear that the matter may become known or witnesses may arrive proving that they lived there. Ramban initially writes this. Rabbeinu Yerucham, Netiv 11, part 2, cites Rabbeinu Chananel and then writes that according to the other authorities they remain interested witnesses, and that this is the principal ruling. Perhaps this is the Tur's intent, though his wording is somewhat difficult.
