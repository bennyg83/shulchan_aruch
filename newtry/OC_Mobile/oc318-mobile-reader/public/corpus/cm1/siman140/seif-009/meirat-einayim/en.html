Even if he lent it to them without charge. This too is considered a benefit derived by the possessor. If other witnesses testify that these residents lived there through his permission as borrowers for three years, and the claimant does not assert with certainty that they left at night, that establishes chazakah. The residents themselves, however, are certainly not believed to say that they lived there by virtue of his having lent it to them, because they are interested witnesses. If the house is removed from this possessor, they may have to pay rent to the claimant. The Tur and Mechaber's principal purpose is to teach that one can establish chazakah even through a form of possession from which the possessor has not yet received direct monetary benefit. It is unnecessary to establish the case specifically where the house is not normally rented and the borrowers are not people who ordinarily rent, as discussed below in siman 363. See the Perishah.
Some disagree. Their reason is that because of the favor he did for them by returning their money and sparing them litigation, they may be unable to act boldly against him and may testify in his favor falsely.
