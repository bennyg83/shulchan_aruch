What constitutes chazakah. See Sema, seif katan 6, citing Responsa Maimoniyot, that a three year chazakah operates against one who challenges ownership of the land itself, but not against a lien for a debt or ketubah. Netivot HaMishpat therefore writes that with our present deeds containing a formal kinyan from the wife followed by her husband, one must preserve the deed indefinitely, lest the wife later collect her ketubah from that property, because chazakah does not defeat the ketubah lien. Responsa Shevut Yaakov, part 3, siman 159, likewise discusses a bankrupt whose later creditor held one of his synagogue seats for three years and argued against earlier creditors that their failure to protest showed waiver of their liens. He rules, following Maharam and the later authorities, that chazakah does not apply against a debt or ketubah lien. Although one might argue that where the debtor is manifestly destitute there is no realistic possibility of collecting from unencumbered property, Maharam's responsum indicates that chazakah does not extinguish such a lien at all. Shevut Yaakov adds that the rationale of the three year rule is that a purchaser ceases guarding his deed after that period. A creditor or wife, however, still retains the underlying debt instrument or ketubah, so that rationale does not apply.
