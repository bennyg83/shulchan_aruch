And if it had been rented in such a manner, etc. Netivot HaMishpat finds this ruling difficult and suggests a distinction between a house that fell, where the landlord merely lacks use during the rental term, and a mortgaged house whose failure to redeem would cause a permanent and substantial loss. According to him this could apply whether the rented house itself or another house of the landlord was mortgaged. Biur HaGra, however, implies that the ruling concerns specifically the very house occupied by the tenant. See also Taz and Pilpela Charifta. Amudei Or, siman 109, connects the issue to the dispute whether one must sacrifice his wine to save another's honey and recover its value, cited in siman 264. Here too the tenant would be required to surrender his present right of occupancy to save the landlord's house only where the landlord's loss is wrongful and the tenant suffers no monetary loss. If the property was validly mortgaged to a Jew on condition of forfeiture at a fixed time, the landlord's loss cannot override the mortgagee's legal right, and the tenant's right of occupancy should likewise not be displaced. This requires study.
