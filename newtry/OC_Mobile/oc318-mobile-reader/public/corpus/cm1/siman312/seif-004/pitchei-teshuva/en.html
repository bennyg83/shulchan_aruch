He may not leave unless he pays the rent. Shaar Mishpat understands the language to mean that even if the tenant leaves, he remains obligated to pay, but questions this from siman 363, where one who merely deprives another of use without himself living there is exempt from rent. He therefore suggests that here the tenant is not judicially compelled to pay damages after leaving, but may be restrained from leaving and placed under ban because indirect damage is forbidden. The same would apply to the local-custom case later in this siman. If he nevertheless left, there would be no collection in beit din. Shaar Mishpat notes that contemporary practice appears otherwise. In Nachalat Tzvi I explained why this conclusion does not appear correct.
