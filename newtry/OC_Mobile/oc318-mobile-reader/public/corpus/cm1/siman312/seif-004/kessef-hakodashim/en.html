The intercalated month belongs to the landlord. Where the rent is one dinar per month and the year is intercalated, the added month is paid to the landlord. At first glance the earlier case is readily understood, because if the year was intercalated after the lease the tenant gains an extra month. In this case, however, it is less natural to say that the intercalation 'belongs to the landlord,' since the landlord would receive his monthly rent whether or not the year was intercalated. Perhaps the ruling teaches that even where local tenants ordinarily rent only in terms of twelve months per year and this tenant happened merely to use the phrase 'a dinar per month,' the added month is still the landlord's, because this particular year's intercalation created a definite additional gain under the language actually used. Had a different year been intercalated, there would have been no such gain. This requires further study.
