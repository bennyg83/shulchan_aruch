(Seif 18: But if it fell) This means even if the entire upper story fell. This is the Tur's view, against the Ramah, who holds that when the whole upper story falls the owner of the lower house need not rebuild it, because the lower house was subordinated to the upper story only while the latter existed. The Tur's reasoning appears to be that in the phrase “this upper story upon this house,” the word “this” cannot mean that the obligation ends if the upper story falls, for then there would have been no need to add “upon this house.” Rather, the phrase means the opposite: the house is subordinated to maintaining this upper story in the form in which it existed. Therefore the Tur writes in his objection that even if the whole upper story fell, the owner must rebuild it, meaning as it was. If the lower house itself falls, however, the Tur agrees that the obligation ends, since “this house” may properly be understood as limiting the undertaking to that particular house. The Rama rules accordingly in this siman. I later found the Rashbam's explanation in Bava Batra 63b explicitly supporting the Tur: saying “upon this house” obligates the owner to rebuild the lower structure if the upper chamber falls. The Tur's view is therefore primary, and the Beit Yosef's disagreement lacks proof.
