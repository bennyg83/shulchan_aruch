[Shulchan Aruch] And if he said to him, a house. Note: The Tur writes that according to the Ramah we do not compel the lessor to rebuild it; rather, the tenant may retain the rent until another house is provided. If he already paid the rent, he may take it back, and this is so even if a kinyan was made. If, however, the lessor had another house at the time of the rental, he must provide it, because he said merely a house and the kinyan takes effect upon that other house. This implies that a kinyan is required in any event and that possession taken in this house does not acquire the other house. This is also the view of Raavad above, siman 306, regarding an unspecified donkey. To me this is difficult: that may be understandable with a donkey, but with a house why should possession taken in one house not acquire another, just as one act of chazakah acquires ten fields, as above, siman 192, seif 3? This requires study. The Rosh holds that a formal kinyan is effective. These are the words of the Tur. See Derishah, who understands that even according to the Rosh a formal kinyan is required and possession in this house does not suffice, and distinguishes this case from siman 310 regarding an unspecified donkey, where the Rosh holds that no formal kinyan is required. See there.
