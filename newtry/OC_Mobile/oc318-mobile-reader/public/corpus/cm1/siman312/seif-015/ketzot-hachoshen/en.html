One who rents a house for a year. Rif was asked about a rental for a year where both parties thought the year contained twelve months and it later proved to be a leap year. He ruled that knowledge and ignorance are equal. The Mishnah says that if a year becomes intercalated, the added month belongs to the tenant. The same applies even where neither party knew in advance that the year would be intercalated.
But one who hires a teacher. Rashba rules that if a teacher was hired for a year under two formulations, one annual and one monthly, and the year was intercalated, the father need not pay separately for the added month after the teacher has already taught it willingly. The decisive point is that the teacher voluntarily rendered the service. A tenant in a house is different because he uses another's property. This resembles workers who perform labor under an unresolved wage formulation: once they worked voluntarily, money remains with the employer. But if the teacher refuses to teach during the added month, the father cannot compel him without paying, because the teacher remains in possession of his own labor. The same applies to a servant hired for a year.
