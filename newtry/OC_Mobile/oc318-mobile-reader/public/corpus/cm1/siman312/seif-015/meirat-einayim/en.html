The leap month belongs to the renter. The Tur writes that Rashba says: when does this apply? When he was standing on Rosh Hashanah and said, "this year," or "the year," which we treat like "this year." But if he says, "one year," he receives only twelve months, like most years, which are not leap years. End of Rashba's words. The Mechaber omitted this, and Rama likewise did not mention it, because the Beit Yosef questioned the Tur's citation. He writes that Rashba himself stated this only as theoretical law, but in practice Rashba wrote to follow the practice of earlier Geonim who did not distinguish. The Beit Yosef and Darkei Moshe also cite several views that do not distinguish between "this year" and an unspecified year. I added a further difficulty: the Tur's ruling here appears to contradict his own words in Yoreh Deah siman 220 concerning vows. There he writes: if one stands in the middle of the year and says "this year," the prohibition lasts until the year's completion and he becomes permitted on Rosh Hashanah. If he says "one year" or merely "a year," he is prohibited from date to date; if it is a leap year he is prohibited for thirteen months from day to day. I also cited Rashba's language, for he derives monetary law from prohibitory law and vice versa and considers them equivalent. With God's help, I reconciled all of this in a clear and internally supported manner. Here the Tur begins, "When does this apply? When he stood on Rosh Hashanah and said, etc." He holds that when one stands on Rosh Hashanah and says, "one year," or simply, "a year," whether regarding rental or a vow, his intent is certainly that the leap month not be included. Had he intended to include it, he should have said, "this year." His choice of "a year" or "one year" therefore reveals that he means an ordinary year, which ordinarily is not intercalated. In Yoreh Deah, however, the Tur begins with a person standing in the middle of the year. There he concludes that if he says "one year" or "a year," his intent is thirteen months when the period includes a leap month. Standing in the middle of a year, he could not have said "this year," because that would mean only until the end of the current year, namely Rosh Hashanah, whereas he wished to rent or prohibit for a complete year. He therefore had to say "one year" or "a year." Although Ran appears not to make this distinction, the Tur understands Rashba's words this way so that they do not contradict one another. This resolves all the difficulties raised by the Beit Yosef, Darkei Moshe, and myself. See also what I wrote in the Derishah. In my opinion this is correct in practice. Examine it carefully.
If he rented by months, the leap month belongs to the owner. This is obvious and is mentioned because of the following clause. Alternatively, it teaches us not to say that since the leap month merely completes the lunar months, once he pays for all twelve months of the year the added month that equalizes the calendar should be included. The ruling teaches otherwise.
He mentioned both months and a year, whether he said, etc. The reference to "year" here concerns one who was standing on Rosh Hashanah and said "the year" or "this year," as explained above according to Rashba. Had he mentioned only a year and not months, the leap month would have been included. Nimukei Yosef, in chapter HaShoel, writes that the uncertainty whether we follow the first expression or the last exists only when the expressions are adjacent. If they are not adjacent, all agree that the final expression controls, as with a promissory note.
The leap month belongs to the owner, etc. The Tur writes that we are uncertain whether to follow the first expression or the last, and land remains in its owner's possession. Even if the owner does not demand the rent until the end of the month, after the renter has already lived there, the renter must pay. Rashi explains that the uncertainty did not arise only now after he lived there; it existed from the beginning of the month. We therefore leave the land in its prior possession, and it turns out that he lived in another's property and must pay rent. This applies specifically to land. With movable property, whenever we are uncertain whether the first or last expression controls, we do not remove the property from whoever presently holds it. See above, siman 200, regarding "a kor for thirty, a se'ah for a sela." This is specifically where the matter is not destined to become clarified. The Tur and Mechaber likewise write this above at the end of siman 311, seif 3, and in Hagahot Darkei Moshe to the present siman, seif 15.
Because land remains in its owner's possession.
