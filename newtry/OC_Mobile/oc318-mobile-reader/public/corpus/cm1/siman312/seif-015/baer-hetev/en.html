For a year. Regarding when the year begins for purposes of house rentals, see the Sema and Shach, who discuss the matter at length and sufficiently explain it. See Responsa David ibn Lev, vol. 2, p. 51, and Mahari ibn Lev, vol. 2, siman 21. See also Yoreh Deah, siman 220, seif 7, and the Shach there, who explains why the law of an intercalated year there does not contradict this case, together with the Sema's reconciliation.
And a year. This concerns one standing on Rosh Hashanah who said "the year" or "this year." Had he merely said "a year" without mentioning a month, the intercalated month would be included. Nimukei Yosef, chapter HaShoel, writes that the doubt exists only where the two expressions are adjacent to one another. If they are not adjacent, everyone agrees that the later expression governs, as we say regarding a promissory note. Sema.
The lessor. The Tur writes that the doubt is whether we follow the first expression or the last, and land remains in its owner's possession. Therefore, even if the lessor does not demand the rent until the end of the intercalated month, after the tenant has already lived there, the tenant must still pay. Rashi explains that the doubt did not arise only now; it existed from the beginning of the month. We therefore leave the land in its established ownership and regard the tenant as having lived in another's property, for which he must pay. The Sema writes that this applies specifically to land. With movable property, where there is a doubt whether the first or last expression controls, possession prevails and we do not remove the property from the one who holds it. See siman 200, seif 7, regarding "a kor for thirty sela, a seah for one sela." The Shach writes that the Rosh in the first chapter of Bava Metzia regarding "the priest seized it" and in chapter 2 of Ketubot regarding two pairs of contradictory witnesses clearly implies that this is because the tenant lacks a certain claim. If the tenant says with certainty, "You expressly rented it to me for twelve dinar for the year even if the year is intercalated," we do not extract from him. Although siman 317, seif 3, states that even a creditor asserting certainty may be dispossessed because land remains in the owner's possession, that case differs. There the amount of produce consumed is known, and the creditor seeks to retain it by a certain claim. Since that produce derives from the body of the land, it is as though it remains in the debtor's possession, so the creditor's certain claim does not help. Here, however, ownership of the land does not itself establish how much rent the land yielded. It may have been rented from the outset for only twelve dinar. If the tenant asserts this with certainty, he is the possessor of the money. See the Shach's explanation of the Gemara on this basis.
Its owner. The Shach cites Hagahot Ashri, chapter HaShoel, that if one rented a house "until Adar" or "until the end of Adar," and one party says first Adar while the other says second Adar, they divide the disputed period. He writes that this apparently derives from Tosefta Bava Metzia, chapter 1, but we do not follow that Tosefta, which accords with Rabban Shimon ben Gamliel and Rabbi Yose, who split the intercalated month where the rent is stated as twelve dinar per year, one dinar per month. We rule like Rav Nachman, as the Gemara in HaMekabel explicitly states, that the entire intercalated month belongs to the lessor because land remains in its owner's possession, and all the poskim rule so. Accordingly, in a dispute whether "Adar" means first or second Adar, the entire amount likewise belongs to the lessor. Or Zarua apparently follows his own view. The Shach concludes that according to our rule in disputes among authorities that the burden of proof rests on the claimant, as stated in siman 25 and at the end of siman 159, the lessor is the possessor here and the tenant must bring proof. Even if the demand is made only at the end of the month, the entire amount belongs to the lessor.
