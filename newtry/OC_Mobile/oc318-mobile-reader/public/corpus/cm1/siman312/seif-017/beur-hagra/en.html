One who rents out, etc. As above, seif 1 in the gloss.
And if he transgressed and demolished it, etc. See Beer HaGolah and Rashi to Erchin 20b, s.v. "nesano, etc." Certainly so in this case. See Tosafos there, s.v. "af al pi, etc.," and their explanation of the statement here, etc.
And even if he rebuilt it, etc. As stated, once it fell the original house is gone and this is a different one. See above siman 153:2.
And even the renter, etc. As stated in Kesubos 103a.
And there are, etc. As stated 79a regarding "this donkey." See Tosafos there, s.v. "ve'im, etc." When the donkey dies, it is gone, whereas here it is still a house and is intended for repair, like the donkey's carcass that can be sold. There.
If there is, etc. See Sema.
And this is, etc. Rif, as stated there 104a, provided it is still called, etc.
But, etc. As above, seif 1 in the gloss concerning one who rents a house, etc.
And although, etc. The Mishnah says only that it became smaller, etc., as stated below, "therefore, etc."
If it burned, etc. Meaning, the rental is reduced, as above, "rather one calculates, etc.," and as stated 79a where a donkey died halfway through the journey. See Tosafos 116b, s.v. "i, etc." In siman 27 the Rama writes that the renter must pay the entire rent because the house was in his possession and burned due to his fortune, as Rashi writes regarding a house that fell, comparable to an unspecified ship and this wine. On this he writes that if the whole city burned, etc., as stated there 105a in the Mishnah concerning a locust plague, etc., which rules equally whether the lessee prepaid or not. See there. The Rama's words require study. See Sema, who emends the text to read, "others say that he pays the full rent; nevertheless, if the whole, etc." Darkei Moshe likewise writes so. See there.
One who rents out, etc. As stated there 101b: the lessor is responsible for a door, etc., and the Gemara there says he must provide doors, etc. Certainly so in this case. There.
