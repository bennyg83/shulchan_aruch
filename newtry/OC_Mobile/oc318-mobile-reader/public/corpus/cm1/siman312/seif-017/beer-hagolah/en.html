The Maggid Mishneh writes so there in chapter 5, law 6, in explanation of Rambam.
Rambam there, law 6. The Maggid Mishneh explains that they said the landlord is not obligated to rebuild only where the house collapsed on its own. If the landlord himself demolished it, he certainly must provide another house. This is simple, and all the more so according to Rashi, who explains that the tenant's mazal caused the collapse, which cannot be said when the landlord demolished it.
Rambam there, law 6, explicit in the Gemara at the end of HaShoel. The Maggid Mishneh cites the commentators that this differs from 'this donkey,' where if the carcass has sufficient value to rent another donkey, he rents one, as above siman 310, seif 2. A dead donkey stands to be sold so another can be purchased, whereas a collapsed house is not ordinarily sold, but is rebuilt. Tosafot likewise write this in HaOmanin, 79a.
Tur seif 16. The Rosh writes so in responsa, klal 35, siman 6, from Tosafot's statement that a house does not stand to be sold but to have additions made and then be rebuilt as before.
