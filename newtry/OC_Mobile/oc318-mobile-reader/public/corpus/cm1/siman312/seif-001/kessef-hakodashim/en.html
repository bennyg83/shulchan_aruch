One who rents a house to another. A tenant who seeks to withdraw because he later heard that the location had acquired a presumption of danger after three persons there became ill may perhaps treat this as a defect in the rental where people generally regard the matter as dangerous and are afraid because of it. If the concern had not become established even twice, mere public fear may not suffice; after a recognized pattern it may be different. This requires further study. A condition. Where, according to the city's fixed custom for house rentals, the tenant had the right on Rosh Chodesh or immediately near it to retract, several days passed without retraction, and only afterward news arrived that her husband had written before Rosh Chodesh instructing her not to rent, this may resemble the case in Ketubot 97a where at the relevant moment the reason for the transaction still existed. Even if the tenant's conduct could be viewed as satisfying her side of the condition, the landlord's intent may have been that the decision be made specifically that day so that he could rent to another. Tosafot write in the case of one who sold only in order to marry that in a sale the minds of both parties are required; the buyer's unexpressed intent does not by itself void the transaction.
