One who rents out, etc. The Mishnah and Gemara 101b, even where he rented an unspecified house, etc. See seif 4, "he rented, etc.," and see 116b in the Mishnah and Gemara, as stated in seif 18 and seif 20, and 103a: "a person may borrow, etc.; one who, etc." See Shitah Mekubetzes.
Even if he doubled, etc. In such a case, with an unspecified rental, he could remove him, as stated there, unlike here. See seif 11. The entire sugya there concerns an unspecified rental, as shown by the Gemara's question, "what is different in the rainy season, for when, etc.?" See Rashi there. Meaning, one who rents an unspecified house to his fellow, etc. The Rif likewise cites the Yerushalmi: "if they gave it for lodging, etc.; but if they mentioned a fixed period, etc." The Rosh likewise writes that the entire discussion concerns an unspecified rental. So too the Yerushalmi: "it was taught, for lodging, etc.; but if, etc." Maggid Mishneh writes this in the name of the Rashba, and the Rosh writes it in responsa, rule 1. He bases it on the principle in chapter HaZahav that rental for its term is like a sale. This is why his pesakim there say, "and likewise if he doubled, etc."
Even if he became poor, etc. See Rosh there. The text "but if he has nothing, etc." is a scribal error and should read "even if he has nothing to eat." Nevertheless he may sell the house, even though the renter remains, etc. Meaning, this is not a sale of something outside his possession such that the sale would be void. Rather, the purchaser simply cannot remove the renter before the end of the term, as stated in the Yerushalmi below. Bach. Rabbi Zeira and Rabbi Ila do not dispute Rabbi Ami; each states a different rule, unlike the Perishah, who understands them as disputing. The text "but, etc., he misled him," is as above. This is also evident from the Yerushalmi, which asks, "do they disagree?" What difficulty would there be if Rabbi Ami also disagreed with them? Clearly it never entered the mind that anyone would dispute the rule that rental for its term is like a sale. The Rosh's words there, "and he holds like Rabbi Ami that he goes out, etc.," are likewise a scribal error and should read that Rabbi Ami means he goes out immediately. Otherwise the Yerushalmi's question has no sense. The Rosh's phrase "from then onward he will give you the rent" means if the purchaser wishes to allow him to remain there. Although the Rosh's language could appear to imply a dispute, the interpretation of the Yerushalmi given here is more correct.
And the sale is valid. As the Rosh writes, "but he may sell it, etc.," as above, and as follows from the Yerushalmi.
And if it was, etc. In the Yerushalmi there, they said that his house had been mortgaged to a certain Roman, etc. Mordechai explains that another house of the lessor was mortgaged to a non-Jewish Roman or pledged as security, and if he did not redeem it the house would become permanently his and never return to the lessor. The lessor therefore needed to sell the house occupied by the renter in order to redeem the other house, and he found no buyer unless the renter would leave immediately. In such a case all agree that the renter must leave, because it is analogous to the lessor's own house collapsing, where the Gemara says, "you are no better than I," whereas mere lack of food does not allow the lessor to sell and remove the renter immediately. See there. Although the halachah does not follow this view regarding the lessor's house collapsing, as above, and Tosafos 102b at the end of s.v. "bechezkas" agree with Mordechai, the Rama learned from there that if the very house in which the renter lives is subject to such a mortgage, the lessor certainly need not lose his house. The Rosh, however, explains that the sold house itself was mortgaged to a Jew, and in that case Rabbi Ami ruled that the renter leaves immediately because a mortgage differs from a rental. Since the owner can redeem it whenever he wishes, he may sell and redeem it. This is why Tur and Shulchan Aruch in seif 3 rule concerning one who mortgaged, etc. Bach likewise writes so. The Rosh's phrase "but one who rents a house for a year" is a scribal error and should read "for a fixed period," as stated below: he must wait until the term ends. But if it was for a year, even a mortgage has that law, as Tur and Shulchan Aruch write there. See what I write there.
He stipulated, etc. Since the condition concerned an unspecified thing, the heir does not acquire it, as stated in siman 210:7. Certainly a purchaser does not. There.
And if he prepaid, etc. See Beer HaGolah. Beit Yosef asks what novelty this adds, since the first clause already states that even without prepayment, if he rented for a fixed term he cannot be removed. He answers that here the novelty is that even where he rented without specifying a term, prepayment protects him for the corresponding period. This is the meaning of "even if he did not, etc." See Tosafos to Erchin 21a, s.v. "hachi ka'amar, etc.": "however it is difficult, for here, etc.; and in the Tosefta, etc.; one may answer, etc." The same rule appears there in such a case. The Gemara there prohibits living in consecrated property without prepayment from the rule that one pays rent to hekdesh, and all of that concerns an unspecified rental. The Tosefta likewise proves that in such a case he cannot be removed, for it says, "if the owner arose and sold it, they calculate with the owner," meaning the purchaser receives the rent from the seller until the renter's paid term ends.
One who rents out a house, etc. As stated in seif 13 and above, because rental for its term is like a sale. In Bava Batra 7a one party says, "tear it down, etc.; I will rent, etc." Mordechai there.
Also, etc. As stated in the Mishnah there 20b, "he may object, etc."
Nevertheless, etc. There. This differs from what is written in chapter 3 of Bava Metzia, as stated above siman 12:2 in the gloss. The rule of acting beyond the strict letter of the law appears in chapter 16 of Shabbos, chapter 9 of Bava Kamma, chapter 2 of Bava Metzia, and Shitah Mekubetzes.
