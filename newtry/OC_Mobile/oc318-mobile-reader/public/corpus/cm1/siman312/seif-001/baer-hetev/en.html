House. A lease of land is acquired by money, document, possession, or kinyan sudar, just like a sale, as the Tur and Shulchan Aruch write at the end of siman 192, in siman 195, and the Tur at the beginning of siman 315. Once one of these acquisitions has been made, the lease is like a sale, for a lease for its term is considered a temporary sale. Therefore the lessor cannot remove the tenant in any manner until the period acquired by the lease ends. So writes the Sema. See Responsa Rashdam, simanim 282, 288, 292, 295, and 300. Responsa Rashdam, end of siman 287, implies that if during the fixed term the lessor told the tenant to vacate the house at a certain date and the tenant said yes, this is ineffective without a kinyan. Maharit Tzahalon, siman 89, writes that when the lessor says this to the tenant it is effective without a kinyan, but when the tenant says it to the lessor it is ineffective without a kinyan. See also Responsa Mabit, vol. 1, siman 103, and Maharit, siman 118. Bnei Chayei.

And even. The Beit Yosef writes this based on an inference from his question on the Tur: why must the Tur say, "if he prepaid the rent," when he already taught the stronger rule that even without prepayment the lessor cannot remove him until the term ends? The Beit Yosef therefore explains that the case of prepayment applies even where no term was fixed. The Sema writes that this inference is not compelling. The passage can still refer to a fixed term, like the opening of the seif, and to a sale of the property to another person. Although we already know that neither the lessor nor the purchaser may remove the tenant during the term, the tenant ordinarily must pay the rent to the purchaser rather than the original lessor. This clause teaches that where the tenant already prepaid the rent, he need not pay rent to the purchaser at all, yet the purchaser still cannot remove him until the term ends. These are the words of the Rosh based on the Yerushalmi. Therefore, the Mechaber apparently shortened his formulation and did not expressly say that the rule applies where no term was fixed, since that conclusion is not established. The Shach agrees, unlike the Bach, and brings a proof. One who rented a house for twelve dinar per year, payable one dinar each month, but is too poor to pay both the arrears and the rent to come, can plainly be removed, because the house was rented to him only on condition that he pay month by month. Maharit, vol. 1, siman 113, p. 143a. Bnei Chayei.
