Even if the landlord's own house fell. The rule that the landlord may say, "You are no better than I am," concerns the notice period of an unspecified tenancy. During a fixed rental term, even if the landlord's own house falls, he may not remove the tenant before the end of the term. Tosafot's statement that a tenant does not ordinarily prepay because perhaps the landlord's house will fall can be understood, following Maggid Mishneh, as referring to the thirty-day notice period of an open-ended tenancy.
Even if no term was fixed. The Shach disputes Rama's rule that the price can determine the duration where no term was fixed. In my view Rama is correct. Price is evidence where it does not contradict the language. The dispute concerning a yoke and oxen is different because the seller can say his wording referred only to the narrower meaning. But where the wording leaves the quantity or duration open, the amount paid can define it. Rashbam's explanation in the case of an ox sold for plowing or slaughter, Rav Hai Gaon's rule concerning sale of an unspecified portion of land for a stated sum, and Terumat HaDeshen's ruling concerning a messenger's unusually high wage all support this. Even in land, although there is no law of overreaching, price can clarify intent where no language contradicts it. Therefore an unspecified rental can be measured according to the amount paid.
