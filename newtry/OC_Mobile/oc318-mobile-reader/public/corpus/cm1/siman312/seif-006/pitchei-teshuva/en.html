And if he became his enemy, he may remove him. Shevut Yaakov III:179 discusses a landlord who, needing money for commerce, rented out his house for several years on condition of immediate advance payment and planned to live in cramped quarters. A non-Jewish creditor attached the rent money, and the landlord refused to vacate, claiming that the renter had disclosed the matter to the creditor. If it can be proved that the renter informed the creditor, the rental is certainly void, for although this may not technically be actionable informing under siman 388, it is a grave wrong and the landlord plainly did not rent on that understanding. This is stronger than the Rama's rule that if one rented because of friendship and they became enemies, the rental ends. Even if the attachment occurred without the renter's knowledge, the rental is still void. The renter's accident does not impose loss on the landlord, and a landlord's disclosed purpose in renting land can be legally significant, as with a seller of land. See there.
