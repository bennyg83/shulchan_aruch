Without specification. The Sema writes that "without specification" cannot be literal. Otherwise, after one day or less the lessor could remove the tenant merely by saying that he never intended a longer term, since land remains in the possession of its owner. He cites the Rama at the beginning of siman 212, who rules that one who says, "So and so shall dwell in this house," without fixing a time, may mean even a single hour, and suggests the same principle should apply to a lease. Rather, the Rambam means, as Maggid Mishneh explains, that the house was rented for a stated sum per month, in which case thirty days' advance notice is required. This applies to an unspecified duration, whereas if the lease is for a known term the tenant may be removed even on the very day the term ends, without notice. The Shach rejects the Sema's proof from siman 212. There the owner granted occupancy voluntarily, whereas one who rents a dwelling without specifying a period ordinarily does not rent for less than thirty days. Different legal categories need not be compared, since an unspecified loan is for thirty days while an unspecified borrowing is returnable immediately, as stated at the beginning of siman 341. See also the first chapter of Rosh Hashanah and Responsa Maharam Alshich, siman 17, and Rashdam, siman 286.
