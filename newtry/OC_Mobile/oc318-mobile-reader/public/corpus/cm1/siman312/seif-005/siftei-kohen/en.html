One who rents a house without specification may not evict him, etc. The Sema says that “without specification” cannot be literal, because otherwise the landlord could evict him after one day or less, and he cites the law of one who grants another permission to live in a house without fixing a time. That case is different because it is a gratuitous grant. In an ordinary rental for residence, people do not rent for less than thirty days. Different transactions have different default terms: an unspecified loan is thirty days, whereas an unspecified borrowing may be demanded immediately. The Gemara likewise states that one who rents a house “for this year” receives no less than thirty days, because a person does not trouble himself to rent a house for less than that. Rashi explains that an unspecified residential lease is for thirty days. Although this explanation appears in the initial stage of the discussion, the Gemara never withdraws from that premise. The Rambam and Mechaber also imply it. The Maggid Mishneh's statement that an unspecified lease means a monthly rent without a stated number of months merely excludes a lease whose number of months was fixed, which may end without thirty days' notice. It does not mean that the default thirty-day minimum disappears. Even if rent was fixed weekly, a tenant who received notice before entering must nevertheless be allowed the initial thirty days. See Responsa Maharam Alshikh, siman 117, and Maharshadam, siman 286.
