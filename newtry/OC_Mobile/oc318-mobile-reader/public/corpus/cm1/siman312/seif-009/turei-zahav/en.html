(Seif 17: But so long as it has not fallen) The Rama's wording implies that if the landlord wishes to provide another comparable house, he may remove the tenant and compel him to live there. This is certainly incorrect. Even to move him to a better house, the landlord may not remove him during the agreed term, as the Rama himself writes at the beginning of this siman when the landlord wishes to rebuild. The reason is also self-evident: a lease for its term is treated as a sale. If one sells an unspecified house and delivers a particular house, he cannot later say, “Leave this one and I will give you another”; the purchaser retains what he acquired. The same applies to a lease. The Rama derived his words here from the Nimukei Yosef at the end of HaShoel, who writes that according to the Rambam, if the tenant began living in the house before it fell, four cubits suffice afterward, whereas the Ritva holds that once he entered, the landlord may not substitute another house. Plainly, this discussion concerns a house that fell. The Rambam means that even though the tenant initially occupied a large house, after its collapse the landlord may give him a smaller house, as though no house had yet been delivered. The Ritva disagrees and requires another large house because the tenant had already acquired that standard of accommodation. Before the collapse, however, everyone agrees that the tenant may not be removed even to a better house, since he does not wish to burden himself with moving, as stated at the beginning of Bava Batra and cited above in siman 164. The Darkei Moshe understood the Nimukei Yosef to mean that according to the Rambam the landlord may remove him even before collapse and place him in a four-cubit house. This cannot be maintained. The Rambam expressly speaks only where the delivered house fell, and the Beit Yosef likewise derives the rule in that context. Nor can one compare this to a day laborer hired generally, whom the employer may assign to another task, for a worker is hired for the employer's benefit and subject to his instructions, whereas a tenant rents for his own benefit and certainly did not intend to be moved from house to house. It appears that the Rama, despite his great acumen, did not examine this point sufficiently.
