The homeowner takes a hesset oath. Although he admits part of the claim, the dispute concerns the right to occupy the premises and is therefore treated as a claim concerning land, for which only a hesset oath is imposed. A leasehold in land is treated as land regarding onaah, methods of acquisition, and other laws. The Maggid Mishneh explains the Rambam accordingly. Once the rental period has ended and the dispute is merely over money, the rent is an ordinary debt. But where one party claims a continuing right to reside and the other denies it, the claim is one to land and only a hesset oath applies. The Rambam's ruling here is the source demonstrating that rental of land is treated as land in this respect.

The Maggid Mishneh explains that this too follows from the principle that land remains in its owner's possession, and that the rule applies where the matter is not expected to become clarified. His words might imply that if witnesses are expected to establish the term, the tenant is not removed. That is not correct. The requirement that the matter not be expected to become clarified applies only to recovering produce or rent already consumed. For future possession, even where clarification may later become available, the tenant is not left in the property against the owner's presumption. This is clear from the laws below and from the Ritva. Thus, even if witnesses may know the term, the tenant is removed. The Maggid Mishneh's qualification should be understood as referring generally to money already consumed.
