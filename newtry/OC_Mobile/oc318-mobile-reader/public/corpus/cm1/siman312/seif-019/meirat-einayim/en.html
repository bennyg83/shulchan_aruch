There were two upper stories, one above the other. The case is where the landlord said, "I rent you this upper story that is above this house." Because he said "above this house," he cannot say that if it falls the tenancy simply disappears. The house is legally bound to support it. If he rented the uppermost story and it fell, the tenant certainly descends to live in the lower upper story, not in the ground floor house, because an upper story is also included in the general term "house," and the burden of proof rests on the claimant. But if he rented the lower upper story and it fell, the Gemara leaves unresolved whether the tenant accepted upon himself only one ascent and not two, or whether the general name "upper story" is the same regardless.
