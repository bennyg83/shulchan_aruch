Terumat HaDeshen, siman 320, discusses one who tells another, “Today or tomorrow I will sell you a particular article,” and promises to procure it for him. The seller did not have it, searched and failed to find it, then later exerted himself, found it, and wished to retract. Terumat HaDeshen ruled that he exerted himself with this intention, namely, to uphold his trustworthiness. He then writes: “One cannot say that the concern of maintaining his trustworthiness does not apply because he thought he would find it in another person’s possession and did not find it, so that he is not lacking in good faith. In any event, he promised to procure it for him. The buyer also said, ‘Do not disappoint me,’ and Shimon responded by assuring him through an act of acquisition. In such a case, he would certainly be lacking in good faith if he did not fulfill it.” This implies that only in such a case do we say that he was pleased to exert himself for that purpose. Why, then, did the Rama state the rule generally regarding one who sells something not in his possession because he thought it was available from another? One must say that here too the case is one where he first clearly promised to procure it. Another possibility is that here the buyer did not know at the time of the transaction that the seller did not possess it. Rather, the seller sold him a particular article on the assumption that he owned it. In such a case we say that he intended to place it before the buyer so that the buyer would not regard him as a liar. But in a case like that of Terumat HaDeshen, where the seller disclosed to the buyer that the article was not in his house and merely said that he expected to obtain it, and they made the transaction on that condition, we certainly do not say that he sold it with the intention of exerting himself to obtain it, since he did not clearly promise to procure it. This resembles the statement in the first chapter of Bava Metzia, page 16, that here the buyer does not rely upon him, concerning an inheritance, because who says that he will inherit from his father? All the more so here, for the law is more lenient concerning inheritance with respect to something not yet in existence, as stated in siman 241, seif katan 1 in the Sema. Siman 374 contains further distinctions regarding the obligation to uphold one’s trustworthiness, and they apply here as well.
