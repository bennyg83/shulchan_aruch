The proceeds of the wine when he sells the wine. See Responsa Rabbi Akiva Eiger, siman 141. He writes that the limitation applies specifically where one says, "when he sells it." If one merely speaks of "the proceeds of the wine," we interpret the wording as the wine for its proceeds, in accordance with Tosafot to Gittin 66a, who in their first answer treat "the money of the wine" as meaning "the wine for its money." The same appears from Shulchan Aruch, siman 257:13, as noted by Netivot HaMishpat there. Likewise, "the money of the house" can mean the house for its proceeds. However, "the money of the house rent" is considered something not yet in existence, as stated in Responsa Mahari ben Lev I, siman 58, and II, siman 43. Although one might think that this too could be read as the house for its rental proceeds, Rabbi Akiva Eiger distinguishes the cases based on Kesef Mishneh, Hilchot Mechirah 23:9. Future proceeds that are merely ancillary to the underlying property are not read as though the property itself was conveyed for those proceeds. Thus, where the proceeds are of equal importance to the wine or house, we may interpret the phrase as conveying the wine or house for its proceeds. Rental income, however, is subordinate to the house itself and therefore resembles the future fruits of a palm tree.
