Since the debtor has not yet acquired that property, such removal can be effective without a kinyan. But concerning assets the creditor has already acquired rights in, language of removal or "I have no claim or dealings" does not work without a kinyan. Rama in seif 5 says that one cannot waive something not yet in existence even though he has not yet acquired it because there the lien had already attached in principle. Sema. Taz writes that even where no lien had yet attached, a person may remove himself from future rights by saying that he does not wish to acquire them, but language of waiver is ineffective because waiver language properly applies only to an obligation already owed. See Beurim: the primary law follows the Sema. Where he has no present relationship to the lien at all, this resembles a husband attempting to remove himself from a woman's property before betrothal, which is ineffective. Where the debtor has already subjected after acquired property but has not yet acquired the specific asset, the creditor already has some connection to the lien, resembling a husband removing himself while she is only betrothed, which is effective. Once the debtor actually acquires the asset, removal no longer helps, resembling a husband removing himself from his wife's property after marriage, unless a kinyan is made. This applies specifically to weak language such as "I have no claim or dealings," which cannot remove existing acquired rights without a kinyan. Strong language such as, "I hereby waive my lien on this field," works even without a kinyan because one can waive the entire debt through such wording. Conversely, when waiving the debt itself, weak language of removal or "I have no dealings" is ineffective.
