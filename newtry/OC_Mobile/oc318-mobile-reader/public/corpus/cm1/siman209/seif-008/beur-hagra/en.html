Although, etc., but, etc. Because it is a condition, as explained above siman 207 seif 1. Compare Gittin 74a, 84a, Bava Metzia 94a, and many other places. The rule that “on condition” is a condition rather than a reservation is the conclusion at the beginning of Gittin chapter 9. See Tosafos there and Nazir 11a; Chullin 134a likewise rules according to the Mishnah that “except” is a reservation while “on condition” is a condition. The Rosh asks here why the Gemara challenges from the principle that one cannot transfer something not yet in existence, since one may condition a transaction upon such a future event. He answers that this Tanna must hold that “on condition” is a reservation, for otherwise the text should have said that if the condition is fulfilled the transaction stands and if not it fails. This wording here follows his analysis. The matter nevertheless requires study, because Chullin says that according to the view that “on condition” is a condition, the recipient may give the item to any Kohen and need not fulfill the condition in the literal manner. See Tosafos there and Nazir chapter 2. The Rosh himself notes this. Ramban discusses the matter at length in Bava Batra. In any event the Tur and Shulchan Aruch’s ruling is correct for ordinary conditions: fulfillment of the condition is required, as Tosafos and Rosh state.
Although, etc., nevertheless, etc. A condition may be made dependent on something not yet in existence, provided that future condition is eventually fulfilled; then the original acquisition takes effect. Examples include Yoma chapter 1, “This is your get on condition that you die; on condition that your fellow dies,” Gittin chapter 7, “if I die,” “if I do not come,” Bava Metzia chapter 3, “as if saying when it is stolen and you choose to pay me,” Eruvin chapter 3, “if rain falls today,” and Kiddushin, “on condition that I speak to the ruler,” “on condition that I work for you,” and many conditions involving intangible matters or matters not presently under one’s control.

Likewise to remove oneself, etc. As stated at the beginning of HaKosev: “I have no claim or dealings, etc.,” with respect to her produce and the produce of her produce, during her lifetime and after her death. According to the second opinion at the end of siman 203 there would be no proof from here, but the view of all the poskim is clearly unlike that second opinion. Its proof from gift and inheritance is not valid, because there the reason is that the gift applies to both, and indeed the source implies the reverse. The Yerushalmi asks whether a husband can renounce future property that will fall to his wife and whether one can stipulate concerning something not yet in one’s possession. Rosh and Ran in Ramban’s name explain that the Yerushalmi deals with a waiver written after marriage, when the husband’s hand is already like hers.

Some say that although, etc. As stated above siman 176 seif 3 in the gloss, some say partners can bind themselves by mutual agreement. See Beer HaGolah there. This may work even without a formal kinyan because of the benefit they receive by agreeing with and relying upon one another, as with brothers who divide by lottery in Bava Batra chapter 7, marriage commitments in Kesubos chapter 12, a guarantor in Bava Batra chapter 2, and a bailee at the end of Bava Metzia chapter 7.
