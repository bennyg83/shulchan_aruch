A person cannot transfer ownership. He can, however, obligate himself concerning something not yet in existence, as explained in siman 60 (Sema).
With a gift of a shechiv mera. One might have thought that it should take effect so that his mind not become distressed; therefore this teaches otherwise (Sema).
“Give what she will bear” refers to a shechiv mera who told his sons to give it (Sema).
The maidservant is pregnant. Since the fetus is not yet independently in existence, it is unlike fruits that have already formed, which are in existence and merely continue to grow, and are therefore regarded as having come into the world (Sema). See Beurim: specifically where he sold it in a manner that the offspring is to become a fully viable offspring, meaning “when she gives birth,” it is something not yet in existence. But where he immediately sold the fetus itself, so that if the animal dies or is slaughtered the flesh of the fetus will belong to the buyer, it is considered something already in existence, comparable to transferring one of the animal's limbs.
And he seized the fruits. This means before the seller retracted; after he retracted, later seizure is ineffective (Sema). This applies specifically where the seller knew that the buyer had seized them and remained silent, because the effectiveness of seizure here is based on mistaken waiver and not on doubt (Shach). The Sema in siman 66 disagrees. See Beurim: seizure is effective specifically where the transaction was through money, but if it was through kinyan sudar, where even if the object were already in existence a transfer to take effect later would fail because the sudar has already returned to its owner, seizure is likewise ineffective. Seizure helps only where there was an otherwise valid kinyan and the sole defect was that the subject matter did not yet exist.
And he delivered the document to him. This means after the subject matter came into existence, because possession of a document is like seizure of the object. For this reason, presentation of a document is considered “here it is” in siman 87 (Sema). The Taz writes that even if he delivered the document at the time of sale and the document remained in the buyer's possession after the subject matter came into existence, it is effective. See Beurim: delivery of the document at the time of sale is effective only in the sale of a debt instrument, because where he wrote him a bill of sale and also delivered the debt instrument, the buyer no longer needs to make a claim against the seller, only against the debtor; therefore it is effective. But where he sold him a field to take effect when he would acquire it, even though the buyer still holds the document after the seller acquires the field, it is ineffective, since he must still demand the field from the seller. Holding a document is considered seizure only where he need not make a claim against the seller. This is explicit in the Ittur. If, after the subject matter came into existence, the seller gave him a pledge to secure delivery of the object, this is like seizure and he must deliver it (Rivash, siman 335; this is also the view of the Rosh cited by the Sema in siman 207, seif katan 32). The Baal HaTerumot, cited by the Beit Yosef in this siman, disagrees and maintains that he must specifically seize the very item sold to him; a pledge does not suffice.
He acquires immediately. This is so even though the fruits have not yet emerged, because since the kinyan takes effect upon the tree, which is existing property, the kinyan also takes effect upon the fruits that will emerge from it (Sema).
The value of the wine. See Beurim. If he transfers the wine for its proceeds, the transfer is effective, because even where something does not grow from the body of the existing property, such as the airspace of a ruin for what will enter it, a trap for fish, or wine for its proceeds, the transfer is effective.
That he should acquire it when it comes into the world, he acquires it. The principal ruling follows those who disagree and hold that he does not acquire it (Taz).
He can create a lien upon it, and there are those who disagree. Even those who disagree concede that one can create a lien upon after acquired property. Their disagreement concerns whether he can retract the lien before acquiring the property and then, after acquiring it, sell it. Those who disagree maintain that the retraction is effective. The principal ruling follows the first opinion, that he cannot retract a lien on after acquired property even before acquiring it (Sema and Shach).
Together with something not yet in existence. See Hidushim above, siman 203, seif katan 23, for what was written on this matter.
He must fulfill his oath. Beit din does not descend to his property; rather, he is compelled under ban to fulfill his oath (Taz).
So too he cannot waive it. For example, if he waived whatever his fellow would later become obligated to him, and afterward his fellow obligated himself to him, the waiver that preceded the obligation is ineffective. See above, seif katan 28, for what was written on this matter.
