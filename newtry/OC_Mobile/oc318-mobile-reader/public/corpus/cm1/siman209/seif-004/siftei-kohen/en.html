Nevertheless, he may impose a lien upon it, while some disagree. The Ir Shushan writes that the dissenting view appears reasonable because the lien, too, has no present object upon which to take effect. This does not appear correct to me. Bava Batra 157 expressly asks whether a lien upon property acquired in the future is effective, even according to the Sages who hold that one cannot transfer something not yet in existence. Rashbam explains that the question is whether a lien differs from an acquisition, and the Gemara concludes that it does. All the poskim rule accordingly, as explained above at the beginning of siman 112. Thus a lien is stronger than an acquisition. The dissenters here must therefore agree that the lien takes effect so long as the obligor has not retracted; they hold only that he may retract before the property comes into existence. This is explicit in the cited Gemara and is how the Sema, seif katan 17, explains it. The practical consequence concerns purchasers. Above in siman 112 I concluded that the first view is primary, unlike the dissenters. See Responsa Mahari ibn Lev, part 2, simanim 23 and 37, and Yoreh Deah 228:4.
