A person cannot transfer something that has not yet come into existence, whether by sale, a healthy person's gift, or a deathly ill person's gift. Thus, statements such as, 'Whatever this field produces is sold to you,' 'Whatever this tree produces is given to you,' 'Give whatever this animal gives birth to to so-and-so,' or, 'Whatever my cow or maidservant gives birth to is sold or given to you,' are ineffective. Even if the cow or maidservant is already pregnant, the recipient acquires nothing. Rama: With fruit of a tree, once the fruit has begun to form it is considered to have come into existence. Either party may withdraw even after the animal or maidservant gives birth or after the fruit grows and comes into existence. If the purchaser took possession of the produce first, it is not removed from him. Rama: The same applies to other things not yet in existence. Even if he did not physically seize them, but a deed was written and delivered to him, delivery of the deed is treated like possession. If, however, one sells the tree for its fruit, or a cow or maidservant for the offspring, the transfer is effective immediately and neither may withdraw. Rama: If one says that another shall acquire the money received when wine is later sold, this is a thing not yet in existence. Some say these rules apply only to an attempted present transfer. If he says that the recipient shall acquire it when it comes into existence, the acquisition is effective then, although it does not yet exist now. Others disagree. Although a person cannot transfer something not yet in existence, some say he can create a lien upon it; others disagree. He can, however, personally obligate himself to provide it, as explained in siman 60, seif 6. If one transfers something not yet in existence together with something that exists, some compare it to an attempted transfer to a person and a donkey, as explained at the end of siman 210. If he swore to uphold the transaction, he must fulfill his oath, but the object itself is not acquired by the kinyan. Therefore, if the oath can no longer be enforced, such as after his death, the kinyan is ineffective. The same applies if he obtains release from the oath. Just as a person cannot transfer something not yet in existence, he cannot waive a claim not yet in existence.
