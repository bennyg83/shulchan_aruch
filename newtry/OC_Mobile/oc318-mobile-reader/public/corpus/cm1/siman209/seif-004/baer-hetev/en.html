That have blossomed. The reason is that fruit that has already begun to form exists in the world and grows automatically, so it is regarded as something already in existence. This is unlike a fetus in the womb of a cow or maidservant, which is not yet independently present. Ibid.
That it will give birth. According to the opinion that a person can transfer something not yet in existence, this applies only if he does not retract before it comes into existence. It follows that according to the opinion that one cannot transfer such an item, which is the halachah, he may retract even after it comes into existence. So writes the Sema. This is obvious and explicit in the Gemara, for Rav concedes only where the recipient seized and consumed the produce, because of waiver. Shach.

The deed. After the property comes into existence, the deed is effective as though the item had come into his hand. Hagahot Maimoniyot explicitly writes this. See also the beginning of siman 87 in the gloss, where a deed is considered equivalent to immediate tender. Thus far the Sema. The Taz writes that there is no proof from Hagahot Maimoniyot, whose wording is: 'Since he sold and wrote a deed, it is as though he seized.' This implies that the deed was written at the time of the sale. This is also evident in siman 210 in the Beit Yosef, which cites the Rif in the name of the Itur and discusses a deed written at the time of sale. Perhaps the Sema means only that the deed must at least be in his possession when the property comes into existence.
