How so, what, etc. Bava Batra there and Kiddushin chapter 3, 62, regarding a fetus. See the Rambam’s explanation. The Yerushalmi, Bava Batra chapter 3, Demai chapter 6, and Kiddushin chapter 1, states: one who sells future tithes to another has done nothing; one who sells the future child of his maidservant has done nothing; one who sells the fetus of his animal has done nothing; one who sells the airspace of his ruin has done nothing. Instead, he may sell the field while reserving its tithes, sell the maidservant while reserving her offspring, sell the animal while reserving its fetus, or sell the ruin while reserving its airspace. If he swore, etc. Kiddushin discusses a promise “after your husband dies,” which concerns something not yet in existence. Yaakov nevertheless made Yosef swear, and Yosef made the children of Israel swear; likewise “and I shall lie with my fathers,” etc. Yaakov also said, “and all that You will give me, etc.” The obligation in such cases arises from the oath alone. This is the meaning of “nevertheless he is not, etc.”
Just as one cannot, etc. Yerushalmi, Kesubos chapter 3, halachah 7, discusses an orphan girl who became betrothed, etc. Rabbi Yochanan says Rabbi Elazar follows Rabbi Akiva his teacher, who holds that the fine belongs to her. The Yerushalmi asks whether this would mean that even a seduced girl can waive it. It answers that a person may waive something not yet his in the case of an orphan, but can one waive something not yet within his legal possession? Rabbi Bun says that the fine does not exist from the outset, as shown by the Mishnah distinguishing whether she stood in judgment before the father died. See seif 5 and Ran there. In a responsum, Ran proves from Bava Batra 171b, after Rav Safra instructed his scribe, etc., that if future rights could be waived, there would be no need for the complicated form there; one could simply write a release effective from today forward. See responsum siman 23.
