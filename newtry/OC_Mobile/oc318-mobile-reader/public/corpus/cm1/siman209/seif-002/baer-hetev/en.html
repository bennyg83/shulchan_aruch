Like one gambling. This does not mean that the Rambam compares it to the law of gambling, for the Rambam nowhere explains that gambling is an asmachta, and there would be no reason to assume the gambling case is simpler than this one. Rather, he means that this is not truly a sale at all but resembles a person playing a game of chance, so the purchaser does not seriously rely on the transaction. This is why he says, 'This is nothing but...' in the language of negation, rather than 'he is like one gambling.' There is also no difficulty from the rule that gambling is not an asmachta, because that case has a separate rationale, as the Tur writes in siman 207, seif 17. Here, by contrast, the person may have assumed the outcome would be a certain way according to his estimate. We therefore do not need the Sema's distinction. See there carefully. See Responsa Mabit, part 2, siman 78. Thus far the Shach. As for why we do not say here that it is not an asmachta because it is within his control, as stated in siman 207, seif 11, see the Taz's distinction.
Who exchanged. A similar ruling appears in Responsa Maharashdam, siman 123, concerning one who sold his ship after it had sunk. See there. This requires study. Shach.
