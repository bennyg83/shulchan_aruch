Seif 4. For a thing that has not yet come into existence. Bnei Chayei on siman 209 cites Maharam Alshich that even where three acts of labor remain, for purposes of sale the item may still be considered something that has come into existence. I saw similarly in his responsum, siman 136, concerning sale of a garment. Perhaps he would nevertheless agree regarding a product such as distilled liquor, that although the grain already exists, the liquor itself is a new product that was not yet fit to be the object of a sale before it was made. Perhaps where three acts of labor remain in such a case it is still treated as something that has not come into existence. Possibly Maharam Galanti, cited by Bnei Chayei there, was speaking of such a case. This requires careful study. From the statement of the Sages that after fruit has reached the stage of chanatah it is considered to have come into existence, one might have inferred that only one remaining process that occurs naturally is sufficient. Yet in the chapter Eizehu Neshech the conclusion is that there is no distinction between work performed by human hands and processes that occur by themselves. Still, the implication may be that only one remaining act, comparable to chanatah, is treated as already in existence, not three acts. Perhaps there too the fruit was not yet fit to be sold at all and the case resembles the distilled liquor discussed above. This requires further study.
