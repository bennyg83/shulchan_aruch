This is merely like one who gambles. This is the Rambam's language, and the Tur cites it. The Rambam follows his own view that gambling is asmakhta and involves theft. The Tur and Rama hold that ordinary gambling is not asmakhta because the parties fully resolve to transfer to one another, as explained in siman 207:13. Even so, they agree with the Rambam's practical ruling here. In ordinary gambling neither party knows in advance which side will prevail, and therefore each fully commits himself in order to acquire the other's stake. Here the seller knows what he is selling while the buyer does not know what he is buying. The buyer therefore never fully resolves to acquire. Thus the Tur copies the Rambam's language and agrees with his ruling but not necessarily with his rationale. For the same reason Rama adds no dissent here.
Two people who exchanged, etc. See Terumat HaDeshen siman 320 concerning two people who gave each other solemn hand pledges and accepted charitable penalties to exchange everything each possessed. He ruled that they must fulfill the arrangement unless they mutually waive it. Darkei Moshe says that this conflicts with Rivash's ruling cited here, and Rama records both opinions at the end of siman 203 without deciding. Here Rama does not intend to decide either. He merely says that the opinion holding that the exchange kinyan is ineffective finds some support from the present law, where one says, "Everything in this house is sold to you for such and such." It appears, however, that even those who uphold the reciprocal exchange would agree that the present sale of "everything in this house" is ineffective. There one party knows what is in his own house while the buyer does not, so the buyer does not fully resolve to acquire. In a reciprocal exchange, neither party knows what is in the other's house. Because each wishes to acquire the other's property, each fully resolves to transfer his own, and neither has a specific expected outcome upon which to rely. It therefore resembles gambling according to Ri and the Rosh. Terumat HaDeshen says "unless they mutually waive it." Their waiver is effective to cancel the charitable penalty because neither party had an already fixed debt to charity. If they fulfill the exchange, no charitable debt ever arises, so they can say, "Let it be treated as though we exchanged." This differs from one who has already incurred a definite obligation, for example one who insults another and thereby owes five gold coins under a communal enactment to the offended party, and then says before the collector that the money should go to charity. After that declaration he cannot release the offender from the charity obligation, as the Rosh rules and the Mechaber states in Yoreh Deah siman 258:9.
