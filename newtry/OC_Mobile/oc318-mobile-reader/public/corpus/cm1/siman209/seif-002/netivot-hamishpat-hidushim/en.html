Like gambling. Even according to our ruling in siman 207:13 that gambling can create acquisition, that case differs because both parties do not know the outcome and we say that since each wants the possibility of acquiring, each correspondingly resolves to transfer. Here the seller knows and the purchaser does not. Sema. According to this rationale, if the seller also does not know what kind of merchandise he has in the house, the acquisition should be valid. Taz writes this. The Shach writes that here everyone agrees it is asmachta in every case, because perhaps the party simply miscalculated his estimate.
Two people who exchanged. At the end of siman 203 Rama cites two opinions on this. Here he does not intend to decide the issue, but merely explains the rationale of the opinion that the exchange is ineffective: it resembles saying, "Everything in this house is sold to you." Even those who validate an exchange agree that saying, "Everything in this house is sold to you," is ineffective, because the seller knows what is there while the purchaser does not, so the rationale that each wishes to acquire and therefore resolves to transfer does not apply. Sema. The Shach writes that even in a mutual exchange everyone agrees the acquisition is ineffective despite neither knowing the precise value, as he wrote in the preceding seif katan. Terumat HaDeshen, who validates an exchange, speaks of a transaction performed through a mode of acquisition that is effective, such as before an eminent beit din. The Sema further writes concerning two people who exchanged promises and each obligated himself to pay a charity penalty if he reneged. If afterward they mutually waive the exchange, both are exempt from the charity penalty. Although asmachta does not apply to charity, since they could have completed the exchange and thereby avoided the penalty, they can likewise mutually declare the exchange as effectively completed and thereby cancel the penalty. This differs from one who insulted another, became liable to pay five gold coins, and said before the charity collectors that the five gold coins should go to charity. He can no longer waive the offender's liability to charity, as stated in Yoreh Deah siman 258.
