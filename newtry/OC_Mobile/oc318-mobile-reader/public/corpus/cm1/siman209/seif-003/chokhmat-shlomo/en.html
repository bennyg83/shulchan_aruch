Seif 4. Even after the cow gives birth. Note: Regarding whether an acquisition of a fetus takes effect once the pregnancy is recognizable, see the later authorities and Ketzot HaChoshen. I have already written at length that the Rambam is correct that a recognizable fetus is considered something already in existence, including with respect to sale. See my Even HaEzer, sixth edition, siman 88, responsum to Kolomyya. I now see a clear proof from Bechorot 56a. A Tanna taught before Rav that an animal given as a harlot's payment enters the pen for tithing if the giver later reacquires it from her. The Gemara asks that it should be disqualified as purchased property, and answers with Rav Asi in the name of Rabbi Yochanan that one who purchases ten fetuses in their mothers' wombs may nevertheless bring them into the pen for tithing, because the verse makes the exemption depend upon the time of actual tithing. This proves that a fetus in its mother's womb can be sold. If the case meant only that one bought the mother for the sake of her fetus, the offspring itself would not be "purchased" property, and no special verse would be needed. Nor can the verse be needed merely for the law of an animal too young to be tithed, because the Gemara expressly gives the verse as the reason why purchased fetuses are included. Moreover, the exclusion of purchased animals is learned from the comparison to one's sons. But if buying the mother for her fetus were sufficient, even the analogy to sons would not fit, for marriage itself is also called an acquisition. We must therefore be dealing with a direct sale of the fetus itself, which can take effect once the pregnancy is recognizable. The same is especially clear according to Rabbi Shimon ben Yehudah, who excludes an animal lacking the required age and therefore must use the verse specifically for a fetus in the womb. Thus, once the pregnancy is recognizable, the fetus is capable of acquisition. This is a clear proof, and it has practical relevance also to the law of firstborn animals, where such a fetus would be exempt because it was purchased.
There, seif 4, gloss: When it comes into existence, etc.; and some disagree. See my responsum in Yoreh Deah, laws of firstborn, siman 320, fifth edition, responsum 205, where I explained why in siman 210 the Rama rules against the dissenting opinion while here he does not decide the matter. I also wrote there that even if a recognizable fetus is still technically treated as something not yet in existence, if one says "when it comes into existence" the transfer is effective according to all opinions. This also resolves an apparent contradiction in the Rambam and Shulchan Aruch in the laws of Kiddushin.
