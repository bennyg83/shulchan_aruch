He is obligated to purchase it, etc. See the Sema, seif katan 23, who writes that the preceding seif rules that an item outside the seller's possession is not acquired because there the market price had not yet been established at the fixed rate, and that the Maggid Mishneh says likewise. This does not appear correct. The preceding seif means only that no acquisition is completed and either party may retract. Here, however, one who retracts is nevertheless subject to Mi Shepara. Even where the item is not readily available, it may be that Mi Shepara applies. The mention here that the market price had been established is because otherwise the arrangement would be prohibited, and it is obvious that Mi Shepara does not apply to a prohibited transaction, as the Rama states immediately afterward. I examined the Maggid Mishneh and found no implication supporting the Sema. I later saw that Bedek HaBayit implies my view, stating that where the price has not been established it is not merely a matter of not acquiring, but is also prohibited because of interest. This is clear.
