One who contracts at the market rate. Although seif 5 rules that he does not acquire, there the distinction is that once a market price exists and the goods are commonly available, they are considered within the seller's effective reach. According to the Sema, if there were a complete acquisition it would therefore fully transfer, and seif 5 concerns where the market price had not yet emerged, so there is not even the "Who exacted payment" curse. The Shach disagrees and holds that seif 5 also concerns a case where the market price has emerged, but there the discussion is whether full acquisition occurs, and the answer is that even a complete kinyan does not fully acquire, only the "Who exacted payment" curse applies. This seif discusses only that curse. What the Mechaber means by saying it applies once the market price has emerged even if goods are not immediately available is as follows. See Beurim: the primary explanation is that seif 5 concerns contracting for a specifically identified item, such as "this wine." That item is genuinely not in the seller's possession, because saying that similar wine is commonly available does not help when the purchaser specifically insisted on this particular wine. Who knows whether anyone will sell that wine to the seller? Here the case concerns ordinary unspecified wine that is commonly available in the market, and therefore it is treated as within his effective possession. The primary law follows the Shach that even a complete kinyan does not fully acquire when contracting at the market rate, but only creates the "Who exacted payment" curse. The same applies when contracting for produce on the basis of the leniency that the seller already possesses produce. The purchaser still acquires only for purposes of that curse, because he has no full property acquisition in those particular wheat kernels. The seller may eat them and later supply others.
