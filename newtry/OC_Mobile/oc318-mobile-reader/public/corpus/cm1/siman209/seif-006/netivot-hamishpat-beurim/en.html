One who fixes a price according to the market. See Sema, seif katan 23, and Shach, seif katan 13. The issue depends on the two answers of Kesef Mishneh concerning agav acquisition of property not presently in one’s possession. According to one answer, an established market price makes the goods sufficiently obtainable even for full acquisition, including agav land. According to the other, it suffices only for mi shepara. There is some proof that a forward transaction at the market rate creates only mi shepara. According to the view in siman 198 that money acquires movable property fully where the concern “your wheat burned in the attic” is absent, a forward purchase at the market rate should also be a full acquisition, because that concern is absent. Yet the law does not say so. This indicates that such a forward transaction itself creates only mi shepara. The same appears true where forward pricing is permitted because the seller already has equivalent goods. Tosafot explain that the permission rests on the fact that the seller may consume or sell his own goods and later provide others, and if his goods are destroyed he must still deliver replacements. The buyer therefore has no full acquisition in the specific wheat presently owned by the seller. If he had full acquisition, even money should acquire because the usual fire concern is absent. Sema’s question from seif 5 is also not difficult. Forward pricing at the market rate works for unspecified wine, which is readily available. It does not work for “this wine,” because there is no reason to assume the owner will sell that particular wine to him. A specific item not presently controlled by the seller is not made transferable merely because similar items exist in the market.
