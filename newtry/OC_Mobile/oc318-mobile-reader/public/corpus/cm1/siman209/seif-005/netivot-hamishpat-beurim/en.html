Except that it is not in his possession. See Shach, seif katan 9, who cites Beit Yosef in the name of the Ittur. Although Beit Yosef and the Rama have already ruled against the Ittur, and similarly in siman 216 concerning unspecified palm trees the seller is not obligated to go purchase palm trees, and the Shach here in seif katan 12 also appears to accept that ruling, the Shach nevertheless cites the Ittur here for a practical difference if the seller exerted himself and acquired the item. If neither party knew that the seller did not own it, there is no issue of failing to keep one's word, because the rationale that he should act faithfully does not apply, and he need not complete the sale.
Nevertheless, if he exerted himself. See Taz, who writes that this applies only if the buyer did not know. This is the principal ruling. Even regarding a stolen field, siman 374 states that if the buyer knew it was stolen, we do not say that the seller exerted himself on the understanding of maintaining the buyer's purchase. The Taz correctly learns the present case from there. It also appears that this entire reasoning applies only to a specific identified object, such as particular pearls. With grain and similar goods in which this seller regularly trades, one cannot say that he acquired the goods specifically in order to fulfill this particular sale.
