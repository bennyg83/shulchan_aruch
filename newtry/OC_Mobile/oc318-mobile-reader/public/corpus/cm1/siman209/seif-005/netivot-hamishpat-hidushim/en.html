If the seller exerted himself. This applies specifically where the seller knew he did not presently own the item while the purchaser did not know. Since the seller sold it as though it were available, we say he exerted himself intending that the sale should stand so as to preserve his credibility. But if the purchaser also knew that the seller did not own it, there is no rationale that the seller worked to preserve credibility, unless he explicitly promised that he would obtain the item for him. In that case preserving credibility does apply. Taz. If the seller also did not know he lacked the item, the credibility rationale does not apply and the purchaser does not acquire. See Beurim: the rationale that he exerted himself to preserve credibility applies only to a specifically identified item. With grain and similar goods that this seller regularly trades in, it does not apply, and the purchaser does not acquire.
