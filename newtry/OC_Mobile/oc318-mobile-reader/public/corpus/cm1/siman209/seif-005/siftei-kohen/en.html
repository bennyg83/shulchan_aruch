But it is not within his possession, etc. The Beit Yosef cites the Baal HaItur: this resembles one who sells an item on the assumption that he owns it and it is then found that he does not; he is obligated to provide the purchaser with the item sold. Responsa Maharashdam, siman 123, suggests that this may apply specifically where the seller knew and the buyer did not, so that the buyer assumed the object was certainly in the seller's possession. Where neither party knew, however, the buyer may not have paid on that understanding. See there further.
It is not within his possession. This appears to mean that it is entirely outside his legal control, such as where one sells another person's property, even though the item already exists. If, however, he owns a deposit held by another and sells it, the purchaser acquires it. Proof follows from the Rambam, who holds in chapter 3 of Hilchot Sheluchin that one may authorize another only over property that he could transfer, as explained above in simanim 122 and 123. Nevertheless, the Rambam writes there that one may authorize and transfer a deposit held by another, and the Mechaber rules likewise at the beginning of siman 123. Tosafot in Bava Kamma 70a also states this explicitly. A further proof is that the laws of acquisition and consecration are parallel, as explained below in siman 212, and a deposit whose custodian has not denied it may be consecrated, as the poskim, Rambam, and Mechaber state in Yoreh Deah 258:7.
