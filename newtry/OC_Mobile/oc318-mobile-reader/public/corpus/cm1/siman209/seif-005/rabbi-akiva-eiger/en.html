[Baer HaGolah, letter 9] And it appears to me that they learned it. Note: In my opinion, there is no proof from there. It may be said that a fetus is merely like a piece of flesh, or part of the mother according to the view that a fetus is considered part of its mother. With respect to kiddushin, it is called something not yet in existence because a fetus, being like a piece of flesh, is not an entity upon which kiddushin can take effect. With respect to sale, however, it may be regarded as already in existence, like one who sells a piece of meat. It appears to me, however, that this ruling follows from what Tosafot write in Bekhorot 3b, s.v. "deka mafka," in the name of Rabbeinu Tam. See Shaar HaMelech, Hilchot Nedarim, chapter 12, halachah 10, and Machaneh Ephraim, Hilchot Mechirah, laws of something not yet in existence, siman 1.
