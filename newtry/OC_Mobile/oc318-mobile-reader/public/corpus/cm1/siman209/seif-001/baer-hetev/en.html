Overreaching. This applies even if the item has no known market price, contrary to the Beit Yosef. The Bach writes likewise. See Responsa Rashach, vol. 2, siman 62. Shach. If the seller demanded 200 and the buyer offered 100, and the buyer said, "Whatever others offer you, I will give you," and then performed meshichah, he acquires because the seller had demanded a specific amount. But if the seller demanded no specific amount, the subject of the agreement is not sufficiently defined and the buyer does not acquire. Tumat Yesharim, siman 196. If the seller then found someone willing to pay more than his original demand, and the buyer had stipulated that if someone offered more he would pay that price, the author there investigated whether the buyer is obligated to pay the addition, but we do not have his conclusion. With a gift, the species of the property need not be specified. Lechem Rav, siman 201. Partners in a house, one of whom sold his share to another before division, effect a valid sale. Rashach, vol. 1, siman 115; Rama MiPano, siman 88. Knesset HaGedolah writes that he found the same in a manuscript responsum of the Rashba, Hilchot Karkaot, siman 65. See Be'er Sheva, simanim 125 and 183. One who gives a sum of money to Reuven in exchange for Reuven's future inheritance does not acquire it. Maharam Galanti, siman 86.
