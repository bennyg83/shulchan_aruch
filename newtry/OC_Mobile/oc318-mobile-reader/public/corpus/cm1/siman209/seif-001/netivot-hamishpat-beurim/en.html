Beit Yosef asks from the law concerning what the goats will milk, where the transaction is permitted even if the disparity amounts to ona'ah, and answers with difficulty. There is a textual error in the Taz. His words beginning, "but we, who rule concerning one who plays dice," do not relate to the preceding discussion of ona'ah, because regarding ona'ah there is no reason to say that the parties' desire to acquire causes full consent. That reasoning pertains only to asmachta. Those words of the Taz should be attached to the Mechaber's law concerning dice, and the text should read substantially as follows: "This applies specifically according to the Rambam, but we rule like Rabbeinu Tam that because each wishes to acquire, he fully resolves to convey, as the Rama writes in siman 207:13. So too here, when neither seller nor buyer knows the eventual result, we say that because each desires the acquisition, he fully resolves to convey." That is the principal point until the discussion of drawing into an alley. In my humble opinion, however, there is no difficulty at all from the case of the goats' future milk. That is property not yet in existence and there is no binding acquisition in any event. The Shitah Mekubetzet in Bava Metzia raises this point and explains that the Gemara's principal teaching is merely that if the seller wishes to fulfill his word, there is no prohibition of interest. The question why the seller should be able to retract because of ona'ah therefore falls away, since he can already retract because the subject matter did not yet exist. The Gemara teaches only that if he nevertheless chooses to give it, there is no interest violation. Moreover, even if a valid kinyan existed, the ona'ah difficulty would not arise. The case concerns one who advances money and receives a reduced price because of the advance, as explained in Yoreh Deah 173. In such transactions ordinary ona'ah rules do not apply in the same manner. When one fixes a price for produce on the basis that the seller already has the produce, it is permissible even to sell produce worth twelve for ten despite the percentage disparity. Likewise, in a mortgage arrangement with deduction of rent, one may rent from the borrower a house worth one hundred for one dinar without a claim of ona'ah, because it is understood that the reduction results from the advance of money. This form of interest, where the lender may also bear a possible loss and the arrangement is structured as commerce, was permitted by the Sages.
