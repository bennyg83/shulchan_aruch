Of the value of a perutah. See Baer Heitev. Urim VeTumim writes that Shitah Mekubetzet to Bava Metzia cites the Ritva that if the claimant demands a specific object that is presently in existence, beit din will address the claim even if its value is less than a perutah. The rule that the court does not attend to a claim of less than a perutah applies only where monetary payment is demanded. It follows that even produce or money, when claimed as the specific property itself, is included. Birkei Yosef, letter 3, likewise cites this. Birkei Yosef, letter 6, further raises a doubt where two partners sue one person who owes their partnership property worth one perutah. Does beit din entertain the claim? Perhaps, since each partner's individual share is only half a perutah, the court does not entertain it, just as if two unrelated people each claim half a perutah from one person. Or perhaps partnership property is one fund and the partners are treated as one person, so the court does entertain the claim. He suggests a proof from Responsa Maharit, Choshen Mishpat, siman 105, regarding the partners' oath, which requires a claim of two silver maah: if all the partners were present and jointly claimed two silver maah from one of them, he would be required to swear, and the same should apply here. He then notes a difficulty with Maharit. See below, siman 93, seif 1, seif katan 4, where I cite Maharit at length and also Birkei Yosef's objection. According to what I wrote there in Nachalat Tzvi to resolve the matter, that in my opinion Maharit spoke only regarding the requirement of two silver maah, Birkei Yosef's proposed ruling here would not follow. The matter requires study.
