(Anthology) If they already began adjudicating, etc. The Gemara's phrase 'to exclude Rav Ketina' must refer to its initial assumption. Otherwise, why does the Gemara ask, 'What, then, does our Tanna exclude?' It could answer that the Tanna follows Rav Ketina, just as it answers concerning overreaching. See Tosafot there, s.v. veTanna. That passage certainly follows the conclusion. Thus, 'to exclude' means to exclude what had initially been understood in Rav Ketina's ruling. Tosafot explain it differently there.
(Anthology) If they already began adjudicating, etc. This is the Rambam's view; see Tosafot there, s.v. veTanna. The Rosh, however, rules unlike Rav Ketina, explaining that the Gemara's phrase 'to exclude Rav Ketina' applies even according to the conclusion. The Tur follows his own position. In the dispute between Rav Kahana and Levi concerning overreaching of less than a perutah, he rules like Levi, as stated above in siman 227:5. He explains that the Mishnah does not disagree with Levi, since the matter is included in theft, just as the Gemara answers regarding delay of judgment. Therefore, the Gemara does not challenge Levi from the Mishnah. This resolves Tosafot's difficulty, because the Gemara could not have answered that the Tanna follows Rav Ketina, since that would make Levi disagree with the Mishnah. The Rambam follows his own view that Levi does disagree with the Mishnah regarding overreaching, as explained there. Tosafot's question therefore remains against him, and he must adopt the interpretation stated above.
