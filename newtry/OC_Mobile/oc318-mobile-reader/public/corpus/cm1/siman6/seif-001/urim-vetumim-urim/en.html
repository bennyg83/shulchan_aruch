They complete their judgment. This applies even if the first case has already ended with a judgment requiring the defendant to pay one perutah, and the defendant then raises a counterclaim for half a perutah against the plaintiff. The court addresses it because the two matters function as one accounting: if the defendant prevails, the amount is deducted from what he owes. Kesef Mishneh likewise holds that if the first claim was a full perutah and the plaintiff later makes a claim of less than a perutah, the court can still address it because it is already sitting in the matter. Bach disagrees. Or Zarua cites Ritva that even if the judges began because they thought the claim was worth a perutah and only afterward discovered it was less, they complete the case. If one claimed a perutah and the defendant returned half, the court addresses the remaining half-perutah. Rosh cites Rabbi Meir HaLevi Abulafia similarly in the first chapter of Bava Kamma.
Even for less than a perutah. Rosh and Tur disagree, and Sema follows them. Bach agrees with Mechaber, and Tosafot according to Maharsha's and Maharam's reading appears likewise to support Mechaber. Other textual versions, including Or Zarua, align Tosafot with Rosh. Had Tosafot clearly disagreed, Rosh would likely have cited them. Nevertheless, the primary halachah follows Mechaber and Bach.
