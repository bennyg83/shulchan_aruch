If Reuven holds a promissory note against Shimon, and Shimon holds one against his brother Levi, and Reuven comes to collect from Levi, while Levi claims, 'My brother did me a kindness and lent me the money to support me, stipulating that he would not compel me to pay until I wished,' then even if beit din recognizes some indication that his claim is true, Reuven collects from him unless this condition is explicit in the document. {Rama: If a person holds a deposit belonging to a non-Jew or to a Jewish apostate, and they owe others who seek to remove their property from the custodian, while the custodian says he fears releasing it lest the non-Jew or apostate later return and collect from him, the law follows the custodian. See Beit Yosef, siman 105, citing Responsa Maimoniyot, Laws of Damages, responsum 7, and notes 40 and 45.}
