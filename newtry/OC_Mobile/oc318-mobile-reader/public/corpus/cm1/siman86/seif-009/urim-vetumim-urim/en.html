I concluded: if Reuven lent Shimon and Shimon lent a gentile, the gentile is certainly subject to Reuven through Rabbi Natan's lien. If Reuven lent a gentile, and that gentile lent Shimon, then if the gentile's loan to Shimon preceded Reuven's loan to the gentile, Rabbi Natan's lien does not apply. If Reuven's loan to the gentile came first, the matter is doubtful. If the gentile expressly encumbered movables together with land to Reuven, everyone agrees Shimon becomes subject to Reuven. If Shimon holds money that he robbed from the gentile, it is certainly subject to Reuven. If a gentile lent Reuven and Reuven lent Shimon, and the gentile sues Shimon in a Jewish beit din, Rabbi Natan's lien certainly does not apply, because the verse 'to the one to whom he is liable' was stated within the law governing Jews.
