If there is no condition. Rosh writes that if the final debt instrument expressly says the creditor may not compel payment until the debtor wishes, the final debtor is no more subjected to the first creditor than to the intermediate creditor. The intermediate creditor himself could have given away his movables or money or left them with another person on generous terms. Since the limitation existed from the creation of the final obligation, no stronger right ever arose for Rabbi Natan to transfer. The Shach notes that where the relevant object is borrowed property and the intermediate creditor had subjected movables together with land, the first creditor might be able to recover even before the agreed time, because the borrower's right cannot be sold like ordinary money. With an ordinary cash loan, however, the borrowed coins have generally been spent or exchanged, and the original coins are no longer available as a specific subject of lien. Even under the older law where after acquired movables could be subjected together with land, once the borrower has spent the original coins and purchased merchandise, the case resembles sale of encumbered land under the Rosh's approach. The first creditor may not necessarily follow the substituted property. If the very coins lent remain intact, however, and the applicable lien language reaches after acquired movables, there is greater room for direct collection even before the term.
