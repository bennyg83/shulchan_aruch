They cannot defer him. This rule is straightforward and is repeated below in siman 111. It is also logically sound. However, I saw in Hagahot HaRosh to the chapter concerning an ox that gored four or five times the following statement: "Reuven does not collect Shimon's debt by virtue of Shimon unless he could have collected it by virtue of his own right." The plain meaning suggests that if Reuven's claim against Shimon is oral, so that Reuven could not himself collect from purchasers, then even if Shimon's claim against the third party is evidenced by a written note, Reuven cannot collect from those purchasers merely by stepping into Shimon's position. It is difficult to say that Hagahot HaRosh disputes the Tur's rule here, which is well reasoned. I therefore understand the Tur as speaking where payment to Reuven causes no loss to the purchasers. But where they would suffer prejudice, for example because Shimon extended their payment date in the hope that Levi might later acquire other assets, or in a similar circumstance, they can certainly defer Reuven and say, "Who says Shimon actually owes you? Shimon is not our litigant on that issue, and perhaps he is colluding with you to our detriment." The Tur's own reasoning points to this distinction. He says they cannot defer Reuven because it makes no difference to them whether they pay Reuven or Shimon. This necessarily implies that where there is a real difference or potential loss to them, they may defer him. Hagahot HaRosh concerns such a case. There is therefore no dispute, and the matter is simple.
