Seif 1: Reuven is owed one hundred by Shimon, etc. Note: I have difficulty with the sugya on page 21b concerning a judge who “acquitted the liable party,” where the Gemara says the case is one in which the judge took and gave the money by hand. Why not establish the case through Rabbi Natan’s lien? For example, Reuven owes Shimon, and Shimon owes Levi. Reuven is therefore directly obligated toward Levi through the lien of Rabbi Natan. Shimon and Levi come before the judge, and the judge wrongly rules that Shimon is exempt from Levi. The judge then takes the money from the debtor Reuven and gives it to Shimon, even though under Rabbi Natan’s lien it belongs to Levi. Why would this not be a case of “he took and gave by hand”? This requires study. Further in the same seif. Regarding a question I received about the Shach at the end of this siman, who holds that Rabbi Natan’s lien applies even where the intermediate debtor has other assets, see my fifth-edition work on Choshen Mishpat, siman 209, where, with Hashem’s help, I discussed the matter.
