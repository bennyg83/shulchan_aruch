If Reuven is a creditor of Shimon and Shimon is a creditor of Levi, we take from Levi and give to Reuven. It contains 9 se'ifim.
If Reuven is owed one hundred by Shimon and Shimon is owed money by Levi, we take from Levi and give it to Reuven. There is no distinction whether Levi had already become obligated to Shimon when Shimon borrowed from Reuven or became obligated afterward, nor between a documented and an oral loan. Since both debtors admit their respective obligations to their creditors, we take from Levi and give to Reuven in every manner in which Levi may be obligated to Shimon, whether through a loan, a sale, or rent. {Rama: If Reuven holds collateral from a non-Jew and the non-Jew owes Shimon, some say Shimon may redeem the collateral from Reuven by paying him the principal and accumulated interest, and may collect his debt from the excess, as stated in Responsa Mahari Weil 188. This appears correct to me. Since the non-Jew has no other means of payment, Shimon possesses all the non-Jew's rights. Although some disagree, as cited in Beit Yosef, siman 74, it appears proper to rule as I have written.}
