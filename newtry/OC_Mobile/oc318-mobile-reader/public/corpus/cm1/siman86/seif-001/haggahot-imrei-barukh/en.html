(Shach, seif katan 5) Know this, for otherwise it is difficult why the Rashba wrote. Note: This requires study. The Rashba did not write there concerning a deposit that collection rests upon Rabbi Nathan's lien. There is no need for that doctrine. A deposited object, wherever it is, is treated as though it remains in the borrower's possession, and the lender may collect it from him. If the borrower has no money or movables, he cannot force the lender to accept land instead.
