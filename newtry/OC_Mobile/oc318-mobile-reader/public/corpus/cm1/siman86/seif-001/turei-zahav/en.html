(Seif 5) “Shimon cannot waive the debt.” So writes the Tur. This is difficult, for in the first chapter of Ketubot, page 19, Tosafot ask concerning the Gemara’s rule that one who says, “This is a note of accommodation,” is not believed when his statement harms others: why should he not be believed through a migo, since he could have waived the debt? Tosafot answer that he does not wish to lose his debt entirely. It follows that he could waive it if he wished, and the Rosh writes likewise there. How, then, could the Tur rule against his father without even citing him? It appears to me that the case there follows the formulation brought by the Beit Yosef from a responsum of the Rosh: Levi does not become obligated to Reuven until it has been established in beit din that Shimon owes Reuven, and Reuven’s mere notice to Levi is ineffective. It is therefore possible in such a case for Shimon to waive Levi’s debt at the moment he says, “This is a note of accommodation,” where Shimon’s debt to Reuven has not yet been established in beit din, but the creditor is protesting that he intends to prove it and then proceeds to do so. At that earlier moment Shimon could still waive Levi’s debt, since Levi had not yet become bound to Reuven. Nevertheless, Shimon is not believed when he then says that the note was one of accommodation, because afterward it becomes retroactively clear that Levi had become obligated to Reuven. This appears correct to me.
