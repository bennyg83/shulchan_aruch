A loan. Hagahot Ashri in the chapter Arbaah VaChamishah, citing Or Zarua, writes that Rabbi Nathan's rule also applies to a debt arising from a loan, to a depositor and custodian, to a tort victim and tortfeasor, to rental obligations, and to a robber and the person robbed. Maharshal writes likewise, adding that there is no distinction whether both obligations are loans, or one is a deposit or some other kind of debt. Although the obligations are not identical in all respects, Rabbi Nathan's rule applies to every kind of claim. Hagahot Ashri further writes that it applies to a custodian and an informer, so that we extract from the custodian of the informer and give it to the injured party. R. Simchah disagrees with respect to an informer. Maharshal explains that R. Simchah's reason is that liability for indirect causation is rabbinic. According to the Ramban, however, who holds that liability for indirect causation is of Torah force, even from an informer we collect through Rabbi Nathan's rule. I wrote in siman 66, seif 32, that whether liability for indirect causation is of Torah force is an unresolved legal question. One might therefore think that we do not collect from an informer here. Nevertheless, another reason indicates that we do. Since the informer is liable at least rabbinically, all rabbinic enactments were framed in the pattern of Torah law. Even if the liability is penal in nature, wherever we can collect from him, we do so. Indeed, nowhere in the Gemara or authorities do we find Rabbi Nathan's rule limited according to whether the underlying debt is Torah or rabbinic. This appears to be the primary halachah. Shach.
Rental. The Shach cites Responsa Maharashach and Gidulei Terumah, who challenge the Rambam because he rules both that one who sells himself into servitude is not entitled to a severance gift and that the law follows Rabbi Nathan. Based on the Gemara in the first chapter of Kiddushin, these rulings appear contradictory. See the Shach, who answers their objections and reconciles the Rambam. He further writes that the Rambam understands that Rabbi Nathan's rule does not apply to forms of charity, such as the poor tithe and similar gifts, as the Rambam rules in chapter 6 of Hilchot Matanot Aniyim. This rule is from the Tosefta. Mordechai in the first chapter of Bava Batra similarly writes concerning a person who owed a maneh and was given charity that he is exempt from repaying his debt from that charity money.
