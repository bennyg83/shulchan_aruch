Whether the obligation arose... Kiddushin 15a. See Rashi there, s.v. "and not to a creditor."
And there is no distinction... Meaning, although seif 2 states that the first creditor collects from the second debtor under the law governing encumbered property, the second debtor's assets are not considered encumbered in the ordinary sense so as to prevent collection of an oral loan. Proof comes from Kesubos 84a concerning one who died, etc. The Yerushalmi there records Rabbi Yehoshua ben Levi saying that the money should be given to the claimant whose proof is weaker, such as where one creditor has only witnesses and the other has a document, and it should be given to the creditor with witnesses. Yet the Mishnah at the end of Bava Basra says that one who lends another before witnesses collects only from unencumbered property. The same follows if Shimon lent Levi orally, as stated in Bava Kamma 40b. According to the opinion that an obligation written in the Torah does not have the status of one written in a document, which appears from the first chapter of Arachin to be the accepted halachah, the two obligations in the Bava Kamma case are oral loans.
Whether by way of. This is Rabbi Nasan's rule.
Whether by way of commerce. Pesachim 31a.
Whether by way of wages. Kiddushin 15a. The phrase "and the other, generally..." implies that according to Rabbi Nasan the rule applies even to wages, and we rule like Rabbi Nasan. Although the first Tanna of Rabbi Eliezer appears to disagree with Rabbi Nasan and we generally rule like the first Tanna, nevertheless we rule like Rabbi Nasan because Rava agrees with him, and Rabbi Nasan himself holds this in Pesachim there. One must say that the first Tanna did not disagree with Rabbi Nasan. Rather, Rav Tavyomi wished to establish the dispute as one where all opinions derive the law of a hired worker from another hired worker, while the statement there, "according to the one who does not derive hired worker from hired worker," refers to a different Tanna, because the Gemara knew that such a Tanna existed. The meaning there is: who is the Tanna, etc.? It suggests Rebbe and then rejects that identification. We therefore return to the first explanation that the first Tanna of Rabbi Eliezer does not derive one law of hired worker from the other, and he can still agree with Rabbi Nasan. Ritva writes this explicitly there. The same can be said even according to Rabbeinu Nesanel's explanation in Tosafos there, s.v. "and the other."
