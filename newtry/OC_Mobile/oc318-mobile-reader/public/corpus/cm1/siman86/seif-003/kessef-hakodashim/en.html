Seif 8. (You have been paid by Shimon.) A creditor proceeds against a third party under Rabbi Nathan's lien, and the third party responds, "Perhaps the original debtor already paid you." If the creditor has no written instrument of a kind against which payment cannot be claimed, he cannot collect from the third party on a doubt. Although the third party need not take a shevuat heset, the creditor may impose a general acceptance of a ban upon anyone who knows that payment is still due. Even if the third party claims with certainty that he knows the original debt was paid, an ordinary shevuat heset may still not apply, because where there is no presumption that payment has not been made, a person generally cannot know with certainty that no payment occurred. In any event, the creditor cannot collect merely because the third party's assertion of payment may have been made to avoid an oath or for some similar reason. Even if the original borrower now admits in beit din that he still owes the creditor, there remains a concern of collusion between them, especially if the creditor had already given the borrower a release that would ordinarily be effective but for Rabbi Nathan's lien.
