It is incumbent upon him. Baal HaTerumot explains that Shimon is not required to trouble himself to sell property in order to repay his debt with cash, as explained below in siman 101, and making this claim is comparable to that burden. The Sema writes that according to this, if Shimon wrote in his note to Reuven that he is obligated to tend to his merchandise, sell it, and obtain cash, as is commonly stipulated today, pursuing the debt from Levi is also included in that obligation. The Shach disputes the underlying rule. Siman 101 is no proof, because there the debtor gives the creditor movable property, and property of monetary value is equivalent to money for repayment. Perhaps Baal HaTerumot and the Tur and Mechaber speak only where Shimon transfers the notes themselves to Reuven for collection at an appraised value, as stated in siman 101, seif 5. Otherwise, a promissory note is not itself money. All the more so where Levi owes Shimon only an oral debt, what can Shimon hand Reuven that would discharge his own obligation? Although Reuven may sue Levi and Levi cannot say, "You are not my litigant," that is only because Levi is directly encumbered to Reuven as well. But until Reuven is actually paid, Shimon plainly remains indebted to him. Therefore it is Shimon's responsibility to pursue Levi. If he refuses to do so, it appears that he falls under the designation, "A wicked borrower does not repay." This appears clear. Shach.
