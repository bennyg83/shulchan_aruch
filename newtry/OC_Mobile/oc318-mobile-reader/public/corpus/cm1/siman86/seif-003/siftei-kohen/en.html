Reuven cannot compel him, etc. This rule, though stated by Baal HaTerumot and Tur, does not appear correct. They write that if Shimon refuses to sue Levi in order to pay Reuven, Shimon is not compelled, because Rabbi Natan says “we take,” meaning beit din takes, and because Shimon need not trouble himself to collect and sell a debt in order to pay. The first proof is no proof. Beit din may collect from Levi when Shimon is absent or when Levi says to Reuven, “You are not my litigant,” but that does not exempt Shimon from his own obligation. If Shimon is before us and owes Reuven, he must take every available step to satisfy his debt, including suing Levi. The analogy to a debtor who tenders movables rather than selling them is also inapposite. Movables are themselves value and discharge the debt. A promissory note is not itself the money owed, and an oral debt is even less capable of being delivered as payment. Reuven may indeed sue Levi because Rabbi Natan makes Levi directly obligated to him, but until Levi actually pays, Shimon remains indebted to Reuven. Shimon therefore bears the duty to pursue Levi. If he refuses, he is a wicked borrower who does not repay. Perhaps Baal HaTerumot, Tur, and Mechaber speak only where Shimon formally transfers the note to Reuven in satisfaction of the debt after appraisal, as described below in siman 101, seif 5. Otherwise, the rule is as I have stated.
