Reuven cannot compel him, etc. The Shach questions this. Why should the borrower not be obligated to take every reasonable step necessary to satisfy his debt? It appears that Baal HaTerumot holds as follows. According to the Sages who disagree with Rabbi Natan, the first borrower is not obligated to collect his own debt from his debtor and then turn the proceeds over to his creditor. If he were, the Rashba's question would arise that the entire sequence is merely a pointless circular transfer. Rather, since the money is in another person's hands, it is considered prospective property and is not directly subject at all, and the borrower has no duty to go collect it for his creditor. Rabbi Natan does not disagree by imposing such a collection duty on the intermediate borrower. He holds only that the second borrower becomes directly obligated to the first creditor as well. The intermediate borrower can therefore argue whichever way one turns: according to the Sages, I have no duty at all to pursue this debt; and even Rabbi Natan does not say that I must personally handle the collection, because the obligation does not rest on me in that manner. Rather, the second borrower is directly obligated to you, so go collect your own debt from him. This is correct and straightforward, and it is indeed implied by the Sages who disagree with Rabbi Natan. Consider it carefully.
