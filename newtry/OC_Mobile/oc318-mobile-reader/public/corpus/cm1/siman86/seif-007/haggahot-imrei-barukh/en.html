(Shach, seif katan 17) And a rabbinic oath cannot come and nullify a Torah lien. Note: This requires very great study. When the first debtor dies, Rabbi Nathan's lien applies only if the heirs collected land from their father's debtor. If they collected movable property, it has the ordinary status of heirs' movable property, and Rabbi Nathan's lien does not apply, as stated explicitly in Pesachim 31a and Tosafot there, passage beginning "I am obligated." The current practice of collecting even from heirs' movables rests only on the enactment of the Geonim that heirs' movable property is subject to creditors. Thus Reuven, the first creditor, may collect movables from Levi, who is Shimon's debtor, but this is not a Torah application of Rabbi Nathan's lien.
