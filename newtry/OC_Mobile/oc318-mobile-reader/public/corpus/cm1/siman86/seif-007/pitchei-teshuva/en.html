And Levi claims that it was paid. The Sema writes that this concerns a document containing a credibility clause in favor of the creditor himself but not in favor of his heirs. See Responsa Shevut Yaakov I, siman 154, which questions this, because the Sema himself above, seif katan 9, clearly indicates the opposite: whenever the document contains a credibility clause, the debtor is not believed to say that it was paid where his assertion would prejudice others, as is clear from the Gemara and all the poskim. See the Tur, siman 47, and the Sema there. Perhaps the text contains a printing error and should read that the document contains no credibility clause at all. See there.
