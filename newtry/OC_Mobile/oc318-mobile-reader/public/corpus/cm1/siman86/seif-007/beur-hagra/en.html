If they have no, etc., they are, etc. This assertion is included in the claim, 'I have nothing,' concerning which one swears under the geonic enactment. The enactment was patterned after the oath of a bailee that the object is not in his possession. Tur calls it a hesset oath because heirs do not swear to exempt themselves, as stated in simanim 69 and 70. Sefer HaTerumot, who requires them to take the ordinary oath of inability, follows his own view that heirs do swear to exempt themselves. Tur follows his own contrary ruling and requires hesset only where the creditor asserts with certainty that the debt remains unpaid, since no hesset oath is imposed on a doubtful claim. With a definite claim there is no distinction between the father and heirs, as stated in Shevuot 47a and siman 107:2.
