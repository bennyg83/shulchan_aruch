Nevertheless, so long as she is alive, he should return it to the woman. This wording is astonishing. If the husband objects, why should the bailee return it to her? If property in her possession is presumed to belong to the husband unless she proves otherwise, then all the more so where it is not in her possession should the husband's objection prevent return to her. It should have said: nevertheless, so long as the husband does not object, return it to her, because property should be returned to the place from which it was received. So writes Rivash, siman 50. Yet Rivash's own words require study. He brings proof from Rambam and Ramban, who hold that a woman is not believed to say about property in her possession that her husband has no rights in it, and nevertheless they rule that the bailee returns it to the place from which he received it. Only afterward may the husband claim rights to the fruits, and then his dispute is with the woman. This is not a proof regarding a woman who conducts all the affairs of the household, where everything is presumed to belong to the husband. Why should the bailee return it to her? Certainly, where the husband does not object the bailee need not appoint himself guardian over the husband's interests. But Rivash's proof sounds as though even where the husband objects it is returned to the woman. If so, his ruling is difficult.
