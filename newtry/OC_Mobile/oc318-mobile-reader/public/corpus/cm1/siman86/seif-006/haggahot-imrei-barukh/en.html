(Netivot HaMishpat, seif katan 5) Perhaps she borrowed after the divorce, etc. Note: See Maharshal, who answers that Rabbi Nathan's lien does not apply to a ketubah because it is not yet collectible and no lien takes effect during the husband's lifetime. But once she is no longer married to him, Rabbi Nathan's lien certainly applies to the ketubah, since it has become collectible. We must therefore say that Maharshal's answer was stated only according to Rashi's interpretation in Bava Metzia that the case concerns a woman still living under her husband.
