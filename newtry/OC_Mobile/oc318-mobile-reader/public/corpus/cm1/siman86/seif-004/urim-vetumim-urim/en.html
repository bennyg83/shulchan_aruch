Money held as a deposit, etc. Even if the custodian accepted the deposit for a fixed period, it does not thereby become directly liened like a debt. Sema. According to what I wrote in Tumim, however, even unrestricted money that the custodian is permitted to use remains legally in the depositor's ownership until the custodian actually uses it, and therefore it may be taken even where the depositor owns other assets. In that framework there is a clear distinction if the custodian accepted it for a fixed period: unrestricted money accepted for a fixed period resembles a term loan, and it should not be extracted before the term.
Beit din may, etc. If a person's utensil is lent to another for a fixed time, then if the creditor's document contains no clause subjecting movable property together with land, the creditor cannot take the utensil during the loan period even where the debtor has no other assets, because lending a utensil for a term resembles a temporary sale of its use. If the creditor's document does contain the appropriate movable property clause and the debtor has no other assets, he may collect immediately, although it requires study whether the Rosh's market protection enactment prevents this. Where the debtor has other assets, the creditor cannot collect from the utensil during the loan term, but may do so afterward. So writes the Shach. See Tumim. I wrote that with an unspecified loan of a utensil, where the borrower may use it until the owner demands return, if the owner has not demanded it we ordinarily do not take it from the borrower for the owner's creditor, except where the owner has no other assets. See there. I wrote that this appears to be the Rama's view in Even HaEzer 70:7.
