They are money held as a deposit, etc. It appears that this applies even if the deposit was made for a fixed term that has not yet expired. Such money is not treated like property sold to another purchaser from which collection is postponed until free assets are exhausted. Since it remains a deposit in another's possession, it is regarded as though it were still in Reuven's own hands and is not subject to the law of encumbered property in that sense. The debtor cannot insist that the creditor take land instead.
