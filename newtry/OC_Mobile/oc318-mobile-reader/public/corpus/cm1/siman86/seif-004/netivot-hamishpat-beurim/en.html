They are money held on deposit. See Shach, seif katan 10, who questions Rama's gloss in Even HaEzer. The primary answer is Tumim's: the case concerns an ordinary loan for use, which entails a potential loss to the lender because the borrower may continue using the object until the lender demands its return. Nevertheless, the rule that property is not extracted for support of a wife and daughters does not apply, because that restriction is based on social policy. Here the lender himself could demand the property, and the wife comes through his right, so her demand is treated as the lender's own demand.
