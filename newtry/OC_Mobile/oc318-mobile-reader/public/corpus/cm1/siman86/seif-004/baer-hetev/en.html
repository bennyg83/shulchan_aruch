With a deposit. It appears that this applies even before the time for return of the deposit has arrived. Nevertheless, the deposit does not have the status of encumbered property, because property deposited with another is regarded as though still in the depositor's own possession, and he cannot say, "Take land instead." So writes the Sema. The Shach writes that this applies specifically to a deposit, but not to borrowed property. A borrower has the benefit of using the property for the agreed period, and therefore, even if Shimon has no other assets, the item is not extracted from the borrower before that period ends, just as with a monetary loan in seifim 5 and 9. The same appears true of one who rents a utensil for a fixed term. See Even HaEzer 70, seif 7. What is implied by the Rama's gloss there, that if Shimon has other property we do not extract even from a custodian or borrower, does not appear correct to me. Since Rabbi Nathan's lien applies even when Shimon has other property, as explained above, why should we not extract from the borrower or custodian? The Rashba likewise writes this in a responsum, and the Rama himself cites it in siman 101, seif 4. If the borrower would suffer a loss, then even where there is no unencumbered property we should not extract from him. If Shimon is absent, then with a loaned or rented object we extract as soon as the term expires, even though Shimon has other property. With a monetary loan, however, we do not extract, because Levi can say, "Since Shimon is not demanding payment from me, I will retain the money until he does." Since Shimon has other property, we do not take it from Levi. If Shimon encumbered movable property together with land to Reuven, then when Shimon has no property we may extract from Levi utensils that Shimon lent or rented to him even before the term ends. But money lent to Levi cannot be extracted on that basis, because a lien of movables together with land does not attach to fungible money, which bears no identifying mark. Shach.
