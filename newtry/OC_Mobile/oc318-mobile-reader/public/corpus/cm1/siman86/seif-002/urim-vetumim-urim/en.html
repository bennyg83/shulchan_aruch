When does this apply, etc. The Shach understands the limitation to apply specifically where collection would cause a loss to the second debtor. If it causes him no loss, or if the second debtor wishes to pay voluntarily, Rabbi Natan's rule applies even though the first debtor owns other assets. See Tumim. The plain wording of the authorities implies that even where the second debtor suffers no loss, if the immediate creditor would suffer a relevant loss through direct collection, beit din does not intervene while the first debtor has other property. See there.
For whom they arranged the protected minimum, etc. Maharshal holds that if the intermediate creditor is absent, the first creditor may collect directly from the second debtor's unencumbered assets, because the first debtor is not present to be paid. The Shach objects that even then collection should not occur until notice is given to the intermediate creditor, as explained below in siman 129:10. This is correct.
Beit din does not intervene. If the payment date has arrived and the intermediate creditor refuses to extend time to his debtor, Beit Yosef holds that beit din may intervene even though the first debtor has other assets. If the intermediate creditor grants an extension, beit din does not intervene. Where the first debtor has no assets, however, the intermediate creditor's voluntary extension is disregarded and beit din does intervene. This is Beit Yosef's view as understood by the Shach. Many disagree. See Tumim. I wrote that Beit Yosef's position itself requires study, and according to what I wrote in seif katan 9 it does not appear to be the practical law. Rather, where the first debtor has other unencumbered property we do not collect through the second debtor. This may be why Beit Yosef and Rama did not codify the special distinction in Shulchan Aruch.
