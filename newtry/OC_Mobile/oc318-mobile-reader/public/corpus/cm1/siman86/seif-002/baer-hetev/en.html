We proceed against them. The Sema explains that this property is considered encumbered property and is not collected where the debtor has unencumbered property, even though the due date of the debt has arrived. This follows from the Ran and excludes the view of the Beit Yosef and Darkei Moshe. The Shach, however, brings many proofs that even when Shimon has other property, Levi is nevertheless directly encumbered to Reuven under Rabbi Nathan's rule. The exception is where Levi would suffer a loss, such as where he claims that Shimon's note has been paid, is a trust document, or has some other defect that voids it, or where Shimon has waived the note, or where Levi still needs the money for a period and Shimon is willing to leave it with him, or similar circumstances. In such cases we do not extract from Levi against his will unless Shimon has no property at all. If Shimon has no property, Reuven must take a Torah oath that he has not been paid, even though Levi does not know whether he was paid, just as one who collects from encumbered property may collect only with an oath. This is the substance of the Shach's discussion; see there at length. See also siman 106.
