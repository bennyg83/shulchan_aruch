When is this said? See Beurim: the primary rule is that even if the first borrower has property, the second borrower has the law of a guarantor. If later the first borrower has no property, the second must pay. Likewise, if the first borrower is violent and cannot effectively be collected from, even if he has assets, the second borrower must pay. Even if the first has only inferior land and the second has intermediate or inferior land, where the first is violent the second must pay from intermediate property or money, if he has it, provided the second suffers no loss, meaning the debt is due and he would have had to pay his own creditor in money or intermediate land anyway. But where the second will suffer loss, for example because his own lender waived the debt, then the second has the law of encumbered property. Accordingly, if the first borrower is violent but has property, the second need not pay at all, because encumbered property is not collected where free property exists, even if the debtor is violent. If the first borrower has no property, the second need pay only from inferior land. If the first borrower has property, even only inferior land, and is willing to pay, then even though the second borrower has intermediate land and would suffer no loss, the creditor cannot collect intermediate land from the second and instead collects inferior land from the first.
