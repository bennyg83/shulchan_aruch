To waive. See the Shach, who discusses this ruling at length. Responsa Maharashach, part 1, siman 12, writes that the party in possession may say, "I rely on those authorities who hold that the debt may be waived." See the Taz, who distinguishes among the cases; see there.
To cause a loss. The Sema writes that this applies specifically where the note contains a trust clause. Otherwise, since the creditor cannot collect without an oath when the debtor says, "Swear to me," and here Shimon admits that the note has been paid, Reuven, who comes only through Shimon's right, likewise cannot collect. The Tur and Mechaber write similarly in siman 47, seif 1. The Shach writes that he already challenged this there and proved that even if the note contains no trust clause, and the debtor says, "Swear to me," while the creditor refuses to swear, that refusal does not void the note. The document remains in force and collection may be made under Rabbi Nathan's rule. See what I wrote there in seif katan 2 in the name of the Taz, and Responsa Maharam Alshich, siman 62.
Liable. The Sema writes: In siman 66, seif 16, where one sells a promissory note and both seller and purchaser come to collect from the debtor, and beit din tells the debtor to pay the purchaser, yet he disobeys and pays the seller, the Tur and Mechaber record a dispute, with some saying that the purchaser cannot then recover again from the debtor. That case is different because the debtor was never entirely removed from his obligation to the seller, since the personal obligation remains with the seller, and assignment of a promissory note operates only rabbinically. Rabbi Nathan's rule, by contrast, is of Torah force. The Shach writes that it appears there is no need for a formal ruling of beit din. Once Levi is directly encumbered to Reuven under Rabbi Nathan's rule, Reuven is Levi's direct litigant. Therefore, if Levi knew that Shimon owed Reuven money and had no assets, yet Levi nevertheless paid Shimon, Levi must pay Reuven a second time. But if Levi claims that he did not know either that Shimon owed Reuven or that Shimon had no assets, Levi is exempt. We do not administer an oath on that point because Reuven's claim is uncertain; only a general cherem is imposed. This is the substance of the Shach's discussion; see there.
