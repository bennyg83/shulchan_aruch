Shimon may not... As stated in Bava Basra 147b: "If you say that it is Torah law..." Here too it is Torah law, from "and give it to the one to whom he is indebted." See also Kesubos 81b and Tosafos there. According to what is written here the matter is clear, as the Gemara there says, "Perhaps it is Rabbi Nasan..." See also there 19a and Tosafos. Although the Ramban challenged this view from the end of the first chapter of Bava Metzia, this was already answered in the gloss above, siman 66 seif 26: the law of Rabbi Nasan applies only where the debtor has no other property. He also challenged it from Bava Kamma 89a, but that too is answered by the gloss there, which distinguishes between a preexisting debt to the purchaser and an obligation created through the sale itself, where the sale is ineffective and the money is treated as a gift. There is support from Bava Basra 147b regarding the transfer of a debt document by a person on his deathbed. Although in Gittin, chapter HaSholeach, a master who designated his slave as an explicit lien and then freed him may do so, even though the slave is encumbered through Rabbi Nasan's rule and freeing him is a form of release, this may be answered as the Yerushalmi states there: a special leniency was enacted regarding emancipation. The Gemara likewise indicates this when it compares the matter specifically to Rava's rule concerning consecration.
And he may not extend the time... This too is a form of release, and it is learned from the preceding rule, for the two share one rationale, as the text continues, "since the lien has been removed..." Thus Kesubos 81b states that he may not say..., but rather the court collects.... Were it otherwise, he could extend the time for himself for as long as he wished.
And likewise if... Kesubos 19a.
Or that it was paid. As the Rosh writes there. The Raavad learned this from the end of the first chapter of Bava Metzia 19b, for according to the initial assumption that she cannot waive it, she likewise would not be believed to say that it was paid.
And if another... As stated in Gittin, chapter HaSholeach, that one who frees the slave is liable because he damaged another person's lien.
One who refuses... As above, and so appears in the responsa of the Rosh there.
