The upper owner. The Tur writes that they divide the produce because the matter is an unresolved question in the Gemara. The Mechaber uses Rambam's wording. The Sema explains that Rambam addresses a case where the root was not detached when the branch was detached. Since the root remains in the lower owner's domain, as long as the branch itself has not been detached we follow the branch after the root, and the upper owner does not receive half unless he actually detaches and takes possession of it, at which point the rule is that one who seeks to extract money bears the burden of proof. The Tur, by contrast, addresses a case where detaching the branch also uproots and detaches the root. Even before it is detached, since it can be detached by simply reaching out one's hand, the lower owner is not considered in possession, and because the question remains unresolved they divide it. The Taz writes that the Sema's distinction does not appear correct to him. In his view Rambam and the Tur disagree with one another in this law. See there. See Even HaEzel, page 90, column 4, for his discussion.
