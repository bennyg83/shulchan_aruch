Whatever the upper owner can reach, etc. The remainder belongs to the lower owner. The Gemara, as explained by Rashi, says the reason: if the upper owner took away his soil, there would be no vegetables there at all. Therefore the upper owner receives whatever he can reach by simply extending his hand. What he cannot reach he is deemed to relinquish to the lower owner, because it is embarrassing for him to seek the lower owner's permission to enter the lower garden and take it. In the Perishah I gave another explanation according to the Tur's wording.
Anything close to the ground within three tefachim, etc. Whatever is within three tefachim is treated as attached to the lower ground and joins the lower owner's land. This is unlike Ir Shushan, who explains that the upper owner is embarrassed to take it because the lower owner would complain that he takes what lies within his domain.
The upper owner should not take it. The Tur writes that this was an unresolved question in the Gemara; therefore the upper owner should not take it, but they divide. The Mechaber follows Rambam, who does not mention division. In the Derishah I explained that Rambam concerns a case where plucking the branch does not uproot the root. Since the root unquestionably remains in the lower owner's domain, and the status of the branch is unresolved, we follow the root and the upper owner takes none unless he already detached and seized it, in which case the burden of proof rests on the claimant. The Tur speaks where pulling the branch uproots the root as well. Since the branch is reachable and can be plucked, no part remains firmly possessed by the lower owner, so an unresolved question results in division. The Tur adds an unresolved case where the upper owner can reach the root but not the branch. In the Derishah I explain why Rif, Rosh, Rambam, and the Mechaber omit it.
