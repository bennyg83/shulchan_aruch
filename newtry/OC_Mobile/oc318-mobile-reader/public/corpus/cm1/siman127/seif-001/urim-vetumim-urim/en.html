For whatever he acquires. Because of this reason, even if the husband or master does not make a definite claim that the money belongs to him, and even if the wife or slave wrote a promissory note for the money and gave it to the claimant, the claimant still has no claim against the husband or master. So writes the Maggid Mishneh; see Sema. See also Responsa Mabit, part 1, siman 89, and Responsa Rabbi Aharon Sasson, siman 198.
And there are those who disagree. They hold that this applies specifically where the money was hidden when the loan was made, because then the borrower was merely trying to reveal where the money was. If it was not hidden, the rule does not apply. Even where it was hidden, the husband or master must make a definite claim. See Sema and Shach.
