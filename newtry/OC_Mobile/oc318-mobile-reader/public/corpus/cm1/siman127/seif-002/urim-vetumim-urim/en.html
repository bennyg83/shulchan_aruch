The borrower. The same applies to one who holds a deposit belonging to a convert. Even though the convert did him no particular favor, if the convert's conception and birth were not both in sanctity, returning it to his children is praiseworthy and the spirit of the Sages is pleased with him. Shach. This is the conclusion of Tosafot, Ran, and Tur.

Their conception. Meaning that the mother too was a gentile. If the mother was Jewish and the father a gentile, the child does not inherit through the gentile father at all, because the child follows the Jewish mother. If both later abandoned Judaism, he still would not inherit through that gentile line and therefore there is no reason to return. Rosh, Ran, Sema, and Shach.
In sanctity, he is not obligated to return. According to Rashi and Rosh, if he does return it, the spirit of the Sages is pleased with him. According to Rabbeinu Yitzchak the opposite is true: in this situation, if he returns it the spirit of the Sages is not pleased with him. See Bach regarding Tur, and see Shach.
