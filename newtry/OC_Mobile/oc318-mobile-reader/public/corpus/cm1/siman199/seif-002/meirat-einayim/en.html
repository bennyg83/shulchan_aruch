But if he owed him a debt, etc. The reason is that ordinary debts arising from loans are common, and the Sages applied the enactment to them. A debt arising from a previous sale is different. One might ask how the new article can be acquired when the original money is no longer present. The answer is that the Sages treat the first sale as though it remains present and is exchanged for the object now being sold. So writes the Maggid Mishneh in chapter 5 of Hilchot Mekhirah.
And both agreed, he did not acquire. See siman 204:10, where the Mechaber holds that a debt arising from a loan is treated like money currently in the buyer's hand and paid to the seller for the new object. Although money does not fully acquire movables, the buyer who retracts is subject to the sanction of "He Who exacted punishment." The same applies to a debt. This is the Rambam's view. The Rosh disagrees regarding an ordinary debt, and the Tur records both opinions there. Therefore the Mechaber here simply says that a general debt does not effect full acquisition, which is accepted by both views. This seif is not discussing the sanction of "He Who exacted punishment."
