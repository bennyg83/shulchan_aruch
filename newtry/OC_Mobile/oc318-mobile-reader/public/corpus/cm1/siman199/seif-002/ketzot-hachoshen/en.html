Maggid Mishneh explains Rambam differently: the Sages treat the earlier sale proceeds as though the original money were still present and exchanged for the new item. Shitah Mekubetzet offers another explanation. If Shimon actually has in his possession the very money he became obligated to pay from the earlier sale, he may not spend it, and therefore it resembles a deposit rather than a loan. I find this difficult. A person who owes money from a sale does not thereby transfer ownership of particular coins in his possession to the creditor. Even if he designates coins, money is not ordinarily acquired through barter, and certainly unspecified coins remain his to spend. If the concern is merely that he not wrongfully delay payment, that applies to every matured debt. Thus this explanation requires study.
