Gimel) There, and so rules the Rambam there, halachah 4. The Maggid Mishneh writes that these are the explicit words of the Rif in HaZahav, Bava Metzia 46b, regarding the statement that there are forms of money that function as exchange: for example, exchanging the value owed for an ox against a cow. Rabbenu Chananel explains likewise. Ramban challenged the Geonim's approach from Kiddushin 6b, where one who betroths a woman with a loan does not effect kiddushin, while in a sale the parties agree that the buyer acquires. The Gemara asks that if a loan is given for expenditure, by what means does he acquire, indicating that a sale is not acquired through a loan, even when the loan money itself still exists, and certainly not where it no longer exists. The Maggid Mishneh explains that Rambam distinguishes between a debt arising from a sale and a debt arising from an actual loan of money. The reason appears to be that an obligation generated by a sale is uncommon, and the Sages did not institute meshichah in such a case. It is therefore treated as though there had never been a monetary debt at all, but rather as though a cow had been exchanged for an ox.
