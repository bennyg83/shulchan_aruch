There are times when payment itself acquires, because the Sages left the Torah law in force. This applies to meat on four occasions: the eve of Shemini Atzeres, the eve of the first day of Pesach, the eve of Shavuos, and the eve of Rosh Hashanah. If a butcher has an ox worth one hundred dinars and accepts one dinar from a purchaser for meat to be delivered after slaughter, but has not yet collected enough from purchasers to cover the ox's value, the butcher is compelled to slaughter it so that he can supply the meat. Therefore, if the ox dies before slaughter, it dies at the purchaser's risk and the purchaser loses his dinar. Rama: Some write that the same applies to money paid for wine for Kiddush on Erev Shabbos, because in such a case the Sages likewise left the Torah rule in force.
