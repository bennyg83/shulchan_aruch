Hekdesh and charity. Ketzot HaChoshen asks that Tosafot distinguish between hekdesh and orphans. With hekdesh, money acquires d'oraisa, whereas with orphans the rule is only a rabbinic enactment modeled after hekdesh. Since we rule that charity is like an ordinary person regarding oaths, ribbis, ona'ah, and similar laws, perhaps it should be like orphans for money acquisition, so that both parties may retract after payment. His statement concerning a generous intention to consecrate, that siman 212 treats charity like an ordinary person, is incorrect. Yoreh Deah at the end of siman 258 expressly rules that charity has the law of hekdesh regarding a generous intention, and even without verbal expression one is obligated, because the primary view there is the first opinion. Nevertheless, explanation is required why charity is treated as ordinary property regarding oaths, ribbis, and ona'ah. The distinction appears to be that only regarding the actual taking effect of a vow and the attachment of sanctity do we learn charity from hekdesh, because Scripture juxtaposes charity to hekdesh in the verses concerning delay and fulfillment of one's words. That comparison concerns precisely the creation and fulfillment of the obligation. By contrast, the exemptions of hekdesh from oaths, ribbis, and ona'ah come from verses excluding dealings with one's fellow. Poor people are certainly one's fellow, so those exclusions do not apply to charity. Here, however, the question is whether the sanctity of charity attaches to the money through the transaction. For that purpose charity is comparable to hekdesh, and the ruling is therefore understandable.
