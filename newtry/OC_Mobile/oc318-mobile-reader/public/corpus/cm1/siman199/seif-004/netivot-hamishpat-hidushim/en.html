He is a paid custodian by virtue of the benefit that the purchaser could consume the produce and use it as he wished before the orphans retract. Therefore the Sages left the transaction under Torah law, where meshichah is not required against the orphans. The concern that "your wheat burned" applies chiefly to common types of loss resembling theft. Here, because the purchaser from orphans is a paid custodian and liable for theft, only fire and other uncommon unavoidable accidents remain, and the Sages were not concerned for those. Sema:
In the other divisions. The reason is as follows. If produce declined in value after meshichah, the purchaser cannot retract, because the rule that money acquires for orphans was enacted only for their benefit. If the orphans received money but the purchaser had not yet performed meshichah and the produce rose in value, the orphans may retract for this reason. If it declined, the purchaser may also retract, though he is subject to the curse of "He Who exacted punishment." Otherwise, when orphans need cash no one would pay them until the produce was physically delivered. If the orphans purchased produce, performed meshichah, and had not yet paid, and the produce rose in value, they acquired it like any other person. If it declined, they cannot retract, because otherwise when they need produce no one would sell to them until receiving advance payment. If the orphans paid but had not yet performed meshichah and the produce declined, they may retract like anyone else. If it rose, the seller may retract subject to "He Who exacted punishment." Otherwise the seller could say, "Your wheat burned." One cannot answer that if it burns the orphans could retract just as when the price declines. It is unreasonable that for purposes of retraction the goods would be in the orphans' domain, while for fire they would remain in the seller's domain. Sema:
Money to consecrated property. Although consecrated property loses through this rule, they nevertheless cannot retract. Sema. The same applies where a communal treasurer gave money to someone for the needs of consecrated property and the price then fell. The treasurer cannot retract, because an ordinary person would be subject to "He Who exacted punishment." But where the treasurer gives money and fixes a market price for goods that are not yet physically in the seller's possession, the treasurer may retract. Shach. See the Beurim:
