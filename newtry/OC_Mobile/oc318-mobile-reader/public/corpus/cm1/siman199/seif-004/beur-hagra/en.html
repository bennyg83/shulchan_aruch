Specifically, etc. Kiddushin 29a discusses four cases. The same rules apply to charity, which is compared to consecrated property, as stated in Nedarim 7a. This follows the Geonim’s approach of ruling according to an “if you wish to say” conclusion, as the Ran explains at the beginning of Kiddushin. Only in this one case is there a distinction between consecrated property and orphans. See Tosafos there, s.v. “Is it so, etc.”
(Collected note) But, etc., therefore, etc. The same applies if the price decreased, as stated in the Gemara there; the text states the greater novelty. Thus “but consecrated property, etc.” means that where one makes meshichah from the Temple treasury, the law is like the law of orphans. Regarding the other four distinctions involving meshichah and money in transactions with orphans, whether the same applies to consecrated property, see Rambam, Hilchos Mechirah chapter 9, and Ran in Kiddushin there. (End of note.)
(Collected note) And charity, etc. Charity too follows the rule that a verbal declaration to the sacred domain is equivalent to delivery to an ordinary person. Rosh Hashanah chapter 1 derives from “that which left your lips” a reference to charity. Nedarim chapter 1 asks whether the law of yad applies to charity, and the Rishonim rule according to the Gemara’s concluding alternative that it does. Ran in Kiddushin there; see also Nedarim. (End of note.)
