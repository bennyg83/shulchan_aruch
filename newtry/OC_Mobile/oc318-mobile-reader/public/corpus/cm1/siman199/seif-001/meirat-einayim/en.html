He had money in his hand without knowing the count, etc. Rambam and the Tur also use this wording, which implies that the purchaser too must not know the amount. However, the Tur and the Mechaber conclude, "and he did not carefully determine their number," rather than, "and they did not determine their number," or, "and he too did not determine their number." This implies that the matter depends only upon the seller. The opening phrase, "he had money in his hand without a count," was written because that is the ordinary case. If the purchaser knows how much money he has, he generally says to the seller, "Sell me your object for the amount of money I have here." Beit Yosef likewise writes that this can be understood even according to Rambam and the Tur, and that the students of Rashba say so explicitly. Rama also cites this here as one opinion. See the Perishah.
Because it is an uncommon circumstance, etc. Meaning, a sale and acquisition with money whose amount is unknown is uncommon. In an uncommon case the Sages did not enact their decree out of concern that the seller might say, "Your wheat burned in the attic." This is from the Rosh.
