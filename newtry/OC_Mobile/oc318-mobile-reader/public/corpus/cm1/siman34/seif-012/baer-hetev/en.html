Mistaken. This is unlike the earlier rule that one is not believed to say, "I acted unintentionally," or "I forgot." There, the prohibition of violating an oath is universally known, and a person naturally pays attention to what he accepted upon himself by oath. The same applies to testimony where he saw an event specifically in order to testify. But when he says, "I thought there was no prohibition in this matter," there was no reason for him to be especially cautious. Similarly, one who tied or untied a knot on Shabbos and says that he did not know this was a melacha, or did not know it was Shabbos, may have forgotten because the obligation came to him externally rather than from something he consciously undertook. Thus the Sema.
