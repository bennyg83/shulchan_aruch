Shach rules like Rabbi Zerachiah HaLevi that written testimony cannot generate the punishments of plotting witnesses, although if the signers are disproved they can be disqualified and the document invalidated. Urim VeTumim proves this from the fact that gittin and kiddushin documents do not require a biblical date for purposes of formal inquiry. The proper understanding is that signature witnesses on a get do not testify before beit din about the completed divorce. They testify that the husband appointed them to prepare the document; delivery later creates the divorce. Such appointment testimony need not be delivered orally before beit din, and therefore the ordinary rule requiring testimony capable of refutation does not apply in the same way. Similarly, monetary deed witnesses often attest to authorization to create the instrument, and oral hearsay can be effective for that agency function. When they are later disproved, the disproving witnesses are believed regarding their personal falsity and the original signers can be disqualified, just as one who was shown to have issued a false ritual ruling can be disqualified, even if formal plotting-witness punishment does not apply. Even where a deed's witnesses attest more directly to completion of a transaction, the special punishment of plotting witnesses may have been limited to oral testimony given before beit din.
