One who shares with a thief, etc. Rivash ruled concerning a person who shared property stolen from a Jew and was compelled by beit din to restore what he received. Rivash says he is not disqualified from testimony, proving from the rule that one who tells an agent, "Go steal," is not liable as the thief. This requires study. That rule concerns lashes, double payment, and similar direct penalties. But one who accepts stolen property and assists the thief still violates a prohibition. Below, siman 369, it is clear that he can violate lifnei iver. Even if there are no lashes, I explained above that in matters of monetary greed a person can be disqualified for such an offense. Sema says so explicitly concerning a butcher. More fundamentally, the stolen property of his fellow is in his hands and he deliberately withholds it. Why should he be better than one who denies a deposit, which came into his possession permissibly but whose denial disqualifies him where evasion is implausible? Here he took another person's money unlawfully and refuses to return it. This is classic monetary wickedness, worse than denying an ordinary loan or deposit. I therefore think Rivash's case must have involved one of two circumstances. Either he received the property after the owner's despair, so that there was despair plus change of possession. Although he may still have owed the value because he knew the circumstances, the specific stolen object was no longer legally the owner's property in his possession. Or the stolen property was no longer in his hands because he had already restored it. Although forced restoration does not ordinarily rehabilitate an actual thief or robber, whose disqualification stems from the original Torah prohibition, this person's disqualification would arise only from presently withholding another's property. Once the property is restored, even under compulsion, he no longer possesses another's money and returns to qualification. But so long as he retains the stolen property and withholds it from the owner, he is, in my view, disqualified according to everyone. He is no better than one who denies a loan or deposit.
