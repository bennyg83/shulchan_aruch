Seif 28. “He is doubtfully disqualified,” etc. There is a question whether, when the relevant uncertainty arises from a dispute among halachic authorities, we also treat the person as doubtfully disqualified, or instead preserve his prior presumption of fitness like other legal presumptions. Responsa Pnei Moshe holds that we preserve the presumption of fitness. Urim VeTumim argues that the case resembles two against two, where we do not rely on the presumption. With due respect, both appear to have overlooked Tosafot in Bava Batra and the responsum of Maharit, part 1 siman 133. Maharit cites Tosafot concerning the rule that the halachah follows Rabbah regarding land and Rav Yosef regarding money. Tosafot there, in the name of Rashbam, write that a doubt arising from a dispute of authorities can be worse than two contradictory groups, because in two against two the two groups can be treated as though neither exists, allowing a prior presumption to remain. Maharit challenges them and argues the reverse: two against two more directly undermines the prior presumption, since according to one group the object certainly left its former status. A legal dispute, by contrast, need not defeat the prior presumption. Thus our issue is itself a dispute between Tosafot and Maharit. According to Tosafot and Rashbam, a doubt among poskim may prevent reliance on the original owner’s possession and likewise prevent reliance on a witness’s prior fitness, perhaps even more strongly than two against two. According to Maharit, two against two can defeat a presumption, while an abstract legal dispute leaves the presumption intact. I cannot presently decide between them. Nevertheless, since the very question whether to preserve the presumption is itself disputed, it appears to me that in that second-order doubt we should follow the original presumption and preserve the person’s fitness. See Maharit’s responsum there also for a novel discussion of who is considered the possessor when one person holds another’s property.
