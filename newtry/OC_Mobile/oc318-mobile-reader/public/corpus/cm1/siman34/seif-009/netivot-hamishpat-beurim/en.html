If there are witnesses there, etc. Sema's point is that the witnesses who saw the signers need not have read the place and date written in the document at the time of signing. If they had seen that the document said it was signed in Jerusalem while they actually saw the signing in Lod, they would have realized immediately that something was wrong and would naturally remember the location as a legally significant fact. The case is therefore one where they saw the act of signing and can identify the document or date, but did not then read the stated place. They now remember the day but not the precise location. Therefore they are not themselves witnesses disproving the signers' stated location.
Some say that with rabbinic interest only the lender is disqualified. Beit Yosef indicates that according to this view even with biblical fixed interest the lender may be disqualified only rabbinically. The Mishnah's list of rabbinically disqualified persons includes lender and borrower in an interest transaction. This must refer to actual fixed interest, showing that even the lender's disqualification is classified rabbinically under that approach. Urim VeTumim likewise states this explicitly.
