Because they assume, etc. See Responsa Chavot Yair, siman 104, who writes that although this wording implies that we attribute such an assumption to them even if they themselves do not make that claim, in his view it is clear that when they are before us we do not assume it on their behalf. Rather, we ask them, and if they do not answer in that manner they are disqualified. At first glance, this does not appear consistent with Responsa Rabbi Akiva Eiger, siman 96, cited in Pitchei Teshuva to Even HaEzer, siman 42, seif katan 18. There he writes that with regard to shaving with a razor, since unfortunately the practice had become widespread, the person may not have regarded it as a serious prohibition. Even though he did not offer that excuse before the beit din, this does not detract from it, as Responsa Maharit, siman 43, explains, because even where he admits the act a person does not render himself a rasha. See there, and see what I write below, seif 7, seif katan 21.
