Witnesses signed on a document do not become refuted witnesses unless they say in beit din, 'We wrote this document on its stated date and did not postdate it.' If they do not say this, then even if the document is dated the first of Nisan in Jerusalem and other witnesses testify that the signatories were with them in Babylonia that day, the document and its witnesses remain valid. They may have written it earlier and postdated it: while in Jerusalem on the first of Adar, they may have written it there but dated it in Nisan. If they say that they wrote it on its stated date and are then refuted, and other witnesses know the day on which they signed it or saw the signed document on a particular day, they are disqualified retroactively from the day it became known that they had signed it, because signed witnesses are regarded as though their testimony was examined in beit din at the time of signing. If no one saw their testimony or the document earlier, they are disqualified only from the time they testified in beit din that the signatures were theirs and that they wrote it on its stated date. They may have signed the years-old document on the very day of their court testimony and lied only by saying they had written it on its date. {Rama: Witnesses who intentionally sign a predated document are disqualified from testifying (Maggid Mishneh, beginning of chapter 23 of Hilchos Malveh).}
