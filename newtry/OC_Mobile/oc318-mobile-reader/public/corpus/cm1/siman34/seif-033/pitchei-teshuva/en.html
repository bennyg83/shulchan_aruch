And one disqualified by rabbinic law. See Sema, seif katan 55, and what I wrote above in seif 2, seif katan 6. Birkei Yosef, note 27, discusses whether the requirement of public proclamation applies only to monetary testimony or also to testimony concerning prohibitions. He cites Maharit, Even HaEzer, siman 43, and Radbaz, part 2, siman 43, as holding that for matters of prohibition no proclamation is required. Knesset HaGedolah, however, in a responsum cited in Edut BeYaakov, siman 17, strongly inclines to the view that even for testimony concerning prohibitions, those disqualified rabbinically require proclamation. This is also the view of Mahari ben Lev, part 4, siman 9, Maharshal in Even HaEzer, siman 36, Maharchash, siman 11, and apparently Mabit, part 1, siman 287, as cited by Masa'at Moshe, Even HaEzer, siman 45. I also found this view in a responsum of Maran, siman 10 in the laws of kiddushin, and in a responsum of Rabbi Avraham ibn Nachmias there, as well as in Tashbetz, part 1, siman 1. See there at length, and what I wrote in Pitchei Teshuva to Even HaEzer in the explanation of the order of the get, note 8, concerning this issue.
