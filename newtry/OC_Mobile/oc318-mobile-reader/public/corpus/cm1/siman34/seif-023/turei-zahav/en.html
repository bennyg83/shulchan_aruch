(Further there: shepherds, one shepherd, etc., through tax collectors and customs collectors, etc.) The Rosh cites the reading: “The tax collectors originally thought, etc.; the customs collectors originally thought that the law of the kingdom is law, but once they saw that they demanded more, they disqualified them.” It is clear that customs collectors were presumed from the outset to take more than authorized; nevertheless, they were initially validated because they assumed the law of the kingdom permitted it, until it became known clearly that they personally exacted more. At that point they were disqualified even without proof concerning a particular collection. Therefore, one who says that an ordinary tax collector is valid refers specifically to a fixed tax collector, because at first he is presumed to collect only the stated amount. An ordinary customs collector, however, is disqualified even without specific proof, since such collectors are generally presumed to take more. This is the reasoning of the Rambam and the Tur.
