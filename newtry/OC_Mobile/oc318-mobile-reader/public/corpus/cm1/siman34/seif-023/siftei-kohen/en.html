The Sema writes in the name of Nimukei Yosef, chapter Zeh Borer, that with respect to the particular testimony through which the witness became disqualified, he is invalid even before a public proclamation. This is difficult. Nimukei Yosef states that only according to the Gemara's initial assumption that a person can render himself wicked and thereby disqualified through his own admission. According to the conclusion, however, that a person cannot establish himself as wicked and we divide his statement, there appears to be no application of this rule. If witnesses testify that he borrowed money at interest, that is a separate testimony. Why, then, did Beit Yosef, Darkei Moshe, and the Sema record this rule? The matter requires study.
