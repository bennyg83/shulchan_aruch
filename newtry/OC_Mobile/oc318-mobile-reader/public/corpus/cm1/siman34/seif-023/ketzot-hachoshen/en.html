Not because of doubt. This is from Rivash. If a witness testified in beit din and afterward witnesses establish that he committed a disqualifying transgression, but we do not know when he committed it, we preserve his original presumption of fitness and say that his status deteriorated only now. In my work Shev Shemateta I discussed a difficulty from the law of a mikvah later found deficient. There, all pure items handled on its basis are treated as impure because there are two adverse presumptions: place the impure object on its original presumption, and the mikvah is presently found deficient. Here too, why not say: preserve the money under its possessor, and the witness is presently disqualified before us? Taz in Yoreh Deah similarly invalidates all prior slaughter when the slaughterer is later found to have forgotten the laws, combining the animal's original prohibition with the present defect in the slaughterer. The difficulty is even stronger in monetary law, where possession is stronger than ordinary presumptions. One distinction is that where a person later becomes an apostate without any prior indication, we have no reason to project his later sin backward. But where we know that this very disqualifying act occurred and merely do not know when, the case resembles the deficient mikvah. Nevertheless Rif's ruling concerning witnesses later shown to have signed falsely supports Rivash. Rif requires proof that the false signature was old. Otherwise we say they signed falsely only now. Shulchan Aruch rules this way below. Perhaps these authorities follow Tosafot's answer in Niddah that the special mikvah rule depends upon a defect that naturally progresses, "it was decreasing and continued to decrease." A moral transgression has no comparable progressive physical process. Bach appears to extend the "progressive defect" idea to a slaughterer who repeatedly produces tereifah, but Tosafot gives other explanations as well. Therefore the matter remains open to doubt in theory, though the explicit rulings of Rif and Rivash support preserving the witness's prior fitness.
