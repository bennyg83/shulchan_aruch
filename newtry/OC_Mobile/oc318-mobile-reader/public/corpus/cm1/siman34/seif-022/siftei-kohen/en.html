Informers. See below, siman 388, seif 8 in the Rama's gloss, where one opinion holds that a person who says, "I will go and inform upon him," becomes disqualified from testimony. See also below, seif 20, and Responsa Rabbi Shlomo Kohen, part 3, siman 15.
An apostate who repented. Terumat HaDeshen, siman 198, explains the reason as follows. An apostate lived among gentiles surrounded by all their desires, yet now comes to separate himself from all of them and accept repentance. The Sages therefore did not impose additional stringencies upon him, and we presume that he will certainly complete his repentance. So the Sema cites in his name. According to this reasoning, it would appear that the rule applies specifically to an apostate who joined gentiles. In truth, that is not so. The real principle is that once he accepts repentance, this suffices because we presume he will fulfill it. Maharik derived the rule from a kohen who married women forbidden to him, who vows to cease, performs the Temple service, and then descends and divorces them. Accordingly, the same rule applies to every apostate.
