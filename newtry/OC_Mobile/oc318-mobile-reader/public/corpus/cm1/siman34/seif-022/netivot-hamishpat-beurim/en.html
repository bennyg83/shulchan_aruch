We leave the person in his prior presumption. Ketzot HaChoshen asks why this differs from a mikveh later found deficient, where retroactive doubt combines with another adverse presumption. The answer is that those mikveh presumptions are evidentiary presumptions that help establish what likely occurred earlier. A presumption of possession over money is different. It does not clarify the historical fact; it is a procedural rule that money cannot be extracted without proof. Therefore it cannot combine with the present disqualification to establish retroactively that the witness was already disqualified. Ketzot HaChoshen himself recognizes this distinction in his rules of seizure.
