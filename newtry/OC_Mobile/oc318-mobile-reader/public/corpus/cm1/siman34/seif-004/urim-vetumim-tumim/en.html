They are therefore disqualified from testimony, but only rabbinically. Beit Yosef asks: if we do not accept the excuse that they thought they were performing a mitzvah, why are they not disqualified by Torah law? There is no difficulty. Ramban and Rashba write that all labor involved in the needs of a corpse is melachah she'einah tzricha legufah. Rashba in Shabbat, Perek HaOreg, explains that the statement that burial does not override Shabbat is according to R. Yehudah, who makes one liable for such labor. According to R. Shimon, there is no Torah prohibition at all, only a rabbinic one. R. Yerucham follows Rabbenu Chananel and Rosh, who rule like R. Shimon that melachah she'einah tzricha legufah is exempt from Torah liability and prohibited only rabbinically. Accordingly, those who buried the dead did not perform Torah-prohibited labor and are disqualified only rabbinically. The matter is straightforward.
