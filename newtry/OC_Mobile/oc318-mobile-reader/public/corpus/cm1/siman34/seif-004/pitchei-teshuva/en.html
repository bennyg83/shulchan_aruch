Anyone who committed a transgression. See Responsa Shevut Yaakov, part 3, siman 147, who writes that it is obvious that one who killed a person unintentionally remains valid for all testimony even before repenting. Although Orach Chaim, siman 53, seif 5 in the gloss, states that one who committed a transgression unintentionally, such as one who killed unintentionally, and then repented may serve as shaliach tzibbur, which could imply that before repentance he may not, this cannot be learned as a rule for testimony. A shaliach tzibbur must have an exemplary reputation, whereas for testimony anyone who is not a rasha is valid. Indeed, the Mishnah in the second chapter of Makkot expressly states that a killer who was exiled to his city of refuge may not leave there at all, not for testimony concerning a mitzvah and not for monetary testimony, indicating that his inability to testify elsewhere is solely because he may not leave the city of refuge, not because he is disqualified as a witness.
