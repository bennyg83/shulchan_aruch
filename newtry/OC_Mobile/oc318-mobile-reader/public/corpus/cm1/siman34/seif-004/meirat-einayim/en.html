Because they think, etc. The same applies to one who lent money against sacred books and then studied from them. Even according to the authority who holds that using them constitutes unauthorized use, he is nevertheless not disqualified from testimony because he believes he is performing a mitzvah. So writes the Mordechai at the end of chapter Elu Metziot, cited by Darkei Moshe.
One who raises his hand against his fellow, etc. Chazal associated this with the verse, 'Wicked one, why will you strike your fellow?' It does not say, 'why did you strike,' but 'why will you strike,' teaching that he is called wicked merely for seeking to strike, that is, for raising his hand. He is therefore disqualified from testimony rabbinically. Although raising a hand is a Torah prohibition, since it carries no lashes the resulting testimonial disqualification is rabbinic. So writes the Beit Yosef. The Mordechai in chapter HaChovel concludes that he is disqualified from testimony and from oaths until he repents from his wickedness and accepts judgment upon himself, even if the injured person did not sue him and another person did. Darkei Moshe cites this.
