That he had relations with his wife. Sema reads Rashba as saying that if one says, 'I had relations with your wife,' we cannot divide the statement to preserve testimony. The principal view, as Urim VeTumim writes, is narrower. In a capital case we may be unable to divide the statement by saying the intercourse was accidental because the case necessarily includes warning and because certain coercion defenses are unavailable. But where the testimony is used merely to disqualify a witness or prohibit the woman to her husband, we can divide the statement. Thus, one who says, 'I had relations with your wife,' can be believed regarding her intentional adultery while his own act is treated as inadvertent, for example because he did not know she was married. Conversely, if one says, 'My wife committed adultery with so-and-so,' we may treat her act as inadvertent because she thought the man was her husband, while his act was intentional.
