The witness and the person who took the oath combine. This is also evident from Tosafot in Bava Batra 34a concerning the ingot of Rabbi Abba, and from the Mordechai there. See Responsa Rama, siman 39, and Responsa Maharashdam, siman 195.
Some say. Maharshal likewise rules this way in Yam Shel Shlomo, Bava Kamma, chapter 9, siman 46.
They do not combine. The Bach rules likewise. Maharshal rules that the witnesses combine to disqualify him from taking an oath, but not to extract money based on the first and second claims, because each witness knows only that he swore falsely and does not know the other claimant's monetary matter. From that point onward, however, he becomes fully suspected, and the opposing party swears and collects, as explained below in siman 92. Maharshal adds that there is no distinction between an oath taken to exempt oneself and an oath taken to collect, nor between a Torah oath and a rabbinic oath. This is straightforward.
