The witnesses must now give fresh testimony for the purpose of disqualification; it is not enough merely to rely on what they said earlier during the monetary litigation, because then they had an interest. Sema. One might ask that if the litigant says the other swore falsely that he never borrowed, the essential testimony concerns the loan itself, and at that time he was an interested party. How can there be a valid beginning and end of testimony? The answer is either that at that stage he was a litigant and not within the category of witness at all, as Raavad writes, or that regarding monetary interest we do not require the beginning of the witness's status to have been free of interest in the same way. See siman 37.
