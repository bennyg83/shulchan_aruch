(Netivot HaMishpat, seif katan 17) For with partners, actual removal of interest is required, etc.; and this astonishes me, etc. Note: Shitah Mekubetzet to Bava Batra 43 states the Shach's question, apparently in the name of the Rashba's novellae: why did they say the witnesses must remove themselves from the interest? Even without removal, accept their testimony and adjudicate for the others but not for them. See there, where this question also rejects the Raavad's answer that a litigant is not considered initially disqualified because, with respect to himself, he never had the status of a witness. The Shach thus intended the words of an early authority, unlike this author's casual dismissal of the question with arguments that are not convincing. Beit din could adjudicate where one says, "give one hundred to the town's poor," so that all residents acquire their shares while the two witnesses do not acquire theirs, leaving the balance to the claimant or the lender's heirs. Likewise, the Gemara's question, "let them remove themselves," can mean that the witnesses waive their shares against the defendants. At first glance one must say that they cannot transfer their shares to others, because with respect to themselves they are disqualified from this testimony and therefore have no future right created through that testimony to transfer. The Ketzot HaChoshen also proves this interpretation. Nevertheless, the Rashba's own words in the passage beginning "but was it not taught" do not imply so. In the Rashba's novellae cited in Shitah Mekubetzet it appears that he considers two explanations: removal vis-a-vis the defendants, or removal vis-a-vis the other partners in this claim.
