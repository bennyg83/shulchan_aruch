(In the Tur, concerning this matter: both the lender and the borrower, if the interest is fixed by Torah law, are disqualified by Torah law, etc.) Tosafot in Zeh Borer write in one answer that even a lender who charges fixed interest may be disqualified only rabbinically, because he does not regard the act as sinful when the borrower gives the money willingly. One may say that the Gemara challenges Abaye, who does not require monetary wickedness, from the baraita interpreting “Do not join a wicked person” as referring to robbers and interest-lenders. This indicates that at least the lender’s disqualification is biblical. One cannot say the verse is merely an asmachta and the disqualification rabbinic, for then there would be no challenge to Abaye, who could agree that rabbinic disqualification requires monetary wrongdoing. Perhaps the principal verse refers to robbers, whose disqualification is biblical, while interest-lenders are included rabbinically, the Sages having enacted a parallel rule. This requires study. This passage is from Maharshal.
