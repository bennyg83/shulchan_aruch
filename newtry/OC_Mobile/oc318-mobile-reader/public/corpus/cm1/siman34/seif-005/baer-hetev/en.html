The community. The Sema writes that one who violates communal cherem or communal enactments should not thereby be disqualified from testimony, for if that were so hardly one person in a thousand would remain qualified. So writes Chiddushei Agudah. The same applies to one who refuses to enter into a communal enactment, citing Maharik. See Responsa Maharam of Lublin, siman 59.
Unintentional. The Rashba explains that otherwise no one could ever escape being considered suspect even concerning an oath of testimony, since he could always claim, "I forgot." For the law of an oath violated under compulsion and when such a claim may be advanced, see Yoreh Deah, simanim 228 and 232. Similarly, if one assisted another in opening windows or the like, which creates immediate waiver with respect to chazakah as explained above in siman 154, seif 7, and he later swore that he had not waived his rights, he is not disqualified from testimony. It is possible that he is telling the truth, because he did not assist with the intent to waive, but merely wished to humor the other person and intended that he later close them again. So writes the Beit Yosef. Thus the Sema. The Shach writes: I do not understand why he should be believed to say, "I was compelled." The Rashba's case expressly involved one who was warned at the time of the act and did not then claim compulsion regarding the oath, so he cannot later claim it. The Beit Yosef likewise cites the Rashba concerning one who swore to pay another by a specified time and failed to do so. He is not thereby disqualified, because perhaps he was compelled and did not have money available. As for a claim of forgetfulness, it depends on the time interval. If the event was so recent that forgetfulness is implausible, he is not believed. Shach.
