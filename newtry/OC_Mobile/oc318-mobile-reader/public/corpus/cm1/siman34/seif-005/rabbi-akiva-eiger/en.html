[Shulchan Aruch] But with an oath concerning the future. Note: The Rivash, siman 266, writes that it is possible that only with respect to taking an oath is he not considered suspect, but he may nevertheless be disqualified from testimony, because he transgressed a Torah prohibition. Even if he gave his testimony under oath, since he is not believed by virtue of his own status, the oath does not restore his credibility; alternatively, he has left the legal category of a witness. I later saw that Maharshak already cites this and writes that it is the primary ruling. See Knesset HaGedolah, siman 28, glosses to the Beit Yosef, letter 106. However, Responsa Rashba, siman 964, writes that he remains qualified even for testimony; see Knesset HaGedolah on this siman, Beit Yosef, letter 31. According to the aforementioned words of the Rivash in explaining Rashi, it is evident that there is no universal rule that everyone disqualified from testimony is also disqualified from taking an oath. We see that one who violated an oath concerning the future remains qualified to take an oath even though he is disqualified from testimony. Accordingly, the same may be true of one who violates other prohibitions, such as eating neveilot or tereifot, and the like: he remains qualified to take an oath, because we rule that one suspected regarding one matter is not suspected regarding the entire Torah. The Rivash derives the disqualification from testimony of one who violates an oath concerning the future from the fact that he resembles one who violates another prohibition. Since such a person remains qualified to take an oath, we can infer that one who violates another prohibition is likewise qualified to take an oath. We do not find an explicit rule disqualifying such a person from an oath except for a robber and similar persons who are suspected regarding that very matter, where even an oath does not make them credible, or one suspected specifically of violating oaths, who is therefore disqualified from taking an oath now as well. But one suspected of a different prohibition is not explicitly disqualified. See Responsa Rashba, siman 730, and Tevuot Shor, siman 2, seif katan 12.
