Or he was under duress. I do not know the source for saying that he cannot claim he was under duress. The Darkei Moshe does not state this. The Beit Yosef in Yoreh Deah cites the Rashba that after violating an oath one is not believed to claim duress, but there the claim concerns duress in taking the oath, and the Rashba states that he was warned at the time of the act and did not claim duress. Maharshal rules that one who swore falsely is disqualified, but one who swore to pay a debt by a fixed date and failed to do so is not disqualified where he may have been unable to sell property. The Rashba likewise allows a claim of duress, and Rivash and the responsum cited by the Beit Yosef indicate that one who failed to pay by the appointed time may have lacked available funds. Maharshal also permits a claim of forgetfulness where there are no witnesses that the lender reminded him. The Rashba, Baal HaTerumot, and Sema are stricter regarding forgetfulness, though the Ran limits that rule to testimony so recent that forgetfulness is implausible. Where sufficient time has passed, forgetfulness may be claimed. Possibly the same applies to inadvertence, though inadvertence may be less credible.
