Those who walk and eat in the marketplace before everyone, etc. If he eats in the marketplace but not openly before everyone, he is not disqualified from testimony. The Sages merely said that it is unbecoming for a Torah scholar to eat in the marketplace. Sema. One may also distinguish between walking through the marketplace while eating, which is particularly degrading and contemptible, and sitting in a fixed place in the marketplace to eat, which is beneath the dignity of a Torah scholar but does not disqualify one from testimony.
One who takes payment to testify, etc. All mitzvot must in principle be performed without payment, as learned from the verse, "See, I have taught you," meaning, just as I teach without charge, so too must you.
For this is not, etc. See Tumim. Not every testimony in which one witness received payment is automatically void by presuming all the witnesses took payment. If there are two additional witnesses who took nothing, their testimony is not invalidated under the rule that one related or disqualified witness nullifies the entire group. Likewise, if the paid witness testified against the interest of the person who paid him, the testimony is not void. See there.
It is permitted to take payment in order to go and observe. Even HaEzer siman 130, seif 21, implies that if the witnesses take more than an evident amount of compensation for lost time, there is still a concern of disqualification. See there. It appears, however, that witnesses who sign a deed of sale may accept substantial payment, because by signing they make themselves unable, at least ideally, to buy that field from the purchaser, and this restriction itself may have considerable value. Rama gives a similar reason there concerning witnesses to a get, who are prohibited from marrying the divorcee.

Because of suffering, etc. See Tumim. Such a statement has no evidentiary status for testimony. Its relevance is only to the law of claims and counterclaims. If a person sues solely because someone told him that a party confessed in a secular court after being tortured that he had money with a certain person, that report does not make the claim a definite one.
