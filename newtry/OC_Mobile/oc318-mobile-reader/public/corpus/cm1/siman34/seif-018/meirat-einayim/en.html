Who walk and eat in the marketplace before everyone. When it is not before everyone, the Gemara says that it is degrading for a Torah scholar to do so, but an ordinary person is not thereby disqualified. See the Perishah, where I discussed an ambiguity in Rambam's and the Tur's wording, and see Darkei Moshe, which cites two additional interpretations from Tosafot concerning one who snatches and eats and is disqualified from testimony.
Who are not particular about embarrassment. Meaning, we also suspect that they may testify falsely and will not care if the matter becomes known and shame and disgrace come upon them, since they are not concerned about embarrassment. So explains Rashi.
Anyone who takes, etc. This was explained in siman 9. It is a Mishnah in Bekhorot and is learned from the verse, 'See, I have taught you statutes and laws as Hashem my God commanded me.' Just as I was commanded by God without payment, and I paid Him no wage, so too you were commanded by me without payment, and so must you act for future generations. This is the meaning of 'See, I have taught you,' namely, learn from me and act likewise. This explanation appears in the Rosh to Bekhorot.
A judge who judged. In Hagahot Darkei Moshe, siman 9, I already cited the Rosh's wording, which resolves the apparent difficulty from the incident of Bar Chama, who killed a man and Rabban Gamliel sought to blind him, but Rav Pappa argued in his favor, after which Bar Chama undertook to pay Rav Pappa's tax for all his years. The Rosh writes that only an exemption from tax was permissible to accept, because Rav Pappa was independently exempt from tax as a Torah scholar. One must still explain why Rama arranged this law of a judge here, in the laws of witnesses, when it seems more appropriate in siman 9 among the laws of judges.
