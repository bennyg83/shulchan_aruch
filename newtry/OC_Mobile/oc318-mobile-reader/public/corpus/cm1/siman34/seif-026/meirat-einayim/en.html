That he lent him with interest, etc. This is likewise Rambam's wording in chapter 12 of Hilchot Edut: 'He said, he lent me with interest,' and nevertheless he is believed regarding the lender so as to disqualify him. This excludes the Mordechai in chapter Zeh Borer, which says the wording must be 'he lent with interest' without saying 'to me.' See Darkei Moshe, seif 37.
We divide his statement, etc. When he says, 'So-and-so lent me with interest,' he testifies about his fellow that he lent with interest and about himself that he borrowed. We divide his statement as though he said, 'So-and-so lent with interest,' without the word 'to me.' The Mordechai in Yevamot writes that we do not apply division of a statement when we must add an explanation or when doing so requires us to posit an uncommon circumstance. Nimukei Yosef likewise writes this. See the Ran at the beginning of Gittin; Darkei Moshe cites it.
Or that he had relations with his wife. See Responsa Rashba, siman 1237, which distinguishes between one who says, 'Your wife committed adultery with me,' where the statement may be divided, and one who says, 'I committed adultery with your wife,' where it is not divided. See there and Darkei Moshe.
Or that he violated his ox sexually. This is not comparable to the previous cases. There we divide his statement and he is not believed against himself or his wife. Here he is believed even regarding his ox, to have it put to death, for the Gemara says that a person is considered related to his wife but not to his property. The Tur writes likewise. Since nowadays capital cases are not adjudicated and therefore the ox is likewise not stoned, as explained in siman 1, the Mechaber was not precise to spell out this detail and cited these laws only for the practical matter of disqualifying the man who committed the act, which applies even nowadays.
