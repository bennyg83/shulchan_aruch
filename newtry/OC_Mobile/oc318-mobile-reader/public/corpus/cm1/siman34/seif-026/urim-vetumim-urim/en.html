A borrower, etc. Sema says this applies even where the witness says that he actually paid interest, contrary to Mordechai, who interprets the case as testimony only that interest was stipulated but not yet taken. Rama later appears to follow Mordechai. There is therefore no proof from Mechaber's wording that he rejects Mordechai, because Mechaber merely copied the language of the Gemara, and that language can be understood either way. Shach's explanation is correct. See Tumim.
We divide his statement. We treat him as though he said only, “So-and-so lent at interest,” and disregard the words “to me.” Mordechai writes that we do not divide a statement where doing so requires adding a new reason or assuming an unusual circumstance. Sema, following Tosafot in Ketubot. See what I wrote above in siman 46.
Or that he had relations with his wife. Rashba distinguishes. If one says, “I committed adultery with your wife,” the statement cannot readily be divided. If he says, “Your wife committed adultery with me,” we can accept the words “your wife committed adultery” and disregard “with me.” Sema. See Tumim, where I explain that under our accepted rules the practical difference is limited.
