To give him a gift. See Baer Heitev, who cites the Rosh that if one exempted the judge from a tax, this is permitted. The Sema explains that this resolves the incident in chapter Zeh Borer where Bar Chama accepted upon himself Rav Pappa's royal tax for all the years because Rav Pappa had argued in his favor. Knesset HaGedolah, above in siman 9, Hagahot HaTur note 8, objects that according to the Ramban cited by the Tur below in siman 163, seif 11, who holds that scholars too are obligated in the king's poll tax and proves this from the incident of Bar Chama, the original difficulty remains: how could Rav Pappa accept this benefit from Bar Chama? See there and Tumim, seif katan 18. Tashbetz, part 1, end of siman 145, offers another resolution. He writes that there is one situation whose permissibility is found in the Gemara: a person who advocates someone's merit before a judge may accept gifts from him, and this is not considered improper. Rav Pappa taught merit on behalf of Bar Chama and thereafter accepted from him payment of the royal tax for all the years, even though Rav Pappa was obligated to advocate for him, whether because of "do not stand idly by the blood of your fellow," as taught in Sifra, because of "do not fear any man," as taught in Sanhedrin, or because of "distance yourself from falsehood," as taught in Shevuot HaEdut. Nevertheless, Rav Pappa accepted the benefit and did not regard it as improper. We do not, however, find such permission with respect to the judge himself. See there. Birkei Yosef, above in siman 9, note 9, discusses this at length. Regarding Baer Heitev's question why the Rama placed this gloss here, Tiferet Yaakov explains that the Rama wished to teach that specifically a judge may not accept a gift from a litigant after having ruled in his favor, since it appears to be belated bribery. A witness, however, after giving testimony, may perhaps accept a gift from a litigant. Bribery does not apply to testimony, and the Sages' penalty against one who takes payment to testify applies because he was paid for the testimony itself; once he has already testified that concern no longer applies. See there, Beit Yaakov responsa, siman 128, Sha'ar Mishpat seif katan 10, and what I wrote above in siman 9, seif 2, seif katan 2.
