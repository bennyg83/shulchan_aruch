He is doubtfully disqualified; therefore he may not testify, and money is not extracted on the basis of his testimony, etc. It appears that this applies specifically to testimony given after the doubt concerning his qualification arose, because from then on he no longer has a presumption of fitness and we therefore leave the money in its prior possession. This differs from above, seif 23, where Rama writes that we retain a person in his presumption of fitness. There, even though he later became certainly disqualified, at the time he testified no defect was yet known concerning him, so we do not retroactively establish a defect merely because of a doubt.
