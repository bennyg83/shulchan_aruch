He is not believed to say, 'I acted inadvertently.' Shach explains this particularly regarding an oath. In ordinary prohibitions, a person is responsible to examine whether what he is about to do is forbidden, so a later claim of inadvertence is ordinarily weak. An oath can concern something otherwise entirely permitted, and one might therefore have thought that forgetting the oath is more plausible. Rama teaches that even there he is not simply believed. This applies especially to an oath regarding future conduct. An oath about a past fact already requires care at the moment of swearing and therefore resembles other prohibitions. The separate discussion of compulsion is also special to oaths, because halachah recognizes broader categories of unavoidable circumstances in vows and oaths than in ordinary transgressions.
