(Seif 8: if there are witnesses who knew that they signed.) The Sema explains that those witnesses did not see the date written inside the document, for otherwise the signatories would be refuted witnesses. This is astonishing. If so, why must the signatories say, 'We wrote it at its proper time'? The claim that they postdated it cannot apply here, since the witnesses saw the document on the very day it was signed. It therefore appears that the case concerns a deed recording an act of acquisition, which remains valid even if written and signed long after the acquisition, as stated in siman 43. The witnesses who saw the signatures on a certain day do not disqualify the signatories, because the acquisition may have occurred on the date written in the document and the writing and signing occurred later. The signatories must therefore say, 'We wrote it at its proper time,' meaning that the acquisition occurred on the date stated in the document. Otherwise, the acquisition may have occurred before the date stated, while the writing and signing were completed afterward, yet the document failed to state that the acquisition preceded the date of writing.
