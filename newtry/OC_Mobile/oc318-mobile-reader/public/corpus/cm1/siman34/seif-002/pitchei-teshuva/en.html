To testify together with him. See Birkei Yosef, letter 4, who writes that it appears permissible for the valid witness to come and testify alone, thereby obligating the litigant to take an oath. If, however, at the time they observed the event both he and the disqualified witness intended to serve as witnesses, there is a dispute among the authorities whether the testimony of the valid witness is also nullified, as explained in siman 36. He further writes that if the disqualified witness committed only a rabbinic transgression and the valid witness knows this, it is possible that he may nevertheless testify with him. Below, seif 23, it is explained that one who committed a rabbinic transgression requires public declaration of his disqualification, and before such declaration his testimony remains valid. The Rambam explains that this is so as not to destroy the rights of people who did not know that he was disqualified. Thus, even when the beit din knows that he is rabbinically disqualified, so long as no declaration was made they accept his testimony. The same should apply to the valid witness, who may testify with him even though he knows of the disqualification, since no declaration has been made. Birkei Yosef nevertheless leaves unresolved the case of one who violated a Torah prohibition that carries no lashes and is therefore, according to the Rambam, the Mechaber, and those who follow them, disqualified only rabbinically, but requires no public declaration. If only the valid witness knows of the transgression, may he testify with him, or is it rabbinically forbidden because the other witness is already rabbinically disqualified and no declaration is required, so that had the beit din known of the matter he would have been immediately disqualified? It appears to me, however, that according to the Shach, seif katan 3, the reason one may not testify with a disqualified witness even where the facts are true is that when three witnesses testify and one is found to be a relative or otherwise disqualified, the entire testimony is void by Scriptural decree, even though two valid witnesses remain. According to that reasoning, where the other witness is disqualified only rabbinically, this concern should not apply. Noda BiYehudah, second edition, Even HaEzer, siman 76, cites Responsa Beit Yosef, Laws of Kiddushin, siman 10, that a rabbinic disqualification is not strong enough to invalidate the valid witness testifying with him. Knesset HaGedolah likewise cites this below at the beginning of siman 36, Hagahot HaTur, letter 4. Tumim there, however, at the end of seif katan 11, disagrees. This will be cited in our discussion there, seif katan 1, and the matter requires study.
