(Rama, seif katan 4) One who raises his hand against his fellow. Note: See Responsa Beit Yosef, siman 12; Responsa Maharit, Even HaEzer, siman 43; below, siman 420; Yam Shel Shlomo, Bava Kamma, chapter 8, siman 68; Responsa Mabit, volume 1, siman 291; and the Bach here, seif 12. From the Mordechai cited by the Bach it appears that one who physically injures another is called a wicked person of violence. Accordingly, one may say that even a blow causing damage worth a perutah disqualifies the assailant from testimony by Torah law. This resolves the Maharit's question there. The Mordechai also disqualifies one who damages another person's property because he is a wicked person of violence, even if at the time he caused the damage he intended to pay. See also Ran's novellae to Sanhedrin 58, in the passage concerning a gentile who strikes a Jew, where he writes that the liability appears to derive from the law of theft: what difference is there between injuring a person's body and injuring his property?
