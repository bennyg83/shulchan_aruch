Once he received lashes, etc. See Tumim, where I clearly conclude that a person disqualified because of wicked conduct must have been qualified both at the beginning and at the end of the testimony process, just as with disqualification due to relationship, unlike Shach in this siman.
Even though they repaid, etc. What does it accomplish that they returned the stolen property? They have not yet received lashes or suffered any punishment for rebelling against Hashem's command and violating a Torah prohibition. Sema.
Until it is known that they repented, etc. Below in siman 46, seif 26, the Mechaber rules without qualification like the Rif that even if witnesses testify that a person repented from his wickedness in Nisan, so long as this was not established before beit din, all testimony he gives remains disqualified. He becomes qualified only from the time the repentance is formally established before beit din. See what I wrote there. Shach.
When they tear up their documents. Meaning that a lender who has a document containing interest must tear it up and forgo collection even of the principal. Sema.
Even to a gentile, etc. Hagahot Maimoniyot is uncertain whether repentance requires that he refrain from lending even to a gentile to the extent of what he needs for basic livelihood, because lending beyond his livelihood needs is in any event forbidden, or whether refraining from any additional lending to a gentile beyond what he requires for livelihood is already considered sufficient repentance because it demonstrates acknowledgment of the prohibition. Sema. For us there is no practical difference, since the poskim write that all ordinary earning needs are treated as livelihood needs. Therefore one may be lenient.
And he must return. Meaning that he is obligated not to retain prohibited money in his possession. Whether the recipient should accept the return is discussed below in siman 366, where the law distinguishes from whom repayment is accepted and from whom it is not. Rabbeinu Yeruham writes that the repentance of one who borrowed with interest requires that he not borrow even from a gentile. Sema.
