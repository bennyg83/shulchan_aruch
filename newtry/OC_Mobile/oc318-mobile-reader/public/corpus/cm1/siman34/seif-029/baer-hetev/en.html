To his qualification. The Shach writes that it appears that all persons disqualified because of sin, once they have performed proper repentance, are qualified even regarding testimony they observed while disqualified, for in this context we do not require that both the beginning and end of the testimony occur while qualified. In my humble opinion this does not appear correct, for why should this differ from siman 33, seif 13? One may also say that while disqualified he did not observe the matter with sufficient precision, since it was not something he regarded as legally relevant, similar to what the Mechaber writes in siman 35, seif 7. See Responsa Maharam ben Baruch, siman 51.
Who returned. Ran writes that according to Rabbeinu Tam and Rif, a robber becomes qualified only from the time witnesses testify before beit din that he repented, and every document he signed in the interim is invalid. Ran calls this difficult, because once witnesses establish that he was already qualified when he signed, it is retroactively revealed that he was fit to testify. The Sema notes that the Mechaber ruled without qualification and leaves the matter requiring study. The Shach responds that there is no difficulty, for the Mechaber's words in siman 46, seif 26, clearly accord with Rabbeinu Tam and Rif, as the Sema himself writes there in seif katan 63. Nevertheless, regarding the practical ruling, the Shach writes there that Ran's view is primary. See there.

To their owners. The Sema writes that this means that the penitent must do what is incumbent upon him, while the victim follows the law applicable to him. If the wrongdoer is a habitual robber, the victim should not accept repayment from him, as explained below in siman 366 and in Yoreh Deah, siman 161. Nevertheless, one who wishes to perform complete repentance must satisfy his obligation before Heaven and remove from his possession that which does not belong to him.
