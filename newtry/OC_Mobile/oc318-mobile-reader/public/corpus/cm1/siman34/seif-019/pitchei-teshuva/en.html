See also Netivot HaMishpat, who writes that there is a scribal error in the Shach and that the text should read: only to disqualify the person, not with respect to monetary matters; see there. See Noda BiYehuda, second edition, siman 6, concerning a case in which Shimon produced a deed of sale stating that Reuven had sold him one room and had also mortgaged another room to him. The witnesses signed on the deed had already died, but their signatures were authenticated from another source and the deed was confirmed by beit din. Reuven then brought witnesses who testified that on the date written in the deed of sale, the signatories were in another place far from the place stated in the deed, and there were many other documents bearing those witnesses' signatures. He responded as follows: Apart from what I established in Noda BiYehuda, Even HaEzer, siman 72, rejecting the Rivash's position, cited by the Beit Yosef in siman 38, that witnesses cannot be disqualified in their absence, in which case the other documents are certainly valid even if this deed stated, "we wrote it at its proper time," we do not even need that point. These very deeds are valid because the deed does not state that it was written at its proper time, as explained in Shulchan Aruch here, siman 34, seif 9. Even with a deed of sale, where a postdated deed is invalid, we nevertheless presume in order to validate it that the witnesses postdated it and informed the seller, as explained in siman 43 in Shach, seif katan 30. The Sema here, seif katan 21, does not disagree. He merely states the basic rule that a postdated deed of sale is invalid; he does not mean that the witnesses are automatically disqualified. I would add that we need to presume the deed was postdated only where it is not a deed recording a kinyan, since an antedated deed of that sort is certainly invalid. With a deed of kinyan, however, there is no need to presume postdating. We may instead presume that it was written after the date stated in the deed, that they recorded the date of the kinyan, and that the kinyan took place in the location where the witnesses actually were on that day. Tosafot state this in Sanhedrin 32a, s.v. chayshinan; see there. Although when recording the date of the kinyan they should also have written the place of the kinyan, as explained in siman 43, seif 20, the Shach there, seif katan 41, holds that if they instead wrote the place of writing the deed is not invalid after the fact. Even according to the Mechaber there, who invalidates the deed because the witnesses have written falsely, it appears to me that the deed is invalid but not the witnesses. Witnesses are not disqualified merely for false testimony unless they sought thereby to cause a monetary loss. Our deeds of sale are all deeds of kinyan, since the seller ordinarily makes a kinyan. We may therefore presume that the witnesses wrote the deed later, recorded the earlier date of the kinyan, and wrote the place where the deed itself was written. In any event, the other documents remain valid. Moreover, even if this deed did state "we wrote it at its proper time" and the witnesses were thereby shown to be false, it is still possible that they signed this particular deed only shortly before their deaths. The other deeds therefore remain valid unless witnesses saw this deed already existing on a known earlier date, as explained later in this seif. There is also room to say that when Sanhedrin 32a and Shulchan Aruch, siman 34, seif 9, say that perhaps they postdated it, this is because they wished to validate both the deed and the witnesses. To preserve the witnesses' personal qualification alone, even if we presume the deed was antedated they would not be disqualified according to those authorities cited in the Rama to siman 43, seif 8, who hold that witnesses are believed to say that they did not sign with respect to the date and did not notice that the deed was antedated. I do not yet decide this final point. In any event, for the reasons stated above the witnesses are not disqualified. End of his words.
