A wicked person is disqualified from testimony, even together with a valid witness, etc. The Tur writes: a wicked person is disqualified from testimony, as it says, "Do not place your hand with a wicked person to be a corrupt witness." From this I proved that even a valid witness who knows that his fellow witness is wicked may not join with him to testify concerning the matter they both saw. Since the wicked witness is legally as though nonexistent, and the judges do not know his wickedness, the judgment would in effect be completed on the testimony of the valid witness alone. The valid witness would thereby become a "corrupt witness" in the sense that he caused the judges to rule as though there were two valid witnesses when in truth there was only one. From this rule itself we learn that a wicked person is disqualified from testimony.
And needless to say a valid witness, etc., where the second witness with him is false. Meaning that the second witness knows nothing at all about the testimony. Although this is obvious and therefore the Tur omitted it, Rambam and the Mechaber state it so that one should not think the verse prohibits only joining with a person who knows nothing of the event. They clarify that this is certainly forbidden, and that the verse comes even to prohibit joining with one who knows the event but is wicked for another reason.
