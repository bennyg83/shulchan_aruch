Even a qualified witness, etc. The verse, "Do not place your hand with a wicked person," teaches that even where the testimony itself is true, a qualified witness may not join with a wicked witness. If he does, beit din may remove money on the basis of what appears to be two witnesses because the judges do not know of the other's wickedness. All the more so may he not join one whom he knows is lying about having seen the event. In that case it is obvious that he may not cause beit din to remove money on the basis of apparent two witness testimony when in truth there is only one valid witness. Shach in seif katan 3 labors to explain why, in monetary matters where witnesses exist only because people may lie, the truthful witness should not testify when he knows the underlying claim is correct. Why should it matter that the other witness lies if the first knows the truth and seeks to save his fellow's money? Shach answers that if one of the witnesses is found related or disqualified the entire testimony is void, so the qualified witness's testimony is itself nullified by joining the disqualified witness. Yet this does not resolve the matter. The qualified witness still knows that his fellow's money is in the defendant's possession. Why should he not use every lawful means to save the oppressed party? Moreover, why may he not join a false witness who was not present when the event occurred, so that they were never joined at the moment of observation, especially if he testifies only after the time needed for immediate speech has passed? According to all opinions, their later statements would not combine in the same manner. What then will Shach say about the Gemara's case of three creditors against one debtor, where one appears as litigant and two as witnesses? The debt may truly exist, yet the Torah forbids the scheme. The answer is simply that we may not use deceptive devices that Hashem did not command. This is a decree of the King, and we should not search for speculative rationales to evade it.
