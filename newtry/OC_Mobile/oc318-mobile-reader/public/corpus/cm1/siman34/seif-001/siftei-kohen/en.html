A wicked person is disqualified from testimony. See the laws of this siman in Responsa Masat Binyamin, siman 51.
Even though the other witness is qualified. See Chiddushei Aggadot at the beginning of Shevuot HaDayanim, and Agudat Ezov, folio 15d.
Even though the testimony is true. This ruling requires great study, for it is difficult to understand why one who knows the matter is true should refrain from testifying merely because the other witness is disqualified. Were it not for Rashi, the Rambam, Tur, and Shulchan Aruch, I would explain the Gemara as prohibiting one from initially joining a disqualified person in observing testimony. Perhaps they discuss cases where the testimony has legal effect only through two witnesses, such as witnesses to kiddushin, or sale of land where collection from encumbered property depends on two witnesses. Alternatively, the ruling may be literal. Since three witnesses who testify together are all invalidated when one is found related or disqualified, even if two qualified witnesses remain, Scripture decrees that the whole testimony is void. So too, one may not testify together with a disqualified witness even where the underlying fact is true.
