He may not testify together with him. See the responsa Chavot Yair, in the addenda at the end of the work, who writes that the practical halachah requires study where Reuven asks Levi to give truthful testimony for him against Shimon, but Levi knows that he himself is disqualified because of personal interest, kinship, or a transgression, while the beit din is unaware of the disqualification. Is Levi permitted to testify? In the case of disqualification because of a transgression the question is even more difficult, for what should he do? Even if he publicizes his disgrace, a person cannot render himself a rasha by his own admission; yet if he does not testify, he violates the obligation to disclose testimony. One might argue a fortiori from the teaching derived from “Distance yourself from falsehood,” that one may not join another witness whom he knows to be wicked. Certainly, then, one who knows this about himself should not join. The Chavot Yair therefore explains that the teaching there means that he should not join the other person at the event in order to become a witness, or sign a document with him. In such a case there may indeed be an a fortiori inference when he knows himself to be disqualified, and the law of repentance for those who violate oaths below, seif 33, likewise concerns such a case. This differs, however, where he already witnessed the event and is now asked to testify. See Ketzot HaChoshen above, siman 28, in the middle of seif katan 8, who cites his brother on this question. See also Urim there, seif katan 3, who cites the Chavot Yair briefly and writes that if the witness is disqualified because of kinship he certainly should not testify, or he should appear and tell the beit din, “I am a relative.” But if the disqualification is because of a transgression known only to himself, a person is not disqualified through his own admission. Moreover, the reason a rasha is disqualified from testimony is that he is suspected of testifying falsely. If he knows within himself that his testimony is true, why should he not testify? The same reasoning might apply to a witness disqualified because of personal interest, since the concern is that he may testify falsely. If he knows that his testimony is true, why should he not testify? See below, siman 37, regarding whether an interested party may testify adversely to his own interest. Netivot HaMishpat there agrees with Urim regarding disqualification because of kinship and also regarding a transgression, but disagrees concerning personal interest and rules that one who knows himself to be interested should not testify. See there. See also Responsa Chatam Sofer, Yoreh Deah, siman 11, cited in Pitchei Teshuva to Yoreh Deah, siman 2, seif katan 3.
