Disqualified rabbinically. Even HaEzer 42 rules that kiddushin performed before witnesses disqualified only rabbinically are valid by Torah law and require a get. This raises a broader question regarding witnesses disqualified by Torah law through wrongdoing. Today we lack ordained experts. Testimony proving their prior sin is received only by our rabbinic courts acting under delegated authority. By Torah law, if formal reception of testimony requires three ordained judges, how do we know that the person became disqualified on a Torah level? Perhaps he should remain biblically qualified. One can answer that our courts act as agents of the original ordained courts even in matters with Torah consequences. Tosafot in Yevamot explains that converts can be accepted today through this delegated authority, and a convert subsequently creates fully valid Torah kiddushin. Thus the Sages' power of judicial delegation can generate consequences extending into Torah law. This may rest upon the broader principle that the Sages have power, in exceptional frameworks, even to uproot Torah effects actively.
