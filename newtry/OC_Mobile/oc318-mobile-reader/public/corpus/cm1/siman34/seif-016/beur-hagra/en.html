(Collected note) And they are people, etc. Tosefta, Sanhedrin chapter 5, and Yerushalmi, Kol HaNishba'in, halacha 6: who are merchants in Shemittah produce? One who sits idle throughout the six years, and when the seventh year arrives begins trading in Shemittah produce. He is not accepted until another Shemittah arrives and he separates from it and makes a complete return. There in the Tosefta it also states regarding all these that Rabbi Yehuda said, "When does this apply? When, etc." In Sheviit chapter 7 and Sanhedrin chapter 3 Rabbi Yehuda says: when he has no occupation other than this; but if he has another occupation, he is permitted. How so? If he sat idle from his work throughout the six years and when Shemittah arrived began stretching out his hand and trading in forbidden produce, if he has another occupation he is fit, and if not he is disqualified. But if he worked at his occupation throughout the six years and when Shemittah arrived began trading in forbidden produce, even if he has no other occupation he is permitted. Rabbi Abba bar Zavda said in the name of Rabbi Abahu in the name of Rabbi Elazar: the halacha follows Rabbi Yehuda of the Mishnah. Rabbi Chiyya taught stringently: if he worked throughout the six years and in Shemittah began trading in forbidden produce, if he has another occupation he is permitted, otherwise prohibited. But if he sat idle throughout the six years and in Shemittah began trading in forbidden produce, even if he has another occupation he is prohibited. The statement of Rabbi Abba bar Zavda that the halacha follows Rabbi Yehuda of the Mishnah means not the Rabbi Yehuda of the baraita, but that of the Mishnah. It follows from both versions that the relevant case is where he sits idle and has no other occupation. So too Tosafot write regarding merchants in Shemittah produce, etc.; and "in all of them," this follows Rabbi Yehuda of the Mishnah because the Mishnah concerns one who sits idle, as stated in the Tosefta. (End of collected note.)
(Collected note) Provided that he does not, etc. The Rambam and Tur explain that Rabbi Yehuda's phrase "When does this apply?" refers only to the first clause, one who gambles with dice. So writes the Beit Yosef, in accordance with Rav Sheshet in the Gemara. Although the Yerushalmi in Sheviit applies it also to merchants in Shemittah produce, showing that it refers to the whole Mishnah, they maintain that the Babylonian Gemara disagrees with the Yerushalmi, from the Gemara's challenge to Rami bar Chama: "we learned, Rabbi Yehuda says, When does this apply, etc." According to Rav Sheshet this is resolved. Yet the difficulty still remains regarding the later clauses of pigeon racers and lenders with interest, etc. Therefore the Rambam and Shulchan Aruch write "and likewise merchants in Shemittah produce, and they are, etc.," even though the Yerushalmi lists both requirements, including that he has no other occupation. (End of collected note.)
