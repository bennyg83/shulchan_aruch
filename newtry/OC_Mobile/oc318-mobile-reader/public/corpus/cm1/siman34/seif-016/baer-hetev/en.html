In an inhabited area. This excludes the wilderness, where the birds are ownerless, and it is obvious that one is not disqualified for taking them, since the reason for disqualification is theft. In the case of setting one's dove to race ahead of another's dove, which falls under gambling, one is disqualified only if he has no other occupation. By contrast, one who sends out pigeons to bring in another person's pigeons is disqualified even if he does not live from the proceeds, because he is included among thieves, although the prohibition is only rabbinic. Sema.
Presumed. The Sema writes that according to Rambam, even if a gambler has another occupation, if it does not suffice for his sustenance and expenses, so that he also supports himself from gambling proceeds, he is disqualified. It follows that if he has other property and does not support himself from gambling money, he is not disqualified even if he has no other occupation. According to Rashi the rule is the reverse. It is surprising that the Rama did not mention the qualification stated there. See Responsa Mabit, part 2, siman 78.
