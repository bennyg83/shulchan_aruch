Who steal pigeons, etc. This is one explanation in the Gemara. Taking another person's pigeons involves rabbinic theft because of the ways of peace, but only in an inhabited area. In the wilderness the birds are entirely ownerless, and therefore the Mechaber specifies an inhabited place. The separate category of those who race pigeons, meaning one wagers that his pigeon will arrive before the other's, was not stated separately because it is included in gambling. Sema.
He is presumed, etc. See Tumim, where I explained Rambam's words. According to Rambam, one who gambles with a gentile is not disqualified. See below in siman 370, where Rama rules that the custom does not follow Rambam.
And likewise not only pigeons, etc. Although the earlier text did not explicitly mention pigeon racing, only those who cause pigeons to take other pigeons in an inhabited area, pigeon racing is included within the general category of gambling, as Sema explained above. Therefore the Mechaber writes, "not only pigeons."
