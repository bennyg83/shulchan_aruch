Tax collectors, etc. A customs collector assesses each person and the merchandise according to his own estimate. Therefore an ordinary customs collector is presumptively disqualified, because he commonly takes more than is due. A fixed tax collector, by contrast, collects the king's prescribed equal assessment from each person, so it is difficult for him to take more than authorized. Such a collector is therefore presumptively qualified.
Even one time, etc. Sema is uncertain how to interpret this. If it is known that he took more than was due only once, is that single act enough to disqualify him? Or perhaps the law means that once we know of one improper collection, we presume he did so many times, while if we know with certainty that it occurred only once, that lone act does not disqualify him.
Those who collect are disqualified. Even if they later returned what they collected, they remain disqualified, because they may not know to whom each amount should be returned. Furthermore, so long as they continue in the same occupation they may easily return to their former misconduct. Tosafos and Sema. See Tumim, where I conclude that until they actually return what they took they are disqualified by Torah law as thieves, and no formal public announcement of their disqualification is required.
Some explain customs collectors, etc. Even if the collector retains nothing for himself and transfers everything to the royal treasury, if he lightens the burden for some and increases it for others contrary to law, the Sages disqualify him. This appears from Hagahot HaRosh. The same applies to communal assessors, whose collections go to the community rather than to themselves.
