If it became known that they took even once, etc. Beit Yosef in Kesef Mishneh is uncertain whether tax collectors shown to have taken improperly even once are Torah-disqualified or only rabbinically disqualified, because they may rationalize that they work for the king and deserve compensation. He inclines toward Torah disqualification. Tosafot in Sanhedrin 25b support this. They explain that the novelty of the rule about ordinary tax collectors and customs officers is that even after restoring what they took, they remain disqualified until proper repentance, unlike an ordinary robber whose restoration is a central part of rehabilitation. Tosafot do not answer simply that their taking appears permitted to them and therefore creates only rabbinic disqualification. This suggests that while the wrongful money remains in their hands, their disqualification is Torah-level. The rabbinic novelty concerns the persistence of disqualification even after restoration. This is especially persuasive according to what I explained above concerning extortionists: once Chazal have disqualified the person and his presumption of fitness is impaired, we cannot automatically preserve him from possible Torah disqualification by saying perhaps he rationalized the conduct. Accordingly, Beit Yosef's inclination is correct.
