A butcher from whose hand, etc. The Tur brought here the law of one who has relations with a forbidden woman and the law of one suspected regarding forbidden relations; see there. The Mechaber and Rama omitted them. It appears that regarding one who actually has forbidden relations they relied on what was already stated, that those liable to death are disqualified from testimony, while the law that one merely suspected remains valid was written by Rama later in this siman, seif 25.
It came out as a neveilah, etc. In the Perishah I proved that the Tur and Rambam hold that one is not disqualified from testimony merely because he slaughtered and inspected animals belonging to others and permitted one that was actually a tereifah. First, it is possible that he erred and believed it was not a tereifah, for a person does not ordinarily sin without personal benefit. Second, even if he did so intentionally, he violated only the prohibition of placing a stumbling block before the blind, and because of that we do not presume he will testify falsely and thereby extract another person's money. Kesef Mishneh, chapter 12 of Hilchot Edut, writes likewise. Rather, the case is a butcher who slaughtered his own animal, it was found to be a tereifah, and he nevertheless sought to sell it to others. Although he himself did not eat it, since for monetary gain he was willing to cause others to stumble, we say that for monetary gain he may likewise testify falsely.
