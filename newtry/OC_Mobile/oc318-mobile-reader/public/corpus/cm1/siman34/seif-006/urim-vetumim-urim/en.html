A butcher, etc. Rambam speaks of one who slaughters his own animals and sells the meat to others. Kesef Mishneh explains that violation of “do not place a stumbling block” does not itself disqualify him because it does not carry lashes. Rather, because he slaughters his own animals and sells the meat, we presume he also eats from it and is disqualified for eating forbidden food. On that reading, Rambam had to say he slaughtered his own animals; if he merely slaughtered for others, there would be no presumption that he ate. Rambam's mention that he sells to others can then teach that even though perhaps he only caused others to sin, we presume he eats as well, or it may be stated because the path of repentance is stricter for one who sold forbidden meat. Sema instead explains that the issue is monetary desire: because the animals are his and he profits by selling them, the transgression itself is financially motivated and disqualifies him. This supports what I wrote above that monetary desire can disqualify even without a lashes-bearing prohibition.
