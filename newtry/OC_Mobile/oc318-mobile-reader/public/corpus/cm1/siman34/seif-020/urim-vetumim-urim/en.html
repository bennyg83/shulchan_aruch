One who hates his fellow, etc. The rule applies where there is genuine hatred such that we have reason to fear that the person may act falsely because of it. Sema. The Mordechai's source speaks more generally of one quarreling with another who publicly threatened, “I will go and inform on you,” and says that because the threat was made publicly it is not dismissed as mere bluster. It is possible that Mechaber added the word “one who hates” because in such a case there is no dispute, whereas in siman 388 Rama cites authorities who disagree in other cases. Yet Maharik, Mahari Weil, and a responsum of Rama imply that even regarding an enemy there is dispute unless he was already known to be suspicious. Therefore this rule remains difficult and disputed, except where the persons were already enemies and under suspicion, in which case Rama ruled that they are certainly not believed as witnesses.
