And likewise the borrower. See Chavot Yair, siman 100, who considers it obvious that the borrower is not disqualified even after the interest agreement has been written and signed, until he actually pays the interest. He writes that this is clear from the sugya in the second chapter of Bava Metzia concerning interest; see there. However, at the end of siman 104 he himself reports that the Gaon Rabbi Gershon of Metz wrote to him that at a gathering of leading Torah scholars they debated this point. Rabbi Gershon argued that the Mordechai in Sanhedrin, cited by the Sema and Shach below in seif 26, in the case of Bar Binitus, proves that the borrower too becomes disqualified from the time the interest is assessed, for the Mordechai writes there, "He lent to me, but took nothing," and the like; see there. In my opinion this is also indicated by Tosafot in Bava Kamma 30b, s.v. shtar, who ask why the lender was penalized so that he may not collect even the principal, since this would make the borrower a sinner who profits from his sin. See there, and see Rabbi Akiva Eiger's Tosafot on the Mishna, chapter Zeh Borer, note 10.
