(Shach 28, end of the gloss, passage beginning "I saw") It is more correct to say that when a person states as testimony, "So-and-so lent me money at interest," he has no personal interest in that testimony even if he originally appeared to assert his claim merely as a litigant and did not say that he was giving formal testimony. There is support for this from Tosafot, Kiddushin 43b, who ask why the witnesses should not be believed after swearing that they repaid the lender, since after the oath they are no longer interested witnesses. Tosafot's intent appears to be that they first swear to establish the truth of their own claim that they paid; after their oath exempts them from the borrower's demand, they return and testify formally against the lender so as to free the borrower from the lender's claim. One might object that once they have sworn that they paid the lender, they are personally interested in later testimony supporting that oath. The answer is that because they are under no obligation to return to beit din to testify, voluntary testimony given afterward is not treated as interested testimony merely because it accords with what they already swore. It is possible to force another interpretation into Tosafot, but the more natural reading is as stated. The Rivash therefore needed his lengthy analysis only to prevent us from saying that the testimony is entirely void because the witness also said, "he lent to me." The Rivash explains that the rule that testimony invalid in part is invalid in its entirety does not apply where the invalid portion is not testimony at all, but merely the statement of a litigant who has a personal interest.
