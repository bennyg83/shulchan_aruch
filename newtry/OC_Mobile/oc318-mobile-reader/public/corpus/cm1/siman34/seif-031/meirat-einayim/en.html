In the desert, the Rambam shall not do so as to his exodus from the ashes of the land which he commanded in June, and he shall bring in other gods, and he shall not return to the desert, nor in the desert
And some say that here too, etc., they must return the money, etc. Beit Yosef and Darkei Moshe cite this in the name of the Rosh. I wrote in Derishah and Hagahot Darkei Moshe that the Rosh actually implies the opposite. Specifically according to the view that gambling is invalid because it is an asmachta and therefore full theft, or in cases of interest and a butcher selling prohibited meat, where there is an actual Torah prohibition in the money, repentance requires returning it. Meisharim likewise writes that the gambling profits should be returned because they are an asmachta, but then cites a dissenting view that return is unnecessary and concludes that this is primary.
