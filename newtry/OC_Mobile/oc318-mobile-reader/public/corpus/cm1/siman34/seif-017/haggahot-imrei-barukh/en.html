(Shach, seif katan 28) I saw in the Rivash, etc. Note: See the questioner's words in the Rivash, where he writes that nevertheless the witness is not considered interested because he is not presently coming to claim payment from the other party. The Rivash's answer addresses that premise. It may therefore be that the Rivash concedes that where there is reason to say the witness has also come to assert his own claim, we suspect him of personal interest and disqualify his testimony. The Gemara's case, "he lent to me with interest," can then be understood as one where the witness did not come to beit din as a claimant, but only to testify in order to disqualify the lender with respect to another person. In such a case his testimony is effective even though he says that interest was taken from him. Regarding residents of a town, however, since the sefer Torah belongs to all residents and the town officials sue on behalf of everyone, all residents are presumptively claimants. Therefore two residents must expressly remove themselves from their interest before testifying. This differs from a case where one litigant seeks to disqualify his opponent and brings a witness who does not come as a claimant but only testifies that the opponent took interest from him.
