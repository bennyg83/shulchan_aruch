Regarding one who has intercourse with a forbidden relation, the discussion in the chapter Zeh Borer continues through “and so is implied by Rashi.” Tosafot, on the statement of Rav Yosef, “So-and-so sodomized me willingly,” explain that Rashi’s phrase “suspected concerning sexual immorality” does not mean that witnesses saw him commit intercourse with a forbidden relation, but that he behaves immodestly and secludes himself with such women. Tosafot therefore do not understand Rashi as the Rosh did. This can also be inferred from the explanation that Rav Nahman validates him because disqualification requires a wicked person guilty of monetary wrongdoing. If he actually committed intercourse, the halachah would not follow Rava; rather, the discussion concerns mere suspicion, which creates only rabbinic disqualification, and for that reason Rav Nahman requires monetary wickedness.
