One who is neither versed in Scripture nor Mishnah, etc. Meaning he has none of these qualities. Rambam explains that one who has descended to such a level is presumed to violate transgressions that come to hand, and therefore we require that he be known as one who observes mitzvot. The essential point is that he must be a person of mitzvot who follows an upright path. An am ha'aretz is not presumed to be pious. There are three categories. If it is known that he lacks all three qualities, he is completely disqualified even after the fact. If he certainly possesses all three, he is valid even initially, because he is presumed observant. If we merely do not know whether he is learned and do not know whether he conducts himself properly and keeps mitzvot, we do not designate him initially as a witness unless he is known to be observant and of proper conduct. If he already testified, however, we do not automatically invalidate his testimony.
We do not entrust, etc. Meaning we do not initially designate him as a witness. If he was nevertheless designated, beit din still should not receive testimony from him under the stated circumstances.
