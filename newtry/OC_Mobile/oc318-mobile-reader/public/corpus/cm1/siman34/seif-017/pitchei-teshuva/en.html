One who denies a deposit or a loan. See Responsa Chatam Sofer, Choshen Mishpat, siman 39, concerning a borrower who produced a receipt against the lender in which an alteration was visibly apparent. The document had originally stated five; the letter indicating five had been erased and the amount was changed to fifty. Some judges wished to say that the borrower thereby became disqualified from testimony and from taking an oath. Chatam Sofer responded that he did not know the source for their assumption that one who forges a document is automatically disqualified from testimony and from an oath. On the contrary, we find evidence otherwise in Ketubot 36b concerning a suspicious document from which collection is not made. Rashi explains that the person himself forged it by learning to imitate handwriting resembling that of the witnesses. Thus the Gemara concerns a case where he personally forged the document. Yet according to Rabbeinu Tam and Rabbeinu Chananel there, collection may still be made with that document after the claimant first takes an oath. This demonstrates that even though he himself forged it, he was not disqualified from taking an oath, even regarding that very money. Although we do not rule this way, but rather follow Ramban as cited in Sefer HaTerumot, that even with an oath he cannot collect that particular money, the authorities nevertheless do not disagree that he is not thereby generally disqualified either from testimony or from taking an oath.
