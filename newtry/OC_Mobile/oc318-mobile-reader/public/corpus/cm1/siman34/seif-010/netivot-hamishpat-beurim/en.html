He takes payment to go and see, etc. Shach points to Even HaEzer, where witnesses on a get are limited to compensation for lost time unless other justifications apply. The distinction is that payment to go and observe can be permitted when both litigants pay equally and the witness's compensation is for his trouble in becoming available, not for favoring one side. If one side alone pays him after he saw the event, even if the fee is formally promised regardless of whether his testimony helps or harms that side, the witness has a direct financial interest. In that case the problem is not merely a penalty against paid testimony but actual interested testimony, and returning the money afterward may not cure it. Even when both sides paid, if a long period has elapsed and the witness would have to return the fee if he forgot the matter, he can acquire a financial interest in maintaining that he remembers. The cleanest permitted case is therefore payment from both sides for the effort of going to observe, with the fee ultimately treated as compensation for trouble rather than for the substance of the testimony. This explains why get witnesses require separate reasons to permit customary fees, especially where they must later remember the event and testify. A get-delivery agent who says, 'It was written and signed before me,' can receive ordinary travel compensation because the document itself supports the matter, just as otherwise disqualified persons may serve as get agents under the special rules.
