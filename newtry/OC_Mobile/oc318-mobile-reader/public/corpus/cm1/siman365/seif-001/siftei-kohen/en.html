One who robbed, etc. See Responsa Maharash Kohen, book 3, siman 57.
And he pays the stolen property to each one. Baal HaMaor, in HaGozel Etzim and HaMafkid, rules like Rabbi Tarfon, that he places the stolen property between them and withdraws. Although Ramban disputes him in Milchamot, careful study shows that his position has not been conclusively refuted. Baal HaMaor's intent is that texts lacking the word 'rather' understand the Mishna as consistent only with Rabbi Tarfon, not Rabbi Akiva. Tosafot likewise state that according to those texts the Mishna does not follow Rabbi Akiva. Rav Hai Gaon and Behag also appear to accept that the conclusion follows Rabbi Tarfon, while requiring payment to each claimant where the robber swore falsely. Even according to texts reading 'rather, Rava said,' it can be understood that Rava retracts only the limitation that Rabbi Tarfon applies solely where no oath was taken, but not the identification of the Mishna with Rabbi Tarfon. Furthermore, although many authorities invoke the rule that halacha follows Rabbi Akiva against a colleague, Rabbi Tarfon may have been Rabbi Akiva's teacher, and Rabbi Akiva's ruling here is in the nature of a penalty. The Raavad and Baal HaMaor therefore maintain that nowadays, when monetary penalties are not adjudicated, the law follows Rabbi Tarfon. Consequently, although most authorities rule otherwise, the robber who is in possession may plead that he relies upon Baal HaMaor, Rav Hai Gaon, Behag, and the Raavad.
