[Shach, letter 3] The beginning of Perek Shnayim Ochazin, and examine this carefully. It appears to me that the Shach’s intent is as follows. Although the Rosh there holds that regarding a found object, where there is no fraud, the law of “two holding” applies even in a manner where it may be said that the object belongs to both of them, whereas with the case of one hundred and three hundred, even though there is no definite fraud, the law is that the money remains deposited, see there. Nevertheless, it is implied there that the rule of “whoever is stronger prevails” applies only when each litigant makes a definite claim, for then one may say that each will exert himself to establish his claim, unlike where they make uncertain claims. See Shach, siman 139. With a found object, however, where neither one is holding it and both make definite claims, even though each may think that he lifted it first, the one who is more convinced that he lifted it first will exert himself to prevail. Therefore, the Shach compares our case, where both make uncertain claims and it is evident that one of them is certainly fraudulent, to the rule of “whoever is stronger prevails”; if the item is in the hands of a third party, it remains deposited. But where there is no fraud and both make uncertain claims, the law is that they divide it. Examine this carefully. See Tosafot, Bechorot 18b, s.v. shemaniach, and examine this carefully.
