One might ask: when a defendant tells his opponent, "Swear and collect," is this not equivalent to accepting a relative as two witnesses, which would involve two defects and perhaps remain retractable? Ramban raises this question in his novellae to Bava Batra. Nimukei Yosef indicates that because the litigant did not simply believe the opponent outright but required an oath, it is not treated as two defects. From this we learn that if one accepts a relative as having the force of two witnesses only on condition that the relative swear, this likewise is not counted as two defects. After final judgment all agree that he cannot retract.
He cannot retract. Even if the defendant reversed the oath on the assumption that the claimant would take a solemn oath while holding a sacred object, and the claimant swore without such an object, the oath already taken still has one effect: the defendant cannot retract the reversal. Nevertheless, the claimant must swear again in the proper manner before collecting. This is Beit Yosef's view. Shach objects. Since the first oath does not enable the claimant to collect at all until he swears again properly, that first oath should have no legal effect and the defendant should still be able to retract. Shach's argument is strong. Perhaps Beit Yosef relied upon the many authorities who hold that once the acceptance was made before beit din the defendant cannot retract even before the claimant swears. Since one oath has already occurred, allowing retraction would create the possibility that one of the parties will have sworn in vain. To avoid desecration of Hashem's name and a purposeless oath, one can rely upon those authorities and require the claimant to swear again properly. This remains a strained explanation, but it is the best defense.
