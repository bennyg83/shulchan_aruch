(See there.) "He shall swear that he did not accept it upon himself." This requires study in light of siman 93, where it is stated that on any claim that does not create monetary liability except through an oath, no heset oath is imposed. Even though failure to swear will result in payment, heset still does not apply. Here too there is presently no monetary liability, so why should he swear heset? The Sema writes in seif katan 13 that this is a heset oath even though there is no denial of money, but I do not understand his reasoning, because heset too requires denial of a monetary claim. Perhaps the case here involves a difference between the claims the parties now wish to advance. In my humble opinion, if he accepted one valid witness in place of two and then denies having accepted that arrangement, he must take a Torah oath.
