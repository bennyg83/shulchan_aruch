(Collected note) And there are those who disagree. "And they exempt him," so that through this he will be embarrassed to retract. Proof is from Bava Batra there, which says, "and he swore, he cannot retract." At the beginning of chapter 3 of Bava Metzia, if he said "I will swear" and then says "I will not swear, etc." But some texts read, "he swore and cannot retract." As for the Bava Metzia passage, although he cannot retract, since he now burdens beit din, as stated there 39a, he does not acquire the double payment. From there, at first glance, there is proof for the opposite, since according to the alternate version the Gemara says he is merely delaying. If so, why must we say he is delaying? One can also say the case is outside beit din, or before he has left beit din. But this is impossible, since there it says, "he said, I will pay." This requires study regarding the Rav, who did not cite the dissenting view against the Shulchan Aruch, which states "until, etc., and he swore," and this dissenting view should likewise have been cited. (End of collected note.)
