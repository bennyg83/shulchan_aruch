Rather, 'I will pay.' See Shach. If before beit din one says, 'I will swear,' this is ordinarily treated as saying, 'I will comply with the law,' not as accepting only the oath while waiving his power to reverse it. This applies to a rabbinic oath. But with a Torah oath, which by law cannot be reversed, if the defendant accepted reversal and said, 'Swear and collect,' he cannot afterward say, 'I will swear myself,' because he accepted something beyond the strict law. Conversely, where a claimant holding a document reversed a hesset oath onto the defendant and the defendant accepted it, he can later reverse it again after leaving beit din, because the original reversal necessarily treated the claim like an oral claim. His acceptance was therefore only acceptance of the ordinary legal framework, not an irrevocable special undertaking. Ketzot HaChoshen's attempt to explain the sugya in Bava Kamma cannot stand, because the discussion is according to Rabbi Chiya bar Abba in Rabbi Yochanan's name, and the sequence proves that where one immediately says, 'I will pay and not swear,' without first asserting an exempting claim, he cannot later change from a liability position to an exempting claim.
