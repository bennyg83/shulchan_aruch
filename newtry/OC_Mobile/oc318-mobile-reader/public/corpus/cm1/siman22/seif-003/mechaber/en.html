If a beit din required a person to take an oath to the opposing party, and the opposing party said, 'Swear to me by the life of your head and be exempt,' or, 'Swear to me by the life of your head and I will give you everything you claim,' then if a formal act of acquisition was made, he may not retract. Without such an act, he may retract until the judgment is finalized. Once the judgment is finalized and the person swore as requested, the other may not retract and must pay. The same applies when one liable for a heses oath reverses the oath: after a formal act of acquisition, or after the other party takes the reversed oath, he may not retract. Likewise, if one who was not obligated to swear said, 'I will swear an oath to you,' a formal act of acquisition prevents retraction. Without one, even if he accepted the oath in beit din, he may retract until the judgment is finalized and he swears. {Rama: If one obligated to swear says before the beit din, 'I do not wish to swear; I will pay,' and leaves the beit din, he may not retract, because this constitutes his final judgment. Others disagree. If one obligated to take a Torah oath tells the other, 'Swear and collect,' and the other swears without holding a sacred object, the first may not retract (Beit Yosef).}
