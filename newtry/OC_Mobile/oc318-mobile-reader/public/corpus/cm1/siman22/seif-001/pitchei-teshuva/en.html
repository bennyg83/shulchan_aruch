One who accepted such a person upon himself. Bach, at the end of seif 4, writes that where the person accepted is a relative, the party cannot retract only if he knew of the relationship. If he claims, 'I did not know he was a relative,' he takes a hesset oath and may retract. Shaar Mishpat, seif katan 1, explains that if the relative is someone with whom he is regularly familiar, the claim of ignorance is not believed, as shown in siman 36, seif 2. If he claims that he did not know the person was otherwise disqualified, one distinguishes: where the disqualification is rabbinic and requires a formal proclamation by beit din, he is not believed to claim ignorance, because judicial proclamations become publicly known, as Shach writes in siman 71, seif katan 31. Where the disqualification requires no proclamation, he is believed, with an oath, to say he did not know of it.
