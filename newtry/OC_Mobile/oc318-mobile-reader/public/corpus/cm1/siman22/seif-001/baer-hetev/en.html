Two disadvantages. The Sema writes that if a party accepted one valid judge in place of three judges and the case was concluded, he cannot retract. This should not be considered two deficiencies, because the judge is one valid person. The Shach disagrees, noting that the Rosh and other poskim speak of accepting one valid judge in place of two, but do not say in place of three. See there.
The judgment. The Sema writes that Rama's earlier gloss, "and some disagree," belongs here as well.
As explained. The Sema writes that this means where the judges said, "You, so-and-so, are entitled, and you are liable," and certainly where they said, "Go and pay him," which is a definitive ruling. By contrast, if they merely said, "You are liable to give him," it may sound as though they are still uncertain. The Shach rejects this distinction between "you are liable to give him" and "you, so-and-so, are entitled," etc. He concludes that for practical halacha the matter is not considered finally adjudicated until they say, "Go and pay him," or until the beit din has concluded the case and the parties have left the court. This is the primary ruling.
Who erred. The Sema writes that the practical difference is that a litigant may then advance a different argument, or be granted time by the second beit din, because the first proceeding is void once the judges retract. The Shach writes that the difference is that the original judgment remains effective so long as the opposing party does not know that the judges erred. He further writes that all of this concerns a case before money has actually been transferred. If money was already transferred, even where the judges erred, what was done remains effective, contrary to the Ramban. See below, siman 25, seif katan 24.
Leaders. Any matter formally undertaken before three persons cannot later be retracted. Likewise, communal enactments do not require a kinyan, such as exempting someone from taxes and similar matters. See the end of siman 163. Sema.
He shall swear. Meaning a shevuat heset. This judge is not considered a witness sufficient to generate a Torah oath. Although he is not disqualified as an interested party, he still is not a valid witness here because the case concerns a relative or otherwise disqualified person. The Shach concludes that if there are two valid judges, their testimony is sufficient to impose monetary liability. See Responsa R. Shlomo Kohen, part 2, siman 83, and below, siman 23.
