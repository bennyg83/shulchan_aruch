(Collected note) And there are those who disagree, etc., but if, etc. Rif. His proof is from Shevuot, chapter HaDayanim, where one said to his fellow, "you are trusted by me like two." This necessarily concerns a kinyan, even though it is after the conclusion of judgment, as with witnesses where he made him a witness. Nevertheless he may retract even from ordinary acceptance of trustworthiness, because there are two disadvantages: he trusted the litigant himself and trusted him as two witnesses. He did not merely trust him so that he could not swear against him, for against two witnesses one cannot swear; rather he trusted the litigant himself as two witnesses. This is the meaning of siman 71, seif 2, that such acceptance is effective, etc., even with ordinary language of trustworthiness. This is unlike the Shach, who understands that the Rif established the case as involving a kinyan only because of the difficulty from our sugya. That is not so, as explained. (End of collected note.)

(Collected note) And regarding testimony, etc. As stated in Shevuot 32, witnesses are like the conclusion of judgment, and as stated in the chapter Yesh Nochalin: once he has sworn, he cannot retract. What "conclusion of judgment" is there in testimony? Rather, anything after which nothing remains to be done is like the conclusion of judgment, just as with judges who say, "So and so, etc.," after which nothing remains. (End of collected note.)
