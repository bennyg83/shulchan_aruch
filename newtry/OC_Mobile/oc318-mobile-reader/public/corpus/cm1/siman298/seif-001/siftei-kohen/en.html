As for the Sema's challenge from the Beit Yosef in siman 90, seif 19, the words of the Beit Yosef there are themselves difficult, because the Maggid Mishneh's statement that the primary view is that of the Raavad does not refer to the issue of whether he should have known. For example, one deposited a purse with him, etc., and he cannot swear, so he pays. The Semag, positive commandment 88, page 171a, writes concerning this: "In this case I say that there is only a rabbinic oath, since he does not admit to a quantified amount by saying, 'there were so many dinars in it and as to the rest I do not know.' With a rabbinic oath we do not apply the rule that one who is obligated to swear but cannot swear must pay." The Bach cites this above, siman 292, seif 15. This corresponds to the Raavad's objection in chapter 5 of Hilchot She'eilah. The Ran in Perek Shevuat HaDayanim, however, already cites Rambam's answer rejecting this objection, and the Maggid Mishneh there likewise writes that Ramban rejected this argument with sound reasoning in his novellae to Shevuot. I also find the Semag difficult, because he himself writes in positive commandment 70, page 148b, in accordance with Rambam and the Mechaber below, siman 388, end of seif 1; and in positive commandment 94, page 176d, he likewise writes in accordance with Rambam and the Mechaber above, siman 72, seif 12. See there. I later found that the Shiltei Gibborim at the end of Perek HaKones and in Perek Chezkas HaBatim, page 177, raises this same difficulty against the Semag and leaves it requiring study. He writes, however, concerning the Maggid Mishneh's statement that Ramban had already rejected this argument with sound reasoning in his novellae to Shevuot, that he did not know Ramban's reason. Had he seen Ramban's words cited by the Ran in Perek Shevuat HaDayanim, he would not have written this.
