Every custodian, etc., with whom a purse was deposited, etc. The Mechaber rules simply like the Rambam. The Beit Yosef also accepts that position. In the Derishah I expressed surprise, because the Raavad, Rosh, Tur, and Maggid Mishneh in Rashba's name all reject the Rambam where one deposited a sealed purse. They hold that the depositor cannot recover whatever amount he claims without a serious oath, because the custodian was not expected to know what was inside a sealed sack, purse, or box. The Beit Yosef himself writes in siman 90 that the Maggid Mishneh concludes that the primary view is like the Raavad in the case of a full purse. In Shulchan Aruch siman 90:10 the Mechaber again rules like the Rambam, but adds that some say the custodian swears that he does not know and is exempt. His words there are ambiguous regarding which earlier clause the dissent addresses. In the Derishah I offered two explanations. One is that it refers to the Rambam's rule where a sealed sack was deposited and the claimant says it contained pearls, while the custodian says he knows it contained pearls but does not know how many. According to the dissenters, the special remedy enacted for a robbed claimant was not enacted for an ordinary deposit. The Yerushalmi source concerns fire, as the Rosh writes in the name of many commentators, and the rule "since he cannot swear, he pays" does not apply because the custodian was not expected to know. If that is the meaning, the Mechaber should not have ruled here simply like the Rambam after citing the dissent there. The second explanation is that the dissent in siman 90 refers instead to a custodian who says, "Perhaps it contained dross," and those authorities hold that we do not presume the claimant's demand necessarily included the sack itself. The case then resembles one who claims wheat and the defendant says he does not know whether it was wheat or barley, and they hold the custodian swears that he does not know and is exempt. According to that explanation there is no contradiction here, because the present case does not involve a claim that perhaps the contents were dross. See my comments there.
