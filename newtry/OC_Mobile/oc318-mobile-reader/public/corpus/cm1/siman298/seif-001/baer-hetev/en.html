A custodian. The Sema writes that one should be astonished that the Mechaber rules without qualification according to the Rambam, since the Raavad, Rosh, Tur, and Maggid Mishneh in the name of the Rashba all rejected the Rambam's view regarding a deposited purse, holding that the depositor cannot recover without a stringent oath because the custodian was not expected to know what had been deposited in a sack, purse, or sealed chest. See there. The Shach likewise writes that in siman 72 he concluded according to the Raavad and those who agree with him.
How much. This applies specifically where the purse contained things ordinarily placed in a purse. Otherwise he is exempt, as below at the beginning of siman 388. Shach.
And pays. Semag, positive commandment 88, p. 171a, writes: In this case there is only a rabbinic oath, because he does not admit to a quantified matter by saying, "It contained such and such dinars and I do not know the rest." With a rabbinic oath we do not say that one who is obligated to swear but cannot do so must pay. This is cited by the Bach in siman 292, seif 15, and parallels the Raavad's objection in chapter 5 of Hilkhot She'elah. But the Maggid Mishneh there and the Ran in chapter Shevuat HaDayanim cite the Ramban, who rejects this objection. I also find difficulty with the Semag, since he himself writes in positive commandment 70, p. 148b, like the Rambam and Mechaber in siman 388, end of seif 1, and in positive commandment 94, p. 176d, like the Mechaber in siman 72, seif 12. I later found that the Shiltei Giborim at the end of chapter HaKones and in chapter Chezkas HaBatim raises this contradiction in the Semag and leaves it requiring study. Ibid.
