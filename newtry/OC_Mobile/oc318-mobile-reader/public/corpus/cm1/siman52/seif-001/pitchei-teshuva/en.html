The principal. See Baer Heitev, through his conclusion that the matter appears to be a doubt in law. See Mishneh LaMelech, chapter 4 of Hilchot Malveh, law 6. Regarding what Baer Heitev further cites from Gidulei Terumah, that where rabbinic interest was included together with principal the Sages did not decree against collection of the principal on account of the interest except in a Torah-level case, Rabbi Akiva Eiger notes that Tosafot do not appear to agree. When Tosafot answer that the passage concerns rabbinic interest, they still add the new rule that for a rabbinic transgression the witness is disqualified only if he is wicked through monetary gain. According to Gidulei Terumah's reasoning, Tosafot would not have needed this addition, because once the case is rabbinic interest they could simply have said that principal and interest were included together and the witnesses did not know they were disqualified. It follows that Tosafot hold that even with rabbinic interest we decree regarding the principal because of the interest. Shaar Mishpat brings the same proof from Tosafot and adds further proofs. He concludes that this also appears from the Mechaber in Yoreh Deah, siman 161, seif 11: even with rabbinic interest, if principal and interest were included together, the principal cannot be collected.
