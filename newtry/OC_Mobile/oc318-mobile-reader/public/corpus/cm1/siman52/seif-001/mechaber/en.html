A Document Containing Interest, or a Torn Document. Two seifim. If a document expressly states interest, the interest cannot be collected, but the principal may be collected even from encumbered property. If the principal and interest were combined into one sum, the document is invalid because it could be used to collect the interest. {Rama: If the debtor admits the debt, some say he must pay the principal (Maharick, shoresh 17), while others exempt him because the lender is penalized. If the lender acted through an agent and the agent drafted a document combining principal and interest, the lender nevertheless collects the principal, because he sent the agent to improve matters, not to corrupt them. See Yoreh De'ah, siman 161, for further laws.} If a document comes before us bearing the characteristic tear made by beit din, namely through the witnesses, date, and essential text, or torn crosswise, it is invalid. If the tear was plainly made with a knife, it is invalid even without a crosswise tear. A document torn in two is worse than one bearing a beit din tear and is invalid. {Rama: If witnesses establish that it was torn accidentally, its law is like that of an erased debt document, as explained above in siman 41. If its tear was not the ordinary beit din tear but the judge sees that it originally had such a tear and was further torn to conceal it, no collection is made; otherwise, no such concern is entertained. See also siman 86 and siman 100.}
