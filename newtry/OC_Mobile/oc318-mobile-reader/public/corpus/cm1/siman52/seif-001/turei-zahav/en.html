(Seif 1: and some say he is exempt.) I have not found anyone who holds this view. The Sema writes that it is the opinion of Tosafos, but this is surprising. Mordechai in Perek Eizehu Neshech writes in accordance with the same Tosafos, namely that the Sages imposed a penalty only where the interest was incorporated into the principal, because in that case there is concern that he will collect the entire amount, whereas they did not impose the same penalty on separately stated interest. Nevertheless, Maharik explains Mordechai to mean that when interest is incorporated, collection may not be made by virtue of the document, but if the borrower admits the debt, the lender may collect the principal. He proves this from the Gemara according to the Sages who disagree with Rabbi Meir. Thus the Tosafos cited above have no relevance to the view presented here. I found no source for this "some say" other than the Tur in Yoreh Deah, siman 161, who writes that where interest is incorporated, neither principal nor interest is collected. This could imply that even if the borrower admits it, the principal is not collected, just as the Gemara's wording regarding Rabbi Meir was analyzed above. It nevertheless appears that we do not rule in accordance with this "some say" at all, since Maharik explicitly rules that the principal is collected, and this is also the implication of Mordechai and Hagahot Maimoniyot. The Tur too can be explained as speaking where there is no admission.
