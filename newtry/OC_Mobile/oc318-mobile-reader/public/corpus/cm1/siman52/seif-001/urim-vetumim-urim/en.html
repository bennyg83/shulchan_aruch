But he collects the principal, etc. The witnesses are not disqualified because they did not understand the prohibition of placing interest as applying to witnesses. See what I wrote in Tumim and above in siman 34.
Invalid, etc. See Tumim, where I wrote that one may possibly claim, "I follow Ramban," according to whom even in this case the principal may be collected. See what I wrote there.
Because he may come, etc. Even if he writes on the document that only the principal may be collected, it does not help, because the document was originally written in an invalid form, Sema. Shach writes that if this notation was written before the defect was presented to beit din, it helps. This requires study. See what I wrote above in siman 43 regarding antedated documents.
If the obligor admits. The same applies if there are other witnesses, or according to most poskim even the same witnesses, because they were not disqualified, since they did not understand the prohibition as applying to them, Shach.
Some say he is exempt, etc. This is a penalty because the document could have been used to collect the interest as well. One cannot object that the sinner thereby profits, because the borrower too transgresses, and the lender cannot force him into the transaction. What could the lender have done? See Tumim, where I established the source of Rama's ruling. Nevertheless, as practical law I concluded with Shach that we do not follow this dissent. If the obligor admits the debt, he must pay the principal.
A beit din tear. Meaning the type of tear beit din makes when it invalidates a get or a document, Sema.
Provided it is torn, etc. See Tumim. Ran holds that a tear in any one of three specific locations invalidates the document. I left this requiring study because Rivash appears to disagree and to prove from the Gemara otherwise. See there.
Or its valid and established formula is torn. Shach holds that the tear must run across the face of the entire document. See Tumim, where I wrote that this does not appear from Rivash. Nevertheless, everyone agrees that if only a few words are torn, the document is not invalid.
Torn into two pieces and not connected at all. Sema writes that if it tore at the folds, it is not invalid. Shach questions this where there is a beit din tear or where it is completely torn and no longer attached. The accepted practice is to rule like Sema. This is common and creates no indication of invalidity.
Its law, etc. Only where it was torn in a location that invalidates the document do beit din prepare a validation and another document, as above. If it was not torn in an invalidating place, beit din do not prepare another document for him, Sema in the name of Rashba.
Otherwise we are not concerned. We do not say that perhaps the document was originally torn where the hole now appears and that the holder later enlarged the tear from that hole. We do not presume a defect in order to invalidate a document, Rashba. Rivash, siman 383, indicates that an actual cut is different and is itself a defect, because such a cut is not ordinarily produced by itself and usually results from deliberate action. We therefore suspect that there may originally have been an invalidating tear there. See Rivash and Tumim.
