Even from encumbered property. Since the Gemara there says that Reish Lakish's statement applies to the dispute, this implies that according to the Rabbis he collects even from encumbered property.
But if. This follows from the Gemara's statement there, "even if you say it follows the Rabbis," etc. If the contrary were true, we should issue the same decree here as well.
Nevertheless, if. We do not rule like Rabbi Meir, who imposes a penalty; rather, the restriction is only because of a preventive decree, as with an antedated document.
And some say. This was derived from Tosafot there and Bava Kamma 32, that according to Rabbi Meir, even if the borrower admits the debt, if there are witnesses he is exempt. This is incorrect. Tosafot there, s.v. "document," write according to Rabbi Meir, "and one should not ask," etc. There he does not cause the lender a loss, and Tosafot derive the inclusion of interest from an antedated promissory note, s.v. "and the Sages say," and likewise in Bava Metzia; see there.
(Collected note) And some say that he is exempt, etc. As Tosafot write according to Rabbi Meir, the phrase "he does not collect," etc., implies even such a case. Furthermore, in Bava Kamma there it is stated that according to Rabbi Meir they penalized the principal because of the profit, and there they penalized him completely. Moreover, it is taught in the Tosefta: one who produces a document containing interest, they tear it; if he comes before beit din, they tear it. Even concerning an oral loan it is taught there: one who lends another person at interest and comes before beit din is penalized and may collect neither principal nor interest. Rabbi Meir would say: a document containing interest, etc. See Tosafot there and elsewhere. From this, the cited authority derived that even according to the Rabbis the same applies in such a case. This is incorrect, however, because the Rabbis impose no penalty at all. Rather, in such a case Tosafot write that it resembles an antedated document, from which collection is barred because of a preventive decree, but there is no penalty. The Taz and Shach have already challenged this view.
Nevertheless. Furthermore, there is no agency for the commission of a transgression. Ibid.
(Collected note) Nevertheless, if, etc. This follows the latter opinion, but according to the first opinion there is no practical difference. Shach.
And if it is evident. There is no distinction based merely on whether the tear was made by beit din; rather, such a tear is recognizable as deliberate. This is the meaning of the statement there, "If it was torn, it is invalid; if it became torn," etc., meaning deliberately as opposed to accidentally. "And what constitutes being torn" means: from where do we know this?
It was torn in two. In such a case it no longer has the status of a document at all, and certainly so where it was done intentionally.
And if there is. As above. This is derived from a document that was erased or burned, as explained in siman 41; see there.
And if it was, etc. Bava Metzia 108.
(Collected note) But otherwise, etc. We do not invalidate a document because of uncertainty. As explained above, whenever it is not evident that the document was torn intentionally, such as with a tear of beit din, we do not invalidate it. This accords with the beginning of the fourth chapter of Sanhedrin and the chapter Get Pashut, "perhaps they dated it later," etc.
