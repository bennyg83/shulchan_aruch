The principal. This applies even where the document expressly includes Biblical interest, as Tosafot, the Rosh, and other authorities write, and as the Tur and Shulchan Aruch rule in Yoreh Deah at the end of siman 161, unlike the Taz there. The Shach states that he already objected to the Taz in Nekudot HaKesef. The Ramban, however, holds that the witnesses are disqualified, and the Bach cites this as his conclusion. The Ran in a responsum likewise cites such an opinion, and the Maggid Mishneh cites the Ramban and calls his view primary. The Bach and Gidulei Terumah likewise conclude this way. Therefore the matter is a doubt in law, with the practical consequence that if the holder of the document seized property, it is not taken from him. Gidulei Terumah further writes that where only rabbinic interest was incorporated together with the principal, everyone agrees that collection may be made from encumbered property, because the Sages did not decree the principal on account of rabbinic interest, and according to the Ramban the witnesses were not disqualified since they violated no Biblical prohibition. This assumes it is known that the witnesses were aware the document contained interest; otherwise even the Ramban would validate them. See siman 34, seif 3, in the gloss. The Shach further writes that the Sema's statement that a guarantor has the same status as lender and borrower follows some authorities, but others treat the guarantor like the witnesses. This latter view appears from the Mechaber in the Beit Yosef to siman 34 and in the Shulchan Aruch there, seif 10, where he lists lender and borrower as disqualified by an interest transaction but does not list guarantor and witnesses with them.
Invalid. The Sema writes that it does not help to write on the document that the interest may not be collected, because the instrument was originally written in an invalid manner. The Shach explains that this is true once the defect became known in beit din, at which point there is no repair. But if the lender writes on the document before it is presented in beit din, the correction is effective, as stated in siman 45, seif 14. See also siman 43, seif 6, concerning an antedated document.

Because we penalize. The Sema cites Tosafot in Bava Kamma that this differs from an antedated document, as discussed in siman 43, seif 7. There the document is invalid but the lender does not lose the underlying loan, whereas here the loan itself was made through a prohibited interest arrangement. The Shach disputes this. He writes that the Sema did not read Tosafot accurately: Tosafot say this only according to Rabbi Meir, who penalizes the permitted amount because of the prohibited amount. According to the Sages, whose view we follow, Tosafot explicitly imply that the case is like an antedated document and the lender does not lose the principal of the loan. The Shach discusses this at length. The Taz likewise objects to the Sema and concludes that we do not follow the cited dissenting opinion at all, since Maharik expressly rules that the principal is collected, the Mordechai and Hagahot Maimoniyot imply the same, and the Tur too can be read as speaking only where there is no admission.
An agent. The Shach writes that this applies only according to the latter view, when the obligor admits the debt. According to the first view, however, there is no distinction where the loan was made through an agent. The principal is always collected from unencumbered property when the obligor admits it, and from encumbered property where appropriate; but when the obligor does not admit it, even the principal is not collected, notwithstanding that the loan was made through an agent, because the document has nevertheless become invalid. This appears correct to me. Thus concludes the Shach.

Nimukei Yosef, in the chapter Get Pashut, writes in accordance with the Ran, and the Bach cites his words without qualification. Shach.
And the guarantor. The Shach writes that it appears that a recognizable judicial tear must extend across the entire written portion of the document. Otherwise, the rule would be left without a fixed measure. It is not reasonable to say that any minimal amount of such a tear invalidates the document. Moreover, a small tear can sometimes occur accidentally at the folds of the document through ordinary handling and storage. Thus concludes the Shach.
Into two pieces. It appears that when a document bears a beit din tear it is invalid even if the tear is along its folds, for neither the Gemara nor the poskim distinguish, regarding a judicial tear, between a tear along the folds and any other tear. Rather, they state generally that if the tear is at the place of the witnesses, the essential text, the date, or the recognizable judicial tear, the document is invalid. The same therefore applies all the more when it is torn into two pieces, which is worse than an ordinary judicial tear. The implication in a responsum of the Rosh that a tear along the folds is valid refers to a tear that is not a judicial tear. The reason is that ordinary handling does not generally cause a document to tear through its folds all at once. It ordinarily tears gradually. He therefore should have gone to beit din before it became torn with a judicial tear or into two pieces. Since he did not do so, it appears that the tear was made intentionally. This is my view, unlike the Sema and the Bach. Thus are the words of the Shach.
Through circumstances beyond his control. The Sema writes that this means the document was torn at a place in which the tear invalidates it. If it was torn only in a place where the document remains valid, beit din does not make him another document, for since the present document is valid he can collect with this promissory note. The Rashba likewise writes this in a responsum.
