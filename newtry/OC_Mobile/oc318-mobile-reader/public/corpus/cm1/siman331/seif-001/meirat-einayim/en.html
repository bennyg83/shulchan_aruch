They must leave their homes at sunrise, etc. This is derived from the verse, "The sun rises, they gather in, etc.; man goes out to his work and to his labor until evening." The phrase "they gather in" refers to the wild animals mentioned just before: "You make darkness and it is night, in which all the beasts of the forest creep." From this verse we learn that a worker leaves his home for work when the sun rises and returns home only after sunset, meaning after the appearance of the stars. In the Perishah I explained the reason. The worker must spend from sunrise until sunset engaged in the employer's work. Travel to the worksite is part of the work's needs, so it is enough that he leave his house at sunrise. Returning home, by contrast, is for his own benefit, so he leaves the workplace only after sunset.
And to roast for himself a small fish. The Tur does not contain the word "small," nor does Hagahot Ashri at the beginning of HaPoalim when quoting this Yerushalmi. Rama apparently added it because in Orach Chaim siman 256 the Tur and Mechaber write from the Gemara in Bameh Madlikin that on Friday six blasts were sounded, and after the third blast people completed preparation and lighting, then waited enough time to roast a small fish or place bread in an oven, after which the final blasts were sounded and work ceased. That source does not prove the measure here. There it concerns householders who had been in the city all Friday and had already baked, cooked, and prepared their needs. Here the workers were in the field and returned home only shortly before Shabbat without yet preparing their household needs, which require more time than merely roasting a small fish. Proof is that our source also gives time to fill a barrel with water, something not mentioned there. Likewise, there the lamps are lit before the period for roasting the fish, while here the lamps are lit afterward. We must therefore say that these preparations of roasting the fish, filling water, and lighting lamps occurred somewhat earlier for field workers than for other people.
