In a place where the custom is to provide food. Terumat HaDeshen discusses a messenger hired for ten gold coins who later demanded travel expenses in addition, claiming that local custom always requires the employer to pay expenses separately. The employer answered that the unusually high fixed wage showed that expenses were included. Terumat HaDeshen ruled for the employer, reasoning that the price demonstrated the agreement and did not contradict the basic wording. He also joined the employer's possession of the money with the evidentiary force of the price.
I find this difficult. The Gemara says that where local custom is not to begin early or end late, even an increased wage does not allow the employer to demand longer hours. Likewise, where the custom is to provide food, he must provide food despite paying more. The law of tenant farmers also says that an unusually favorable share does not displace customary rights unless the change is stated expressly. Thus established custom is stronger than price evidence. A simple majority does not ordinarily extract money, but commercial custom defines the contract and can. If the custom in Terumat HaDeshen's case truly was that expenses are always additional, the employer should have expressly stipulated otherwise. The ruling therefore requires study.
