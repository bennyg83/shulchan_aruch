And they told him, "Like one or two of the townspeople." Maggid Mishneh cites Ramban and Rashba that where payment is fixed by reference to local appraisal, such as "as one or two of the townspeople" or "as people sell in town," the middle rate governs. The Tosefta similarly gives the middle price where produce is promised for delivery at the threshing season and prices then vary. This seems to conflict with the rule that a worker hired without a fixed wage receives the lowest current rate even if only a minority work that cheaply. Ritva distinguishes because "like one or two of the townspeople" expressly refers to the town's valuation. But the future-produce case does not expressly mention town sellers, so the comparison requires explanation.
The distinction is this. A worker hired now without any reference to local valuation receives the lowest current rate actually available. But if the employer says, "like one or two of the townspeople," he has made communal valuation part of the bargain, and the parties intend the middle rate. The produce case is similar because there is no present threshing-season price. The agreement necessarily refers to the future market that will be created by buyers and sellers at that time. It is therefore equivalent to saying, "according to the price that will prevail in town," and the middle rate governs. It follows that if a worker is hired now to begin at harvest and the agreement refers to the rate that will prevail then, the middle future market rate should govern. This is the force of Ramban and Rashba's comparison.
