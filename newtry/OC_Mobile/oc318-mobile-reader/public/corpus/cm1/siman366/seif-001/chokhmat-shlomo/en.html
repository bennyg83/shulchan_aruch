Seif 1. "If the stolen property is no longer extant, we do not accept repayment from him." Note: See what I wrote on this subject in my novellae to the chapter HaGozel Etzim of Bava Kamma, where I developed several novel rulings with the help of Heaven. What the Shulchan Aruch writes here, that if the robber returns the money in order to fulfill his obligation before Heaven we accept it from him, is very difficult to me, because it appears contrary to the language of Bava Kamma 94b. The Gemara says, "They said: they return, but we do not accept from them. If so, why do they return? In order to fulfill their obligation before Heaven." Thus the word "return" itself refers to fulfillment of the obligation before Heaven, and nevertheless the Gemara says that we do not accept it from them. This indicates that even when they return it for the sake of Heaven, it should not be accepted. Moreover, the Gemara asks, "If so, why is their repentance difficult?" What is the question if they are obligated before Heaven to return it and, when they do so, the victim accepts it? Their repentance would certainly remain difficult. It must therefore be that even a payment made to fulfill the obligation before Heaven is not accepted. In Yoreh Deah siman 171, seif katan 13, the Rivash is cited as saying that despite the rule that repayment is not accepted, they remain obligated to return it in order to fulfill their obligation before Heaven. His language, "nevertheless they are obligated to return," implies that only they are obligated to offer repayment, but that the recipient should still not accept it, unlike the ruling here. It is possible that interest is different, as Tosafot write in Bava Kamma 94b. Even so, the difficulty from the Gemara itself remains and requires study. See there and examine carefully.
