Still intact. Even if the stolen beam still exists, if the robber built a structure upon it he need not return the beam itself. This follows Hillel's ruling that if one stole a beam and fixed it into a building, he need not demolish the entire structure to return the beam, but instead pays its value to the owner. This applies only where he stole movable property. If, however, he encroached upon another's land and built a wall on his neighbor's property, then even if he erected great structures upon it he must demolish everything, because the enactment for penitents was not made regarding land. Rabbeinu Yerucham writes this in the name of the Geonim, and the Darkei Moshe cites it in this siman; the Rama likewise rules so in the gloss at the beginning of siman 360. They wrote it regarding a robber who is not notorious and the duty of restitution, and it appears that the same, all the more so, applies regarding a notorious robber. Ibid.
