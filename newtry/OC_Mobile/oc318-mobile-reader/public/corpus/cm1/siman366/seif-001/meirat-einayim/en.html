And his repentance is difficult. For a habitual robber of this type, all his property may not suffice to repay everyone he robbed. Even if he is wealthy, the prospect of dissipating and paying out a very large sum may deter him from repenting. Moreover, he often does not know to whom he must return the money because robbery was his regular business and he robbed many people.
If the stolen object no longer exists. Even if it still exists, if he built it into a structure he need not return the object itself. This follows Hillel's enactment: if one stole a beam and fixed it into a building, he need not demolish the entire structure in order to return the beam, but instead pays its value to the owner. This applies specifically to movable property. If one encroached upon his fellow's land and built a wall on it, even if he erected very substantial structures, he must demolish them all. The enactment encouraging repentance was not made for land. Rabbeinu Yeruham writes this in the name of the Geonim, and Rama cites it in Darkei Moshe here and in his gloss at the beginning of siman 360. They wrote it concerning an ordinary robber; all the more so it applies to a notorious robber.
We do not accept repayment from him. Meaning, the Sages enacted that people should not accept repayment from such a robber, in order to open a path for him to repent. The Gemara and Tur state that one who accepts repayment from him is not viewed favorably by the Sages, meaning that a spirit of wisdom and piety is lacking in him.
If he wishes to satisfy Heaven, etc. Meaning, if the robber says, "Although you do not wish to accept it from me and have waived the payment, I still wish to return it in order to satisfy my obligation before Heaven," we do not prevent him. A similar law appears in the Gemara and is recorded by the Tur and Mechaber in siman 67 regarding shemittah: the lender must tell a borrower who comes to pay after shemittah, "I release the debt," but if the borrower then says, "Nevertheless, I wish to pay," the lender may accept it.
