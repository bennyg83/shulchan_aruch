One of them took it and used it, etc. Since the Mechaber does not say that he snatched it from one person's hand, as the Tur does, nor that he took it specifically from the owner's minor son, it implies that even if he took it from the owner's adult son with the son's permission, he is still a borrower without permission and becomes liable for unavoidable accidents. The article belongs to the father, not to the son.
Therefore, if he returned it to the minor who had held it, etc. This is the Rambam's language at the end of chapter 3 of Hilchot Gezeilah. It means that even if the item had already been in the minor's hand with his father's knowledge, and the person took it from the child, used it, and later returned it to the same child, he remains liable for it. The Gemara has a similar case of one who sends a flask with his minor child to buy oil. The Tur and Mechaber write it above in siman 188. The Rambam also writes that the same rule applies if he returns it to a slave. The Maggid Mishneh explains that a slave is like a minor until the object is returned to the owners themselves. The Tur and Mechaber likewise write in siman 291 that even an adult slave is treated like a minor for this purpose. Specifically return to a minor leaves him liable for unavoidable accidents. If he took the item from the owner's adult son and returned it to that adult son, he is exempt from unavoidable accidents, as Rama writes at the beginning of siman 292: if he returns it to the place from which he took it, his special borrower without permission liability ends and he returns to the prior custodial status. That ruling is from Mordechai and concerns a borrower without permission as well.
