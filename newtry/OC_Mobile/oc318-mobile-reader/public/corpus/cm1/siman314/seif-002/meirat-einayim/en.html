And a mezuzah, etc. Rambam also writes "and a mezuzah," and this is how the precise editions of the Tur must be corrected. The reason is that the obligation of mezuzah falls upon the occupant. The Tur concludes that nevertheless, when leaving the house, the renter should not remove the mezuzah if the house belongs to a Jew. Since this is not the proper place for that law, and the Tur and Mechaber already wrote it in Yoreh Deah at the end of the laws of mezuzah, siman 291, the Mechaber omitted it here. The reason is so that harmful forces not gain dominion over the house, and removing it is regarded as though one caused damage with his own hands. The Tur mentioned it here only incidentally.
Likewise, if he wishes to make a ladder, etc. Nimukei Yosef explains that this means a ladder to the roof. A ladder to an upper story is the owner's obligation, because without it the premises cannot be inhabited. See the Derishah for further discussion.
Or a gutter, etc. Rashi explains that their roofs were plastered with clay and sloped on all four sides. Boards were set against the wall near the roof to distance the water from the wall. If one fell, even an ordinary person could replace it.
