Likewise, if the plaster layer and ceiling were breached by four handbreadths. Maimonides explicitly speaks of renting an upper story. Since he gives a measure of four handbreadths, the case is one where the tenant of the upper story loses the use of an area sufficient for one utensil, as the Tur and Mechaber explain in siman 312:18. In the Perishah I offered further proof from the Rambam's and Mechaber's statement that these repairs are among the essential requirements of dwelling. An upper story too is included in the general term "house," as explained in simanim 214 and 312. The Tur writes that the plaster layer also strengthens the ceiling. Therefore repairing it falls upon the landlord as skilled structural work. This excludes a mere leveling of holes, which is ordinary unskilled work and falls upon the tenant.
