One who rents a house to another. If the oven or a similar fixture breaks, the tenant should properly show it to experts and repair it under the direction of beit din. If he did not do so, but nevertheless spent money on a repair that he claims was necessary to make the house habitable, his claim may be accepted even where the experts cannot now verify the necessity, although he may have to swear to it, as in siman 53 regarding one who incurs expenses with authorization, even if the landlord cannot state a definite contrary claim. The tenant may then deduct the expense from rent still in his possession. If, however, the rent was prepaid and the tenant now seeks affirmative reimbursement, and the landlord says that he never authorized expenditures and knows that no necessary expense existed, the landlord may swear and be exempt. Even where the landlord cannot state this definitely, he may perhaps argue that the tenant had no authority to incur expenses except through beit din and experts, and that because he failed to do so his expenditure was unauthorized. In that event the tenant could recover only according to the rules governing one who descends into another's property without permission. Perhaps this is true even where the tenant still holds rent, because his failure to use beit din and experts makes his spending unauthorized. On the other hand, every tenant may resemble a partner or a husband managing his wife's property, who is treated as one who incurs expenses with permission even though he could have first consulted beit din and experts. The Sages did not distinguish, and the tenant too enjoys the produce or use of the property. This requires careful study. As I wrote elsewhere, the issue depends in part on the rules in simanim 91 and 93. Urim VeTumim writes that a partner is considered one who descends with permission, while the Sema indicates that this is not literally so in every respect. At minimum, when the tenant is in possession, perhaps no oath is imposed merely because of the landlord's doubt. Urim VeTumim in siman 93 similarly supports the idea that where one had authority to act, he may deduct the expense. This still requires study.
