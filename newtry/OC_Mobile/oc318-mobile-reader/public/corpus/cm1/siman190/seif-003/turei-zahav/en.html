There, in the gloss: He gives according to the present market price. The Sema's question against the Rama can be answered by recognizing a third approach in the Rambam. The baraita says that the buyer collects from superior land. According to the Rambam, this refers to the buyer's demand for land where the sale remains effective in proportion to the money already paid. Why then use the language of collection, which ordinarily describes recovery of a debt? According to the Rosh, the collection is from the seller's other property, and according to the Ran the original acquisition failed, so the language is natural. The Rambam, however, treats the transaction from two perspectives. The buyer receives land according to its present value, like a creditor collecting a debt, but the rule that the seller may give inferior land reflects the continuing status of a sale, like one who sold half his field. Conversely, where the seller retracts, the buyer receives according to the original agreed price, preserving the sale, but may take superior land because as to collection he resembles an injured party rather than an ordinary creditor. This resolves the wording.
