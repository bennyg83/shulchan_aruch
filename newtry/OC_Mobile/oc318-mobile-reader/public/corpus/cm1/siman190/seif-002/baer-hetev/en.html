The Gemara in the chapter HaIsh Mekadesh makes clear that just as one who betroths a woman by forgiving a loan does not effect kiddushin, because money lent is given for expenditure and no longer exists as the lender's money, so too one who purchases property through forgiving a loan does not effect acquisition, for the same reason. The Itur and Ritva in Kiddushin write likewise, and the Ritva records that the Yerushalmi states in the name of Rabbi Yochanan that with respect to land, kiddushin by means of an outstanding loan does not acquire, and with respect to movable property, the seller is not even subjected to Mi Shepara. The Rosh likewise writes this in the first chapter of Kiddushin. See Bava Metzia 15a, Tosafot beginning "Hai Shtara," and the Maharsha there. Consider this carefully. Shach. The Shach further writes: A case came before me in which Reuven purchased land from Shimon and did not take possession of it, but instead gave Shimon a negotiable note that Reuven held against Levi as payment for the land. Some wished to argue that because promissory notes are not themselves corporeal money, and the laws of overreaching, guardians, and similar matters do not apply to them, the land could not be acquired through them. I ruled that the sale is acquired through delivery of the negotiable note and neither party may retract. See there, where he proves this from the Gemara.
