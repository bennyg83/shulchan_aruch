With money, how? See Shach, seif katan 1, who writes in the name of Rashi and the Ran that if one purchases land by transferring a promissory note representing a debt owed to him by others, he does not acquire, because the seller does not rely upon that payment, fearing that the debt may be forgiven. Ketzot HaChoshen cites Tosafot in Bava Batra 157 and Tosafot in Bava Metzia 16, s.v. Hai Shtara, who distinguish a sale from marriage. In marriage the woman does not rely upon such a note because if the debt is forgiven she is embarrassed to sue her husband, whereas in an ordinary sale the seller is not embarrassed to sue the buyer and therefore acquires. Ketzot's question on Tosafot in Bava Metzia 16 is the Maharsha's question there.
Money worth a perutah. See Sema, seif katan 1, who writes that if the money was not given as the beginning of payment but only as an act of acquisition, the land is not acquired. The Taz disagrees. His proof from the rule of "let my earnest money acquire" will be discussed below. The Sema's explanation is also difficult because he says that acquisition by money is learned from the field of Ephron, whereas Kiddushin 26 explicitly derives it from the verse "fields shall be acquired with money." Tosafot there, s.v. Amar Kra, show that it cannot be learned from Ephron's field. The Perishah explains that the requirement that the money be at least a perutah is learned from marriage, and marriage is learned from Ephron's field, whose money represented the value of the field. But this too does not fit Beit Yosef's words. Beit Yosef does not say that the minimum value is learned from marriage and Ephron. Rather, he cites Kiddushin 3 that a woman does not transfer herself for less than a perutah and Tosafot's explanation that the word "taking" is learned from Ephron, where "money" is written, and less than a perutah is not called money. Beit Yosef's intent is therefore that once Tosafot establish that less than a perutah is not legally called money, acquisition of land, which Scripture states occurs "with money," likewise requires at least a perutah. There is a further difficulty with the Sema's claim that the perutah must be given toward payment of the price. Even if the buyer gives money on condition that the seller return it, the acquisition is effective although the money does not remain credited toward the land's price. One would have to say, with difficulty, that payment on condition of return is still considered payment. The principal ruling therefore follows the Taz: even if he gives the money solely to effect the acquisition of the field, it acquires. See further what I write in seif 10.
