Capable of division. With land, however, even if after division his share will not amount to the minimum measure of a field or garden, as stated in siman 171, seif 3, nevertheless he acquires land corresponding to his money, or else he has the inferior position. The reason is that the land can be sold to one who owns an adjoining field and can combine it with his own. The Beit Yosef writes that according to Rambam, even with a field, if each party will not receive the requisite measure, he acquires nothing at all, as with movable property. The Tur, however, writes that Rambam maintains that even with movable property there is no distinction whether or not there is the requisite measure, and the Tur himself disagrees with Rambam. The Mechaber's words here follow the Tur, although he did not state explicitly whether he holds that the same law applies to land. This is the Sema's conclusion. The Taz writes that the Sema overlooked the Ran in the first chapter of Kiddushin, who states explicitly that with land as well one must distinguish whether there is enough to divide so that each receives the requisite measure. Moreover, logically, how can we compel a person to accept something unsuitable for him merely so that he may sell it to one who needs to attach it to his field? This is not reasonable. There is also an a fortiori argument from a kosher animal, which is deemed unfit for division because once slaughtered it is not worth as much as while alive.
