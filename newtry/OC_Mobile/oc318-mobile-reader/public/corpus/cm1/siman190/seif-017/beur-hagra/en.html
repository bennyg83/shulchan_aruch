If it is, etc. Rava's statement there that he does not acquire implies no acquisition at all. Partners may compel one another through the rule of either divide or buy out, even though they entered the partnership willingly. All the more so here, where neither purchased nor sold with the intention of becoming partners. Milchamot explains that this is the meaning of Rav Ashi's uncertainty: in such a case, does he acquire proportionately to his payment, as with a field, or is this case different because the property is indivisible? The conclusion is that he acquires nothing. Rif's version omits the phrase, 'Money is like money,' and the Gemara asks from Rava's rule that he acquires fully, initially assuming that because the property is indivisible, just as he cannot retract from the portion paid for he cannot retract from the whole. It answers that the case concerns one who sold, etc. Milchamot compares this to the Yerushalmi in the chapter HaZahav: one person gave dinars for silk and retracted; Rabbi Chiyya bar Yosef said he must either forfeit twice his earnest money or accept the curse of one who fails to keep his word, while Rabbi Yochanan said he must either complete the entire purchase or accept that curse. Rabbi Ila explained that it was earnest money, and they follow their respective views. The Gemara likewise states that Rabbi Tarfon agrees regarding an item not ordinarily purchased in halves, such as a cow or garment. Here the inverse applies: because the remainder cannot be acquired, repeated demands for payment prevent acquisition of the whole.
