There is a distinction regarding movables. Taz explains that with movables the rule differs from land. If the object can be divided, each party receives the share corresponding to what was paid; if it cannot be divided, the transaction is completely undone and the buyer receives back his money. With land, by contrast, even where division is possible the sale is not necessarily undone. If the seller retracts, the buyer can demand compensation according to the special rule favoring the injured party. This explanation fits the Shulchan Aruch's view that the buyer can take from the superior portion of that particular land. With movables there is no meaningful distinction between superior and inferior property because all movables are treated as best quality. According to Rosh and Tur, however, the buyer may collect from the superior quality among the seller's general assets. That right is stronger than the ordinary right of a tort claimant, because even if the seller offers money, the buyer can insist upon land. A tort claimant generally receives either money or best quality land, and if money is offered there is no need to give land. Even so, the special land remedy arises because the buyer had actually acquired land. He can say, "Give me land," and then the law protects him with superior quality. A buyer of movables never acquired land and cannot demand land in substitution. If he demands movables, there is no superior versus inferior category among them. Thus the distinction between land and movables can stand even under Rosh and Tur.
