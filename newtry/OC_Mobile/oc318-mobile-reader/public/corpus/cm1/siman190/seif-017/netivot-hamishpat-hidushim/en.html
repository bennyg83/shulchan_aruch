The Sema writes that land that is not legally divisible is nevertheless acquired corresponding to the amount paid, and the party who retracts has the inferior position, because the portion can be sold to an adjoining landowner and joined to his field. Beit Yosef holds that even land that is not divisible causes the entire sale to be void. The Taz concludes that this is the practical law. Where the land is divisible, there is a distinction between land and movables. If the seller retracts from a land sale, the purchaser has the upper hand and may compel the seller to give him superior land. With movables, he cannot compel such a choice because all movables are treated as equally acceptable payment. If the purchaser retracts, there is no distinction between land and movables.
