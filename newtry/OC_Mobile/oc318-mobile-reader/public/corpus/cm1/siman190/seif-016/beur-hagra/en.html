There are, etc., two times. This appears from the doubled phrase, 'he goes in and out, goes out and in,' and so Rambam writes. The Gemara commonly speaks this way, as in: a person should always enter, and climb and descend; in the blink of an eye one enters and one leaves; she will close and open. See Rosh, third chapter of Bava Batra, section 29. Logically the order should have been 'open and close,' but the language follows its customary flow.

And if, etc.; before, etc.; for every, etc. A demand to 'go and return' is not so characterized until after the appointed time, as stated there at 110b.
Likewise, etc. As stated above in seif 7, 'When does this apply,' etc. It is like a deed without any payment, from which the seller may retract entirely even if he did not repeatedly demand payment, as stated above, siman 191, seif 2. This is the Gemara's meaning in saying, 'until he pays the money,' meaning all the money. Although partial payment ordinarily acquires the whole where the seller does not repeatedly demand payment, and earnest money acquires proportionately where the money was given for acquisition, here the money itself does not acquire. Rather, the deed acquires proportionately to the payment, while the deed was written for the whole. Just as it is ineffective for the remainder, it is ineffective entirely.
(Supplement) Likewise, that which we said, etc. Ran writes this according to his view that the purchaser acquires proportionately. According to Rosh, one may explain that only here, where the retraction itself voided the transaction, does the entire sale fail, unlike a deed. Rosh similarly distinguishes between land and indivisible movable property in seif 17. According to Rambam, however, the sale is always void, as explained above. (End of supplement.)
