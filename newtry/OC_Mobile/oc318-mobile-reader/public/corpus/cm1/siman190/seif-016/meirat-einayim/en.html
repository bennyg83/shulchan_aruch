On the day fixed for payment, or on a day close to it. The phrase cannot mean the day on which the sale itself was made, because then the alternative, "or on a day close to it," could not mean a later day, since the ruling immediately says that even if he presses for money the next day he cannot retract. Nor can it mean a day shortly before the sale, because one cannot demand payment before the sale has taken place. It must therefore mean the day fixed for payment of the sale price. For example, he sells today on condition that the purchaser pay on the third or fourth day. If he presses for payment on that appointed day, or shortly before it and says, "Prepare my money because I will need it tomorrow at the time we fixed," this counts as pressing for payment. The Tur's wording likewise should be read so that the word "for payment" applies both to the day fixed for the transaction and to the nearby day. See the Perishah.
And likewise what we say, that he acquires corresponding to the money paid, etc. Rama's word "likewise" requires explanation. Perhaps he means that just as the rule of a seller pressing for the price has various distinctions, so too the rule that the purchaser acquires at least corresponding to the amount paid has distinctions. Although the Mechaber did not earlier state expressly that he acquires corresponding to the money paid, one might think Rama refers to seif 10, where the Mechaber says that the purchaser does not acquire all of the field, implying that he acquires at least against the amount paid. But according to what I wrote there in the Sema, that is not the correct interpretation. Moreover, in seif 17 the Mechaber writes concerning movable property that where the item is divisible the sale is void only corresponding to the unpaid portion, implying that regarding land in such a case the entire sale is void. We must therefore say that Rama, following the Ran whose gloss this is, refers to the Gemara's case where one gives part of the price and says, "Let my earnest deposit acquire," regarding which we hold that he acquires only corresponding to the amount paid. The Tur states that law, and Rama also cites it in the following seif.
