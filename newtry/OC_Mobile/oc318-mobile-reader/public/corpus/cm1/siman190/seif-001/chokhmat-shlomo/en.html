Note: See what I wrote elsewhere regarding whether banknotes acquire property from a non-Jew according to the view that money effects acquisition, and my explanation of Rambam against the objections drawn from the Gemara. I also explained why banknotes are legally documents rather than intrinsic money, contrary to a responsum of Chatam Sofer, and discussed whether the rule “there is no maneh here, therefore there is no collateral here” applies when banknotes themselves are given as collateral. There is an additional compelling proof against treating royal designation alone as turning banknotes into Torah money. Surely if a king declares a paper currency to have a fixed value, that does not make it sufficient for the Torah amount required for pidyon haben. For that mitzvah the Torah requires the prescribed value in silver. Royal designation can matter in interpersonal transactions because value and commercial benefit are relevant there, and because a coin containing at least a perutah of silver has intrinsic Torah value. But paper that has no intrinsic value of a perutah and derives worth only from the claim or representation embodied in it does not become intrinsic Torah money merely by royal decree. This is clear and correct.
