A cloth. See Responsa Rabbi Betzalel Ashkenazi, siman 18. Mahari ben Lev, volume 1, siman 80, was uncertain whether a kinyan sudar is effective even in a locale where deeds are ordinarily written. Maharashach, volume 1, siman 73, writes that it is obvious that it is effective. Maharit, siman 65, writes that Mahari ben Lev later resolved in volume 2 that a kinyan sudar alone is not effective. Mahara Sasson, siman 206, cites the Ritva's novellae, which explicitly state that a kinyan sudar alone is ineffective in a place where deeds are customarily written. Mahara Sasson himself adduces support from Rabbeinu Yerucham, whose words imply that a kinyan sudar alone is effective. See there. Pnei Moshe, Choshen Mishpat, siman 64, writes that if the kinyan was performed before witnesses in the buyer's presence, then because an ordinary kinyan of this kind is intended to be written in a deed, the buyer acquires. He derives this conclusion from the cited responsum of Maharit. See at length Lechem Rav, simanim 152 and 153, and Responsa Maharashdam, simanim 247 and 290. Bnei Chayei.
