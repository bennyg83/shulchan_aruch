If one sold his field because it was undesirable to him, then even if he repeatedly demands the balance of the price, the purchaser acquires the entire field and cannot withdraw. The seller's pursuit of the money is not because he has not completed the transfer, but because he fears the purchaser may withdraw. The same rule can apply to movable property. Ordinarily, even if the purchaser drew the produce into his possession, if the seller repeatedly demands the unpaid balance, the purchaser does not acquire fully and the withdrawing party is at a disadvantage, as explained above. But if the seller sold because the property was undesirable to him, the purchaser acquires all of it.
