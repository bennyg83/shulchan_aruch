One who sells, etc. Everything stated by the Shulchan Aruch in these two seifim is Rambam's language.
(Supplement) One who sells, etc.; he did not acquire, etc. Rambam means that he does not acquire even proportionately to the money paid. Rosh and Tur agree, except that they hold that the sale proportionate to the money is not void until one party retracts, and therefore the opposing party has the advantage. According to Rambam, the entire sale is immediately void even without retraction. He derives this from the Gemara's statement that such a person does not acquire, implying not at all, and from the statement that money is like ordinary money and does not acquire. Milchamot explains that the first statement concerns one who paid no money, while the second concerns an indivisible item, as stated in seif 17. Ran, Nimukei Yosef, Ramban, and Rashba hold that the purchaser acquires fully in proportion to his payment and cannot retract from that portion. They explain that 'his hand is on the upper side' means either that he may compel completion of the transaction or that both parties may retract as in any sale where the other party retracts. The Gemara's principal teaching concerns the concluding rule, from which property he collects, etc. See Bach and Derisha and Perisha. The practical differences are that according to Rambam and Rosh, when one retracts the sale is void and land is given at its present value, whereas according to Ran and Rashba land is given at the agreed value. Further, according to Rambam and Rosh, the question from which quality he collects concerns land; according to Ran and Rashba it concerns money. (End of supplement.)
Although he wrote, etc. The case in the Gemara involves a deed, yet acquisition is not complete until the unpaid balance is converted into a loan. The same applies to possession, formal acquisition, and drawing movable property. See Tosafot there, s.v. ayil. Furthermore, since the Gemara finds acquisition only where the balance is converted into a loan, why require so much? It could have said that he should acquire by deed or possession. The Gemara also states in the name of Rava that one who sells, etc. Ramban infers that since it did not answer that he gave earnest money while repeatedly demanding payment, etc., the case is not one of earnest money. Moreover, the statement that he does not acquire implies total non-acquisition, whereas with earnest money he acquires proportionately and has the advantage. The case must therefore concern movable property drawn or land possessed. It cannot concern a deed, because a deed does not acquire until payment is made. See Milchamot, Ran, and Nimukei Yosef.
And he gives, etc. This follows Rav Nachman bar Yitzchak there.
(Supplement) And he gives him, etc. This is Rambam's language and follows Rav Nachman bar Yitzchak. Rosh, Tur, and Nimukei Yosef rule like Rav Acha. Baal HaMaor explains that both rulings are authoritative: land is collected from the debtor's land, while money is collected from his general assets. According to Ran's interpretation, however, the discussion does not concern land. (End of supplement.)
And he gives, etc., at the current value. Ran writes this according to his explanation that the question, 'From what does he collect?' concerns money. In that case both parties retract from the sale, and since the seller lacks cash the purchaser collects as a creditor. With land, however, he always collects inferior-quality land, because the transaction is necessarily upheld, like one who sells half his field at the end of the seventh chapter of Bava Batra, and he receives land at the agreed valuation. But when he says, 'Give me my money,' or, 'Take your money,' both retract, and he receives at the current value because the transaction is void. Milchamot writes similarly and also infers this from Rashi's phrase, 'From what does he collect his debt,' meaning when he demands his money. Rambam, however, applies the phrase, 'And he gives him,' to land, and therefore necessarily holds that whether land or money is returned, the sale was void from the outset because the seller repeatedly demanded payment. Rosh's words imply the same. Accordingly, the purchaser always receives at the current value. Maggid Mishneh's citation of Ramban means that both in the first and second clauses he receives at the current value: according to Ramban, the statement concerns money; according to Rambam, it concerns both land and money. Sema was misled by Maggid Mishneh's wording. Milchamot explicitly agrees with Ran. Rama's words are imprecise, because according to all views there is no distinction between the first and second clauses. According to Rambam the valuation is always current; according to Ramban and the other authorities, money is valued currently and land at the agreed value, from inferior-quality land.

And both, etc. Meaning, with ordinary earnest money there is no distinction whether the seller repeatedly demanded payment. Regarding the unpaid balance, both may retract; regarding the amount paid, neither may retract. The statement, 'They instruct him,' etc., shows that in such a case even if the seller repeatedly demanded payment, the purchaser acquires the entire property. The same applies where he said, 'Let my earnest money acquire,' regarding the portion corresponding to the money paid. This is the meaning of, 'He acquired only,' etc. Whenever the sale remains effective, he receives the inferior portion, as explained above from Bava Batra.
