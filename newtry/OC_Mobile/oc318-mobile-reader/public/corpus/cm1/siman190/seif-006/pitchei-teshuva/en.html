Netivot HaMishpat below, siman 191:2, cites Responsa Maharit, Even HaEzer 65, who considers it clear that the same rule applies even to one who sells a field because it is undesirable to him. This is unlike the rule that a deed without payment can acquire such a field. There, the reason that a deed alone normally fails is that the seller does not rely on the sale without receiving payment; when he is eager to rid himself of an undesirable field, he fully intends to transfer it. By contrast, where money without a deed fails in a place where deeds are customary, the lack of reliance is on the purchaser's side. If the seller is eager to rid himself of the field, the purchaser has even more reason to insist on a deed, since his money has already left his possession and he has not yet received even a written instrument. Responsa Be'er Yitzchak, Orach Chayim 7, argues that Responsa Rashba 1226, as cited in Responsa Rabbi Betzalel Ashkenazi 18, appears to prove the opposite: even in a place where deeds are written, a seller who sells because the property is undesirable effects acquisition through money or chalipin without a deed.
