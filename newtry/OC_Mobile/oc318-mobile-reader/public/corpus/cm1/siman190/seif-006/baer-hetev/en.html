Both rulings are stated in the Mordechai at the end of Ketubot, in connection with the rule in a locale where money is ordinarily paid, that one may say, You should not have paid your own debt first, as the Tur and Shulchan Aruch write in siman 85. The distinction is that with movable property there is concern that if Shimon gives it to Reuven, Reuven will remove or conceal it, leaving Shimon with nothing from which to collect his debt. This concern does not exist with land, which Reuven cannot conceal. Nevertheless, the underlying law is the same in both cases: even the movable property is acquired by means of the one hundred that Shimon received, but Shimon retains it as collateral until Reuven pays the one hundred he owes him. Each acquisition then follows its own law. Land, which is acquired by money, is fully acquired, while movable property, which is not acquired by money, is acquired only to the extent that the seller becomes subject to Mi Shepara. Sema.
