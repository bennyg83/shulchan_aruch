Reuven, etc. From Kesubos 110a, “This one was clever, etc.” It follows that he can retain it only as collateral. The Gemara there says, “where they disagree, etc.” At first glance, what purpose would a declaration of coercion serve, and why would the Sages need the principle “your friend has a friend, etc.”? Rather, he should not have caused himself the loss of his field by selling it, for now he can retain it only as collateral. He should have paid and first issued a declaration of coercion, thereby voiding the sale, after which the money would remain as his debt. This shows that without that declaration he could not simply seize the purchase money as payment of the debt if the other party paid him, because the field had already been acquired. The Gemara’s statement concerning a place where they give, etc., is because there the purchase had not yet been acquired by money and he could still withhold it, as explained in seif 7. See Mordechai and Hagahos Ashri there.
