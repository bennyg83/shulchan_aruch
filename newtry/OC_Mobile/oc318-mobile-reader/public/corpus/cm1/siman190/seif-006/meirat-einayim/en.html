"Here is a maneh, and let the field be sold to you," etc. Rama does not write here that the purchaser must say, "Your field is acquired to me through the maneh I received from you." The reason is that all these statements are needed only to reveal that the party is satisfied with the acquisition. Here the seller speaks directly with the purchaser and gives him the money for that purpose, so the purchaser's acceptance itself shows that he agrees. Otherwise he would not have accepted it. Likewise, in the first clause, if the seller tells the purchaser, "Give a maneh to Levi and acquire my field," then if the purchaser did not desire the transaction he would not give Levi the money at the seller's instruction.
Reuven owed Shimon one maneh, etc. The ruling concludes that Shimon cannot say, "I will collect this maneh toward my debt." Below in siman 204:1, however, the Mechaber rules differently regarding movable property. There, if Reuven owes Shimon one maneh and Shimon says, "I will sell you this movable item for a maneh," Shimon may say, "I will retain this maneh in payment of my debt." Both rules appear in the Mordechai at the end of Ketubot. The difference is that in the case of movable property, if Shimon were to deliver the object to Reuven there is concern that Reuven could conceal or remove it, leaving Shimon unable to collect his debt. With land there is no such concern, because Reuven cannot remove the land. Therefore Shimon may not retain the sale money as payment of the prior debt. Nevertheless, in both cases the object is sold to Reuven through the money Shimon received. Shimon merely holds the movable item as a pledge until Reuven pays the preexisting debt. Each sale then operates according to its legal form. Land, which is acquired by money, is fully acquired immediately, especially because Shimon can afterward collect it again for his debt. Movable property, which is not acquired by money alone, is acquired only to the extent that the buyer who retracts becomes subject to the sanction of "He Who exacted punishment."
