An important person. Here it is unnecessary for him to say, "Your field is acquired to me," as in seif 5, because the seller is speaking directly with the purchaser and his willingness is evident from his acceptance of the benefit. The same is true in seif 3, where the giver acts at his instruction. Sema. See Beurim: if he sells the entire item for this benefit, even movables are acquired through the law of barter according to the opinion that equal value barter does not require a utensil. But if the benefit is worth only a perutah and he gives it merely so that it should count as monetary acquisition, while the balance remains to be paid, it has only the status of money. Therefore it does not acquire movables, except for purposes of the "Who exacted payment" curse.
I am collecting this one hundred against my debt. The same law applies to movables, as explained in siman 204, that the movables become bound for purposes of the "Who exacted payment" curse. With movables, because the debtor can hide them, the creditor may hold them as collateral until paid. This does not apply to land. Sema. See Beurim in siman 85: if within the brief interval he says that he is taking this against his debt and does not want the sale, he can cancel the sale.
