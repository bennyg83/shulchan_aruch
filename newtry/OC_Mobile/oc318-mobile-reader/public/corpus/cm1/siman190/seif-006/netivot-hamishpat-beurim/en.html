Provided he is an important person. Ran in Kiddushin writes that in such a case even movable property can be acquired, because it resembles chalipin. Ketzot HaChoshen asks that chalipin requires a utensil, while benefit is not a utensil. If one gives produce he certainly gives benefit, yet chalipin does not work because produce is not a utensil. There is no difficulty. Ran follows Rabbeinu Tam, cited by Rama in siman 203, seif 1, that an exchange of equal value can be effected even with produce. Here, where the owner transfers his movable property solely in exchange for the benefit of receiving from an important person and receives no other payment, that benefit is worth the entire consideration to him and resembles an exchange of equal value. Mechaber here speaks only where the benefit takes the place of a perutah in acquiring the field, while the buyer must still pay the purchase price. That is not an exchange of equal value, so it works only as money, which acquires land but not movable property. This is why Mechaber states the rule only regarding land. But if one transfers movable property solely for this benefit and no further payment is due, he indeed acquires even movable property, as Ran writes, and I have found no one who disagrees.
