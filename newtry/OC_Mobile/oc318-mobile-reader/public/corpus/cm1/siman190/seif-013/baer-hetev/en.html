To retract. It appears to me that the meaning is as follows. With respect to the portion corresponding to the money already paid, the seller certainly cannot retract. Even with respect to the unpaid portion, possession may determine the outcome. Thus, if the seller received one hundred gold coins toward property worth two hundred and the seller is in possession, he need not give the buyer any of the field until the buyer pays the remaining one hundred. If the buyer paid one hundred fifty, the seller need give him only half the field. The same applies if the property appreciated and became worth three hundred gold coins. If the seller seeks to retract, the buyer may retain rights according to this calculation. Conversely, if the property depreciated so greatly that it is not even worth the one hundred already paid, the buyer cannot seize other property from the seller to complete the one hundred, because the seller is not legally required to pay the buyer from his own pocket. Sema. The Taz writes: One should not ask that we ought to leave the property in the possession of the original owner, as in the case of exchanging a cow for a donkey in siman 223, where the buyer must bring proof. There, there is uncertainty about the acquisition itself. Here, however, there was already a valid sale through kinyan, deed, or partial payment, and we seek to undo it retroactively only because of an inference that the seller repeatedly demanded payment. Since there is uncertainty, we have no power to destroy an established sale. It therefore appears to me that even if the buyer is not physically holding the land and neither party presently controls it, it belongs to the buyer. This is the implication of the Rambam's statement that the seller cannot retract. Only if the seller is in possession of the land and says with certainty that he repeatedly demanded payment in a manner that voided the sale does his possession assist him. The Sema's view that each side acquires half and that the buyer's rights also depend upon possession is, in my humble opinion, exceedingly difficult.
