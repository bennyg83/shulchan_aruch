He purchased a field worth one hundred for two hundred, etc. The Rambam's version in chapter HaOmanin leaves this as an unresolved question. Our Gemara has a different version, where the question concerns one who sought to sell a field worth one hundred for one hundred but could not find a buyer, and therefore had to sell a field worth two hundred, as the Mechaber writes in the following seif and as appears in Rashi's text. That version too remains unresolved. We rule leniently for the defendant in an unresolved monetary question. The Mechaber therefore records both textual versions as practical law, one after the other. The Tur, although using Rashi's version, also cites the Rambam's ruling according to his text.
As a clerk of his field, for his wife, Demacht, who sold him with a fear that he would not return to him because he was not jealous of the land of A.D., which in double can return to him on the ground and as a result, in the S.C.C., and he said:
The one who wishes to retract from both, etc., if he seized, etc. In my view the meaning is that regarding the amount already paid he certainly cannot retract, while regarding the unpaid balance he may seize the advantage if he currently holds it. For example, if the seller received one hundred gold coins toward a field worth one hundred that he sold for two hundred, then if the seller has the upper hand he need not give any of the field until the buyer pays the remaining one hundred. If the buyer already gave one hundred fifty, the seller need give only half the field until the balance is paid. The same applies if the field appreciated to three hundred and the seller seeks to retract. The buyer may retain his position according to the same principle. The Rambam merely states the common case, beginning with a field worth one hundred sold for two hundred, because it is not ordinary for it to increase so dramatically to three hundred. The statement that the seller "seizes from the sale" may also mean to exclude requiring additional payment from the purchaser's other assets. If land values later fall so that the entire field is now worth only fifty, though he sold it for two hundred and received only one hundred, the seller cannot retain the whole land, now worth fifty, and then seize another fifty from other property of the purchaser. That would not be justified. See the Perishah for another possible resolution.
