Seif 13: He cannot retract. One should not apply the presumption favoring the original owner as in a doubtful exchange of a cow for a donkey. There the doubt concerns whether the acquisition itself occurred. Here a complete sale already took effect by acquisition, deed, or partial payment. The sale is undone only by an estimate that the seller was persistently demanding the balance. Where that fact is doubtful, we lack power to impair an otherwise certain sale. Therefore, even if the purchaser does not physically possess the land and neither party controls it, it belongs to the purchaser. This is the Rambam's implication. If, however, the seller possesses the land and claims with certainty that he was persistently demanding payment in a manner that voided the sale, possession may support him. The Rambam's later rule about the seller retaining against the unpaid balance concerns only that balance. As to the portion already paid, even definite persistent demand does not undo the proportional acquisition. The Sema's statement that each party acquired half and that the purchaser's half also depends on possession is very difficult.
