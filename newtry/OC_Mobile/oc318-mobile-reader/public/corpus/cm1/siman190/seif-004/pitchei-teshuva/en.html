But in a place where... he does not acquire. Regarding whether one who retracts in such a case must accept the curse of Mi ShePara, see below, siman 204:1, seif katan 12. Mishneh LaMelech, Hilchot Mechirah chapter 1, cites Responsa Mahari ben Lev I, end of kelal 13, that where a person swore to uphold the sale, this is equivalent to an express stipulation that the property be acquired through money alone or through the deed alone. Since he swore, if we said his mind did not rely on that acquisition he would thereby be committing a prohibition, and we therefore treat him as though he expressly intended that mode of acquisition alone to suffice. Mishneh LaMelech analyzes this position and notes that Mahari ben Lev himself, in part II at the end of the work, later retracted from that reasoning and held that even an oath does not amount to an express stipulation. See also Responsa Rabbi Moshe Rothenburg, Orach Chayim, end of siman 11.
