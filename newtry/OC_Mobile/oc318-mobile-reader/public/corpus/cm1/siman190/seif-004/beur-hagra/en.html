Some say, etc. The Rambam omitted all three of these seifim, and therefore this opinion is introduced as “some say.” His reasoning is that the statement, “and likewise regarding monetary law,” refers only to the first rule, that of a guarantor, which certainly applies to monetary matters. The other two cases concern specifically a woman, because the modes of acquisition of marriage are compared to one another in Kiddushin 16a, likewise 12a according to Beis Shammai, and 4b concerning acquisition by money. As for the fourth rule, Rava himself remained uncertain, and the Rambam does not normally codify a matter not stated explicitly in the Gemara. See Rashi there, s.v. “and likewise,” Ran there, and the Rif and Rosh, who omitted the phrase “and likewise regarding monetary law.”
(Collected note) Some say, etc. See what was written in the name of the Ran explaining the Rambam. Nevertheless the Ran writes that this explanation is not clear. It also requires study, for there is an explicit Gemara in Bava Kamma 102b: “This implies that he acquires, etc.; if because of this, there is no difficulty, etc.” (End of note.)
And this is, etc. Bava Kamma 102b does not appear so, for there they say, “Shall we say they disagree, etc.? There is no difficulty, etc.” Certainly according to us, who do not accept the view of the Western scholars, there is not even a requirement to notify him. So wrote Knesses HaGedolah and the Rashba. The Tur, however, holds that according to our ruling one must say that the agent is carrying out his agency even where wheat was exchanged for barley, as indicated there regarding wheat for wheat, and that agency is cancelled only where there is a loss. See the end of siman 184 in the gloss. But in the first rule of seif 4 he need not say “your field,” even though in the case of a woman she must not be the one making the declaration, because that concern does not apply here.
