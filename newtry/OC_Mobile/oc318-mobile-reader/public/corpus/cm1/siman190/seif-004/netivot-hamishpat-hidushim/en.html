Take one hundred and let your field be sold to so and so. This is derived from the law of a Canaanite slave. When another person gives money to the master to free the slave, the slave acquires his own freedom even though he personally gives nothing. The same principle applies here. Sema:
For example, where he appointed him as agent. See the Beurim. This is specifically where the field is worth substantially more than one hundred and the third party gives only one hundred to effect the acquisition, with the purchaser expected to pay the balance. In that case the transfer is not entirely for the purchaser's benefit, so he must specifically have appointed the giver as his agent. But if the entire field is worth only one hundred and the giver gives the full hundred as a gift, acquisition is an unqualified benefit, and one may acquire a benefit for a person in his absence. The purchaser therefore acquires even without appointing an agent. See siman 195, where I wrote that even when the giver gives all the money as a gift, appointment as agent is nevertheless required:
