Your field is acquired to me. The Tur requires the beneficiary later to say, "Your field is acquired to me through the money that so and so gave you." Beit Yosef rejects this requirement in the preceding case and explains that kiddushin is different because one cannot automatically acquire a wife for someone without his participation. In ordinary property law, however, if one person gives money in order to acquire property for another and the transaction is an unqualified benefit, we can acquire on the beneficiary's behalf without his presence. This appears to be why the Shulchan Aruch omitted the requirement of a later declaration not only in the preceding seif but here as well. The principle that one may acquire a benefit for another in his absence already suffices. A husband who buys clothing for his wife can acquire it for her when he takes possession even though she did not know or say anything. Likewise, a sudar kinyan can be made for a beneficiary in his absence without requiring him later to repeat an acquisition formula. Rama's requirement can be understood where the person who supplied the money did so as a loan to the beneficiary rather than as a gift. Such a purchase is not necessarily an unqualified benefit because the beneficiary may not want the transaction or the resulting debt. In that case later acceptance can be needed. But where the payer gave the money gratuitously specifically so the other person should acquire the land, even Rama should agree that no later declaration is required. Moreover, according to the Shulchan Aruch's view elsewhere that even a purchase may be acquired for someone in his absence and the purchaser can later choose whether to accept, the seller cannot retract once the beneficiary accepts. This further explains why the Shulchan Aruch omitted the Tur's special formula.
