Even if they later agreed. The Sema explains that a new act of acquisition is required, by money or by deed, because the money originally given did not effect an acquisition. Beit Meir to Even HaEzer 90:7 writes that the Rama and Sema's statement that later consent is ineffective even while the original purchase money remains in the seller's hands requires study. In the Tashbetz itself, I:13, the facts were that the seller immediately returned the money and did not hold it at all when the later consent was given, and that is how Beit Yosef and Shulchan Aruch there present the case. Had the money still been in the seller's hands when the parties agreed, no new payment should have been necessary. Beit Meir suggests, were he not hesitant to amend the text, that the Shulchan Aruch may contain a scribal error. The Sema, not having the Tashbetz before him, was forced into a strained explanation. See also Shaar Mishpat.
