It appears that specifically where the accident is the worker's accident, meaning an accident affecting the agent himself, the employer need not pay him even for what he already did when the employer received no benefit. But where the accident occurred to the employer, the worker must certainly be paid for what he already performed. What concern is it of the worker that the employer suffered an accident? Even if the employer obtained no benefit, he must pay for what was already done, as is proved by Tosafot in Bava Metzia 79 regarding an unspecified ship. As to wages for work not yet performed, that depends on the distinctions explained in siman 334. Where the accident is the worker's own accident, however, even what he already did need not be paid if the employer received no benefit. This is why, in the case of an agent from whom the entrusted item was lost, Bava Metzia 58 implies that he nevertheless receives wages. There the accident is the employer's, because the object was lost, and the worker can say, "I am here and ready." Therefore he is paid at least for the distance he traveled, even though the employer obtained no benefit. He is not paid for the distance he did not travel, even where the accident is the employer's, as in siman 334. An exception exists where a ship itself is lost because of the hired work. According to many poskim the employer must pay even for the part of the voyage not completed, as explained in siman 311. But for the part already traveled he is always paid where the accident befell the employer, and Shulchan Aruch, siman 66, seif 40, says similarly. By contrast, where the agent found another merchant and the accident therefore lies in the agent's own circumstances, he cannot say, "I remain ready to work." Accordingly, even for half the journey already traveled he receives nothing if the employer obtained no benefit.
