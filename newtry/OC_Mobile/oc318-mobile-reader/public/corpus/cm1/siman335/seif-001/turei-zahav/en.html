(Seif 1: For a specified task, etc.) This means he was hired specifically for a known task, though the employer did not show him how much work it contained. If it ends at midday and the employer cannot give him comparable or easier work, he must pay him as an idle worker. If the employer showed him the task in advance and both knew it would occupy only part of the day, the worker bears the burden of proof. The Sema says the first clause involves no mention of a single day, but that explanation is incoherent. If no daily term was stated, the worker is a contractor, and the phrase “it was completed at midday” has no meaning. The Sema's further claim that when the employer says “for one day” the worker may assume a full day's work has no source where a specific task was named. Both parties are equally informed, and the worker should have clarified. The concluding clause, “if he hired him generally for a day's work,” itself proves that the opening clause also concerns a daily hire, distinguished only because a particular task was specified.
