To bring him on an errand. See Sema, seif katan 7, citing the case where Reuven hired Shimon to serve him on a journey and Reuven died halfway. Sema says Shimon receives his full wage. The same applies where one sends a worker to bring his wife and the relevant need disappears. At first glance this differs from a teacher hired for a child who then dies, where siman 334, seif 4, treats the loss as the teacher's. The distinction appears to follow Raavad, cited by Ran and Nimukei Yosef in HaOmanin and in Gittin. Raavad distinguishes an hourly or time-based worker whose whole period is canceled by an accident affecting the employer from a contractor hired to go to a certain place and perform a particular task. In the latter case, if the employer's accident prevents final completion, the employer can still owe the wage for the undertaken mission. A teacher is hired for a period of time. When the child dies, the teacher's remaining period is canceled, like a day-worker whose day is lost. The ordinary distinctions of siman 334 therefore apply. The present case concerns one hired generally to serve during the journey, not for a fixed number of days. That resembles a contractor. According to Raavad, the loss falls on the employer. Nevertheless, our halachic ruling does not generally follow Raavad in making this categorical contractor distinction. Many places show that contractors too are subject to the rules of siman 334, and even a house rental can be treated similarly. Therefore the present rule should be limited. If the hiring expressly identified the destination, and Reuven died before they reached it, the heirs are exempt from paying for the unperformed part because the contract's objective was not completed. But if he hired Shimon simply to serve him for as long as the journey lasted, without fixing a destination or term, then even if Reuven dies after only one day, the entire condition has been fulfilled. Shimon undertook to serve for whatever duration the journey actually continued. He therefore receives the agreed wage.
