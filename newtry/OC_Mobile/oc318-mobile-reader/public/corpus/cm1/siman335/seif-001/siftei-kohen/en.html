One who teaches another's son without his knowledge, some say he must pay, etc. In my view no authority truly rules so broadly. Maharam's statement that the case is no worse than one who descends into another's field without permission means only that where the father said, 'Teach my son,' thereby revealing that he desired the service, he must pay for the benefit received. Even were Maharam understood as the Rama suggests, the principal ruling follows the Rashba, whose proof from Nedarim is clear. The Rosh, Ran, Tosafot, and the other authorities likewise indicate that one may teach another's child in a place where teachers ordinarily receive payment without thereby automatically imposing liability upon the father absent a request or other manifestation of consent. Liability arises from the father's request or demonstrated acceptance of the benefit, not from the mere fact that instruction was provided.
