(Anthology) One from whom an act of acquisition was made, etc. Yerushalmi, chapters HaOmer and Mi SheAchazo: where a person stipulates, 'If I do not perform such and such for you by a certain day, such and such shall occur,' and an unavoidable accident occurs, Rabbi Yochanan says the accident is regarded as nonperformance, while Reish Lakish says it is regarded as performance. The Semag and Hagahot Maimoniyot write that the law follows Rabbi Yochanan against Reish Lakish, and cite this as support for the ruling of the Geonim and Rambam. At first glance it proves the opposite. One must therefore say that their text read 'he transgressed' with a resh. Alternatively, they relied on the conclusion in Nedarim 27a and 27b, which limits enforcement to a case where no unavoidable accident occurred. It nevertheless requires study why the requirements of a court of sufficient standing and the depositing of the rights documents were not mentioned according to their view. The Tosefta in the first chapter of Bava Metzia indicates that the matter depends on the dispute between Rabbi Yose and Rabbi Yehudah concerning conditional forfeiture. It teaches: two who became embroiled in litigation, and one said, 'If I do not come by a certain day, I shall owe you such and such,' and the date arrived and he did not come, the condition is fulfilled, according to Rabbi Yose. Rabbi Yehudah said: how can the other acquire something that never came into his possession, etc.? The same dispute appears regarding one who mortgaged a house and one who gave earnest money. Perhaps the authorities relied on the Yerushalmi, which mentions no further condition. But this is no proof, because that passage addresses only whether unavoidable accident is a valid excuse, not the validity of the conditional forfeiture itself. The condition remains governed by the ordinary law of conditional forfeitures. This is the gloss's reference to siman 207.
