A time. Rashba, in his responsa, states this law regarding documents where a person stipulated before beit din that if he did not pay by a certain date, the documents should be delivered to his opponent. The day passed and he did not appear, and Rashba ruled that the documents are to be delivered immediately. Thus concludes the Sema. The Shach writes that, in any event, if he asks for time to pay what he owes, he is granted time, etc. See there.

When he demands it from him. The Mechaber could have stated the greater novelty that he may later come and swear and collect. Rather, he continued with the case with which he began. Alternatively, he may hold that only where the oath is to exempt oneself is it regarded as though he never acquired the right at all, unlike an oath taken in order to collect. This requires study as a matter of practical law. Thus concludes the Sema. The Shach, however, concludes that there is no distinction at all, and this is also the Bach's view and the primary ruling. The Shach further writes that with present-day shidduchin, where the engagement is initially made without specification and only afterward are conditions written, such as fixing the wedding date, and it was not stated that the engagement was made on that condition, then even if one party later misses the date, the other remains obligated to honor the engagement if the delay was due to circumstances beyond that party's control, because the essence of the match does not depend upon the date. But where the engagement was initially made expressly on that condition, even if the essence of the act does not depend upon the condition, the other party is released if the condition was not fulfilled, even though the defaulting party was prevented by circumstances beyond his control. This is contrary to the Taz at the end of Yoreh Deah, siman 236. See there in the Shach.
When they made a kinyan. The Sema writes that in the first case, where money is to be taken away from a person, the kinyan must be made before an important beit din and with an immediate-effect clause. In the latter case, where failure to appear merely exempts the other party, even a kinyan before an important beit din is unnecessary, and a simple kinyan suffices. The Shach writes that the Bach disagrees, holding that since there is no definite extraction of money even in the first case, a simple kinyan suffices. The Levush follows the Sema. See there.
