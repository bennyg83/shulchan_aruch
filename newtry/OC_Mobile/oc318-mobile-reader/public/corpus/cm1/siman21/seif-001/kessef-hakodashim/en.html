Gloss. For it is an asmachta. Arbitration agreements sometimes state that if a party fails to appear on a specified day to complete his claims, the beit din may issue a ruling based upon the claims already heard even without him. Even if the document does not contain the standard cancellation of asmachta, perhaps this is not an exaggerated penalty, because the court will still rule only from claims actually presented. The party merely accepts that he will lose the ordinary ability to add or revise arguments, which resembles an admission rather than an arbitrary forfeiture. Nevertheless, because the clause is conditional upon nonappearance, it appears preferable to include an effective remedy against asmachta. The matter requires further study.
