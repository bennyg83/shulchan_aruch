Or that if he does not come, etc. The text states a case that is all the more obvious after the preceding one. If a person can stipulate that his fellow will receive money if he fails to appear, certainly he can stipulate that if he fails to appear his fellow will be exempt from paying him. Alternatively, the Shulchan Aruch divided the cases because the first requires a kinyan before an important beit din, while the second does not. Sema. See the end of this siman.
They do not give him additional time, etc. If he asks for more time before taking the stipulated oath, they do not grant it. He must either lose according to the stipulation or allow his fellow to swear and collect. For actual payment, however, ordinary beit din time is granted, as explained below in siman 100. Shach, and this is obvious.
If he brought proof that he was prevented by circumstances beyond his control, etc. See Tumim, where I discuss this at length. If he was not prevented throughout the entire period but only on the final day when the deadline expired, Yoreh Deah siman 232, seif 12, in the gloss records two views whether that final day impediment excuses him. Rambam's wording here implies that even if only the final day was prevented, the excuse is valid. See there. I also conclude that he must prove the unavoidable circumstance. If he has no evidence and merely asserts, "I was prevented," even if he currently holds the money we do not simply apply the rule that the claimant bears the burden of proof. Rather, he is presumed not to have been prevented until he proves otherwise.
He is released from the kinyan. Sema explains that it is as though no kinyan had ever been made. Even after the unavoidable circumstance passes, if he does not immediately appear he may come whenever he wishes and swear as though the original kinyan never existed. See Tumim, where I note that Perishah's purported proof actually points in the opposite direction. Nevertheless, Rambam and the Shulchan Aruch say, "he is released from the kinyan," which does imply Sema's understanding. This requires study.
And he shall swear when his fellow claims from him. Whether the oath allows him to collect or merely exempts him from payment, the rule is the same. Sema was uncertain, but Shach correctly concludes that there is no distinction.
All this is when they made a kinyan from him, etc. Meaning a kinyan before an important beit din. According to this approach, formal deposit of the right or document with beit din is not required, because the stipulation does not certainly forfeit an existing right. Even if he had appeared, who says he would have sworn? Perhaps the opposing party was correct and he would never swear falsely, Heaven forbid. For this reason Bach holds that even a kinyan before an important beit din is unnecessary. Sema, Ir Shushan, and Shach disagree and require at least such a kinyan. However, the Mechaber himself, in Beit Yosef below in siman 207, holds that even this arrangement requires both an important beit din and deposit of the right with beit din. To understand what "deposit of the right" means where the stipulation is that the other party will swear and collect, one may say that money is deposited with beit din to be handed over if the oath is taken. More likely, the other party holds a document on which he would ordinarily swear and collect, while the first litigant possesses a receipt whose validity is doubtful. Beit din rules that the first may swear that the receipt is valid, and he deposits the receipt with beit din on condition that if he fails to appear by the fixed date he loses the receipt and the creditor then collects on the document after the required oath. In the second clause, where the person himself was entitled to swear and collect but stipulated that if he failed to appear by a certain date the other party would be exempt, Sema holds that even a kinyan before an important beit din is unnecessary. For that reason Rama writes generally only "when a kinyan was made from him." Yet Beit Yosef in siman 207, in the Berlin edition page 203, holds that even this case requires a kinyan before an important beit din, although no deposit is required because the opposing party is already in possession. This requires study. If the case is legally one of "I waive it for you," Rambam holds there is no asmachta and no special beit din kinyan should be needed. If it is not treated as a simple waiver because beit din compelled the stipulation and deposit as the Maggid Mishneh distinguishes in Rambam, Hilkhot Mekhirah chapter 11, then a formal deposit should also be required. This is especially so because the context appears to involve a document against the other party. Otherwise, why would the claimant swear and collect? Yet below, siman 207, seif 15, even where a document is deposited, the law requires a kinyan before an important beit din together with the deposit of the right. Distinguishing these cases requires study. Since the proper place for this issue is siman 207, I have been brief here. See there, where with God's help I discuss the details and governing principles at length.
