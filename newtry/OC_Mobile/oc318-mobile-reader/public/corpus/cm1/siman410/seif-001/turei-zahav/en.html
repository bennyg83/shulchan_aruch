Seif katan 9, and if it caused damage he is exempt. The Sema cites the Rif as distinguishing temporary permission from permanent authorization. This is difficult from the Mishnah in the last chapter of Bava Kamma concerning a swarm of bees: one may enter another's field to rescue his swarm, but if he causes damage he must pay, although the entry is only temporary. One must say that entering another's field differs, as does placing refuse in the public domain. Yet the last chapter of Bava Metzia explains the Mishnah that one who places refuse in the public domain is liable for resulting damage, unlike Rabbi Yehudah, who exempts one whose Chanukah lamp caused damage. Thus, the first tanna imposes liability even where the person acts with permission. Why should this differ from digging adjacent to the public domain here? A Chanukah lamp is placed on one's own premises and only temporarily, yet the Sages impose liability. This requires study.
