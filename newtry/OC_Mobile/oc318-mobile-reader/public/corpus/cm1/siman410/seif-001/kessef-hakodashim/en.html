Seif 30. One who entrusts his pit to a custodian. Regarding liability for his pit, it requires study upon whom the obligation rests here, whether upon the renter or upon the lessor, since the physical pit belongs to the lessor. In seif 30 of siman 410 it is explained that if one entrusts his pit to another, he appoints that person in his stead, and perhaps the same applies here. Yet perhaps, since the principal rental was not for the pit, and the pit is also designated for use by the public, this is not considered entrusting one's pit to an agent. This requires study. Perhaps this may be learned from mezuzah, regarding which we rule that the obligation rests upon the resident; no obligation rests upon the lessor, but only upon the renter. Perhaps the same applies here. Regarding a parapet as well, it requires study whether we follow the resident in this respect, so that the renter is obligated. It also requires study whether the lessor must, at least, reimburse the renter for the expense. In a partnership with a non-Jew, this also requires study with respect to liability for a pit. The Shach, regarding a parapet, writes that he is uncertain about this. In the laws of mezuzah, the Shach questions the Levush, who wrote that the reason a partnership with a non-Jew is exempt is because the verse states, "so that your days may be prolonged." The Yam Shel Shlomo, Chullin, chapter 11, halachah 2, cites the Hagahot Mordechai as giving this reason; see there. This still requires further study. It is reasonable that the obligation rests upon the Jew, though this too requires further study.
