This is his property. Mekhilta: why does the verse say, "if a man opens a pit"? Without the verse one might have reasoned that since the pit is his property and the ox is his property, etc. See Bava Kamma 3a and Tosafos there, s.v. "and your property," and 22a, s.v. "his fire because of his property."
That causes damage, etc., for one who passes, etc. Bava Kamma 3b. This shows that its creation begins with danger; see Rashi there.
That does not move, etc. The opening Mishna of Bava Kamma, "neither this nor that," and the baraisa later. This is evident.
Therefore, etc. Bava Kamma 3b. I already explained that calling it "his property" is not literal in every case, though the characterization sometimes has practical significance, as with a rope on 19b, with damage after an unavoidable fall on 29, and with dung where the Gemara distinguishes cases. See Rashi there, s.v. "when it overturned," and similar cases.
