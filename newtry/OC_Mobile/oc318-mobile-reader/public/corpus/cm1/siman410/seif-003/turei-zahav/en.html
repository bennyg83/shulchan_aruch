Seif 24, and some distinguish. Maharshal writes: The opening chapter of HaMafkid teaches that we do not say “since he was negligent” merely because an animal was taken to a meadow, as Tosafot and the Rosh explain. The rule that an event began through negligence imposes liability for a later accident only when the accident resulted from that negligence. It appears to me that the expression “since” and the doctrine “its beginning was through negligence” are distinct. We say “since he was negligent” only where the negligence and the accident concern the same subject and the same danger. Here the negligence was the failure to prevent the ox from stumbling or falling into the pit, and the accident was that this very ox fell into the pit. It is therefore proper to say “since.” In HaMafkid, however, the negligence was taking the animal to a meadow, exposing it to theft or loss, whereas the accident was death. The two are not one matter. Liability there could arise only from the broader principle that the event began through negligence, and where the accident did not result from the negligence, he is exempt.
