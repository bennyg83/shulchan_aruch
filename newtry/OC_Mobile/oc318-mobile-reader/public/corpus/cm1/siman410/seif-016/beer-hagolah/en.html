There, law 13. The Maggid Mishneh writes that this is an unresolved question there. Rambam combines the two versions of Rav Ashi stated there, 51a. He explains Rambam as follows: the first person dug a pit deep enough to kill, and the second widened it on one side. If the ox fell from the side widened by the second, the second is liable, even if it died from the vapor, because without his widening it would not have fallen. If it fell from the other side, which he did not widen, and died from vapor, the second is exempt because he reduced the vapor; if it died from the impact, the second is liable. The Maggid Mishneh wonders how Rambam combined the two versions, perhaps Rambam holds they do not disagree in practical law. Kesef Mishneh explains that Rambam thereby answers Tosafot's questions on each version: on the first, why should the second be exempt for death by vapor even if the fall was from the side he widened, since without the widening the ox would not have fallen? And on the second, why should he be exempt for impact if he fell from the other side, once he altered the pit? Kesef Mishneh concludes that Rambam understands the two versions as complementary rather than conflicting. In my humble opinion it is far-fetched to obligate the second when the ox fell on the side he did not widen, since the widening did not cause the fall and the ox did not fall into the portion he dug at all. There is no difficulty in the latter version. As for Tosafot's question on the first, in my humble opinion Rambam may explain that the parties do not know from which side the ox fell. If it died from vapor, the second is exempt because it is uncertain whether it fell from the side he widened, and the actual death from vapor was not caused by him. But if it died from impact, since it is uncertain from which side it fell and the second also altered the pit, both share liability. Rambam's statement that 'the latter is liable' means that the latter too is liable. A similar rule appears below in seif 25. Rambam's final statement that if it fell from the side dug by the first, the first is liable because the latter reduced the vapor, and certainly if it died from impact the second is exempt, is because it neither fell nor struck where the second dug.
