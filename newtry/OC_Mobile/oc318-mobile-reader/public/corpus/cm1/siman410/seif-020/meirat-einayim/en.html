Likewise, if a person fell into it and died, etc. Scripture says, "and an ox or donkey falls there." The terms ox and donkey are not literal exclusions of other animals, because through a comparison with Shabbat we learn liability for every animal. Why then mention ox and donkey? The Sages interpret: ox, but not a person; donkey, but not utensils. The exclusion of a person applies specifically when the person falls into the pit and dies, because the verse speaks of death, as it says, "and the dead animal shall be his." If a person falls in and is injured, however, the pit owner is liable for the injury. Utensils are excluded even when they merely break, because breakage is their equivalent of death. The Mechaber states this in the following seif.
Some say that with a sighted animal he is exempt, etc. An animal's eyes are directed downward and it ordinarily watches the path before it and protects itself from obstacles. A human being walks with his eyes roaming upward and forward rather than constantly upon the ground, as the Mechaber explains in siman 412:3. Therefore the law can distinguish between a sighted animal and a person.
