kaf) Explicit in the third chapter on 28b: these limitations apply only regarding death.
lamed) The Maggid Mishneh writes that liability for damage to an intelligent animal is not stated explicitly in the Gemara, and the Raavad objects. It appears to me that the Rambam derived it from Bava Kamma 27b, which says that people do not ordinarily examine the roads carefully. The Rambam reasons that this is all the more true of an animal, which lacks human understanding. When the Gemara excludes an intelligent animal by day, it excludes only liability for its death, because intelligent animals do guard themselves against a major obstacle involving danger to life, but they do not guard themselves as carefully against lesser injury. Therefore the owner of the pit remains liable for damages. It is possible that the Raavad holds that a thinking person may fail to examine the road, whereas an animal ordinarily watches its path, and therefore such damage is an unavoidable accident. The Rashba explains similarly.
mem) In the name of the Raavad and Rashba, as explained above. The Bach likewise derives this from the Ramah in seif 23 and writes that this is the ruling we follow.
