Tosafot explains that the exclusion is needed for a slave or a gentile slave owned by a Jew, because such a body may otherwise have monetary value. The principal application is to a gentile slave or one who has not fully entered Jewish status. A slave who has accepted the commandments has the status of a Jew for these purposes and his corpse is prohibited in benefit. Raavad likewise distinguishes a slave who accepted the commandments from one who did not. A difficulty arises from the law that a gentile does not fully transfer bodily ownership of a slave without the required acquisition and immersion. Until then the Jewish purchaser may have only a lien for the slave's labor. Damage to such a lien is ordinarily *dina de-garmi*. If *dina de-garmi* is only rabbinic, why is a Torah verse needed to exclude the person from pit liability? According to Ramban, however, *dina de-garmi* is Torah law and includes monetary damage even where the damaged interest is only a lien. Then the verse excluding a person is necessary to exempt damage to a slave or gentile whose body is not yet fully owned but whose labor is monetarily encumbered. This also answers Tosafot's question why the verse excludes "ox" and "donkey" in the pit laws. Once the exclusion of a person is needed even for a monetarily valuable lien, we cannot say that documents and other nonbodily monetary interests are automatically excluded from pit liability merely because their bodies are not money. The Torah's separate exclusions therefore retain force. The discussion has a parallel in the law of ritual impurity and prohibition of benefit from a corpse. Rashba argues that both Jewish and gentile corpses are prohibited in benefit. The verse comparing idolatrous offerings to the dead can therefore teach prohibition of benefit and impurity through contact and carrying. Yet impurity by tent can still be limited to Jewish corpses according to the authorities who hold gentile graves do not convey tent impurity. This resolves Tosafot's question why the comparison is used for some forms of impurity but not necessarily for tent impurity.
