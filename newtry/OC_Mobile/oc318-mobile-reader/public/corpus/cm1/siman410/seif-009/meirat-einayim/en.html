This does not resemble the law below in siman 414 concerning placing refuse in the public domain, or siman 417 concerning leaving beams and stones there for construction. In those cases he also acts with permission, yet if they cause damage he is liable. There the hazardous material itself is placed in the public domain. Here the pit remains within his own property, and the condition is temporary. As soon as the excavation is finished he will fill it with the stones of the building foundation. For the same reason it differs from one who places an oven in his house at the prescribed distance from the upper floor yet is still liable if the upper floor burns, as explained in siman 155. There the use is ongoing, whereas this excavation is temporary. The Rif writes similarly, as I cited in the Derishah.
