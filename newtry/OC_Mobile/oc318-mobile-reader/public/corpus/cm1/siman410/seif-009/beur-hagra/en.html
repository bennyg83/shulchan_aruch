Even, etc.; and if, etc. The Gemara establishes the case as one where he widened the space. See Rashi. Yam Shel Shlomo and Bach maintain that Rashi's text contains a scribal error. The phrase, 'in private property adjacent to the public domain, such as one who sets aside a place,' should appear under the words, 'where he widened it toward the public domain.' Thus the exemption is because of the noise, but in the public domain itself he is liable even though he acted with permission, as stated on 30a. Their objection is directed against Tur. Shulchan Aruch can be explained consistently with their reading. See Be'er HaGolah.
