(Supplement) Some say, etc. Nimukei Yosef writes similarly at the end of the third chapter regarding innocuous oxen that caused damage. He explains that because the question remains unresolved, one collects only one quarter from each animal. If the full quarter cannot be collected from one, the balance is collected from the other according to Rav Nachman. See 391.
(Supplement) Others disagree, etc. Mordechai at the end of the fifth chapter adopts this view. See siman 388, seif 7.
(Supplement) Others disagree, etc. See Tosafot 46b, s.v. you have revealed. Even Rav Nachman agrees there because the ox owner is not liable at all, unlike here, where liability exists but collection is prevented by uncertainty. The decisors derived this ruling from there.
(Supplement) Others disagree, etc. See Mordechai, end of HaParah, siman 70, and Hagahot Maimoniyot. Another responsum adopts the opposite view. At the end of Mishpatim, siman 63, Mordechai follows the first opinion and uses this rationale to explain why two joint borrowers each become fully obligated, so that if one cannot pay the entire debt may be collected from the other. See the beginning of siman 77. See 63, 70, and 388.
(Supplement) According to this, etc. Compensation for pit damage is collected today, as shown by the incident on 27b in Pumbedita where Rava imposed liability. Nimukei Yosef writes this in the name of Ramah. It is strained to say that the case involved seizure. Rashba likewise explains the incidents of the donkey and the goat in the second chapter as cases without seizure, because common forms of damage are adjudicated in Babylonia.
