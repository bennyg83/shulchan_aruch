Or if his animal or a wild animal dug it, etc. Although Scripture says, "if a man digs a pit," excluding a pit dug by an animal in the public domain, the present case concerns a pit dug by his animal in his own courtyard. That is no less than a pit that appeared there on its own. A property owner must at least prevent people from being injured by a dangerous condition in his own land. This assumes that afterward he abandoned the courtyard around the pit but did not at that same time abandon the place of the pit itself. If he never abandoned the courtyard, he can say to others, "What were you doing in my property?" If he later abandoned both the pit and surrounding courtyard simultaneously, he likewise does not become liable merely from the earlier excavation because at the time it was dug everything was private property and he then performed no new act of excavation. But if he first had the pit in his courtyard and afterward abandoned the surrounding area, thereby allowing people to approach right up to the pit, it is like opening a pit into the public domain and he must guard it. Even if he later abandons the pit itself, he cannot cast off a dangerous condition after liability to remove it has already arisen. This is learned from the Tur and Mechaber below in seif 6.
