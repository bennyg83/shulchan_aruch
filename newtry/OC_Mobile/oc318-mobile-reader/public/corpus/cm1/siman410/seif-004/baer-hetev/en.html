Although the verse says, 'when a man digs a pit,' as stated in the next seif, that limitation applies where the pit is dug in the public domain, since the ground is not his and, if he did not dig it, he has no duty to fill it. If his animal dug a pit in his own courtyard, however, it is no different from a pit that arose there by itself, for he is at least obligated to ensure that a person not be injured on his land by a pit. This applies specifically where he abandoned the surrounding courtyard but did not at that time abandon the place of the pit itself. If he did not abandon the courtyard at all, he may say: what right do others have to enter my courtyard? He is exempt, analogous to damage caused by an animal's foot in the injured party's domain, as discussed in simanim 390 and 391. If he abandoned the courtyard and the pit simultaneously, he likewise never became liable for damage caused by the pit, since at the time it was dug everything belonged to him. But if he first dug in his courtyard and afterward abandoned the area around the pit, thereby permitting everyone to approach it, this is like opening a pit in the public domain, as in seif 6. If he abandoned only his property, then even if he later also abandoned the pit itself he remains liable for its damage, because he cannot free himself by abandoning a hazard after the obligation to remove it has already taken effect.
