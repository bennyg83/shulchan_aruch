(Seif katan 37: and some disagree, etc.) Maharshal writes: I found in an old gloss to the Rosh that Behag wrote in a responsum in the name of a Gaon that if a Jew and a non-Jew were partners in business and traded with another Jew, and they deceived that Jew in the transaction, the Jewish partner is liable for the entire onaah. Even though the amount cannot be recovered from the non-Jew, it is collected from the Jew under Rabbi Natan's principle. This requires study. It is unlike a case where the other liable party fled or became poor, because there remains the possibility of collection from him if his circumstances later improve. Furthermore, the Jewish victim relied upon the Jewish partner to be careful not to deceive him, making it as though the Jew alone caused the damage. Nevertheless, where the transaction was a fundamental mistake that even non-Jewish law would reverse, such as selling red wheat that was found white, or silver that was found to be tin, the Jewish partner certainly does not pay the entire loss. This is the end of Maharshal's words.
