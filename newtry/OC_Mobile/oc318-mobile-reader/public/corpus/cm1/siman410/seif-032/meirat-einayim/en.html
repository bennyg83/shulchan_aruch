I have against you the depreciation of this carcass. For I found the cause of my loss in your pit.
Even though it was large and perceptive, etc. The Tur places this "even though" at the beginning: an ox pushed an animal into a pit even though it was daytime and the animal was perceptive, etc. That wording is clearer. According to the Mechaber and Rambam's version, one must explain that the injured party addresses the owner of the ox and says: the depreciation of the carcass is your responsibility alone, even though, etc. Therefore, whatever amount I can collect, etc.
Since it was pushed, etc. The owner of the pit should have anticipated that this ox might push another animal into the pit, causing his pit to damage the owner of the animal.
And you must pay the remainder. This is the rule of Rabbi Natan in the Gemara: whenever one cannot collect the full amount from one liable party, he collects the balance from the other.
