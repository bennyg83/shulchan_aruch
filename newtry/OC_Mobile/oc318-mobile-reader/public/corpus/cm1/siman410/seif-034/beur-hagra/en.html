Hagahot Maimoniyot resolves only a case involving two oxen, thereby answering Tosafot's question why the Gemara did not establish both rulings according to Rav Nachman, one following the first version and one the second. In the present case the matter remains difficult. It appears that the statement, 'my ox is in your pit,' does not depend specifically upon who completed the damage. Rather, at that moment the pit alone causes the damage without the ox, and all the more so the ox that began the damaging process and is its principal cause. The Gemara itself says, 'the digger caused it.' Tosafot's distinction concerns specifically two oxen that acted together from beginning to end, as do Hagahot Maimoniyot.
(Supplement) If, etc. Even according to the latter version, 'my ox is in your pit' is not limited to that exact case. It applies equally to the case of this seif and similar cases, unlike Tosafot. Ramban writes so at length in Milchamot.
(Supplement) Seif 35, if a person, etc. In seif 32 the reason involving the owner of the carcass was needed because the pit caused only half the damage. With two oxen, even the latter version agrees that each caused the entire damage, unlike Tosafot. Here the matter requires study. Even according to Tosafot, the first version is authoritative because Rava and Ravina follow it. Rif and Rambam, however, hold like Maharam that even under the latter version liability applies here, and therefore cite that version. See 12.
