Although, etc. See Tosafos there, s.v. "he consecrated it." According to Rabbi Akiva, whom we follow, the ox is not sold even for plowing. Rashi likewise understands this. The Rambam, however, holds that it may be sold for plowing even according to Rabbi Akiva. This follows the baraisa, "a tam ox," etc., where the Gemara discusses "he sold it," in a form apparently accepted by all opinions. It also says that consecration takes effect because of Rabbi Avahu's principle, a point needed specifically even according to Rabbi Akiva. The later discussion, "he sold it, the sale is ineffective, according to whom? Rabbi Akiva," initially assumes that the sale is wholly ineffective only according to Rabbi Akiva, and then answers that in this respect it is ineffective even according to Rabbi Yishmael. The Rashba also agrees that according to Rabbi Akiva it is not sold for plowing, though he disagrees with Rashi's explanation of the phrase. He holds that the phrase means an initial sale for use in plowing, as shown by other sugyos concerning use of stolen property. It appears that this is also the Rambam's interpretation. Thus "although," etc., means that even initial use is permitted according to the rule of sale.

He sold it, etc. There, where the Gemara contrasts the injured party's sale. We rule like Rabbi Akiva, whose view is accepted against an individual colleague and is also reflected in the anonymous Mishna there.
And when he seizes it, etc. This applies even nowadays, when the injured party acquires only through seizure, for even in the time of ordained courts he did not acquire until judgment, yet the owner's sale was ineffective. See Tosafos there, s.v. "the difference is," etc.
