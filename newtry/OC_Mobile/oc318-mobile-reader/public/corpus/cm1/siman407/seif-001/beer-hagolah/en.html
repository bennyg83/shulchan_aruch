a) The language of the Rambam, chapter 8 of Hilkhot Nizkei Mamon, from the baraita in the third chapter of Bava Kamma 33b.
bet) Ibid., in the Gemara, 'sold for plowing.' The Maggid Mishneh explains that the purchaser may initially plow with it, and that the sale remains valid and neither party may retract. He appears to understand the baraita according to Rabbi Akiva, who says there that the injured party and owner are partners, and derives this from the law that if the owner consecrated the ox the consecration takes effect because of Rabbi Abbahu's principle. According to Rabbi Yishmael, who treats the injured party as a creditor, Rabbi Abbahu's rule would not be needed.
gimel) Ibid., in the Gemara. The meaning is that in our time, when beit din does not collect the penalty, if the injured party seized the ox it is not removed from him.
dalet) Explained in the sugya there according to Rabbi Akiva, whose view is followed against an individual colleague.
