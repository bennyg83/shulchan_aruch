Sema seif katan 2, "sale." Note: Nevertheless, the words of the Perishah are difficult. How can this person sell something in which he has no present right, but only a possible right if he eventually seizes it? See Bava Kamma 70a, the statement of the Sages of Nehardea, and see what I wrote there. There is a further difficulty: if his sale is effective, the purchaser should be able to seize it himself, since he stands in the place of the injured party. Why then does the Perishah write that when the injured party seizes it, the purchaser takes it from him? It therefore appears clear to me that the sale certainly does not take effect so as to make the property the purchaser's until after the injured party has exerted himself to seize it. At that point he must give it to the purchaser, because we say that he exerted himself for this purpose, so that the transaction should stand, as in Bava Metzia 15b, "it is agreeable to him," etc. See there. The Perishah also assumes as obvious that after seizure the tortfeasor must pay the injured party the value of the animal's plowing capacity and the depreciation caused by slaughter. This too is difficult in my eyes. In my humble opinion, it is clear that this applies only if he actually seized from him the value of the plowing capacity or the amount of the slaughter depreciation. If he did not seize it, he cannot collect it.
