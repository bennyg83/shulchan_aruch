heh) Ibid., law 6, from the baraita there: 'alive, I only know...' The Rambam writes that if the damaging party consecrated it, the consecration takes effect so that people should not say that consecrated property leaves sanctity without redemption. The Maggid Mishneh writes that this is Rabbi Abbahu's rule cited above, meaning that the injured party redeems it for a nominal amount and then collects from it. The commentators likewise explain this. I do not know why the Mechaber omitted it.
vav) From the Rosh's comment on Rav Huna's statement that one who damages another's lien is exempt. The Rosh writes that later we rule according to the view that adjudicates dina d'garmi, and therefore one who damages another's lien is liable. Hagahot Ashri and the Tur likewise write this. In my humble opinion, we do not compel him to pay the injured party unless the injured party already seized, as written above.
zayin) This authority holds that the interpretation 'sold for plowing' applies to a baraita that follows Rabbi Yishmael. According to Rabbi Akiva, whose view we follow, the sale is not valid even for plowing, unlike what was written in the previous siman in the name of the Maggid Mishneh regarding the Rambam's words that although it was sold, the sale stands. In my humble opinion, the Rama should have placed this gloss above. Here too, it seems to me that it applies where the injured party seized. The Rashba, however, writes that according to Rabbi Akiva the damaging owner who sells the ox is a robber, and a robber does not pay rent to the owner, as written in siman 363, seif 3.
chet) Ibid., in the Rambam, from the baraita there, following Rashi's explanation that what was done remains effective for purposes of plowing, as written in the previous siman in the name of the Maggid Mishneh. The Tur does not agree. In my humble opinion, the Rama should also have glossed here with the Tur's view.
