Some say he must pay the depreciation caused by the slaughter. The Shach says this follows from the accepted rule that one who damages another's lien is liable. According to Tosafot's reading of the baraita, the case follows Rabbi Yishmael, under whom the injured party has only a lien on the ox because the owner can discharge him with money. Slaughter is therefore damage to a lien. According to the explanation I gave in Rambam and the Shulchan Aruch, even Rabbi Akiva agrees that before judgment the injured party has not yet acquired the ox itself. His eventual right to collect it resembles a lien until judgment. Therefore slaughter before judgment is likewise damage to the injured party's lien rather than destruction of property already owned by him.
