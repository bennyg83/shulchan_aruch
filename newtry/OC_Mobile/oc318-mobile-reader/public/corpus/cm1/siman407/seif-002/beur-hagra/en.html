And some say he must, etc. The Gemara says, "this teaches," etc., and associates the issue with Rabbah. Since we do not rule like Rabbah, as shown on 98b, where the Gemara says "whoever adjudicates," and we rule that dina d'garmi is compensable as explained at the beginning of siman 386, all the more so one who destroys another's lien must pay. In Gittin the poskim likewise rule like Rabban Shimon ben Gamliel that one who destroys another's lien is liable. The Rambam himself rules accordingly, as stated above in siman 117 seif 6 and Yoreh Deah 267 at the end.
Likewise if, etc. On this point he disagrees with the Rambam, as above, and follows Rashi. They explain the baraisa as referring to Rabbi Yishmael. The statement that consecration takes effect because of Rabbi Avahu applies even according to Rabbi Yishmael because monetary consecration does not remove a lien. See Tosafos, s.v. "because." Yam Shel Shlomo. This remains difficult, for then why does the Gemara say, "consecration takes effect, we have arrived at Rabbi Yishmael," and why force the case into monetary consecration?
(Collected note) Likewise if, etc. The statement that a sale is effective for plowing refers to Rabbi Yishmael. The statement that consecration takes effect because of Rabbi Avahu also applies according to Rabbi Yishmael, as Tosafos explains. Tosafos' reference there concerns the present statement. The earlier statement that consecration takes effect even according to Rabbi Akiva refers to bodily sanctity, yet because Rabbi Akiva holds that the ox has been absolutely awarded to the injured party, one might have thought such consecration would not take effect. Tosafos therefore writes that "here" specifically refers to monetary consecration. The Gemara did not answer bodily sanctity here because the clauses of sale and gift are effective only for plowing, not absolutely. (End of note.)
