For even if it were with a creditor, etc. The phrase "he would collect from its body" means that if the ox had belonged outright to the creditor, the injured party could collect from it after it caused damage. All the more so when the creditor has only a lien. In the Derishah I noted that the Gemara itself gives this reasoning, while Tosafot observes that it is not literally an a fortiori argument. If the creditor had always owned the ox, or had already collected it for his debt before the damage, he would be liable because he was responsible for guarding it. Here the damage occurred before it came into his possession. The Gemara's point is only that the creditor's lien upon the body of the ox is weaker than the injured party's lien created by the tort.
An expressly designated apotiki for the creditor. Meaning, the debtor wrote, "You shall have no payment except from this property." In such a case the creditor cannot collect from other assets if this apotiki is stolen or ruined, unless both parties agree otherwise or the debtor pays him cash, as explained in siman 117.
The creditor has priority. Meaning where he first seized it. In the Perishah and Derishah I wrote that even so, if the injured party first seized the ox, it is not removed from him. An express apotiki of movable property is no stronger against another claimant than other movables designated for payment. If the debtor sold them first, or a later creditor collected them first, the disposition can be effective, as explained in siman 117. Even if neither party first seized and both the injured party and creditor come to collect this ox together, beit din divides it between them equally, because there is ordinarily no priority in movables, as explained in siman 104. Conversely, if the injured party's claim arose before the loan to this creditor, then even if the creditor first seized the ox that had been designated to him as an express apotiki, the seizure is ineffective. A goring ox has a public legal designation as the ox that gored so and so, and the injured party's lien upon it has publicity similar to a lien upon land. Regarding land, a later creditor who first collects does not retain it against an earlier lien. See siman 104.
