Likewise, if he used it. Rashba asks why the tortfeasor should pay for the ox's labor. Robbers pay according to the value at the time of robbery, and a purchaser from a robber is treated similarly. The answer is that before judgment the tortfeasor is not a robber of the injured party's ox. He is legally entitled to use it, because the fine has not yet been judicially awarded and he is not even obligated to pay the fine to satisfy Heaven before beit din imposes it. When the injured party later obtains judgment under Rabbi Akiva, Scripture awards him the ox retroactively. That retroactive entitlement includes the value of its use during the intervening period. But because the tortfeasor's use was lawful when performed, he never acquired the special status of a robber who keeps all benefits under the rule that robbers pay only according to the time of theft. This resembles land, which is not subject to robbery. Even one who entered intending to wrongfully possess it must pay rent because no robbery acquisition transferred the land's benefits to him. So too here, the tortfeasor's interim use does not acquire the ox's rental value for himself. Once the injured party's retroactive right is established, the use value follows that right.
