(Supplement) Some say specifically, etc. See Rashi 36a, s.v. the first, who writes that this differs from an ordinary creditor. Rashba objects that this works only where the debtor has other assets, as the Gemara itself asks on 40b. He therefore adopts Raavad's explanation: as against the injured party, the ox is like expressly designated land, because payment comes only from its body, as though it had been sold to him for the damage. This is unlike an ordinary creditor. Even an express designation does not allow a creditor to collect after sale. Hence the first claimant collects first, just as with designated land. This is the first, anonymous view of Shulchan Aruch. Tur and Rama follow Rashi and Tosafot. See Tur, Bach, and Derisha uPerisha. See 40 and 117.
