(Collected note) Some say that Reuven acquired, etc. For the Gemara establishes acquisition by a courtyard specifically where he runs after them and can reach them. Even if the seller unloaded the goods and brought them into the buyer's house, chapter HaSefinah and chapter 2 of Avodah Zarah state that the buyer does not acquire because his mind is not settled to acquire. Even if the seller said, "I am selling to you," and transferred by a kinyan, he does not acquire, as stated there. His courtyard is no better than a kinyan. These words are correct, and this is the conclusion of the Mordechai there; this is the primary view.
(Collected note) And if one merchant, etc. The first opinion is primary, as was proven there with strong proofs that cannot be refuted from the requirement that he run after them, etc., and from the statement that even if he unloaded and brought them into his house, etc., his house does not acquire them for him so long as the price was not fixed, even though he brought them in for acquisition. Even a kinyan is ineffective until the price is fixed, as stated in Avodah Zarah 72a. See there.
Others say that he acquired, etc., through his courtyard, etc., like one who saw, etc. This is the second opinion. The first opinion writes that the cases are not comparable, for there the Gemara says he must run after them, etc., while here perhaps he will not immediately obtain the money. Moreover, Bava Batra 86a states, "he unloaded them, etc.," and there, "since he unloaded them, etc."
And if afterward, etc. As stated in Yevamos 37b, and we rule like Rav who says that the original adjudication stands; otherwise he acquires in either event.
(Collected note) But in a field, etc., and he said, etc. There 102a. See Rosh there, seif 29: "the Rabbis taught, one who says, etc." The case concerns an unguarded courtyard, etc.
Others say, etc. See Tosafos there, s.v. "zachtah, etc." Rambam distinguishes a guarded courtyard, where no verbal statement is necessary, from four cubits. See Beer HaGolah.
(Collected note) Others say that where he is standing, etc. See Rosh there. His proof is from the statement, "when he did not say, etc.," and the dictum of Rabbi Yehoshua ben Levi that one's courtyard, etc. Rambam avoids this difficulty by writing that four cubits and a guarded courtyard require no statement, but an unguarded courtyard does. The Rosh's proof from Bava Kamma 49b is compelling, but the Rosh follows Tosafos there, who explain that "he is not there" refers to the owner. So too the Tur and Shulchan Aruch in siman 72:39 and the Rosh in Bava Kamma. Rambam, however, follows Rashi's explanation there, as stated in siman 275:28. The only practical difference between the explanations is this rule. Therefore Ulla did not establish the Mishnah as a fenced field where standing alongside is unnecessary, because the Mishnah states that he said it, and therefore it must concern an unguarded courtyard, as above.
