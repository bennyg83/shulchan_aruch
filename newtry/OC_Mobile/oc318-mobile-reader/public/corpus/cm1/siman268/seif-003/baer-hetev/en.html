His courtyard. Meaning, even if he is not standing alongside it, since it is guarded. The Gemara derives this from agency, for the hand of a person's agent is like his own hand to acquire for him something that is beneficial to him. So writes the Sema. See the Shach, who discusses at length and disputes Maharshal regarding ye'ush without the owner's knowledge when the object is in a courtyard. He also wrote part of this above in siman 262, seif katan 3.
The guarded courtyard. This implies that with a guarded courtyard, according to all opinions, he need not say, 'My field has acquired it for me,' since it acquires even without his knowledge. What the Tur writes at the end of this siman in the name of the Rambam, that even when it is guarded he must say that it has acquired it, must therefore be a scribal error, for the Gemara proves in several places that no statement is required. The Rambam also refers only to an unguarded courtyard when he is standing alongside it. The Bach likewise writes that this is a scribal error. Shach.
When he knows. The same is true of his hand: in such a case it does not acquire for him without his knowledge. This is evident from the poskim who are the source of this ruling, and is also implied above in siman 232, seif 18, in the gloss. See Raavan at the end of the chapter Shnayim Ochazin, folio 93d, Responsa Maharshal siman 148, and the cited work folio 102a. Ibid.
To purchase it. Perhaps the money needed to purchase it would not have become available to him. The rule of 'a poor person turning over a cake,' which restricts another from interfering with an impending purchase, does not apply to a found object. Even if the seller said, 'If I sell the merchandise I will sell it to you,' the prospective buyer has not acquired it so long as they did not fix the price. Since he did not acquire the merchandise, he did not acquire the profit either. So writes the Mordechai in the name of Raavyah. See below, siman 260, seif 4. Sema.
The ruling was issued. This is from Terumat HaDeshen, siman 310, who brought proof from the chapter HaCholetz, Yevamot 36. See my work Tekfo Kohen at its end, where I challenged him both logically and from the Gemara. On the contrary, I proved from the Gemara there that even after judgment was rendered, he must give him one item of merchandise by virtue of a definite claim arising from either possibility. This is only when both events occurred to the same person. One who comes by virtue of another person, however, involves two different persons, and we do not apply that definite claim from either possibility. There is no distinction whether judgment was already rendered. See there at length. Shach.
One. A similar rule is written by the Tur in the name of the Ramah in siman 200, seif 8, regarding the buyer's utensils on the seller's premises. If both situations happen with the same seller and buyer, the buyer can proceed against him with a definite claim from either possibility, and one of the items is deposited. See also the Tur at the beginning of siman 197 regarding a camel acquired by leading and a donkey by pulling, and the Tur at the beginning of siman 390 regarding an animal that caused damage by wagging its tail or its organ, together with what is written there. Sema.
