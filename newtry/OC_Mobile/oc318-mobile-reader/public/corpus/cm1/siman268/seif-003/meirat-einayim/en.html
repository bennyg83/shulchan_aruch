A person's courtyard acquires for him, etc. Meaning, even if he is not standing beside it, so long as it is guarded. The Gemara derives this from agency, because the hand of one's agent is like one's own hand for acquiring a benefit on his behalf.
But something not ordinarily expected to come there, etc. At the beginning of siman 260 it was already explained that for this reason an old cache found in a wall, which might belong to ancient inhabitants, belongs to the finder. We do not say that the householder's courtyard acquired the cache for him. Mordechai likewise writes that if a non-Jew lost money in a Jew's house, the courtyard does not acquire it for the Jew because such a thing is not ordinarily expected and he did not know of it. See siman 260 and Darkei Moshe here.
See above, siman 232, seif 18. There Rama cites Mordechai that if a broker bought from a non-Jew an article thought to be tin and sold it to another, and it was afterward discovered to contain silver or gold, the purchaser acquires it rather than the broker. It is not a mistaken transaction in the broker's favor, since he himself did not know what was in it while it was in his possession.
Which is not a complete found object, etc. Mordechai, citing Ravyah, explains that a found object is different because no further acquisition act or money is lacking. In a bargain purchase, by contrast, perhaps the person would never have had the money available to buy it. The rule of a poor person pursuing a cake does not apply to a found object. Even if the seller says, "If I sell the merchandise, I will sell it to you," so long as they have not fixed the price the first person has not acquired the merchandise, and therefore his courtyard has not acquired the profit either. See also siman 260, where the Tur cites the Rosh and the Mechaber rules in seif 4 that if one rents a house and a wounded deer comes there, a one-day rental is treated as a sale and the premises are like a courtyard jointly belonging to both, so the find belongs to both. But if a thief enters carrying stolen property, the householder does not acquire it through the courtyard; whoever first takes it acquires. In the Perishah I explained the reason.
If merchandise later came. Meaning, after merchandise already came to Shimon's house, Reuven acquired it and left with it, other merchandise later came to Reuven's house and Shimon acquired it. Shimon refuses to give Reuven the profit and argues against him from either side: if the law follows the opinion that a courtyard acquires even bargain merchandise, then you were correct regarding the second merchandise, but give me the first as well because my house acquired it for me. If the law follows the opinion that the house does not acquire such merchandise, then leave me the profit of the second merchandise that I bought in your house. This is the question and answer in Terumat HaDeshen, siman 310.
The burden of proof rests on the one seeking to extract. Meaning, he must prove which opinion the halacha follows.
Reuven must give Shimon one of the items, from whichever side. A similar law is stated by the Tur in siman 200:8 in Ramah's name concerning the purchaser's vessel in the seller's domain and the seller's vessel in the purchaser's domain, or where both doubts occur between one seller and one purchaser. The purchaser can proceed against him from whichever side and recover one of the items. From here we learn there too that if judgment has already been rendered on the first item, then regarding the second we apply the ordinary rule that the burden of proof rests on the one seeking to extract. See also the Tur at the beginning of siman 197 regarding a camel by leading and a donkey by pulling, where one of the two modes does not acquire, and at the beginning of siman 390 concerning an animal that moved with its tail or reproductive organ.
If he stood beside his field. I already explained that whenever a person stands next to his field, the field is like his hand and acquires an item placed into it if the item does not move from its place, such as money or utensils, because his presence there guards it.
