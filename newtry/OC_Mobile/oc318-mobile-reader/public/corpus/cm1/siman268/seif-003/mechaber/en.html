A person's guarded courtyard acquires for him without his knowledge, and a found object that falls there belongs to the courtyard's owner. Some limit this to an object of which he knows or whose arrival he contemplated; an unusual object is not acquired by his courtyard, and another who takes it before the owner learns of it acquires it. If a merchant brings discounted goods into Shimon's courtyard and Shimon says, 'Let my courtyard acquire them,' but Reuven later buys them, some say Reuven acquires because this is not a complete find and requires payment, while others award them to the courtyard owner. If a reciprocal incident later occurs, and the first case was already adjudicated, the second is judged independently and the claimant bears the burden of proof. If the first had not yet been decided, Reuven must give Shimon one of the two lots because of the unavoidable alternative. In an unguarded field or garden, if the owner stands beside it and says, 'Let my field acquire for me,' he acquires; otherwise the first possessor acquires. Some say standing beside the field alone suffices. See siman 200.
