And he said, "My field has acquired it for me." According to the alternative opinion Rama cited in the preceding seif, here too it is unnecessary for him to say, "My field has acquired it for me." The Tur writes this explicitly. Nimukei Yosef, however, distinguishes between the cases. Regarding a wounded deer, he rules like the Rambam that one must say, "My field has acquired it for me," while regarding other found objects he rules like the Rosh that no such statement is necessary. Darkei Moshe cites this. It is possible that for this reason Rama did not cite the alternative opinion here.
Since another person's intention is directed toward transferring it to him. Since the donor wishes to transfer it to him, the acquisition is stronger and his courtyard acquires it even though he cannot reach the object. Nimukei Yosef writes in the chapter Elu Metziot that if one declares an object ownerless and throws it into another person's courtyard, its law is like a gift and the courtyard acquires it for him even though he runs after it and cannot reach it. Darkei Moshe cites this.
