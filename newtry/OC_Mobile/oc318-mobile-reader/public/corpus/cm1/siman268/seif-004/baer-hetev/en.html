And he said. According to the dissenting opinion cited by Rama above, here too we do not require him to say, 'My field has acquired it.' The Tur states this explicitly. Nimukei Yosef, however, distinguishes between the cases and writes that regarding an injured deer we rule like the Rambam that he must say that his field has acquired it, whereas for other found objects the Rosh rules that he need not say so. Darkei Moshe cites this. It may be for this reason that Rama did not record the dissenting view here. Ibid.
And another. Since the giver intends to transfer ownership to him, his acquisition is stronger, and his courtyard acquires it for him even though he cannot reach it. Ibid.
His field. If a person declared an object ownerless and threw it into another person's courtyard, its law is like a gift and the courtyard acquires it for him, even though he runs after it and cannot reach it. So writes Darkei Moshe in the name of Nimukei Yosef. The Rosh likewise reads the Gemara's unresolved question as including 'and he declared it ownerless,' unlike Tosafot there. See the Tur at the end of siman 243 and the cited work folio 101d. Shach.
