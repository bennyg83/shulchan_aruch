These four cubits acquire for him. The Sages gave these four cubits the law of his courtyard. When he stands beside them, they acquire for him even though they are not guarded, through the law of a hand adjacent to him, as explained in the next seif. The Tur writes that the Raavad holds the enactment of four cubits applies only when a person is standing still, not while walking, whereas the Rosh said there is no distinction. Although the principle that a moving courtyard does not acquire, discussed in siman 202, does not literally apply here, because the land within the four cubits is stationary, nevertheless the person must stand beside it for it to function like his adjacent hand. The Raavad may hold that the Sages did not enact four-cubit acquisition while he is moving about, only while standing in one place.
In a side alley or at the sides of the public domain. Rashi explains a side alley as a corner adjacent to the public domain that is open for public use, allowing those who need to escape crowding to step into it. The same is true of the sides of the public domain, meaning the strip next to the houses for that purpose.
But one standing in the public domain, etc. The reason is that many people ordinarily stand there, so no one has four cubits uniquely allocated to him.
