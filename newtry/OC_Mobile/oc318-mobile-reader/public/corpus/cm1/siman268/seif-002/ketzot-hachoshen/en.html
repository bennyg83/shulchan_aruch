Two people who entered the same four cubits together. Gittin discusses two parties standing within four cubits and asks how a half and half result can occur. Rabbi Shmuel bar Rav Yitzchak answers that they are both standing within the same four cubits. The Gemara asks which one arrived first, since the first person's four cubits should already have acquired, and adds that exact simultaneity is impossible. This shows that the first person to enter acquires the four cubits, and a later arrival cannot acquire within those already acquired four cubits. Even HaEzer rules similarly for divorce and kiddushin. Rama's rule that two people who arrive together divide is therefore only a doubt, since exact simultaneity is impossible and we do not know who arrived first. His additional case, where both were already standing and a found object later fell within their four cubits, is not entirely precise. If one had arrived first, that person's four cubits were already acquired before the object fell. If the uncertainty is which one arrived first, this is the same doubt as the first case. Whether the object was already there or fell afterward does not change the essential issue, which is priority in the four cubits.
