(Seif 3, without his knowledge.) It appears to me that the same applies to a person’s four cubits. Proof is from Nimukei Yosef in the first chapter of Bava Metzia: “The Sages enacted acquisition by four cubits only where the person stood there and the object was placed within his four cubits. But if the owner preceded him there, the four cubits do not acquire for the recipient, because the place occupied by the object is acquired to its owner.” Thus the four cubits belong to the owner even though the owner does not intend to acquire. Indeed, his intent is to transfer to the buyer. The basic reason for the enactment of four cubits, to prevent quarrels, also proves this. Otherwise people would still quarrel, each saying, “You did not intend for your four cubits to acquire it.” This appears correct.
