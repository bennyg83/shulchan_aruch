He acquires it, and it is no worse, etc. Mishneh LaMelech, Hilchot Zechiyah 2:9, explains why this does not conflict with the rule cited by the Tur in siman 275 in the name of Ramah, that if bricks belonged to a convert who died without heirs, one who lifted them did not acquire them if he intended to acquire only later through building. Here, when the object enters his four cubits, he intends to acquire it at that moment. Although the four cubits are legally ineffective in that circumstance, his present intent to acquire combines with the act. There, by contrast, when he lifted the bricks he did not intend to acquire them until afterward through the building, and when he later intended to acquire there was no act of lifting. Rabbi Akiva Eiger, Responsa 37, gives the same answer and derives a new rule: if a person runs toward a found object and says that he intends to acquire it specifically by falling upon it, and another person takes it before he falls, the second acquires it. Since the first intended to acquire only through the later fall, he had no intent to acquire while it was within his four cubits, so those cubits did not acquire for him. See also above, siman 200:8:7. Responsa Rabbi Moshe Rothenburg, Orach Chaim 10, offers another resolution on behalf of the majority of authorities who do not accept this distinction, addressing the rule in siman 250 concerning a deathly ill person's instruction accompanied by a kinyan, which fails because he demonstrated that he wished to acquire only through the kinyan, and a kinyan cannot take effect after death.
