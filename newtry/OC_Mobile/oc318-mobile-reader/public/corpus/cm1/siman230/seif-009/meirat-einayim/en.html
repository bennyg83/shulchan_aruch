Likewise, one who receives barrels of wine, etc. This is Rambam's language in chapter 17. It means that just as where one sells wine to another to resell little by little the risk may remain upon the seller, so too where one receives barrels from another to sell in a specified place, since the giver knew they would remain for some time and the price fell in the interim, the loss is the giver's. Yet the cases are not entirely identical. In the first clause the Mechaber speaks even of an outright sale. Here he says 'one who receives from another.' The Tur cites Rabbeinu Yonah and writes that specifically in such a receiving arrangement the risks of price decline and souring remain upon the owner. If he bought the wine outright, even if he said that he was buying it in order to transport it to a place where wine was expensive, a later decline in price is the purchaser's risk and he cannot retract. Rabbeinu Chananel disagrees and holds that even in an outright purchase he may retract in such a case. Ramah also agrees but distinguishes that this applies specifically when the purchaser has not yet paid and stated that he would not pay until he brought the wine to the destination. See the Tur and Maggid Mishneh for the variant readings and opinions.
