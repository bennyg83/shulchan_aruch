Although when the smell is vinegar but the taste is beer we rule that it is still beer, a person does not pay the price of proper beer for beer that has already begun the process of spoilage that will ultimately ruin it. Even if he tasted it and both smell and taste seemed like beer, he clearly did not taste it carefully enough. If it soured after three days, the loss is the buyer's responsibility, and even if he has not yet paid he must pay, since he tasted it and it was then good. If he did not taste it, however, the burden of proof rests on the claimant, because the matter is doubtful. So writes the Tur. This differs from the case of a perforation found in the reticulum of an animal more than three days after purchase, where the Tur and Mechaber rule at the end of siman 224 and in siman 232 that although it is uncertain whether the perforation arose in the seller's or buyer's possession, the buyer bears the loss and must pay even if he has not yet paid. Here it is different, because all beer is liable to sour when kept, so we cannot preserve it on the basis of a prior presumption. An animal, by contrast, has a presumption of health. Ibid.
