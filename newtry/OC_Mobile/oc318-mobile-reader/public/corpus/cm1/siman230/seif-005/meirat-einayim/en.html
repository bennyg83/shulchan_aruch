And the purchaser placed it into his own barrels, etc. Even if the purchaser's barrels are new and good, and even if he never carried the barrel on his own shoulder, we still attribute the spoilage to the transfer from the seller's vessel to the purchaser's vessel. This is the meaning of the Gemara's expression that the wine 'jumps upon the owner's shoulder.' It does not mean specifically that he carried it. Rather, once he dealt with it through shoulders, meaning through workers who assisted in pouring from one barrel to another, the change of vessel may have caused the spoilage. Ran explains it this way, and the same must be understood according to Rif, Rambam, Rosh, and Tur. See the Perishah.
