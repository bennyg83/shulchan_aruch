Seif 2. For preserves and the like, that all of it be sound. Concerning a kind of salt known as talfin, softness that makes it unable to keep may constitute a defect. Since a retail seller must expect the purchaser to store the goods for some time, this may be comparable to one who says that he needs produce for preserving. The matter must be evaluated according to commercial practice. If the purchaser is uncertain whether the defect existed before the sale and suggests that the problem may have arisen because the product had not yet completed its manufacturing process, no special presumption may resolve the issue. The burden remains on the claimant, and the seller can say, 'I do not know whether I ever became obligated.' Even if the uncertainty is more like 'I do not know whether I repaid,' in light of the Taz's discussion of a counterfeit coin one may still say that the seller had no reason to know the item's exact condition while it was in his possession. I encountered such a case with salt that had been moist and soft. It was uncertain whether it softened only after the purchaser acquired it, while being transported through snow and frost, or whether it had already been soft before final drying by the fire. The allocation of the burden of proof depends on these circumstances.
