An unpaid custodian may stipulate to be exempt from an oath. Hagahot Ashri at the end of the seventh chapter of Bava Metzia cites Rabbi Baruch of Regensburg, who infers that because the Mishna says only that an unpaid custodian may stipulate to be exempt from an oath, whereas regarding a paid custodian it speaks of exemption from payment, an unpaid custodian cannot exempt himself from liability for negligence even by stipulation. Tiferet Shmuel rejects the proof, noting that Rosh elsewhere adopts the scriptural formulation when stating that an unpaid custodian does not swear regarding documents. He suggests that this is why the later poskim omitted the Hagahot Ashri's statement, and Tosafot on 93a likewise implies otherwise. Responsa Zichron Yosef, siman 1, records a claim that the silence of the poskim supports Hagahot Ashri and that the defendant therefore cannot assert that the law follows Rabbi Yonatan, cited in Shita Mekubetzet, who permits a complete exemption by stipulation. Zichron Yosef rejects that argument. The Mordechai cited in Darchei Moshe, siman 291, seif katan 10, implies that one may stipulate even for exemption from negligence without a kinyan. Moreover, Rabbi Baruch may have followed the Rambam and those who hold that although an unpaid custodian does not swear regarding land and slaves, he remains liable if they are lost through his negligence because negligence is akin to direct damage. According to our accepted ruling, following Rif and Rosh, that even negligence does not create liability for those excluded categories because negligence is not direct damage, there is no reason to doubt that an unpaid custodian may likewise stipulate to be exempt from negligence.
