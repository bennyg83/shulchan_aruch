I said only, "Place it before yourself." Shaar Mishpat writes that at first glance the case appears to be where the defendant admits that he was negligent but says he never accepted custodial responsibility. If he also claims that he guarded properly, perhaps no heset oath should be required, because oaths are administered on denial of money, not merely on denial of an oath obligation, as stated in siman 93, seif 9. He then concludes that even if the defendant also says that he guarded properly, he must take a heset oath that he said only, "Place it before yourself." According to Rav Hai Gaon, cited by the Tur in siman 87 and accepted by Shach there, where a person was obligated in a Torah oath and later says, "I already swore," we do not impose one oath upon another with respect to heset, but where the original oath was a Torah oath he must take heset. The reason is that one who refuses a Torah oath must pay and his property may be seized; therefore denial that such an oath obligation exists is itself a denial of money. The same applies here. By saying, "I only said, place it before yourself," he denies the very Torah oath obligation that would otherwise rest upon him, and he must at least take a heset oath. See there.
