But if he claims, etc. Meaning, even though we do not exempt from a Torah oath merely because he could have claimed otherwise, if he actually claims that nothing was ever deposited with him or that he returned it, he is exempt from the Torah oath of custodians and swears only hesset. This applies where the depositor has no written document proving the deposit. If there is such a document, he is not exempt by saying, "I returned it," except through a Torah oath based on a migo that he could have said the deposit was lost by unavoidable accident, a claim that would have exempted him even against the document. We do not say with a deposit, "If you truly returned it, why is the document still in the depositor's hands?" because the custodian may have relied upon the alternative defense of unavoidable accident. This differs from a loan document, where the borrower bears liability even for unavoidable accident and cannot use such a defense.
