But if he claims, etc. The Sema writes that if the owner holds a document, the custodian is exempt only by a stringent oath with an article in hand, through a migo that he could have claimed unavoidable accident. The Tur and Ir Shushan call the oath on a claim of return a Torah oath, and the Bach understands them literally, distinguishing Tosafot, who call it rabbinic. In my opinion, they mean an oath patterned after Torah law, not an actual Torah oath. Torah law imposes the custodial oath specifically on a claim of unavoidable accident, because otherwise every custodian could make that claim and the owner could not expose the falsehood. A claim of return, however, is directly contradicted by the owner and does not carry a Torah oath. The Rif's expression that both are stringent oaths taken with an article means only that the rabbinic oath on return is administered in the same form as the Torah oath on unavoidable accident. Thus the Rif, Tur, and Tosafot do not disagree. Tosafot compare it to the rabbinic oaths of a woman who impairs her ketubah and one contradicted by a single witness, both taken with an article. Rabbeinu Shimshon of Kinon and the Baal HaMaor state explicitly that the oath on unavoidable accident is the Torah custodial oath, whereas the oath on return is rabbinic but patterned after Torah law. Therefore the rule that one who is obligated to swear and cannot do so must pay does not apply to the latter. The Rambam and Mechaber likewise distinguish a Torah oath on unavoidable accident from an oath patterned after Torah law on return, even though they hold that a custodial oath does not require partial admission. The practical differences include collection from property, reversal of the oath, and the law of a person disqualified from swearing. This is clear.
