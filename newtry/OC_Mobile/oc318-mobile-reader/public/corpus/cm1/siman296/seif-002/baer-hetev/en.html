By an oath. At first glance one might think this means a Torah oath, and the Bach expressly writes so. In my humble opinion, however, this is not correct. Rather, it is an oath modeled on a Torah oath, requiring the holding of a sacred object. We find an actual Torah oath only where the custodian claims an unavoidable accident, because the Torah imposed an oath lest every custodian simply claim an accident where the owner cannot know the truth. Where he claims, "I returned it," and the owner directly contradicts him, there is no actual Torah oath. There are practical differences between an actual Torah oath and an oath modeled upon it regarding descent into his property, reversal of the oath, and a person suspected of false oaths, as explained in siman 87, seifim 3, 10, and 13, as well as other differences. See also the Rosh at the beginning of Bava Metzia, who writes that one supporting witness exempts him from the oath, which conflicts with the gloss above in siman 87, seif 6; see what I wrote there.
