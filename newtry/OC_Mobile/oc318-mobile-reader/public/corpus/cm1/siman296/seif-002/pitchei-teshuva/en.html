Responsa Radbaz I, siman 6, writes that one who borrowed silver utensils under a written instrument is not believed to say, "I returned them," by a migo that he could have claimed that they broke through normal use, because that migo is defective. Had he made the latter claim, he would be told to produce the broken pieces. Radbaz formulates the rule: for an item concerning which the borrower could have claimed a complete loss through normal use, he is believed to say that he returned it by virtue of that migo. For an item concerning which he could not make such a complete claim, the written instrument defeats him and he must pay. Shaar HaMishpat notes that on Radbaz's view the Rambam and Mechaber would have to be speaking where the carcass or broken remains are worthless, for otherwise there is no migo. He disagrees. Based on Nimukei Yosef and Rama below, siman 344, seif 3, if the borrower told the lender, "Take the broken remains," and they were later lost by unavoidable accident, this is treated as though the loss occurred in the lender's domain and the borrower is exempt. Thus the borrower still has a complete migo: he could have said that the item broke through normal use, that he told the lender to take the remains, and that the remains were thereafter lost by unavoidable accident.
