Dalet) This is the language of the Tur, seif 2. Rambam likewise rules there, halachah 12. The Maggid Mishneh writes that this is straightforward: the Torah's special oath of custodians does not apply to a custodian who entirely denies receiving the property, but to one who admits custody and claims an unavoidable accident. Where the depositor knows the facts just as well as the custodian, there is no Torah oath unless the defendant admits part of the claim, as is clear from the Mishnayot in Shevuat HaDayanim. Nevertheless, there is certainly a rabbinic heset oath.
Heh) The Maggid Mishneh writes that even where there are witnesses to the original deposit, the custodian is believed to say, "I returned it," with a heset oath, as explained at the beginning of HaNishba'in 45b.
Vav) Rav Amram's question and Rav Chisda's resolution in the fourth chapter of Bava Batra 70a, with the reason explained there on 70b.
