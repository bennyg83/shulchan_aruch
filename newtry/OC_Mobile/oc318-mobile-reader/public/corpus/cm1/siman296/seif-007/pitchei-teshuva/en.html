I said only, "Place it before yourself." Shaar HaMishpat first suggests that the case is one where the defendant admits that he was negligent but says that he never accepted custodianship. If he also claims that he guarded properly, there would seemingly be no need for a hesset oath, because oaths are imposed on denial of monetary liability, not merely denial of an oath obligation, as stated in siman 93, seif 9. He then prefers the view that even if the defendant also says that he guarded properly, he must take a hesset oath that he said only, "Place it before yourself." According to Rav Hai Gaon, cited by Tur in siman 87 and accepted by Shach there, one who was obligated in a biblical oath and later says, "I swore," must take a hesset oath to support that assertion. The reason is that refusal to take the original biblical oath would result in monetary payment, so denial that the oath remains due is effectively a denial of money. The same reasoning applies here. By saying, "I never accepted custody; I only said place it before yourself," he denies an obligation that would otherwise require a biblical oath and, upon refusal, monetary payment. He therefore must at least take a hesset oath.
