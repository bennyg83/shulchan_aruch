From the oath. Meaning, where the deposit was made without witnesses. Although the custodian could have denied the entire deposit and said, "You never deposited with me," thereby avoiding a custodian's oath, we do not apply a migo to exempt him from an oath. The Sages were lenient to employ migo when money would otherwise be extracted, because it is difficult to remove money from one who possesses it, but where he can be exempt only through an oath they did not wish to exempt him merely by migo. The alternative opinion cited by the Rama holds that migo does exempt from an oath. The Tur likewise cites the Rosh in siman 93, seif 7, that there is no distinction between monetary liability and an oath, since an oath may lead to monetary liability if he refuses to swear. Nevertheless, here even that opinion agrees that he is not exempt by the migo that he could have said, "You never deposited with me," because it is an audacious migo. It is surprising that the Ir Shushan strained to provide another reason. Sema. The Shach agrees with the Ir Shushan and writes that his words are correct and compelled. See there, and Responsa Mabit, vol. 2, siman 292.
