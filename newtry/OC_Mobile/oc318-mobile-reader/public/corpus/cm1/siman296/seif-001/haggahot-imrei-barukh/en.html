(Shach, seif katan 3) And we do not say that one who is obligated to swear but cannot do so must pay. Note: These words are also from Baal HaMaor. See Shach above, siman 51, seif katan 23. Know that the Shach's concluding statement regarding one who is suspected appears at first glance to mean that where the guardian is suspected and therefore cannot swear concerning a claim of return, he is exempt without an oath. This requires study, for then there is no migo where, had he claimed unavoidable accident and his fellow denied it, the claimant would swear and collect. See above, siman 92. The source, however, is Baal HaMaor according to the view that when a guardian dies, if the heirs would rely on a claim of unavoidable accident the law would treat them as one obligated to swear but unable and require payment. Therefore we do not plead unavoidable accident for the heirs, but rather return. With return there is no Torah oath. To sustain the migo, the living guardian himself would have had to swear when claiming return, because without an oath there would be no migo. Yet when he dies the oath on the return claim falls away, and we do not make the heirs pay under the rule of one obligated to swear but unable, since that oath was never a Torah oath on their father. By Torah law the only oath is on unavoidable accident, not on return; the return oath is imposed solely to sustain the migo.
