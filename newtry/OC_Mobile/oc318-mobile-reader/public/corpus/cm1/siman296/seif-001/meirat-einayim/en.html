We do not use a migo to exempt him from an oath. For example, where the deposit was made without witnesses, one might argue that if he wished he could deny the deposit entirely and thereby avoid the custodian's oath, so perhaps his present claim should also exempt him. According to this view we do not use such a migo to remove a Torah oath. The Sages were lenient with a migo primarily in extracting money, because it is difficult to remove money from its possessor. Where the defendant remains exempt from payment by taking an oath, they did not use migo to remove the oath itself. The dissenters cited by Rama are the Rosh and those with him, who hold that migo can exempt even from an oath. The Tur cites the Rosh in siman 93:7: there is no distinction between money and an oath, because an oath can lead to money if he refuses to swear. Nevertheless, here even the Rosh agrees that the particular migo of "you never deposited anything with me" is not effective, because it is an audacious claim. The depositor stands opposite him and knows that he deposited it and did not receive it back. By contrast, a claim that it was stolen or died concerns facts the depositor does not know and may indeed be true. It is surprising that the Ir Shushan struggled to supply another reason for the Rambam's view when the reason is straightforward.
Therefore we do not say it to him. This applies specifically to a Torah oath of custodians. In siman 93:7, concerning partners, sharecroppers, and guardians who swear on a doubtful claim because of a rabbinic enactment based on concern that they may rationalize taking more for their labor, the Rosh holds that migo can exempt from the oath. There, even though the migo may also involve the audacious claim that no partnership ever existed, since the entire oath is only rabbinic the Sages were lenient. It is surprising that Rama did not cite the Rosh's view there, where there is an actual practical dispute. The Mechaber rules like the Rambam that migo does not exempt. Here the parties largely agree in practical result and differ more in reasoning.
