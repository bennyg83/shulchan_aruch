The custodian swears hesset, etc. This also applies to the depositor's claim, "I deposited one hundred with you and these are only fifty." The custodian need swear only hesset. He says, "These are what you deposited and no more," and regarding what he admits he is effectively saying, "Take what is yours." The admitted amount is presently intact and ready to be returned, which has the law of "here it is." He denies the rest entirely. The later case, where the owner says, "I deposited one hundred se'ah," and the custodian says, "You deposited only fifty," and a Torah oath is imposed because he admitted part, concerns a case where the fifty admitted are not presently intact for immediate return or he is not offering to return them. The difference is that the Torah oath there is based upon partial admission, not upon the law of custodians. A partial admission is avoided by saying "here it is," while the custodian's oath would otherwise apply regardless. Some authorities hold that any intact deposit has the law of "here it is" automatically because the deposit remains legally in the owner's domain. According to them the custodian need not expressly say, "What you left is what you are taking." The Gemara's case where one says the grain reached the beam and the other says only the window, and a Torah oath is required, is understood by them as a case where the produce deteriorated and therefore is no longer regarded as standing in the depositor's domain. See siman 88.
One hundred of barley, he swears hesset, etc. It appears that he swears hesset that the depositor did not deposit wheat, and then he is exempt even from the barley he admitted. The rule has already been explained in siman 88 and again in siman 400: if one claims wheat and the defendant admits barley, the defendant is exempt even from the barley. According to Rama's explanation in siman 88:12, even if the defendant knows that he owes barley, he is exempt because the claimant is considered to have waived it by claiming only wheat. On that understanding, the oath here can be formulated regarding the barley. Even if he swears that barley was deposited, he may still be exempt from returning it because the claimant effectively waived the barley by making only the wheat claim.
