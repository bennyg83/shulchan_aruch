When a bailee returns property and the depositor says, 'This is not my deposit; mine was different,' or, 'Mine was whole and you broke it,' 'Mine was new and you used it,' or, 'I deposited one hundred se'ah and these are only fifty,' while the bailee says, 'This is exactly what you deposited and what you gave is what you receive,' the bailee takes a rabbinic oath of denial. The Torah's bailee oath applies only when he admits the item described by the depositor but claims it was stolen, lost, or captured. When they dispute the identity, condition, or quantity of the deposited property, ordinary oath rules apply. If he admits part, such as admitting fifty of one hundred se'ah, he takes a Torah oath because of partial admission, not because he is a bailee. If the depositor claims wheat and he admits only barley, he takes a rabbinic oath like any defendant facing such a claim.
