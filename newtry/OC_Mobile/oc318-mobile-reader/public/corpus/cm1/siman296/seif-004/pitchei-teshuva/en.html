In a place where witnesses are commonly available, he is not believed to say, "I returned it." Shaar Mishpat writes that if the bailee dies and collection is sought from his heirs, the matter depends on the dispute in siman 108, seif 4, whether we advance for heirs the claim that an unavoidable accident occurred. According to those who do not advance that claim because such accidents are uncommon, all the more so here we do not advance it. According to those, including Shach, who do advance the claim, we do so here as well even in a place where witnesses are normally available. The father himself would not have been believed to claim an accident without proof because the Torah says that where there is no witness the oath of Hashem shall be between them, implying that where witnesses exist he must produce them. If the event occurred as he says, he knows who was present and could bring proof. The heirs, however, may not know who was present. We therefore may plead for them that an accident occurred even though their father himself could not have prevailed on that bare claim. Shaar Mishpat concludes that this reasoning is correct.
