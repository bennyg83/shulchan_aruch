In a place where witnesses are available. This was already explained in siman 294 and elsewhere. We derive it from the verse, "There is no witness; the oath of God shall be between them." This implies that where there are witnesses he is not believed through an oath and must bring witnesses. Rambam's wording, cited by the Tur, is somewhat different: "for example, in a place where witnesses are available, where the circumstances are such that it is impossible for the accident to have occurred without the matter becoming known," implying that ordinarily he need not bring proof. The Mechaber did not copy that wording because he holds that ordinarily he must bring proof, as implied by Ramah's wording cited by the Tur in siman 294, seif 2, and by the Mechaber there in seif 3. I wrote there that the Ramah there does not disagree with Rambam cited here. It is surprising that the Ir Shushan left the Mechaber's corrected wording and copied Rambam's wording, which is open to misunderstanding.
But initially we tell him, etc. The reason is that we do not advance any claim for the defendant until he himself raises it, after we first tell him, "Go and pay him." A similar rule is written by the Tur and Mechaber above, siman 82, concerning a lender who produces an unvalidated document against a borrower. See there.
