In a place where witnesses are ordinarily available, he is not believed to say, "I returned it." Shaar HaMishpat writes that if the custodian dies and the depositor seeks payment from the heirs, the law depends on the dispute in siman 108, seif 4. According to the authorities who do not plead unavoidable accident on behalf of heirs because such an occurrence is uncommon, all the more so would we not plead it for them here. According to the authorities who do plead unavoidable accident for heirs, as Shach rules there, we do so here as well even in a place where witnesses are ordinarily available. Their father himself could not have claimed unavoidable accident without proof, because the verse indicates that the custodian's oath applies where there is no observer. Where observers are ordinarily present, his failure to bring evidence undermines his claim, since he knows who was present when the accident occurred. The heirs, however, may not know who was present and therefore cannot be expected to produce the evidence. We may therefore plead unavoidable accident for them even though their father himself would not have been believed on that claim. Shaar HaMishpat concludes that this reasoning is sound.
