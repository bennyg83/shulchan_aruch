Sema, seif katan 11, writes that the custodian can be believed where the witnesses were present only when he initially requested permission to deposit, but they were no longer present when the object was actually handed over. If witnesses were present at the actual delivery, the custodian cannot ordinarily later deny accepting custody. Shach's statement that where the deposit was made "without qualification" he can be believed concerns a case where the parties discussed the deposit before the witnesses arrived, and the witnesses later saw only the physical delivery without hearing the terms. Even where witnesses saw the object in his hands and later saw it damaged, the custodian can say, "I never became a custodian at all; he merely said, 'Place it before you.'" He does not need a migo for that. We do not create custodial status merely from possession. There are several textual errors in Shach's discussion. The sense should be: even though there are no witnesses that he returned it; and unless he became obligated to swear; and where there is no proof that it was lost and perhaps it remains in his possession; and even if the truth is that there were conditions between them.
