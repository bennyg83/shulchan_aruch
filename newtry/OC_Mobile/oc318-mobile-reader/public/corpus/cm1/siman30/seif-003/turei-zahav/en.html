There: “A black maneh” Tosafot explain that payment of the smaller amount applies only where people commonly err, such as between one hundred and two hundred. Wine and oil barrels are likewise treated as examination details, and even explicit contradiction does not disqualify the witnesses. Why then does Tur require the claimant to claim both versions and not contradict either witness? The answer is that if the claimant identifies which witness erred, that witness’s testimony cannot support collection, even though he is not generally disqualified. Where the claimant merely says, “I lent one maneh,” without knowing whether it was black or white, he has not contradicted either. This parallels possession testimony where one witness says wheat and the other barley, and the possessor need not specify both.
