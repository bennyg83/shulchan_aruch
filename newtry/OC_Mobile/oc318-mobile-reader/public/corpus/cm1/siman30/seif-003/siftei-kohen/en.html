And he takes a Torah oath concerning one hundred, etc. Why does he not take an oath concerning the entire amount by force of Rabbi Chiyya’s rule, as below, siman 75, seif 4? One must say that here, because the evidentiary obligation arises only through combining separate witnesses, Rabbi Chiyya’s rule does not apply. This also appears from the Sema’s words here and below, seif katan 19. Examine this carefully. The Bach answers that here several groups of witnesses are combined, whereas the responsum of the Rosh involved only the combination of a single group of two witnesses. His words are astonishing. Even if the second group is disregarded, the first combined testimony should itself create liability. The presence of further witnesses cannot weaken the first group. Regarding the fundamental law, one who studies the Baal HaTerumot will see that the Ramban’s responsum there does not mention an oath concerning one hundred. It is possible that with respect to the oath the Ramban holds that he indeed swears concerning the entire balance under Rabbi Chiyya’s rule, and that the question’s reference to the oath imposed by one witness was merely incidental. The practical law therefore requires study, for we find no source distinguishing ordinary testimony from testimony created by combining witnesses. The Tur’s apparent position, that once one witness has been joined to another to establish monetary liability he no longer imposes an oath concerning the remaining sum, is likewise not stated in the Baal HaTerumot and requires study. A practical difference exists even if one concedes that Rabbi Chiyya ordinarily obligates him without reliance on a single witness. Suppose the claimant does not remember with certainty how much he lent, but claims with certainty on the basis of the witnesses, saying, “I forgot how much I lent him; I claim whatever amount the witnesses establish,” as discussed below, siman 75, seif 23, concerning an uncertain claim based on one witness. Rabbi Chiyya’s rule cannot impose an oath in such a case because the claimant lacks a personally certain claim. Yet a single witness can impose an oath. Thus, if one witness testifies that he lent him two hundred and another that he lent him three hundred, while the claimant forgot the amount, the defendant pays two hundred and swears concerning the remaining one hundred by force of the single witness. According to the Tur, however, he would not need to swear in such a case, because the witness was already joined to establish the monetary award. Consider all of this carefully.
