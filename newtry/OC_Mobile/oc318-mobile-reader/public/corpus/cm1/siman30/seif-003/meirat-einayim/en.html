The correct reading is. 'And he brings five witnesses,' etc.
If each testified to a separate item of testimony, such as, etc. This implies that if they testified without specifying any time, we treat them as contradicting one another and assume their intention concerned one time. This is why the Mechaber later writes, 'Likewise, if they testified without specification,' as I explained above in Sema seif katan 11. This is obvious, and I write it only to exclude Ir Shushan, who explains 'without specification' merely to mean that neither expressly said 'No, rather,' while both still testified about one day. That is not correct.
He pays seven hundred and swears, etc. The Mechaber abbreviated. The Tur explains the calculation: monetary testimony can combine even when each witness testifies explicitly to a separate loan, provided the claimant demands all the loans and there is no contradiction. We therefore combine the witness for five hundred with the witness for four hundred for four hundred; the remaining one hundred from the five-hundred testimony combines with one hundred of the three-hundred testimony, making five hundred; then the remaining two hundred of the three-hundred testimony combines with two hundred of the two-hundred testimony, making a total of seven hundred. The witness for one hundred has no partner and therefore obligates a Torah oath on that one hundred. Once the defendant swears concerning that hundred, the other disputed amounts may be included through gilgul. A witness whose testimony has already been used to create a monetary obligation by combination does not also independently create an additional oath.
On one day, in contradiction. Rama says 'in contradiction' here, unlike the following case of unspecified testimony, to teach that even if one expressly says to the other, 'No, rather,' creating an obvious contradiction, the defendant still pays the amount established by the noncontradictory overlap according to the rule explained. Conversely, where they gave unspecified testimony without expressly contradicting each other, he still does not pay more than the amount supported by the common testimony.
He pays only two hundred. There are two witnesses whose testimony establishes that the loan was no more than two hundred: the witness for one hundred and the witness for two hundred agree, at a minimum, that there was not more than the smaller common amount in the legally combinable testimony. Even though the remaining witnesses speak of larger sums, money cannot be extracted beyond what the valid overlapping testimony establishes, as the Tur explains.
