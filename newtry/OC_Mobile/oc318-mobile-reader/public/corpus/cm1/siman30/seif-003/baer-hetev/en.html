And he shall swear. The Tur explains the reason. In monetary cases the witnesses need not have seen the same event together. Even if each explicitly testifies to a separate loan, if the claimant demands both loans their testimony combines. Here too, where each witness testifies to a separate loan and the claimant demands the entire amount, all the witnesses combine. It is as though there were two witnesses establishing three hundred, with one additional witness remaining to obligate him in an oath concerning the balance. If no witness remained beyond those needed for the monetary obligation, however, no oath would be imposed, because a witness whose testimony contributes to a monetary obligation through combination cannot additionally generate an oath. Sema.
In contradiction. The Sema explains that the Mechaber uses the term "contradiction" here, but not in the following case where the witnesses testified without specification, to teach a novelty. Even if the second witness explicitly says, "No, that is not what happened," creating an overt contradiction, the defendant nevertheless pays two hundred. The following case also teaches a novelty: even though the witnesses did not explicitly testify about one day and did not say "No" to one another, he still pays no more than two hundred. The Sema then notes difficulty. His statement that even if one witness says "No" the defendant still pays two hundred necessarily treats this as a contradiction only in collateral details. Otherwise all the testimony would be void and no oath would be required, as explained above. Yet in seif 2 the Mechaber states that if one witness says Nisan and the other says "No," the testimony is void because the contradiction concerns formal interrogation. Perhaps the distinction is that there both witnesses testify to the same sum but contradict one another concerning the time, so the testimony is void. Here, however, each testifies to a different sum, and therefore it is possible that both accounts are true.
