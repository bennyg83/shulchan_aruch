But he cannot swear, because by denying everything he has been established as a false denier regarding the deposit. Therefore he must pay the full claim. Rabbi Chiya's rule that one who denies everything and witnesses establish part of the claim must swear on the remainder applies specifically to a loan. Money lent for expenditure may have been denied temporarily until funds become available, and therefore he is not necessarily established as a false denier. Although one who denies a deposit also does not become suspect unless witnesses show the deposit was in his possession at the time, because otherwise he can claim he denied it only because he did not know where it was, that logic does not apply here. The deposited item was money given to him for investment, not a specific object that could have been misplaced. Even though he had permission to expend the money for the depositor's benefit, he should have said he had used it in the course of the investment rather than deny receiving it. This differs from a loan, where admitting the debt would subject him to immediate collection although he had already spent the funds for his own purposes.
