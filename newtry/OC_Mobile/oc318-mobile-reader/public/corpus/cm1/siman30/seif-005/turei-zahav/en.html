Seif 5: “He was established as a denier,” etc. The Sema explains that testimony combines for both payment and oath even when concerning different times because it can refer to one deposit. But if so, where one witness says one hundred and the other two hundred, their testimony should likewise create an oath after payment of the smaller amount, which the authorities do not say. The distinction is that testimony cannot be divided, half for payment and half for an oath. Here the testimony itself obligates payment of fifty, and that very payment creates a Torah oath under the rule that an admission by one’s own mouth is not stronger than witnesses. Ramban’s first case, however, involves separate testimony that cannot impose a fine and therefore cannot generate the oath derived by an a fortiori argument from witnesses that impose fines. In the Rosh’s responsum both witnesses heard one admission at the same time, one hearing fifty and the other one hundred fifty, so this is not separate testimony. If they testified to different days, only fifty would be paid without a Torah oath. If they expressly refer to the same event, one saying one hundred and the other two hundred, he pays one hundred and swears, as in the Rosh’s deposit case.
