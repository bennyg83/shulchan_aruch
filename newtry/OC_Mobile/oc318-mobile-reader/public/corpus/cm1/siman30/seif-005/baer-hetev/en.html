A proven denier. The Rosh writes that according to both witnesses the defendant holds fifty gold coins belonging to the claimant and must swear concerning the remainder. The rule is that his own partial admission cannot have greater force than the testimony of witnesses. He cannot swear, however, because he has been established as a liar by having denied everything. Therefore he pays the entire claim. Although a custodian who denies holding a deposit is not rendered suspect until witnesses testify that they saw it in his possession at the time of the denial, because otherwise he can excuse himself by saying he did not know where he had put it, this case is different. The deposit consisted of money, and although he was permitted to spend and invest it for the depositor's benefit, he should have made that claim rather than deny the deposit entirely. Thus the Sema.
And he pays. Here we cannot object that the same witnesses are being used through combination both to impose monetary liability and an oath, as discussed in seif 3. There each witness testifies to a distinct loan, one in Nisan and one in Iyar. Here, by contrast, although they heard his admissions at different times, one concerning fifty and the other one hundred fifty, it is possible that both statements concerned the same deposit. Their testimony therefore is not considered testimony that creates liability only through combination. Thus the Sema. The Shach writes that in truth we find no basis anywhere for distinguishing between ordinary testimony and testimony that works through combination. He therefore leaves the practical law requiring study. A practical difference arises where the claimant does not state with certainty how much he lent, but says, "I claim whatever amount the witnesses testify to," as discussed in siman 75, seif 23. In such a case an oath cannot be imposed through Rabbi Chiya's rule, but one witness can impose an oath even if his testimony has already combined with another witness to establish money. According to the Tur, however, no oath would be required in such a case. Examine carefully. It appears to me that the difficulty does not begin. In the earlier case of loans, if the defendant is made to swear because he contradicts the witness, that witness can no longer be combined to impose payment. Here, however, the defendant's monetary liability results from his status as an established denier, and he is not swearing to contradict the witness. The Taz answers similarly; see there. The Shach writes that this passage does not discuss the claimant's oath and relies on what was written in siman 75, where the claimant must swear. See there.
