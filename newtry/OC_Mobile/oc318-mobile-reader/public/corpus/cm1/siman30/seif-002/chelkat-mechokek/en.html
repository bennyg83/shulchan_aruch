Or into her courtyard or her field. Tur writes that if it enters a courtyard guarded for her, she is betrothed even if she is not standing beside it. Beit Yosef asks that if kiddushin are compared to a gift, Rosh has already ruled regarding gifts like Rav Pappa that the donor's guarding is equivalent to the recipient's guarding, so the courtyard need not be guarded specifically for her, since one may acquire a benefit for a person in his absence. Bach answers that kiddushin are weaker than gifts. I do not understand this, for Rosh rules similarly even regarding a sale, as brought in Choshen Mishpat 200. Mechaber here uses Rambam's language, and Rambam relies on Hilchot Zechiyah, where he requires either a guarded courtyard or that the recipient stand beside the field. Since Mechaber in Choshen Mishpat 200 rules generally that movable property must be in the buyer's domain guarded for him, the same should apply here: definite kiddushin should not occur unless the property is guarded for her. Rama, who in Choshen Mishpat rules like Rosh that guarding by the seller or donor is enough, may nevertheless concede here, because of the stringency of kiddushin, that without guarding for her there are only doubtful kiddushin.
