Although witnesses in monetary cases do not require formal examination and interrogation, if they contradict one another regarding matters that are part of the required examination or interrogation, their testimony is void. If they contradict one another regarding secondary checks, their testimony remains valid. For example, if one says that the loan was made in Nisan and the other says that it was made in Iyar, or one says it occurred in Yerushalayim and the other says it occurred in Lod, their testimony is void. Likewise, if one says that he lent him a barrel of wine and the other says it was a barrel of oil, their testimony is void, because they contradicted one another regarding an essential point. But if one says the loan was one hundred darkened coins, meaning coins blackened by age, and the other says it was one hundred bright coins, and the claimant demands both alternatives, or one says they were on the upper story when the loan was made and the other says they were on the lower story, their testimony remains valid, provided the locations are close enough that one could see from one to the other. Even if one says he lent him one hundred and the other says two hundred, the borrower must pay one hundred, because one hundred is included within two hundred. Likewise, if one says he has in his possession the value of a barrel of wine and the other says the value of a barrel of oil, and the claimant demands both, he pays the lesser of the two values. Ran, responsa siman 40. The same applies in all similar cases. Whenever the witnesses' statements can reasonably be reconciled so that they do not contradict one another, we reconcile them, as explained in siman 29, seif 1.
