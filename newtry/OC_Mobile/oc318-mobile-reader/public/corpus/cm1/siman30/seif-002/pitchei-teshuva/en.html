In the glosses of the Gaon Maharam to Shulchan Aruch it is written: It appears to me that where one witness says one hundred and one says two hundred, if the claimant demands two hundred, that is sufficient and the witnesses combine according to all opinions. If he claims only one hundred, however, one can say that they do not combine according to any opinion, because the witness who says two hundred is contradicted by the claimant himself, since two hundred is not included within a claim of one hundred, as I wrote in my glosses to Beit Yosef. Nevertheless, regarding the case in the chapter Yesh Nochalin where a borrower claims that he repaid half of a written debt and the witnesses testify that he repaid all of it, the borrower swears and collects half, as stated below at the end of siman 82, seif 13. We see that the witnesses are believed with respect to the portion of their testimony that accords with his claim. One can answer that there, through the witnesses, the document is nullified and the matter becomes an oral claim, and therefore he must swear a Torah oath even though the witnesses support his statement that he repaid. See the Sema there and examine carefully. End of quote. See Netivot HaMishpat.
