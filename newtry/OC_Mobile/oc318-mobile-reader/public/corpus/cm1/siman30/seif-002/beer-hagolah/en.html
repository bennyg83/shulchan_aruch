d) The wording of the Rambam, chapter 3 of Hilkhot Edut, from Rav Yehudah's statement that contradictory testimony, etc., is valid in monetary law, there 30b. This implies that if the contradiction concerns the formal inquiries and examinations, the testimony is invalid, as explained explicitly in the Mishnah there 40a.
e) From the incident before Rabbi Ami, etc., there 31a, like the Nehardeans there and like Rabbi Shimon ben Elazar, who said that Beit Shammai and Beit Hillel did not disagree, etc., there. It is known that the halachah follows Beit Hillel.
f) The Beit Yosef adopts the Rambam's wording that the claimant need not claim both amounts. He writes likewise in the name of Rabbeinu Yerucham, because one maneh is attested by both witnesses. Although one says black and the other says white, people do not ordinarily pay close attention to such details, and this is considered a contradiction in secondary examinations, which remains valid. The same law applies where one says that he ate wheat and the other says he ate barley, for the testimony combines, Bava Batra 56b, as the Nimukei Yosef writes. The same applies to the value of a barrel of wine and similar matters in which people may err.
