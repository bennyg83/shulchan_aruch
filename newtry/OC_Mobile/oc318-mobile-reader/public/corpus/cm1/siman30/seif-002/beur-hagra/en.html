No, rather. This wording is specifically required. Otherwise, the witnesses are not disqualified. Sanhedrin 32b.
(Anthology) No, rather, etc. Only an express contradiction introduced by 'No, rather' disqualifies them. If they testified without that expression, they remain valid, because we rule like Rabbi Yehoshua ben Korcha, as explained below. The same applies where the context itself shows that they refer to distinct times, unlike a contradiction concerning the substance of the event, where an unqualified statement is treated as contradictory. This is proven from the Gemara's statement, 'The Nehardeans say,' etc., followed by, 'According to whom? Rabbi Yehoshua ben Korcha. Perhaps Rabbi Yehoshua ben Korcha said,' etc. How could the Gemara have thought that Rabbi Yehoshua ben Korcha validates witnesses who expressly contradict one another? If so, why did Rabbi Yehudah speak specifically of subsidiary examinations, when the same would apply to essential examinations? Rabbi Yehudah must therefore address an unqualified discrepancy. Regarding the substance of the event, silence is treated as contradiction, while in the case of Rabbi Yehoshua ben Korcha an unqualified difference is not contradiction. The Gemara's initial assumption is now understandable: although here the unqualified discrepancy is treated as contradiction, the witnesses nevertheless remain valid.

(Anthology) Likewise, if one said, 'a barrel,' etc. Although he did not say, 'No, rather,' witnesses ordinarily testify about the same time, as stated in seif 3. With respect to time, however, silence does not create contradiction, as the Nehardeans state there: even separate loans, etc. The Gemara says, 'Perhaps Rabbi Yehoshua ben Korcha said this where they do not contradict,' etc., implying that even an unqualified statement regarding time is not contradiction. Regarding a barrel of wine versus oil, however, even silence constitutes contradiction. The same is true of place, where silence is treated like silence regarding time only when the locations can coexist.

(Anthology) And he claims both from him. This is also implied by Rashi there, s.v. vetziref, explaining the rule like Rabbi Yehoshua ben Korcha and Rabbi Yehudah. Rabbi Yehoshua ben Korcha disagrees with the first tanna only where the claimant demands both sums, as stated in seif 7. If, however, Rabbi Yehudah's reason is that people confuse a dark maneh with a light maneh, this has no connection to Rabbi Yehoshua ben Korcha and even the first tanna may agree. The same applies to the discussion of an upper story, which is not merely an ordinary error but concerns the law of refutation, like an inability to identify the place in capital cases, as stated on 41b. The same applies to contradiction in monetary cases. This is the Tur and gloss's basis for saying, 'if the places,' etc. The Rivash, however, explains that people likewise fail to remember whether an event occurred on one upper story or another. All these cases therefore rest upon ordinary error, and there is no need that the claimant demand both sums, as stated in seif 7: 'we assess,' etc. This is the Shulchan Aruch's view, for it omits these two glosses.
