How? The Gemara states that formal examination and interrogation comprise seven matters: in which sabbatical cycle, in which year, in which month, on what date of the month, on which day of the week, at what hour, and in what place. Other questions are termed checking questions. Rambam writes that a matter upon which the substance of the testimony and the case itself depends is likewise included among the formal inquiries. Thus concludes the Sema.
Oil. Even if the second witness does not explicitly say, "No, rather," the testimony is treated as contradictory. When witnesses testify without qualification we assume they speak about the same time, so it is as though the second said, "That very loan which you say involved wine, I say involved oil." If one witness testifies to a barrel of wine in Nisan and the second to oil in Iyar, and the claimant sues for both, the defendant must pay the lesser amount if the second witness did not say "No, rather." If he did say so, the testimony is plainly void. This follows a fortiori from the case where one witness says one hundred in Nisan and the second says, "No, rather, in Iyar." Although both agree that the debt was one hundred, their testimony is invalid; all the more so where one says wine and the other oil, where the substance of their testimony differs. Sema.

Both. If the claimant did not sue for both, the first witness would be contradicted by the claimant's own mouth and there would no longer be grounds to collect through combining the testimonies. But when the claimant sues in accordance with both witnesses, although they contradict one another, since the contradiction concerns only checking details, money is extracted according to the lesser testimony, because one hundred is included within two hundred. Nimukei Yosef holds that where one says a black hundred and one a white hundred, the claimant need not sue for both. According to that view it appears simple that the same applies where one says one hundred and the other two hundred, unless they explicitly testify to different times. This is unlike the Levush, who distinguishes these cases. Thus concludes the Sema. See Bach; the matter requires study. Shach.
