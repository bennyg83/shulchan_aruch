And they testified before a third beit din. I do not know why a third beit din is necessary. If judges in two different batei din received the testimony, they can testify to what they heard. A judge who received testimony is not treated as an ordinary witness repeating hearsay. The point must therefore be procedural. If two judges now need to appear before a third beit din and report what they heard, they function as witnesses, and a witness cannot simultaneously become a judge in that same proceeding. But if all three judges who received the testimony are themselves assembled, they need not testify to one another. They already possess the judicial knowledge and can act as judges. Thus the "third beit din" is required only when the prior judges must now present their knowledge as testimony rather than adjudicate from their own judicial reception.
