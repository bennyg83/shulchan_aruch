One of every single D. A. is brought in such a way that they have gone for years from each of them:
But a judge before whom two witnesses testified. Meaning that the original proceeding was held before a valid beit din of three, which properly received testimony from two witnesses, and then two judges left. The remaining judge may not join with one of the original witnesses and testify before another beit din so that the latter rules on their combined testimony. This is not ordinary hearsay testimony, because the judge properly received the testimony in his judicial capacity and has the status of a judge rather than a witness. Nevertheless, the two do not testify to the same thing. The judge says that testimony was received before a court of three; the witness says that he personally saw the event. In Perishah I proved that the Tur likewise speaks of joining before another beit din, not of the judge and witness joining to adjudicate themselves, unlike the Ir Shushan.
With one of the witnesses. This is not literal. The same rule applies if the judge joins with another witness who saw the event but never testified before him or another beit din. The reason remains the same: they are not testifying to the same evidentiary fact. The ordinary case was stated.
