And one judge from each beit din came. In the glosses of the Gaon Maharam, author of Tiferet LeMoshe, he writes that the same applies if two judges come from two different courts, one from each court, and each testifies that while he sat on a panel of three, two witnesses testified before that panel that a certain person owed one hundred to another. Their testimony is effective and the matter may be adjudicated on its basis. This is not testimony from one witness heard from another, because the men now testifying were themselves the judges before whom the witnesses testified. The rule that a witness and a judge do not combine implies that two judges do combine. This is no worse than validation of documents, where the court may write that it sat as three even though one is no longer present. See siman 46, seif 30, and Netivot HaMishpat.
