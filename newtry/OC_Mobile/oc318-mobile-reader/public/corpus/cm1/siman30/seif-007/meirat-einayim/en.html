The rule that one loan after another combines applies when he claimed from him, etc. Specifically where both witnesses testify to separate loans must the claimant demand both, as the Mechaber concludes. If both testify to admissions, or one testifies that he saw a loan of one hundred and the other that the defendant admitted owing one hundred at another time, they combine, because the admission may refer to the earlier loan. Even if one says the defendant admitted in Nisan and the other says the loan occurred in Iyar, they need not be treated as contradictory; the earlier statement may have been an acknowledgment of an obligation he intended to receive and the actual loan occurred later. The Rosh and Nimukei Yosef support this understanding.
One says that he lent him on Sunday, etc. It appears the same law applies if the claimant simply demands two maneh and says he forgot whether the loans were together or separate. The Tur and Mechaber conclude that only where the lender himself admits there was only one maneh do we know that one witness must be false. Thus if he claims two maneh generally, even without matching the witnesses' dates, the testimony can combine. Beit Yosef also cites Maharik that where one witness is contradicted by another but the matter is objectively capable of clarification, the one who says he can clarify remains believed until the clarification is produced.
And they erred concerning the intercalation of the month. For example, Rosh Chodesh was two days, Sunday and Monday, and the loan occurred Tuesday. Ordinarily the days of the new month are counted from the second Rosh Chodesh day, while the first belongs numerically to the previous month. One witness may have been unaware that the preceding month was full and counted from the first Rosh Chodesh day, calling Tuesday the third of the month, while the other correctly counted it as the second. We attribute a one-day discrepancy of this type to calendar error, provided they do not also contradict each other regarding the weekday.
If the matter became clarified, etc. Such as one saying the second day of the month and the other the fourth, for a two-day or greater discrepancy cannot be attributed to the two-day Rosh Chodesh. Likewise, even a one-day discrepancy after the middle of the month is not ordinarily attributed to such an error, because by then people know which month was full. Similarly, one saying before sunrise and the other at sunrise, or analogous plainly recognizable times, can constitute a real contradiction.
He must take a Torah oath against him. We do not say the witnesses are mutually contradicted so that both testimonies vanish. Each independently testifies to a loan at a different time, and as far as the witnesses themselves are concerned both could be true. Only because the claimant demands a single maneh do we know that one of them is false. Since according to the claimant one of the witnesses is certainly truthful, the defendant must take the Torah oath generated by that witness. So writes the Rosh.
If he established chazakah in land properly, they combine, etc. But where one witness says that in his presence and that of a now-deceased witness the donor gave the property in Nisan, while another says that in his presence and that of a now-deceased witness the same donor gave it in Tishrei, this resembles two gift documents concerning the same property, where the later nullifies the first. Each transfer is then supported by only one surviving witness and the recipient loses. Rabbeinu Yerucham, cited by Darkei Moshe, rules this way because each witness is effectively contradicted by the other.
