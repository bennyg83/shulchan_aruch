The Sema cites a gloss from the Beit Yosef, column 68: Although one witness is ordinarily of no consequence when another contradicts him, nevertheless, if the matter can be clarified, he is believed. Maharik writes this in shoresh 1. The Sema explains that this means the second witness says, “I will clarify my statement,” and until he does so the first witness is believed. This is not persuasive. Why should the first be believed more than the second? Rather, the case is where the first says, “I will clarify my statement,” while the second refuses to clarify his. This is explicit in Kiddushin 66 concerning “send and show it.” Maharik proves from there that where the matter can be clarified and the contradicting witness refuses to clarify it, the first witness is believed. See there carefully. Nevertheless, I am astonished at the basic rule that the Beit Yosef in siman 28 attributes to Maharik and Tosafot in Kiddushin, and that Darkei Moshe and the Sema repeat here. That rule was stated in Kiddushin only regarding ritual prohibitions, because one witness is generally believed in matters of prohibition. When another witness contradicts him, he is not believed, but where the matter can be clarified we accept the stricter account until clarification. In monetary law, however, no such rule has ever been established, and none of the decisors states it. Maharik himself did not rule this way regarding money; he merely cited the case as an analogy showing that where a matter can be clarified we do not rely upon an evasive denial. See there. The law therefore appears as I have written: in monetary matters there is no distinction between a fact capable of clarification and one that cannot be clarified. The words of the Beit Yosef, Darkei Moshe, and Sema on this point require study.

And if the matter was clarified, etc. The Sema writes that this includes a case where one witness says the event occurred on the second of the month and the other says it occurred on the fourth, or where they differ by only one day after the middle of the month, one saying it occurred on the sixteenth. These are his words. This is also the ruling of the Beit Yosef, Darkei Moshe, and Ir Shushan, contrary to Agudat Ezov, folio 77d. Maimonides states explicitly at the end of chapter 9 of Hilchot Edut that after the middle of the month we do not attribute such a discrepancy to error. The Hagahot Ashri at the end of Gittin, chapter Get Pashut, in the name of Ri from Mahariach, rules likewise, as do the responsa of the Rosh, rule 45, siman 25, and the Rivash, siman 382.
