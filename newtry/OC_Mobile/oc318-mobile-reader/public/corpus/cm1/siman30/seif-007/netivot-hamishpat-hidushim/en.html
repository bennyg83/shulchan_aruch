When he claims two maneh. But in successive admissions or an admission after a loan, the witnesses join even if he claims only one maneh. Even where the loan followed the admission, the Sema holds they join, because his willingness to lend afterward supports the earlier admission. If the claimant says he lent two maneh but does not remember whether in one transaction or two, the testimony is likewise valid. Sema. The same applies where the claimant does not assert certainty but sues on the basis of the witnesses, because he does not thereby contradict them. Maharik writes that where one witness contradicts another and the matter can be clarified, the party who can clarify must do so. The Shach explains this by analogy to Kiddushin, where one says a slave has a defect and says, "Send him and show it," while the other denies it. The person able to produce the slave must clarify. This appears primary, even though the Shach questions whether the principle applies outside ritual prohibitions.
A calendrical month error. Even with two witnesses we may assume an error of one day, but not two days. If the event occurred after most of the month had passed, we do not assume even a one day calendrical mistake. If the event occurred before most of the month, then even if the testimony is later given after most of the month, we may still say the error was fixed in their minds at the time they saw the event and therefore they now testify that way. He established possession of the land. If one witness says that in his presence and that of another witness who later died, the owner transferred the land to Shimon by kinyan in Nisan, and a second witness says that in his presence and that of another now deceased witness the same land was transferred to Shimon in Tishrei, or even that he himself alone saw the Tishrei transfer, this resembles two deeds concerning one field, where the later deed cancels the first, leaving only one effective witness. Maharik. Beit Yosef questions this without fully explaining the objection. See Netivot, which finds Beit Yosef's view primary. Even according to Maharik, the rule applies specifically if the deceased witness was still alive in Tishrei. If he had already died, it can be said that the owner repeated the kinyan in Tishrei precisely because the earlier witness had died.
