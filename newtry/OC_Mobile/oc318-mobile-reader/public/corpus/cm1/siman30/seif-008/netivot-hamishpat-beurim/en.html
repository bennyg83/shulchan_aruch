Beit din should come to another beit din. If two members remain from one original beit din, they can still act with judicial status. They can write their received testimony as a court record, noting the absence of the third member, and another beit din can adjudicate on that record. Therefore two members from one beit din can combine with members of another beit din more readily than a single member from each court. A lone judge from one prior court cannot independently establish that another witness's testimony was formally received before a valid beit din. But where each original beit din received two witnesses, even a single surviving member from each can sometimes suffice for the later adjudication, because each one personally heard a complete evidentiary basis and does not need the other to establish that a second witness was properly received.
