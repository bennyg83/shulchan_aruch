Or one testifies that he repaid, etc. If one witness says that the borrower admitted the debt and the second says he later repaid it, their testimony does not combine. Sema explains that this is where the borrower says he paid only one maneh. We cannot say that the admission referred to a payment that would occur later, because although such reasoning may apply to a borrower who needs money, it is not ordinarily said regarding a lender. Taz objects that sometimes a lender also trusts the borrower. That is not compelling, because such conduct is uncommon, and here we follow the majority pattern, as explained in Tumim.
They were for another debt. The law is as explained at length below in siman 58. Here the author abbreviated the arguments of “they were for another debt.” The authorities dispute whether this plea completely invalidates the document or merely weakens it. Sema correctly applies the same dispute here. Even if the creditor admits receiving the money but says, “I applied it to another oral debt, or to another written debt whose document I already returned to you,” he is not automatically believed. Sema in seif katan 31 even says that this is so where it is known that another oral or written debt existed. That requires study; see siman 58. The novelty here is that one might have thought the plea of another debt is especially plausible because otherwise why did the debtor not take back his note? One might answer that he relied on repayment witnesses, but how could he know separate witnesses would ultimately combine? In successive repayments the point is even stronger, because the debtor claims to have paid two hundred while our ruling recognizes only one hundred. Nevertheless, the creditor can still plead that the payment was for another debt.
