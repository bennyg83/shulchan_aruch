He repaid one hundred. Where one witness says one hundred and another says two hundred about the same time, while the borrower himself supports only one version, the testimony at first seems unable to combine, just as a claimant must generally assert both versions where testimony is offered for collection. Upon analysis, however, the discrepancy of one hundred versus two hundred is a secondary detail in the sense that, between witnesses themselves, we say one may have erred and at least one hundred is established. When a litigant contradicts a witness in such a detail we do not automatically say the litigant erred, but the resulting contradiction is still not absolute. Proof comes from Bava Batra 128: the borrower says he repaid half, while witnesses say he repaid all, and the lender swears and collects half. If the borrower's contradiction rendered the witnesses certainly false, the debt instrument would remain wholly intact. Yet the witnesses do defeat collection of half. Thus the contradiction creates uncertainty, not total nullification. Therefore, where two witnesses say one hundred and two hundred at the same time and the borrower agrees with one of them, the other is not certainly contradicted in a manner that allows extraction. At least one hundred is treated as repaid. The Sema's requirement that the borrower expressly say like both witnesses applies where the witnesses speak of distinct times, such as one in Nisan and one in Tishrei. If he admits only one payment there, he certainly contradicts the other event. But where both testify about the same payment and differ only as to its amount, there is no definite contradiction and one hundred is established.
Before Nisan. The wording is difficult, because if the receipt simply says payment was made in Nisan, the same result should follow. It may be explained according to Mishneh LaMelech's view that within the due date a lender can claim that money received was for another debt, because people ordinarily do not repay before the due date. Thus if the receipt was written in Nisan itself and the loan was still within thirty days, the lender might claim the payment was for another debt according to the opinion that the first thirty days are like an unexpired term. Although we generally rule that people may repay within thirty days, Shulchan Aruch sometimes cites the stricter opinion, as in siman 59. By saying the receipt reflects payment before Nisan, the possibility of attributing it to another debt is excluded in the relevant setting.
