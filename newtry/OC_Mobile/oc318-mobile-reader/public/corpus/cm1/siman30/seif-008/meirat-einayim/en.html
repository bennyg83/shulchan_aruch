In Perishah I noted that the Tur does not state the greater novelty that the admission witness may refer to an admission occurring before the payment witness's event, as it did above regarding combining an admission with a loan. This implies that for payment they do not combine in that arrangement. With loan witnesses we can say that because "the borrower is servant to the lender," the lender may have required an advance admission to secure a loan he intended to make later, fearing witnesses might not be available at the time of lending. Regarding acknowledgment of payment, however, a creditor does not ordinarily admit before receiving payment that he has been paid. Once payment occurs, he simply returns the debt document.
And the lender cannot say, "They were for another debt." The Tur also states this without qualification. Below, siman 58, the Tur discusses a creditor who produces a debt document and the debtor claims payment, bringing witnesses that he gave money before them. The document is thereby impaired. Some Geonim hold it is entirely void, and the Mechaber rules like them. The Rosh holds only that it cannot be used for collection, but it is not torn up, and if the creditor seized property we do not remove it from him. Rama rules accordingly there. Here neither Tur nor Rama mentions the issue. It appears that the same rule applies here a fortiori. The Tur's purpose here is only to teach that the separate witnesses combine so that the creditor cannot simply say the payments were for another debt and collect under the document without prior seizure. When the preceding clause says the document is paid to the amount of one maneh, that is where the creditor does not claim that the payments were for another debt. Alternatively, "paid" is not literal and means only that he cannot collect here. The discussion was abbreviated because its proper place is siman 58. It is also possible that the present case specifically concerns witnesses who say he paid on this very document, in which case the rule is stronger. The first explanation appears primary.
They were for another debt. Meaning whether the creditor says, "I received this money for an oral debt you owed me," or, "I received it for another documented debt and returned that document to you." So the Tur writes explicitly. The reason is that witnesses established the payment while the assertion that it was for another debt rests only on the creditor's oral claim. Beit Yosef writes so. It requires study where witnesses establish that the debtor in fact owed the creditor another oral debt, or another documented debt that was later returned. Can the creditor then say, "I received the payment for that other debt"? In Perishah I wrote that even then he is not believed. See there.
