They combine. If one witness says, "In my presence and in the presence of so-and-so, who has died, he gave the gift to so-and-so in Nisan," and another says, "In my presence and in the presence of a different deceased witness, he gave it in Tishrei," this is like two documents concerning the same transaction, as stated below in siman 240. The second gift revokes the first, yet the second gift is itself supported by only one surviving witness. The recipient therefore loses the gift. This is the correct practical ruling, contrary to Beit Yosef. Sema.
Before. The Sema writes that "before" is not literal. The same applies if the note states simply that the loan was made in Nisan, and the debtor brings witnesses that he paid on the second of Nisan. We presume the note was executed on the first of Nisan and the payment was made the following day, because the holder of a document bears the disadvantage arising from its ambiguity.
He admitted. If the witness who testifies to an admission places it before the date on which the second witness says he saw payment, the testimonies do not combine. The reason that separate loan witnesses can combine is that a borrower is subject to his lender, and we can say that the lender asked for an admission before advancing the money because he feared witnesses might not later be available. But with an admission of payment, a creditor does not ordinarily admit that he has been paid before payment actually occurs; rather, upon payment he returns the note. Sema.
For another debt. This means whether the creditor says that he received the money on a different oral debt, or on another documented debt for which he returned the note. It requires study where witnesses establish that the debtor in fact owed another oral debt or another documented debt and the creditor returned that document. Is the creditor still not believed to say that the payment was applied to that other debt? In my commentary I wrote that even then he is not believed. See there. Thus concludes the Sema.
