(Netivot HaMishpat, seif katan 4) The reason they gave is that the kiddushin were not completed, etc. Note: The Ran at the end of chapter Mi SheAchazo, cited by the Beit Yosef here, writes that in matters of marriage, which resemble capital cases, the witnesses must be together. See Beit Shmuel, Even HaEzer 133, seif katan 1, who remains in difficulty as to why the Ran required the reason that such matters resemble capital cases. Since the concern arising from seclusion is kiddushin, it should suffice that kiddushin by one witness are ineffective, and therefore the witnesses must be together. One may add that if the first seclusion was intended for kiddushin but occurred before only one witness, she remained unmarried. That first act cannot later combine with another seclusion before a different single witness. Moreover, a seclusion witnessed by only one witness was not fit for intercourse that could effect kiddushin and therefore has no halachic character of kiddushin. The Yerushalmi, chapter Mi SheAchazo, halachah 4, says concerning one witness in the morning and another in the evening, "this is not seclusion," and this appears to mean as stated above. Nevertheless, the Ran's reason could have a practical consequence where a man gives kiddushin before one witness in the morning, by money or document, stipulating that they take effect after thirty days, and then again gives kiddushin in the evening before one witness. According to the Ran's reason this too would not constitute kiddushin, whereas according to the two reasons suggested above there is room to distinguish. In particular, the Yerushalmi is discussing an old get, where he did not explicitly state that the seclusion was for kiddushin. We infer such intent only from the presumption that a person does not engage in promiscuous intercourse. When the morning seclusion had only one witness, that presumption cannot establish intent for kiddushin, because even if he intended kiddushin, intercourse before a single witness would still not effect kiddushin. The Rashba's novellae to Kiddushin 80b likewise support the Beit Yosef's approach. I later found Responsa Shaagat Aryeh, printed in Responsa Beit Ephraim, Even HaEzer, siman 42, discussing the Ran's words at length.
