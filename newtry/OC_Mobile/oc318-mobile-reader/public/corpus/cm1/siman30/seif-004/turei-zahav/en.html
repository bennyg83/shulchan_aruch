Seif 3: “We treat them as contradicting, to be lenient” This is difficult, for Ramah cited by Tur says that whenever contradiction is not established, we reconcile the witnesses, such as by assuming an error about the intercalated month, and collect money on their testimony. All the more so where no defect appears. Tosafot’s concern in capital cases arises because the day is a required examination and an apparent defect exists. In monetary law we preserve the witnesses’ presumption of fitness and reconcile even remote implications, as Beit Yosef and Rama rule. Ramban’s rule that uncertainty is resolved leniently applies only to contradictions in examination details, such as the amount of the loan, where error is common and the witnesses are not disqualified. Where the contradiction concerns essential inquiries and would disqualify them, we do not presume contradiction without necessity. Thus in the wine-and-oil case, if the second witness did not expressly say “not so,” the witnesses remain presumed fit, contrary to the Sema. The rule is: in examination details we may treat them as contradicting; in essential inquiries we do not presume contradiction. Leading scholars approved this distinction.
