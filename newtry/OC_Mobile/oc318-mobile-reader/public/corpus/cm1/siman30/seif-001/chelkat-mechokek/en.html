Rather, once she agreed that he should throw the kiddushin to her. The plain language implies that she must consent to the throwing itself. Bach indeed writes that if she said, 'Give the kiddushin into my hand,' and he changed and threw them to her, there are no kiddushin at all. If she merely said, 'Betroth me,' and he threw them to her, the kiddushin are doubtful because it is uncertain whether she objects to receiving them by throwing. But above in siman 28, the Ramah's words quoted by Tur, and likewise the ruling below in seif 6, imply that the only prior consent required is consent to kiddushin, not consent specifically to throwing. The Gemara itself likewise contains no such requirement. Furthermore, according to Bach, even if she consented to throwing and he threw them into her courtyard, we could say that she consented specifically to receiving them in her hand and not in her courtyard. See Beit Yosef citing Hagahot Mordechai at the end of Gittin that even if he placed the ring in her hand against her will, the kiddushin take effect once she did not throw it away. Maharam Alshakar likewise rules this below at the beginning of siman 42. See what I write there.
