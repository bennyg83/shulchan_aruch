They do not require formal examination and investigation. Although the Torah says, 'one law shall be for you,' linking monetary and capital law, and capital testimony requires searching examination, the Sages relaxed that requirement in ordinary monetary cases. Otherwise lenders would fear that borrowers might deny debts and loan witnesses could be invalidated by technical inconsistencies, closing the door to lending. They likewise wanted to close the door before wrongdoers who might rely on witnesses failing under detailed examination. Therefore common monetary cases involving financial loss, including inheritances and gifts, do not require the capital-law form of examination even before expert judges and even in the Temple era. Uncommon personal-injury cases and similar matters remain closer to Torah law in this respect. The Tur and Mechaber mention injury as one example; the principle follows the reason.
