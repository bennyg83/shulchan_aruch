Whether it is a matter likely to become known. This is Rivash's view in responsa 155, 181, and 183, where he disputes Rashbatz. There is no difficulty from Perek HaMafkid, where regarding one who heard that a person died everyone agrees a relative may be placed into the property, and Tosafot explain the case as relying on one witness. The reason is indeed that the matter is likely to become known. But Tosafot in Ketubot 107a explain that such a relative may enter and preserve the property, yet may not sell it on the basis of one witness, because entry without sale does not create the same monetary consequence. Or Zarua says similarly in the name of Ritva in Perek HaMafkid. Likewise, below in siman 48, seif 2, even a relative is believed to identify a person for purposes of writing a document because the matter is likely to become known. There, however, it has not yet been established that a monetary consequence exists at all; there is only a concern that perhaps this is a different person. For that limited identification we rely on a matter likely to become known. Rivash responsum 181 also implies this distinction. See what I wrote above on this subject.
