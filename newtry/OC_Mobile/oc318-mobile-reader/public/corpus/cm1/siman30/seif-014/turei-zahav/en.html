Further there: “At the end of the first chapter of Makkot,” through “the dispute of Rabbi Yehoshua ben Korcha and the Sages” This is imprecise; the dispute there parallels Rav Natan and the Sages. Nevertheless, the sugya provides some evidence because it compares Rabban Shimon ben Gamliel’s prohibition case to Rav Natan’s monetary rule, showing that testimony need not be simultaneous. However, according to Maggid Mishneh and Ran in Rashba and Ramban’s name, that passage concerns only rabbinic teruma, with no capital prohibition. It therefore proves nothing where a death penalty prohibition is involved.
