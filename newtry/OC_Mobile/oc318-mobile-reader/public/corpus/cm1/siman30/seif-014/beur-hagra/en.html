Testimonials for Weeks for G B:
C.C.:
There is no distinction. A single witness is believed regarding a woman's husband's death because the Sages were lenient in a matter destined to become known, as the Rambam writes in chapter 12 of the Laws of Divorce, halakhah 15, and as stated in Yevamot 116b. Although the Gemara there raises a question, Tosafot explain that it asks which rationale is primary. In any event, the fact that the matter is destined to become known certainly remains a reason. On 117a the Gemara states that brothers do not enter the estate, etc., and Ketubot 107a states that when she heard through one witness that he died, his children, etc. One cannot explain these passages as involving mere rumor, for then the same rule would apply even with two witnesses. See Rivash.
(Anthology) There is no distinction, etc. Meaning that although a single witness is believed concerning prohibitions in a matter destined to become known, as stated in the second chapter of Rosh Hashanah 22b, here there is no distinction. This follows from the unresolved question in Yevamot 116b, which the Gemara does not resolve from the Mishnah that the brothers do not enter the estate on her testimony.
