The Rosh and Tosafot explain that all this concerns... One who studies Rosh will see clearly that he does not limit the case of 'place it upon my rock' to where he threw the money into her lap. What difference is there between her lap and her courtyard or her own rock? Tosafot and Rosh mention the lap because they hold that a jointly owned rock does not acquire by way of courtyard. They therefore had to explain that the sugya is not based on courtyard acquisition. If she says 'place it on a rock,' she is not betrothed even if he places it in her lap, because her words reject the kiddushin. But if she says 'place it on my rock,' she is betrothed even without her lap, for her own rock is no different from her courtyard or her lap. In my view this is clear. Bach unnecessarily creates a dispute between the Ramah and Tosafot. Rambam, however, disagrees with Tosafot and holds that although a jointly owned courtyard does not acquire for ordinary purchase, regarding kiddushin it creates doubtful kiddushin. He derives this from the case of a jointly owned rock, in accordance with Rashi, and so rules above in seif 3.
