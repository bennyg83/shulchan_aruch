Needless to say, if she threw it away in his presence. Kiddushin 8b teaches: if he says, 'Be betrothed to me with this maneh,' and she takes it and throws it into the sea, fire, or any place where it will be lost, she is not betrothed. The Gemara initially infers that if she merely threw it in front of him there might be kiddushin, then rejects this and says: not only where she throws it before him are there no kiddushin, but even where she throws it into the sea, one might have thought that because she becomes liable for payment she accepted kiddushin and merely wished to test whether he is quick-tempered. The novelty is that even there she is not betrothed. Rama's wording here follows the Gemara's conclusion and treats throwing it before him as the simpler case of no kiddushin. Tur reverses the order, listing throwing into fire or another place of loss and then 'even' throwing it before him, following the initial line of reasoning. It appears that specifically throwing it before him negates kiddushin. If she throws it merely in front of herself, close to her or within her four cubits, perhaps kiddushin remain. See Bach.
