Contradictory groups of witnesses. The sages of Brisk questioned whether one witness who says, 'He borrowed,' and another witness who denies it may later combine together for an unrelated testimony. The Shach concludes they may not, based on Ran. In my view there is room to distinguish. One witness contradicted only by one other witness has not been established as a deliberate liar. A lone witness is generally not relied upon because he may have perceived the matter incorrectly, and where only one other witness contradicts him it remains possible that his testimony arose from an honest mistake. This resembles the rule that one witness accusing another of a transgression does not automatically disqualify him from joining future testimony, because the alleged act may be attributed to compulsion or mistake. When two witnesses formally join as a pair, however, we presume they were careful enough that contradiction between them is more serious. Therefore two witnesses who came to testify jointly and contradicted one another in formal inquiries cannot later be treated like independent trustworthy witnesses. This preserves the rule concerning two witnesses who disagree on the height of an object, while also answering Ran's case of one witness saying a woman was divorced and another denying it: there the first witness's own testimony that she was divorced necessarily implies prior marriage, and once his divorce testimony is rejected, there is no second independent witness to her marital status.
