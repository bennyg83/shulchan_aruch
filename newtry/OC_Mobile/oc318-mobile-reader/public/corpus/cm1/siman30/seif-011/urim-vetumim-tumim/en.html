But others, etc. Sema writes that even if a person heard two witnesses testify properly in beit din and their testimony was fully examined, he cannot later testify to what he heard, because that is testimony from the mouth of other witnesses. Taz disagrees, and his view is correct. If Sema were right, how could witnesses testify concerning the contents of a document that was erased or burned? A valid document is treated as though the testimony of its signatories had already been examined in beit din. If one may not repeat orally what two witnesses testified in court, why may one testify to what two witnesses recorded in a document? Baal HaMaor writes in Yevamot that witnesses who merely saw a document cannot come to beit din and testify as though they personally know the underlying event, for that would be testimony from writing. This can be reconciled. They may testify, "This is what we saw written in the document." They then testify about the document itself, not falsely present the underlying transaction as personal knowledge. The same applies to two witnesses heard in beit din. A later witness may testify that he heard those two give this testimony in court, because he is testifying to a judicially established event. The comparison to a document is exact. However, if there was only one original witness, a person who heard him cannot convert that into a new witness to the underlying matter. That remains ordinary testimony from one witness through another. Thus, even according to Taz, if only one witness was recorded in a document and the document was later destroyed, others who saw it cannot create a second witness merely by repeating what it contained.
