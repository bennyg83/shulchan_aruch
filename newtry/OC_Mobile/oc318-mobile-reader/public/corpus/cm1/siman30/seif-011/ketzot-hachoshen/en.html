The Sema explains that even if two people heard witnesses testify in beit din and the testimony was fully examined there, those hearers may not later testify before another beit din about what the witnesses said, because that would be testimony from the mouth of other witnesses. In my view this is plainly incorrect. Tosafot in Bava Kamma 98 says that the rule against testimony from writing does not apply to a document because witnesses signed on a document are treated as though their testimony had already been examined in beit din. Other witnesses who later testify, "We saw that this testimony was examined in beit din," are therefore not giving testimony from hearsay. Tosafot in Ketubot says the same, and Nimukei Yosef, citing Ritva regarding a destroyed debt instrument, writes that witnesses testifying about the prior document are not testifying to the original maneh but to the existence of a validated instrument, as though they say, "We saw a deed of gift or sale." It follows that witnesses who actually heard oral testimony fully examined in beit din can certainly testify to that judicial fact. Makkot likewise says that wherever two witnesses come and say that the judgment of a person was concluded before a certain court and named witnesses, the sentence can be carried out. Rambam codifies this in capital law. If even in capital cases it is not deemed hearsay to testify that a judicial proceeding took place, all the more so in monetary law. Perhaps the Sema would distinguish between a final judgment, which has an independent legal status, and mere examination of testimony, or between a formal document and oral testimony. But I found the matter explicit in the novellae attributed to Raah in Ketubot 28. He raises only a doubt whether relatives may testify about testimony previously examined in beit din. His doubt itself shows that qualified unrelated witnesses certainly may. Taz also rejects the Sema. Thus qualified witnesses may repeat before another court that such and such testimony was duly examined before the first court.
