Where the law provides that the testimony combines, etc. Sema explains that where one witness says one hundred and another says two hundred, Beit Hillel treat one hundred as included within two hundred, so the testimony combines at least for one hundred. This rule must be understood together with the distinction above. We do not say that every difference in amount is a fatal contradiction. Where one testimony literally includes the smaller amount within the larger, there is a common core upon which both witnesses agree. Thus, even though one witness adds an additional hundred not stated by the other, the shared hundred can be established through both. The excess remains supported only by one witness and has the law of a single witness. This is not an artificial reconciliation of contradictory testimony. It is the direct content of the testimony itself: one says at least one hundred and the other says two hundred, which necessarily contains one hundred. Accordingly, the smaller amount is established, subject to the remaining rules concerning whether the litigant claims both amounts and whether the differing details are of a kind that invalidates testimony.
