Their testimony combines. Shaar Mishpat writes that there is room for inquiry regarding two witnesses whose testimony combines only by accumulation, such as witnesses concerning repayment of a document, as explained in seif 8, or two witnesses who combine to disqualify a person, where one testifies that he stole and the other that he lent at interest, and they combine to disqualify him as explained in siman 34, seif 28 in the gloss. Suppose two other witnesses contradict both incidents and say that neither ever occurred. Do we treat the matter as two against two? If so, regarding repayment of the document we do not extract money from the party in possession, and regarding disqualification the person has the status of one whose disqualification is doubtful, as stated in siman 34, seif 28. Or perhaps, since the two later witnesses support one another and contradict both incidents, the matter is like one witness against two, because each of the first witnesses testifies only about a distinct event and the other does not support him, while two witnesses say that each event is false. The word of one cannot stand against two, and the later witnesses should be believed. He discusses the matter at length; see there.
These combine. See Responsa Zichron Yosef, Even HaEzer, end of siman 7, who writes that in the case of one loan after another, the second witness combines with the first only if the defendant has not yet sworn on the basis of the first witness to contradict him. If he already swore against the first witness and was thereby exempted, then a later witness who testifies about another loan does not combine with the first to obligate the defendant in a maneh. Rather, the defendant swears against the second witness as well and is exempt. He writes that this is how he would rule. This is also explicitly implied by the Ramban cited in the Rama's gloss to siman 34, seif 27, and by the Sema there, seif katan 67. Although the Maharshal, in Yam Shel Shlomo, chapter 9 of Bava Kamma, siman 46, whose view is cited by the Shach there, holds that even after the defendant has sworn, two witnesses relating to two different litigants combine to disqualify him, the Rashba cited there and the aforementioned Ramban hold that they no longer combine to disqualify him. We therefore have not escaped doubt. See there at length. I cited this briefly in Pitchei Teshuva to Even HaEzer, siman 115, seif katan 33. I have now seen Shaar Mishpat, seif katan 5, who cites Zichron Yosef, disputes him, and concludes that the law is clear: with witnesses whose testimony combines, whether concerning a prohibition where the litigant contradicted them, or in monetary law after he already swore to contradict the first witness, when the second witness later arrives the first testimony is revived and the two combine, both regarding prohibitions and even to extract money. See there at length.
