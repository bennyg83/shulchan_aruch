See Responsa Zichron Yosef, Even HaEzer, end of siman 7, who writes that in the case of one loan after another, the second witness combines with the first only if the defendant has not yet sworn on the basis of the first witness to contradict him. If he already swore against the first witness and was thereby exempted, then a later witness who testifies about another loan does not combine with the first to obligate the defendant in a maneh. Rather, the defendant swears against the second witness as well and is exempt. He writes that this is how he would rule. This is also explicitly implied by the Ramban cited in the Rama's gloss to siman 34, seif 27, and by the Sema there, seif katan 67. Although the Maharshal, in Yam Shel Shlomo, chapter 9 of Bava Kamma, siman 46, whose view is cited by the Shach there, holds that even after the defendant has sworn, two witnesses relating to two different litigants combine to disqualify him, the Rashba cited there and the aforementioned Ramban hold that they no longer combine to disqualify him. We therefore remain in doubt. See there at length. I cited this briefly in Pitchei Teshuva to Even HaEzer, siman 115, seif katan 33. I have now seen Shaar Mishpat, seif katan 5, who cites Zichron Yosef, disputes him, and concludes that the law is clear: with witnesses whose testimony combines, whether concerning a prohibition where the litigant contradicted them, or in monetary law after he already swore to contradict the first witness, when the second witness later arrives the first testimony is revived and the two combine, both regarding prohibitions and even to extract money. See there at length.
