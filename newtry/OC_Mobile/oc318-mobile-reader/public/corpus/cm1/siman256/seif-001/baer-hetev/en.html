Inheritance. Tosafot write that if a convert had sons conceived in holiness and he gave property as a gift of a dangerously ill person to another convert, the recipient acquires. Since the donor has heirs, we are not particular that the recipient himself be capable of inheriting. See there. Shach.
An admission. We follow his own admission, even though it is certain that the property is not his, provided it was a complete admission such that he cannot say he spoke merely so as not to appear wealthy. Sema.
252. There it is stated that we apply the mitzvah to fulfill the words of the deceased only when the property was presently placed in the hands of a third party for that purpose. Ibid.
A dispute. This does not refer to the immediately preceding question whether the mitzvah to fulfill the words of the deceased applies to a convert. Rather, it refers back to the earlier dispute whether a gift of a dangerously ill person applies where the recipient is not fit to inherit. What was written, that if both are in possession they divide, applies equally where neither is in possession, in which case they divide as well. So the Rama writes at the end of siman 139. He merely stated one of the cases. All this is clear to one who examines Terumat HaDeshen, contrary to the Ir Shushan. Thus writes the Sema. The Shach writes that according to the Sema's explanation it is difficult, for this is not the Rama's manner. Since he already wrote that the Rosh's view appears primary, how could he then return and write, 'and since...' Therefore, it appears that the Ir Shushan understood this correctly. The Rama derived a practical distinction from the Terumat HaDeshen concerning a convert and a shetuki with respect to the mitzvah to fulfill the words of the deceased, where there are differing views and no clear decision whether that mitzvah applies to them. So it appears to me. Shach.
