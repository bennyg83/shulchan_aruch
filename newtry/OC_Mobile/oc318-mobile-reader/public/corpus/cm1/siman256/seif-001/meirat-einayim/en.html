Since he is not an heir, etc. Meaning, even the convert's son does not inherit him, because the Torah treated his offspring born while he was a non-Jew as unrelated to him for Jewish inheritance, as the Tur and Mechaber write at the beginning of siman 283.
So too he cannot give him his property. The Beit Yosef writes that the principal reason is a decree: people may say that he received the property by inheritance and learn to do the same where no valid gift was made. This concern does not apply if the property was given to another. The Rosh rejects this, and Rashba likewise holds that the reason is different. A shechiv mera's gift ordinarily works because the Sages treated it like inheritance. A convert cannot transmit by inheritance to his children from before conversion. Therefore his shechiv mera gift does not work, because the mechanism that gives such a gift legal force does not apply. On this explanation there is no distinction whether he gives to someone who would otherwise be fit to inherit him or to someone else. This is the reason of the "some say" that Rama cites below.
To another convert, etc. All the more so if he gives to other Jews. He mentions a convert only to exclude giving to a non-Jew, whose gift does not take effect as the Mechaber writes in seif 3.
Only by way of acknowledgment. Meaning, he acknowledges and says, "This belongs to so-and-so," whether or not that person would be fit to inherit him, as the Mechaber himself writes below in seif 3. Even if we know the property was never that person's, we follow his full acknowledgment, provided it is made in a manner that cannot be explained as merely avoiding the appearance of wealth. See the Perishah.
If it is done in the manner explained in siman 252. Meaning, seif 2 there, where the Tur likewise explains that "it is a mitzvah to fulfill the words of the deceased" applies only where the money was presently deposited with a third party for this purpose.
A shetuki has the law of a convert. See Terumat HaDeshen, siman 352. The responsum concerns a shetuki who, while a shechiv mera, said that his house should be given to his mother. After his death Reuven took possession of it, and no one had previously possessed the house. Terumat HaDeshen rules that he has the law of a convert. According to Rif, if a convert makes a shechiv mera gift to someone not fit to inherit him, the gift is valid. This man gave to his mother, who is not considered fit to inherit him, because inheritance does not come through his father. Terumat HaDeshen brings proof for this, and likewise writes that even if the mother says "I conceived from so-and-so," she is not believed for purposes of causing that father to inherit. According to the Rosh, even a convert's gift to one not fit to inherit him is invalid. Where the authorities dispute and neither party is in possession, they divide. Therefore the mother and Reuven, who had taken possession after death, divide. This is not the same as an ordinary unresolved factual doubt, where we do not necessarily divide. It follows that Rama's statement, "a shetuki has the law of a convert, since there is a dispute, etc.," does not refer to the immediately preceding rule of fulfilling the deceased's words. Terumat HaDeshen does not discuss that doctrine there, nor was the money deposited with a third party, which is required for that doctrine as explained in siman 252. Rather, Rama refers back to the dispute whether a shechiv mera gift by a convert to one not fit to inherit him is valid. Although Rama could have stated the division rule regarding any convert, he states it regarding a shetuki because that was the practical case before Terumat HaDeshen and because we learn incidentally that even a shetuki who has a mother is treated this way, since the mother is not considered fit to inherit him. The same law of division applies where neither party is in possession. This is clear to anyone who examines Terumat HaDeshen, unlike the Ir Shushan, who did not and therefore understood the "some disagree" as referring to the doctrine of fulfilling the deceased's words and even implied that it could apply without the money being deposited with a third party, contradicting his own citation of siman 252.
