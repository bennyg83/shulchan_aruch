(Seif 1, since there is a dispute.) Terumat HaDeshen there discusses the law of a shetuki, who has the status of a convert, as the Rama writes here. He cites the dispute between Rif and Rosh concerning the gift of a dying convert to another convert, mentioned at the beginning of this siman, and concludes that the parties divide the property. Had the Rama intended here to rule according to that compromise, he should not first have ruled categorically according to the second opinion, for he writes, “and this appears primary,” which implies that even if the recipient seized the gift, it is removed from him. It therefore appears to me that the Rama’s statement refers to the dispute whether there is a mitzvah to fulfill the words of a deceased convert. Levush likewise explains it this way. The Sema expresses surprise at him based on the ruling of Terumat HaDeshen, but I am astonished at that surprise. Even if one says that the Rama’s words refer to the dispute at the beginning of the siman, he still had to rule regarding this additional dispute whether there is a mitzvah to fulfill the deceased’s words, since the halacha has not been decided in accordance with either opinion. The property must therefore be divided where both parties are in possession. The Rama ruled properly on this issue. Regarding the dispute at the beginning of the siman, however, he ruled entirely according to the second opinion, unlike Terumat HaDeshen.
