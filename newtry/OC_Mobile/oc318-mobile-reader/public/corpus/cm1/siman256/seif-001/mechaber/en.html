A non-Jew who converted and made a deathly ill person's gift or freed his slave. It contains four seifim.
A convert who has a son conceived before his conversion cannot give that son all or part of his estate as a deathly ill person's gift, because the son does not inherit him. With respect to one who would otherwise be an heir, language of inheritance and gift are equivalent; allowing the gift would effectively make the son inherit his father. If the convert gives to another convert, however, the gift is valid. Rama: Some say a convert cannot give any deathly ill person's gift to anyone except through an acknowledgment of debt, and this is the primary view. Some say that although such a gift is ineffective, the mitzvah to fulfill the deceased's wishes may still apply when the requirements of siman 252 are met. Others disagree. A person of uncertain paternity has the same status as a convert for this law. Because the authorities dispute the matter, the burden of proof rests upon the claimant; if two parties are in possession, they divide.
