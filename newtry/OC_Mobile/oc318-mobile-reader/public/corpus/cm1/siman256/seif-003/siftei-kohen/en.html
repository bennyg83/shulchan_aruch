If two are in possession, they divide, etc. According to the Sema's explanation, this refers back to the earlier dispute whether the gift of a dangerously ill person applies to someone who is not fit to inherit him. That explanation is difficult, because the Rama has already ruled that the Rosh's position is the principal one. It would be unlike the Rama to reopen the dispute by writing, 'Since there is a disagreement.' The Ir Shushan understood the passage correctly. The Rama derived a practical case from Terumat HaDeshen concerning a convert or a person of uncertain lineage who gave an instruction concerning sanctification of the day, where authorities disagree and no decisive ruling exists. In such a case, if two parties are already in possession, they divide. This appears correct to me.
