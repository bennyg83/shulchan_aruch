The son acquired the orphan girl's maneh for her. Ketzot HaChoshen asks that this resembles one who hoes the property of a convert thinking it is his own, who does not acquire because he lacked intent to acquire. Here too, the son thought the orphan had already acquired through the shechiv mera gift. In my view there is no difficulty. The rule concerning hoeing requires acquisition through chazakah, and chazakah requires intent to acquire. But where acquisition occurs through courtyard, a person's courtyard can acquire for him even without his conscious intent. Here the orphan acquired through the law of courtyard because the son transferred to her his power and domain, as Sema, seif katan 7, explains. Know also that the orphan acquires the full maneh specifically where an actual identified maneh was given to her. If the donor said only "the value of a maneh," or, "a maneh from my assets," then if part of the estate is stolen or lost the reduction is proportional. The donor gave her a maneh out of the entire estate, and certainly did not intend the orphan to receive a larger proportional share than the son himself. If a stranger later acquired part of the assets, that is no better than partial theft or loss of the estate.
