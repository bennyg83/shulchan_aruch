The heirs are compelled to free him. The Jerusalem Talmud discusses one who says, "Give this bill of emancipation to my slave," and dies. Rabbi says the slave did not acquire; the Sages say he acquired, and the heirs are compelled to fulfill the deceased's words. Rabbi Zeira explains that if the master said "free him," everyone agrees the heirs must do so, and if he said "write and give," everyone agrees otherwise. The dispute is over "give": Rabbi reads it as "write and give," while the Sages read it as "free him." This resolves a difficulty in Tosafot. If our Mishnah says, "Give this bill of emancipation," the word "this" is critical. After the master's death, that specific document cannot free the slave, because emancipation after death is impossible and the heirs cannot use a document written in the deceased master's name. Therefore the mitzvah to fulfill the deceased's words does not operate through that document. The Sages' rule compelling the heirs applies where he said generally, "give emancipation," meaning, in their view, "free him," and the heirs can write a new document in their own names. Thus our Mishnah can still follow the Sages because it speaks of "this" document, which cannot operate after death. This also answers Tosafot's question on Rabbeinu Tam's text containing the word "this" both for divorce and emancipation. The word is principally needed for the emancipation case, precisely to exclude the general mitzvah to fulfill the deceased's words. Rashi's wording suggesting one could equally teach "write this get" remains difficult, because it is unclear what additional force the word "this" gives one sheet over another. See siman 66.
