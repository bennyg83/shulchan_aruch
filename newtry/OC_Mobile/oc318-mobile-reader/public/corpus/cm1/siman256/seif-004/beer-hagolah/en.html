The Maggid Mishneh writes that the latter two cases are explained in the Gemara in chapter 4 of Gittin, 40b, and in the Rif: whenever he uses language of emancipation, the heirs are compelled to free the slave. The commentators ask how we may heed him to free a slave and thereby violate Torah law, for one who frees slaves violates a positive commandment, as explained in Yoreh Deah siman 267. The Rashba writes that whenever he said to free the slave we assess that the slave must have rendered him a benefit for which such recompense is warranted. The Torah's prohibition concerns freeing slaves without a corresponding benefit. See Yoreh Deah siman 264, seif 74.
