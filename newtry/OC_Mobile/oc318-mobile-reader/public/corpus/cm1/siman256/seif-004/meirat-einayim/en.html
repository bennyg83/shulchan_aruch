We compel the heirs, etc. Although one who frees his slave violates the positive command, "You shall leave them as an inheritance to your children, they shall serve you forever," and we do not obey an instruction to perform a prohibition with the estate, we presume the deceased was a law abiding person. Since he instructed that the slave be freed, we assume that he had already received the redemption payment from the slave or another person. Once the redemption money was given to the master on condition of release, he is obligated to free the slave, because the Torah prohibited gratuitous manumission, not release in exchange for payment. Nor does the person giving the redemption money violate a prohibition by doing so, since a slave is obligated in mitzvot. This differs from an instruction to make a gift to a gentile. The Ran and Rambam write this. See the Perishah for another reason.
