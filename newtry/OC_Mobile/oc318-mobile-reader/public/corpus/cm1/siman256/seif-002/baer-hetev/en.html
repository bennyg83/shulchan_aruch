For the orphan girl. The Sema relates the case in the Rosh's responsum as follows. A female convert gave her property as a deathbed gift to Shimon on condition that he give a small portion of the property in his possession to a certain orphan girl. Reuven lived in the convert's house, and after her death he took possession of the house in which he lived on the ground that the property of a convert who dies without heirs is ownerless. When Shimon heard what Reuven had done, he said, 'I too no longer wish to carry out her will by giving to the orphan. Instead I will take possession of all the property under my control as ownerless property, just as Reuven acquired what was under his control.' The Rosh ruled that Shimon is not heard. Initially he intended to acquire for the orphan that which the convert had instructed him to give, and he acted as the orphan's guardian to protect her property. Only after hearing what Reuven had done did he newly conclude that the will was ineffective and seek to retract. He cannot do so, because we rule that one who lifts a found object on behalf of another causes the other person to acquire it. Here too, Shimon intended to acquire on her behalf and lent her his power and possession, so that if she could not acquire by virtue of the gift, she would acquire by virtue of ownerless property. Sema.
