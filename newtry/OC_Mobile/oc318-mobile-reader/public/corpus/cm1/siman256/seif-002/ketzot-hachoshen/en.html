The convert gave a gift to his son. Rosh was asked about a female convert who, while dying, said that her property should go to Shimon and his wife, and that Shimon should give a certain amount to an orphan girl. Shimon had acted as the orphan's guardian and originally took possession intending to carry out the convert's direction. Later he learned that the testamentary gift might be invalid and wished to keep the property as ownerless property. Rosh ruled that he could not. One who lifts a found object for another acquires it for the other. Since Shimon intended to acquire the property for the orphan, his later discovery that the testamentary mechanism was defective does not let him retract. The case resembles one who performs hazakah on the property of one convert while mistakenly thinking it belonged to another convert. Since he intended an act of acquisition upon ownerless property, the precise identity of the former owner does not prevent acquisition. Rama adapted the case to a convert's gift to his son because under the Shulchan Aruch's approach a convert can make a valid deathbed gift to an unrelated person but not through the special inheritance like structure to his son. There is still a conceptual difficulty. If the orphan was to receive only through Shimon after Shimon first acquired, then Shimon himself never acquired through the deathbed gift, and one cannot acquire one's own property for another without a separate transfer. If instead the orphan's right arose directly from the dying convert, then even though Shimon's share failed, the orphan might acquire independently, analogous to "you and a donkey," where the valid recipient takes his share. I also find the comparison to mistakenly acquiring one convert's property instead of another difficult. There, the person performed hazakah specifically for acquisition of ownerless property. Here Shimon believed the orphan had already acquired through the deathbed gift, so his possession may have been merely custodial, not an act intended to create acquisition. If so, why can he not later acquire for himself once he discovers the gift was ineffective? This point requires study.
