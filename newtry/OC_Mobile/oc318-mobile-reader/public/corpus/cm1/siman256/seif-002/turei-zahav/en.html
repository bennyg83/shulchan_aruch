(Seif 2, the son acquired it.) Rosh rules that the son acquired the property by virtue of its ownerless status, as the Sema explains. Rosh brings proof from Yevamot 52: one who hoes in the property of a deceased convert, thinking it belonged to a different convert, acquires it. Although he did not intend to acquire the property of this particular convert, since he intended to acquire ownerless property, he acquires. So too here, if the son did not acquire by virtue of the gift, he acquired by virtue of the property becoming ownerless. This is difficult in light of Bava Metzia 10. One who sees a found object and falls upon it does not acquire it. The Gemara asks why his four cubits do not acquire it for him, and answers that by falling upon it he revealed that he wished to acquire through the fall and did not wish to acquire through his four cubits. Rashi explains that he did not intend to acquire through the rabbinic enactment, because he thought the fall itself would suffice. If his own valid mode of acquisition does not benefit him because he mistakenly intended to acquire by falling, all the more should an acquisition from ownerless property not benefit the son here, since he intended to acquire only as a gift and not from ownerlessness. One may answer that Rosh does not accept that resolution of the Gemara, as Beit Yosef writes in siman 268. Yet I have still not understood Rosh’s proof from Yevamot. There, one who acquires ownerless property has no concern who abandoned it, and there is therefore no relevant mistake. Here, however, the question concerns the very mode of acquisition. Why should we not say that she did not acquire from ownerlessness, since that was not her intent? Moreover, there is a clear assessment that had he known from the outset that the orphan girl would not acquire by virtue of the gift, he would not have allowed her to acquire from ownerlessness, for he himself would have precedence to acquire it, as he revealed by his present conduct. This resembles the Tur’s ruling in the name of Rashba in siman 253, seif 23, regarding one who was counting and handing over money, where his conduct reveals his intent. So too here, we know that initially he thought all the property would be his except for the share belonging to the orphan girl, whereas now he himself has precedence to acquire the ownerless property. This requires extensive study, particularly because the ruling extracts money from one who subsequently took possession of that gift.
