The convert gave a gift to his son, etc. The Mechaber's wording is obscure unless one sees the source in the responsum of the Rosh. A female convert gave her property as a deathbed gift to Shimon and instructed that Shimon give a small amount from the property in his possession to a certain orphan girl. Reuven lived in the convert's house and, after her death, seized it as ownerless property, since under the Rosh's view a convert's deathbed gift is ineffective. When Shimon heard this, he said that he too would not carry out her instruction to give the orphan the amount designated for her, but would instead keep all the property in his hands as ownerless property, just as Reuven kept the house. The Rosh ruled that Shimon is not heard. At first he intended to acquire the designated amount for the orphan and acted as her guardian, safeguarding it for her. Only after hearing what Reuven had done did he realize that the original testament was ineffective and seek to retract. He cannot, because one who lifts a found object on behalf of another acquires it for that person. Shimon had intended to acquire for her and lent her the legal power of his hand and domain, so that if she could not acquire by gift she would acquire by virtue of the property becoming ownerless. The Mechaber means this. He writes that the convert gave the gift "to his son" because he follows the Rif and Rambam at the beginning of the siman, who hold that only when the convert gives to his son is the gift ineffective, whereas a gift to an unrelated person is effective. The Rosh and Tur, who hold that even a gift to another is ineffective, therefore did not specify a son.
