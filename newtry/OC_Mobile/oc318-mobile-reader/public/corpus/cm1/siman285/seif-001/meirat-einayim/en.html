We do not remove them from their possession. This is also the Rambam's language, following the baraita. Tosafot explain that "we do not remove" is not literal, because even initially a relative may be placed over the property of a captive, as the Tur and Mechaber state in the following seif. The phrase was used only to contrast with the latter clause, where one who left voluntarily is removed even if he is a relative. According to the Maggid Mishneh, however, no such explanation is needed. He writes that the Rambam's phrase "a captive who was taken and it was heard that he died" is necessary only where relatives descended in order to sell, take possession, and treat the property as their own. If they descended merely to work the land like sharecroppers, they may do so even when no report of death was heard. According to this, the wording "we do not remove" is precise. The case concerns brothers who descended as heirs, divided the estate, and intended to treat it as their own. In such a case they are not removed where the owner was a captive and a report of death was heard. But initially we do not give them the property with that degree of ownership. We place them only to work the land and eat its produce according to the applicable rules. This also resolves the Rambam's conclusion that if one left voluntarily, a report of death was heard, and his heirs descended and divided his property, we remove them. At first glance this is difficult, because even for one who left voluntarily, once a report of death is heard we do place a relative over the property, as the Rambam and Mechaber write in seif 5. The difference is that here they divided it and intended to own it as inheritance. The Maggid Mishneh explicitly uses this to answer the Raavad's objection. He further explains the Rambam's reason: regarding a captive, even without a report of death we place a relative over the property as a sharecropper. Therefore, once a report of death is heard, we advance the relative one level and if he descends as an heir we do not remove him. With one who left voluntarily, before a report of death we do not place the relative even as a sharecropper. Therefore, after a report of death we advance him only one level, allowing him to enter as a sharecropper, but not so far as to let him keep the property if he descended as heir.
But one who left voluntarily, etc. This applies specifically where he went to a distant or dangerous place where caravans are uncommon. A person traveling in that manner ordinarily makes arrangements for his household before leaving. Since this one did not, we assess that he did not want others to descend to his property. But if he knowingly went only to a nearby place or to a place where caravans are common, we say that he did not give instructions because he expected to return today or tomorrow. Since he did not return, he was presumably prevented by circumstances beyond his control and his law is like that of a captive. The Beit Yosef and Darkei Moshe cite this in the name of Rabbi Yechiel son of the Rosh, and Rama states it briefly below in seif 4.
