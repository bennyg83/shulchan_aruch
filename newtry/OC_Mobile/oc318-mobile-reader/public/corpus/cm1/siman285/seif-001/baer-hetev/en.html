From their hands. The Sema writes in the name of Tosafot that the phrase 'we do not remove it from their hands' is not literal, because even initially a relative may be placed in possession of the property of a captive, as the Tur and Shulchan Aruch state in seif 2. The formulation 'we do not remove it' was used only because of the concluding case, where, if the owner left knowingly, even relatives who already descended into the estate are removed. See there for the Sema's reconciliation of the Rambam's words on this point. The Shach writes that the Sema's explanation is well founded, unlike that of the Maggid Mishneh; the Bach likewise rules so. See Responsa Mahara Sasson, siman 93, and Responsa Ra'anach, siman 15.
Knowingly. This refers specifically to one who knowingly traveled to a distant and dangerous place where caravans are uncommon. A person setting out on such a journey normally gives instructions concerning his household. Since he gave no instructions, we assess that he does not want others to take possession of his property. But if he knowingly went to a nearby place, or to a place where caravans are common, we say that he did not give instructions because he expected to return today or tomorrow. Since he did not return, he was presumably prevented by circumstances beyond his control, and his law is like that of a captive. So write the Beit Yosef and Darkei Moshe in the name of R. Yehudah son of the Rosh, and the Rama cites it briefly in seif 4; see there. Sema.
