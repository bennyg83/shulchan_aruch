Law 5 there. The Maggid Mishneh writes that Rambam holds that a report of death regarding one who departed knowingly has the same law as no report of death regarding a captive. There is no proof for this, and Ramban and Rashba disagreed. They say relatives do not descend into the property, but their request may be heard to appoint a stranger through beit din as an aris so the property not be ruined. The Bach cites the Nimukei Yosef that 'we appoint the relative as an aris' means a formal aris arrangement, with annual accounting and payment of his standard aris share. Since beit din reviews his management each year, there is no concern that he will cause loss. The Bach concludes this is primary.
The Kesef Mishneh at the end of chapter 7 of Hilkhot Nachalot wonders why Rambam omitted the baraita at the end of 38b: if he heard that the heirs were approaching and hurried to detach and eat the produce, he is diligent and gains thereby.
