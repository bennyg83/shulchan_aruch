Or until he comes. Meaning that when the owner returns, the relative is compensated like a sharecropper even for the appreciation. The Rambam uses this same language regarding a captive where there had been no report of death, as the Mechaber writes in seif 2, and everyone agrees there that if the captive returns the relative receives a share even in the appreciation like a sharecropper. It appears that even the Rosh, who holds that where a report of death had been heard about a captive and the owner later returns the relative receives no share in the appreciation, agrees here that where one left voluntarily and a report of death was heard, if he returns the relative receives a share like a sharecropper even in the appreciation. This also appears from the Tur's language. He cites the Rambam and writes that the Rosh says similarly that after a report of death a relative is placed over the property, without noting a disagreement regarding what happens if the owner returns. Rama likewise records no dissent here. It is difficult, however, to identify a strong conceptual reason to distinguish the two cases. Even here, after hearing a report of death, the relative assumes it is true and expects the land to remain his, just as in the captive case. This requires study.
