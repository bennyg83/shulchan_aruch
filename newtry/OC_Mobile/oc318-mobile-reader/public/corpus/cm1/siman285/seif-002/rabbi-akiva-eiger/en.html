[Shach, letter 5] And in Responsa Maharam Lublin. Note: See there in Maharam regarding one who died while others held a loan owed to him or a deposit belonging to him, and the beit din did not know whether the deceased had heirs. He discusses the matter at length and concludes that if the person holding the property is not regarded by the beit din as financially secure and trustworthy, the beit din must intervene and remove the property from his possession until the proper heir arrives, for there is no person who has no heir. This applies not only if that person was secure during the creditor's lifetime and later deteriorated, but even if he was already not secure during the deceased's lifetime, upon the latter's death we remove the property from him even though we do not know who the heirs are. Similarly, in a place where the custom is that a husband returns the dowry to his wife's heirs if she dies within one year, even if her heirs are not before us and are unknown, if the husband is not financially established and trustworthy we remove the property from him. If he is secure and trustworthy, we do not remove it, but we make an accounting with him and he must provide a written acknowledgment, certified by a person trusted by the beit din, stating what he holds. See Responsa Rabbi Aharon Sasson, siman 13, whose view is the opposite. It appears that Maharam Lublin's discussion was written specifically to challenge Rabbi Aharon Sasson's position. See there.
