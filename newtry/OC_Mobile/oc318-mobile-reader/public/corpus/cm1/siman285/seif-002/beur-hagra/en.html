(Collected note) And when they return, the property is appraised, etc. The statement that it is appraised like a sharecropper was said regarding Shmuel and Rav Nachman, as the Gemara asks, "what does 'all of them' include, etc.?" It was not written regarding the baraisa itself because the baraisa can be established only according to Rabban Shimon ben Gamliel, whereas the halachah follows the first Tanna, as stated in seif 1. Rambam's practice is to record only what is stated explicitly in the Gemara. It nevertheless requires study why he omitted the further statement there, "and not only that, but even, etc." The Tur and Rama likewise omit it.

(Collected note) Others say specifically, etc. Tosafos there, s.v. "vechulan, etc." Meaning, produce harvested before he heard that the owners were approaching. Before they arrive he consumes all the produce, for he entered the property with that understanding. Once the owners arrive, if he has not yet harvested, and likewise regarding improvements, he receives like a sharecropper. This differs from a case where he heard that the owner died. There, once he heard that they were approaching, he hurried and profited, whereas where he did not hear, we do not allow him to remain. See Tosafos. There is another distinction: if he heard, even a sharecropper's portion is not assessed for his improvements and he receives nothing, as the Rosh writes, and this is also implied by Tosafos. Their phrase "but here, with abandoned property, etc., since he does not lose, etc." implies that this does not apply to captives. Rashi, however, holds that even with a captive he is assessed like a sharecropper, while with abandoned property he may not eat all the fruits even before the owner returns but only like a sharecropper. This is the difference between captive property and abandoned property, as stated in the Gemara. See Tosafos, s.v. cited above, who explain Rashi, and Rashi there, s.v. "de'ilu hatam, etc.," "and other fruits, etc.," which implies even before they return. There 38b, s.v. "beshe'shamu, etc.," he likewise says he receives like a sharecropper. Rambam and Shulchan Aruch, however, hold that the accounting as a sharecropper is made only once the owners return, retroactively. Before that he takes everything. The Tur likewise writes this in Rambam's name.
