They do not place a minor in possession, etc. But they do place an adult in possession, even though there is also a minor. We do not fear that perhaps the captive died, as we fear below in seif 9, where we do not place a relative in control of a minor's property. The Bach explains that with a female captive we are concerned, but with a male captive we are not so concerned. These are astonishing words without basis, for where did he derive such a distinction? The Beit Yosef, citing Rabbeinu Yerucham at the end of section 13, writes explicitly that there is no distinction. Whenever there is both an adult and a minor, we must be concerned that the captive may have died, and we therefore do not place the relative in possession. This appears to me to be the primary ruling. Examine carefully.
