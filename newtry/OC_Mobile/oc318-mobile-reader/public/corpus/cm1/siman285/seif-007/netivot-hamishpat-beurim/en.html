And they are supported at one table, we appoint a relative. In my view this applies specifically according to the authorities who hold that even as apotropos a close relative is not appointed, because we fear he may later establish chazakah. In that framework, where all are supported together, the concern is reduced, as the poskim prove. Otherwise Bava Batra 144 would be difficult. There, adult and minor brothers can say before beit din, "See what we are doing," and the appreciation can belong to the adults. If beit din were never permitted to let the adults enter the minors' property at all, they would simply be persons entering without permission, like the case of Mari bar Isak in Bava Metzia 40. But according to Shach, who holds that the prohibition is only against letting the relative descend and consume produce, while he may be appointed merely to improve the property without consuming, that proof disappears. Accordingly, even when they are supported together, one should still not appoint the relative to consume the fruits. The fact that they share support from other property does not eliminate the concern. If the minor sees the adult consuming the produce of this particular field alone and receiving none of it, he may later think the adult owns the field. Likewise, according to Nimukei Yosef's concern that older brothers may conceal the truth from the minor, sharing a table does not solve the problem. They can still fail to explain why the adult alone consumes this field's produce.
