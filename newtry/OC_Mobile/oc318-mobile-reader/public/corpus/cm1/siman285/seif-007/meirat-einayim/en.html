Even a relative through the maternal brother, etc. I already explained this, and the Tur states it explicitly. For example, the minor has a maternal brother, and that brother has a paternal brother who is a stranger to the minor. Even that person is not placed in possession. Perhaps the minor's maternal brother will die, and this paternal brother will then say, "The property in my hands belonged to my brother's mother, who was also the minor's mother. Their mother died, this property fell to my paternal brother as her son, and I inherited it from him." The Tur adds that we do not even place in possession a maternal relative who cannot legally inherit the minor if there is some way he could later assert title. For example, if the minor's mother had been married to that relative's father, he could later say, "This property belonged to the minor's mother, who was my father's wife. She died during my father's lifetime, he inherited her, and I inherited it from my father."
Even if there is a deed of division between them. Meaning, although a deed of division becomes publicly known, the Sages were concerned that over time the matter might be forgotten, as the stated reason later explains.
Whether houses, etc. Even houses, where neighbors live alongside and know that it belonged to the minor's father and therefore belongs to the minor, remain subject to the concern that over time the facts may be forgotten.
And they already divided. Once they divided, the relative has at least a plausible claim: "We already divided, and this property fell to my share." Therefore, even if they continue eating at one table, he is not placed in possession. Conversely, if they do not share one table, even before a formal division he is not placed in possession, because their households are already economically separate. This is implied by Rivash cited in Darkei Moshe. Thus Rama's phrase "and they already divided" means "or they already divided." This explains his later statement, "but if the brothers have not yet divided and they share one table": both conditions are required, namely that they have not divided and that they remain together at one table. This resolves the textual implications.
This means when he consumes the produce, for then people may err and think the property is his.
Likewise, if the property did not come to the minor by inheritance, it is permitted, etc. Then there is no concern that the relative will later claim, "It came to me by inheritance," as explained in seif 7. See Darkei Moshe there.
