He left of his own accord. Baer Heitev refers to Responsa Rashach and Maharam Lublin 12. Chatam Sofer, Choshen Mishpat 152, explains that Maharam Lublin dealt with a traveler who deposited his property with a householder and died with no known heirs. Maharam Lublin maintained that beit din should remove the property from the householder and appoint a guardian, or, if the householder is wealthy and reliable, require security so that the principal will remain available until heirs appear or Eliyahu comes. The purpose is preservation of the principal, not productive administration, since one does not ordinarily appoint a guardian for an adult absent owner. Chatam Sofer writes that Maharam Lublin's proofs are not compelling, and Yaavetz I, 142, already disagreed. In his view there is no need to remove the property at all, especially because it may be virtually impossible for later claimants to prove that they are heirs of a man whose heirs were unknown in his lifetime. The holder may therefore be treated as having acquired property with no claimant. If he wishes to act beyond the letter of the law, he should use some of it for the deceased's spiritual benefit, which can constitute a form of restitution. In another responsum, Chatam Sofer discusses a widow who died without known paternal heirs after having made and then revoked a deathbed will. Maternal relatives seized her movables. He rules that the revoked will gives its holder no claim, but because the seizure occurred only after death, the holders should provide adequate security and the community should record that if true heirs later appear, the property must be delivered to them. If the holders are not reliable, the community should appoint a wealthy guardian and require sufficient security. The matter requires study.
