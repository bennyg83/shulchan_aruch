A relative. One might ask: In any event, the Tur and Shulchan Aruch state in siman 290, seif 2, that women are not appointed as guardians. The answer is that the reason women are ordinarily not appointed is that it is not their usual manner to go out and come in and trouble themselves with the property. Here, however, she must in any event exert herself, or appoint a sharecropper and supervise him, for the sake of her own share. Her effort would incidentally preserve the minor's share as well, were it not for the separate rule that a relative is not placed in possession of a minor's property. Sema.
The property. The Tur concludes in the name of Rabbeinu Yonah that this guardian is not appointed for the minor's benefit, meaning that he does not manage matters for the minor as an adult relative manages the property of a captive, with the minor consuming the produce. Rather, the guardian is appointed for the benefit of the captive. He places a sharecropper over the property and each year takes the captive's share and preserves it for him. The Rosh writes the same and brings proof for it. This also explains what was stated above: although it is generally difficult to find a person willing to serve as guardian over the property of an adult captive, here guardians can be found, because the arrangement also benefits the minor by preserving his share in case the captives have already died or later die. The minor. Regarding the rule that the property is given into the minor's own hands rather than placing a guardian over him, the Nimukei Yosef writes that this refers to a minor who knows how to manage his property. Therefore, whatever is his own is given to him, but he is not fit to be appointed guardian over the property of others. The Rosh, commenting on the Gemara's statement that one third is given to the child, writes: If he knows how to manage his property, he should attend to it; and if his father appointed a guardian for him, or entrusted him to a householder, they should manage it.
