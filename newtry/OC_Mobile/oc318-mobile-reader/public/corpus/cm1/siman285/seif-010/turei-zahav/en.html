(And they have already divided.) At first glance this implies that if they have not divided, we are unconcerned that they are not supported at one table. This cannot be said, for Rivash explicitly wrote that whenever they are not supported together, observers will assume that they have already divided and that each has taken possession of his portion. Rather, the meaning is: when they are no longer supported together, the circumstances appear to show that they have already divided. The Sema explains that the phrase "and they have already divided" means "or they have already divided," for either circumstance alone suffices that the property is not placed in their possession; but when both favorable circumstances are present, it is placed in their possession.
