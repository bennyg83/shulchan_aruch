They conduct themselves with the property, etc. The Tur writes in the Rosh's name that this is not comparable to one who eats forbidden carcasses merely to provoke, concerning whom we may remove his person and certainly treat his property as abandoned. There he could eat permitted food but deliberately chooses forbidden food to provoke. Those captured among gentiles, however, act under coercive circumstances. Whatever they do is for their own benefit, not to provoke. The Rosh concludes that throughout their lives their property remains legally theirs, meaning the property of those who apostatized. They may increase one person's share, diminish another's, or give it to whomever they wish. The Mechaber abbreviates this, but it is included in his statement that their property is treated like the property of a captive who did not apostatize. The property of an ordinary captive unquestionably remains his and he may give it to whomever he wishes.
