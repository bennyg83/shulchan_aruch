Although, etc., because there is no, etc. Shevuot 45b discusses the oath of bailees. The answer there involving a written deposit is not accepted, because Bava Batra rules that one who received a deposit under a document need not return it before witnesses and is believed with an oath. A migo generally assists regarding money but does not exempt from an oath. Thus a partial admitter, one contradicted by a single witness, and one who recognizes his utensils may still have to swear despite a possible alternative claim. The worker is different because his oath is itself an enactment, and a migo may therefore defeat it when he was not hired before witnesses. Rosh adds that denial of the entire claim is an audacious migo, while a claim of accident is not; and one witness, with an oath, is equivalent to two witnesses for monetary liability. The Yerushalmi explains that the owner who recognizes his utensils swears only to deter householders from dealing with thieves. See citation 296.
