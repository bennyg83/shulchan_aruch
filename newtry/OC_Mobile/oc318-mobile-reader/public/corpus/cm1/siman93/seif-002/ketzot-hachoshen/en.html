The Sema asks about the rule that a migo does not exempt from an oath, since elsewhere Rosh is cited as allowing such a migo, although a migo involving audacity is weaker. There is in fact no practical disagreement between the explanations of Rosh and Rambam here. In the employee case the Gemara allows the employer to rely on a migo where there were no hiring witnesses, and Ran explains that even an audacious migo can be accepted because the employee's special oath is only rabbinic. The Gemara then asks how the Torah custodial oath could ever exist. Ran explains that the Sages were lenient specifically in the employee enactment. We do not extend that leniency to every rabbinic oath. The partnership oath was instituted in a form modeled after Torah oath and the Sages did not say that every migo, or even every audacious migo, should remove it. Thus both Rambam, who says a migo does not ordinarily exempt from an oath, and Rosh, who focuses on the weakness of an audacious migo, can accept the same practical result. The Tur's statement that Rosh disagrees refers chiefly to the conceptual reason, not to a meaningful difference in this law.
