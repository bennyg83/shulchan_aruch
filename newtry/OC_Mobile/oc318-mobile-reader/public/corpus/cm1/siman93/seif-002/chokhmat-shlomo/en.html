There, seif 13: Reuven placed four hundred maneh into the partnership fund, etc. Note: See Netivot HaMishpat, who writes that if one received property as an iska, so that half is treated as a loan, he is liable regarding that half because his claim is comparable to “I do not know whether I repaid you.” His words do not appear correct. Mishneh LaMelech holds that where the defendant had no reason to know, even “I do not know whether I repaid you” can be exempt, and Netivot himself in siman 75 agrees with that position. It is true that above, siman 72, seif 1, if the lender claims that the collateral was lost through unavoidable accident and the borrower does not know, the lender swears and collects his debt. The Tur and Sema explain that this is because the borrower’s plea resembles “I do not know whether I repaid you.” At first glance this seems to show that even where the defendant had no reason to know he is liable in such a case. But that is not correct. The collateral case is different because the borrower’s claim is really “Perhaps I have something in your possession to offset the debt,” which is weaker than an ordinary claim of payment. Proof is that if the borrower claims with certainty that the lender sold the collateral, the Shach there and in seif katan 29 still hold that the lender swears and collects where the borrower lacks a migo. This is necessarily because a claim of “I have an offset in your hands” is not believed without a migo of payment. Accordingly, in an ordinary case of “I do not know whether I repaid you,” where the defendant had no reason to know, he may be exempt. But in an iska partnership where he received the very merchandise purchased with the iska funds, it is possible that receipt of that merchandise counts as actual repayment, not merely as a possible offset. Bach, cited in several places, writes that property produced from the same merchandise is regarded as the merchandise itself, and likewise what derives from the same money is treated as that money. If he returned the actual money of the iska, it would certainly constitute repayment. The same should therefore be true if the goods purchased with the iska money came into his hands and he sold them and received their proceeds. This may count as repayment itself and not as a mere claim of an offset. Where he had no reason to know, he would therefore be exempt. See my Chiddushei Mishpat Tzedek on Choshen Mishpat to this siman, where I discussed the matter at length.
