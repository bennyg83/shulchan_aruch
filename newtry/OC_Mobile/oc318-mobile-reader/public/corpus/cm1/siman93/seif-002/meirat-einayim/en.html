We do not apply a migo to exempt a person from an oath. See the Tur, who writes that the Rosh disagrees and holds that in such a case the partner is exempt from an oath by virtue of a migo. In the Rosh's view there is no distinction between money and an oath, because an oath can lead to monetary liability if the person refuses to swear. Although this appears to be a migo involving brazenness, since had he claimed, 'I never entered a partnership with you,' he would have had to brazenly contradict his fellow concerning a fact that the latter knows to be false, whereas by saying, 'Nothing of yours remains in my possession,' his fellow does not know with certainty that anything remains, nevertheless the Rosh distinguishes. Below, at the beginning of siman 297, the Rosh holds that a custodian who says a deposit was stolen is not believed through a migo that he could have said, 'I was never a custodian,' even where the deposit was made without witnesses, because that would be a migo involving brazenness. The oath of partners is different. By Torah law no oath is required at all, and it exists only by rabbinic enactment. Therefore even a weaker migo is sufficient to restore the Torah law under which he need not swear. I wrote similarly in the Perishah to siman 89:5 regarding a hired worker where there are no witnesses that he was hired, according to the Tur's view that he is exempt from an oath by virtue of a migo, even though it involves brazenness. This is not a complete proof, as explained there. The Rosh likewise writes this, and I cited his language in the Perishah. It is therefore surprising that my teacher Rama, below at the beginning of siman 296, also cites the Rosh and writes that some say a migo is used to exempt from an oath, but that there the migo is not strong because it involves brazenness, and therefore it is not accepted. He writes this because there is a practical difference between the views, namely where no Torah oath is otherwise required. If so, Rama should also have cited the Rosh here to teach that according to him, since there is no Torah oath, the partner is exempt by the migo that he could have said, 'I never entered a partnership with you.' See also siman 96:1, where the Mechaber writes that if one admits that he was a partner, the partner takes an oath. I wrote there that this liability, despite an admission without witnesses, follows the Rambam, who holds that a migo does not exempt from an oath. There it is less difficult that Rama did not cite the Rosh and Tur, because the Mechaber there rules that the person must take the Torah oath of a custodian. Although the Tur disagrees with that premise, Rama may have chosen not to write that no oath is required once, according to the Mechaber's own ruling, a Torah oath applies. Examine this carefully.
