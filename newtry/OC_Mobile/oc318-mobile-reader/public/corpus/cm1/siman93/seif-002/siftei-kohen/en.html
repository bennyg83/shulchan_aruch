A migo is not invoked to exempt one from an oath. The Sema says that the proof from the hired worker is not complete. In my opinion it is complete. The Rosh explains that the custodian's case is different because the alternative plea would be brazen. Yet regarding a hired worker, even a weak migo suffices because the worker's right to collect through an oath is only a rabbinic enactment. This shows that a weak migo can restore the original law where the monetary obligation itself is rabbinic. The same applies here, where the oath of partners is only rabbinic. The Rosh expressly equates exemption from money and exemption from an oath for purposes of migo. The Bach's reading that the Rosh still requires the partner to swear because the alternative plea is brazen is therefore incorrect. The Rosh, Tur, and the abridged rulings of the Rosh all indicate that the partner is exempt. The Ran also explains that a weak migo suffices against the worker's enactment, and suggests the same concerning partners, although he ultimately hesitates to extend the enactment. The Rosh plainly does extend the principle. There is another distinction. In the cases of custodians and partial admission, complete denial is brazen because the other party originally entrusted him with property or money. In a partnership oath, however, even the plea “I never became your partner” is not a comparable denial of entrusted property. Tosafot in Bava Kamma distinguish between denying entrusted money and asserting ownership or purchase where no such prior trust existed. This distinction also explains the Rosh's rulings concerning objects normally lent or rented, Rama's ruling in siman 81:23 that one may claim computational error through a migo of repayment, the Mordechai, Terumat HaDeshen, Rivash, and the other authorities cited above and below.
