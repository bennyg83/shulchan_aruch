That beit din. See Baer Heitev in the name of the Sema, who writes that the Mechaber follows his own view that in these oaths based on uncertainty there need not be an admission in part, etc. See Responsa Rabbi Akiva Eiger, siman 53, who expresses surprise at these words of the Sema, because on the contrary, the Mechaber's view is that an admission in part is required, as the Sema himself writes below in seif katan 7, and as the Shulchan Aruch rules without qualification below, siman 290, seif 19. He leaves the matter requiring study. The questioner in Noda B'Yehudah, second edition, Choshen Mishpat, siman 21, already raised this difficulty, and the author answered that according to the Mechaber, the Sema distinguishes between a guardian and other persons who swear on a claim of uncertainty: other such oath takers require no admission, whereas a guardian does. See there. In the gloss of the author's son there, he writes that there is a scribal error in these words of the Sema, and they should read that these oaths based on uncertainty do require an admission in part. His intent is that this explains why the Mechaber omitted the law of a guardian appointed by the father of the orphans: since the Mechaber holds that even one appointed by beit din need swear only if he admits part of the claim, it follows that one appointed by the father of the orphans is entirely exempt. See there.
