And Shimon knew of the matter. The Tur's language is: 'Shimon knew that Reuven's brother had money in partnership with them.' This wording implies that he also knew that Reuven had promised his brother a share of the profit, but claims that since Reuven did not obtain his permission, Shimon need not honor that condition. In siman 176 the Beit Yosef questions this reading, 'Shimon knew,' because silence is treated as admission. In the Perishah I wrote that the version of the Rosh's responsum reading 'he did not know' appears primary. Meaning, Shimon did not learn of it until the time of division, when it became known to him through witnesses, or Shimon believes Reuven concerning the fact that his brother had placed money in the partnership but does not wish to give the brother profit. Otherwise, Reuven certainly would not be believed merely by his own statement that his brother had invested money in the partnership. The ruling that the brother receives profit only in proportion to the amount of his money follows because Reuven himself claims that he expressly stipulated with his brother that his profit would be proportionate to his capital. Otherwise, we have already ruled that according to all opinions profits are divided equally, regardless of which partner contributed less or more principal. See the Perishah.
