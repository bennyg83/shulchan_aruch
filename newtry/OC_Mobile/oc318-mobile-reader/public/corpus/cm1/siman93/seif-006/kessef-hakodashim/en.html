Seif 4. (To purchase produce for him.) Whether one may impose an oath or an acceptance of a ban upon an agent that he did not waste or misappropriate money in making the purchase requires study. Perhaps even through gilgul shevuah one cannot extend the oath to an allegation of concealed expenditure, because no one gives money for nothing and the rationalization represented by "the ox eats from its threshing" does not apply. Such a matter was not included in the Sages' enactment. Perhaps even an acceptance of a ban should not be imposed against a strong logical presumption. On the other hand, the poskim write that even where a single witness supports the defendant, an acceptance of a ban may still be relevant, which suggests that it can exist despite an evidentiary argument. Nevertheless, today an acceptance of a ban is itself treated severely. The poskim note that the modern practice is for the person to pronounce it explicitly, and litigants often compromise rather than accept the degradation associated with it. Therefore it should not be imposed against a sound circumstantial inference or established presumption. This requires careful study.
