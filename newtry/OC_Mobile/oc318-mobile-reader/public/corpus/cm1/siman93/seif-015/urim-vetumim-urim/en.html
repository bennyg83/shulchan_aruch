That Levi did not pay the non-Jew. Sema holds that this must be established by proof. If Reuven cannot prove it and Levi claims that he did pay the non-Jew, then whatever Reuven is now required to pay the non-Jew is treated as an unjust exaction, and Levi is exempt because one person is not seized for another's liability. See Tumim, where I wrote that this should not be said. Levi should have made the payment in the presence of witnesses, and if he failed to do so he caused his own loss. It is enough for Reuven to prove that he paid the non-Jew, while Levi has no witnesses that he previously paid and thereby discharged them from the non-Jew.
For whatever Reuven proves through witnesses. Certainly, if the expense can be proved by witnesses, Sema's question based on the principle that where one party knows and the other does not, the one who knows swears, does not arise. When they first come to beit din to seek guidance, it is plainly better to advise him to prove the expense through witnesses. What need is there then for an oath or for a rabbinic enactment? If, however, these are expenses that cannot be proved, it appears from here that he loses them. Sema therefore asked above, siman 91 seif katan 15, why we do not apply the rule that where one party knows and the other does not, the knowledgeable party swears. Sema answered there that the expenditure was not for the benefit of the second party, which also resolves the question of Shevut Yaakov. According to what I wrote above in Tumim, seif katan 10, however, the question does not begin. There the expense was unavoidable and the person undertook the matter with the understanding that he would have to make such expenditures. Here it was not clear to him that he would incur an expense, and he did not enter into the matter on that understanding. It is therefore not included in the rabbinic enactment allowing one who incurs expenses to collect by swearing. See there.
