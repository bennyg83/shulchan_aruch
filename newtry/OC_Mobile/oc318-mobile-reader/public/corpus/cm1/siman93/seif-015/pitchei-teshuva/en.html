That Levi did not pay. See Baer Heitev through the statement that the gentile came against Reuven with a false claim and one person is not held liable for another. Tummim, seif katan 14, writes that these words are difficult, for Levi was negligent in failing to pay the gentile before witnesses. Moreover, since the discussion initially asks what he should do, why was he not instructed not to pay the gentile except before witnesses or against a receipt? See there. Responsa Beit Ephraim, Choshen Mishpat, siman 6, offers a basis for the Sema from the Rama in siman 55 regarding a third party who returned a document, where the loss is treated as indirect causation, and from the Responsa Rashba, which indicates that even if he handed the document to a gentile he is exempt. Thus Levi's paying without witnesses is no worse than handing over a paid document, for which he is exempt. Tummim follows his own view at the beginning of siman 55 that one who hands it to a gentile is actually liable. Beit Ephraim then writes that he does not understand Tummim's objection to the Sema, because the Shulchan Aruch is not adjudicating a claim against Levi but against Shimon, the partner. Even if Levi was negligent, where payment cannot be collected from Levi, such as where he has no assets, it is not collected from Shimon, because Shimon says that he believes Levi paid and that the gentile is falsely seeking a second payment. Levi's negligence is therefore irrelevant to Shimon's liability. Perhaps Tummim means that Levi should not be believed to have paid the gentile, as he concludes that we are witnesses, as it were, that he did not pay; but the beginning of his language does not indicate this. His further objection, that since the law initially tells them what to do it should have instructed Levi to pay only with witnesses, is likewise difficult, for the present litigation is not against Levi. Levi may not even be before us; only Reuven and Shimon are litigating with one another. See there.
