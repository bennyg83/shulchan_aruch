Reuven had undertaken liability toward the non-Jew only if Levi failed to pay, and it is possible that Levi had already paid the non-Jew and that the latter came against Reuven unjustly. A person is not automatically held liable for another's debt, as stated in siman 72 regarding a sword, and below in siman 128 and by the Tur in siman 378. So writes the Sema. The Taz writes that the case appears to involve no promissory note held by the non-Jew against Levi. If the non-Jew did hold a note against Levi, and at the time Reuven paid he took that note from the non-Jew, there is no clearer proof than that. This is also implied in siman 131, where a document is treated as proof. Here further proof is needed because perhaps Levi paid afterward. One cannot object that Levi should then have taken the document, since failure to take the document is not a valid claim, as stated in siman 130, seif 3. See also siman 131, seif 7.
