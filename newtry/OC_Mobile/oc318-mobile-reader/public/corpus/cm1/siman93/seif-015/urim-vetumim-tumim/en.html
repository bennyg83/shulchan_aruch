That he did not pay, etc. The Sema understands that so long as Reuven cannot prove that Levi truly failed to pay the gentile, even if Levi himself says that he paid, Levi is nevertheless exempt from reimbursing Reuven for what Reuven was compelled to pay to the gentile, because this is treated as an external imposition and a person is not seized for another's debt. This is astonishing. Levi was negligent by paying the gentile without witnesses. He knew that Reuven was a guarantor and that gentile creditors pursue the guarantor. Many authorities hold that even in the ordinary case of a shopkeeper on his ledger, Torah law would exempt the employer because the shopkeeper should have paid the workers before witnesses, and it is only because of a special enactment that we say the employer never instructed him, "Pay only before witnesses." All the more so here, where there is no such enactment and the payment is made to a gentile whose word cannot be relied upon, Levi should not have paid without witnesses. The Radbaz writes in a responsum that perhaps even the enactment that excuses payment without witnesses does not apply when paying a gentile, because with a gentile it is ordinarily as though the payer was expressly told to document the payment. That reasoning is even stronger in our case. Furthermore, the law itself tells us in advance what should be done and says that Levi should give a written acknowledgment, etc. If so, why would beit din not simply advise him that he must not pay the gentile except before witnesses or against a written receipt? That is plainly the prudent instruction that should be given to anyone who comes for guidance. It would be unreasonable for the law first to permit Levi to act in a way that creates the very risk that Reuven will lose money without justice, while failing to tell Levi to obtain proof of payment. The clear rule is therefore that if Levi merely claims that he paid, we do not accept that claim against Reuven. He should have paid before witnesses. If Reuven later proves by witnesses that he himself had to pay the gentile, Levi must reimburse him, for we treat it as established that Levi did not make a documented payment. There is no greater negligence than paying in such circumstances without proof.
