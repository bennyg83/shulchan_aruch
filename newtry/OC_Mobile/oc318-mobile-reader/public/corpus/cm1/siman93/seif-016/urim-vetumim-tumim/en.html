And the witnesses should write Shimon's acknowledgment. The Sema, seif katan 37, writes that this differs from Shimon himself making a document. If Shimon executes a document, he incurs an absolute obligation, whereas if witnesses merely record his acknowledgment, the later dispute remains to be adjudicated if the debt is in fact paid. This explanation has no substance. What later dispute can remain after his acknowledgment? If the obligation is conditional upon Reuven actually having to pay the gentile, an executed document can be written with precisely the same condition. The Sema offers another distinction: the witnesses do not write an obligation, only an acknowledgment. This too is difficult, because an acknowledgment of liability is itself the basis of the obligation. The simple distinction is that in the first case, where Shimon himself makes a formal document, it has the law of a written debt, creates publicity, and carries the ordinary incidents of a shtar. Where witnesses merely record his acknowledgment, the underlying obligation remains an oral loan established by witnesses. There is no reason to turn an oral contingent obligation to the gentile into a full written debt against Shimon. There is still a difficulty. If Shimon does not tell the witnesses to write, their written record ordinarily has no evidentiary force because it is written testimony. Tosafot labor extensively to explain how a written protest can be effective when the witnesses were not expressly instructed to write. If, on the other hand, Shimon says to the witnesses, "Write," then an acknowledgment before two witnesses with an instruction to write is a full document, as explained above in siman 39. The source of the present law is a responsum of the Rosh, and according to the Rosh there is no problem, because I demonstrated above in siman 39 that the Rosh holds that even without an instruction to write, once the writing is authenticated it can have the force of an oral obligation supported by witnesses. Thus the witnesses may record the acknowledgment on their own, and it will preserve the evidentiary force of an oral debt without becoming a formal shtar. But the Mechaber rules above in siman 39 and in many places that writing is ineffective altogether unless the party told the witnesses to write. His citation of the Rosh's responsum therefore requires study. Perhaps the distinction is that where the document is written in full shtar form, ordinary property liability follows and omission of the liability clause is treated as a scribal error. Here, even if Shimon tells the witnesses to write an acknowledgment, the writing is not intended to create that broader property liability and therefore remains a more limited acknowledgment. This still requires study.
