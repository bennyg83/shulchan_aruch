Asking. This means that some of them have asked, because they are separated from each other in their wings and as such, in the name of the Rashi answer. Name:
A document. The reason is that in the preceding case the obligation against Reuven was known and Reuven had already given the non-Jew a document obligating himself. Therefore Shimon too had to give Reuven a document for his share. Here, however, Reuven gave no document obligating himself and the debt against him was not known, so it was highly likely never to become known at all. Sema. See Responsa Rashach, book 3, siman 63, and Responsa Maharshdam, siman 121.
They shall write. This is more favorable to Shimon because the witnesses write no language creating a new obligation; they merely record his admission. That differs from giving a promissory note against himself. Sema. The Taz writes that there is a practical difference: with a promissory note, Reuven can collect from property that Shimon sold, because purchasers should have guarded against the possibility that Shimon would not pay the non-Jew. The Sema's explanation is not clear to me, for I do not understand what legal distinction he sees between the two forms.
