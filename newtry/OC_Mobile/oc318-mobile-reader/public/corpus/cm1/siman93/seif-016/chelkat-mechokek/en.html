A dying man who borrowed, etc. One who studies Responsa Rivash, siman 480, the source of this ruling, will see that he ruled this way because the dying man said, "I leave instructions that my wife shall be supported," etc., and the word "I leave" is a language of gift, as the Rosh writes in a responsum. But if he did not use language of gift, one could say that his intent was only that she eat by virtue of the beit din condition, especially where he used the same language as that condition or similar language. It is therefore difficult that the Mechaber states simply, "a dying man who instructed," implying even without language of gift. Perhaps he relied on the second reason Rivash gives there, that the wording of the will also stated, "in addition to her ketubah." Nevertheless, this is not entirely clear, for perhaps Rivash mentioned that only as support rather than as an independent foundation.
