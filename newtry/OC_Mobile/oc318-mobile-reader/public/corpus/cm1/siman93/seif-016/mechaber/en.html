Reuven, Shimon's partner, conducted business with a non-Jew, caused the non-Jew to make an error in the accounting, and put the resulting money into the partnership. He fears that the non-Jew will remember the error and asks Shimon to give him a document obligating Shimon to reimburse his share if Reuven must return the money. Reuven also claims that he held collateral belonging to others, sold it, and fears sustaining a loss because of the sale; he therefore asks Shimon for a document obligating him to pay half of any resulting loss. Shimon is not required to give such a document. Instead, Shimon must admit before witnesses that Reuven caused the specified non-Jew to make an error of such-and-such sum and brought that money into the partnership. The witnesses write Shimon's admission, sign it, and give it to Reuven. The same applies to the collateral Reuven sold: Shimon admits before witnesses all the events as they occurred, and they write the admission and give it to Reuven.
