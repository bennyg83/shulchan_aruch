[Sema, subparagraph 9] By virtue of a migo that "I was never your partner." Note: Nevertheless, it implies that if there are witnesses that he was a partner, even if they do not testify that he remains a partner, he must swear and is not believed by virtue of a migo that they already divided the partnership. See Derishah and its reasoning. According to the Rosh, who requires witnesses that he was his partner, it appears to me that if there is only one witness that he was his partner, he need not swear. This is unlike the law above in siman 89, seif 3, because here, had he said, "I was never a partner," he would not need to swear to contradict the witness, since even if he admitted partnership he would owe no money, only an oath. According to Shach, subparagraph 11, however, one can say that he must take a rabbinic oath to contradict the witness, and this is not considered one rabbinic enactment imposed upon another, because the oath to contradict the witness is fundamentally Torah based. The practical ruling requires study.
