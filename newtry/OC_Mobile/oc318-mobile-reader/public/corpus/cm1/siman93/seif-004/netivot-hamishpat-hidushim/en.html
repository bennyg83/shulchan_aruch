The principal who sent him. This applies specifically where he sent him to buy or sell. The agent may rationalize retaining something by saying that through his effort he gained some benefit for the principal, for example by buying cheaply. But if he merely sent money to another person, there is no comparable rationale.
Only when he comes to collect. This opinion holds that specifically a household manager who is continually occupied with another's property must swear. An occasional agent does not ordinarily swear except where he comes to collect money he claims to have advanced for the principal, such as where he was instructed to buy something with his own money and now seeks reimbursement. But if he comes only to collect his agreed wage, he need not swear. Likewise, if the expenses are known and the only concern is that he sold at too high a price, no oath is imposed. Urim VeTumim.
And some disagree. The same applies wherever he receives payment for his labor. In that case there is no concern that he will rationalize taking something additional. Regarding contemporary business notes structured under a permissible profit arrangement, an oath is nevertheless required because that was the enactment. See Beurim.
