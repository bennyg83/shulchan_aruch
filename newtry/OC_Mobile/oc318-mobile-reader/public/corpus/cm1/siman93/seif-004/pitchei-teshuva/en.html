Some disagree with this. See Baer Heitev. Noda BiYehudah, at the end of siman 30, discusses judges who disagreed whether a salaried agent, R. Wolf, was obligated in the Mishnaic oath. He rules that the judge who exempted him erred in accepted halachic judgment, because common practice follows the Rambam that even one who receives wages must swear. Ordinary heter iska documents demonstrate this, since they are permitted only by paying compensation for labor, yet every beit din requires an oath when the managing partner claims he earned no profit. Moreover, in that case R. Wolf wished to withdraw from the undertaking, showing that the compensation was insufficient in his eyes, whether because the work was beneath his dignity or for other reasons; thus there remained concern that he would rationalize taking more. Netivot HaMishpat, however, does not treat the matter as definitively resolved and explains that the oath customarily required in heter iska documents derives from the enactment of Maharam governing such instruments. Teshuvot Bigdei Yesha, siman 47, offers another explanation: the dissenting opinion exempts only where the worker receives full compensation for the true value of his labor. In ordinary heter iska documents he receives merely a token amount or a minimal benefit, so even that dissenting view would require an oath. The same responsum rules that if the borrower dies during the agreed term, his heirs are exempt from the profits their father had undertaken to pay, contrary to Teshuvot Tur HaEven, siman 404.
