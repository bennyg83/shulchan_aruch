They hold that with one who receives an iska, and similarly whenever he receives compensation for his labor, there is no longer room for the rationalization that he is permitted to take for himself, because he already receives wages for his efforts. Shach. Mechaber writes, "even though he receives wages," which implies that receiving wages makes the oath more, not less, appropriate. According to Mechaber this is understandable: where he works for free one might say that if you burden him with an oath, people will refuse to accept the role, as was said regarding one appointed guardian for orphans. But where he is paid, the requirement of an oath will not deter him from accepting compensation. This explains the wording. Sema.
