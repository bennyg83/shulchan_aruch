(Tur, seif 4, wrote) In the name of Rabbi Yeshayah. It appears to me that the text should read, “even if he denied everything,” and the word “that” is a scribal error. I have indeed found this reading. Beit Yosef strained greatly in his interpretation. The requirement of two silver ma’ot here applies to the Mishnaic oath. A hesset oath, however, is required even on a claim of one perutah, because hesset has no minimum measure. One might object that the Sages did not enact hesset here because the claim is uncertain. That is incorrect. Since an uncertain claim of this type had sufficient force in earlier generations to require an oath while holding a sacred object, the later enactment of hesset likewise treats it as a definite claim, and hesset has no minimum. There is further proof from Mordechai at the end of Shevuat HaDayanim. After citing the Mishnaic cases in which one swears on an uncertain claim, he includes one who suspects another because he found his chest broken open, and rules that hesset is required. Rama rules this way in siman 75, seif 16. Thus the same applies to all the Mishnaic uncertain-claim oaths: where the claim is less than two silver ma’ot, hesset now applies. This appears entirely clear, although not stated explicitly here.
