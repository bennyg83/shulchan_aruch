Rather, less. The Sema writes that nevertheless this does not constitute a partial admission creating a Torah oath. He explains the case as follows: the claimant says, "Two dinars are due to me from the partnership and you gave me only one," while the defendant says that only one dinar was ever due and it was already paid. I do not understand how this solves the problem. The Shulchan Aruch, and certainly the Tur which also states this rule, hold that the partners' oath itself requires a partial admission. There must therefore be at least an admission of a perutah, and the question remains why the liability is only hesset rather than a Torah oath. According to what I explained above in seif katan 2, there is no difficulty. For the rabbinic partners' oath even heilech can count as the necessary resemblance to a partial admission. We may therefore establish the case where the defendant says regarding the admitted amount, "Here it is." That is sufficient to trigger the partners' oath, but it does not create the Torah oath of partial admission. As to the remaining definite denial, only hesset applies. There is another possibility. The claimant may definitely claim wheat but merely suspect that some barley was also withheld. The defendant denies the wheat and admits half a measure of barley. There is no Torah partial admission because the definite claim and the admission are not of the same kind, and the barley was never definitely claimed. For the partners' oath, however, the uncertain suspicion concerning the barley and the admission concerning barley are of the same type, so the rabbinic structure is satisfied. On this reading the Sema's phrase "rather, less" refers to the amount of barley. The difficulty is therefore resolved.
