That they suffered a loss. He swears only concerning the amount for which he is entitled to payment. He does not swear that they suffered a loss of five hundred when the other party will not pay him that amount. What purpose would the oath serve if he is not paid according to his oath, since the other party is not believed to that extent? This is Sema's explanation.
That he knows with certainty, etc. For purposes of imposing an oath through gilgul, it is unnecessary that he know with certainty. Even where he is uncertain, he may impose an oath through gilgul on the possibility that the other party knows the amount of the loss. The requirement of a definite claim arises only from the concluding clause, where, since there is no partners' oath, he may nevertheless impose hesset. For hesset, he must claim definitely that the other party knows, because an oath is not imposed on an uncertain claim. This is the position of Beit Yosef and Sema. See what I wrote below in siman 94 regarding their meaning. Shach writes that he must necessarily make a definite claim that the other party knows, because otherwise he certainly cannot know, since he did not conduct the partnership dealings with him. His words are strained. Just as it is possible for one to claim definitely that the other knows even though he did not engage in the partnership, it is even more readily possible that he might know, perhaps because he was present when the loss or destruction of the business occurred.
That he does not know with certainty, etc. If he knows with certainty, he must pay from his own funds. See Tumim, where I wrote that this appears to refer to a case in which he already received profits from the business as his share. Otherwise, even if he knows of the loss, he need not pay anything from his own pocket. Bach disagrees and holds that where the loss came through the business activity, the partner must pay half the loss even from his own funds, while below in siman 176 the discussion concerns a loss not arising through the business. The correct view is as I wrote.
They swear and collect, etc. See Tumim, where I asked why we do not apply here the rule that whenever one party knows and the other does not, the one who knows swears and collects. See there. I also concluded that if two people entered a partnership, one contributing money and the other contributing nothing but merely obligating himself to bear half the loss, the investing partner is not believed by an oath alone so as to require the other to pay the claimed loss. The loss must either be admitted or proved by witnesses. See there.
And they collect. If the loss is admitted or established by witnesses, but the other partner claims, 'I do not know, perhaps you profited elsewhere and I am entitled to a corresponding share,' the managing partner swears that he did not make such a profit and the other must pay. Otherwise no partnership loss could ever be recovered. See Responsa Radbaz, part 1, siman 102.
And he takes what is in his fellow's possession. But fifty he takes, because that amount is regarded as lying in the possession of both of them, and therefore it is as though he himself is in possession of those fifty.
