If Reuven contributed four hundred dinars to a partnership fund and Shimon contributed two hundred, they conducted business together, and all the money is now in Reuven's possession, and Reuven claims that five hundred dinars of the principal were lost, we do not say that Reuven takes the partners' oath that this amount was lost and Shimon must pay fifty from his own property. Rather, Reuven takes the partners' oath that the loss occurred and departs with only the one hundred remaining in his possession; Shimon pays nothing. If Reuven claims that Shimon knows with certainty that this loss occurred, Reuven may require Shimon to take the partners' oath and append to it that he does not know the exact amount of the loss with certainty. If Shimon took no part at all in conducting the partnership's business, he takes a heses oath that he has no certain knowledge of the loss and is exempt. Furthermore, if the remaining one hundred were in Shimon's possession, they divide it equally. A partner is not among those who swear and collect, so that he could swear and take what is in the other's possession. He swears and is exempt, or takes only from what is under his own control. If Shimon claims that the partnership owes Levi one hundred, then if Shimon has enough partnership money in his possession to pay the debt and could give it to Levi, he is believed. They pay the debt and then settle the accounts. If Shimon does not hold enough to pay and seeks to remove the money from Reuven or from merchandise known to belong to the partners, he is not believed, lest Shimon and Levi be colluding against Reuven's property. Even if the loan is documented, Reuven is not liable for any of it. But if Shimon claims that Reuven knows with certainty that Shimon incurred this debt for the partnership and that it is their joint debt, Reuven takes a heses oath, or swears by means of an appended oath, that he does not know the debt belongs to the partnership, and Shimon must pay the debt from his own property.
