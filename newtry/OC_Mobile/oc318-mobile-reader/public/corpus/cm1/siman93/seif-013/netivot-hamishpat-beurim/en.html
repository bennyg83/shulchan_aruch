And Shimon shall pay nothing. It appears that this applies specifically in such a case, where Shimon did not become a borrower at all with respect to the money. Rather, profit and loss are shared equally because, when the shares are joined together, the smaller share earns and loses like the larger share, as the Sema explains in siman 176. Shimon becomes a borrower only once a loss has occurred. Since he does not know whether a loss occurred, his position is like one who says, “I do not know whether I ever became obligated to you.” By contrast, in an iska, the recipient immediately becomes a borrower with respect to half the money, because an iska is half loan and half deposit. Therefore, even if the merchandise is lost while in the investor's possession and the recipient does not know what happened, the investor swears that it was lost through unavoidable circumstances and the recipient must pay his half. The recipient's share is deemed a deposit in the investor's possession, and the investor is believed concerning it upon swearing that it was lost through unavoidable circumstances. The recipient is then like one who says, “I do not know whether I repaid you.” Likewise, if Reuven and Shimon each contributed equally to a joint fund, part of the merchandise is in Reuven's possession and part in Shimon's, and the merchandise in Reuven's possession is lost through unavoidable circumstances, Reuven is believed upon taking an oath that it was so lost, and they divide the merchandise in Shimon's possession. The share in Reuven's possession was only a deposit in his hands, and he is believed upon swearing to the unavoidable loss. Similarly, if Reuven contributed more to the joint fund in the form of a loan rather than as an iska, he swears and collects. I later found that Ketzot HaChoshen in siman 176 writes the same. They divide it equally. This applies specifically where the money was not given to Reuven so that his share in the merchandise would correspond proportionately to the amount of money he contributed. Rather, if the capital decreases by one hundred gold coins, Shimon becomes personally obligated to pay one hundred, while the remaining merchandise is regarded as half in Reuven's possession and half in Shimon's. Since part of the merchandise is Shimon's responsibility, it is considered to be in his possession, because with respect to loss, property for which one bears responsibility is regarded as his. The same principle applies to interest law in Yoreh Deah: anything for which one bears responsibility is treated as a loan. Here too, for this reason Shimon is regarded as in possession of half, and therefore they divide equally. But where Reuven gave the money on terms that profit and loss be divided according to the proportion of each party's capital, the remaining merchandise is likewise regarded as belonging to Reuven and Shimon in those proportions, and they divide it according to their respective contributions.
