Reuven... We do not say... He swears only in order to be exempt. The category of those who swear and collect is limited to the cases enumerated by the Sages. Therefore he swears only regarding what is needed to exempt himself, as in Bava Metzia where each litigant swears that he owns no less than half, even though each claims the whole. Rambam derives a general rule from there in Hilchos To'en VeNit'an: whether the oath is light or severe, one swears only concerning the portion necessary for the ruling, not the whole formulation of his claim.
And Shimon must pay... As stated in Kesubos regarding two who placed money into a common purse. This is unlike the Ramah's cited opinion that the partner is not personally liable from his own assets. See siman 176 seif 6, which follows that view.
And if he claimed... and if not... As above. See Shach, seif katan 15.
Shimon claimed... if it was... This is why a third party depositary is believed, as Rashi explains in Gittin 64a, and as stated at the end of the third chapter of Sanhedrin: if he wished, he could have destroyed it.
And if there is no... perhaps... As stated in Bava Basra 174b regarding a dying person who says, "A maneh..." There is a presumption that a person does not..., but with another person there is no such presumption. The Gemara establishes the case where the claimant holds a document. Thus even here, since the second partner did not borrow personally, perhaps the arrangement was originally a collusion.
But... As above, and as stated that when one partner borrows, the other becomes bound as a partner. This is only where the claimant says he knows..., whereas on a doubtful claim by the plaintiff, even through gilgul the defendant does not swear. Tosafos derives this in Perek HaShoel from the Mishnah of a borrowed and rented animal. See siman 94 seif 2.
