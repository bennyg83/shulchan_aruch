The Tur explains that the Rambam follows his own view that where two partners contributed unequally, one one hundred and the other two hundred, profit and loss are nevertheless divided equally. Thus, if it were known that they lost five hundred, each partner's share of the loss would be two hundred fifty, and Shimon would have to pay fifty from his own funds. Here, however, where the amount of the loss is unknown, Reuven is not believed by oath to extract money from Shimon, but only to retain what is already in his possession. Further. This refers back to the previous law. The point is that not only need Shimon not pay fifty from his own pocket, but even more, Shimon takes fifty. Nevertheless, Reuven also takes fifty from the one hundred remaining in Shimon's possession, because Shimon admits, or it is otherwise known, that those funds belong to the partnership, so they are regarded as lying within the possession and chazakah of both partners.
