The correct reading is: Reuven, from whose principal there was a loss, etc.
The partners' oath concerning the amount that was lost, etc. The Beit Yosef writes that Reuven should not swear that five hundred was lost, since Shimon does not bear any share of that loss merely on the strength of Reuven's oath. He compares this to the ruling below in siman 138 concerning two people holding a garment, where each swears that he owns no less than half. Here too, Reuven should swear only that there was a loss and that, in any event, nothing remains for Shimon from his principal. In the Perishah there I wrote that the unqualified language of the Rambam and Tur implies that they hold he may swear that five hundred was lost. That case is different because both parties take oaths against each other. See there.
And Shimon must pay from his own property. It appears from the Tur that the Rambam follows his own view that where one partner contributed one hundred and the other two hundred, profit and loss are divided equally. Therefore, if it were known that the partnership lost five hundred, each partner's share of the loss would be two hundred fifty gold coins. Since Shimon originally contributed only two hundred, he would have to pay fifty from his own property. But because the amount of the loss is not known, Reuven is not believed merely by his oath to extract money from his partner. His oath can only enable him to retain what is already in his possession. This differs from the Raavad, who holds that losses are borne in proportion to each partner's capital, although regarding profits all agree that the division is equal. I explained the reason in the Derishah. It is also clear there and here that according to the Raavad, even if one accepted the view that losses are divided equally, Shimon still would not have to pay anything from his own pocket toward the loss, even if the loss were fully established.
