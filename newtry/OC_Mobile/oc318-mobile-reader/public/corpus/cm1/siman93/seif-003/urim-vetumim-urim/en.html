On an uncertain claim. See Tumim. I explained that the heirs certainly say, 'We suspect you.' If they do not suspect him, they effectively admit that he is trustworthy, and what purpose would an oath serve? Rather, the case is where they say that they suspect him, but do not claim that their father expressly instructed them that this was so. Even so, they may require him to swear, for what difference is there between the father and the heir? According to the view cited in Even HaEzer siman 96, that if the father died without requiring the oath, the heirs may not later require it because his failure to demand the oath during his lifetime shows that he waived it, one must say that here the case is where he granted an exemption from oaths but did not mention the heirs. There is then no waiver, because during his lifetime there was no possibility for the heirs to demand the oath. See there.
