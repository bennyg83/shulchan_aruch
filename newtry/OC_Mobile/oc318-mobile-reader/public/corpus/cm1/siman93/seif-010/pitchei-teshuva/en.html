And the debt remaining due to them from others is a known matter. See Responsa Zikhron Yosef, siman 4. The questioning rabbi explained this to mean that they know with certainty the amount of the debt and how much will reach each partner, such as where the debt is very secure or collateral has been given for it. He therefore wished to say, in the case before him where they had to litigate with the debtor and did not presently know how much would ultimately be adjudged in their favor, that this is not called a known matter and is not considered as though they had divided. The author discusses the matter at length to refute him, and concludes: "It appears to me clear and true, without any doubt or hesitation, that the Rambam, Tur, and Shulchan Aruch speak even where debts remain due to them from others and the amount of those debts is not presently known. It is nevertheless considered as though they divided, because the existence of the debt is known to both partners and they know how much each will take from every dinar collected. The poskim did not initially write 'known debts due from others,' but simply 'debts due from others,' which implies whether their amount is known or not, such as an iska or brokerage arrangement whose amount is not fixed, an oral loan whose amount they do not know, or a written loan on which payments were made and they do not know how much was paid. They then write, 'the debt is a known matter,' not 'the amount of the debt is known.' This explicitly implies that they speak even where the amount is presently unknown. What they mean by 'the debt is a known matter' is that the partners know that this debt remains part of the partnership and know how much each will take from whatever is collected. For that reason it is properly considered as though they divided. You can see this from the fact that the above poskim distinguish between debts and an item whose weight is unknown. They should have distinguished within debts themselves. If you ask what difference there is between a debt whose amount is unknown and an object whose quantity or weight is unknown, the answer is that debts cannot be divided in another manner. Thus, regarding the debt they have already made every division presently possible. Since they did not demand the partners' oath from one another, they have already waived it. By contrast, if an unmeasured object remains and they could divide it if they wished, each can say: I have not yet waived the partners' oath, because I could have divided that unmeasured object and did not do so in order to retain my claim to the partners' oath concerning it. Debts are different. Since they cannot divide them differently, even though the amounts are not fixed, so long as they know how much each will receive from every dinar collected, it is regarded as fixed and divided. This appears to me clear and true as practical halachah." See there.
