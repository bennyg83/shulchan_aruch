The Sema asks from Rav Hai Gaon's view above in siman 87 that one may impose hesset on a person by claiming that he is obligated to take a Torah oath. Even if the defendant admits the underlying allegation, he incurs only an oath, yet hesset is imposed regarding that obligation. The Sema answers that here the defendant has not yet been formally obligated by beit din to take the oath. See what I wrote above in siman 77 concerning Rav Hai Gaon's words. There is no difficulty, because there the claimant asserts a definite claim, "You owe me," as explained there, seif katan 23. The Shach writes that there is a simpler answer: the present oath is itself rabbinic, and we do not create one rabbinic enactment upon another. This does not appear correct according to the authorities who follow the Rif that a suspect creditor seeking to collect on his document loses it. The Rosh in HaKotev expressly explains the Rif that the rule against an enactment upon an enactment applies to hesset, but a Mishnah oath is treated like a Torah oath and can support an additional enactment. The case here concerns a Mishnah oath. Thus, according to Rambam's own approach, which follows his teacher the Rif, the Sema's question is legitimate, and the Shulchan Aruch should not have stated the rule without noting the dispute recorded above in siman 92.
