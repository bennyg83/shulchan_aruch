If a partner claims that from the outset they stipulated that he would be exempt from the partnership oath, he is believed and need not swear that such an exemption was granted. His admission would not create an immediate monetary liability. Moreover, unlike a later claim that the other party waived an oath, an initial stipulation exempting partners from the partnership oath is entirely normal business conduct and can stand as an independent plea. It therefore does not depend solely on a migo of denying the partnership. This remains so even according to Rambam, who generally does not use migo to exempt from an oath. See siman 296, seif 8. Taz also writes that a claim, "You waived the oath for me," can sometimes be believed even without migo, but in the present case an original partnership stipulation is stronger still.
