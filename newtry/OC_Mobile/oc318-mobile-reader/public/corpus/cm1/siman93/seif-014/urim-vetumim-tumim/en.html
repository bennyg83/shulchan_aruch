Shimon is not believed, etc. The Sema asks, later in siman 108, seif katan 29, from the Rosh's view that even when the document is physically held by the person who says it has been paid, he is not believed to prejudice another person's share. Here, however, the ruling is stated simply that if the document is in Shimon's possession he is believed even with respect to Reuven's share, although it is known to be partnership property. The Sema answers that here the document was written in Shimon's name. Even if it states that the money belongs to the partnership, because it is formally in his name and is also physically in his possession, he is believed. This is strained. Once the document itself identifies the debt as partnership property, why should the name in which it was written alter the rights of the other partner? Moreover, at the end of siman 71 we rule regarding a document stating that the debtor owes Reuven or Shimon, where both are partners in the debt, that one partner is not believed to say it was paid so as to destroy the other's share merely because the document is in his hand. The implication there is that if the document had been entrusted to the acknowledging partner under circumstances giving him control, the analysis would turn on that control rather than on whose name appears in the instrument. The principal answer is therefore the Bach's. In an active partnership, where the money was originally placed in Shimon's hands to transact business, lend to others, and receive repayments, Reuven trusted him from the outset regarding this very money. Shimon is therefore comparable to a shalish, a trusted stakeholder. He could have taken the money and destroyed or diverted it, and the partnership necessarily empowered him to administer the debt. When the document remains in his possession, he is believed as a shalish to say that it was paid. The later case in siman 108 is different. There the document reached an heir from his father, and brothers may each seize property from the other. No original act of trust by the co-owner placed the debt in that heir's hands. The Rosh therefore rules that he cannot prejudice the other's share merely because he happens to possess the document. See what I wrote at length above in siman 108. The distinction is not the name on the document, but the original fiduciary authority granted to the partner.
