That Levi did not pay. See Baer Heitev, who writes that the non Jew comes against Reuven by a false charge and one person is not seized for another's liability, etc. See Tumim, seif katan 14, who writes that his words are surprising, because Levi was negligent in paying the non Jew without witnesses. Moreover, we are instructing them from the outset how to proceed, so why do we not tell Levi not to pay the non Jew except before witnesses or against a receipt of discharge? See there. See Responsa Beit Ephraim, Choshen Mishpat, siman 6, who writes that a reason can be given for the Sema's view based on the Rama in siman 55 concerning a third party who returned a document, where the act is only indirect causation. Responsa Rashba explains that even if he handed the document to a non Jew he is exempt. Thus, Levi's payment without witnesses is no worse than handing over a paid document, for which he is exempt. Tumim follows his own position at the beginning of siman 55, where he holds that one who hands it to a non Jew is indeed liable. Beit Ephraim continues that in truth he does not understand Tumim's objection to the Sema. The Shulchan Aruch is not discussing litigation against Levi, but litigation with Shimon the partner. Even if Levi was negligent, where collection from Levi is impossible, for example because he has no assets, Shimon does not pay. Shimon says that he believes Levi paid and that the non Jew is falsely demanding payment a second time. Why should Levi's negligence affect him? Perhaps Tumim means that for this reason we should not believe that Levi paid the non Jew, as he concludes that we are effectively witnesses that he did not pay. Yet the beginning of his language does not imply this. His further question, that since we are explaining from the outset how they should act, why do we not instruct them accordingly, is likewise surprising, because the litigation is not with Levi. It may be that Levi is not before us at all and only Reuven and Shimon are litigating with one another. These are his words. See there.
