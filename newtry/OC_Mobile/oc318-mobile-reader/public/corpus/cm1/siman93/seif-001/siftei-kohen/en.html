Until he suspects him of two silver ma'ot. The Sema says that Rashi and the Ramah require both a claim of at least two silver ma'ot and an admission of part, paralleling a Torah oath. In my opinion Beit Yosef is correct that according to the Rif and Rosh no partial admission is required. The Ran states that this is also the view of Rabbi Yosef HaLevi and Maimonides, and Darkei Moshe understands Maimonides likewise. Maimonides' commentary to the Mishna also implies this. The Sema's attribution to the Ramah is incorrect, because the Tur below in siman 290:24 cites the Ramah explicitly as requiring no admission. The Ramah requires only a claim and denial of two silver ma'ot. Rabbi Yeshaya requires merely some claim and denial. Beit Yosef understood Rabbi Yeshaya this way, though he strained to reconcile it with the Gemara. In my commentary to the Tur I explained my father's account of Rabbi Yeshaya's reasoning. The Tur's preference for the first opinion need not mean that partial admission is required. It may merely reject Rabbi Yeshaya's view. Indeed, in siman 290 the Tur presents both positions without deciding. The Mechaber is difficult, because in Kesef Mishneh he explains Maimonides as not requiring partial admission, while below in siman 290:19 he rules that it is required. This requires study.
