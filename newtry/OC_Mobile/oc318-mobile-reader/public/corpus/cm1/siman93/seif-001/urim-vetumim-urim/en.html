Until he suspects, etc. The wording here implies that no actual admission is required, only that the claimant suspect him regarding an amount of two silver ma'ah. Beit Yosef and Ran understand Rambam this way, and Mechaber copied Rambam's language here. Yet below in siman 290:19 Mechaber requires a partial admission. Sema says Mechaber relies here on the later rule. Shach leaves the matter unresolved. See Tumim. I wrote at length that some form of partial admission is certainly required. Otherwise, even with a definite claim there would be no Torah oath, all the more with an uncertain claim. The Sages ordinarily patterned their enactments after Torah law. However, if property from the partnership or guardianship is still in the defendant's hands, even though there is no dispute about that property itself, possession of those assets counts as an admission. Here the discussion concerns relationships not yet fully wound up, so no additional admission is necessary. Below, siman 290, the discussion can involve a guardian who has already returned all known guardianship property. In that case a separate partial admission is required.
