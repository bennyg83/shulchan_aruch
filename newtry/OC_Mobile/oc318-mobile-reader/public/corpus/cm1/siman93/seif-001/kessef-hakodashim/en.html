Seif 1. (The partners.) Although the Sages imposed the oath of partners, perhaps this applies only where there is a plausible self-justification such as the idea that an ox eats from its threshing. If a group are not business partners at all, but a communal levy is allocated among the tavernkeepers of a city according to the volume of their sales, perhaps no comparable license to underreport exists and therefore no oath of partners applies. On the other hand, siman 163 provides for an oath where communal contributions are assessed according to wealth. If so, perhaps the same applies here, and even an individual can demand an oath because a person may rationalize reducing his share. If, for purposes of such a communal payment, the group gives an iska fund to someone from whom they borrow or with whom they transact for the communal need, perhaps each participant must provide at least one perutah as the worker's compensation, because they are not literally partners and compensation for labor should amount to a perutah, as the Nesivos Shalom writes. I wrote elsewhere that although the Gemara mentions a dinar, the fact that the compensation is paid in advance indicates that a perutah should suffice. Still, less than a perutah appears ineffective. In a communal arrangement of this type, it therefore seems that a perutah should be allocated for each participant. This requires careful study.
