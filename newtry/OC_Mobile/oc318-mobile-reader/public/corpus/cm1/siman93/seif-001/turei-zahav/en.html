(Shulchan Aruch, seif 1, an uncertain claim) The Tur discusses this at length and writes: “Although he does not say with certainty, but says, ‘I believe that you took some of mine.’” It appears that the novelty is that the claimant must at least genuinely suspect the defendant based on some indication, and therefore say, “I believe,” etc. But if he admits that he has no indication at all upon which to suspect him, he cannot require an oath, as stated in siman 87, seif 11, that he must swear because the claimant suspects him of that conduct. Sema, seif katan 3, asks from the beginning of siman 290, which implies that an oath is required only upon a certain claim. He answers that there the claim is for less than two silver ma’ot. If so, that passage must concern a hesset oath, for a stringent oath plainly requires two silver ma’ot as stated immediately afterward. This is difficult, because the ordinary oath of a guardian is a Mishnaic oath. Moreover, according to Sema, the Tur should have written that the guardian must render an account so that he will become liable to a stringent oath if the heirs make a definite claim of two silver ma’ot. It appears to me that the distinction at the beginning of siman 290 is taken by the Tur from Rif in Gittin, in the chapter HaNizakin. Rif rules there in accordance with Abba Shaul: a guardian appointed by beit din must swear; one appointed by the father of the orphans does not swear. This applies to an uncertain claim, but if the orphans grow up and make a definite claim and he denies it, he certainly swears. The Tur himself writes this afterward. There is therefore no difficulty. In siman 290 the Tur speaks of one appointed by the father, for he did not write, “when beit din appoints him,” but simply, “when he is appointed,” encompassing appointment by beit din or by the father. He therefore had to add that only a definite claim obligates the latter.
