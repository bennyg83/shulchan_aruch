In my view the principal ruling follows Rashi, Rabbeinu Yerucham, Ri in Tosafot, the Baal HaIttur, and the implication of Tosafot in Ketubot, that in an ordinary deed this does not invalidate the instrument after the fact. The Nimukei Yosef's objection from bills of divorce is answered by the distinction that only a get is invalid after the fact for such an appearance, whereas other deeds are not. This is also the view of the Ran, Mordechai, and Agudah. Ramah and the Tur's ruling below, allowing alteration of the stated place because of currency differences, also indicates that the deed is not automatically invalid after the fact. Otherwise they should have required wording that records both the place where the instructions were given and the place of signing so as to preserve validity.
