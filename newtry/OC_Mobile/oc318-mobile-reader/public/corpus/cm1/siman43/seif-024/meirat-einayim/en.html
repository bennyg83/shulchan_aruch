Since the amount of money in the document and in the receipt is the same. They need not be identical in every respect. The Tur and Mechaber write below in siman 71:9 that if a document contains a clause of credibility and the borrower produces a handwritten note of the lender stating that he received such-and-such sums on certain dates, while the lender says that note concerned another transaction, the law favors the borrower. The ruling here and the ruling there both come from responsa of the Rosh. Here the point is only to exclude a case where the receipt expressly states that it relates to a document in the lender's possession for a specified amount, and the document produced by the lender is for a different amount. In that case the receipt certainly does not refer to this document. They used to record the amount of the document in the receipt for the lender's benefit, so that the receipt could not later be used against unrelated transactions. Thus the Tur and Mechaber write below in siman 54:2 that if they do not remember the date written in the document, they should write in the receipt and sign that it concerns a document for such-and-such amount. That is the proper place for the laws of how receipts are written to protect both parties. Likewise, at the end of siman 53 the Mechaber writes, 'Perhaps he paid the one hundred and wrote him a receipt,' again describing the customary form of a receipt, not an indispensable requirement.
