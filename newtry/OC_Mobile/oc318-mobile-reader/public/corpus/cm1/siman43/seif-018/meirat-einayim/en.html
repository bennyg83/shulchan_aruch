They associate the time of writing, etc. This is also the reading in the copies of the Tur in our possession, both at the beginning and at the end: 'they associate the writing.' One must then explain that 'associate' means merely secondary association, like something subordinate to the principal matter. But this is difficult in the latter clause, which says that they associate the writing with the day on which they sign, apparently in every case, even where the writing and signing occurred on the same day. Why then call the writing merely subordinate rather than primary like the signing? And how would its subordinate status be evident when the document says, 'We made a kinyan from So-and-so and wrote on such-and-such day'? One must emend the text to read, 'or they associate the kinyan,' and since an emendation is needed, it appears preferable to emend the first clause to read, 'they associate the kinyan with that day,' as I wrote in Perishah. According to this reading, 'associate' retains its simple meaning, as the Maggid Mishneh writes: the document specifies that the kinyan specifically took place on that day, not the writing. But when they write the date of the day on which they are presently standing, they associate the writing with that day and not the kinyan. See there carefully.
Otherwise it appears false. Meaning, they must be careful about this. The document is not necessarily invalid because of it. So write Beit Yosef and Darkei Moshe, seif 15. See there, however, where he cites many opinions that disagree and hold the document invalid.
However, if they wrote without qualification, 'in such-and-such place,' etc. Rabbeinu Yerucham concludes there that it does not appear false even if the testimony was delivered in another place, because without specification the statement refers only to the writing. This gloss was printed here out of place, for the discussion here concerns time rather than place. Its primary location is nearby, at the end of seif 20, on the Mechaber's statement that when they write the place of writing they say, 'So-and-so said to us, and we wrote in such-and-such place.' Meaning, he made the acknowledgment or obligation by kinyan, as stated in the next clause. The gloss teaches that even if they do not state this explicitly but merely write, 'In such-and-such place we made a kinyan from So-and-so,' while the kinyan did not actually occur in that place, it still does not appear false. We interpret 'we made a kinyan from So-and-so' as though it said, 'and we made a kinyan,' with the conjunction, meaning: in such-and-such place we wrote the document, and beforehand we made a kinyan from So-and-so. It does not mean that the kinyan occurred in that same place. This is what Rabbeinu Yerucham means when he says, 'even if it was delivered in another place,' meaning that the testimony was delivered there through an acknowledgment or kinyan. His statement that without qualification it refers only to the writing means that 'in such-and-such place' modifies only the writing and not the delivery of the testimony. Rama placed the gloss here because the law of place teaches the parallel rule concerning time, for example if they write, 'on such-and-such day.' Although Darkei Moshe also cites Rabbeinu Yerucham here in the discussion of writing the day of writing rather than the day of the kinyan, it nevertheless appears as I have explained.
It does not appear false. Ran in chapter HaZorek writes that if they postdate the document and write it later, we are not concerned that the person may not have been in that city at the later date written in the document, for we do not suspect a merely possible appearance of falsehood. So writes Darkei Moshe, seif 17.
