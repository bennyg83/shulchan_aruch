Every document. The Sema and later authorities question the Tur because the Tur accepts the principle that a person's witnesses can acquire for him in his absence. Bach answers that this principle applies where the borrower leaves the document with the witnesses for the lender or leaves it without specifying delivery to himself. But if he expressly tells them to give it back to him, the principle does not apply. In a found document, the very fact that it was lost creates concern that perhaps he told the witnesses to return it to him. The Shach objects: according to Abaye, who says witnesses may sign for a borrower even without the lender because the borrower's witnesses acquire for the lender, how could they have written it if the borrower told them to give it to himself? There would be concern that the document was written in Nisan but not delivered until Tishrei. The answer is that Abaye himself does not ordinarily suspect that the witnesses improperly wrote under such circumstances. But the Tur can agree with Rav Ashi that when a document has actually fallen, we do consider the possibility of an irregular writing. Thus the Tur can accept the general principle while still treating a found document as weakened by concern that the witnesses were instructed to return it to the borrower. This is clear.
