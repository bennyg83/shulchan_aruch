Except from unencumbered property, etc. The document is torn up, or they write on the document itself that it may not be collected from encumbered property at all, only from unencumbered property. They do not leave the document in his possession without notation, lest he go to another beit din that does not know of its defect and collect from encumbered property. This is obvious, Shach.
As a decree, etc. Even if he says that beit din should write on it that he may collect only from the later date, nevertheless they penalized him once the document came before beit din with a defect in it, Sema and Shach. Shach concludes that if he himself wrote on the document, before it came before beit din, that this document may be collected only from the later date, it is valid. See Tumim, where I brought support for this from the Tur. I nevertheless concluded that on the very day when he writes this notation, the lien begins from that day, and therefore it must specifically be written through witnesses and not in his own handwriting, lest we suspect that he later antedated the notation after the lien had already been waived. See there for a proof from the Gemara.
Entirely invalid. He is believed to say, "I paid." Whether he is believed to say, "There never was such a debt," meaning that the witnesses delivered the document to the borrower and now the borrower claims, "You never lent me, and it fell from me," or the like, Sema holds that he is not believed, whereas Shach holds that he is believed. See Tumim, where I brought proof for Sema, and his view is primary. If the borrower admits that he owes the money, everyone agrees that he must pay. The Sages did not impose a penalty that would cause his money to be forfeited, Shach, and this is obvious.
