Only from unencumbered property. Even if beit din takes possession of the note and prevents collection from the original date, or writes upon it that it may be collected only from the present date, it still cannot be collected from encumbered property. This is a penalty, because when first written it was capable of being used to collect from the earlier date, as the Sema explains. Although this is not explicit in the Gemara or authorities, which may discuss a document whose surface leaves no room for an added notation, the Sema's practical ruling is reasonable. It resembles the rule in siman 45 that once a document's defect has appeared before beit din, it can no longer be repaired. It follows that if the creditor himself wrote upon it, before it was presented to beit din, that collection may begin only from the later date, the document is valid. This appears correct.
Some say that it is entirely invalid. This is the opinion of Ri and those who follow him in the Tur. The Sema claims that “entirely invalid” does not mean utterly void. According to him, the borrower cannot deny that the loan ever occurred and is deemed a proven liar on the strength of the document, but he may claim payment. This is not established by the Tur. Although Rabbeinu Yonah takes that position in siman 45, Ri cited here does not. On the contrary, Tosafot in Bava Batra indicate that Ri regards an antedated document as entirely void. The Ribash likewise understands Ri this way, as does the Bach and Responsa Torat Emet. The language “invalid for collection even from unencumbered property” naturally means that the instrument is wholly invalid. Terumat HaDeshen similarly writes that according to the Rosh and Tosafot such a note is like mere scrap paper. This also appears to be the Rama's understanding.
It is entirely invalid even though the witnesses are qualified. This applies in the manner explained below. It is unlike Gidulei Terumah, who understood the Rosh's reason to be that the witnesses themselves are disqualified. That is a serious error. If the witnesses were disqualified, all would agree that the instrument is void, and there would be no dispute to cite. Moreover, the Gemara's discussion of whether it may collect from the later date could not arise. The Rosh expressly discusses a case in which the witnesses remain qualified, yet Tosafot rule that an antedated promissory note is entirely invalid. This is very clear.
