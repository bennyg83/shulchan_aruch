And if he claimed, "This never happened." The Sema writes that he becomes established as a false denier. The Shach holds that because the early dated document is completely invalid, it is mere pottery and cannot itself establish false denial. However, if the witnesses are present, remember the loan, and are themselves qualified, then the borrower certainly becomes a false denier when he says the loan never occurred. The invalidity of the document does not erase their oral testimony. This siman deals with a case where the witnesses remain qualified, because if they deliberately predated the document they themselves would be disqualified and the document would be void according to everyone. Knesset HaGedolah cites Mahara Sasson accordingly. Tosafot asks how we know a document is early dated and suggests a case where the signatures cannot otherwise be validated. According to Rambam, where signatures cannot be validated from elsewhere the witnesses must remember the loan. If they do, the borrower cannot deny the loan. Yet as I wrote in siman 46, remembering merely that they signed at the borrower's direction can sometimes count like remembering the loan. Here, because the document itself was early dated and invalid, remembering only that they signed it at his instruction is not enough to defeat a total denial. They must remember the loan itself.
