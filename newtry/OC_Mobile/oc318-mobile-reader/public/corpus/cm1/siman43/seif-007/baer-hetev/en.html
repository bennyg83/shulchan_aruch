Unencumbered property. Even if beit din takes custody of the document and refuses to allow collection from the original date, or writes on it that collection may occur only from the present date, the creditor still may not collect from encumbered property. This is a penalty, because from the outset the document was fit to support collection from the earlier date. Thus writes the Sema. The Shach writes that this is reasonable. Once the document's defect has been exposed before beit din, it can no longer be repaired. Accordingly, if before the document was ever shown to beit din the lender himself wrote on it that it was not given for collection from the original date, it appears valid.
Entirely. The Tur implies that "invalid entirely" does not mean void for every purpose. Rather, if the debtor claims that the loan never occurred, this document can establish him as a false denier; but if he claims that he paid, he is believed. Thus writes the Sema in the name of Maharshal. The Shach writes that nothing of the sort is proved. Rather, the document is like a shard of pottery and is completely invalid, even though the witnesses themselves are fit, subject to the qualifications explained later, contrary to the Gidulei Terumah. Nevertheless, all agree that one may collect from the borrower himself if he admits the debt or if there are independent witnesses to it. Responsa Maharshach, part 2, siman 158, and Responsa Mahara Sasson, siman 145, state likewise. See also the gloss of the Chacham Tzvi to the Taz, who accepts Maharshal's view that the document is not entirely void.
