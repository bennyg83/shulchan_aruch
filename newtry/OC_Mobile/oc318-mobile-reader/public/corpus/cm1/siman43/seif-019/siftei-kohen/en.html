They should not say, etc. This ruling is not convincing. The Beit Yosef and Baal HaTerumot infer that if the witnesses know a kinyan occurred in Tishrei but do not know the date, they may not date the deed from the beginning of Cheshvan, because that would appear false. But the Gemara can be read differently: if they merely think the kinyan may have occurred at the beginning of Tishrei, they should not write that date while uncertain, even intending to deliver the deed only if later verified, because at the time of writing it appears false, just as an authentication written before the witnesses identify their signatures is invalid. The Rosh omitted the phrase about appearing false because in ordinary nonjudicial documents he follows the view that this concern is disregarded. The Baal HaIttur likewise rules that since we generally do not fear an appearance of falsehood, they may use a date they know to be certainly after the kinyan. I therefore see clear permission, where they know the kinyan was made in Tishrei but not on which day, to write the deed from the beginning of Cheshvan. Rashba's commentary, however, appears to follow the Tur and Baal HaTerumot, allowing only the actual day of kinyan or the actual day of writing. The matter therefore requires study.
