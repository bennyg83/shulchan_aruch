Because this appears false. See Tumim. Although ordinarily we are not concerned with appearance of falsehood in this way, here there is a specific concern that the public will view the witnesses as plotting or false witnesses. Perhaps on the date written in the deed, which is neither the kinyan date nor the writing date, the witnesses or parties were not even in the location stated. People would then regard them as liars. See there for reconciliation of Rosh. Some authorities say that if such a deed was nevertheless written, it is invalid after the fact. The Gemara appears to support this. See Tumim for proof from Tosafot and Rashi in Rosh Hashanah. Sema asks why the document is not already invalid for the concern stated above in seif 12 regarding a postdated deed. He answers either that an after-acquired-property clause was written, or that the deed stated expressly, “We postdated it.” The first answer, that it contains an after-acquired-property clause, is correct, subject to the uncertainty I noted above whether such a clause is always required. The second answer is difficult. If it expressly states that it was postdated, what appearance of falsehood remains? Responsa Maimoniyot already raised this against Baal HaIttur. Under the ordinary explanations of the authorities, such a notation removes the concern, contrary to Sema.
