Even postdated documents are invalid. Below in siman 239 the reason is explained. The seller may not know that they postdated the document, and he may later repurchase the field from the buyer and receive a deed from him. The buyer could then produce this first deed, whose date is later than the seller's repurchase, and claim, "I later bought it back from you." See there. See Tumim, where I discussed this at length in explaining Rashbam, and why here we do not use the device employed with a loan, where we say that if he repays he should take an undated receipt. Here too, when he repurchases the property, why should he not take an undated deed of sale from the buyer? See there.
Postdated. Shach writes that the same applies to a gift, following the reason for the law. See Tumim, where I wrote that there is room to question this. I also concluded that if the deed of sale does not state that the seller already received the purchase money from the buyer for the field, as below in siman 238, it is possible that a postdated deed is valid. There is then no concern that he will repurchase the property and the first buyer will later come with the postdated deed, because that buyer would still have to pay the purchase price, and there is therefore no major risk of loss. See there.
They are invalid. Shach writes that if they informed the seller that they postdated the document, there is no longer any concern. Therefore, when a postdated deed of sale comes before us, he says that we presume that the witnesses acted properly and informed the seller. See Tumim, where I wrote that this does not appear from the plain meaning of the Gemara and the poskim. Although the first ruling may be correct, that if they actually informed the seller it is valid, nevertheless where we do not know that this occurred, the document should be invalidated. The presumption that they acted properly does not apply here, because perhaps they forgot or erred in the law. See there.
They should not postdate, etc. Unless it is impossible to do otherwise, such as where they forgot the date of the kinyan, Shach.
