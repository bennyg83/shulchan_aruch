But with deeds of sale. See Shach, who adds deeds of gift. Although a gift deed ordinarily contains no responsibility and serves principally to prove title, and Mechaber earlier validates an antedated deed against a total denial, there are practical differences. If the witnesses intentionally antedated it they themselves can become disqualified. More fundamentally, where the instrument is a constitutive deed, once the Sages invalidated a deed that can create serious legal error it is treated like worthless paper and cannot effect the acquisition. Tosafot in Gittin make the same distinction regarding documents produced in gentile courts: even if such an instrument can have evidentiary value against a denial, it may be incapable of creating the transfer itself. A deed written on erasable material is different where proper delivery witnesses prevent any realistic fraud and the Sages did not strip it of all constitutive force. This may also explain Mechaber's later case where the holder is required to swear that the document was not antedated. The document can retain limited evidentiary force while lacking the full legal force of a constitutive instrument.
