The Sema writes that between the end of Pesach and the beginning of Shavuot there are forty two days for residents of Eretz Yisrael, and for those outside Eretz Yisrael, who observe an additional festival day because of doubt, forty one days remain. The Mechaber's phrase "most of the days" is not exact; as soon as more than half the interval has passed, the obligation takes effect, analogous to the expressions "before Shabbat" and "after Shabbat." The Ran likewise writes explicitly that half is sufficient. Thus, for residents of Eretz Yisrael the obligation takes effect immediately after twenty one days, and outside Eretz Yisrael immediately after twenty and a half days. Likewise, if a document says "until after Sukkot," he has three months. The Shach challenges this and writes that it is unreasonable to grant such a long period. Rather, Rabbeinu Chananel's explanation appears correct: "after the festival" means fifteen days after the festival, without distinction among the festivals. It is well known that Rabbeinu Chananel's interpretations are received traditions from the sages of the Gemara, since he was an early Gaon, as the Rosh and other authorities write in many places. The Shach also finds a source for this in the first chapter of Taanit. He further writes that the Mechaber's phrase "most of the days" is precise, contrary to the Sema. The Sema's calculation is also imprecise: from the end of Pesach until the beginning of Shavuot there are forty three days in Eretz Yisrael and forty two outside it. Thus twenty one days is not a majority anywhere, and in Eretz Yisrael it is not even half.
