Until most of the days have passed. This is as though he said 'after Shabbat.' We rule that through the third day is called 'after Shabbat,' while from the fourth day onward is considered before the next Shabbat. So too if he says 'until after Pesach.' Between the end of Pesach and the beginning of Shavuot there are forty-two days in Eretz Yisrael, and for us outside Eretz Yisrael, where each festival day is observed for two days because of doubt, there remain forty-one days. The phrase 'until most of the days have passed' is not exact if it means a strict majority. Rather, as soon as half has passed the obligation begins, just as with the rules of before and after Shabbat. Ran explicitly says 'from half.' Thus for residents of Eretz Yisrael the obligation begins immediately after twenty-one days, and outside Eretz Yisrael immediately after twenty and one-half days. See also Hagahot Darkei Moshe to siman 42:15. The same source states that if the document says 'until after Sukkot,' he has three months.
Up to here is day twenty-one. This has nothing to do with the preceding law. The Mechaber divided the four sections of the Shulchan Aruch into thirty portions, intending that one portion be studied and reviewed each day so that within thirty days one would review all four Turim and become proficient in them, as he writes in his introduction. Thus he marked here the end of the twenty-first day's portion. In an old Venice printing I saw the endpoints of all thirty daily sections printed. Since the endpoints of the other sections are absent from the editions before us but this one remains, it appears a later printer misunderstood it as connected with the preceding discussion, as though 'most of the days between Pesach and Shavuot' meant the end of twenty-one days. According to that reading the word 'here' would be erroneous and it should say 'until day twenty-one.' In my view this is clearly a printing error in transmission, for otherwise there is no reason to state the count here.
