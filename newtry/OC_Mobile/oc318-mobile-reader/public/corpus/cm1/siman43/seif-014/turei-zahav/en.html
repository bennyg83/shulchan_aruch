(Further there: but postdated documents are valid, etc., through “and in chapter 23 he did not write so.”) I found a sound reconciliation. The Rambam understands the Gemara’s question, “If you think after-acquired property, why are postdated documents valid? It is after-acquired property,” to mean that every postdated document is equivalent to an express grant of a lien on after-acquired property. By choosing a later date, the obligor indicates that property acquired before that date will also be subject to collection. This is a powerful inference. Thus every postdated document, according to the Rambam, contains an express after-acquired-property clause. This explains why the Mishnah states without qualification that postdated documents are valid rather than invalidating those that omit such a clause. For the Rambam, all postdated documents are necessarily treated as containing it.
