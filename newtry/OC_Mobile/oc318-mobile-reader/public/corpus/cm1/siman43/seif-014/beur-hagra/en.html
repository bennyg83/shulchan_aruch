And some say, etc. We rule that the holder of the document has the weaker hand, but we do not presume witnesses to be disqualified. Therefore this applies specifically to an authenticated document. If it is not authenticated, it is invalid even where no one contests it. Baal HaTerumot.
(Collected note) There is one who, etc. Since the document contains a defect, as stated in HaKotev: "we weaken the document based on his statement." The Geonim explain that the creditor may collect only with an oath. (End of collected note.)
(Collected note) Specifically, etc. In such a case the debtor could even claim that it was a trust document, as stated at the beginning of siman 46. Although the witnesses are not believed to say so, even when their handwriting is not verified elsewhere, as stated in Ketubot 19a, all the more so here, where according to all opinions the witnesses are believed regarding this point, as stated in seif 8. See Beer HaGolah. So writes Baal HaMaor, explaining that we validate the document only so as not to disqualify the witnesses and preserve their chazakah. In such a case, however, that rationale does not apply. (End of collected note.)
