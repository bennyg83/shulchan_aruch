And we do not suspect that it was antedated, etc. The reason is that every written and signed document is maintained in its presumption of validity and is treated as though its testimony had been examined in beit din. See the Gemara and Perishah.
And on Sunday, etc. The Tur writes, 'perhaps it was written on Monday.' The Mechaber follows Rambam's wording. In Perishah I wrote that there is no practical legal difference between them; the Tur merely used the ordinary case.
If he says, 'Swear to me,' etc. But if he does not demand it, beit din does not make the lender swear on its own initiative. This teaches that although a lender is made to swear only when the borrower says, 'Swear to me that I did not repay you,' because every promissory note stands to be repaid, and not where the borrower claims, 'It is forged,' 'It is an accommodation document,' or 'It contains interest,' as the Tur and Mechaber write in siman 82, seif 10, nevertheless he is made to swear where the borrower claims that the document is antedated, for the document necessarily was not written at its stated time if that date fell on Shabbos. See Perishah.
