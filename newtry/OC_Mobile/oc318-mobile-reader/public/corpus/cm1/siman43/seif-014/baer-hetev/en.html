He said. Beit din does not raise on his behalf a demand that the lender swear. The point is that although a lender is ordinarily required to swear only when the borrower specifically demands an oath that he did not repay, because every debt document is expected eventually to be paid, no such oath is imposed merely on a claim that the document is forged, a document of accommodation, or contains interest, as stated in siman 82, seif 10. Nevertheless, where the borrower claims that the document is antedated, the lender must swear, because the document clearly was not written on its stated date, since that date fell on Shabbat. Sema. The Shach writes that this implies that where no defect appears before us, the borrower cannot require the lender to swear that the document is not antedated, since this is no stronger than a claim of "I wrote it intending to borrow but never borrowed" or that it was an accommodation document. One cannot ask: if the borrower demands an oath that he did not repay, the lender must already swear that; and if he did not repay, he should simply pay. The answer may be that the borrower claims to have paid but does not wish to demand an oath on payment because he suspects the lender may rationalize that oath, while he knows the lender will not swear falsely that the document is not antedated. Alternatively, the borrower may claim that he never borrowed, in which case a valid document would not ordinarily require the lender to swear. He therefore alleges that the document is antedated, and because the document contains an evident defect the lender must swear. This is difficult, however, because the Mechaber ruled in seif 7 that an antedated document still collects from unencumbered property. Why then is an oath required? It is forced to say that the discussion concerns purchasers, but against purchasers the lender must swear in any event. It therefore appears that the borrower claims the witnesses intentionally antedated the document and are disqualified because they knowingly signed falsely. Shach.
