Seif 12. Postdated promissory notes are valid. Whether such a document may be collected even from the borrower himself, and the status of postdated sale documents written with the seller's consent, are discussed in my responsum on Orach Chaim, laws of Pesach. Seif 17. Some say that the rule validating a document when the parties remained engaged in the same matter applies only in a particular way. See Sema's reason. According to it, the same should apply to a ketubah. If the chuppah and kinyan occurred only at night while the ketubah was written during the day, the ketubah is invalid even if the parties remained occupied with the same matter. The husband received nothing from the wife corresponding to what he adds to her ketubah. His obligation arises only through the chuppah, when he brings her under the chuppah and thereby becomes obligated in the ketubah. Although Shach says Rambam implies there is no such distinction, the matter remains at least a doubt in law, and money cannot be collected on a doubt, especially against the Shulchan Aruch's ruling. This is therefore comparable to a promissory note or kinyan document, where remaining occupied with the same matter does not cure an earlier date. It differs from a loan document, because the debt begins when the money is given during the day. Even according to the view in Gittin 18 that a ketubah written by day and signed at night is valid because it resembles an act of beit din, Rashi expressly limits that ruling to a case in which the chuppah took place during the day. If the chuppah occurred only at night, it does not help. The same is true according to the Gemara's later explanation that the validity rests on their remaining occupied with the same matter. The chuppah must still have occurred during the day, making the case resemble a loan document. If the chuppah took place only at night, continued discussion does not help and the ketubah is invalid. This appears correct as practical law. Beit Shmuel in siman 66 simply states that a ketubah written by day is valid, and it is surprising that he does not cite the views Shach brings here in seif katan 34. See what I wrote in Even HaEzer 66.
