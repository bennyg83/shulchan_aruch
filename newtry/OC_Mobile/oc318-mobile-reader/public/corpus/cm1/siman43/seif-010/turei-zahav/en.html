(In the Tur: it does not appear proper to distinguish, etc.) The Tur’s words are astonishing. In Eizehu Neshekh 72, the Gemara asks why Rabbi Yohanan invalidates an antedated document because of a decree lest collection occur from the first date rather than because it was not authorized to be written. Rabbi Yohanan’s reason is necessary, for if invalidity rested on lack of authorization, a document written for a loan intended in Nisan but actually advanced in Tishrei should be valid for collection from Tishrei onward. The decree invalidates even that. According to the Ramah, who validates such a document, it is excluded from the Mishnah’s rule that antedated loan documents are invalid, so no difficulty arises. But according to the Tur, such a case must be included in the Mishnah. Otherwise the Mishnah should have taught the greater novelty that even this document, which was authorized to be written, is invalid. Rabbi Yohanan was therefore compelled to invoke the decree. This is a major difficulty on the Tur and I found no satisfactory answer. The gloss suggests that according to the Tur, the Gemara asks: since Rabbi Yohanan’s purpose is only to establish the Mishnah according to the Sages, why assume a decree that erases the distinction between a document written in Nisan for a later loan and one deliberately antedated? Let the reason be lack of authorization, thereby validating the former. The answer is that lack of authorization cannot explain total invalidity, since it was authorized from the later date and could collect from then. The reason must be the decree, and once the decree is adopted no distinction remains.
