The witnesses' signatures. If the corrective notation appears in the last line, one might at first connect its validity to the dispute in siman 45 whether correction language can validate a document even if not written on the same day. But even according to the more lenient view, a notation in the final line remains problematic if the lender could have written it himself merely to cure the defect. Shach's earlier statement that the lender may write a correction before the document reaches beit din must therefore presuppose independent witnesses to the later effective date. Otherwise who proves when the second period begins? If the witnesses to the document themselves attest the later date within the body before their signatures, then their signatures support that date. This is why the correction must appear before the witnesses' signatures and, in practice, before the final line in a manner that cannot simply be added later. If the fact of antedating is known only from the lender, he may rely on a migo as explained above. But once the document is independently known to be antedated, a self-serving last-line notation is insufficient without supporting witnesses.
