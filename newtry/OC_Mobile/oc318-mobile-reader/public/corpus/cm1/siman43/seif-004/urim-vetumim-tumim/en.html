And he shall not collect with an antedated document, etc. Shach, seif katan 11, writes that if the creditor himself writes on the document, before it is produced in beit din, that it may not be used to collect except from the present date, the document is thereby validated. This appears supported by Tur below, siman 45, citing Rabbenu Yonah. A document with two blank lines between text and signatures is invalid because the holder could add material. Nevertheless, Rabbenu Yonah says the debtor cannot deny the underlying transaction entirely, since the document could have been validated by filling the blank space with signatures of relatives. Rabbenu Yonah adds that he would say the same regarding an antedated loan document. The reasoning seems to be that if an available correction can preserve the paper, it is not treated as meaningless parchment for purposes of denying the transaction. This supports Shach. However, the correction must operate from the day it is actually made. Suppose the loan occurred on the second of Tishrei but the document was antedated to Nisan. If the creditor does not write the limiting notation until the third of Tishrei, he cannot say, "The lien shall begin from the second." Until the notation was written, the paper was still an invalid antedated instrument for lien purposes. The creditor's own later handwritten declaration cannot create retroactive creditor rights from yesterday. He can validate the document only from the date of the corrective notation itself. Indeed, below in siman 45, seif 15, where an invalid blank was filled with relatives, the lien begins only from the time the defect was cured. The same applies here. Moreover, the creditor's own handwriting may not be sufficient where priority against others is at stake. He could have written the notation today and falsely dated it yesterday. Therefore, to create a lien date enforceable against third parties, the corrective notation should be made before witnesses or by beit din. From the date those witnesses verify the correction, the document can operate prospectively. This resolves a difficulty from Bava Metzia 15a. R. Yochanan says a loan document that was paid cannot be reused for a second loan because its original lien was extinguished. The Gemara asks: if the second loan is on a later day, the document is in any event antedated. Why do we need the additional reason that the lien was extinguished? If Shach's corrective notation solved antedating freely, one could answer that the creditor writes on the old document, "This instrument shall collect only from the date of the second loan." Then antedating is cured, while the problem of extinguished lien remains. The answer is that if the creditor himself writes the correction, he cannot retroactively establish a lien from the second-loan date unless the correction is made then and properly witnessed. If witnesses are present and write the new effective date, they are effectively creating a new loan lien from that moment. At that point the problem of the old extinguished lien disappears as well, because the witnesses are creating fresh documentary force. Thus the Gemara correctly says antedating independently prevents reuse. This also clarifies Rabbenu Yonah's rule. The reason an antedated document is not worthless for purposes of "this never happened" is not that the creditor can single-handedly resurrect its original priority. It is that the document is capable of being regularized prospectively through a witnessed correction. That limited possibility is enough to preserve its evidentiary force regarding the underlying loan. Accordingly, Shach's ruling should be qualified: the creditor can limit the document prospectively, but third-party lien consequences begin only from the reliably established date of correction.
