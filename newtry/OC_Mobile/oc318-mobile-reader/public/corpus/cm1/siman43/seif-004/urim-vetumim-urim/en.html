If the weekday was omitted, it is valid. Sema says the omission is evident from the document itself. I do not understand why this entire rule matters. Even if the document omitted the weekday designation altogether, what difference would it make? Omitting the weekday count does not invalidate ordinary documents, as is clear from the laws of divorce, all the more so in monetary documents. Rosh's responsum actually involved a document dated the twenty-first but said it was written on Wednesday, although the twentieth was Wednesday. Rosh discussed whether the discrepancy should be treated as proving that the intended date was the twenty-first, because the written weekday did not match the twentieth, or whether the weekday wording should be treated independently. His conclusion addressed that conflict. Therefore the Shulchan Aruch must also be read as involving such a contradiction: the document gives a calendar date known to fall on another weekday, but its weekday phrase shows the mistake. Then the document is valid because the error is apparent from within it. The fact that it did not explicitly say “Wednesday of the week” does not matter; ordinary usage itself supplies that meaning.
