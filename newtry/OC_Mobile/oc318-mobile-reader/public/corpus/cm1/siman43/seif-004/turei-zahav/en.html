(Seif 15: until he brings proof.) The Sema asks that even proof should not suffice, because payment and collusion should still be suspected. He answers that the document was not impaired by a fall, since the holder himself found it. I am astonished. Although Rashba recognizes that reasoning where one finds in his own courtyard a document that fell there before reaching another person, the present case cannot concern that. Otherwise there would be no basis to suspect it failed to reach the holder on the stated date, since such suspicion arises only after impairment through a fall. The original question is therefore no question. Tosafot and the Rosh at the beginning of the second chapter of Gittin explain that generally we do not suspect antedating unless the document was impaired by a fall. Their principal purpose is to reject Rashi’s implication that a woman seeking to collect from purchasers with her divorce document must always prove when it reached her hand. Rather, proof is required only where a fall created concern for antedating and there is no payment issue, only possible collusion, as where purchasers know the debt was not paid or with a deed of sale containing responsibility. In ordinary cases no such concern exists.
