(Collected note) And some say the witnesses, etc. Yerushalmi, last chapter of Sheviit: antedated promissory notes are invalid because they strengthen the creditor's position; postdated documents are valid because they weaken his position. Who informs us? Shimon bar Va in the name of Rabbi Yochanan: those signed on the document. But did Reish Lakish not say that the statements of signatories on a document are treated as testimony examined in beit din? There, they say, "we never signed at all"; here they say, "we signed regarding this, but not regarding that." Rav Zerachyah at the end of chapter 1 of Rosh Hashana. Ramban disagrees and establishes the Yerushalmi where their handwriting is not verified elsewhere, so they are believed through a migo, as stated in chapter 2 of Ketubot. His words require study. If so, what is the Yerushalmi's question from Reish Lakish, "the signatories' statements are treated, etc."? When their handwriting is not verified elsewhere, Ketubot 18b explicitly says they are believed. The question should instead have been that they make themselves wicked, as the Gemara says there. See above, beginning of siman 29. (End of collected note.)
(Collected note) And some say, etc. The Rav's wording here implies that the dispute concerns whether the witnesses themselves become disqualified when the document is known to be antedated. So the Sema explains. The Tur, however, implies that the dispute concerns whether the witnesses are believed to testify that it is antedated and thereby invalidate the document, which also appears from the Yerushalmi cited by Rav Zerachyah, "who informs us?" The Tur's words, "and how is it known that it is antedated? Some say, etc.," point that way. But from the continuation, "according to Ri, etc.," based on Tosafot in Bava Metzia at the end of s.v. "Shtar," and the Beit Yosef, the issue seems to concern whether the witnesses themselves are disqualified. One must say the Tur's words "therefore they are believed to say, etc." mean that they are believed in such a way that they themselves are not disqualified. Just as they are believed to invalidate the document because "we signed regarding this and not that," so too regarding their own status. On this the Tur writes, "but according to Ri, etc." This is also the Rav's meaning. It is true that the same dispute also affects whether the document itself may be invalidated, which is the disagreement between Rav Zerachyah and Ramban. (End of collected note.)
