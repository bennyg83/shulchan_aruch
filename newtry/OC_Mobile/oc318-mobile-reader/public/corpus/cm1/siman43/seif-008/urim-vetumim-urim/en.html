Even though creating such a document without kinyan is prohibited, if they did so they are not automatically disqualified. Perhaps they mistakenly believed the money was being advanced, while in truth it was merely an accommodation document, or they made another type of common mistake. In Tumim I conclude that even where their signatures are independently authenticated and even where antedating is otherwise established, whenever the error can reasonably be attributed to a common mistake we preserve the witnesses' presumption of fitness according to all opinions. All the more so if other witnesses say they acted under mortal duress. If the document witnesses themselves seek to justify their conduct when their handwriting is independently known, see Tumim for the limitations.

And some disagree. See Tumim. This view holds that even regarding a common mistake, where the signatures are independently authenticated the witnesses are not believed to explain away the defect. Although siman 29 states without dispute that witnesses may correct themselves concerning a matter in which people commonly err without violating the rule against retraction, that applies to oral testimony. Here they seek through oral explanation to undermine a written instrument already established independently. That is different. If their handwriting is not independently authenticated and they say they erred in the regnal year or another commonly mistaken detail, they are believed through migo. If they say, “We wrote in Nisan expecting the loan then, but the loan did not occur until Tishrei,” see Tumim, where I remain uncertain. Although such conduct may not itself disqualify them, they may nevertheless be unable to invalidate the deed because the act resembled writing an accommodation instrument. Likewise, if they say they acted under mortal duress, the result depends on the two explanations discussed below in siman 46 concerning whether a protest may be written. If other witnesses testify that the deed witnesses acted under mortal duress, and the signatures are independently authenticated, the case becomes two against two: the document is neither torn nor collected upon.
