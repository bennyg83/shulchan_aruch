Intentionally. Nimukei Yosef gives an example where witnesses wrote a loan document for the borrower without the lender present, but the borrower did not actually take the loan until later. Although witnesses ordinarily may write such a document for the borrower only when it is a document of acquisition, one who improperly did so is not thereby disqualified as a witness. The students of the Rashba give another example: at the time the witnesses signed, they mistakenly thought the loan had already taken place, but afterward they actually saw the lender give the borrower the money corresponding to the document and then testified to that fact. Thus the Sema.
Common. The Sema writes that Rivash, siman 382, discusses witnesses who should have written thirty-four years but wrote four years and then said they had erred. According to Darkei Moshe, Rivash ruled that they are not believed because people do not commonly make such an error. The Shach writes that this citation is astonishing because the Rivash does not say this, and the Beit Yosef explicitly validates the witnesses in such a case. See there.
They disagree. The Shach writes that Rama's wording implies that according to the dissenting opinion, even regarding a type of error witnesses commonly make they are not believed to say they erred. That is incorrect, because all opinions agree in such a case. The Mechaber himself writes in siman 29, seif 1: "In every error that witnesses commonly make, they themselves are believed to say they erred, and this is not considered retracting testimony." He records no dissent there and Rama makes no gloss. Rama's wording here therefore requires study. One must read the phrase "and some disagree" as referring only to the opening portion of his statement.
