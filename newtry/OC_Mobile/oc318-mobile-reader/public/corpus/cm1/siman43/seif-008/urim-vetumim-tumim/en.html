To invalidate the document, etc. One should not object that this ruling concerns a gift without warranty, as Shach indicates in seif katan 22 from the fact that Rashba permits even other witnesses who were present at the kinyan to write a new document, and as Sema explains in seif katan 16. Mechaber ruled above like the authorities who say an antedated loan document is invalid only for collection from encumbered property but remains valid against free assets. Here, however, the whole purpose of the gift document is to establish the gift itself rather than to collect from purchasers. Why, then, should antedating invalidate the gift document entirely? The answer is that Mechaber distinguishes between a loan and a gift. In a loan, the defect of an earlier date is specifically that the creditor may improperly collect from encumbered property before his lien actually arose. Therefore the Sages penalized only that improper purchaser-collection effect. Against free assets, where no wrongful earlier collection occurs, the document can remain effective. In a gift, by contrast, antedating can corrupt the very title to the property. Suppose the donor truly gave the field to Shimon in Iyar, but in Nisan he had borrowed from Yaakov or sold the field to Yaakov. If the gift document is falsely dated to Adar, Shimon appears to hold an earlier title and can defeat Yaakov. The false date therefore does not merely affect a collateral collection right. It can alter the core ownership established by the gift instrument. Accordingly, the rabbinic penalty can invalidate the gift document as an instrument of title itself. Thus even according to Mechaber, who preserves an antedated loan document against free assets, an antedated gift document can be wholly ineffective for proving the gift.
