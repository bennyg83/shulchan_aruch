Where the witnesses did not intentionally antedate it, etc. Nimukei Yosef in chapter Eizehu Neshech writes that a case can occur in which they remain valid witnesses, for example where they wrote a document for the borrower without the lender present, and the borrower did not actually borrow until later. Although one may write a document for the borrower without the lender only where it is a document of kinyan, nevertheless one who improperly did so is not thereby disqualified. The students of Rashba write there that another case is where, at the time they signed, they mistakenly thought the loan had already taken place, and later they saw the lender give the borrower the amount stated in that document and testify accordingly. So writes Darkei Moshe, seif 8.
For the witnesses are believed, etc. Since the document was properly eligible to be written from the date on which the loan actually took place, they are believed to say that they intended to sign it only as of that date and did not realize, when signing, that the written date was earlier. But according to Ri, if their signatures are authenticated from another source, they are not believed unless they testify that they were coerced under threat to life. If their signatures are not authenticated from elsewhere and they say that they erred in the year of the king, they are believed through a migo, for they could have said, 'This is not our handwriting.' The Tur writes this and says that the Rosh likewise agreed. Rivash, siman 382, discusses witnesses where the document said four years although they should have written thirty-four years, and the witnesses said they erred. He ruled that they are not believed, because people do not ordinarily make such an error. So writes Darkei Moshe, seif 8.
