(Further there: nevertheless, if they wrote, etc.) The Sema places this gloss under seif 20. His explanation that the phrase “we acquired from him” lacks a conjunction and should be read as though it said “and we acquired” is strained. The gloss indeed relates to seif 20, where the document is invalid because the witnesses appear false. It means that it suffices to write, “In such-and-such place we acquired from so-and-so, and we wrote and signed on such-and-such day.” This does not appear false, because the place mentioned first may refer to the later writing. Had they written, “We acquired from so-and-so in such-and-such place, and we wrote,” the conjunction would separate the place of acquisition from the writing. As phrased, however, it can mean: in that place this writing occurred concerning an acquisition already made, and now we wrote and delivered it on the stated day. Rabbenu Yeruham explains this. Nevertheless, however Rabbenu Yeruham is interpreted, the Rama should not have cited it as settled law, because the Mechaber’s ruling in seif 20 disagrees, saying they are found false and the document is invalid. Baal HaTerumot explicitly writes that when the day of acquisition is stated, the document must identify the place where the acquisition occurred, for it appears false to write a prior month’s date and the scribe’s current location. This shows that ambiguity linking the place to the acquisition itself is enough to create false appearance. The Beit Yosef therefore properly cites Rabbenu Yeruham as disagreeing with Rashba. The Rama’s presentation as though no dispute exists requires study.
