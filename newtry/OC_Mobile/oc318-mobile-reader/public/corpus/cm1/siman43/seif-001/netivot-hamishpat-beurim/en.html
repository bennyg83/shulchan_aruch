There is one who says. Shach accepts this opinion. Urim VeTumim asks why a deed with no date differs from an antedated deed, where an intentionally delayed or altered date can postpone the lien. The answer is that omission of the date would otherwise destroy the lien entirely. Since responsibility clauses are ordinarily treated as scribal omissions because a person does not throw away his money, the same logic can treat omission of the date as a scribal error where otherwise the whole lien would disappear. By contrast, a delayed deed or two deeds dated the same day do not eliminate the lien; they merely limit the time or priority. That resembles a deed that expressly includes only partial responsibility, where we do not invoke the scribal-error doctrine. It is also possible that the authorities do not truly disagree: those who reject outside proof may speak where it is known that the date was intentionally omitted at the borrower's direction. As to the Shach's proof from gittin, one can understand it from Reish Lakish's view that the wife should recover fruits from the time of signing. If an undated instrument created no publicity even from signing, she would improperly lose fruits, and there would be no reason to invalidate it merely for lacking a date. The fallen-get cases likewise show that documentary publicity can exist even where the exact effective date later requires outside clarification.
