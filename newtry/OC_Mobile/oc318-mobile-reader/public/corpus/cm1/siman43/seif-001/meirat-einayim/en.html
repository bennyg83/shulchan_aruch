But he cannot collect from purchasers, etc. In Perishah I wrote that there is a practical difference even with respect to unencumbered property. If the borrower has two promissory notes against him but owns only enough land to satisfy one, we collect for the creditor whose document contains a date, for he can say that his loan preceded the other loan whose document has no date.
He collects from them. The reason is that from the time the witnesses write the document, they generate publicity, and the purchasers certainly heard of the obligation and brought the loss upon themselves. But if the witnesses did not see the loan, the purchasers can say that their purchase preceded the loan. This is clear in my eyes, unlike Eir Shushan, whose wording implies that the witnesses who merely saw the event generate the publicity. That is incorrect, for we find publicity generated only by witnesses specifically designated for the kinyan or for the signing.
A receipt without a date is valid. See below in seif 24 for what the Mechaber writes further on this, and below siman 54, seif 3, where it will be explained that the original enactment regarding a receipt was that if the witnesses do not know the date written in the promissory note, they should not write any date in the receipt, lest the witnesses to the note have postdated its date.
