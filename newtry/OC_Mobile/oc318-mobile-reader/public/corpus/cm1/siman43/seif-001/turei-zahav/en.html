(Seif 7: some say it is entirely invalid.) The Sema cites Maharshal that an antedated document is not entirely invalid. Yet Tosafot in Eizehu Neshekh show that Rabbi Yohanan invalidates it completely. Perhaps Maharshal derives his view from the Tur’s citation of Rabbenu Yonah in siman 45, seif 7, that even with an antedated document one may distinguish between a plea of “this never occurred” and a plea of payment. I initially thought Rabbenu Yonah there represents a third opinion and was cited only regarding the requirement to leave two blank lines, where the document can be repaired by filling the space. The gloss, however, argues Maharshal is correct. The Tur labors to identify how the document became antedated rather than simply saying the lender admits it is antedated. Although the lender cannot disqualify the witnesses, he can bind himself. This indicates the witnesses were not invalid in the underlying loan; rather, the writing lacks the legal force of a document. Thus if the borrower denies the loan entirely, he is contradicted by valid witnesses and established as a denier, but if the lender admits the witnesses intentionally antedated it, even a denial would be accepted. Tosafot in HaManiah likewise imply the document loses only documentary force while the oral testimony remains. Were the concern of collecting from the earlier date absent, collection could proceed from the later date. This applies even according to the Rosh, not only the Rif.
