Seif 1. The time of the loan is written in the document. Avodah Zarah 10 relates a document dated six years later than expected. Rav Acha bar Yaakov asks: from where do we know that the scribe counted from the beginning of Greek rule? Perhaps he counted from the Exodus, in which case the document is postdated. I have found the plain meaning difficult. If mere doubt creates a concern that the document is postdated, why invoke the possibility of counting from the Exodus? The Gemara could simply ask that perhaps the scribe counted years differently and the document is postdated. If, however, we ordinarily do not suspect postdating, as Rashi explains because people do not usually waive several years of lien rights, then that very consideration should answer the Gemara's question as well. If counting from the Exodus would make the document postdated, the fact that people do not ordinarily surrender their lien should itself prove that the scribe counted from the beginning of Greek rule. It appears that the rule is this. Where the uncertainty concerns only what this particular scribe intended, the presumption that people do not ordinarily make a postdated document is enough to clarify the scribe's intent. But that presumption cannot establish the meaning of an entire calendrical system or rabbinic enactment. A particular scribe is not strong enough evidence to determine how everyone counts years. A further principle is that where an explanation requires an error people commonly make, we may prefer that explanation over postdating; but where it requires an unusual error, postdating becomes the more plausible concern. Thus, if we know that the prevailing system counts from the Greek kingdom and only wonder how this scribe counted, the fact that people do not usually postdate points to an ordinary calendrical mistake. But if it is possible that the prevailing system itself counts from the Exodus, then to say that this scribe both departed from the system and also miscounted the beginning of Greek rule would require an unusual double error. In that case, postdating is the more plausible concern. This appears to be the Gemara's meaning. I later saw that Torat Chaim noticed part of this difficulty, though his resolution is strained; what I have written appears more correct.
