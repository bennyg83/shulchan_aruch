Bava Metzia teaches concerning a woman's bill of divorce or a slave's bill of emancipation that was lost that they do not collect produce or property from the date written in the instrument, because it may not have been delivered until later. When they seek collection, they must prove when the instrument reached their hands. Thus, the written date itself is ineffective, essentially leaving the instrument undated. Nevertheless, once witnesses prove when it was in their possession, collection is effective from that time. The same implication follows from the authorities' discussion in Gittin concerning why a date was instituted in bills of divorce.
