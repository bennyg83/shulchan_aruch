He did not write property acquired later. Maggid Mishneh leaves Rambam's unqualified validation of later dated documents requiring study because Rambam does not expressly require a clause encumbering later acquired property. Rama in siman 209 cites an opinion that one who cannot transfer something not yet in existence in the ordinary way can nevertheless transfer it by saying that acquisition should take effect once the thing comes into existence. Mordechai takes this view. I argued there that ordinary kinyan cannot acquire future property even with such wording, from the Gemara's question: if one cannot transfer future property, why are later dated documents valid when the property was acquired only later? The Gemara's question itself shows that postponing the effect until the future does not automatically solve the problem. Nevertheless, Tosafot in Bekhorot teaches an important principle concerning a firstborn son's redemption. Assets acquired after the son becomes an adult can become subject to the redemption obligation even without an express later acquired property clause, because the obligation of redemption rests upon him continuously at every moment until he pays. It is therefore as though the obligation were newly written after he acquires the assets. This differs from an ordinary loan, where the debtor incurs the obligation at one fixed moment and later assets require a special clause. A later dated document can be understood similarly. Since a person can impose a new personal obligation upon himself through a document even where no prior debt existed, the document dated later can be treated as creating a fresh obligation from its written date. Assets acquired before that written date are therefore not "later acquired" relative to the new documentary obligation. The difficulty is the property responsibility clause itself. If one assumes that later acquired property cannot be encumbered because one cannot partially transfer a future asset, then writing the responsibility clause before the asset exists should not help. The conclusion of the Gemara, however, is that later acquired property can indeed be encumbered. Once that is accepted, no explicit later acquired clause is needed for property acquired before the later date appearing in the document, because from that date onward a fresh documentary obligation rests upon the debtor and the ordinary responsibility language extends to assets then owned. This resolves Rambam. It also follows that witnesses should not write a later dated document without the debtor's knowledge where the later dating would create a new obligation he did not intend, just as they may not insert a later acquired property clause without authorization according to authorities who do not treat it as a scribal presumption.
