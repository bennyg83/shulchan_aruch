Later-dated documents are valid. The Shach, seif katan 25, cites the Beit Yosef, who writes in the name of Baal HaTerumot, Gate 56, that one who produces against another a postdated document cannot collect on it until its stated date arrives. The Shach asks why the Beit Yosef needed to cite Baal HaTerumot, since this is an explicit Gemara in Avodah Zarah 10. See Noda BiYehudah, responsum 11, who gives a well-reasoned explanation of the Beit Yosef. See also Shivat Tzion, responsum 104, on the Noda BiYehudah. It is explained there that a postdated document has two consequences. First, even from the borrower himself the lender cannot demand payment before the date written in the document, because the obligation to repay does not mature until then. Second, even after that date, the lender cannot collect from purchasers who acquired property between the actual loan and the written date, because the lender effectively waived his lien for that intervening period, and their purchases preceded the commencement of his recorded lien. Although Rashi’s wording emphasizes inability to collect from purchasers, the Gemara itself treats “we hold it until its date arrives” and “he does not collect” as two distinct matters. See there further regarding whether the debtor may claim payment during the intervening period. This will be cited below in siman 69, seif 2, seif katan 4, and at the end of siman 78, seif katan 5.
