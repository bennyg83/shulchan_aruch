Later-dated documents are valid. They collect only from the later date and not from any earlier property, as though the earlier lien had been waived until that day. This is the Gemara's rule in Avodah Zarah. See Tumim and below, siman 54.
Where it says “property I will acquire.” Perhaps he borrowed in Nisan, bought land in Iyar, and sold it in Cheshvan. Without an after-acquired-property clause, the lender from Nisan would not have a lien on land that was bought and sold after the loan. If the deed is instead postdated to Tishrei, however, the land already belonged to the borrower when the deed's apparent lien began, and the lender could improperly collect it from a purchaser who bought in Cheshvan. See below, siman 112, whether after-acquired-property responsibility is treated as an omitted standard clause. I discuss the matter there at length.
