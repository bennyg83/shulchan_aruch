(In the name of Maharshal in the Beit Yosef, beginning “If he antedated the document, etc.” through “but our master wrote, etc.”) I found that the Tur’s position is explicit in a responsum of the Rosh cited in Even HaEzer, siman 157: if a woman was divorced with an antedated get and its invalidity was not discovered until after another man betrothed her, the second betrothal does not take effect. If the Rosh held the document valid for collection from unencumbered property, why would the betrothal not take effect at all? It follows that he invalidates the witnesses completely. Terumat HaDeshen, writings no. 13, gives this as the Rosh’s reasoning. Rivash writes that even one who validates collection from unencumbered property agrees a woman may not remarry on such a get, but that addresses initial remarriage. If she already remarried, the one who validates collection should not require her to leave, analogous to an undated get, which is invalid yet does not require divorce after remarriage.
