On the fifth of Nisan. Ran in Perek Mi SheHaya Nasui explains the difference between loan documents and gift documents dated the same day. For loan documents, even if one lender actually received his document first, both divide equally because the lien of a document takes effect only from the time ascertainable from the document itself. A document merely dated Nisan without a day cannot ordinarily collect from purchasers until the end of Nisan. The public notice on which purchaser liability rests exists only from the time indicated by the document. I find Ran's proof difficult from the immediately following episode: one document was dated simply Nisan and another the fifth of Nisan. Rav Yosef gave priority to the fifth of Nisan document. The holder of the undated Nisan document asked whether he loses everything. Rav Yosef said his hand is lower because he might be dated the twenty ninth. When he asked for a collection order from Iyar onward, Rav Yosef said the other might be dated the first of Nisan. This implies that if it could be proven that the undated document was actually delivered on the first of Nisan, it would collect from then. If Ran means that an undifferentiated Nisan document can never create collection before the end of Nisan because that is not evident from the writing, how can proof of the actual date help? Perhaps Ran interprets that episode specifically with documents of kinyan, where an independently proven kinyan can fix priority from the actual date. But Ran himself appears to discuss loan documents generally. The most one can certainly prove from the episode is that in doubt the undated document cannot collect before the end of the month. Once that is so, perhaps for ordinary non kinyan documents the lack of an ascertainable earlier date itself prevents collection even if one later learns the actual day, because purchaser liability depends on the publicity of the document as written. This remains difficult and requires further study.
