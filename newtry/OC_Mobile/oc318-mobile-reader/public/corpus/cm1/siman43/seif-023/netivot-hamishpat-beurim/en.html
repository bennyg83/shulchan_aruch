Likewise, Shimon cannot collect from purchasers. See Tumim, who writes that according to the opinion that a document without a date cannot be used to collect from encumbered property even where the document was seen in the creditor's hand, purchasers cannot argue, "We left you property from which to collect," because with respect to the month of Nisan he waived his lien, and so forth. See there. In my humble opinion, even according to that view the purchasers can make this argument according to the opinion that an earlier oral loan has priority over a later documented loan. They can say: this field remained unencumbered property; you are the creditor of the first of Nisan, and we heard the public notice generated by your loan on the first of Nisan, since every document generates publicity. Although with respect to encumbered property your lien is treated like an oral loan, we nevertheless left you unencumbered property from which to collect, because you precede the creditor of the fifth of Nisan. You caused your own loss by failing to write a date in the document. According to the opinion that an oral loan does not receive such priority, however, Tumim's ruling is correct.
