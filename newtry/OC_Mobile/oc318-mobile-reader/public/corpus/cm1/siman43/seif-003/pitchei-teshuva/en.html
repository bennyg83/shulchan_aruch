Postdated documents are valid. The Shach, seif katan 25, quotes the Beit Yosef as writing in Choshen Mishpat 11 that from Baal HaTerumos, gate 56, it appears that one who produces a postdated document cannot collect with it until the date arrives. The Shach asks why the Beit Yosef needed to cite Baal HaTerumos, since this is an explicit Gemara in the first chapter of Avodah Zarah 10. See Responsa Noda B'Yehuda, siman 11, who resolves the Beit Yosef's words with sound reasoning. See also Responsa Shivat Tzion, siman 104, regarding Noda B'Yehuda's words. It is explained there that with a postdated document, in addition to the fact that the creditor cannot collect from purchasers until the date written in the document arrives, he also cannot collect from the borrower himself before that date, because the obligation of repayment does not take effect until then. Although Rashi there explains the phrase 'until the time arrives he cannot collect' as referring to purchasers, the Gemara itself does not phrase it that way. Rather, it says, 'This is a postdated document, let us hold it until its date arrives, and he will not collect.' These are two separate matters. First, even from the borrower himself he cannot demand payment until the written date arrives. Second, he can never collect from purchasers who bought between the actual loan and the date written in the document, even after the due date arrives, because he waived his lien during that intervening period and those purchasers acquired before his lien began. See there further regarding whether the borrower may claim that he repaid during the interim. This will be cited below in siman 69, seif 2, seif katan 4, and at the end of siman 78, seif katan 5.
