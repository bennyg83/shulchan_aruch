(In the Tur, seif 10: until he proves it reached his hand on the date written.) This is difficult because the Rosh rules that the signatures themselves acquire for the recipient. If so, even if the document did not reach him on the stated date, he acquired from the time of signing. Some answer that we fear the borrower instructed the witnesses to write and deliver it back to him, in which case the signatures do not acquire for the creditor. This is insufficient, for then the Gemara in Bava Metzia would not need to limit Shmuel’s rule that repayment is not suspected to a case where the debtor does not admit. Even when he admits, the document should not be returned because perhaps he instructed delivery to himself. The Gemara evidently does not entertain that concern. According to the view that signatures acquire, the document is like one containing a kinyan and the lien arises from signing in every case. It therefore appears that although the Rosh wrote in the first chapter of Bava Metzia that the law should follow Abaye, who says the signatures acquire, his position was rejected in practice by his rulings elsewhere, which do not follow Abaye. His language in Gittin also presents this as an unresolved answer rather than settled law. This explains numerous rulings of the Tur that assume signatures do not acquire for the recipient.
