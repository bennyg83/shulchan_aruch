It was on the twenty-fourth. Shach asks why Even HaEzer cites dissent while here Mechaber rules simply that it is valid. Urim VeTumim explains the dispute as whether we attribute an error to the weekday, since people are less likely to err about the weekday. In my view the proper explanation is different. The instrument is necessarily postdated, for the written date is Shemini Atzeret. It can therefore have been prepared earlier with a future effective date. Before the month was fixed by beit din, the witnesses could reasonably err concerning the calendar, including in an intercalated year, because they had not yet heard the formal fixing. The ambiguity is whether the principal instructed them that the obligation should begin on the twenty-second and they mistakenly thought that date fell on Wednesday, or instructed them that it should begin on Wednesday and they mistakenly thought Wednesday was the twenty-second. In monetary law the holder of the document has the inferior hand. Thus, for later-acquired-property questions we treat the written calendar date as primary where that worsens his position, while against purchasers who bought on the intervening days we treat the weekday as primary where that worsens him. In a get, by contrast, the date serves protective purposes concerning adultery accusations and fruits, so uncertainty itself can defeat the instrument's dating function. The authorities therefore needed to explain why such an error can still be attributed to calendar calculation rather than deliberate false dating.
