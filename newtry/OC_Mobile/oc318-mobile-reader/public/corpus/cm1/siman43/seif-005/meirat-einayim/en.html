It was on the twenty-fourth day, etc. The printed "twenty-two" is a scribal error and should read twenty-four. Mordechai at the beginning of Rosh Hashanah records that Raavyah was asked about a document dated "Wednesday, the twenty-second of Tishrei," although in that year Rosh Hashanah fell on Monday and therefore the Wednesday of the fourth week was the twenty-fourth, not the twenty-second. Moreover, the twenty-second of Tishrei was Shemini Atzeret, when such writing would not be done. Raavyah answered that the document is not invalid. A baraita teaches that a document dated on Shabbat is treated as postdated and is valid. As to the mismatch between Wednesday and the twenty-second of the month, perhaps the scribe erred in calculating the month's intercalation. Although one ordinarily says after most of the month has passed that witnesses do not err about intercalation, that is specifically regarding oral testimony. In a written document the mistake is evident from the contradictory dating language itself. And if one objects that the error amounts to two days, perhaps he erred in two month calculations, or he treated the two days of Rosh Hashanah as one sanctity and one long day for purposes of counting the month, so that the following Thursday was counted by him as the second day of the month and the Wednesday of the fourth week as the twenty-second. I have added some explanatory words to clarify Mordechai.
