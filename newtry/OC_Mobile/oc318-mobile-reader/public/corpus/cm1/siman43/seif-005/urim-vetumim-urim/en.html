It was on the twenty-fourth, yet it is valid. See Tumim. If the document contains an after-acquired-property clause, so that a later-dated document is generally valid, it is certainly valid here. We can say the deed was prepared considerably earlier and intentionally postdated to the twenty-second of Tishrei; because it was written long before that time, the witnesses were uncertain about the future calendar and mistakenly thought Wednesday would fall on the twenty-second. Even if no after-acquired-property clause was written, so that an intentionally later-dated document might ordinarily be defective, the document can still stand because the contradiction between the date and weekday shows that the witnesses erred rather than deliberately antedated or postdated it, and there is no reason to disqualify them. Nor is there concern of improper collection because the discrepancy is visible from the document itself. If, however, it is independently known that the transaction occurred on the twenty-fourth and the witnesses wrote the twenty-second, there is room to invalidate it despite the weekday contradiction, because perhaps the later statement constitutes retraction and we follow the final formulation. See Tumim.
