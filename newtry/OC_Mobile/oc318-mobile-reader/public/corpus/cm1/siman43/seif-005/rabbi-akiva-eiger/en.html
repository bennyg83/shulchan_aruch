[Shulchan Aruch] They give it to Reuven. Note: This means that Reuven's date is definite, whereas Shimon's date is not definite. The Raah writes that the same law applies where a borrower whose deed is dated the fifth of Nisan seeks to collect from a purchaser whose deed of sale is dated simply "Nisan." In my humble opinion, this law requires great study. One can say that only in the case of two debt instruments, where the field is certainly encumbered to one of the two creditors and Reuven certainly has some lien, do we give it to Reuven because his date is definite and neither party is in possession. But to take property from a purchaser on the basis of a doubtful lien, what basis is there? See the Ran and Maggid Mishneh, who cite the Rashba regarding two purchasers, one dated the fifth of Nisan and the other simply Nisan. A creditor may collect from the one whose deed is dated simply Nisan, and that purchaser cannot say, "Perhaps I purchased on the first of Nisan and the creditor left property available to collect from." The reason is that the rule against collection from purchasers is an enactment, and therefore the purchaser bears the burden of proving that he preceded the creditor. This too implies that only where the lien is certainly upon him, and the limitation on collection is merely an enactment, does the burden fall on the purchaser. But under the Raah's rule, where we are deciding whether the lien ever attached at all, one should not be able to take property on the basis of doubt. This requires study.
