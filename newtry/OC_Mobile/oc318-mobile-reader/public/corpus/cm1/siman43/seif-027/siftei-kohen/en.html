For in common speech, 'until' excludes the endpoint, etc. The Darkei Moshe rejected this ruling because of the Rosh's responsum concerning a release stating that Shimon waived all monetary claims against Reuven 'until this day,' where a note dated the same day was construed against the note holder. He treated that responsum as contradicting another responsum that 'until the fifth of Nisan' excludes that day. This is incorrect. The latter ruling accords with the settled principle that contractual language follows ordinary speech, as with vows, where 'until Pesach' excludes Pesach itself. The two responsa are not contradictory. 'Until this day,' written during the day, naturally means 'until now,' and therefore creates doubt concerning a note dated that same day; indeed Scripture repeatedly uses 'until this day' in the sense of continuing until the present moment. By contrast, 'until the fifth of Nisan' is like 'until Pesach' and means until the beginning of that date. The Mechaber therefore properly rules in accordance with both responsa, and the Rama apparently withdrew his objection.
