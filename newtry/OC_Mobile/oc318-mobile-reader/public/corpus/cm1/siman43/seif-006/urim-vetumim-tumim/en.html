And some say the witnesses are believed. Sema cites Darkei Moshe in the name of Rivash regarding witnesses who should have written the year 134 but mistakenly wrote 104. This is the correct reading, as Shach notes, and so it appears in Rivash, responsum 382. Rivash rules that they are not believed to say they erred. Sema asks that Rama already ruled above, seif 2, that if the witnesses omitted the tens digit the document is invalid. Shach answers that the novelty here is that even if the witnesses themselves are now before us and say they made the mistake, they are not believed. Shach leaves the matter requiring study. That answer is not correct. Even if there were room to distinguish, Rivash's responsum gives no proof for the proposition that the witnesses themselves would not be believed, because in Rivash's actual case the witnesses were not before beit din at all. We therefore cannot infer from that responsum what would happen if they personally appeared and explained their error. In truth, there is no difficulty from repetition. Above, the novelty is only that the document is invalid as an antedated document. According to Mechaber's position, such an instrument can still be valid against free assets. Here, by contrast, the issue is the witnesses themselves. If such an error is not one witnesses ordinarily make, then signing the false year can disqualify them. Once the witnesses are personally invalid, the document fails even against free assets. Rivash's responsum makes this clear. He invalidates an antedated chalitzah document even though the act of chalitzah itself does not require a document. His reason is that the witnesses are disqualified, and if so, who remains to testify to the chalitzah? Still, I do not understand why Rivash decided so confidently that confusing 134 with 104 is not a type of mistake people make. Before accepting the student's ruling, one should hear the teacher. Ran, Rivash's own master, in a responsum printed at the end of his responsa, addressed Rivash's case and validated the witnesses explicitly, because he treated this as the kind of numerical mistake people can make. Thus Ran himself regarded such an error as plausible. Accordingly, the categorical ruling that the witnesses become disqualified requires study.
