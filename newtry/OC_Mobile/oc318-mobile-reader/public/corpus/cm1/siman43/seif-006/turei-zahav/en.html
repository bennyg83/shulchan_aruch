(Seif 13: specifically documents of loan.) The Rambam does not imply this distinction, as the Ran himself notes. Moreover, in Gittin 18, according to those who reject Shmuel and require continuous engagement in the same matter even for other documents, those documents resemble a ketubah. A ketubah creates no lien on the day it is written; the nighttime act of acquisition creates the obligation, since it is not a loan but a new self-imposed obligation, even an increased ketubah as in siman 60. Other documents are similar. Further, why should a divorce document differ, where writing by day and signing by night is valid if they remained engaged in the matter according to the Rosh, while the Beit Yosef cites Rashba that the Rambam invalidates it for another reason? No authority says the defect is absence of a daytime lien. The Ran’s distinction therefore stands alone against the Rambam and Rashbam.
