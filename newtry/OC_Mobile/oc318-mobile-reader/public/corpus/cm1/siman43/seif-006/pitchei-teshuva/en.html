The Ra'ah writes that the same rule applies where a lender's document is dated the fifth of Nisan and he seeks to collect from a purchaser whose deed merely says Nisan without specifying the day. In my humble opinion, this ruling requires great study. Perhaps the rule applies specifically to two debt documents, because the field is in any event subject to both liens and Reuven certainly has a lien. Since his date is earlier and neither creditor is in possession, we give it to him. To extract property from a purchaser, however, on the basis of a doubtful lien, what source is there? See the Ran and Maggid Mishneh, who cite the Rashba concerning two purchasers, one whose deed is dated the second of Nisan and one whose deed says merely Nisan. A creditor collects from the purchaser whose deed says only Nisan, and that purchaser cannot say, 'Perhaps I bought on the first of Nisan, so leave me and collect from the other purchaser.' This is because the rule that one does not collect from purchasers is an enactment, and the purchaser must prove that he is earlier. This too implies that the rule applies only where the creditor's lien is certainly upon the property, and the only issue is the enactment protecting purchasers. In the Ra'ah's case, however, where we are deciding whether the creditor's lien ever attached to this purchaser's property, he should not be able to extract on the basis of doubt. This requires further study. End of quote.
