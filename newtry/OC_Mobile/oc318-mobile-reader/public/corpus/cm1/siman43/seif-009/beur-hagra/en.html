(Anthology) A document that was, etc. Since the defect is not evident from the document itself, unlike a date on Shabbat or Yom Kippur, because not everyone knows whether the witness was his son-in-law at that time.
(Anthology) A document, etc.; none of this, etc. Meaning, even what appears from the document itself does not save it, because this is not an error obvious to all, since not everyone knows whether he was his son-in-law. It is unlike the ruling in seif 4. The decree that he may collect from the original date still applies. Nor does the signature of a great scholar save it. We may assume that he did not sign intentionally, but the document remains antedated, as stated in seif 8. Rashba follows Tosafot that it is entirely invalid even against unencumbered property, although it was not intentionally antedated. This is why the recipient of the gift collects neither from encumbered property nor, according to this view, even from unencumbered property, since the document is invalid. This is also why siman 146, seif 25 states that he must take a hesset oath. Were it a full document, no oath would be required. Rashba writes in his responsum, cited by Beit Yosef there, that if other valid witnesses saw the acquisition in the year 58, they may write a deed of gift dated from that year, because witnesses to a gift need not have been formally designated, as is evident in the chapter Yesh Nochalin. This must concern a gift without a warranty, for otherwise, where many witnesses saw the acquisition, they could write repeated documents, which is not allowed in such a case, as stated in Bava Batra 171a and Sema, seif katan 16, and above in siman 39, seif 5. It follows that the first document is invalid even against unencumbered property. This is so even where their handwriting is not independently authenticated, as shown by Rashba's statement that other witnesses may sign, whereas the first document remains invalid.
(Anthology) Nevertheless, if the witnesses, etc. See Sema and Shach.
(Anthology) Nevertheless, etc. This follows Ramban in the gloss to seif 8, as Rama explains there regarding the witnesses themselves, so that they are not disqualified. Therefore, if they wrote, etc., they are not believed, because they are disqualified. Regarding the statement that they may write, see Baer HaGra there. This is not difficult, as explained in Shulchan Aruch, siman 49, seif 6. This ruling is necessary even for a gift containing a warranty, so that they may write the warranty into the new document, for in such a case a second document ordinarily may not be written. Yet they may write it from the later date because the acquisition was made in the year 58, as Rashba writes in the responsum, where witnesses saw the acquisition in that year. His statement that other witnesses may write refers to a gift without a warranty, as explained. Therefore he later adds that, alternatively, they may testify before beit din, meaning so that the recipient may collect from encumbered property.
(Anthology) Nevertheless, the recipient, etc. Meaning, to collect from unencumbered property, for he certainly cannot collect from encumbered property with an invalid document. According to this view, as explained, he cannot collect even from unencumbered property.
