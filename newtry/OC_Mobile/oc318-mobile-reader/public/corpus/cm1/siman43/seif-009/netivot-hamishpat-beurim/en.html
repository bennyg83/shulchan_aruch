They may sign another document. Shach asks why they cannot write a new instrument from the second date even if their signatures on the first are independently authenticated, since their mission was not properly completed. He also asks why they may be believed to invalidate the first date, perhaps a kinyan was actually made in year 57. He therefore explains that an ordinary document is presumed valid and that, when the signatures are not independently authenticated, the witnesses can say the kinyan occurred only in 58 and write a new deed, contradicting the recipient's claim of a 57 kinyan. If their signatures are independently authenticated, they cannot retract and contradict the instrument. Urim VeTumim gives a different explanation but his migo is difficult, because even if the witnesses say they never signed this instrument, the recipient's own admission that they signed an antedated deed disqualifies them as against him. In my view the witnesses themselves remain qualified and can say there was a common dating mistake, with or without migo. The real difference concerns practical effect on the recipient's title. Suppose the donor proves that between years 57 and 58 the recipient sold the field to another person. If we maintain the deed as a 57 deed, the recipient owned the field when he sold it. If the witnesses are believed that the kinyan occurred only in 58, the earlier sale was a sale of something he did not yet own. When their signatures are independently authenticated, they cannot retract from the established 57 instrument to defeat that prior sale. When the signatures are not independently authenticated, they can explain that the year was mistaken and that the true kinyan was in 58. Similar consequences arise where a creditor without an after-acquired-property clause seeks to foreclose. Thus the issue is not the witnesses' personal fitness but whether their later explanation can change the established legal date of the instrument.
From the second date. Shach says that because the original instrument is truly invalid even from the second date, the witnesses cannot write another. This is difficult. The case involves a kinyan, as Rashba and Sema indicate. Where the kinyan itself occurred on the second date, the witnesses certainly may write a document correctly reflecting that kinyan from the true date.
