None of this saves, etc. This is not evident from the document itself, as above in seif 5, for who can determine whether at that time he was already his son in law in the year 57 or not, Sema. It is also possible that he was betrothed in the year 57 and was already called his son in law, Rashba in a manuscript responsum cited in Responsa Maharash, siman 34. See Tumim.
From invalidating the document. Although the Mechaber holds that an antedated document is valid against unencumbered property, with respect to a gift he agrees that it is entirely invalid. See Tumim for the reason.
They may sign, etc. Since they have a migo, they are believed to say that they erred, and they therefore remain valid witnesses and may sign. Likewise, with all antedated documents, if the witnesses remain valid they may sign a second document, since they have not yet completed their agency. See Tumim.
Another document, etc. Certainly, if the donor does not object, they may in any event write even ten gift documents, as above in siman 41. Rather, the case is where he objects. Rashba holds that since they have not completed their agency, he cannot object, and they may write it against his will. But had they already completed their agency, and the donor then objected, they could not write another document against his will.
From the later date. Meaning from the year 58, since a postdated gift document is invalid, as above in siman 41. See Tumim.
They are no longer believed. Meaning that they are not believed to say that they erred, since witnesses are not presumed to make such an error. The witnesses are therefore disqualified and cannot write another document. There is no migo, because this is a migo of two people acting together. See Tumim for an explanation.
Nevertheless the recipient, etc. All of this applies where the recipient does not know whether they erred or intentionally antedated it. But if he says that he knows they erred, he may obtain a new document from them and is believed to validate it through a migo, etc. Even though the witnesses contradict him and say that the kinyan was made only in the year 58, they cannot do so after they have already given their testimony. See Tumim, where I explained that the entire seif concerns a case where the recipient admits that the document was written in the year 58.
And in the year 57 they made a kinyan from him. The same would apply if he said that everything occurred in the year 57, and the description "his son in law" is no proof to the contrary, as stated above, since he may already have been betrothed. The case discussed here is one in which there are witnesses who saw him sign in the year 58. See Tumim. Sema writes in the name of Rashba that the same applies if other witnesses saw the kinyan being made. They may write a gift document without a warranty clause, because even ten gift documents may be written, etc., or they may testify before beit din and beit din will write the document. See what I wrote above in siman 41, that with beit din this may be done even if the donor objects, whereas witnesses may not. See there.
