They may sign another document from the second date. Shach, seif katan 22, asks a substantial question. The case concerns a gift without warranty, for Rashba says other witnesses who were present at the kinyan may also write a new document. If there had been warranty obligations, how could unrelated witnesses create a new instrument imposing them? If so, why does Rashba need the reason that the original witnesses never completed their agency because the donor instructed them to write the year 58 and they mistakenly wrote 57? In a gift without warranty, the Gemara allows ten documents to be written over one field. They should be able to write another document regardless of whether their original agency was completed. The answer follows what I explained above in siman 39 and siman 41. When we say other witnesses may write another gift document, that is where the donor does not object. If the donor expressly protests, ordinary witnesses may not write over his objection because their writing becomes unauthorized written testimony. The original kinyan witnesses are different. Rashba's responsum involved a gift made by kinyan. Where the donor originally instructed those witnesses to write and a kinyan was made, the kinyan strengthens their authority. Rashba, responsum 1037, and Shach above, siman 39, seif katan 13, recognize that those witnesses can sometimes write even though the donor later protests. But that is only so long as they have not yet performed the agency entrusted to them. If they already completed the agency correctly once, they become like ordinary witnesses. Then, if the donor protests, they cannot simply create another writing over his objection. In Rashba's case, because they wrote the wrong year, they did not perform the agency at all. They were commanded to write 58 and instead wrote 57. Therefore their original authority remains and they may issue the corrected document even if the donor now objects. This explains why Rashba needed the "agency not completed" rationale despite the general rule allowing multiple gift documents. The phrase "from the second date" is likewise straightforward. It means from the true kinyan date, year 58, the second of the two dates under discussion. Although the first paper is void, the kinyan itself occurred in year 58. Particularly in a gift, the kinyan creates the transfer independently of the defective paper. The witnesses may therefore write from the kinyan date. They cannot write from a later date merely because they are writing now if doing so would turn the gift document into a postdated instrument, which is problematic in gifts as explained above. Shach further asks: in every antedated loan document, where we rule that the creditor cannot collect even from the true later date using the defective paper, why may the witnesses not simply write a new document from the true later date on the theory that they never properly completed their agency? The cases are being confused. When the law says the antedated paper cannot collect even from the later true date, it means the existing defective paper remains unusable for that purpose. For example, the document was written in Tishrei of year 58 but mistakenly dated year 57, and the defect was discovered in year 60. One might think the existing paper should at least collect from Tishrei 58, when the real transaction occurred. The law says no; the defective paper itself is penalized. That does not mean competent witnesses are forever barred from creating a fresh valid document. If they write a new document in year 60 without a prior kinyan, its lien begins from year 60, because before then the old paper was defective. If there was a valid kinyan in year 58, however, the witnesses can write from that kinyan date, subject to the dispute whether a protesting obligor can block renewed writing. Thus the question whether the old paper retains force and the separate question whether witnesses may create a new paper are entirely different. Shach's objection joins the two and therefore does not stand. Accordingly, Rashba's law is coherent: because the original witnesses never fulfilled their instruction, they retain authority to write the corrected gift document from the true kinyan date.
