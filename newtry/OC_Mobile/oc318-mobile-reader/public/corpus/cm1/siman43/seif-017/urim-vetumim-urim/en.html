In loan documents. The money was given during the day, so the borrower's property became encumbered immediately. Because the parties remained engaged in the same matter, publicity is continuous. According to this reasoning, if the money was not actually given until night, the document is invalid under this approach, because nothing created a lien during the daytime.
Who obligates himself through a kinyan. Sema explains that the kinyan was made at night and during the day he merely instructed the witnesses to write and perform the formal sudar kinyan. In that case there was no daytime lien. Even if the witnesses' activity created publicity, there was no actual obligation for the publicity to attach to. All the more so where no kinyan occurred at all; then the lien begins only from the signing of the document, not its writing. But if a kinyan itself was made during the day, the lien takes effect immediately, just as if money had been lent. In Tumim I suggest that even a kinyan might not create the lien if the obligor still retained a right to retract. See there and my answer to the difficulty raised by Bnei Yaakov.
