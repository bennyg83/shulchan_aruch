In the sages of the Levites, the taste of Hashem is enslaved from the same time that they are employed in the same matter:
But in instruments of obligation, where he becomes obligated by kinyan, etc. Baal HaTerumot explains: "By what would he become obligated during the day? Even though there is publicity, mere words do not create a lien." In my view he means an obligation by kinyan analogous to one created by a document. Just as the case of obligation by document concerns a document not actually executed until night, so too the kinyan case means that the kinyan itself was not performed until night. During the day they merely discussed and agreed upon the obligation, and at night the obligation was finalized through either the document or the kinyan. If a sudar kinyan had actually been performed during the day, he would certainly be bound from that time according to Rif, Rambam, and those who follow them, even without any document. According to the Rosh, if a document is later written at night, the lien reaches back to the time of the kinyan. The phrase "although there is publicity" means that because they were engaged in the matter during the day, the discussion itself generated publicity, but publicity without an actual legal act does not create the lien.
