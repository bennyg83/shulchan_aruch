But with documents of obligation. See Taz, who asks from the law of a get. I do not understand his question. According to Rabbi Yochanan, who holds that the relevant time begins from writing, the words of the Rosh are independently difficult, and one must answer as I wrote in Torat Gittin. See there. The question could arise only according to the Rosh, who understands the rule that a document is valid where the parties are still engaged in the same matter as referring to the Mishnah in Gittin, and only according to Reish Lakish, who holds that the relevant time is from signing. Yet even this is no difficulty. Tosafot in Gittin 13, in the passage beginning "Reish Lakish said," write regarding the concern for produce that according to the Rabbis, although the document is fit for divorce, because it generates no public notice the husband does not lose the produce. Their words show that loss of produce depends upon both present fitness for divorce and public notice. A get is therefore comparable in this respect to other documents, and not to an ordinary document creating a monetary obligation.
