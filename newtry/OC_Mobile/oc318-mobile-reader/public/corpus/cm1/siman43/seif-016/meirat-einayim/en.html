But it was written on the day and signed at the time of the High Court of Justice, and it was signed in the morning and signed at night, and it was not planned earlier:
Even if they did not sign until much later, it is valid. See the beginning of siman 39, where the Mechaber writes that if there are witnesses to the kinyan, the creditor may collect from encumbered property even if no document was written, because the kinyan itself creates publicity. According to that, the present rule does not mean the witnesses must ultimately write and sign. Rather, if the creditor wishes them to write, perhaps because the testimony may later be forgotten or the witnesses may travel away, they write the date of the original kinyan, as stated. This is somewhat forced. In Derishah and Hagahot Darkei Moshe I offered another reconciliation of the Tur and Rosh. There is a distinction between a case where the actual witnesses to the kinyan are before us and remember its date, in which case we say the generous kinyan created publicity from the outset and the creditor may collect from that time even without a document, and a case where those witnesses are absent and we are concerned that they themselves did not retain the date in mind and therefore did not spread precise publicity. In the latter case, collection from the earlier time requires a document recording the kinyan date. Even if that document was written long afterward, its existence shows that the witnesses had not forgotten the date. This resolves several apparent contradictions in the Mechaber's rulings.
