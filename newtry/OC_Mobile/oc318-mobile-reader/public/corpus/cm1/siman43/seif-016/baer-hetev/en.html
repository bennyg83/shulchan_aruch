Invalid. The Sema cites the Darchei Moshe: the Gemara in the second chapter of Gittin states that an act of beit din may be written by day and signed at night, because the obligation takes effect from the time judgment is completed, and the document is not considered antedated. The Shach writes that with regard to a ketubah there is a dispute among the authorities whether it is treated like an act of beit din. The Rashba rules in his novellae that it is treated as an act of beit din. See there.
A long time. The Sema writes that at the beginning of siman 39 the Mechaber rules that if there are witnesses that a kinyan was made from the obligor, the creditor may collect from encumbered property even if no document was written. According to this, the statement here that the witnesses may sign even a long time later cannot mean that writing and signing are legally necessary. Rather, if the lender wants a document because he fears the testimony will be forgotten, they may write it and date it from the time of the kinyan. One can also distinguish between a case where the kinyan witnesses are presently before us and one where they are not. The Shach writes that this distinction is unpersuasive and unnecessary. The Rosh and Tur indeed hold that collection from encumbered property requires the matter to have been written, whereas the Mechaber follows the majority of authorities. The Taz distinguishes differently: there, the discussion concerns collection within the due period; here, the debt was claimed after the due date, while the witnesses signed during the due period. He writes that the Sema labored unnecessarily over the issue.
