(See there, and he has witnesses) Maharshal writes that it is unnecessary for the borrower to admit that the object belonged to Reuven. If he admits this, then even an article not ordinarily lent or rented belongs to Reuven, since witnesses establish that it was originally his. The Tur writes that two witnesses are specifically required and that one witness is insufficient. Sema asks that wherever two witnesses are effective, one witness should likewise be effective when the opposing party does not contradict him. That is, if the creditor says, “It is true that it was yours, but I know that you sold it to this broker,” then when one witness testifies that it originally belonged to Reuven, the law should favor Shimon just as it would with two witnesses, since he does not contradict the witness. Sema answers that even with two witnesses, the broker would have to swear that he did not purchase it from Shimon. Therefore one witness is insufficient. This is Sema’s intent.
