If a debtor transfers everything he owns to another person to prevent collection of his debt, his scheme is ineffective. The creditor collects his debt from the recipient of the gift even if the loan was oral. The recipient takes an oath, subject to the intent of beit din and without deception or fraud, stating how much he received as a gift from Reuven, and beit din collects the creditor's debt from everything he received from Reuven. If the recipient earned profit through commerce or interest using the gift, however, beit din does not collect the profit. Even with land encumbered to the lender, if a purchaser or gift recipient consumed its produce for several years, the creditor later collects the land as it stands but does not recover the produce already consumed. {Rama: If it appears to beit din that they did not intend a scheme but a genuine gift, the recipient acquires it, even though the donor's purpose was to place the property beyond the creditor's reach.}
