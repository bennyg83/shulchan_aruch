Baer Heitev cites the Shach that this refers only to the deposit half of an iska, while the loan half has only the status of an ordinary creditor's debt, and that this appears from siman 108:4. Dagul MeRevavah responds that he finds no proof there beyond the familiar rule that an iska is half loan and half deposit. In any event, he writes, the Shach's ruling here is incorrect, because the Gemara in Bava Metzia 104b expressly says that even the loan half is called 'iska' for this purpose and does not become ordinary movable property in the hands of the recipient's heirs. Rif, Rosh, Rambam in Laws of Agents and Partners chapter 7, and Tur Yoreh Deah siman 177 likewise state that even a creditor cannot collect from it. The Shach's words therefore contradict the Gemara and all the authorities and have no basis. Tummim and Shaar Mishpat likewise challenge the Shach, as does Beit Meir in Even HaEzer siman 102. Dagul MeRevavah further notes that the Bach at the end of siman 96 rules that where merchandise was supplied on credit and the merchandise itself, or property traceable to it, remains intact, the other creditors have no share in it. Beit Shmuel, siman 90, seif katan 38, similarly writes that where it is known that the debtor purchased merchandise of this type, the goods now in his possession are presumed to be those goods.
