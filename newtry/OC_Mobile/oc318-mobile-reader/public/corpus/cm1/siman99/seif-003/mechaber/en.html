If Reuven gave Shimon an object to sell, and Levi, Shimon's creditor, came and took it for his debt, then if witnesses establish that the object belongs to Reuven, Levi must return it. If witnesses do not establish this, then although Shimon admits that it belongs to Reuven, it is not removed from Levi. If Reuven claims to Levi, 'You know that this object is mine,' Levi must take a heses oath. If Shimon is a broker, objects found in his house of the type he regularly sells are not presumed to be his such that his creditor may seize them. The broker or the owner of the object is believed upon taking an oath. Similarly, if a creditor finds in his debtor's house objects commonly lent or rented, and another person claims that he lent or rented them to the debtor and has two witnesses that they belonged to him, he is believed, and the creditor cannot seize them for his debt.
