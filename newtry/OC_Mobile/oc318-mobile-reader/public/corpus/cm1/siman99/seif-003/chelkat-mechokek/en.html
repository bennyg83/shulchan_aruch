But Shabbat and festival garments are appraised for her. This ruling follows Rivash, who understands Rambam to hold that since a creditor can collect a woman's Shabbat and festival garments, the woman did not acquire them fully. Therefore, they are likewise appraised even for a divorcee because they remain the husband's property. Rosh, however, explicitly disagrees and writes that there is no distinction: whatever he bought and dyed for her, including Shabbat garments, he fully gave her. Above in siman 90:15 the ruling was stated against Rambam, that a husband cannot sell his wife's Shabbat garments even if he lacks means of support, following Maharam cited by Maharik. It therefore appears that if the divorcee is in possession of the amount of her ketubah, we do not remove these garments from her. She can say that she relies on Rosh and Maharam, who are later authorities. See what I wrote above in siman 90, seif katan 51.
