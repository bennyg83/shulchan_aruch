They are believed with an oath. Bedek HaBayit and Bach hold that this applies only where Levi makes a definite claim that the property belongs to Shimon, because one does not swear on an uncertain claim. The Shach holds that even an uncertain claim suffices. Since Levi is a creditor, we make claims on his behalf just as for a purchaser or heir. See below, siman 133. See Tumim, where I cited Rif as also inclining toward requiring an oath even on uncertainty. This is the correct view.
And he has two witnesses, etc. The same applies to a broker: two witnesses are needed to establish that the object belonged to Reuven. Otherwise, who says the broker took it from Reuven? Perhaps he acquired it from someone else who is not this litigant. The reason two witnesses are needed, and one witness does not suffice, is that if Shimon denied the one witness he would be believed with an oath. We make that same claim for Levi, even though Shimon himself admits what the witness says. Shach, correctly. There is no need for Sema's strained explanation. See Tosafot to Bava Batra 30 and examine carefully.
