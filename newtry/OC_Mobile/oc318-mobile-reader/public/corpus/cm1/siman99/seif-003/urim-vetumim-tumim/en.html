If it is known by witnesses, etc. See Urim. The Shach holds that witnesses must also have seen the specific item continuously, for otherwise the debtor could be believed through a migo that he returned it. This requires study. The object is presently in Levi's possession, and Levi himself does not know whether it truly belongs to Reuven. Levi's possession therefore functions like witnesses who presently see the object. How can Shimon claim that he returned it while the very object whose ownership is disputed is sitting in Levi's hands? Even according to the authorities discussed above in siman 72 and at length below in siman 297, who sometimes allow a purchaser to assert whatever defense the seller could have asserted, the cases are different. There the seller is not before us, so we reason that had he been present he might have made a particular claim with a migo, and the purchaser inherits that legal position. Here Shimon himself is present and affirmatively says that the object belongs to Reuven. Because it remains in Levi's possession, the alternative claim of return is unavailable. It therefore requires study whether separate witnesses of continuous observation are necessary. At minimum the matter is disputed among major authorities, and the Shach should not have stated it as settled law.
