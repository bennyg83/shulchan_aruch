And if money later appears to him. See Shach, seif katan 1, who cites the Bach that before the debtor acquires means he is believed, because at that time his statement causes no loss to the holder of the document since he has nothing from which to pay. The Shach disagrees because there remains concern for collusion. Tummim challenges the Shach, arguing that collusion is inapplicable where no assets are known: if the debtor truly had hidden assets, he could simply give them secretly to Shimon, and where he has a migo that he could have given them he should be believed. One cannot answer that he fears secretly giving them lest the matter become known, for that concern would undermine the reasoning in the chapter Chazakat HaBatim. Tummim therefore regards the Bach's words as correct. Ketzot HaChoshen disagrees with Tummim and writes that whenever one's statement harms others he is not believed even where collusion as such is not a concern. Responsa Pri Tevuah, siman 59, argues that Ketzot's reasoning has no foundation, but concludes for another reason that the Shach is primary against Urim VeTummim, because there is indeed a real concern for collusion; see there at length.
