If it is known through witnesses that this article belongs to Reuven, etc. In the Perishah I explained that the witnesses must testify not merely that the article once belonged to Reuven, but that it still belongs to him. For example, they were present when Reuven delivered it to Shimon and instructed him to sell it on Reuven’s behalf. In that case it remains presumptively Reuven’s. It is not enough that witnesses recognize it and know that it previously belonged to Reuven, because perhaps Reuven sold it to Shimon. On what basis could it then be removed from Levi, who presently possesses it? Only with objects normally lent or rented, where Reuven claims that he lent it to Shimon, is testimony that it belonged to Reuven sufficient. In that situation we say that it remained his and came into Shimon’s possession by loan. I proved this from the Tur’s wording. See there.
