They compel him. The Shach agrees with this ruling, because we do not take account of the debtor's oath in such a way that beit din would act contrary to law merely because he swore. This is contrary to the Sema. See there and Responsa Maharit, siman 128. The Taz writes that one cannot object that this is like an oath not to repay a debt, which does not take effect. Here no right of priority exists in property not yet acquired, and the debtor could choose not to engage in any profitable activity thereafter. He can therefore validly swear that what he earns will be used for the later creditor.
