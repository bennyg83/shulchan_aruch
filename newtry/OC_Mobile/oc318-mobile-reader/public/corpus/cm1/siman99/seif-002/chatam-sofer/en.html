Ibid., Shach, seif katan 6, s.v. "the conclusion," "half of the iska." Note: Ketzot HaChoshen concludes that the entire iska is taken first, since it does not become ordinary movable property in the hands of the recipient's heirs, as explained in Bava Metzia 104b. All the more so under the laws of the country, if one purchased merchandise on credit and that very merchandise is still extant, the merchant from whom it was received first takes his own property. The same applies to deposited money even if the custodian had permission to use it, so long as he has not yet used it, for the depositor takes his own money first. So too wrote Chacham Tzvi in his glosses to siman 73; see there, seif 19, in his glosses to the Taz. See also Tzemach Tzedek, siman 117, and Beit Shmuel, siman 90, seif katan 38, concerning one who bought merchandise on credit and it remains extant, or property that came directly from that merchandise. However, Shach, seif katan 7 and 8, implies that this applies specifically to an iska, in accordance with the proof of the Tzemach Tzedek. I likewise proved in Responsa Chatam Sofer, Choshen Mishpat, siman 55, that neither strict law nor enactment grants such priority in the broader case. See there.
