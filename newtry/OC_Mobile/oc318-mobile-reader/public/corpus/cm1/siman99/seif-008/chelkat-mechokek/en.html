Jewelry that others gave her, etc. The same applies to money, and also to gifts given at times other than the marriage, because with third-party gifts there is no distinction. See above, siman 90:15. One should note that here the husband's rights appear stronger in jewelry he gave her than in jewelry others gave her. Jewelry he supplied is appraised against her in widowhood and divorce, whereas jewelry given by others is her nichsei melog and is not appraised. Above, however, siman 90 rules that he cannot sell Shabbat and festival clothing he gave her even if he has no means of support, while items others gave her can be used to support himself from their produce. This can be reconciled. While she remains married, he cannot sell what he provided for her adornment. Others, however, cannot by an ordinary gift deprive the husband of his ordinary rights unless they use an effective express condition. Here we are dealing with widowhood and divorce. Then the woman's right in what others gave her is stronger, because the principal belongs entirely to her as with all nichsei melog. The husband's own gift, by contrast, was given only for as long as he lived with her and wanted her adorned before him.
