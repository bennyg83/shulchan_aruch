For anyone who comes, etc. See Tumim. In the first case stated in the main text, this reason is not needed. If a creditor later comes to collect, the sale itself will be voided, and the buyer can demand return of the purchase money from the seller, as explained below in siman 111. The practical difference is where the parties expressly stipulated that if a creditor or ketubah claimant comes to collect, the sale should be void but the purchase money should remain a gift with the seller, or where the seller afterward gave the money notes to his sons. In those situations the wife has no priority in the money, and therefore an additional reason is needed to defeat the sham. See there. I also discussed a responsum of Chavot Yair concerning one who gave a complete debt document to a relative, later married, and when collection occurred the relative produced the earlier document ahead of the wife's ketubah. Chavot Yair ruled that the wife nevertheless collects her ketubah. In Tumim I wrote that his ruling does not appear correct. See there at length.
