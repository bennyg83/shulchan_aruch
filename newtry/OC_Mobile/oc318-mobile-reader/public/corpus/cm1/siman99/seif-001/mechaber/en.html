How a borrower is required to swear concerning whether he has property; an unspecified ban upon anyone who knows of his property; whether he is honest or deceitful; and whether he transferred his property as a gift. It contains 8 se'ifim.
If the borrower has no claim against the document except that he has no means to pay, and nothing is found beyond the property left to meet his essential needs, the Geonim instituted that he take an oath resembling a Torah oath while holding a sacred object. He swears that he owns nothing beyond what is left for him, concealed nothing with others, and made no gift on condition that it be returned. This oath includes a commitment that from everything he earns or that comes into his possession or domain, to the extent his means allow, he will not feed or clothe his wife or children, care for them, or make a gift to anyone. He may retain only food for thirty days and clothing for twelve months, each appropriate for him: neither the fare of gluttons, drunkards, or princes, nor the clothing of lowly people or officials, but his ordinary standard. Everything beyond his own needs must be given to the earliest creditor, and then successively to each creditor, until every debt is paid. {Rama: If he did not swear concerning the future that he would give the creditor everything he acquired, the creditor may require him every thirty days to swear that he owns nothing. First, an unspecified ban is imposed upon anyone who knows that so-and-so has disclosed or concealed property and does not inform beit din. If he has many creditors, he need not take this oath separately for each; one oath covers them all. If money is seen in his possession before or after this oath, or debt instruments written in his name are in his possession, and he says the assets belong to others, were designated for other creditors he owes, or are an investment entrusted to him, he is not heard without proof. Even though he has a migo that he could have returned them or said, 'This never occurred,' he is not believed, because it is a migo against the presumption that whatever is found in a person's possession belongs to him. Others disagree. If it is known that he holds an investment belonging to others, however, he is believed to say that the money in his possession belongs to that specified investment through a migo that he could have given it to its owner. See above, siman 98, and notes 11 and 40.}
