(Gloss to seif 1, against a presumption) Although siman 78 states that a migo may prevail against a presumption, Maggid Mishneh distinguishes a particularly strong presumption such as this. A migo is not accepted against it, because a person is prone to collude in order to remove another’s money. See what I wrote concerning Sema in siman 47, seif katan 4, regarding a deposit in his hands. Rivash, responsum 481, rules that if a note states that payment is due whenever a particular creditor demands it, and that creditor himself owes others, those other creditors may collect the debt even if he refuses to demand payment.
