If nothing further is found belonging to the borrower, etc. The phrase “nothing is found” is not exact in the sense of excluding a case where the borrower owns part of the amount but not enough to satisfy the entire debt. Even in that case he is required to swear. The Mechaber merely follows the Rambam’s wording at the beginning of chapter 2 of Hilchot Malveh, where the Rambam first says that according to Torah law, if nothing at all is found belonging to the borrower, he goes on his way, and then explains that when the Geonim saw that fraudulent debtors had multiplied, they instituted an oath. The Rambam’s opening phrase was intended as a stronger statement concerning Torah law. The Mechaber abbreviated the discussion and wrote only the practical institution of the oath, while retaining the Rambam’s opening wording. Alternatively, “nothing” does not contrast with “some portion,” but refers to the three categories from which payment may be made: cash, movable property, and land. Payment is primarily from cash when available; if there is no cash, from movable property; if there is no movable property, from land. If the borrower says he has no cash or movable property and wishes to pay in land, a general ban is pronounced against anyone who has cash or movable property but refuses to pay from them and insists upon paying only in land. He is not, however, required to swear merely because he chooses land, since he is in fact offering payment. The Geonic oath was instituted only when the borrower says that he has no property at all, not even land, as the Tur and Mechaber explain in siman 101. Thus, when the Mechaber says here that “nothing” was found, he means that not even land was found, and then the oath applies.

The Baal HaTerumot says the same. In the Derishah I explained from the Baal HaTerumot that this is why the borrower must swear on this point. If he gave property as a returnable gift for six months specifically as an evasion device, then the gift is not treated as a valid gift at all, and the creditor may immediately take the property from the recipient. If, however, the gift was not made to evade creditors, it remains a valid gift until the agreed return date. The borrower has already sworn that whatever property later comes into his hands will be used to repay the creditor, and certainly this includes property that returns to him when the temporary gift expires.
