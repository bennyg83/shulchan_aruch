The claimant has been established as one who is not satisfied, etc. Some explain that he is not doubtful about the debtor's poverty but knows that he is poor. The phrase "the claimant has been established" does not fit that reading well; it should have said that this is also known to the claimant. Rather, the meaning is that this claimant has been established as a person who is not content to leave the poor debtor alone: even though he knows the debtor has nothing, he wishes to distress him. In the Tur's Beit Yosef edition the wording is "regarding this person's matter."
You shall not be to him as a creditor. Although the Tur and Mechaber in siman 97, seif 15, write that when one comes to collect from a debtor who refuses to pay it is permitted in certain circumstances to enter his house and coerce him until he pays, that applies specifically where the debtor is presumed to possess means.
