(Seif 3) “If it is known through witnesses,” etc. The Tur writes that the object is known to belong to Reuven. Below, regarding articles ordinarily lent, he writes that there are witnesses that it previously belonged to him. These both mean that it was originally Reuven’s, in accordance with Sema and not Bedek HaBayit. Bedek HaBayit asks why the first clause requires the possessor to return it; perhaps Reuven sold it to Shimon. He answers that since Shimon does not claim purchase, beit din does not make that claim for him. This is incorrect. Since whatever is in a person’s possession is presumed to be his, we certainly presume that Reuven sold it to him, provided there was a written conveyance where one is required. In Sefer Bedek HaBayit, regarding the statement that the broker must swear, the author asks why any oath is required when the owner does not make a definite claim. He answers that if the owner makes a definite claim the broker must swear, but otherwise only a general ban is imposed; the text was abbreviated because the distinction was understood. Bach cites this. That answer is incorrect. The presumption just mentioned is itself equivalent to a definite claim. Rama likewise appears to hold this, for he rules without qualification that an oath is required.
