Even so, his gift remains hers. The reason is that clothing and jewelry are provided so that the woman adorn herself before her husband. His intent is not a complete gift, but only that she beautify herself while married to him. If she offends against him, he did not intend that she take the items and leave. But if he gives his wife land, money, or other movable property not intended for wearing or adornment, she collects it whether widowed or divorced, so long as she is not a rebellious wife. This is no worse than the additional third that he adds in the ketubah. A person who gives a gift to his friend or beloved does not require a guarantee that the recipient will always remain beloved. See Responsa Rivash, siman 301. If the husband is one whom beit din compels to divorce, the gift follows the law of the additional ketubah, as explained below in siman 154. See Rambam, Hilchot Ishut chapter 15.
