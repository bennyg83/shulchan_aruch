This constitutes a pledge for the money. Since without the pledge he would not become obligated because it is an asmachta, he likewise does not become obligated by means of the pledge, for where there is no principal obligation there is no effective pledge. Rather, he must obligate himself through a kinyan sudar, and then the lien takes effect upon the pledge (Sema).
The words “not as an asmachta” should read “before an important beit din,” and the conjunction should be deleted, meaning that “not as an asmachta” refers to an important beit din (Sema).

Although in seif 11 he wrote that there are those who disagree and maintain that even where the earnest money is to be forfeited it is ineffective, there the reason is that since the seller did not become obligated, the party who waived is likewise exempt (Sema). The Taz disagrees and maintains that here the case is one in which he said “from now”; without “from now,” even concerning something in his possession it is an asmachta, as is evident from Tosafos and the Mordechai.
