They play on credit. See Baer Heitev and Responsa Shevut Yaakov II, siman 164. Reuven claimed that he had lent Shimon a sum of money and demanded repayment. Shimon answered that he had gambled with Reuven and won all of Reuven's money, then asked Reuven to lend him money, received the loan, gambled again, and Reuven won it all back in the game; Shimon therefore refused to repay. Shevut Yaakov rules that if, when the money was lent, it came into Shimon's possession and he lifted it or placed it in his pocket in a manner effecting acquisition, the debt immediately arose and he must repay it. But if Reuven merely placed the money on the table and Shimon never acquired it, the money remained in the lender's possession, and the situation is like gambling on credit, where one cannot recover gambling losses from the other party. A comparable distinction appears in Ketubot 31 and in Shulchan Aruch, siman 351, regarding one who drags property out of the owner's domain.
