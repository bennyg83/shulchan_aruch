You shall acquire such and such money. Beit Shmuel writes that where collateral is given for an engagement penalty, the party must say, "If I retract, you shall acquire the body of this object." Otherwise there is no underlying debt and therefore no effective pledge, under the rule, "There is no maneh here and there is no pledge here." Taz says that engagement penalties suffer from two possible defects, asmachta and the rule of no maneh and no pledge. Embarrassment can remove the asmachta, but the pledge itself does not become effective unless the recipient acquires the body of the pledged object. At first this is difficult. The reason an ordinary pledge is ineffective without a debt is that a person's property is only guarantor for his personal obligation. Here, if the withdrawing party is truly liable for the embarrassment, there should already be a debt to which the pledge can attach. The answer is that the embarrassment liability arises only later, when he actually withdraws. A pledge cannot presently be subjected to a debt that does not yet exist. That would be a pledge for future property. But if he transfers the body of the pledge now, subject to the condition that it become final upon withdrawal, the object itself is acquired now conditionally. That is not a transfer of future property. This also explains Tosafot's discussion of dedicating an object to secure a statutory fine before the fine has been adjudicated. Since the offender's body is not yet obligated, an ordinary pledge cannot attach. The fact that the underlying event occurred does not mean the fine can be transferred to another before judgment. The victim may in some contexts waive because the original event that could generate liability has occurred, but assigning the future fine to someone else is still a transfer of something not yet in legal existence. Accordingly, in engagement cases the decisive point is this. If there was a proper kinyan creating the penalty obligation, then the debt exists as a present binding undertaking and the pledge can secure it. If there was no kinyan, mere embarrassment does not itself create the debt. In that case one must transfer the body of the pledge conditionally. This is also why Beit Yosef's real question concerned the need for an eminent beit din to cure asmachta, not the need for a kinyan itself.
