This applies when he acquired through one of the methods of acquisition, etc. The Tur cites the Rambam with explanation: When do we say that upon fulfillment of the condition the acquisition is effective? Where he presently acquired through one of the valid methods of acquisition, but made it conditional. If the condition is fulfilled, the sale stands. But if he did not presently acquire, and only stipulated that if the condition is fulfilled he will later acquire, then even after the condition is fulfilled he does not acquire. For example, if he sold or gave him a house on condition that he go to a certain place on a certain day, and he made chazakah now, then if he later fulfills the condition he acquires, and if not he does not. But if he said, "If you go to such and such place on that day I will sell you this house," and the recipient fulfills the condition and only afterward makes chazakah, he does not acquire, because this is an asmakhta. In the Perishah and Derishah I wrote that although the wording appears to distinguish merely between making the kinyan now and making it later, that is not the Rambam's intent. Even a kinyan made now, if it is conditional, can itself involve asmakhta unless he says "from now," which according to the Rambam removes asmakhta. Nor is it correct to say that the Rambam silently meant a present kinyan "from now," because in his illustrative first case he does not mention those words at all, and only later in a separate law does he say that "from now" eliminates asmakhta. Moreover, in the second case the Rambam uses the formulation "if you go," while in the first he says "on condition that you go," and he concludes that the second is asmakhta because the acquisition was made dependent upon doing some act. The Rambam's real distinction is between a case where the seller's principal intention is to transfer the property and he merely wishes the recipient to fulfill a condition, which resembles the condition of the tribes of Gad and Reuven and is not asmakhta, and a case where his principal intention is that the other perform the condition and he uses the promised sale only as an inducement. That is asmakhta. This is reflected precisely in the Rambam's examples. His statement that afterward the recipient made chazakah is a greater novelty, teaching that even though he took possession after fulfilling the condition, he still does not acquire because the original undertaking was only an asmakhta. The Mechaber abbreviates here and omits the illustrations because he follows his own understanding of the Rambam. In any event, when the Mechaber concludes, "unless he said to him, acquire from now," this does not refer merely to the immediately preceding words. Rather, it means that in such a form he does not acquire at all unless the acquisition is made presently with "from now," as I explained in the Derishah.
