Seif 16, gloss. There are places where the custom is to write two documents, etc. Note: The Rama abbreviated here. The source is explained in Even HaEzer siman 50, as Baer HaGolah notes, and the Mechaber rules there that the document must be structured so that the obligation takes effect immediately, while if the party performs the stipulated act the creditor forgives the debt. This indicates that the obligation must arise unconditionally at once, with subsequent performance operating as forgiveness. If instead the document is deposited without such wording, it can be understood that the debt is to take effect only if the condition is not fulfilled, which is an asmachta. Therefore the debt must attach without condition, and performance must merely trigger forgiveness. It also appears that even with this wording, both sides must deposit corresponding documents. Then there are two reasons supporting enforceability. Even without this, one opinion above holds that where each party undertakes a corresponding risk there is no asmachta, because each is motivated by his desire to acquire and therefore fully obligates himself. Although others disagree, when both have deposited documents they agree that it is effective. But if only one party deposits such a note, it appears that even with the wording described above the undertaking remains an asmachta. Certainly this is so if the other party deposits not a promissory note but money or an object. The first party undertook his obligation only because he assumed that if the other party defaulted, the other too would have to pay. He may not have known the law of asmachta. Since in truth the other party would be exempt upon default, the first party never undertook his obligation on that basis, and he too is exempt. This appears correct to me.
