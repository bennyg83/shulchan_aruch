Some say that these matters apply. According to the Beit Yosef's explanation cited by the Sema, every condition stated without "from now" is an asmachta and ineffective unless a kinyan had already been made. Once a kinyan exists, the situation resembles one who already possesses the relevant property and waives a right to what is presently in another's hands, which is effective according to the Rambam. Therefore the first clause, where a kinyan was made at the time of sale, is effective. The latter clause concerns a kinyan made only after fulfillment of the condition, so it is ineffective. The Sema explains differently. In the first clause the parties said "on condition that," which indicates that the seller's principal intent was to uphold the acquisition, merely subject to performance of the condition, and therefore it is effective. In the latter clause they used "if," which indicates that the seller's principal concern was fulfillment of the condition and that he offered the sale only to induce the purchaser to perform it, so it is an asmachta and there is no acquisition. See the Beurim. The principal distinction is that in the first clause the seller said, "It is sold to you on condition," and therefore it is effective, whereas in the latter he said, "I will sell." The Rambam follows the opinion in siman 245 that language such as "I will sell" or "I will give" is ineffective because it remains an asmachta unless he says "from now."
