Some say this applies only, etc. The Rambam’s words here are based on Rav Hai Gaon. He writes that if one says, “This sale is sold to you if you do such and such,” he did not complete the transfer at that time because he did not say “from now.” Even if the condition is later fulfilled, the transaction is void as an asmachta, because acquisition of the land was deferred until after fulfillment of the condition rather than taking effect at the time of the kinyan. But whenever he states that the field is acquired immediately and only then imposes a condition to be fulfilled later, there is no asmachta. Examples are where he says “from now” or “on condition,” as in the case of one who lends money on a field, established by the Gemara as one where he said “from now.” In Kiddushin 60, “on condition” causes kiddushin to take effect immediately, with the performance to follow. The same applies to divorce, as explained there and in Gittin 74. Even if the get is torn or burned before the condition is fulfilled, it remains effective. The restriction on marrying another before fulfillment is only a stringency lest the condition fail and the get be retroactively void. By strict law she may remarry immediately, because every “on condition” is like saying “from now.” At the end of Gittin chapter 7 all agree that “on condition” means “from now”; their dispute is only concerning “if,” which is an Aramaic conditional expression of the sort described in Bava Metzia as an asmachta. Rav Hai Gaon develops this at length. The general rule is that he distinguishes “if” from “on condition.” The Rambam likewise states the distinction generally and then explains it in Hilchos Mechirah: if a seller says “on condition, etc.,” acquisition is immediate; but if he says “if, etc.,” it is not. He later records the law of “from now.” Thus the phrase here, “when he acquired by one of the modes of acquisition,” means that if he used “on condition,” he acquires immediately and later fulfills the condition, exactly as kiddushin and divorce take effect immediately. But this is only if an actual mode of acquisition was performed. If no kinyan occurred, even if the party later did what had been stipulated, the sale is not acquired, for mere words do not transfer property. This is the Rambam’s general formulation, later explained in detail, and it is likewise Rav Hai Gaon’s intent. This rejects those who misunderstood it as an independent rule.
