The Taz explains the Rambam as follows: whenever the acquisition preceded the making of the condition, meaning that they performed a kinyan from him so that the acquisition would take effect immediately, and only a condition was attached to that acquisition, this indicates that he presently transfers possession by virtue of the acquisition and merely asks the other party to do something. Since the property is already in the recipient's possession, this is not an asmachta. But if they did not perform a kinyan from him now, meaning that the acquisition did not take effect unconditionally from the present, but rather he first stated the condition, "If you do such and such, then you will acquire," his principal intent is the performance of that act, and he transfers ownership only by way of asmachta, because the condition is primary. According to this, there is no need for the Beit Yosef's explanation of the Rambam's phrase "when he acquired by one of the methods," that it means one particular method, namely chazakah, which the Beit Yosef was compelled to say because it is effective without saying "from now." According to my explanation, the reason is that this is deemed as though he said "from now," and the phrase "one of the methods" retains its plain meaning, any one of the methods of acquisition. So it appears to me. Taz.
