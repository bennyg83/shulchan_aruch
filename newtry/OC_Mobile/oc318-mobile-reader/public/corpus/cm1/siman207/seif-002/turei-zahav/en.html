(Seif 2: Some say that this applies only when he acquired through one of the recognized modes of acquisition.) It appears to me that the Rambam’s words mean the following. Whenever the kinyan precedes the stipulation, that is, a kinyan was made from him that the acquisition should take effect immediately and he merely attached a condition to it, the wording indicates that he presently places the property in the other party’s possession by virtue of the kinyan, while requesting some additional act from him. Since the property is already in his possession, this is not an asmachta. But if no kinyan was made from him at present, meaning that the acquisition did not take effect unconditionally from now, but rather he first stated the condition, “If you perform a particular act, then you will acquire,” his primary intent is focused upon performance of that act. It follows that he transferred the property only by way of asmachta, because the condition is the principal matter. According to this, there is no need for what the Beit Yosef writes, at the end of page 254b, concerning the Rambam’s words, “When does this apply? When he acquired through one of the modes,” that this means one particular mode, namely chazakah. The Beit Yosef was forced to say this because the acquisition is effective even without the expression “from now.” According to my explanation, however, the reason is that the case is regarded as though he said “from now.” Thus the phrase, “When does this apply? When he acquired through one of the modes,” is understood literally, as referring to any one of the modes of acquisition.
