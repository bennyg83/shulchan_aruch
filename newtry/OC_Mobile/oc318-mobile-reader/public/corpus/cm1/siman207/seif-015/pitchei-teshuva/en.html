For example, if he said to him: Buy wine for me, etc. See Responsa Chavot Yair, siman 128, concerning Reuven, who sued his brother-in-law Shimon on a deed executed on Reuven's wedding day, when he married Shimon's sister. Shimon had obligated himself by formal kinyan to use his efforts, at his own expense, to obtain for Reuven a right of residence in a certain city. Shimon argued that the deed was void because the undertaking concerned something not within his power, and the deed did not specify what sum he would owe if he failed. Chavot Yair rejects this argument. Indeed, if obtaining such a right were deemed beyond his control, then had the document imposed a fixed sum upon failure, that monetary obligation might have been an asmachta, effective only under the special requirements for asmachta. But where the deed simply obligates Shimon to exert himself, he remains obligated to make the effort at his own expense, even if the expense is considerable. In fact, seeking a municipal right of residence and similar matters is ordinarily considered within a person's practical ability, because one who is prepared to expend money can generally pursue such a right. This is unlike the case in siman 207:3, from the Tur and the Gemara at the end of Bava Metzia, where one gives money to another to buy wine at a particular place. There the concern is that the wine may not be sold to him at the contemplated low price, since the market rate can change. Chavot Yair says this is the reasonable basis of that rule, and he later found it stated in Tosafot there and supported by Terumat HaDeshen, siman 320. Nevertheless, if Shimon actually exerted himself and the authorities demanded an unusually excessive payment, beyond what is normally required of others, he would be exempt because that constitutes an unavoidable circumstance, as explained in the rulings of Mahari, siman 254.
