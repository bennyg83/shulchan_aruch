That he did not. This language implies that even when we know with certainty that no kinyan was made in a distinguished beit din, his admission is effective. The reason is that once he admits and says that he acquired, it is as though we know that he fully resolved and transferred ownership. Therefore his admission is more effective here than where one purports to transfer movable property agav land. At the end of siman 113 it is evident that if we know with certainty that he owns no land, his admission that he transferred movable property agav land is ineffective, because in that case there is no land through which acquisition can operate. Here, however, everything depends on full intent to transfer, and he admitted that he acquired through a distinguished beit din. It is therefore as though witnesses testified that he fully resolved and transferred it. Still, there is some difficulty, because perhaps the effectiveness does not depend solely on his intent but on the principle that beit din may declare property ownerless, as the Mordechai writes.
