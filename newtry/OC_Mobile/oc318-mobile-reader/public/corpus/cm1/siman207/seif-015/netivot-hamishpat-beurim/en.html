The admission of a litigant is like one hundred witnesses. See Sema, seif katan 42, and at the end of siman 113, Sema seif katan 12, where an admission that one owns land is ineffective when it is known that he owns none, because we treat him as having erred. Accordingly, if a document states that a davar shelo ba la'olam was acquired “in an effective manner,” but the claimant admits that the only act was kinyan sudar and no other effective method was used, the acquisition fails. In a davar shelo ba la'olam we do not say that the party certainly resolved to transfer; rather, we may attribute the statement to error, assuming he mistakenly believed kinyan sudar works for future property. This is analogous to Bava Metzia 66, where taking produce from a davar shelo ba la'olam can be treated as mistaken waiver. Witnesses may likewise say that they erred and thought the form used was effective, because in matters where error is common witnesses are believed to explain their mistake, as in siman 13 concerning an antedated document under R. Assi. If the statement is in the party’s own handwriting, he too can say, when supported by witnesses that there was no other kinyan, “I erred and thought kinyan sudar works for a davar shelo ba la'olam,” even if the recipient agrees with the written language. Without such witnesses, however, he is not believed to say “I erred” unless he has a migo, as explained in siman 40 regarding an admission made under the mistaken belief that one was liable. Likewise, taking future fruit can be mistaken waiver even where it was delivered willingly. All the more so can a statement that a future thing was acquired “effectively” be attributed to error. Rama’s ruling in siman 212 that a document should be interpreted as using an effective form applies where neither the witnesses nor the claimant concede that they erred.
