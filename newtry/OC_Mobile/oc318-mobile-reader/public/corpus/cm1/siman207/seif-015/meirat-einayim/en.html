Even though they did not make a kinyan from him, because a litigant's admission, etc. Rama's wording, and likewise Hagahot Maimoniyot there, implies that even where we know with certainty that no kinyan was made in an important beit din, his admission that such a kinyan was made is effective. The reason is that once he admitted and said that he made the kinyan, it is as though we know that he fully resolved to transfer ownership. Therefore his admission is more effective here than in one who says he transferred property agav land. At the end of siman 113 it is evident that if we know with certainty he owns no land, his admission that he transferred agav land is ineffective, because there is no land through which the transfer can take effect. Here, however, the matter depends upon full resolve to transfer, and he admitted that he made a kinyan in an important beit din; it is therefore as though there are witnesses that he fully resolved to transfer. Still, there is some difficulty, because perhaps the matter does not depend solely upon his intent but upon the principle that what beit din declares ownerless is ownerless, as Mordechai writes. For this same reason Rama writes below in seif 19, "Likewise, if the document states that he accepted it upon himself with a severe ban and a Torah oath, as explained above at the end of seif 15 concerning an important beit din." There too the meaning is that even if we know he did not actually accept a severe ban and Torah oath, it is nevertheless effective because by instructing that the document state he accepted this severity, he showed that he fully resolved to transfer.
What he writes, "and even, etc., but he instructed them to write a good document." "A good document" is not precise. Whenever he tells witnesses, "Write a document for me," we presume his intent is for a document drafted with the customary effective clauses. This is evident there from Hagahot Maimoniyot and also from Responsa Maimoniyot, Sefer Kinyan, where the word "good" does not appear. This is why Rama concludes, "See above siman 61, seif 5," meaning that there the source of this rule is stated: one who instructs witnesses to write a document intends all clauses customarily written. See there. Nevertheless, it is possible that Rama added the word "good" on his own based on the conclusion there in siman 61, seif 5, that clauses of special credibility and nullification of moda'ot are not presumed in a gift unless expressly specified. Rama may hold that likewise a clause of acquisition in an important beit din is not automatically included unless he instructed them to write a "good document." This requires study.
