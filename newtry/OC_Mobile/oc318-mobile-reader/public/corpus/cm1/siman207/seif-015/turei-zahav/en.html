(Seif 19, in the gloss.) See above, siman 209. In seif katan 53, the Sema explains the Rama here as applying specifically where there was no act of acquisition. Where there was an act of acquisition, however, the oath reinforces the acquisition. He therefore distinguishes between this case and siman 209, concerning something not yet in existence. He brings support from Responsa Rivash, siman 335, where the questioner assumed that an oath reinforces an acquisition. The Rivash answered that this could be said according to Rabbeinu Tam, who holds that an acquisition made at an important beit din is effective. According to the other authorities, however, who hold that it is effective only if made “from now,” an oath cannot take the place of the words “from now.” Since we rule like Rabbeinu Tam, that an important beit din suffices, the questioner’s reasoning would remain valid. I am astonished that such words should issue from his holy mouth. How can he distinguish between our case and the Rivash, and siman 209? The Rivash later gave the same questioner another answer, in siman 341: “What will you say regarding the Rosh’s ruling about one who transferred to another half of whatever he would earn and swore to him? Will you say that the oath validates the acquisition and treats that which has not yet come into existence as though it had? Rather, the master’s intent is that he is obligated to fulfill his undertaking by force of his oath.” Thus, the Rivash explicitly compares our case, an asmachta, to the case of something not yet in existence in siman 209, whereas the Sema placed a great distance between them. It must therefore be that the Rivash maintains that here as well the oath does not reinforce the acquisition. When the Rivash wrote that according to Rabbeinu Tam one could say that the oath reinforces the acquisition, he did not adopt this as his final conclusion. Rather, he confronted the questioner, who wished to rule that way, and said: According to your reasoning, you could say this under Rabbeinu Tam’s view, but not according to the other authorities, since for them an important beit din makes no difference. In truth, however, even according to Rabbeinu Tam we do not say that an oath reinforces the acquisition. This is why the Rama makes no distinction at all between our case and siman 209. Apart from all this, the plain meaning of the Rama’s words above indicates that in truth we do not rule like Rabbeinu Tam, and that an important beit din alone does not suffice; the words “from now” are also required, as stated in seif 17. After writing all this, I examined the Darkei Moshe, who writes here: “In Responsa Rivash, siman 335, he wrote regarding an asmachta that although it does not effect acquisition, he must nevertheless fulfill his oath. If he dies, his heirs are exempt, and if he obtains release from his oath, he is exempt.” This explicitly accords with what I have written. The master of the Sema did not examine this matter sufficiently.
