An asmachta supported by a kinyan before an authoritative beit din is binding. Rama: Any three judges knowledgeable in the laws of asmachta are considered an authoritative beit din for this purpose. Others require the important beit din of the city or a judge recognized by the public. If a person directs that the deed state that a kinyan was made before an authoritative beit din, the admission is effective even if no such kinyan actually occurred, because a litigant's admission is equivalent to one hundred witnesses. Even directing that a proper obligation document be written is effective as though he had stated this expressly. See siman 61, seif 5. This requires that he deposit his legal rights with beit din and that he not be under compulsion. For example, if one deposits his deed or receipt with beit din and makes a kinyan that if he does not appear on a specified day the document will be delivered to his opponent, then if the day arrives and he does not appear, beit din delivers it, provided it is an authoritative beit din. Rama: Some say depositing the rights is unnecessary where a kinyan was made before an authoritative beit din, and the Rosh writes that this is common practice. If a river prevented him from coming, or he was ill, they do not deliver it. The completion of this law is explained in simanim 55 and 21. Rama: Some say that if the parties deposited pledges against one another under non-Jewish law and documents, even an asmachta is binding by virtue of the law of the kingdom. See siman 68 above.
