Asmachta. This implies that were it not for the asmachta, for example if he removed the asmachta problem, he would have to give the money. This is where he stipulated that the money itself should become a gift, because otherwise this would be a promissory note whose lien has been extinguished, as stated at the end of siman 55 in the name of the Ramban, Rashba, Ran, and other authorities. Although the Rambam holds in seif 11 that asmachta does not apply to something already in a person's own possession, this case is different because the document is in the hands of a third party and he made the result depend on return of the document. Indeed, if he gave the document to the lender himself and told him that the money should become a gift, the lender acquires what is in his possession and there is no asmachta. The Maggid Mishneh writes this according to the Rambam, and the Beit Yosef cites him. The same appears in Responsa Ramban, siman 1, in explaining the Rambam. This appears to me the principal understanding of the Rambam. See above, siman 21, Responsa Rashdam, siman 240, and Responsa Mabit, vol. 1, siman 60. Shach.
