(See there: he waives it from now.) Our master asks that once the words "from now" are present, according to Rambam that alone should suffice. In my humble opinion, "from now" alone does indeed suffice. The concern was different. Sometimes a person obligates himself to pay a very large sum if he does not complete the match, yet he presently has no assets to secure that obligation. He is therefore trying to create a lien on property not yet acquired. Although our master writes in this siman in the Rosh's name that a person may obligate himself in a large ketubah even when he presently has nothing, the discussion there shows that the Rif does not accept that approach. Alternatively, here, because the matter is an asmachta, everyone agrees that the obligation does not attach to property he does not yet own. Since the effectiveness depends on "from now," if he presently owns no property there is nothing upon which "from now" can operate. Therefore, they structured the arrangement in reverse: one party admits that he presently owes the other a large amount, and the creditor can waive that existing debt from now if the match is completed.
