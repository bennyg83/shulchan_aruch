(Seif 3: In order to go to a certain place.) The Gemara states that a man sold his property intending to ascend to Eretz Yisrael. He ascended, but could not establish residence there because he lacked food or lodging. Rava said: Anyone who ascends does so intending to reside there, and this man did not manage to reside there. According to this version, the sale is void where he expressly stated that he was selling on that condition. According to another version, his intent was merely to ascend, and he did ascend. The Rosh and the early editions of the Tur rule in accordance with the second version: if at the time of sale he expressly said that he was selling in order to reside in a particular place, the sale remains valid once he ascended there. This is astonishing, for that wording can only be interpreted where he mentioned ascent alone. If he explicitly mentioned residence, and not merely going, then so long as he did not reside there, even according to the second version the sale should be void. I indeed found in the rulings of the Rosh that the text should be emended accordingly, for there he agrees that the sale is void. It therefore appears to me that the early editions of the Tur contain a scribal error, and that the text should read, “in order to go and reside in a particular place,” as it appears in the Rosh’s abridged rulings. The reason is that although he also mentioned residence, going is the principal matter, and the reference to residence merely explains where he intends to go. The Rambam likewise writes, “and he is unable to ascend,” indicating that he rules in accordance with the second version and does not mention residence. It therefore appears to me that if a person sells his house and says that he wishes to perform a particular act in a certain place, but goes there and does not find an opportunity to perform it, the sale is void even though he went, because he did not make the journey itself the condition. It further appears that if he said, “to go and to reside,” residence is a distinct additional condition, and residence therefore also prevents the sale from taking effect. But if he said, “to go in order to reside,” the wording implies that the going is for the purpose of residence, so residence itself is not part of the condition. In any event, even if his uncertain wording also reflected an intention concerning ascent, such unspoken intention is merely a matter in the heart and is ineffective. An express disclosure of intent is required.
