To go. The Tur states that if at the time of sale he specified, "on condition that I reside in such and such a place," the sale remains valid once he went up there. He thus rules like the second formulation in the Gemara, that the sale was made with the intent to go up, and he did go up. This is astonishing, for that formulation can only be understood when he used language of going up alone. If he mentioned residence rather than going, then so long as he has not actually resided there, even according to the second formulation the sale is void. It therefore appears to me that there is a scribal error in the Tur and it should read: "on condition that I go to reside in such and such a place." This is explicit in the Rosh's rulings. Although he mentioned residence too, the going is primary and he merely specified where he intended to go. The Rambam likewise writes that he was unable to go up, indicating that he rules according to the second formulation and does not mention residence. From this it appears to me that if one sold his house and stated that he wished to perform a certain activity in a particular place, then went there but found himself unable to perform it, the sale is void even though he went, because he never made the going itself the stated purpose. It further appears that if he said "to go and to reside," residence is an additional condition and residence too prevents the sale from taking effect. But if he said "to go to reside," implying that the going is for the purpose of residence, then residence is not an independent condition. In any event, although his intention undoubtedly included going up, unexpressed intentions are not legally effective; explicit disclosure is required. Taz. See Responsa Mahara Sasson, simanim 77 and 78; Responsa Rashach belonging to vol. 2, siman 1; Responsa Maharam Alshich, simanim 30 and 78; and Responsa Maharam of Lublin concerning these laws in the Get of Vienna. Regarding the Tur's statement that if he stipulated "on condition that I reside there" and he went up there the sale stands, Maharshal in responsum 38 likewise cites this in the Tur's name and writes that this also appears from the Rosh. I have not understood their intent, because neither the Gemara nor the Rosh indicates this at all. This alludes to the Taz's explanation above. There the case is one who sold with the intent to go up, not with the intent to reside. This requires study. See Responsa Rashdam, simanim 190, 232, 247, 260, 289, and 397. Shach.
Movable property. The Taz writes that according to this, when the Tur writes in seif 7, "and the same law applies to one who sells an article in order to purchase with its proceeds," it means that the seller of the article intended to purchase a particular field with the proceeds. This is explicit in Rashi, cited by the Beit Yosef. But with respect to a buyer there is no distinction between land and movable property. This also appears from the Beit Yosef, who writes: "One who sells any item and reveals that he wishes to take the proceeds to such and such a place may retract." So writes Responsa Shaar Ephraim, siman 140. He accordingly ruled in practice concerning a buyer who, at the conclusion of the sale, revealed that he was purchasing an article for a certain person, but did not explicitly stipulate that if that person did not wish to take it he could return the article. The buyer had not yet paid, and afterward the intended person refused the article. He ruled that the sale is rescinded and the seller must accept the article back, provided that the buyer had not yet paid. See there.
