Seif 19. A vow, oath, and handshake undertaking are effective even where the monetary undertaking is an asmachta, etc. Note: Although the Mechaber rules this simply, in my opinion it is not compelled by the sources. Sanhedrin 25a states, according to Rabbi Yehudah in the name of Rabbi Tarfon, that neither of two people whose nazirite vows depend upon an uncertain condition becomes a nazir, because naziriteship requires clear designation. Rashi there indicates that asmachta applies even to naziriteship, and vows follow the same law. The same would apply to a handshake undertaking and an oath. The Ramban's interpretation, cited by Tosafot there, likewise implies that vows and monetary obligations share the same law regarding asmachta. According to Rabbeinu Yitzchak's explanation there is no proof that asmachta applies to naziriteship, but at least according to Rashi and Ramban it does. True, we rule there that the naziriteship takes effect, and we also rule regarding dice players that their undertaking is not void because of asmachta. But that is because, as the authorities explain, the particular case of gambling does not constitute asmachta. For that same reason such a conditional nazirite vow is not an asmachta, since the Gemara compares the two cases. Nevertheless, the comparison shows that monetary undertakings and vows are governed by the same principle. One who holds that gambling is indeed invalid because of asmachta accordingly rules in the nazirite case like Rabbi Yehudah in the name of Rabbi Tarfon. Thus there is at least a dispute: according to Rashi and Ramban, wherever monetary law would classify the undertaking as an asmachta, vows and oaths are subject to the same concern. This is not the place to elaborate further. Perhaps Hashem will grant me the opportunity to explain it more fully elsewhere. A learned man once asked me about Maharsha to Sanhedrin 24b, s.v. "in such a case it is not an asmachta," who asks, according to Rabbeinu Tam, why the Gemara compares the matter to naziriteship. The questioner noted that this is the very question Tosafot themselves ask in Bava Metzia 74a. I answered that he was correct, and there is no real distinction. With due respect to Maharsha, the Tosafot there must have escaped his notice at that moment. The question in Bava Metzia and Maharsha's question here can be answered simply in light of Tosafot, s.v. "that is Rabbi Yehudah in the name of Rabbi Tarfon," namely that the Gemara's reasoning concerning naziriteship is not based upon asmachta. There is also another possible resolution: Rabbi Yehudah follows his own position that it is better than either extreme to vow and pay, as stated in Nedarim and Chullin. According to him, a nazir too is called holy, and therefore each party may genuinely prefer to gain the spiritual status of naziriteship. I write this only as a brief observation, for I am occupied with other matters.
