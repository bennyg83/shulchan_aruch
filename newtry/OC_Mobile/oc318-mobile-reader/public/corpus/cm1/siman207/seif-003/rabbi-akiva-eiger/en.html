[Shach, subparagraph 8] See Darkei Moshe. Note: Darkei Moshe cites the Ran that some authorities hold that if one transfers property to another on condition that the recipient give him one hundred, and the recipient tenders the money but the transferor refuses to accept it, the recipient acquires, because tender against the other’s will is considered giving. Other authorities hold that this is an unresolved monetary question. Accordingly, with land the recipient does not acquire, while with movable property already delivered to his hand we do not remove it from him. See there. If one says, "My field is sold to you if I do not give you one hundred," and he wishes to pay but the other refuses to accept, the buyer does not acquire, because whenever he tenders payment he fulfills the condition, and he is also prevented by circumstances beyond his control. See Ran, chapter 3 of Shevuos, page 296. If he says, "On condition that you give to so and so," and that person refuses to accept, the Rashba cited in Beit Yosef at the beginning of this siman implies that in such a case tender against the recipient’s will certainly counts as giving. See there.
