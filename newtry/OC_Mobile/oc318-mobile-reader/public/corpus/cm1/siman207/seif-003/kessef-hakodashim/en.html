The laws of conditions. The practice of the local beit din is that in conditions attached to sales of movables and similar monetary transactions, the formal technical rules of conditions are generally not insisted upon. Beit Shmuel, siman 38, cites the rule that in monetary matters a condition need not be doubled, and Netivot HaMishpat writes similarly. Accordingly, the requirement that the affirmative precede the negative has no independent role here: if either the affirmative or negative statement alone would suffice, then when both are stated their order should not matter. Likewise, the requirement that the condition precede the act may not require close scrutiny, because most sales are completed through acts of acquisition external to the body of the property, and while the parties remain engaged in the same matter they may still retract. Thus the condition can still be regarded as connected to the transaction. Even though a person may always retract within the brief time of immediate speech, and the sources do not expressly distinguish whether “condition before act” means only after that brief period, perhaps the same reasoning applies. This requires study. In Yoreh Deah 236 there is a dispute regarding a condition tied to a time and whether the time is exact. The matter must be judged according to context and whether the circumstances show that punctuality was material. If afterward the other party, knowing of the condition, nevertheless accepts or delivers the merchandise, it appears that according to all views the transaction remains valid. If a buyer has already received some of the animals he purchased, while others were to remain with the seller for a further period, and the buyer fails to take the remaining animals at the scheduled time, the entire sale should not thereby be cancelled. Once the buyer has taken, slaughtered, or sold part of the herd without separate accounting, all the more so if he transported them elsewhere, the parties plainly no longer contemplated rescission of the whole transaction. The seller may have the remaining animals received or sold under beit din supervision. Where merchants fixed a strict date for payment of the purchase price, in light of the discussions of Taz and Nekudot HaKesef, and at the due date the buyer paid almost all but a small part of the sum, even though merchants sometimes accept substitutes for money, one may say that failure to satisfy the precise payment condition means the condition was not fulfilled at all. If the timing was expressly exact with respect to the entire payment, it is exact even as to a small unpaid remainder.
