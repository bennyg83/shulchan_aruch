One who, etc., in order to go, etc. Kiddushin 50a records the two incidents of a man, etc. This is the source for the alternatives “or he was prevented, etc.” and “or he did not, etc.” See Tosafos there, s.v. “that an accident occurred, etc.”
Or he was prevented, etc. According to Rava’s latter version, where the sale was made with the intent to ascend. Therefore the rule applies specifically where the road was blocked, or where he did ascend but an unavoidable event occurred on the way, according to Rabbi Elazar’s latter version.
Or he was unable to ascend. According to Rav Ashi’s latter version there.
Or to purchase wheat. See Rif in Kesubos there.
Specifically, etc. Rashi there, 49b, s.v. “his property, land,” and Rosh there. Tosafos, s.v. “matters,” writes that the inference is difficult and Ri explains that the surrounding circumstances must be so clear that “we are witnesses” to his intent. Such an inference ordinarily applies specifically to land and not movable property. This follows Tosafos, who require the formal rules of conditions even in monetary law, unlike Rashbam’s approach. See Even HaEzer siman 38.
