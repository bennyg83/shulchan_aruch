Within. This distinction is explicit in the Rosh. After three years it is a mistaken waiver, because the borrower allowed the lender to consume the produce believing that under the condition the land had become absolutely vested in the lender. Later he discovered that it had not. This resembles a person who allowed his fellow to take an object from his domain because he thought it belonged to that fellow, and it turned out not to be his. Within the three years, however, he knew the field remained redeemable and nevertheless allowed the lender to consume the produce as interest. Ultimately this is only rabbinic interest. Thus far the Sema. See Responsa R. Moshe Alshich, siman 23, and Responsa Maharashdam, siman 263.
They disagree. In other matters we do not say that a mistaken waiver is no waiver at all and must be returned. Rather, once the property has come into the recipient's possession, we do not remove it from him. This case is different. Here, where he lent money on a field, even though the produce already came into the lender's possession, it is nevertheless removed from him because the entire transaction began as a loan. During the first three years he consumed it as interest, though only rabbinic interest, and after three years it was a mistaken waiver, as explained above. Other transactions did not begin as loans. According to the first opinion cited by the Mechaber, which requires returning even the produce consumed within three years, there is all the more reason to distinguish between this case, which began as a loan and is considered fixed interest, and other asmachta cases that did not begin as loans. The Gemara itself distinguishes: there it is a loan, here it is a sale, at the end of folio 66 in the chapter Eizehu Neshech, although there are differing explanations. Sema.
