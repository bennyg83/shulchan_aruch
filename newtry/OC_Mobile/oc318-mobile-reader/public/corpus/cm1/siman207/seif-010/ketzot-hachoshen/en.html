Mistaken waiver or mistaken gift. Bava Metzia says that now that the Sages have ruled asmachta does not acquire, the land and its fruits return in the loan case. The Gemara asks from Rav Nachman's ruling concerning one who sold future palm fruits, where if the buyer seized and ate them they are not removed from him. It answers: there it was a sale, here a loan. Tosafot, citing Rabbeinu Tam, explains that in the loan case the borrower never intended a true sale. The lender's taking of fruits rests on a mistaken waiver. In the palm fruit case, however, the seller knowingly sold the future fruits. Although he can legally retract because the property was not yet in existence, morally he should not retract because he wishes to preserve his reliability. The later nonretraction is therefore a valid waiver, not a mere mistake. Rivash cites another formulation in Rabbeinu Tam's name. In the loan case the transaction was conditional from the outset upon repayment and the borrower never consciously waived the fruits. In the palm fruit case, the seller knew exactly that he was purporting to sell the fruits. His only mistake was in law, believing the sale effective. A waiver made under a mistake of law can still be a waiver. Rosh records this second explanation as Rabbeinu Tam's position. See what I wrote in siman 17 regarding the distinction.
