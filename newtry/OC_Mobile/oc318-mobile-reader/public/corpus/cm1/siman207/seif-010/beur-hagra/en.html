He deducts, etc., because, etc. Bava Metzia 67a records Ravina's ruling. Rif writes that this applies all the more to a mortgage without deduction, which constitutes fixed interest. Rif holds that in a place where the borrower may remove the mortgagee, a mortgage without deduction is Torah interest, while with deduction it is rabbinic interest. In a place where removal is not permitted, a mortgage without deduction is rabbinic interest. A mortgage with deduction in such a place is the dispute later in the passage, and the conclusion prohibits it even for an ordinary person except under the Sura form of mortgage. Raavad agrees, except that he permits deduction where removal is not allowed, understanding Ravina's practice as a stringency. Rashba permits a mortgage with deduction whether removal is allowed or not, while distinguishing only a mortgage without deduction. Tosafot hold that where removal is permitted and there is no deduction, the interest is rabbinic, while the other arrangements are permitted. Rashi appears similar. Rambam has a fifth approach. According to Rif, Raavad, Rashba, and also Rambam in the Mishna's case where the borrower can remove him, the interest is Torah law. According to Tosafot it is rabbinic. Tosafot explain Ravina's ruling as limited to a sale, because the issue is mistaken waiver, not a loan. Ran's statement that the fruits are returned refers to fruits eaten after three years, because they were consumed under the belief of a sale and therefore involve mistaken waiver.
