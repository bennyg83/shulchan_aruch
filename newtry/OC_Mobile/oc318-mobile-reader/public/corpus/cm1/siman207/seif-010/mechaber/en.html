Whenever the lender does not acquire the field because the agreement was an asmachta, all produce he consumed must be deducted from the debt, because it constitutes Torah-level interest. Some say that produce eaten during the first three years is not deducted, because it is only rabbinic interest. Rama: Produce eaten after the three years must be returned. Likewise, whenever property was not acquired because of asmachta, even if it came into the recipient's possession it must be returned, because it was received in error. Others disagree and say that once it reaches the recipient's possession he acquires it, and it is not regarded as a mistaken waiver or gift. Only produce eaten after three years must be returned because the arrangement has some element of prohibited interest, being structured as a loan. In other cases he acquires what came into his possession.
