This applies when he acquired. Rambam’s language, cited at the beginning of siman 207, that the underlying property must first have been acquired by an effective kinyan, otherwise the later stipulation is asmachta, may perhaps include a case where the conveyance occurred before the price was fixed or while the object was not yet sufficiently identified. In such circumstances even a declaration that the arrangement applies “from now” may not cure the asmachta. Nevertheless, where the condition is that the buyer may choose whether to keep the transaction, perhaps asmachta does not apply. The seller has made a completed conveyance and has no further basis to object; he fully resolved to transfer the property, while the buyer alone retains the option to return it. The uncertainty in the buyer’s monetary obligation does not necessarily undermine the seller’s completed transfer. But where it is known that the seller sold because he needed money, and according to those who hold that if it later turns out he did not need the money the sale is void, then perhaps even here the seller never fully resolved to convey. According to what the later Baer Heitev writes in seif katan 8 in the name of responsa Shaarei Ephraim, that a buyer’s disclosure of intent can be effective even with movables, the issue becomes more complex. I looked at that responsum some time ago and believe Baer Heitev may not have quoted its precise intent. As I recall, the responsum states only that the matter is an unresolved dispute because two unqualified rulings in Shulchan Aruch appear inconsistent. If, according to that approach, the buyer said within the period of immediate speech that he chooses the merchandise only if it will be of a certain kind or condition, perhaps this too can involve asmachta even where the parties said “from now.”
