In order to go. See Baer Heitev in the name of Taz and Shach, and Chatam Sofer, Choshen Mishpat, siman 70, who explains the Tur. Although unexpressed thoughts ordinarily have no legal effect, some circumstances are so explicit that no verbal statement is required. Thus, where a seller said at the time of sale that he was selling because he intended to move to Eretz Yisrael, it is self-evident that he meant to reside there; no reasonable person sells his property merely in order to ascend and then immediately return. His statement is therefore equivalent to expressly saying that he intended to live there. The Gemara's case merely reflects what occurred and does not imply that a more explicit statement would change the law. The dispute among the Gemara's versions concerns only a case where he found housing there only with great difficulty, and whether such constrained housing counts as successful fulfillment of his intent. Chatam Sofer adds that even the authorities who disagree with the Tur can accept that the ordinary statement “I am going up to Eretz Yisrael” means to reside there. If he found no housing at all, the sale certainly fails. Their disagreement concerns finding housing only with difficulty. If he expressly said “to reside,” that extra language may indicate an intent to reside comfortably. See there.
