Although beforehand, etc. The Gemara says that the sale was made with that intention, and asks whether the intent existed at the moment of sale. The implication is that a statement made beforehand suffices. This is the meaning of 'with that intention, etc.'
Nevertheless, etc. This is like a dangerously ill person who conveyed all his property, one who heard that his son died, and the case of a woman transferring property to evade her husband. In all these cases the transaction may be void even without an express statement, as explained in Bava Batra and Ketubot. See Tosafot in Kiddushin and Ketubot. See cited source 6.
Some say, etc. Riaz derived this from the cases of a dangerously ill person, one who heard that his son died, and a woman evading her husband.
(Supplement) Some say, etc. Under this view one need not invoke the first opinion's broad rule concerning a clearly demonstrated presumption. The cases of the evasive transfer and one who heard that his son died differ because they involve gifts. The same applies to the ruling like Rabbi Elazar ben Azarya at the beginning of the fifth chapter of Ketubot. This resolves Tosafot's question there. After their lengthy distinctions, Tosafot themselves ultimately distinguish a donor, whose transfer depends upon his own intent. (End of supplement.)
