How so? If I come, etc. Rambam likewise has this wording. The Tur, however, cites Rambam as saying, "if I do not come," and there is a practical distinction. Some distinguish and say that asmachta applies specifically where the condition required a positive act and the person failed to perform it, in which case "from now" is needed. But where the condition was that he refrain from an act, and he says, "If I do such-and-such, I will become liable for such-and-such," and then does it, there is no asmachta, because he was supposed not to act. Others say that even in this case there is an asmachta, because perhaps he changed his mind from his original intention and did the act. See Darkei Moshe, who discusses this at length, and see my gloss there.
"Acquire this house from now." He had to write "this" because he holds that a kinyan takes effect only with respect to an identified object. See the Perishah.
And they made a kinyan from him concerning this. The same applies to other methods of acquisition. The novelty is that even if they made a kinyan sudar from him, we do not say that an unspecified kinyan is necessarily "from now," because otherwise how could it take effect after the sudar has returned to its owner. Rather, he must state "from now" explicitly. See the Perishah.
"From now" alone is ineffective. In the Perishah I discussed at length their reasoning and explanation. The Rosh holds that an ordinary kinyan sudar also operates from now, because otherwise how could the matter later take effect after the sudar has already returned to its owner, and nevertheless in a case of asmachta it does not acquire. See there.
That they make the kinyan in an important beit din and from now. In the Derishah I proved that Tosafos, the Rosh, and the Tur hold that kinyan sudar in an important beit din alone suffices and one need not expressly state "from now." An ordinary kinyan in an important beit din is understood as effective from now, unless he expressly stipulated that the acquisition not begin immediately. In that latter case even a kinyan in an important beit din does not help. This explains why the Mechaber concludes in seif 15: "An asmachta upon which they made a kinyan in an important beit din is binding," without Rama adding that according to this opinion both an important beit din and "from now" are required. Likewise, Rama later writes, "Some say that even though he did not deposit his rights, it is effective since they made a kinyan from him in an important beit din," without adding "since he also said from now." We must therefore say that Rama's phrase "from now" here excludes a case where he expressly delayed the start of the acquisition. Alternatively, it refers back to the Mechaber's preceding view that "from now" alone is effective, and Rama means that some say that even when "from now" is used, that alone does not suffice and an important beit din is also required. I wrote this in the Perishah and above in the Sema. Do not object from the law where one lent one hundred gold coins against a field worth more and the borrower said, "If I do not repay within three years it shall be yours from now," where the Tur and Mechaber rule in seif 9 that he acquires, showing that "from now" works without an important beit din. That case differs because the lender rendered him a benefit through the loan and the field also came into the lender's possession initially with some element of acquisition. See there.
If the asmachta is in the form of a penalty. Rashba writes in a responsum that the phrase "on condition" is treated like "from now" only where the condition concerns fulfillment of the act and the condition, such as, "This is your get on condition that you give me two hundred zuz." There he desires both that she give him two hundred zuz, which is the condition, and that the get take effect, which is the act. But where the condition concerns preventing an act, such as where she undertakes not to marry and says, "on condition that if I marry, etc.," we do not find that "on condition" is treated as "from now." To the contrary, we see that she is imposing upon herself a penalty if she marries. Since she says "if I marry," even the phrase "on condition" is not equivalent to "from now"; it means, "subject to this condition, if I marry." Since the obligation is formulated as an "if" and contains no "from now," it is not acquired. These are Rashba's words.
