“From now” alone does not suffice. Rama is too brief here, where fuller explanation was needed. Above, seif 13, he ruled that even when a seller transfers to the buyer on the condition, “If you do such-and-such,” this can constitute asmachta; it is on that case that the statement that “from now” alone does not suffice is directed. It cannot mean that “from now” never validates ordinary conditions, for the Gemara is replete with cases in which “on condition that” or “from now” is effective, such as a gift on condition that it be returned, a transfer on condition that a document be written, and the rule that one who pays for a stolen animal is treated as though the owner said, “When you steal it and pay me, my animal shall be acquired by you from now.” Rather, the limitation applies specifically to an asmachta in which a person imposes a penalty upon himself, such as “If I do not repay you...” and similar formulations. With ordinary conditions, “from now” certainly works. One should not ask: if a distinction must be drawn between self-imposed penalties and other conditions regarding “from now,” how do we know that an ordinary condition without “from now” is asmachta at all, since the Gemara’s classic cases involve penalties? The source is Rava’s broad formulation in Bava Metzia 66 that “every ‘if’ does not acquire,” indicating that conditional language can create asmachta even outside a classic penalty case.
