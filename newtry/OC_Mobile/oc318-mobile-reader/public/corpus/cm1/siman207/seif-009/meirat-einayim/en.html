Even if it was not worth more, etc. The reason is that ordinary people do not wish to sell their property, so this is regarded as an exaggerated undertaking and an excessive statement.
It is an asmachta and he does not acquire. The Gemara says, "Every statement formulated as 'if' is an asmachta and does not acquire." The meaning is that a person who does not finally and absolutely resolve to sell but says, "If this event does not occur, the property shall be sold," is presumed to rely upon his expectation that the event will occur and the sale will therefore never take effect. Had he known that the event would not occur, he would not have conditioned the sale upon it. For example, here he expected to have money and to redeem his field within three years. It follows that from the outset he did not fully resolve to transfer it unconditionally, and therefore the other party does not acquire. This is unlike the condition of the tribes of Gad and Reuven. There Moshe Rabbeinu's intent was clearly to transfer the land fully, but he imposed a doubled condition that if they did not fulfill their obligation, the grant would be reversed. See what I write further below.
"It shall be mine from now," he acquires. Even according to the opinion Rama cites below in seif 14 that "from now" alone is insufficient, this case is different because the property was immediately given into the lender's hand as collateral and security for his money. The arrangement therefore resembles a sale, and the lender also rendered the borrower a benefit. We therefore say that the borrower's intent was for the lender to have a complete acquisition from now, subject only to his right to redeem it within three years. The Rosh writes this. Nevertheless, the lender may not eat the produce during those three years, because the borrower may redeem it within the period, in which case the arrangement was retroactively a loan and the produce would be interest. Rashi explicitly explains this. See the Perishah.
He was particular that it not be pledged, etc. This shows that he fully resolved to transfer it. Otherwise he would not have cared, since he expected to redeem it.
Likewise, if he said, "If I do not repay you, you shall have no collection except from this." The wording of the Gemara, Rosh, and Tur is different: "If he said, 'You shall have no collection except from this,' it is an apotiki." Rashi explains that he said, "If I do not repay you within three years, the entire field will be yours, and even within the three years you shall have no collection except from this field." Thus he can never collect from anything else, whether he ultimately receives the whole field or only part of it. The novelty is that although the field does not become entirely his after three years because that clause is an asmachta, nevertheless the debtor cannot satisfy him with another piece of land. The Rosh explains likewise and writes that the debtor can in any event satisfy him with money, as with an ordinary apotiki. Rama's wording, however, implies a different interpretation. It appears that he does not speak of one who said, "If I do not repay within three years, the whole field is yours." Rather, he said only, "Lend me money for three years, and if I do not repay you in money, you shall have no collection except from this field." The novelty is that although the statement uses an "if," namely, if I do not repay in money, it is not an asmachta with respect to this point and he cannot satisfy him from different land. The students of Rashba cited by Beit Yosef explain it this way. But if he also said, "If I do not repay within three years, the entire field shall be yours," perhaps Rama holds that just as the clause transferring the entire field does not take effect because it is an asmachta, the accompanying clause barring collection from elsewhere likewise fails, since both were included in one statement. According to this, however, the ruling seems obvious. Is the purpose merely to teach the ordinary law of an apotiki? That is already learned from the baraita, as explained in the Derishah to siman 117, where every apotiki is treated as though he said, "If I do not repay you in money, you shall have no collection except from this." See the Derishah.
