(Seif 14, in the gloss: Some say that “from now” alone, etc.) The Sema, in seif katan 40, concludes that when the Rama writes “from now,” he does not mean this literally, because he understands Tosafot and the Rosh in that manner. I am astonished that the Sema would extract money on the basis of an uncertain interpretation. I do not understand his reasoning. Rather, I will rely upon the sources available to me. The Rif writes explicitly concerning the incident in Nedarim cited there, where a man deposited his documents of right and said, “If I do not return within thirty days,” etc., that the Gaon wrote that this rule applies only where he deposited his documents of right. It appears from the Rosh that he understood the Rif in two possible ways. In the chapter Get Pashut he writes regarding these words of the Rif: “Tosafot rule that even without depositing his documents of right, the acquisition is effective, and this is the universal practice.” This implies that he understood the Rif to mean that an asmachta is never effective except where the person deposited his documents of right, but without that it is entirely ineffective. The Rosh states this explicitly in the third chapter of Nedarim in the name of some authorities, and rejects it there, concluding that asmachta may be effective in every context. It appears to me that the expression “from now” must be accompanied by a kinyan performed before an eminent beit din. This is stated explicitly in the Hagahot Maimoniyot, chapter 11: “R. Yitzchak rules that when we say that an undertaking made before an eminent beit din is effective, this applies where he also says ‘from now,’ even though he did not deposit his documents of right.” End quote. These are the Tosafot cited by the Rosh in the chapter Get Pashut. The Rosh did not need to emphasize that the case involved “from now,” because he cited the view only to establish that there is at least some form of asmachta that is effective even without depositing documents of right. He mentioned an eminent beit din only because that was the context in the Gemara. In responsum 72:7, the Rosh writes: “An asmachta is not effective unless there is a kinyan from now and before an eminent beit din. Rabbeinu Chananel so ruled in the name of Rav Hai Gaon, as did Rabbeinu Tam and R. Yitzchak. The Rif, however, wrote that an eminent beit din is unnecessary. His position lacks reason and proof. Since most of the Sages of Israel wrote that an asmachta is effective only before an eminent beit din, we do not extract money on the authority of the Rif.” End quote. It appears to me that he understood the Rif’s citation of the Gaon as follows: only where one deposited his documents of right are both an eminent beit din and “from now” required, but for other forms of asmachta “from now” alone is sufficient. The Rosh responds that this lacks reason and proof. Rather, in every case both an eminent beit din and “from now” are required. It therefore appears that the Rosh’s words in this siman, “if a kinyan was made before an eminent beit din or from now,” must read “and from now,” for it is obvious that “from now” alone is insufficient. Other editions indeed read this way. This accords with the Rosh’s responsum. The Rama followed that ruling. Although afterward he mentions only an eminent beit din, he relies upon his initial statement that both are necessary. The Tur likewise writes in siman 143, “before an eminent beit din and from now,” and the Hagahot Maimoniyot cited above also state this in the name of R. Yitzchak. When the Rama writes below in seif 15, in the name of some authorities, that the undertaking is effective even without depositing the item, he means to exclude the view that without depositing the item there is no possible way to overcome asmachta. That is the view cited by the Rosh in Nedarim and rejected there. The Rama therefore writes that common practice is that a kinyan before an eminent beit din is effective, meaning together with “from now,” as it appears to me. We have no power to extract money contrary to the later authorities, who require both elements, and contrary to the plain meaning of the Rama’s words.
