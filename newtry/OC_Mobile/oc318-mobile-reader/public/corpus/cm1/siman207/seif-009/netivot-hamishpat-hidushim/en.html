It is an asmachta and he did not acquire. Meaning that one who does not fully and finally commit to the sale but says, "If this occurs" or "if this does not occur," is presumed to rely on his expectation that the event will turn out as he anticipates and the sale will never need to take effect. This differs from the condition of the tribes of Gad and Reuven, where Moshe Rabbeinu's intent to transfer the land was clear. Sema:
"It shall be mine from now," he acquires. Even according to the dissenting view nearby in seif 14 that "from now" alone does not ordinarily eliminate asmachta, this case is different because the property was actually delivered to the lender as collateral, making it resemble a commercial transaction, and the lender also benefited him by extending the loan. We therefore say that his intention was that from now it should become a complete sale if the condition is met. Nevertheless, the lender may not consume the produce, because perhaps the borrower will redeem the property within three years and the arrangement will retroactively have been a loan, making the produce interest. Sema:
He insisted that it not be mortgaged. This insistence demonstrates that he fully resolved to transfer ownership; otherwise he would have no reason to object. Sema:
It is an apotiki. Even if he says, "If I do not repay you, from now the entire field shall be yours, and during the next three years you shall have no source of payment except this field," although the condition making the whole field his is void as an asmachta, the separate apotiki condition remains valid even though both were stated in one sentence.
