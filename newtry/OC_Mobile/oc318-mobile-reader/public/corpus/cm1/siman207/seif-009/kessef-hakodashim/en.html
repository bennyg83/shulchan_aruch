See Ketzot HaChoshen, seif katan 6. Ketzot discusses a board jointly owned by both parties. Perhaps the matter can be explained not as an acquisition in the ordinary sense. According to one view, even where no money is present, once they have finalized their agreement they obligate themselves to one another, like the formulation 'I am obligated' in siman 40, because that view holds there is no asmachta here. According to the other view, the obligation takes effect only where the money is prepared before them, for then they fully commit and convey to one another even without a formal kinyan, as in siman 176 regarding partners who fully commit and convey to one another. Therefore it may work here even with a board jointly owned by both parties, just as partners can acquire through property placed into their joint fund. This still requires study.
