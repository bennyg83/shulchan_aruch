A vow and an oath, etc. See the Sema, seif katan 53, who concludes that although the transaction itself does not obligate him to perform, according to our ruling the oath is also effective. The Bach disputes the Sema and explains that the Rivash answered in that manner only according to the questioner's assumption, while the Rivash himself did not accept it. This appears primary, and is explicit in Responsa Rivash, siman 341. The Bach writes that if one swore to uphold a sale subject to a penalty, payment of the penalty releases him from the oath. See Responsa Maharam Mintz, siman 16. Responsa Rabbi Aharon Sasson, end of siman 64, holds that he remains obligated, while Responsa Rabbi Avraham ibn Chaim, siman 66, page 160, column 4, writes that the matter is doubtful. Mahari Weil, siman 137, writes that were he not afraid to differ from his colleagues, he would say that the stringent rule concerning a handshake applies only when one gives the handshake directly to the person for whom he undertakes the act, as in the covenant of Tzidkiyahu with Nevuchadnetzar and the covenant between the pieces. In his case, since the handshake was not given to the prospective groom himself, one should not be so stringent. This is a strong argument because the stringency rests upon its resemblance to making a covenant. See Responsa Rama, simanim 49 and 108, page 220; Responsa Rabbi Shmuel Kohen, part 2, siman 63 and siman 144 at its end, part 3, simanim 34 and 105; and Responsa Rabbi Avraham ibn Chaim, siman 30.
