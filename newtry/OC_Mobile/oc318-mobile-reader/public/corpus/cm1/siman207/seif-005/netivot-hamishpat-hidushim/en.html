Or when he gives the money. This refers back to the phrase, "at a certain time." The meaning is: whether he stipulated that the property would be returned to him as a gift at a certain time, or sold back to him at that time, while until then the field remained under a complete sale, he may therefore eat the produce. If he is to return it for free, it resembles a Syrian mortgage where the document states that after these years the land returns without money. If he returns it when the original seller gives him money, it resembles a mortgage without deduction in a place where the borrower cannot compel early redemption, which the Rosh permits. Even those who prohibit that arrangement may agree here, because the money was not initially given as a loan but as the price of a time limited purchase, which is no worse than a gift on condition to return and therefore permits eating the produce. Sema. Taz writes that even here the case can be one with no fixed time. Although he may not then eat the produce, the sale is effective in the sense that the seller cannot redeem the land with other movable property. So long as he does not return the money, he cannot recover the land. See Beurim: the distinction is that if he says merely, "When I have money I will return it to you," and does not formulate a condition, he may not eat the produce because this was never a full sale. But if he sold through express conditional language, such as "on condition," it is a complete sale and the condition is a separate legal matter. He may therefore eat the produce. Machaneh Ephraim.
