When he gives. See the Sema's explanation of this seif. The Bach explains that there was first an unconditional kinyan, and afterward they performed a kinyan from the seller that he would return it to him, as stated in seif 7. See there. The Kesef Mishneh likewise writes this. Shach. The Taz writes: the Sema was troubled by this and forced an explanation. It appears to me that this case does not concern eating the produce, but rather the basic continued validity of the sale. The practical difference is where the seller does not have cash with which to repay the buyer as he stipulated, and therefore wishes to cancel the sale on the ground of asmachta, leaving the money as a loan in his hands to be repaid with movable property appraised by beit din. The ruling teaches that if he does not repay the buyer in accordance with his condition, the sale is absolute and he cannot remove the property from the buyer. Taz.
