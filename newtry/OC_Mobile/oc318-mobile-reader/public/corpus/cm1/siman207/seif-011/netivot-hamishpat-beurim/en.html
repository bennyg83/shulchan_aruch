One who gives earnest money. Tur cites Rosh concerning Reuven, who was afraid to slaughter a cow. Shimon said, "Slaughter the cow. If it is found treifah I will give you such-and-such, and if it is found kosher I will give you such-and-such," and he placed collateral in Reuven's hands. The animal was found treifah and Shimon retracted. Rosh ruled that he could retract because no valid kinyan had been made. "There is no maneh here, there is no collateral here." In addition, money does not acquire movable property. Beit Yosef asks why Shimon should not be liable under the law of a guarantor. Siman 305 discusses a case of "Throw one hundred into the sea and I will become obligated to you," where liability can arise because the person acted on the other's instruction. Here too Reuven slaughtered on Shimon's word. Acharonim write that Rosh must follow the opinion in siman 305 that one who says, "Throw one hundred into the sea," is exempt. Ketzot HaChoshen asks why Shimon should not at least be liable under dina d'garmi, like siman 39 where one promises to lend another money and instructs him to go to a scribe to write the document, and then must reimburse the scribal expenses. Likewise, one who instructs a worker to perform labor is liable for the value of that labor, as Rosh writes in responsum kelal 24. In my view there is no difficulty. Siman 232, seif 23, concerning one who sells an ox for slaughter and it turns out to be a goring ox, distinguishes numerous cases depending on whether it was sold for slaughter or plowing. Siman 232 also rules concerning one who buys an animal, slaughters it, and discovers a healed internal wound that the defect can void the sale. Tur states this broadly concerning one who bought an animal and found it treifah after slaughter, indicating that even where it was sold without specification, treifah is a defect. The seller cannot simply claim, "I sold it for plowing." The reason is that treifah status is a defect even for plowing. The animal is sick and not expected to live. Even an illness that weakens the strength of a slave is considered a defect, as stated there in seif 10. Certainly treifah is a defect. Accordingly, the same applies if one kills another person's animal and it is afterward discovered to have been treifah. The damager pays only the true value of a treifah animal. The owner cannot say, "I could have sold it to someone else for plowing at the price of a healthy animal." The later discovery shows that the animal was never truly worth that amount. A damager pays actual market value, not the amount the owner might have obtained by deceiving someone. If one damages impure silver, even though the owner might have deceived a non Jew and sold it as pure silver, the damager pays only its true value. So too here. The alleged agreement is not independently binding through kinyan. At most Shimon might be liable because Reuven expended property on Shimon's word or because Shimon caused a loss. Such liability reaches only the actual loss suffered. If one says, "Throw one hundred into the sea and I will give you two hundred," even according to the view imposing liability, he would owe only the one hundred actually lost, not the extra promised hundred merely because of the promise. Likewise a damager pays only actual value. Here, after slaughter, it turned out that the cow was treifah. The slaughter did not destroy a healthy cow. It merely revealed that the animal had already been worth only the value of a treifah animal. There is therefore no compensable loss corresponding to the promised premium. This differs from siman 39, where the plaintiff truly incurred scribal expenses because of the other's instruction, and from other cases of a worker who actually performed valuable labor at another's request. There the loss or benefit truly occurred. Here there was no newly caused loss, only revelation of a preexisting defect. Therefore Shimon need not pay the amount he promised above the true treifah value. Nor must he pay even the treifah value, because he neither damaged the animal nor acquired it through a valid kinyan. See Shach, seif katan 27. The cross reference printed there is mistaken and should be placed at the end of seif 12, concerning one who paid part of a debt.
