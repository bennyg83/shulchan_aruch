For this is an asmachta. This implies that were it not so, such as where he removed the asmachta, the third party should deliver the document. This applies where he stipulated that the money should become a gift, for otherwise the document is one whose lien has been extinguished, as explained above at the end of siman 55 in the names of the Ramban, Rashba, Ran, and other poskim. Although the Rambam holds above in seif 11 that asmachta does not apply to something within a person's control, this case is different because the document is in the hands of a third party and the matter was made dependent upon return of the document. Indeed, if the lender himself held the document and the borrower told him that the money should be a gift, the lender would acquire what was in his possession and there would be no asmachta. The Maggid Mishneh explains the Rambam in this manner, as cited by the Beit Yosef, page 291, beginning of column 3, and this is also stated in Responsa Ramban, siman 1, according to the Rambam. This appears to me the correct explanation of the Rambam. The Beit Yosef, however, explains the Rambam to mean that the third party should deliver the document in the presence of witnesses to delivery, following his own view that an antedated promissory note is valid for collection from unencumbered property. This does not appear correct to me, for how may he deliver it when the lender might then collect improperly from encumbered property from the original date? The law that an antedated promissory note may be collected from unencumbered property means that beit din may collect immediately, not that the document should be handed to the lender, as is evident from Bava Metzia 17a. Perhaps the intent is that the third party should accompany the lender to beit din so that he may collect immediately from unencumbered property, but this is strained. Nor does the Beit Yosef's statement that the Rambam follows the first explanation of the Rosh appear correct, since the Rosh ultimately gives a sound reason, namely that letters are acquired only through writing and delivery, as explained above in siman 66. Moreover, even according to the Beit Yosef's approach, this was unnecessary, because the Rosh said only that private delivery without more is ineffective; he did not discuss delivery in the presence of witnesses. Examine this carefully.
