This one acquires, etc. Nedarim 27b says that the case differs because he declared his rights void. In such a case no kinyan before an important beit din is required. Ran explains that saying his rights are void is tantamount to, etc. Rambam agrees. Bava Metzia 48b and 77b, which state that the earnest money is sufficient to acquire, indicate that the dispute concerns only whether it must be doubled.
Others disagree, etc. This follows Rashi's explanation. Ran explains the Nedarim case as an admission that his proofs are false. He further explains that the Gemara's conclusion, 'The halakha is that an asmachta, etc.,' refers back to the earlier ruling and means that a declaration voiding one's rights is effective only through a kinyan before an important beit din. They bring proof from the Mishna concerning one who lent upon a field, even though the field was already in the creditor's hand, and from the Mishna concerning a deposited promissory note, which must mean that the money is to become a gift while the original debt remains. Otherwise the document's lien would already have been waived and the note could not be returned. Nevertheless Rabbi Yehuda says not to deliver it because it is an asmachta. Kesef Mishneh and Beit Yosef answer that a document whose lien was waived may still be used through witnesses of delivery. Their answer requires study, because delivering such a note creates a risk of improper collection. Further proof appears in the Tosefta: where litigants stipulate that failure to appear by a fixed date creates a monetary obligation, Rabbi Yose says the condition stands and Rabbi Yehuda says the other cannot acquire what never entered his possession. The same dispute appears where a house or field is pledged, and even in the case of earnest money, showing that Rabbi Yehuda disputes even a waiver. The Tosefta also distinguishes a pledge of movable property, where Rabbi Yehuda does not disagree, from land, which remains in its owner's possession and is therefore an asmachta. This offers some support for Rambam's distinction. See cited sources 1, 48, 161, 201, 205.
