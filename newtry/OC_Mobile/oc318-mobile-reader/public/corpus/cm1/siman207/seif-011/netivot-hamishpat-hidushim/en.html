One who gives earnest money. Siman 190 explains the law regarding the acquisition itself. Here the discussion concerns the penalty. Sema. The case is where the earnest money was cash, because language of waiver can apply to money. With an object, language of gift is required. Taz.

He placed it in the hand of a shalish. According to Maggid Mishneh, even Rambam and the Mechaber agree in such a case. Shach. The Tur cites here a responsum of the Rosh concerning one who had a cow to slaughter and feared it might prove treifah. His fellow said, "Slaughter the cow. I hereby buy the meat from you now, if it is kosher for such and such a price, and if it is treifah for such and such a price," and gave him collateral. The cow was slaughtered and found treifah. The Rosh ruled that the purchaser may retract because there was no underlying monetary obligation, so the collateral had nothing upon which to rest. See Beurim: even though the cow was slaughtered at his instruction, he is not liable under direct causation. Even if one kills another's animal and it turns out to have been treifah, he pays only its actual treifah value. A tortfeasor pays according to the object's real value, not according to a hypothetical price at which the owner might have deceived someone else.
