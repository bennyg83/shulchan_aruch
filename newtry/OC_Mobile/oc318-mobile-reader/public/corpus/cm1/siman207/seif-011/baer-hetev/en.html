This is difficult, for we rule in siman 241 that one who has an article in another's possession and wishes to transfer it to him should not say, "It is waived to you," but rather, "Acquire it." How then does the seller acquire the earnest money here merely through waiver? One cannot answer that the payment was given as part payment, because if it was given as payment we rule that the buyer acquires according to all opinions. It must therefore be that the earnest money here is money, which can be acquired by mere waiver. Alternatively, "waived" is not literal, and it means that the seller transfers it by whatever legally effective method applies. Taz.
