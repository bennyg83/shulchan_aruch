Two complete debt instruments. Why do they not write everything in one document, namely that Reuven obligates himself to Shimon while Shimon waives that obligation from now subject to a condition? Because if the release is written beside the obligation, it appears that the very obligation is conditional and resembles asmachta. They therefore write two separate complete instruments. They also do not hand the release document directly to the opposing party, because the holder could then display it and claim that the obligation was created only conditionally and therefore resembles asmachta. See Beurim: the Sema means only that he could raise such an argument. If witnesses establish that the original obligation was unconditional, one document can nevertheless be effective. See Beurim further: where the release instrument is given to a shalish and not directly to the opposing party to acquire the document for him, the giver must instruct the shalish, "Acquire it for so and so."
