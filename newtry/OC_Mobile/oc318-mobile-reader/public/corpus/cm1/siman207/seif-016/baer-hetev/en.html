From now. The Beit Yosef asks: since the phrase "from now" appears, according to the Rambam that alone should suffice. It appears to me that they were concerned because sometimes a person obligates himself to pay a large amount if he fails to carry out a match, yet he presently owns no money or property on which the obligation can take effect. He would thus be attempting to obligate himself with respect to property not yet in existence. Although the Beit Yosef cites the Rosh in this siman as holding that a person can obligate himself in a large ketubah even if he presently owns nothing, it is evident there that the Rif does not accept that principle. Alternatively, because this case involves asmachta, all agree that he cannot become obligated merely through his declaration, since "from now" is required. If he presently owns no property, "from now" cannot operate, because upon what can the lien now take effect? They therefore structured the matter in reverse: each party acknowledges owing the other a large sum, and each can waive his debt from now if the other fulfills the match. Ibid.

Kinyan. This follows what is written in the Beit Yosef and Darkei Moshe. But in the chapter Eizehu Neshech, when the Mordechai records Rabbeinu Tam's words, he writes that the same applies to marriage agreements: even without acquisition in an important beit din and without saying "from now," a kinyan is made. In my humble opinion, regarding marriage agreements all agree that no kinyan is required. This is explicit from Tosafot and the authorities, who explain that the purpose is to obligate the party who retracts to pay a penalty corresponding to the embarrassment caused, comparable to one who says, "If I leave the field fallow, I will pay." Examine this carefully. This is also explicit in Hagahot Mordechai to Bava Metzia in the name of Semag regarding a teacher: even without a kinyan he is liable for the penalty, and even one who requires a kinyan for gambling agrees here.
