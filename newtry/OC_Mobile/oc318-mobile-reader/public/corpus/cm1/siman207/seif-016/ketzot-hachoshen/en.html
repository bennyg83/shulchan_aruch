Nevertheless, a kinyan is required. The Shach writes that in engagement agreements everyone agrees that no formal kinyan is needed, because the payment for withdrawing is compensation for the embarrassment caused. He compares it to the rule, "If I leave the field fallow and do not work it, I will pay from the best," and to the rule concerning a teacher who causes loss. I do not agree. There is no full Torah liability for embarrassment in an engagement withdrawal. Embarrassment is ordinarily compensable only where an act is done to the person's body. Verbal embarrassment is exempt by strict law. Rosh's statement that verbal humiliation can be worse than physical humiliation concerns communal enactments and penalties against abusive speech, not a general tort obligation. When Tosafot and the decisors say that embarrassment removes the defect of asmachta, they mean that once the party has formally obligated himself, the real loss or embarrassment gives him sufficient seriousness of intent to make the obligation binding. The embarrassment itself is not the independent source of liability. The same is true of the condition, "If I do not cultivate, I will pay." The loss caused to the other side removes asmachta, but an obligation must still be created through a valid instrument, such as a written undertaking or a beit din condition. Thus an engagement penalty likewise needs either a kinyan or a written obligation. The cited teacher case is different because the worker already began the employment, and commencement of the labor itself supplies the kinyan of employment. This is why the stipulated terms can then bind without a separate sudar kinyan.
