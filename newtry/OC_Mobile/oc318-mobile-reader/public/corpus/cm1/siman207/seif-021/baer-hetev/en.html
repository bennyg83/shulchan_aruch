According to the Rambam. The Tur and Shulchan Aruch likewise write this in his name in siman 60, seif 2. His reason is that people do not ordinarily conclusively resolve to obligate themselves in a matter whose amount they do not know. For the same reason they write at the end of siman 131 that a guarantor does not become obligated for an amount that is not fixed. Similarly, in siman 232, seif 7, regarding one who sells an item and says, "It has a defect, on condition that you do not return it," the buyer may nevertheless return it until the seller specifies the defect. The Tur explains there that a person cannot transfer to another an obligation whose measure is not fixed, and adds that the Rosh disagrees and holds that such an obligation can be transferred. This requires study, because here the Mechaber writes that all later authorities disagreed with the Rambam; similarly in simanim 70 and 134 he concludes that this is the accepted ruling. Yet in siman 232 he rules without qualification like the Rambam and neither the Mechaber nor Rama mentions the dissenting view. Some say that the Rambam appears there to have another reason, deriving the law from the rules of overreaching, and the Mechaber likewise writes that reason. Nevertheless this is difficult, because the Beit Yosef writes there that it cannot be derived from overreaching and that the principal reason is that the amount is not fixed. This requires study. See what I wrote on this in siman 60, seif katan 7, and siman 131, seif katan 27.
