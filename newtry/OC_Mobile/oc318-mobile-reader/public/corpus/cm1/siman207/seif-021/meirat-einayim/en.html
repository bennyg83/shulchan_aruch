He obligated himself in an amount that is not fixed, etc. Even where he specified "for five years," the obligation is still considered unquantified because the total amount of support is unknown and prices may rise. The Tur and Mechaber state this in siman 60. In the Perishah there I brought proof that even a five year obligation remains unquantified. The Rambam himself asks from the Mishnah concerning one who obligates himself to support his wife's daughter, and that Mishnah explicitly speaks of five years.
According to the Rambam, he did not obligate himself. The Tur and Mechaber rule similarly in the Rambam's name in siman 60:9. His reason is that people do not ordinarily fully resolve to obligate themselves in an amount whose extent they do not know. For this reason the Tur and Mechaber also write at the end of siman 131 that a guarantor is not obligated for an unquantified amount. Likewise in siman 232:7 they cite the Rambam that if a seller says, "This article has a defect, on condition that you do not return it because of that defect," the buyer may still return it until the seller specifies the defect and the buyer explicitly waives it. The Tur explains that the Rambam's reason is that one cannot transfer or waive an unquantified right, while the Rosh disagrees and holds that a person can obligate himself in an unquantified amount. It requires study that here the Mechaber writes that all later authorities disagreed with the Rambam, and says similarly in siman 60 and at the end of siman 131, ruling accordingly. Yet in siman 232 he states the Rambam's defect rule without mentioning any dissent, and Rama also says nothing there. Perhaps this is because in that context the Rambam himself appears to have another reason based upon the law of overreaching, and the Mechaber copied that reasoning. Still, this is strained, because in the Derishah there, and in the Beit Yosef itself, it is explained that the principal reason is the Rambam's view that one cannot obligate himself in an unquantified amount. This requires study.
