Absolute. Meaning, the agent intended to say: "I will not involve myself in changing my agency by purchasing on condition. I am purchasing it from you without any condition. But since she is your relative, it is reasonable to assume that she will return it of her own free will." If we were to say that the seller relied on the agent's words, the acquisition would be absolute, because even if she later returned it, it would be like a buyer who says, "I will return it to you voluntarily." Therefore the Rambam and Mechaber write that the seller did not rely on him. When the Gemara says that his mind relied on the statement, it means that the seller regarded the agent's words as a mere asmachta, did not rely on them as an enforceable promise, and did not conclusively transfer ownership except on condition. Regarding produce consumed during the interim, see the end of siman 182 and Yoreh Deah 174:3. Sema. The Shach writes that Responsa Mahara Sasson, siman 49, discusses this law at length, explaining the views of the authorities, the Gemara's approach, and the Rosh. The Beit Yosef cites Responsa Rashba concerning Reuven, who owed Shimon one hundred and sold him land for that amount, writing him a deed of sale but without Shimon taking possession. Shimon then wrote to Reuven that if Reuven repaid the hundred he owed, the sale would be void. Reuven later claimed that he had repaid Shimon. The Rashba ruled that Reuven is believed, and Shimon cannot say, "Why is your deed of sale still in my possession?" Since Shimon received the land only on account of the debt, the transaction remains in the nature of an oral loan.
