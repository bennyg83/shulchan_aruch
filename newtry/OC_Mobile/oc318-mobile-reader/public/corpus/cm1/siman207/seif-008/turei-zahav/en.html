(Seif 13, in the gloss: Therefore, when the seller transfers ownership, etc.) This means that he made the acquisition dependent upon the other party’s performance, rather than transferring ownership now on condition that the other party later perform a particular act. It is therefore an asmachta, because anything in the purchaser’s possession is regarded as being in the possession of others, as stated nearby. It must be understood that the object being acquired is in the recipient’s possession as security that he will fulfill the condition. Otherwise no kinyan could operate at all, even absent asmachta, because the symbolic object used for the kinyan returns to its owner. The Tur follows his own view that even an undertaking concerning something presently in one’s possession can constitute an asmachta.
