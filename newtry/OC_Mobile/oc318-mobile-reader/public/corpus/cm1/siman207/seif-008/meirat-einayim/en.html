For the relative did not rely upon it, etc. The Gemara and the poskim use the opposite wording: "In such a case he relied upon it and did not fully resolve to transfer." That reading is more logical. If, as the Rambam's text says, he did not rely upon the agent's assurance, then the seller should have transferred fully. Yet the Rambam concludes that he did not fully resolve to transfer. One can explain the Rambam as follows. The agent said, "You and so and so are relatives." His intention was: I will not alter my agency and buy upon an express condition. I buy from you without a condition, but since she is your relative, it is likely that she will voluntarily return it to you. If we were to say that the seller actually relied upon the agent's words, then the acquisition should be complete, because even if the relative later returns it voluntarily, that would merely be a purchaser voluntarily giving it back. Therefore, when the Gemara says that he "relied upon it," the Rambam understands it to mean that the seller treated the agent's words only as a contingent assurance and never fully relied upon the unconditional sale. He therefore did not fully transfer except subject to the expected return.
It follows that he did not fully resolve and transfer it. Nevertheless, according to the Rosh and Tur, the produce already consumed is only rabbinic interest, as the Tur states explicitly in Yoreh Deah at the end of siman 174 and in Choshen Mishpat at the end of siman 182. See Rama below in seif 10. In Yoreh Deah siman 174:3 the Mechaber rules that the produce already consumed is recoverable. That follows specifically the Rif and Rambam, as I explained at length in the Derishah there and at the end of siman 182.
