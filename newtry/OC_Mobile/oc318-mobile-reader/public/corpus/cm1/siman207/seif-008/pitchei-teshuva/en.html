A compelling presumption. Chavot Yair, siman 69, discusses Levi, who wished to buy a house from Shimon. Shimon demanded five hundred Reichstaler; Levi offered three hundred. Shimon claimed that Naftali had already offered three hundred fifty and swore a stringent oath that even if someone came that day offering four hundred he would not sell it. Relying on this, Levi paid four hundred ten and bought the house. When people complained that he had paid far too much, Levi asked Naftali whether he had truly offered three hundred fifty, and Naftali denied it. Shimon admitted that he had lied about Naftali in order to improve his price, claiming this was ordinary commercial practice. Concerning his oath, he said he had spoken the words while nullifying them in his heart, and alternatively that he had truly meant that if another person, rather than Levi, had offered four hundred that day, he would not have sold to that person. Chavot Yair rules that if Levi already paid, Shimon is exempt in human law regarding the sale itself. Although Shimon deceived him by falsely claiming Naftali's offer, Levi failed to ask Naftali before buying and relied on Shimon's credibility because he assumed a person would not lie about something easily verifiable. Levi therefore has grounds for complaint both against Shimon and against himself, but not to rescind after payment. As to the oath, Shimon's claim that he mentally nullified his words is insufficient even in human law. His alternative explanation may suffice in human law because a person is believed to explain his own words, but he remains liable in the heavenly court even if that was truly his intention. Chavot Yair distinguishes, however, where Levi has not yet paid. He can then say that he relied on Shimon's false statement and oath, and such circumstances may constitute the compelling presumption that the Rosh recognizes even in a sale and that Rama cites in siman 207:4. Taz, siman 332:4, on the other hand, remarks that merchants daily say that another buyer offered a certain sum and that they refused it, and it later proves false, yet we do not normally void the sale on that account. The matter therefore requires study.
