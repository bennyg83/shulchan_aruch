(Seif 1, in the gloss: Before the sale was completely concluded.) In the early editions of the Tur it is clear that “completely” means that the purchaser had already paid the money but the deed had not yet been written. This implies that before payment of the money it is called the beginning of the sale. Yet the continuation, “But if at the beginning of the sale,” implies the literal beginning, for the Tur concludes that the true beginning of the transaction is required. This means that so long as the parties had not yet agreed upon the price, if the seller then stated a term for the purchaser’s benefit, the purchaser may certainly say, “I relied upon this price only after hearing that you were stipulating for my benefit.” But if they had already agreed upon the price and the seller afterward stated a term for the purchaser’s benefit, then even though the money had not yet been paid, this is called a point before the sale was completely concluded, and the seller’s stipulation in favor of the purchaser is ineffective. The Tur’s example, where the money had been paid but the deed had not yet been written, is stated only to teach that we should not think this is already after the sale was completely concluded, in which case even a stipulation by the purchaser for his own benefit would require a new kinyan. The Tur therefore teaches that it is still considered part of the sale.
