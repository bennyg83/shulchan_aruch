One who sells property subject to a condition, the laws of asmachta, and one who obligates himself in something he does not otherwise owe. It contains twenty-one seifim.
If one transfers land or movable property and stipulates a condition that can be fulfilled, whether the condition is made by the transferor or the recipient, the property is acquired if the condition is fulfilled and is not acquired if it is not fulfilled. Rama: Some say that when a seller adds a condition solely for the purchaser's benefit, it is not a binding condition but merely reassuring words. This is only when the sale was initially unconditional and, before its final completion, the seller added the condition. If from the outset he said, 'I sell to you on such-and-such condition,' then even though the condition benefits the purchaser, the purchaser acquires only on that understanding and it is a full condition. Likewise, if they first stipulated a condition and later wrote an unconditional deed, the deed was certainly written subject to the original condition. The laws governing valid conditions have already been explained in Even HaEzer. Rama: See Even HaEzer, simanim 38 and 144, and below, siman 241.
