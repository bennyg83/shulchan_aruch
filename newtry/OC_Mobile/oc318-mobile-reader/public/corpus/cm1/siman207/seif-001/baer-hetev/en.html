To fulfill them. This excludes conditions that are impossible to fulfill. In such a case the transaction is valid and the condition is void, as learned from the condition of the tribes of Gad and Reuven, where fulfillment was possible. From there we also derive the other laws of conditions, as the Mechaber alludes when he says that the laws of conditions have already been explained. He mentions only this detail, that fulfillment must be possible, because that is the matter under discussion here, namely that when the conditions are fulfilled, the acquisition takes effect. Thus far the Sema. See Responsa R. Moshe Alshich, siman 37. On laws of asmachta, see Responsa Maharashdam, simanim 58, 108, and 128.
The buyer. The Tur explains, for example, where the seller said: If the property is seized from you, I will collect another superior field for you, from which you will recover your principal, the appreciation of the field, and its produce. Sema.
Words. The Rosh explains that since the buyer did not repeat the condition that was to his benefit, this shows that he too did not regard the seller's promise as a binding term. Rather, the seller said it only to calm the buyer's distress. The buyer thought: If I repeat the condition, the seller may immediately retract his promise; better that I remain silent, perhaps he will be embarrassed to retract later. But in his own mind the buyer agreed to buy even without responsibility. See there. This is the Rosh's intent, restated more explicitly. Ibid.
Completely. The Tur's words clarify the phrase 'completely,' meaning that the money had already been paid but the deed had not yet been written. This implies that before payment is made it is still called the beginning of the sale. Yet the Tur later says, 'But if at the beginning of the sale,' which implies the initial stage, meaning while the parties have not yet agreed on the price. If at that stage the seller states a term for the buyer's benefit, the buyer can certainly say: I agreed to this price only after hearing that you were stipulating for my benefit. But once the price has been agreed and afterward the seller states something for the buyer's benefit, even before payment, this is called before the sale is completely finalized, and the seller's favorable condition no longer has effect. The Tur's example that the money was paid comes to teach that this is not yet considered after the sale was entirely completed, in which case even a condition made by the buyer for his own benefit would require a new kinyan. Rather, it is still within the transaction. Taz.
