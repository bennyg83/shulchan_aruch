A deed, etc. In the third chapter of Bava Batra the Gemara required acquisition of movable property together with land even though the deed stated that the obligation was not an asmachta. That language helps there only because the arrangement is not a full asmachta: the original loan principally relies upon the land. Movable property alone would be an asmachta because the debtor could sell it unless it was subordinated together with land. Therefore the document must state that it is not an asmachta, so that the debtor cannot say the phrase was merely standard boilerplate. See Ramban and Responsa Rosh. This case differs. Baal HaTerumot cites a Gaon who compares it to one who merely declares a bill of divorce invalid, an ineffective declaration. It is not comparable to a guarantor after the money was given, who becomes obligated through a kinyan despite the lender's lack of reliance upon him. See Rashi, Bava Metzia 67b. See cited source 514.
Some disagree, etc. Their proof is the passage in the third chapter of Bava Batra.
(Supplement) Some disagree. The first opinion answers that the case there is not a complete asmachta, since a guarantor after disbursement may become obligated through a kinyan without an important beit din. See Rashi, Bava Metzia 67b. There is support for the first opinion from the requirement that movable property be subordinated together with land even where the document says it is not an asmachta. (End of supplement.)
(Supplement) Although some disagree, etc. See Rashbam there, who says it resembles an asmachta. This is not decisive, because it is precisely because it is not a full asmachta that the language is effective. (End of supplement.)
