(Collected note) When does this apply, etc., but, etc. Meaning where the matter consists of words without a kinyan, as the Rosh writes in chapter Eizehu Neshech. Although there are matters acquired by verbal agreement, etc., with a kinyan they are acquired. The printed phrase there suggesting that an asmachta is acquired even with a kinyan is a textual error; the text is deficient and should follow Tosafos, Eruvin 82a, at the end of s.v. “he said.” Their discussion of those who gamble on trust distinguishes the views as to whether such an arrangement constitutes asmachta. The same omission appears in Tosafos, Sanhedrin 25a. There is plainly no role there for an important beis din, and the Derishah was forced on this point. (End of note.)
Some say even if he gave, etc. As in Kiddushin chapter 1. Here too he otherwise has no obligation without the collateral, since the parties are gambling on trust.
Unlike those who disagree, etc. They follow Rami bar Chama that it is an asmachta. Rambam rules likewise. See what was written at the end of siman 203.
Some say it is not permitted, etc. Even though it is not an asmachta, an actual kinyan through one of the recognized modes of acquisition is still required; it is no stronger than any other transaction. Tosafos, Eruvin 82a, asks how gambling on trust could effect acquisition by words alone merely because one defeats the other, according to the view that it is not an asmachta. Mere words do not even incur Mi Shepara. This is also the Rambam and Shulchan Aruch’s meaning in seif 2: the case is effective only “when he acquired through one of the modes of acquisition.” Even absent asmachta, as with “on condition,” a valid mode of acquisition is still required. If the location and gaming board are legally owned by the eventual winner, however, his domain acquires for him, as in Bava Batra chapter 5. See Tosafos, Sanhedrin there. The Tur follows the first opinion and does not require that the place be transferred, analogous to partners. See siman 176 seif 3 in the gloss, and Bava Batra chapter 7 concerning the benefit each receives by agreeing with and relying on the other.
