And there are those who disagree. See Responsa Noda BiYehuda, siman 36. He writes that although there appears to be room to favor the defendant because Shulchan Aruch, siman 207:11, records two opinions whether a waiver is effective even when connected with an asmachta, and the Mechaber follows the Rambam while the Rama cites those who disagree, the defendant cannot rely on the Rambam here. The reason is that where the claimant still holds a promissory note, this is not properly termed a waiver. Even according to the view cited by the Rama in siman 241:2 that a waiver can be effective although the creditor holds a document, that position, derived from the Mordechai in Sanhedrin, rests on the inference that the parties had settled the account and nothing remained owing. Here, however, it is explicit that the waiver was imposed as a penalty, so that rationale does not apply, and even the Mordechai would agree. Furthermore, even granting every leniency to the defendant, namely the Rambam's view that asmachta does not invalidate a waiver and that this case can still be called a waiver although the creditor holds a document, a kinyan was nevertheless made. Ordinarily a kinyan subject to asmachta is ineffective except before an eminent beit din, but here an ordinary kinyan suffices because the act is a waiver. The rule that waiver requires no kinyan applies only where the waiver is made explicitly, as explained in siman 12:7 and the Sema there. See the Noda BiYehuda for his full proof.
