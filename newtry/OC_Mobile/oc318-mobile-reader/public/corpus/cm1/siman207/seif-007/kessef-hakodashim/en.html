Seif 13. If such and such occurs. Where a contract provides that if the employer strikes the worker he must immediately pay him proportionately for the period worked until then, there may be no practical cure needed for asmachta. Even without such a condition, a worker may withdraw, and if he was struck not for proper instruction he may likewise withdraw; payment is then due through that time because the employment has ended. If the wages vary by period, one must determine what amount is due through the termination, even if the condition itself is void. If the document sets a payment date before the end of the employment term, and the worker withdraws before that payment date, perhaps payment need not be made immediately, for the stipulated payment date remains effective. If the payment date and the condition were established in one act, the withdrawal may be viewed as undoing the payment date as well. Still, a withdrawal that the law certainly permits may be treated as though the invalid condition were absent, leaving the payment date intact. If the condition provides that when the worker acts insolently the employer may postpone payment for a specified period, asmachta may not apply because the employer is in possession and may claim the opinion that there is no asmachta where the matter is in his control, as discussed in seif 11. Even if the employer struck him immediately, once the payment date had been postponed, the condition that the blow would trigger payment does not take effect if it is subject to asmachta.
