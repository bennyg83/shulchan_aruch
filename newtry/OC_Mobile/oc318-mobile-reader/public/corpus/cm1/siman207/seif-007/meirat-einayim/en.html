The condition is valid and the purchaser eats the produce. The Rambam writes this, and the Tur cites it but objects: If this is truly a condition that the purchaser must fulfill, how may he eat the produce? Rather, there should be no effective condition at all. The Tur means that since this is a condition benefiting the seller, the seller should have stipulated it. When the purchaser alone states it, it should be treated as mere appeasement, as in seif 1 according to the Rosh. If the Rambam disagrees and holds that the purchaser's condition is effective, then the arrangement should resemble a loan and the purchaser should be forbidden to eat the produce. The Beit Yosef resolves the Rambam by saying that the purchaser imposed the condition after the sale had already been completed and then made a kinyan upon that additional undertaking. Thus the sale remains valid and the separate condition, being supported by a kinyan, must also be fulfilled, because the original sale was already completed permissibly without any condition. I cited this in the Derishah. The Kesef Mishneh gives another explanation. Both the first case, where the seller stipulates, and the second, where the purchaser stipulates, concern a condition stated before completion of the sale and without a separate kinyan. In the first case, where the seller makes the condition, the purchaser may not eat the produce, because the seller never intended to sell except upon that condition and can recover the land whenever he brings the money. But where the seller said nothing and the purchaser volunteered the promise, even if the seller repeats and accepts it so that the purchaser must fulfill it, since the seller had already despaired of imposing such a condition and the purchaser merely promised it to ease the seller's distress, the transaction is not treated as a loan with interest. Proof is that the seller has the lower hand: if the land rises in value he can bring the money and recover it, while if it falls he will leave it with the purchaser. Therefore the purchaser may eat the produce while it remains in his possession.
