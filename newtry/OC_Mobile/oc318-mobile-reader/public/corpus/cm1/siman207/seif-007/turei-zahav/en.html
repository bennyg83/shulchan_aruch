(Seif 11: “My earnest money is forgiven to you.”) This is difficult, for we rule in siman 241 that one who has an object in another person’s possession and wishes to transfer it to him should not say, “It is forgiven to you,” but rather, “Acquire it.” By what means, then, does the seller acquire the earnest money here? One cannot answer that the money was initially given as part payment. This would indeed explain the Beit Yosef’s citation in seif 15 from the Ran and the Tosefta, where R. Yose says, “How can the other person acquire something that is not his?” There the item was not given as payment but as an independent kinyan. Yet this answer is untenable, because if the earnest money here was given as payment, why did the purchaser not acquire the entire property? We rule that where money is given as payment, the purchaser acquires the whole. It therefore appears to me that here the earnest money consists of coins, and for that reason the seller acquires it through the word “forgiven” alone. Alternatively, the phrase “is forgiven” is not literal. The Tur means that he said, “My earnest money is forgiven to you,” in a legally effective manner.
