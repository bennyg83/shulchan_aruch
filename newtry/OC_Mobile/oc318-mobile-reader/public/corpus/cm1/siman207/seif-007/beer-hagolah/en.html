Nun) The Kesef Mishneh writes that according to Rambam one must explain that whether the buyer or seller made the stipulation at the time of the sale, the property must be returned. When the seller makes the stipulation, it resembles one who seeks to borrow at interest rather than truly sell. But when the buyer stipulates, his intent may have been merely to spare the seller distress, not to lend at interest. A proof is that the buyer is at a disadvantage: if the property rises in value, the seller may bring the money and take it back, while if it falls in value he may leave it with the buyer. This is a sound explanation. Alternatively, after the sale was completed the buyer said, "When you have money," etc., and they made a kinyan from him. Then the stipulation is valid because the sale was already complete and it is as though the buyer now resold the property to the seller. The Kesef Mishneh found this explanation in the Hagahot Ashri in the name of Rabbenu Tam, and with it he resolves the Tur's difficulty. See there at length.
