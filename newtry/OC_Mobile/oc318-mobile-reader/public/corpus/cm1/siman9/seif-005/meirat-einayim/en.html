One who takes payment to judge, etc. One who takes payment to testify likewise has invalid testimony. This is explicit in the Mishnah and Rama writes it below in siman 34, seif 18.
Unless it is known that he did not take, etc. Then that particular judgment remains valid even though he took payment for other cases before or afterward. This differs from one who commits a disqualifying transgression, whose judgments and testimony are invalid until he repents. Here he violated no negative commandment; invalidation of the judgment is a penalty imposed for taking payment. So explains the Rosh. See Darkei Moshe and its glosses.
And he says to the litigants, 'Give, etc.' The text should read with the conjunction 'and he says.' This rule applies where he stipulated with them initially. He may then take compensation equal to the full value of the work from which he was diverted, based on the principle that a person should not be impoverished through a mitzvah. If he did not stipulate, judged, and afterward seeks compensation for lost work, they need give only the amount a person would ordinarily accept to remain idle from that work, like one returning a lost object, as explained below in simanim 264 and 265. Parallel rules appear regarding business arrangements in Yoreh Deah siman 177 and inspection of firstborn-animal defects in siman 312.
The correct reading is: but if it is not recognizable. For example, he has no known occupation but merely says, etc. This is the Tur's text.
For example, he has no known occupation, etc. See the Derishah. Responsa Rosh implies, though not conclusively, that in such a case his judgments may also be void. This applies specifically where he says perhaps he would have earned money through buying merchandise, something not dependent upon him and not regularly available. But if he is a craftsman, even if he was not actually engaged in the craft at the moment he came to judge, the matter is within his control. Although taking some payment in advance is ugly, after the fact his judgment stands. See the Derishah for further discussion.
