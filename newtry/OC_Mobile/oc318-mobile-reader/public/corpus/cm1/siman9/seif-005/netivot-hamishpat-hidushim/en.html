If he was not then working but it is known that he has work available and he says he wishes to perform it, taking compensation is improper, but his judgments are not invalid. If he has no work at all and merely says that perhaps he might find work, his judgments are invalid. Sema. Urim VeTumim justifies the practice of fixed judges who therefore do not engage in commerce. It is known that if they had not been appointed judges they would surely have engaged in some occupation. That is considered recognizable work, since everyone knows that their judicial appointment is what causes them to refrain from ordinary employment.
