When the giver demands it from him. So long as the giver has not demanded return of the bribe, the judge is not yet obligated to return it. Sema distinguishes this from interest, where the verse commands restoration so that the borrower may live. Bach and Taz maintain that even interest requires a demand, because until the lender demands return there is implied waiver. Knesset HaGedolah writes that if the bribe was taken to rule correctly for the entitled party, no return is required. Sema and Bach disagree, and Rambam supports them. Rambam does not list the prohibition against taking a bribe among lashes-bearing prohibitions because bribe money is subject to restitution. If Knesset HaGedolah were correct, Rambam should have listed the prohibition at least where the judge took a bribe to rule correctly and no restitution obligation existed. It follows that even such a bribe must be returned.
