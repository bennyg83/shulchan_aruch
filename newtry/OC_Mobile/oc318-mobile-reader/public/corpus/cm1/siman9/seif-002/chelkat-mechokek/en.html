Some say this applies specifically if they died natural deaths. Yevamot 64b discusses a woman whose first two husbands died, whom Rabbi says may not marry a third and Rabban Shimon ben Gamliel says may not marry a fourth. The Amoraim dispute the reason: one says the woman's source causes the death of her husbands, and one says her mazal causes it. A practical difference is where she was merely betrothed and he died, or where he fell from a palm tree and died. According to the view that her physical source causes death, neither case establishes the pattern: in the first he never had relations with her, and in the second he did not die from illness. Accordingly, this 'some say,' which follows the distinction of falling from a palm tree, must also disagree with what the Rama wrote earlier regarding a man who merely became betrothed and died, for the Gemara lists both cases together as practical differences. The Rama was too brief here where he should have elaborated.
