Seif 5, “and he said to the litigants: Pay” The Sema writes that if the judge made no stipulation initially, the parties must later pay compensation for lost time as to an idle laborer. This does not appear correct. One who pays another’s debt without authorization cannot recover because the debtor may say he could have appeased the creditor without payment; all the more so here, the litigant may say he would have gone to another judge who was free from work. Tosafot likewise state that one who acts for another without authorization, even at a loss to himself, is not reimbursed where he merely drives away a danger and performs a mitzva. The analogy to returning lost property is inexact, for there the loss is certain and the object would otherwise be permanently lost. Had compensation been compulsory, Rambam and Tur should have said so here as they did for a lost object. Thus no payment may be compelled absent an advance stipulation. Voluntary payment is permitted only up to actual lost-time compensation, for more would be payment for judgment. The Tur’s citation of Rambam regarding judges who remain in their towns also means that, unlike Samuel’s sons, they should circulate among Israel’s cities and thereby reduce the parties’ expenses for scribes and attendants.
