A bribe consisting of words. See Baer Heitev and the Bach concerning the dispute among the early authorities whether a verbal favor constitutes a legal disqualification or merely a matter of pious conduct. It is evident from the Bach that the rule concerning any judge who regularly borrows items from others applies, according to all opinions, as a legal disqualification. Birkei Yosef discusses this at length and concludes that although Mahari ben Lev, part III, siman 96, appears somewhat uncertain whether the case of one who habitually borrows is more severe than the other cases, Maharikash in a manuscript responsum, siman 131, writes that from the language of the Gemara and Tur it is clear that in this case the judge is disqualified even after the fact. So too is implied by Chukkot HaDayanim, and the Bach and Responsa Devar Shmuel, siman 54, write likewise. Birkei Yosef further cites Chukkot HaDayanim, siman 8, that some say all cases that are not legally disqualifying but are only matters of piety apply where the judge sits alone. If another judge sits with him, there is not even a requirement of pious withdrawal. See Tiferet Yaakov on this matter.
