Even on condition to pay, etc. The Gemara derives this from the verse, "The wicked borrows and does not repay; robbery he shall repay." Meaning, even though the robber pays for what he takes, he is still called wicked. See the Perishah.
That is, if there is no, etc. The Rosh explains that the concern is that afterward he may not have the means with which to pay.
If the replacement items are present, etc. Meaning, where the replacement consists of a different type of property and the robber needs the very type he wishes to take. This resembles the actual case in which David's men needed barley for their animals and wanted to substitute lentils, which were not suitable for their animals.
And he should transfer ownership of them to the owners through another person. Meaning, if the owners are absent, we presume that a beneficial transfer is acceptable to them, and one may acquire for a person in his absence. But if the replacement is not presently available, even though the taker accepts upon himself to give something better later, the owners presumably do not want to be forced into litigation and perhaps ultimately find that he lacks the means to pay. If the owners are present and say, "We prefer our own property and not another item, even one better than it," it appears obvious that according to all opinions taking it is prohibited.
