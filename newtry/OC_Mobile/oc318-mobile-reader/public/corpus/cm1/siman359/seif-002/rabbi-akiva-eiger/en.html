[Shach, continuation of his words] That it is only rabbinically prohibited. Note: Regarding Rabbi Akiva, that even after he has been sold, the verse, “he shall have redemption,” is merely an asmachta, so writes the Yam Shel Shlomo there. This is astonishing to me, for in Bechorot 13a the Gemara says: This is satisfactory according to the one who holds that robbing a non-Jew is prohibited, etc. Likewise, in Perek HaMekabel, regarding “your fellow’s vineyard,” it says: This is satisfactory according to the one who holds that robbing a non-Jew is prohibited. Since Scripture is required regarding overreaching and regarding a vineyard, it is evident that according to the view that robbing a non-Jew is prohibited, the prohibition is by Torah law. This requires great study. I also saw in the old printings of the Talmud, for it was omitted in the newer printings, that in Bava Kamma it states: If a Jew and a non-Jew came for judgment, one may circumvent him, according to Rabbi Yishmael; Rabbi Akiva says that one may not circumvent him because of sanctification of Hashem’s Name. The implication is that only where there is sanctification of Hashem’s Name is it prohibited, but where there is no sanctification of Hashem’s Name it is permitted, etc. The Gemara distinguishes: here it concerns robbery, and there it concerns evasion of a debt. Thus it is explicit that according to Rabbi Akiva, robbery is prohibited by Torah law.
