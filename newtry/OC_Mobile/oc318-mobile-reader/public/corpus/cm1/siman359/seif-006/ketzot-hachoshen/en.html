One who seizes collateral. Sema treats one who forcibly seizes collateral as a robber, disqualified from testimony and oaths. The Shach says that although he can be liable for unavoidable accidents, people do not necessarily regard this act as robbery sufficient for disqualification. Where a creditor takes property strictly for immediate collection under beit din's eventual supervision, Rabbeinu Tam distinguishes it from prohibited seizure for security. Rivash clarifies that Rabbeinu Tam did not mean that a creditor may simply value and appropriate the collateral himself. Rather, the creditor takes it after the court has fixed a time for payment, intending later formal collection by beit din. He may not decide its value or sell it without judicial appraisal. Even this explanation remains difficult, because the authorities generally prohibit private seizure of collateral even where the creditor ultimately intends to collect from it. The distinction and its limits therefore require study.
