A robber. Above in siman 34, Bedek HaBayit writes: 'It appears to me that he is not disqualified from testimony by this, because people do not perceive such conduct as making one a robber.' I do not know why he writes this so categorically. The Raavad, Maggid Mishneh, and Tur imply that according to the Rambam he is disqualified from testimony, and the Maggid Mishneh agrees with the Rambam. There is also difficulty regarding one who enters another's house, for the Tur and Mechaber write at the beginning of siman 90 that this disqualifies him. See what I wrote there, seif katan 8. Regarding one who forcibly seizes a pledge, however, Bedek HaBayit's practical ruling appears correct. This is proven by Rashbam to Bava Batra 34 and expressly stated by Raavan. Yet the Raavad at the end of chapter 3 of Hilchot Gezeilah and the Maggid Mishneh there imply that according to the Rambam both cases disqualify him. The Raavad challenges this, holding that he is a robber only if it is found that the other person did not owe him. The Maggid Mishneh explains that the Rambam calls him a robber only so that he acquires the object and bears liability for it, not to disqualify him from testimony or oaths. See his lengthy discussion. This appears primary as practical law, at least for one who seizes a pledge, unlike the Sema, who writes that he is disqualified from testimony and oaths. Rashbam and Raavan further prove that even an ordinary person who seizes a pledge and does not establish that the other owes him is not disqualified. Shach.
