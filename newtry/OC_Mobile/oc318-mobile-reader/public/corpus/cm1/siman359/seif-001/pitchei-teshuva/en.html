Where no one is particular about it, it is permitted. Responsa Noda BiYehudah, second edition, Even HaEzer siman 77, cites what the author had written in the margin of his Choshen Mishpat: although one may take such an item because the owner is presumed not to object and there is therefore no prohibition of theft, the taker nevertheless does not acquire it as his own. Until the owner explicitly declares it ownerless, one cannot acquire title to it. Magen Avraham, siman 636 seif katan 3, cites Sefer Yereim that even according to the opinion permitting theft from a non-Jew, the object still does not qualify as "yours" for a mitzvah requiring personal ownership. See there, and see what I wrote in Pitchei Teshuva to Even HaEzer siman 28 seif 17, seif katan 26.
