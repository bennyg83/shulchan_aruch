A responsum from the eminent Rabbi Yissachar, av beit din of the holy community of Arla, to my father and teacher, the author. The question was: In Arla, when arranging a get, I refrained several times because of uncertainty about spelling the city's name. In old ketubot and other Jewish documents written here, the name is always written "Arla," with alef at the beginning and end. In the gentile registers, however, I investigated and found it written "Arle," with ayin at the end. If we write only one form, perhaps we change the city's name, which is a complete invalidation. This cannot be compared to the example of Radash and HaRadash mentioned here in seif 3, because there the pronunciation itself does not change materially; the gentiles pronounce one expanded form and the Jews a shortened form. In our case, however, the final letter changes the preceding vowel and therefore the pronunciation itself differs. It therefore appears to me that we should write, "Arla, which is called Arle." I did not wish to rely on my own judgment alone and ask the master's view. There is also water here without a specific name. It appears unnecessary to name it, because it is only a pond and falls under ordinary springs as in Maharik. We should therefore write simply, "waters of wells and waters of springs," without naming a river. Please instruct us. The author's answer was: Study Responsa Maharik and the rulings of Terumat HaDeshen, from which the Rama's gloss here derives. You will see explicitly that the established Jewish-language name is primary. Rothenburg is written in Jewish usage with a final kaf and in gentile usage with a final gimel; the examples of Teivra and Tovra likewise involve different vocalization; and Kazmir, which is called Kazimiro in the Cracow get formula, differs by several letters. Certainly, where the difference is merely one vowel point we follow the Jewish-language form. This is what I found. No doubt my father and teacher had more to say, but the year brought calamity and his words were lost. May Hashem quench our thirst. I copied these few words as halachic guidance, not as a practical ruling.
