The language of Rambam, chapter 26 of Hilkhot Malveh VeLoveh, law 6. Maggid Mishneh writes that it is explicit in Nedarim, chapter Ein Bein HaMudar, 33a, and in Shnei Dayanei Gezeirot, Ketubot 107-108, and in the Halachot.
From the Yerushalmi cited in the Halachot there.
The language of the Tur.
In accordance with Chanan there, whose view the Gemara establishes as halachah on 109a. Tur writes in his name that the rule concerns every type of debt, and Rashi likewise explains so in Nedarim 33b. This is also Rif's view, as shown by his citation of the Yerushalmi. Tosafot write likewise on Ketubot 108a in the name of Riva, s.v. "This is Chanan." Beit Yosef writes that this also appears to be the Rosh's view in his commentary to Nedarim and in his rulings in the last chapter of Ketubot, contrary to what Tur writes in his name. Bach writes that, in his opinion, if the person who paid his fellow's debt seized the promissory note where the debt was certain, the note is not removed from him; he brings proofs. See what was cited in his name in Yoreh Deah siman 221, seif 2.
Ran writes in the name of the Yerushalmi that even where the payer can show that his relationship with the debtor is not close enough to suggest that he intended to forgive the payment, the debtor is still exempt.
The language of Rambam there.
Sema writes in the name of Levush that, according to the practice of gentile merchants who compromise with debtors by granting payment over days and years when adequate guarantors are provided, there is a question in a case where a Jew owes such a merchant and another Jew purchases the promissory note from the merchant and demands immediate payment. It is clear that the debtor may say, "I could have appeased the merchant with guarantors and paid over an extended period." These words are correct.
