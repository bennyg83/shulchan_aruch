The law of one who pays another person's debt or gives collateral for another. It contains 2 se'ifim.
If a person pays another's debt without the debtor's knowledge, even if the debt was documented and even if collateral secured it and the payer took that collateral, the debtor need not reimburse him. The debtor takes back his collateral without payment, and the payer loses his money. This applies even if the creditor was pressing the debtor to pay, because the debtor might have appeased the creditor and obtained forgiveness of the debt. {Rama: This appears to be the primary view, contrary to those who say the debtor must reimburse him. Some say this applies only when he paid a Jewish creditor. If he paid a non-Jew, all agree that the debtor must reimburse him, and certainly if he redeemed the debtor's collateral from the non-Jew, as stated in Responsa Mahari Weil 82. This is the proper ruling. See Yoreh De'ah at the end of siman 252 regarding whether one who redeems another from captivity must be reimbursed. Some say all the foregoing applies when the payer held none of the debtor's property. If he held the debtor's property and used it to pay the debtor's debt, his act is effective. Some say that even if he gave his own collateral for another, since he could have given the other person's property had he wished, the debtor must reimburse him although he did not actually use the debtor's property. See note 166.}
