["His fellow is not obligated to pay, etc. Even though his fellow is legally liable to the authorities. So appears in Mordechai, chapter HaGozel Batra, and in the Rashba responsa cited by the Beit Yosef in siman 163, beginning of section 9."]
["A teacher who was, etc. In my view this ruling is not correct where the teacher caused the loss of the non Jewish pledges and is legally liable, as appears from Hagahot Mordechai, the source of this ruling, from the Rama's own language, and explicitly from Sema seif katan 8. Why should the homeowner not retain the teacher's money for what he was forced to pay on his account? Even according to those who exempt one who pays another's debt without authorization, even to a non Jew, and even according to those who exempt one seized for another although the other is legally liable, this case is different. The liability arose because the teacher was staying in his house. The teacher was genuinely liable, and the homeowner was forced to pay for him. The teacher therefore must reimburse him, and the homeowner may retain money in his possession. The Rama derived his ruling from Hagahot Mordechai at the end of Bava Batra in the name of Maharam, but that passage appears to mean the opposite. There seems to be a textual error there. It means that the claimant sought to seize money belonging to the teacher that was held by another homeowner, and Maharam ruled that he could not do so until first notifying the teacher. This is why the passage proves its rule from the principle that a person's property is his guarantor and one does not collect first from the guarantor. If the Rama's reading were correct, that proof would be irrelevant. This is explicit in Maharam, Prague edition, responsum 37, cited by Mordechai and Hagahot Ashri at the end of HaKotev and briefly by the Beit Yosef at the end of siman 86. It follows that once the teacher is notified, the claimant may even detain funds held by another homeowner, all the more so funds in his own possession. Terumat HaDeshen, responsum 304 and Pesakim UKetavim 64, likewise supports this. He further explains that Maharam addressed actual delivery of the defendant's property to the claimant, as does the proof from the principle that a person's assets are guarantors. Merely freezing the defendant's money in a third party's possession until the claimant proves his claim is permitted. Mahari Weil, responsum 165, likewise indicates that one may retain money in his possession because he had to compromise with a non Jew on the other's account. Perhaps the Rama's text should read that the teacher left his money with another homeowner, but that is strained. Moreover, this rule would then belong in siman 129 under guarantors rather than here. The Ir Shushan and Sema both understood the Rama literally, and the Sema therefore struggled to distinguish this case from the preceding rule that one who pays another's debt to a non Jew must be reimbursed and from the case of coercers attacking a deposit at the end of siman 126. All this difficulty disappears according to my explanation: here the homeowner may retain the money in his possession. Indeed, the case is unlike coercers attacking a loan at the end of siman 126, where the seizure was unlawful. Here the teacher was legally liable."]
["And he left some of his money, etc. The non Jews did not seize the homeowner because he held the teacher's money. Rather, he had to compromise with them because the teacher was staying in his house, as Sema seif katan 8 explicitly explains. If they detained the homeowner's money because the teacher's money was in his possession and he therefore had to compromise, even the Rama would agree that the teacher must reimburse him, as follows from the end of siman 126. This is true even with ordinary coercion, all the more so here where the teacher is legally liable. Rabbi Shmuel de Medina likewise writes that if she owed the judge because of a bribe and another had to pay it, she must reimburse him. Therefore, so long as Shmaya is in possession he is believed with an oath; if he is not in possession but can prove that he had to pay because they detained him on account of her document that he held, she must reimburse him."]
["There is no one who is seized, etc. See Mabit II, responsa 123 and 124, and Maharshal, responsa 55, 176, 340, 325, and 329."]
["Because of the tax, etc. Shiltei Giborim writes that this applies only to the tax of that kingdom. If Reuven, who belongs to another kingdom, was seized for Shimon's tax in this kingdom, since the king wrongfully robbed and coerced him, Shimon owes nothing. See also siman 369."]
["Every year, etc. Maharshal writes that nowadays the king's law permits collection indefinitely, especially because rulers often grant Jews extensions for several years. Therefore property may be seized even for older tax obligations. See there."]
["For the gift, etc. The Bach writes that this refers only to a fixed assessment imposed on every individual. A private gift voluntarily undertaken by one person to the king each year does not make others liable to seizure for that individual's promise, as appears from Mordechai cited by the Beit Yosef at the end of this siman."]
["That every individual gives to the king, etc. The Maggid Mishneh writes that since these are royal dues, one may say that where one person paid them he is entitled to reimbursement even if the king collected them from him unlawfully. Certainly, if the king collected from him lawfully because of his fellow, the fellow must reimburse him. See also the end of siman 369."]
["And some say, etc. I do not know why the Rama presents this as merely some say. The Darkei Moshe and Beit Yosef cite it from Hagahot Mordechai, and Sema seif katan 7 cites Mahari Weil 106, but it is an explicit Gemara in Bava Kamma 113b: Rava said that one townsman may be seized for another townsman with respect to the current year's tax, but not for a past year's tax once the king has already been appeased. Maharshal wondered why the Tur and Rambam omitted this Gemara, but he overlooked the Tur below, siman 369 seif 10, who writes it explicitly, and the Rambam's language, every year, as the Kesef Mishneh explains. The Rama's formulation can be reconciled only with difficulty. In any event, the view cited as some say is the primary law."]
