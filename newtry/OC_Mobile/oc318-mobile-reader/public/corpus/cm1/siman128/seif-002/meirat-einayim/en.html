His fellow is not obligated to reimburse him. It appears that even those who disagree above and hold that one who voluntarily pays another's ordinary debt must be reimbursed agree here. There the payer acted voluntarily and conferred a benefit, and the Sages required reimbursement so that the door not be closed before those who perform acts of kindness. Here, however, he did not pay voluntarily. He was compelled to pay because he was seized on the other person's account. Therefore, even if he paid a gentile, which in the preceding rule ordinarily requires reimbursement according to all opinions, here where the gentile seized him and forced payment the other person need not reimburse him. This is my view and I wrote so in the Perishah, contrary to the Ir Shushan's strained distinctions. The Gemara states broadly that one person is not seized for another except in matters of tax.
And he wishes to detain them, and he had to compromise with the gentile, etc. Although Rama wrote immediately above that according to some, when one pays a gentile on another's behalf reimbursement is required according to all opinions, the present case differs. There, the payment benefits the debtor, because had this person not paid the gentile, the gentile would have returned against the debtor and collected from him. Here, however, the teacher had already left, and the gentiles could not pursue him. They merely made a claim against the householder, saying: The teacher lived in your house, so you must pay for him. This is also unlike the rule at the end of siman 126, where if coercive officials come upon Reuven because of a deposit belonging to Shimon that is in Reuven's hands, Reuven may satisfy them from that very deposit. There they know the deposit is in his possession and come because of that specific property. Moreover, that rule applies specifically to a deposit, because the deposited property remains intact and is the very thing for which they came. A loan is different because money lent is given for expenditure. Here too, the money the teacher left in the householder's hands is not considered the very property for which they came.
Every year. Rama uses this precise formulation because Hagahot Mordechai states that the rule applies only to the current year's tax. For a prior year, once the king has already been appeased, one person should not be burdened on account of another. Mahari Weil writes similarly, and Darkei Moshe cites it. Rama himself later writes this as an alternative opinion. Since it is already implicit in the Mechaber's unqualified language here, it would have been preferable not to restate it merely as 'some say.'

But if there is a local enactment, we follow, etc. The Rashba writes that if the ruler imposed coercion or a false charge upon part of the community, the others need not contribute unless there is a local enactment making all townspeople partners for such matters, in which case local custom governs. However, if some members of the community themselves did something that caused the accusation, the others need not contribute. This also explains Rama's continuation, 'Nevertheless, if some of them did, etc.'
