His fellow is not obligated to pay. See Sema, seif katan 6, who writes that even according to the opinion that one who pays another's debt is entitled to reimbursement, one who is seized on account of another is exempt, and Sema labors to explain why. In my view the reason is simple. Shach in siman 391, seif katan 20, explains that one who descends into another's property without permission is reimbursed because he intended to improve it, but if Reuven takes Shimon's money and uses it to improve Levi's property, Levi is exempt. Thus, when one pays another's debt, the debtor's assets were initially subject to the creditor's lien, and payment removes that lien. This is comparable to improving another's property without permission, for which reimbursement is due. By contrast, where one person's property is seized for another, it resembles Reuven improving Shimon's property with Levi's money, for which Shimon is exempt. Even according to the second distinction, that the exemption in the case of dye is because the improvement is not visible, removal of a lien is likewise not a visible improvement. I am nevertheless troubled by why the person whose property was seized should be exempt. If Reuven seizes Shimon's property for Levi's debt, Reuven is a complete robber. Were Shimon to sue Reuven before us, Reuven would unquestionably have to return it. Since Levi owes Reuven and Reuven owes Shimon, Levi should become obligated to Shimon by the lien of Rabbi Natan. This doctrine applies even where the creditor is a non Jew. We must therefore say that the case is one in which, at the time Reuven seized Shimon's property for Levi, he took it as actual payment and thereby discharged Levi's debt. The lien of Rabbi Natan then no longer applies, as Shach writes in siman 66, seif katan 58. Accordingly, if he seized Shimon's property only as collateral for Levi's debt and not as payment, Levi must reimburse Shimon by virtue of the lien of Rabbi Natan. See my Mekor Chaim on the laws of Pesach, siman 441, where I wrote that if Shimon's property was in Levi's possession and Levi's creditor seized it, Levi must reimburse Shimon, with proof from the law of a king who seized a person's threshing floor for his debt, where tithes must still be separated.
A teacher. See Taz, who writes that whenever the non Jew could have legally claimed against Shimon under their law, the rule of one being seized on account of another does not apply, and Shimon must reimburse him.
