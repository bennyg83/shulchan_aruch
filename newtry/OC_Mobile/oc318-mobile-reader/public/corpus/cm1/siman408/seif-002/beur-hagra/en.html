And we do not accept, etc. This is derived from Bava Kamma 24a: "the ox does not become muad," etc., "they testified before," etc., "it does not become," etc. The repetition teaches that even where no alternative procedure is possible, as in this case, testimony outside the required presence is ineffective. This accords all the more with the final opinion in siman 28 seif 15, which infers from the rule permitting testimony where witnesses are dangerously ill and similar cases that after the fact it is ordinarily valid. Here the Gemara says that even after the fact it is ineffective. At first glance this is a proof for that opinion, for otherwise the present rule would be unnecessary. One may answer that one might have thought the ox's presence suffices because the death of the ox is compared to the death of its owner, as stated on 45a. The rule therefore teaches otherwise, as in Sanhedrin 19.
