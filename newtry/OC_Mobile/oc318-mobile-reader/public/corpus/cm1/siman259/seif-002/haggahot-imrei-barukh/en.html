(Rama, seif 5) If the finder is poor and the owner of the lost item is wealthy. Note: Mordechai states: "it is as though he cries out," etc. Raavyah explains that the reason we do not compel one there to act beyond the letter of the law may be that the finder was poor and the owner wealthy. Shitah Mekubetzet, in the name of Rabbeinu Yonatan, answers that the phrase "it is as though he cries out" refers to one who is not such an important person that we would compel him to act beyond the letter of the law. See there also what it cites from the Ritva, that when Shmuel says "he is obligated," he means obligated as a matter of piety, implying that there is no compulsion. See above, siman 12:2.
