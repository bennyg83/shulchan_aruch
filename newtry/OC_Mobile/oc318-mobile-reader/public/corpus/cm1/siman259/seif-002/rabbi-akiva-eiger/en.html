Note: In my humble opinion, this matter requires considerable analysis in light of the principle explained by Ramban in Milchamot, in the sugya of one who saw a sela before the owner's despair, Bava Metzia 26b. Despair does not take effect for a lost object after it has come into the finder's possession because he is then a custodian of the lost object, and as a deposit in his hands it remains in the owner's possession. It is therefore as though it were resting in the owner's courtyard, where despair is ineffective. But if he lifted it with intent to steal it, since he is not holding it on behalf of the owner, the owner's subsequent despair does take effect. See there. Accordingly, one may say that here, since the majority are non-Jews and the finder may lawfully acquire the object by attributing it to a non-Jew, he holds it for himself and does not have the status of a custodian. The owner's subsequent despair should therefore be effective so long as the finder has not learned that it belonged to a Jew. The practical ruling requires study.
