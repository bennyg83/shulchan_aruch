The owners. The reason is that the owner thinks perhaps a Jew will find it and return it to me. Ibid.
Those passing by. We do not apply here the rule that when majority and proximity conflict we follow the majority, namely the majority population of the city. That principle applies only when the nearby source is not itself at the place where the item was found, so that we must say it came there from another place. In such a case, it is reasonable to say that it came there from among the majority of the city's inhabitants. Ibid.
When it fell. For we hold that despair without knowledge is not despair, since the item came into the finder's possession while prohibited to him, because the owner did not yet know of its loss when it came into the finder's possession. Ibid.
Books. Since such books are ordinarily purchased only by Jews, the owner does not despair of recovering them, reasoning that the Jewish purchaser will announce them and that he can provide an identifying sign. See below, siman 236, seif 8 in the gloss. Ibid.
