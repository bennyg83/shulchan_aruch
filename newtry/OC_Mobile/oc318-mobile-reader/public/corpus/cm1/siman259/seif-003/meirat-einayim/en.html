We will not give up the owner. The taste that the husband may find Israel and return to me:
In a place where most passersby are Jews. In the Perishah I wrote that the requirement of a Jewish majority among the passersby is deliberate. Where most passersby are gentiles, we presume the owner despaired, unless most of those passing there are Jews. An even division is insufficient. Conversely, where an item is found in a city whose population is mostly Jewish, one must return it unless most of the passersby at that location are gentiles. I also explained why we do not apply here the rule that when majority and proximity conflict we follow the majority, meaning the majority population of the city. That rule applies where the nearby source is not at the actual place of discovery and we must infer that the item traveled there from somewhere else. In such a case it is reasonable to attribute it to the majority source. Here the relevant majority is that of the people actually passing at the place where the object was found.
And like a qua. He knew immediately in his fall, that he was not afraid of Hashem’s presence, that he would not be cursed as a result of Hashem’s presence in his hands, and that he did not know his fall when he came near him and as a result of Hashem’s promise.
If books were lost, etc. Books are ordinarily purchased only by Jews. Their owner therefore does not despair, because he assumes that a Jewish purchaser or finder will announce them and he can identify them by signs. Rama writes similarly in siman 236:8.
