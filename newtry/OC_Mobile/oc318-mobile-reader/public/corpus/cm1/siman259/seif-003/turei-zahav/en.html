(Seif 3: where most passersby there are non-Jews.) I do not understand what the Sema, seif katan 3, resolved with his explanation. In Perek Lo Yachpor 24, the Gemara discusses wine jugs found among vines in a Jewish vineyard. Rava permitted them, and Rashi explains that Jews were more common there than non-Jews. The Gemara asks whether Rava rejects Rabbi Chanina's rule that between majority and proximity we follow the majority. Rashi explains that the local Jews were more common than non-Jews, yet the general population majority was non-Jewish. This shows that according to our ruling that we follow the majority, we look to the general majority and not merely the local Jewish majority in that vineyard. If so, that is exactly our case. Why then must the finder announce the item? Let us attribute the loss to the city's non-Jewish majority. It appears to me that the difference is that here the matter can be clarified by announcement. We therefore announce it so that if the item belongs to a Jew, that can become known. The majority affects only whether the owner is presumed to have despaired. In a place with a non-Jewish majority, the item belongs to the finder because even if it fell from a Jew, the owner despairs. But where there is no such despair, the finder must announce even when the majority is non-Jewish and only a minority Jewish. Majority does not override a readily available clarification by announcement. Tosafos in Perek Lo Yachpor 23b express a similar principle.
