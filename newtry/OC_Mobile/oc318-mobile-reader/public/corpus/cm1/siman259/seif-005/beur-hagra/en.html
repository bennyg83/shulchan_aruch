And if, etc. Mordechai writes this according to his view that the finder is compelled to act beyond the strict letter of the law. This is the meaning of 'he is obligated to return it.' The same appears at the end of chapter HaOmanin. See above, siman 12, seif 2, in the gloss. Ran's and Rashba's cases concern a finder who, etc.
(Supplement) If he is, etc. Mordechai writes: 'He is obligated to return it.' He asked whether this means only beyond the strict letter of the law, and found similarly in chapter 2 of Bava Kamma that one is obligated when seeking to fulfill his duty before Heaven, and at the end of chapter HaOmanin. This shows that compulsion is applied. Raavan and Raavyah rule likewise. The statement that the owner is treated as one crying over his lost house and is not compelled may be reconciled according to Raavyah by saying that the finder was poor and the owner wealthy.
