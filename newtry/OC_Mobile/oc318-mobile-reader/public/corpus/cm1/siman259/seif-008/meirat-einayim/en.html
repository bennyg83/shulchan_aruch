A river in which they make a barrier, etc. This means a dam or partition made in the water to stop the flow so that fish are trapped there. Automatically, an object lost in the flowing water may also be stopped by that barrier. If most of the workers regularly occupied with that barrier are Jews, then even if most people living around the river are gentiles, a Jewish owner who loses something there does not despair. He assumes that the Jewish workers will find it, announce it, and return it upon receiving identifying signs. The ruling then states the reverse case. Even where most people generally present are Jews, if the permanent guards are gentiles and are the people most likely to find what is lost, Jewish owners despair and the finder need not announce it. Some texts read "and every place," but "and likewise" is preferable, because the law presents the inverse principle.
