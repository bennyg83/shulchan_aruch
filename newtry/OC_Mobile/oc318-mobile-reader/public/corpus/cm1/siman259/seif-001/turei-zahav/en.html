(Seif 1, one who sees a lost article.) The Sema writes that the text means one who sees it and lifts it. He overlooked the explicit words of Nimukei Yosef concerning the case of a kohen who is in a cemetery: “Immediately upon seeing it he violates a prohibition and a positive commandment. When we say that he waited before attending to it, this is because he delayed and did not recognize it as a lost article, while he could still have returned it and fulfilled the positive commandment. Certainly, one who sees a lost article and does not immediately take it, so long as he guards it, has not yet violated the positive commandment. He violates it only when he nullifies the opportunity and walks away. When the owners despair of recovering it, the positive commandment is no longer capable of fulfillment, but that nullification did not occur because of the finder.” Rambam’s words, “one who sees a lost article belonging to a Jew,” likewise refer to mere seeing.
