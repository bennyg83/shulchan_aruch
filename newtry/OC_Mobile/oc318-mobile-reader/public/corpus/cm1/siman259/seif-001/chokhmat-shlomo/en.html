Seif 1. Wait until after despair. Note: See above siman 198, where we proved that even if an object does not physically reach a person until after the owner's despair, if at the earlier stage he could not have acquired it immediately, he may be unable to acquire it afterward because the situation is treated like one in which it came to him in a prohibited state. This follows the Shitah Mekubetzet and Hagahot Ashri. According to the Rosh and Tosafot, who give other resolutions, one can say that he does acquire later. Alternatively, although an acquisition may fail when attempted successively, it may work when both legal effects occur simultaneously, if we do not follow Rabbah's rule that whatever cannot occur successively cannot occur simultaneously. Thus his courtyard and the acquisition may take effect together. There, same seif. Bava Batra teaches that when majority and proximity conflict, we follow the majority. I find it difficult why the Gemara does not challenge this from Makhshirin 2:3, concerning two bathhouses, one pure and one impure, where vapor near the impure bathhouse is impure and vapor near the pure bathhouse is pure. Why should we not follow the majority if the water in one bathhouse is more abundant? The later mishnayot explicitly distinguish cases according to majority, so this cannot simply be a case with no majority. The Mishna therefore seems to give priority to proximity even where there is a majority, and even where the proximity is demonstrable. Nor can this be explained by saying that immersion makes the matter one that can be permitted later, for that might justify stringency when one is close to impurity but does not explain leniency when one is close to purity despite a contrary majority. This requires study.
