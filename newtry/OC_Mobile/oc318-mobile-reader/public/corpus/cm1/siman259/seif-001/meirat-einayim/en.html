One who sees a lost object belonging to a Jew is obligated, etc., as it says, "You shall surely return them." The word "sees" is not literal. As the Tur and Mechaber later explain from the Gemara, if he waited and did not pick it up until after the owner despaired and then took it for himself, he violates only "You may not hide yourself," not the positive command of return. Here "one who sees" means one who sees and lifts it, so that it comes into his possession. The wording follows the verse in Ki Tetzei: "You shall not see your brother's ox or sheep wandering and hide yourself from them, you shall surely return them to your brother," continuing, "so shall you do with every lost thing of your brother which is lost from him and you find." The verses show that once he finds and takes hold of it, the command of return applies.
Even if the owner later despairs, etc. Since the lost object came into his hand before despair, when it was still prohibited property, later despair does not permit him to keep it. He is already obligated by the mitzvah of return and must restore it to the owner. Despair can transfer rights only when the object comes to the finder after the owner's despair.
