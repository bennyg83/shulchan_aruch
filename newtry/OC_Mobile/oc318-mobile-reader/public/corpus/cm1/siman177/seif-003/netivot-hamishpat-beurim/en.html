But not another profession. See Sema, seif katan 2. According to Rambam there, even if the improvement came through personal effort or personal funds it belongs to the common estate. One might therefore think that here too, even income from another profession belongs to the common estate. In my view the cases are entirely different. There the heirs improved partnership property. Here one became a scribe or artisan and did not improve partnership assets at all. Certainly everything he earns belongs to him personally. This is true even if he obtained the other profession because of his father, such as where his father had been an artisan for a wealthy man and, after the father's death, that patron employed one of the sons. The income belongs to that son, because the patron's favor is shown to the orphans generally, whereas ordinary customers pay only for the artisan's own work. For this reason, according to the opinion that even work for the king is shared, the worker first takes ordinary labor wages for himself, and only the additional amount the king gives as a kindness to the orphans enters the common estate.
