Partners where one enters royal service or becomes ill. It contains five seifim.
If one of several brothers is appointed by the king as a tax collector, scribe, financial officer, or similar royal servant, then if the appointment was made on account of their father, for example because their father had been known in that role and the authorities said they would appoint his son as a kindness to the orphans, the stipend and all earnings belong to all the brothers, even if the appointed brother is especially capable and independently worthy of the post. If he was appointed on his own account, the earnings belong to him alone. The same distinction applies to loss: if the king took money from him because of the brothers' joint property and wealth, all bear the loss; if it was because of his own wealth, he bears it alone. Rama: This applies while the brothers remain partners in their father's estate, but not after division. Some say the same rule applies to other partners: if one receives an appointment on behalf of all, profit and loss are shared. Others limit the rule to royal service, while earnings from another occupation belong entirely to the individual. Others say that even in royal service he first receives ordinary wages for his daily labor.
