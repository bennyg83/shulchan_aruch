The king. See Responsa Mahara Sasson, siman 135.
Another. See what the Tur and Shulchan Aruch write at the beginning of siman 287. Although they said that where a man dies leaving adult and minor sons, and the adults improve the estate, the improvement belongs to all, nevertheless, if the adults exerted themselves physically, the improvement belongs to them. See there. The same principle applies here. Sema. The Taz writes: It appears that service imposed by the king is a common occurrence, and from the outset the partners rely upon that possibility. One may ask from the Rama in siman 176, seif 12, for the case here should certainly be included where an opportunity came on account of all of them, even if it did not come from the king. One may answer that on this point the Rama follows the Itur, whom I cited there, that theft is not shared among the partners. The opinion brought by the Rama there does not distinguish here between service for the king and other service. This supports what I wrote there, that the halachah should follow the Itur.
