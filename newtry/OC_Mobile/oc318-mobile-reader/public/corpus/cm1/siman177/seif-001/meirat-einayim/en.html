To perform kindness for the orphans. This is Rambam's wording and rationale. Ramban gives another reason: as long as brothers have not divided an inherited estate, we view the household property as though all are one unit and as though their father were still alive in the inherited assets. They and their children are supported from the common estate. In the Derishah I wrote that Ramban thus regards undivided brothers as ordinary partners, with everything gained falling into the partnership. All the more so would he hold this regarding other partners who expressly agreed that all profits and earnings be shared. The Tur states this explicitly, and it is the 'some say' Rama cites nearby. This is unlike Beit Yosef and Darkei Moshe, who understand Ramban's rule as limited to brothers. According to Rambam's rationale of kindness to orphans, the rule would not apply to other partnerships, though even for Rambam the brothers must still remain legally partnered in some sense. See the Perishah carefully.
But earnings from another trade belong entirely to him. See the beginning of siman 287, where the Tur and Mechaber write that although where a father dies leaving older and younger sons and the older sons improve the estate, the improvement is ordinarily shared, if the older sons exerted their own bodies in a separate occupation the gain belongs to them. The same reasoning applies here. See the Derishah.
