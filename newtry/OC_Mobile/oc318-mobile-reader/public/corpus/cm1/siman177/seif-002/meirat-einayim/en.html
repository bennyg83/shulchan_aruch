If one of them became ill, if through negligence, etc. The Mechaber follows what the Beit Yosef wrote, citing Tosafot, the Rosh, Rabbeinu Yerucham, and Ramban, and expressing surprise at the Tur for ruling like the alternative view cited by Rama. In the Derishah I explained Tosafot and the Rosh and demonstrated that they in fact agree with the Tur. Briefly, the Mishnah states: Brothers who are partners, if one was taken for royal service, the result belongs to all; if one became ill and was healed, the cost is his own. The Gemara explains in the name of Rabbi Ilai that this applies specifically where he became ill through negligence, but if through unavoidable circumstances he is healed from the common fund. The Rosh records this. He then cites a Tosefta in the name of Rabban Shimon ben Gamliel: This applies where the treatment has a fixed cost, but where it has no fixed cost, he is healed from the common fund. The Beit Yosef understood the Tosefta's distinction to qualify the Gemara's case of negligent illness, so that unavoidable illness would always be paid from the common fund. That is not compelling. Rather, these are two independent interpretations of the Mishnah. According to Rabbi Ilai, the Mishnah speaks of negligent illness, regardless of whether the cure has a fixed cost. According to the Tosefta, it speaks of unavoidable illness, with the distinction between a cure with a fixed cost and one without. This is evident from Tosafot's language and from their statement in the name of Ri. Thus, under Rabbi Ilai's interpretation used here, there is no difference in negligent illness between a cure with a fixed cost and one without, and the ill partner pays his own costs. This is precisely the Tur's ruling. Although Rabbeinu Yerucham and the Ran understood the matter like the Beit Yosef, that is their own approach, not necessarily that of Tosafot and the Rosh. Rama's citation of the alternative opinion therefore accords with this explanation.
