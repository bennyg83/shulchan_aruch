But where two partners engage in the enterprise, and so forth. See Responsa Chacham Tzvi there, who writes that although the words of the authorities appear to require that both partners personally engage in the partnership business, that is not literal. Once each contributes money to the partnership and they stipulate the division in whatever manner suits them, by halves, thirds, quarters, or otherwise, even if one sits in the study hall while the other, like Zevulun, goes to the seacoast to trade, if one becomes ill his medical expenses are not paid from the partnership fund. The one who performs the labor receives additional compensation for his effort. Even if we say that the partners acquire rights in one another through the benefit they receive from joining together, because the enterprise becomes larger and the one who trades gains the reputation of being a substantial and trustworthy merchant, it is considered as though each is engaged in the business with respect to the rule of illness, because a person's money performs his brokerage for him. He further explains that there are three distinctions in this matter. Where brothers are partners and only one conducts the business, if he becomes ill through an unavoidable illness, his medical expenses are paid from the common fund even if the illness has no fixed duration. If each brother works in the business, an illness with a fixed duration is paid from his own funds, while an illness without a fixed duration is paid from the common fund. The same applies to ordinary partners who are supported from the common fund, according to the Rosh, whose reasoning is that medical expenses are comparable to sustenance. But ordinary partners who are not supported from the common fund, if one becomes ill, even while traveling on partnership business, his medical expenses are his own, because a partner is not entitled to sustenance from the common fund merely because he travels on partnership matters, as the Rashba writes in a responsum, unless there is an established custom to the contrary. This accords with Shulchan Aruch above, siman 176, seif 45, and what I wrote there. On this basis the wording of the Rashba cited by Beit Yosef and the wording of Rabbi Yosef Halevi cited by the Tur and by Shulchan Aruch here are readily reconciled, so that the opening and closing clauses are not contradictory.
