Some say the reverse, etc. In my view, the Mechaber's ruling is primary. The lengthy discussions of the Sema and Bach here do not appear correct, particularly their explanation of Tosafot concerning the unavoidable circumstance in this case. The Rosh's Chiddushim also support the Mechaber's interpretation. Whoever explains Tosafot otherwise, in my opinion, reverses the words of the living God. Tosafot plainly mean that with respect to a widow's medical expenses we distinguish between treatment with a fixed cost and treatment without a fixed cost, and that this distinction is relevant whether the illness arose through negligence or through unavoidable circumstances. But in the unavoidable circumstance discussed here among partners, there is no distinction between treatment with a fixed cost and treatment without a fixed cost. There is no need to elaborate. The text in Piskei Tosafot contains a scribal error. The Rosh's intent is unquestionably the same, and the Remezim, the Tur's abridgment of the Rosh's rulings, state this explicitly. My heart tells me there is also a scribal error in the Tur. Rabbeinu Yerucham and Nimukei Yosef likewise state explicitly like the Mechaber. At first glance one might still question the practical ruling, since Rif, Rambam, and the other authorities do not cite the Tosefta. In truth there is no difficulty. They relied on what they wrote regarding a widow, distinguishing treatment that has a fixed cost, and the same rule therefore applies here. The Yerushalmi in the chapter Mi Shemet also implies that the cases are analogous, for it cites the ruling of Rabban Shimon ben Gamliel concerning a wife's medical treatment in the discussion of partners.
