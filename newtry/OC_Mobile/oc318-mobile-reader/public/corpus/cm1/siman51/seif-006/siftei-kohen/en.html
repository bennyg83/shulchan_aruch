Unless that person withdrew, etc. The Mechaber’s ruling that withdrawal cures the defect appears incorrect. No authority states that withdrawal repairs a deed invalidated because part of its testimony was void. The Ramban’s discussion concerns oral testimony where a witness may be suspected of testifying for the benefit of a relative, such as property stolen from two persons, one of whom is his relative. There, withdrawal before testimony removes the personal interest. Here, however, the deed is void because when part of a body of testimony is invalid, the whole body is invalid. Even if the unrelated beneficiary says, “Give me only my share and not the relative’s share,” he is not heard, because the defect concerns the testimony as a whole. Once the deed was void, the relative’s later withdrawal cannot revive it. The Rosh’s responsum cited by the Mechaber does not address this case, and the Rivash indicates expressly that withdrawal does not help where one witness was found related or disqualified. One might force the Mechaber’s language to mean that after withdrawal the witnesses may testify orally before beit din, but the Rama and Ir Shushan understand the deed itself to become valid. Perhaps the case is where the relative withdrew before the deed was written, and the instrument records the event as it occurred, but that is strained. There is also reason to invalidate even then, lest people learn from the document that a relative may testify, since the withdrawal is not evident from the deed. Unless the deed itself records the withdrawal, the ruling requires study.
In oral testimony, in such a case, they are qualified, etc. This applies specifically where one writes his property to two persons and the witnesses have no personal interest arising from the relationship. The unrelated beneficiary would receive only his half in any event. But where the relationship creates an interest, such as where the deed is not authenticated and witnesses related to one beneficiary but unrelated to the other come to authenticate it, they are disqualified even orally. Without them the deed cannot be authenticated at all, so they may be testifying for the benefit of their relative. The same applies where an item was stolen from two persons and witnesses related to one but unrelated to the other identify the thief. They may not testify unless the relative first withdraws, because they cannot testify concerning only half the item.
