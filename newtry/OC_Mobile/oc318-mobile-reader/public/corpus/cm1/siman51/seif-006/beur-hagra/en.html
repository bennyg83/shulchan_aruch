(Collected note) One who writes, etc. This follows Rabbi Yochanan in the Yerushalmi. Although the Yerushalmi compares it there to his get and his gift, we do not rule that way, because when disqualification is due to kinship, testimony of which part is invalid is wholly invalidated, as derived there from the Mishnah, "just as two," etc. Only with respect to the litigant himself is the testimony not invalidated, because there is no testimony concerning a person against himself at all, as the Rosh writes there in the name of the Raavad; see what I wrote in siman 33, seif 16. It is therefore unlike his get and his gift, where he himself is the litigant.
Unless he withdrew his interest. Bava Batra 43a; see Tosafot there, s.v. "and let them withdraw," etc.
And with oral testimony. We rule that the statement is divided only because this will not be confused with ordinary documents, unlike oral testimony. See the Rosh to Makkot and Hagahot Ashri, first chapter of Gittin.
(Collected note) And with oral testimony, etc. This is according to Rabbeinu Yoel's explanation, because otherwise it could be confused, as the Rosh and Mordechai write in his name. According to the Raavad, however, who holds that when witnesses are disqualified because of kinship all of the testimony is invalidated, as explained above, the same applies to oral testimony. Withdrawal of interest is effective only before they testify, as above, and likewise with a document in such a case.
(Collected note) Unless, etc., and with testimony, etc. This requires study. How does withdrawal of interest help, since there is no present testimony other than what they already testified in the document before withdrawing their interest? Only with oral testimony can withdrawal help, and two elements are required there: they must testify now, after withdrawing their interest. The Shach writes likewise.
But if. Bava Batra 159a teaches that we divide the statement concerning two fields and two people according to Reish Lakish, whose view we follow there. It appears that Rabbi Yochanan and Reish Lakish, who disagree in the Yerushalmi, disagree in such a case and follow their respective positions, like Ravin in the name of Rabbi Yochanan, for Ravin is a reliable transmitter of his teachings, as stated in Yevamot. This is the meaning of the Yerushalmi's statement, "Rabbi Elazar said well," and "Rabbi Elazar did not say well," etc. Rabbi Yochanan and Reish Lakish follow their own positions, whereas Rabbi Elazar, who in Bava Batra agrees with Reish Lakish, therefore said that Rabbi Elazar did not say well. The ruling follows the one who says he did not say well, for the Rambam's practice is to rule according to the later opinion, and moreover we rule like Reish Lakish.
