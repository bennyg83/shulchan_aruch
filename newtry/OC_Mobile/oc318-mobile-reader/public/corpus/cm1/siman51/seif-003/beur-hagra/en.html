A document. This is the Rambam's ruling. It requires study, since he previously ruled that one written witness is valid, and the same should apply here, as the second opinion, that of the Behag, holds. Perhaps the Rambam does not apply the presumption that a blank space was intentionally left where only two witnesses signed. Yet this too is explicit in Gittin 12a. It appears that the case is one where it is known that both witnesses signed together, as explained above in seif 7. This is why the Rama says, 'namely, in the manner, etc.' The Rif and Rosh likewise state both rules. The Shach also explains it this way. Shulchan Aruch's presentation as 'some say' is difficult, because there is no practical dispute on the second opinion of seif 2, which regards a single written witness as nothing. Thus, 'its law is like a document, etc.' means that it depends on the dispute stated above.
