Some say that it has the law of a deed, etc. The Mechaber’s wording implies that the Rambam, who gives the first view, does not treat the remaining valid signature as a one witness deed. In my view there is no basis for distinguishing a deed with two signatures from one with several. Two witnesses for monetary liability correspond to one witness for an oath. When the Rambam says that the instrument is like a shard, he means only that delivery witnesses cannot validate an instrument that is internally defective. The remaining valid signature still has the force of one written witness and can require an oath. We may also say that space was left for an elder, as the Baal Halachot Gedolot rules. This is clear from the Rif in Makkot. Where three witnesses signed and one is disqualified, the deed is sustained by the other two unless it is known that all sat together to sign. Where only two signed and one is disqualified, the deed cannot extract money, but the valid signature still functions as one witness. The Nimukei Yosef and the Gemara in Gittin support this. The Bach’s proof from the Nimukei Yosef in Sanhedrin is not decisive, since that discussion follows the Baal HaIttur, who adopts the Yerushalmi’s view that one written witness is ineffective. According to our ruling that one written witness does have force, no additional oral testimony is needed unless it is known that the witnesses joined together and the testimony was thereby nullified. The Rosh’s responsum concerns that latter case. The Rivash likewise explains that where it is not known that the witnesses signed together, the Rif agrees that one valid written witness imposes an oath. The Mechaber himself later reached this conclusion in Bedek HaBayit. Thus the phrase “some say” here does not signal a true dispute. The Baal Halachot Gedolot’s ruling is accepted by all: the valid signer requires an oath, even though the instrument cannot collect as a two witness deed.
