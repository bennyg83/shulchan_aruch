Its law is like a document signed by only one witness. Shach writes that even with two signatures and one disqualified signer we can sometimes assume extra space was left and the disqualified person merely signed for honor or to fill the space. One may ask why we do not decree against relying on the remaining witness, all the more so because a document containing invalid signatures appears internally defective. The answer is that once the document was already completed by the valid signature or signatures, a later added invalid signature does not retroactively make the earlier testimony false. The defect of internal falsification applies primarily when it exists at the moment the document becomes a legal instrument. Tosafot and Rosh therefore explain that where invalid persons sign at the end and the document has already been completed above them, one may sometimes assume they signed merely for honor or filling. The stricter authorities hold that this leniency depends on the principle that a signed document is already treated as judicially examined. Rivash, cited by Rama in siman 46, says that with one witness we do not apply the full rule that his signed testimony is already judicially examined. Therefore, if after removing the disqualified signer only one valid signer remains, we cannot use the same presumption that the invalid person signed merely for honor. The remaining testimony never acquired full documentary status.
