[Shach, letter 1] And in my humble opinion there is no difficulty, for there is a practical difference. Note: In my humble opinion, this is insufficient. Why must the oral witness say, "I acquired it"? Even if he testifies only to the loan and does not say, "I acquired it," the borrower is still not believed to say, "I repaid," because there is a deed supported by one witness. He has no migo that he could have denied the loan entirely, since he cannot say, "It never happened," when there is one witness in the deed and one oral witness. As for the Shach's underlying point, that because the witness says he was not repaid, the litigant must swear to contradict the witness, this appears consistent with the Shach's own view in siman 75, seif katan 43, where he seems to accept Rabbeinu Yitzchak's explanation that in the case of one who is obligated to take an oath but cannot do so, the reason a migo is ineffective is that he would have had to swear in order to contradict the witness. Thus, where even under his present claim he contradicts the witness, he is believed through the migo. It appears to me that this is also evident from the Ran in the chapter HaKotev, in the sugya of the cattle herder. The Ran asks that he should have to swear to contradict the herder. At first glance, why did the Ran not ask even more strongly that he should not be believed to say, "I seized it during the owner's lifetime," through a migo that he purchased it, because if he claimed purchase he would have to swear against the herder and would thus be one who is obligated to swear but cannot? Necessarily, because even under his present claim he contradicts the herder, the migo is stronger. According to Tosafot's second answer, however, also cited by the Shach there, whenever a witness is not contradicted by an oath he is equivalent to two witnesses. Thus, although the witness also says that he was not repaid, with respect to his statement that the loan occurred there is no single witness contradicting him, and on that point the witness is equivalent to two. Consequently, the borrower is not believed to say it was repaid. Examine this carefully.
