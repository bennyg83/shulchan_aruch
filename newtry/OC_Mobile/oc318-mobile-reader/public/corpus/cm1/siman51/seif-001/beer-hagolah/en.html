a) The wording of the Rambam, chapter 4 of Hilkhot Edut, from the message the scholars sent to Rabbi Yirmeyah: one witness in writing, etc. The halachah was ruled accordingly, and the Gemara concludes, "Because of this they admitted him into the study hall," Bava Batra 165b.
b) So Rabbeinu rules. For with an oral loan, since we validate testimony of a loan combined with testimony of an admission, as stated above in siman 30, what difference is there whether both are oral or one is written and one oral? Kesef Mishneh there.
c) But not to collect from purchasers, because we say an ordinary kinyan is intended to be written only when the kinyan was made before two witnesses. Kesef Mishneh according to the textual version before us. The Sema and Bach challenge this, because even one witness in writing alone prevents the debtor from claiming payment, as explained below in seif 2. They write that the Tur's version is primary, according to which one may collect even from encumbered property.
d) Tur, and so write Tosafot there 165a, s.v. "and Abaye," and the Rosh there in his rulings.
e) So it appears in the Beit Yosef.
See the Beit Yosef for his discussion. The Bach writes that the words "I received" in the Tur are a scribal error, and that Maharshal emended the text to: "But if only one witness is signed upon it, or it was delivered only in the presence of one witness, one does not collect from encumbered property." So it appears in the Rosh and in Tosafot there. See there.
