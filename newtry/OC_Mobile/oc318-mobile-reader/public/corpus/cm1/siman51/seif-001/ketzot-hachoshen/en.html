Otherwise, he asks, why is a second witness needed to defeat a claim of payment, since Rambam holds that one signed witness is already enough to prevent the borrower from claiming payment. The Shach rejects the proof and says the practical difference is where the signed witness himself comes and says the debt was not paid, or the document expressly says that he knows it was not paid; the borrower would then have to swear against that witness. In my view the Sema's proof is correct. According to Rambam, one witness in a document creates the same structure as the metal bar case of Rabbi Abba: one who is obligated to swear but cannot swear must pay. The claim of payment has no independent force because a person does not ordinarily leave even a one witness document in the creditor's hand after paying. A migo of complete denial does not help against this structure, just as it does not help in Rabbi Abba's case. Therefore even if the witness himself also testifies that there was no payment, his additional statement cannot make the borrower's position worse than his silence already did. In Rabbi Abba's case, even where the witness says the defendant snatched an object belonging to another, and the defendant admits the snatching but says, "I snatched my own," he must pay because his claim of ownership is supported only by a migo of total denial. The same applies here. See what I wrote in siman 58, where the Shach himself adopts this reasoning, and in siman 30.
