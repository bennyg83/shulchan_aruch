And she said to him, "How much will you give me or write for me?" The word "me" is not essential. The same applies if she stipulated concerning her daughter, saying, "How much support will you provide for my daughter?" The daughter acquires that right. The Gemara in Ketubot 101 explains the mishnah of a man who marries a woman and agrees to support her daughter as a case of stipulations made at the time of the marriage arrangement, in accordance with Rav Gidal: where the father says how much he will give his son and the mother how much she will give her daughter, and they immediately stand and effect kiddushin, those undertakings are acquired through speech even without a formal kinyan. The same is not limited to her daughter, but applies to her other relatives, and likewise if he stipulates with her that she will provide produce or support for his children or relatives.
