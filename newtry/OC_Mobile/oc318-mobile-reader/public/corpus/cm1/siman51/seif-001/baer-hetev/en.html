They combine. The Shach explains that the Mechaber's words describe three distinct cases. First, one witness in writing and one orally, without a kinyan, combine so that the debtor cannot claim that the transaction never occurred, according to all opinions, because both directly contradict such a denial. If he claims payment, the matter depends on the dispute in the next seif. Second, if the oral witness says, 'I made a kinyan from him,' the witnesses combine to give the obligation the status of a documented loan even regarding the debtor's inability to claim payment, according to all opinions, because an ordinary kinyan is presumed intended for writing and it is as though both witnesses signed a document. Nevertheless it is not a fully documented debt for collection from encumbered property, because the two forms of testimony are unequal: one is in writing and the other only testifies to a kinyan. Third, if the oral witness says that the document was delivered in his presence, it is a complete document and may collect from encumbered property, because we presume it was also delivered in the presence of the signed witness. This differs from the Sema, who understood the Mechaber's statement about the oral witness testifying to a kinyan as permitting collection even from encumbered property. The Shach demonstrates from the Kesef Mishneh that the Rambam means only that the debtor cannot claim payment, not that purchasers may be seized. The Tur states in the Rambam's name that it becomes a full document, but the Kesef Mishneh says his own reading is more correct. The Sema's difficulty, that according to the Rambam one signed witness alone already prevents a claim of payment and thus the oral witness seems unnecessary, is answered by practical distinctions discussed by the Shach. He concludes that his interpretation is primary, especially since some authorities hold that even a kinyan by itself does not permit collection from purchasers. Although the majority hold that it does even if no document was written, here the case is weaker because only one witness testifies to the kinyan. See the responsa cited by the Shach concerning one witness in writing and one orally.
Was delivered. The Mechaber follows his own view at the end of this siman that delivery witnesses establish the instrument. In truth, many authorities disagree. Shach.
From encumbered property. The Tur is uncertain in siman 45 whether these witnesses even combine for collection from unencumbered property. The Rama's statement here that collection cannot be made from encumbered property means only that this is certainly unavailable; as to unencumbered property, the matter remains doubtful. Sema.
