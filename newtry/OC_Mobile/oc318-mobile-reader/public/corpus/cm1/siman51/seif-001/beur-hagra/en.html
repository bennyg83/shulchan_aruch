Without a kinyan. Meaning that it permits collection from unencumbered property. This is the view of the Rosh in the name of the Ramban and Rabbeinu Yonah.
(Collected note) Without a kinyan. Meaning collection from unencumbered property, so that the borrower cannot claim, 'The matter never occurred.' This is unlike the Rashbam, who permits collection even from encumbered property, understanding the Gemara's question only as whether the testimonies combine. Tosafot, however, hold that even for collection from unencumbered property they do not combine, whether there was a kinyan or witnesses of delivery. The borrower may even deny the transaction entirely, because the testimony is regarded as written testimony submitted outside the court. See page 205.
And if he said. The Rambam rules this way. The language of Shulchan Aruch implies that collection is only from unencumbered property, as the Kesef Mishneh writes, but the Bach proves that collection may even be made from encumbered property. See the glosses of the Vilna Gaon.
(Collected note) One witness, etc.; and if he said, etc. This is the Rambam's language. He means that the testimonies combine for this purpose. The Rambam holds that two separate written documents certainly combine, as the Gemara says, 'two in writing, etc.' Tosafot and the Rosh disagree and maintain that even two written testimonies do not combine. They explain that the Gemara's case of 'two in writing' means that the witnesses observed the loan together. The unresolved question is instead whether the document was delivered before one witness.
And if that witness. This follows Tosafot and the Rosh. Otherwise, even two written witnesses would not combine to collect from encumbered property, as explained above, whereas the Gemara treats it as obvious according to Rabbi Yehoshua ben Korcha that two written testimonies combine. This is certainly true according to the Yerushalmi, which holds that they do not combine even for collection from unencumbered property, as explained above.
And specifically when they can say. We rule like Rabbi Eliezer that witnesses of delivery are essential, not witnesses of signature. A signed document is nevertheless presumed valid because we assume it was properly delivered; otherwise Rabban Gamliel's enactment requiring signatures would accomplish nothing. See the Rosh to Gittin, chapter two.
(Collected note) And specifically, etc. This is Tosafot's view: even two documents do not combine, and certainly one oral witness does not combine with one written witness, whether or not there was a kinyan. This differs from Shulchan Aruch.
(Collected note) And specifically, etc. The gloss disputes Shulchan Aruch and follows Tosafot and the Rosh. Shulchan Aruch follows the Rambam. Its statement that the witness says, 'It was delivered before me,' concerns collection from encumbered property: without such testimony, even a document arising from a kinyan does not collect from encumbered property, while with it collection may be made. The later opinion in seif 2 is not based on Tosafot's principle that one written witness is worthless, but on the Rashba and Maggid Mishneh: since there was only one witness, the debtor was not careful to retrieve the document, so possession of it does not strongly prove nonpayment. Accordingly, they say only that if he pleads payment he is believed; they do not say that if he denies the transaction he need only take a rabbinic oath, for he must still swear against the witness. The Rama relies here on his ruling above. See also siman 46, seif 10, where the Rama rules that the witness need not remember the testimony, rejecting the Rambam's view that a single written witness helps only when he remembers it. This follows Tosafot's first answer, which is accepted in Bava Batra, contrary to the Shach's reading. See page 205.
Even if there were. This is from the Rosh. See Ketubot 21a and Tosafot there regarding a witness who remembers the testimony.
(Collected note) Even if there were two, etc. A witness of delivery, who is an oral witness, does not combine with a written witness. Likewise, two separate documents each signed by one witness do not combine, nor do two documents each delivered before only one witness. This is so regarding collection from encumbered property. From unencumbered property, however, the two witnesses of delivery combine, because we rule like Rabbi Yehoshua ben Korcha. The written witnesses do not combine even for that purpose, since their testimony is written testimony outside court. The Gemara's question is whether we presume that the document was also delivered before the signing witness, as we ordinarily presume when there are two signing witnesses, or whether such a presumption is not made with only one. Although this is merely an evidentiary document and does not inherently require witnesses of delivery, since it has only one signature it can become valid only through two witnesses of delivery together. See page 205.
And it was delivered. Even if it was also delivered before only one witness, whether that was the signing witness or a different witness. So writes the Rosh.
(Collected note) From encumbered property. From unencumbered property he may collect by virtue of the two witnesses of delivery to the two documents. By virtue of the documents themselves, however, he may not collect even from unencumbered property, because this is written testimony outside court, as Tosafot explain in Bava Batra and Ketubot. See page 205.
