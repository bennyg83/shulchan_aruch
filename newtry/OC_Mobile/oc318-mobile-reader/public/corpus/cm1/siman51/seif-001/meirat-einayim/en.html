Without a kinyan, they combine. Meaning, they combine to prevent the borrower from claiming, 'It never happened.' He is not believed on that claim even with an oath, because together they are two witnesses. But if he claims that he paid, that depends on the dispute the Tur and Mechaber cite nearby in seif 2. According to one view, it is like two oral witnesses to a loan without a kinyan, and he is believed to say he paid upon taking a hesset oath. Maggid Mishneh, chapter 14 of Hilchot Malveh, likewise implies this.
'I made a kinyan from him,' etc., both combine to make it a documented loan, and he cannot say he paid. Rambam writes this in chapter 4 of Hilchot Edut, halacha 5. Kesef Mishneh there and Beit Yosef here explain that Rambam's phrase, 'and he cannot say I paid,' is the conclusion of 'to make it a documented loan,' meaning only that he cannot say he paid, but he still cannot collect from encumbered property. In my humble opinion this does not appear correct. If so, why is the second oral witness needed at all? Rambam holds that even one witness signed on a document prevents the borrower from saying 'I paid,' and he is not believed even with an oath, as the Tur and Mechaber write in the next seif. Rambam likewise writes in chapter 4 of Hilchot Malveh, halacha 10, that the debtor is obligated to swear against the one witness and, because he cannot swear, must pay. Moreover, the Tur cites this law at the beginning of this siman in Rambam's name and writes only that the two witnesses combine and it becomes a complete documented loan. He does not add the phrase copied here by the Mechaber, 'and he cannot say I paid.' It is also somewhat difficult because the Mechaber himself rules without qualification at the beginning of siman 39 that if witnesses made a kinyan from the debtor, even if no document was written, one may collect from encumbered property. It is strained to say this case differs because it is not known whether the kinyan was made also before the witness signed on the document, and therefore the oral witness says, 'I alone made the kinyan.' The Mechaber later writes, 'if the oral witness says, "the document was delivered before me,"' where 'before me' plainly means also in his presence, as Rama explains, and Beit Yosef himself writes. Therefore it appears simple that when Rambam and the Mechaber say the two combine to make it a documented loan, they mean that one may collect even from encumbered property. This is what the Tur means by 'it becomes a complete documented loan.' Rambam adds that the borrower cannot say 'I paid' because we sometimes find a case where one may collect from encumbered property yet the debtor can still say 'I paid,' as the Tur cites in the name of Tosafot in siman 39, seif 10, concerning an acknowledgment before beit din. Although Rambam holds that even a single signed witness ordinarily prevents a claim of payment, he may have stated the rule here incidentally because he was describing the full status of a proper document. It also matters even according to Sheiltot, Raavad, and Behag, who disagree with Rambam and hold that one signed witness does not create the rule of 'one obligated to swear who cannot swear must pay.' When there is an additional oral witness who says a kinyan was made, all agree that the debtor cannot say he paid. If so, why does the Mechaber explicitly state in the latter case, where the oral witness says, 'the document was delivered before me,' that it is a complete document for collection from encumbered property, but not state this expressly in the first case of a kinyan? The answer is that the Mechaber is following the Tur's wording. The Tur first cites Rambam's language and afterward adds the law where the second witness says, 'it was delivered before me,' using the wording copied by the Mechaber. This interpretation appears clear. See what I wrote further in Perishah.
And it was delivered only before one witness. Meaning, each document was delivered only before one witness: one before the witness whose signature appears on it and the other before a different witness. It was not delivered also before the witness signed on it. Such witnesses do not combine to collect from encumbered property. Even regarding collection from unencumbered property, the Tur in siman 45, seif 10, is uncertain whether they combine. His uncertainty appears to be whether the halacha follows Raavad, cited by the Tur in this siman, seif 4, who holds that they do not combine at all because Chazal enabled documentary testimony only through two witnesses and not one, a view shared by Sheiltot, or whether the halacha follows Behag and Rambam, who disagree. When the Tur in seif 2 and Rama here say, 'he does not collect from encumbered property,' they mean at minimum that encumbered property cannot be collected; whether unencumbered property may be collected remains doubtful.
