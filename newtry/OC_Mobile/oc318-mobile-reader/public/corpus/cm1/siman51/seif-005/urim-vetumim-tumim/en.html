And it was nullified in the manner explained, and so forth. Rama added these words to Mechaber. Otherwise, why would the signed valid witness need to testify again orally? According to Rama's addition one must say that the disqualified witness did not intend to testify when the will was first heard, and only later intended to sign. If he already intended at the time of the will to join as a witness, how can the valid witness testify orally? The combined testimony would have been nullified. This is especially true according to Mechaber above in siman 36, who holds that even seeing the event with testimonial intent can invalidate the others. It is strained that Mechaber would have left such a significant qualification unstated, particularly where the person ultimately did sit and sign as an actual witness. I therefore think Mechaber deliberately wrote the law without Rama's limitation, for the reason explained above. Even if the relative signed only as filler and never joined the actual testimony, the written instrument itself is still invalid where exactly two signatures appear and one is disqualified, because the instrument can be falsely validated through the relative. In that respect the authorities do not truly disagree with Rosh. It is analogous to the tied document case. The will as a document is therefore void, yet the qualified person's underlying testimony was never personally disqualified because he did not join the relative as a witness. He may accordingly testify orally. This reading removes the difficulty and is, in my opinion, clear.
