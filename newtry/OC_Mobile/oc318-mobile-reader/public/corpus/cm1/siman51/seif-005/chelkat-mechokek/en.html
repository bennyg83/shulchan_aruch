Therefore, even if they were written, they are not treated as a formal document. Meaning that if witnesses merely write in a document, "Before us, so-and-so said to so-and-so, how much will you give..." and record the words exactly as spoken, the parties did not intend through that writing to create a lien on encumbered property. Their verbal obligation arose from the benefit of the marriage connection and reaches only unencumbered property. So writes the Maggid Mishneh in Hilchot Zechiyah, chapter 6. It is clear, however, that if the document states that the parties instructed the witnesses, "Write and sign a document and deliver it to each party," it is a complete formal document, no worse than other instruments written with the obligors' authorization. If the undertaking concerned a particular existing object, however, the obligor may still be able to claim payment, as Rivash discusses at length in siman 345.
