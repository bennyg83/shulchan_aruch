(Seif 3: Only in a manner that did not invalidate, etc.) At first glance this means that we know the valid witness’s signature remained effective, such as where he left space and the disqualified witness signed afterward, yet the Rambam still treats the instrument as a shard and does not even require an oath against the valid witness. That is astonishing, for why should the valid witness be disqualified, unlike the many-witness case in seif 4? Rather, the Rambam presumes that where only two signed, both came to testify together; we do not presume that space was intentionally left. The dissenting view presumes even with two that the valid witness left space. If we know the entire testimony was joined and thereby invalidated, the valid witness must testify orally. If we know that he left space, or where there were many witnesses and that presumption applies, all agree that his written testimony obligates an oath. The dispute is only where exactly two signed and it is unknown whether they joined together.
