And there is no distinction in this respect between a first marriage and a second marriage. This appears to follow from the Gemara. The case just mentioned of one who marries a woman and undertakes to support her daughter involves a daughter from the woman's first husband, and yet he is bound by what he stipulated even though this is her second marriage. One could reject the proof, however, because perhaps the Yerushalmi's distinction between first and later marriages concerns the person making the undertaking, not the recipient. The Gemara's case may involve a bachelor for whom this is a first marriage. For that reason Rivash did not decide the matter absolutely, but wrote only that one can say so and that Rambam's words appear to support it.
