And he pays. The Sema explains that wherever two witnesses impose monetary liability, one witness ordinarily imposes an oath. To be discharged through that oath, however, the defendant must contradict the witness's testimony. Here the witness testifies that the defendant borrowed. The defendant cannot swear that he did not borrow, because he admits borrowing and only claims repayment. Although a person who borrowed before two witnesses ordinarily need not repay before witnesses and is believed to say he paid, that applies where there is no written instrument. Here there is a document, and although only one ordinary witness signed it, Rambam holds that because the obligation was framed as a document and signed by an independent witness, had it truly been paid the debtor would not have left it in the creditor's possession. The dissenters hold that such a document has no more force than a handwritten acknowledgment. Sema.
Swears. The Sema writes that this applies even if the document contains a credibility clause in favor of the creditor. Otherwise the rule would be obvious, because even with two witnesses on a standard document the creditor may have to swear before collecting, as explained at the beginning of siman 82. The Taz writes that this additional case is unnecessary to establish the novelty. The rule teaches that although the debtor resembles one who is obligated in an oath but cannot swear, the creditor does not collect automatically without an oath as in an ordinary case discussed in siman 75. Here the creditor must still swear because the instrument contains only one witness. Nevertheless, in a document containing a credibility clause the practical law is certainly as the Sema writes.
With an oath. Meaning a shevuat heset. This is implied by the poskim, the Bach rules so, and it is primary. Shach. See below, siman 75, seif 13.
