Both. The Tur writes that the creditor may say to the purchaser, "Take your share in the land." The purchaser cannot demand his share in money, and the lender cannot compel the purchaser to take money instead of land. Sema. See the Shach for his distinctions. He further writes that the word "become partners" is imprecise. It would be more accurate to say, "they divide," as in siman 171. All the authorities say that the purchaser takes his share in the land. The Maggid Mishneh writes that from here they learned that where the debt is small and the piece of land corresponding to it is too small to constitute the legal minimum size of a field, beit din does not compel the creditor to accept such a tiny parcel. Rather, the land is sold, or rented for a period, and the creditor is paid in money. The Ramban and Rashba write likewise in their responsa. See also siman 103, seif 5.
Apotiki. The Maggid Mishneh records another approach according to which designation as an apotiki does not strengthen the creditor beyond the amount of his debt. It merely prevents the purchaser from insisting that the creditor take land rather than money, or according to the formulation there, allows removal by money. Regarding improvement, however, an apotiki creates no distinction. The Shach inclines to this view and brings proofs. He concludes that in his view this approach is primary in both Gemara and logic, but because many early authorities disagree he is not in a position to decide conclusively. Nevertheless, it appears clear to him that the purchaser may say, "I rely on those authorities."
His expenses. This applies where the amount originally lent equaled only the value of the land. If the creditor lent an amount equal to both the land and its improvement, he need not reimburse the purchaser even for the expenses. The Tur states this explicitly in seif 6. Sema.
