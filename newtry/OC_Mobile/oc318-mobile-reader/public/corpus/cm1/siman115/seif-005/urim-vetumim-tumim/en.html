Some say that if he did not make it an apotiki, etc. The Shach seeks to decide like the Mechaber because, when the Gemara adduces proof from the heirs' case, it had not yet considered establishing the matter as an apotiki. This is not compelling. The Gemara did not lack knowledge of the actual facts. Only regarding Shmuel's general statement that three parties have enhancement appraised for them did the Gemara initially not think of an apotiki; at the conclusion it says that even that statement can involve an apotiki. But the particular incident where the heirs said, "We enhanced," may well have been an apotiki from the outset. The Shach further argues that if it was an apotiki, how could Rav Chisda initially think of removing the heirs by paying money? This follows the Shach's own position against the Mechaber below, that where there is no apotiki the creditor can remove the purchaser with money even when the debt equals the value of the land and enhancement. According to the Mechaber here and above in siman 109, however, the heirs themselves can remove the creditor with money in an ordinary case. Why then did the Gemara say that the creditor could remove them with money? They could answer, "Give us our share in the land." Necessarily the incident involved an apotiki. Even so, the Gemara initially thought the creditor could pay them money. The Shach therefore has no proof. His separate question, how the Gemara could think of removing them with land if the field was an apotiki, is no question. The enhancement itself was not made an apotiki and remained in the heirs' domain. That is simple. Tosafot in Sotah 25b, s.v. "Beit Hillel," ask how we can say that land stands ready for collection when we rule like Beit Hillel that an instrument standing to be collected is not treated as already collected. This indicates that Tosafot there follow the Rambam that the case does not concern an apotiki. Otherwise their question would have no place, since the dispute of Beit Shammai and Beit Hillel concerns a case without an apotiki. At least this explains the Rambam's reason for placing the burden of proof on the heirs. The Sema explains that they say, "Here the enhancement is found, and here it arose," but that is contrary to the Gemara's stated reason that land stands ready for collection. According to Tosafot in Sotah, however, the point is that even Beit Hillel agree in this limited setting that the instrument is sufficiently strong to give the creditor a possessory status, because no one disputes the validity of the instrument itself, only the execution of collection. The creditor is therefore treated as in possession, like the owner of a tree who is in possession and need not pay disputed compensation. The heirs consequently bear the burden of proof. Examine this carefully.
