The statement of Shmuel in the first chapter of Bava Metzia 15a, according to the Gemara's explanation of its reason.
Tur; the Rosh writes likewise there, and Rambam at the beginning of chapter 21 of Hilkhot Malveh. Maggid Mishneh writes there in accordance with Rambam's view in chapter 18 that an unspecified clause does not have the same force as an express one, citing the Geonim and Baal HaTerumot, Shaar 3.
Rif and Rosh explain it this way, and Rambam writes likewise there. Maggid Mishneh writes that this interpretation is compelled.

In the name of Rashba, who writes that this is not the view of the Geonim; Ran and Ramban likewise write this.
Maggid Mishneh explains the reason, which Rambam sets out later in this chapter: it is like one who borrowed, borrowed again, and only afterward acquired property, in which case the creditors divide it. This is explicit at the end of Mi SheMet, Bava Batra 157b, and in the Halachot there.
Tur in the name of Ramban, who writes that leading authorities ruled that the purchaser's position should not be worse than that of one who descends into another's field without permission, whose law is explained in siman 375. This is also Maggid Mishneh's text. He writes that this view is recorded in the Halachot as "some say" and rejected there, but Rambam decided in accordance with it.
Rif writes likewise, as does Baal HaTerumot in Shaar 3, who writes that Rambam likewise rules so according to the text of Rambam before him.
He agreed with the textual versions of Baal HaTerumot and writes that this was the text before him. But in Bedek HaBayit he writes that he later found an exact, corrected version of Rambam matching Maggid Mishneh's text, and that we therefore rule according to Rambam's decision.
Maggid Mishneh writes that this is explicit in the Gemara at the end of Mi SheMet, Bava Batra 157b.
As to why the creditor can collect from the purchaser and the purchaser cannot tell him to go to later properties, Maggid Mishneh explains that the case is where the creditor did not have a future-acquisitions clause and, at the time of the loan, the debtor possessed only properties already encumbered to later claims; those properties therefore never entered the first creditor's security, whereas the property later sold to the purchaser did. Sema explains differently. See there.
From the Mishnah in Gittin 48b, explained in the first chapter of Bava Metzia 14a.
Since the creditor's claim to the appreciation is based on the future-acquisitions clause, any appreciation that arose after the seller's death was never acquired by the seller.
There, law 2. Bava Metzia there states that appreciation is collected but not produce.
Maggid Mishneh writes that Rambam follows the Halachot there; Rabbeinu Chananel explains likewise. Tur, seif 10, and Rosh likewise write so, and this is also the view of Baal HaIttur.
