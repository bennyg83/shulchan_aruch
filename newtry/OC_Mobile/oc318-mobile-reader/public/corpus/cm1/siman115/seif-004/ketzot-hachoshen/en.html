The statement that a creditor does not collect appreciation from heirs does not mean that an after acquired clause is generally ineffective for a ketubah. The Shach derived a novel rule that after acquired property is never subjected to a wife's ketubah or its conditions. This cannot be sustained. The Gemara's discussion of a house falling upon a man and his father expressly uses a wife's ketubah as one of the claims from which the law of after acquired property is analyzed. Likewise, the sugya concerning a man who distributes property among his sons assumes that if he later acquires property his wife can collect from it. Rashi, Rosh, Tur, and the Shulchan Aruch all indicate that an express after acquired clause can benefit a ketubah. The distinction is only appreciation. Appreciation is not ordinary after acquired property because the borrower never owned it. The Sages treated it constructively as though acquired by the seller in order to strengthen ordinary lending and prevent the closing of the door to borrowers. They did not necessarily extend that special constructive enactment to a wife's ketubah. Thus after acquired property itself can be subjected to a ketubah, even against purchasers, while appreciation may be excluded from her collection. This resolves the sources.
