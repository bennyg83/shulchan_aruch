And if it appreciated on its own, etc. The reason is that even if the property had remained in the giver's possession, this appreciation would have arisen there as well. This differs from appreciation produced through expenditures. For this reason, the Rambam and the Mechaber hold that even from a purchaser the creditor collects all appreciation that arose on its own, as stated in seif 11.
And some say that even appreciation arising on its own, etc. The Rosh, who is the authority behind this opinion, likewise holds with respect to a purchaser that appreciation arising on its own has the same law as appreciation resulting from expenditures, as stated in seif 1.
But a purchaser who expressly stipulated, etc. This is not comparable to a case where it was stipulated with the purchaser that the creditor would not collect after acquired property, for the purchaser has no claim to the appreciation in such a case and the creditor collects all of it. Writing that after acquired property is not subject to collection does not imply that appreciation is excluded, because that clause concerns collection of principal, not appreciation. There, even if the creditor cannot collect after acquired property, namely property the borrower acquires and later sells after this purchaser's acquisition, he can still collect from any land or movable property found in the borrower's possession, even the garment on his shoulders, by reason of the purchaser's warranty claim. Thus the purchaser does not necessarily suffer a loss. Here, however, the purchaser expressly stipulated that there would be no warranty at all.
