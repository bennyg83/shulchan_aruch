Rabbeinu Yerucham, however, writes that according to the Rif, appreciation in excess of the expenditure has the same law as appreciation that came of itself, and Ramban writes likewise. See there at length, where the Shach concludes that the primary ruling in this matter appears to him to follow the Rif.
That came of itself. The Shach writes that the primary ruling appears to him to be that the creditor may seize all appreciation that came of itself. See there, where he discusses this at length.
Its law. This is not comparable to a case in which the creditor stipulated with the purchaser that he would not collect property later acquired by the debtor. There the purchaser has no right in the appreciation and the creditor collects all of it. The reason is that even if the purchaser cannot recover from property that the debtor acquired and then sold after the purchaser's acquisition, he can nevertheless recover his warranty from any land or movable property still found in the debtor's possession, even from the garment on his shoulders. Thus the purchaser is not certain to suffer a loss. Here, however, the purchaser stipulated that he would have no warranty claim against the seller at all. Sema.
