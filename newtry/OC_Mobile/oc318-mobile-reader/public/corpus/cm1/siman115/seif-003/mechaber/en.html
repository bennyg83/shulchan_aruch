All the preceding rules apply only to a purchaser. If a gift recipient improved the property through expenditures, a creditor collects none of the appreciation. Rather, the property's value at the time of the gift is determined, and the creditor collects that amount. If the property appreciated by itself, the creditor collects all the appreciation. {Rama: Some say the creditor does not seize even appreciation that arose by itself, unless the property was made a designated lien, in which case it has the same law as property held by orphans, as will be explained shortly. If the donor accepted responsibility for the gift, the creditor collects from it in the same manner as from a purchaser. If a purchaser expressly stipulated that he would have no right of recourse against the seller, he has the law of a gift recipient.}
