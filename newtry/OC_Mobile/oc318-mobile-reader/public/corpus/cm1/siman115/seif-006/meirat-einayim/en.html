And some say that this applies specifically when the debt was not, etc., and he gives him none of the expenditure. Rama's words here are exceedingly difficult, because the Tur states explicitly that even in this case the creditor gives the heirs the amount of their expenditure. Tosafot in the first chapter of Bava Metzia and the Rosh there likewise state explicitly that the heirs must always be given either their expenditure or the amount of the appreciation, whichever is less, even where the debt owed by their father equals the land together with its appreciation and even where the land was made an apotiki. Only where the creditor collects from a purchaser did they write that he takes all the appreciation without reimbursing the expenditure, because the purchaser can recover from the seller, unlike heirs, as I explained in the Derishah. Although the Beit Yosef writes at the end of the siman that the Rif appears to hold that if the debt equals the land and its appreciation the creditor collects everything and gives the heirs nothing, careful examination of the Rif shows otherwise. Indeed, immediately before this the Beit Yosef himself cites the contrary view, that the heirs must receive their expenditure, and writes that this is also evident from the Rif. Some have attempted to resolve the difficulty by saying that a printing error occurred and that Rama's gloss belongs at the end of seif 2, where the Tur speaks of collection from a purchaser and states that if the land was made an apotiki the creditor collects all the appreciation but reimburses the purchaser's expenditure. According to that proposal, Rama's gloss would teach that this is only where the debt did not equal the land and its appreciation. This does not appear correct, because there is no dispute in the law concerning a purchaser, and the Tur states it there without qualification. Moreover, this still would not resolve the language here, for once Rama wrote regarding a purchaser that in such a case no expenditure is reimbursed, he should have written here that in the corresponding case involving heirs their expenditure is reimbursed. This requires study.
