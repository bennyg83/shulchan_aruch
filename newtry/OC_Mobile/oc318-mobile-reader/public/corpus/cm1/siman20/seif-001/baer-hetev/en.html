And he may overturn it. The Sema writes that the Rosh disagrees. Since the son had already become an adult after his father's death, his law is like that of any other adult, and he presumably investigated before coming to court; he therefore should not have said, "I have no further proof." Beit Yosef writes, however, that this is insufficient to reject the view of Rambam and Ramah, because it may be that since he did not know at the time of his father's death, he no longer knows which persons are aware of his defense and therefore has no one from whom to investigate. This is the Mechaber's view, for he rules without qualification in accordance with them.
And knew. Mordechai raises a doubt where witnesses say that the evidence was in his possession, but they do not know whether he knew of it: do we say that he ought to have known, or not? Darkei Moshe concludes that where he had already reached adulthood at the time of the litigation, all agree that we say he ought to have known. Thus, when the Rama writes in the gloss "and knew," his meaning is that we presume that he knew. Sema.
