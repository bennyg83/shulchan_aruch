Until When Evidence May Be Brought to Overturn a Judgment. One seif. If a person was found liable by a beit din and then brings witnesses or evidence establishing his right, the judgment is overturned and reconsidered, even if it was already finalized and even if he already paid. Whenever he brings evidence, the judgment is overturned. If the judges told him, 'Bring all your evidence within thirty days,' evidence brought after thirty days nevertheless overturns the judgment, for what could he have done if he did not find it within thirty days but found it later? If they asked him to bring witnesses or evidence and he said, 'I have none,' evidence later found has no effect. This is certainly so if they separately asked whether he had witnesses and evidence, he denied both, they found him liable, and only after seeing that he lost he said, 'Bring so-and-so to testify for me,' or produced evidence from his money belt; it has no effect and is disregarded. {Rama: If he did not say, 'I have no evidence,' then even if he remained silent until he was found liable and afterward asked that specified witnesses testify, the judgment is overturned (Responsa of the Rosh, klal 26 and klal 13, siman 19).} The foregoing applies when the evidence was accessible to him and the witnesses were in the same country. If he said that he had no witnesses or evidence, but witnesses later arrived from overseas, or his father's portfolio containing documents had been deposited with others and the custodian produced his proofs, he may bring them and overturn the judgment. {Rama: Some say the same applies to his own documents (Tur).} He can explain that when he said he had no witnesses or evidence, he meant that they were not then available to him. {Rama: This applies specifically where witnesses establish that the documents were among those deposited (Mordechai, end of chapter Zeh Borer).} Whenever he can give a substantial explanation for why he said he had no witnesses or evidence, he has not contradicted his original claim and may overturn the judgment. If he explicitly said, 'I have no witnesses anywhere, neither here nor overseas, and no evidence at all, neither in my possession nor in anyone else's,' he cannot overturn it. This applies to an adult. An heir who was a minor when the decedent died, and who after reaching adulthood was sued on account of the decedent and said that he had no witnesses or evidence, may overturn the judgment if others later tell him that they know testimony for his father or that the decedent deposited a proof, because a minor heir does not know all of the decedent's proofs. {Rama: All this applies ordinarily. If an adult later brings proof and witnesses that he did not know of the witnesses he now presents, or witnesses testify that a minor knew, when he first litigated, that his father's documents were in his possession, the ruling follows that evidence (Mordechai, end of chapter Zeh Borer). See also siman 50.}
