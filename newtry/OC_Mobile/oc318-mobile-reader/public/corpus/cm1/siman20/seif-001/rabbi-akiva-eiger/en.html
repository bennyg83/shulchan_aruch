[Shulchan Aruch] Even though he found them afterward. Note: The Bach writes that this implies that even if he found the evidence before the final judgment, it is of no effect, because we are concerned that he may have forged it. The Mordechai likewise writes that he received this ruling from Rabbeinu Yakar; see there. However, I saw in a handwritten marginal gloss of Rabbi Yaakov Weil to the Tur that he wrote in the name of the Mordechai: "If he said it before the final judgment, he has said nothing." Rabbeinu Yerucham writes upon this: "It appears to me that so long as the judgment has not been completed, if he said, 'I have no evidence,' it is of no effect and is as though he said nothing; he may still bring evidence. This is the practical ruling, and the plain sense of the Gemara proceeds this way." See Chiddushei Anshei Shem on the Mordechai.
