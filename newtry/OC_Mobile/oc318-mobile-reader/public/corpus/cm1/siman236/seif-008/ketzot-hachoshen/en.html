We appraise how much he would have been willing to pay. Rosh in chapter HaKones cites Rabbeinu Gershom's ruling that this does not resemble one who merely drives a lion away from another's property. Even though the purchaser acted on his own initiative, once the house had left Jewish possession and would not ordinarily return to a Jew without payment, the person who spent money and restored it to Jewish possession is like one who rescues property from a lion and is entitled to the value of the benefit he conferred. He is not reimbursed everything he spent, as in the case of a shepherd who precedes danger by hiring other shepherds and using sticks, because there the shepherd acted for the flock owner's benefit. Here the purchaser acquired the property for his own needs. Therefore he receives only the value of the benefit conferred. Responsa Rosh, klal 95, records a question seeking clarification of Rabbeinu Gershom's ruling regarding a violent person who seized a Jew's house and another Jew purchased it from him, with the original owner required to reimburse the purchaser for the benefit. The question asks whether 'the benefit' means the purchaser's actual expenditure to recover the house, even if he spent more than the ordinary purchase value because the violent person was his friend and gave him favorable terms. Rosh answers that the benefit extends up to the property's full value, if he spent that much, just as a shepherd may spend up to the full value on shepherds and sticks because of the value of preserving the animal. This response sounds as though the owner must reimburse everything spent, like the shepherd case, rather than only the objective benefit, and it therefore requires study. For practical law, however, we follow Rosh's rulings in his halachic decisions, which expressly say this is not governed by the shepherd rule. See what I wrote in siman 356, seif katan 4. If, however, the purchaser acted from the outset for the original owner's benefit rather than for himself, then even according to Rosh's halachic decision he has the law of the shepherd and may recover his full reasonable expenditure.
