He need not return it without payment. This excludes Rashi's view that the purchaser must return it without compensation because he merely drove away a lion from his fellow's property. See the Derishah.
Rather, we appraise, etc. In the Perishah I proved that the owner need not reimburse the purchaser every amount the purchaser actually paid the gentile. He must pay only what the owner himself would have needed to pay had he been able to obtain the property more cheaply, and no more. The statement that he pays up to the value of the house applies where the owner could not have obtained it for less. The law does not discuss a purchaser who foolishly paid more than the house's value. Indeed, an oppressor ordinarily sells cheaply, all the more so he does not demand more than market value.
