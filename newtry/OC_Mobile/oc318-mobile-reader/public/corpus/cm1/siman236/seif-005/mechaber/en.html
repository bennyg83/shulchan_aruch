If a person purchased the land from the non-Jew and held it for three years, then sold it to another, the original owner has no claim against the second purchaser. We plead on his behalf that the first purchaser acquired it from the owner and paid him the required third. The same applies if the first purchaser held it only one day and the second held it for three years. Rama: Some say this applies only when the first purchaser was Jewish. If a non-Jew purchased it and sold it to a Jew, the Jew is treated as though he bought directly from the oppressor.
