If the owners have the means to purchase. See Sema, seif katan 1. There is a textual error in his words. The passage beginning "According to this one must say..." through "but this is not so" belongs at the end of the seif katan, next to his discussion of twelve months. His initial suggestion was that if another person already purchased the property, the original owners cannot remove him when they lacked funds. He then asks that if so, seif 3 should distinguish whether the owners had money. He therefore concludes that if the purchase from the sikarikon occurred within twelve months, the owners may remove the purchaser even if they lacked the money at the time of his purchase. Mechaber's phrase, "if they do not have the means to purchase," means that they do not have money now to pay the purchaser. Because the owners have priority to purchase, they can remove the purchaser even though they lacked funds when he bought. Sema accordingly says that after twelve months an outsider may purchase even initially, because these rules depend on one another. Sema further writes that according to Ran and Rama, even after twelve months the owners retain priority if they have the means. His wording suggests that even if another already bought, the owners can remove him, even where the property had remained before the coercive holder for twelve months. This is very difficult. The Mishnah implies that after twelve months whoever first purchases acquires. In my view there is no difference between property that remained with the coercive holder for twelve months and property that remained with the Jewish purchaser from him for twelve months. In both cases the original owners can no longer remove the purchaser. Rivash explains that after twelve months we say that because the owner did not bring a claim, he completed his intention to relinquish. This applies all the more once a Jew bought from the coercive holder and the owner remained silent for twelve months. He has greater fear of the coercive holder than of the Jewish purchaser. If he did not sue the Jew during that period, his silence shows relinquishment. The Mishnah's wording, "if it remained before the sikarikon for twelve months," is therefore stated as the greater novelty. Within twelve months, however, Sema is correct that even if another purchased first, the original owners can remove him. Rivash says so explicitly. Accordingly Rama's words are straightforward. Where the owners say that they had the means to purchase within the twelve months, while the purchaser says that they did not, the purchaser is believed. We do not simply leave the land in the chazakah of the original owner, because the land now stands presumptively in the purchaser's possession, and the one who asserts that something occurred which invalidated that acquisition must prove it. We do not assume an unusual new event without evidence, as Rashba writes concerning a condition involving passive nonperformance.
