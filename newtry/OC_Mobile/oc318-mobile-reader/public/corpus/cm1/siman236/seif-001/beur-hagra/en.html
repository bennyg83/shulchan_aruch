(Supplement) The non-Jew, etc. This entire seif is Rambam's language. It implies that even if the original owners had funds to repurchase, a later purchaser who bought first acquires, since the Mishna says only that the owners have priority. Yet Rambam's later phrase, 'if they lack the funds, he acquires,' appears to imply that if they had the funds they may remove him at any time. The plain language and logic do not support that reading, for their right is no stronger than a neighboring landowner's right. The Yerushalmi and Rabbenu Yerucham indicate that if the owners lacked funds when the purchaser bought, they cannot later remove him. This also follows from the Mishna's enactment, which was intended to encourage purchasers. Rabbi's later enactment added that even if the owners had funds, once twelve months passed the purchaser acquired. Tur, however, does not distinguish whether they had funds at the time of purchase and understands that any purchase within twelve months remains subject to removal, while if the owners leave the land with him they receive one-quarter. This approach is difficult in light of the Yerushalmi and the Mishna's purpose. Rashba interprets the earlier enactment differently, that the purchaser initially had to return the property without payment and the later court required the owners to pay one-quarter. Shulchan Aruch in seif 3 follows Tur. (End of supplement.) Reference 80.
