Land extorted from a Jew by non-Jews through violence or false charges. It contains nine seifim.
When non-Jewish oppressors threaten to kill a Jew until he redeems himself by giving them his field or house, and they then release him, the original owner has first right to purchase the land whenever the oppressor seeks to sell it, if he can afford it. If he cannot, or if the land remained with the oppressor for twelve months, whoever first buys it acquires it, provided he gives the original owner one-quarter of the land or one-third of the purchase money. Since the oppressor sells property that is not his, he generally sells at approximately a one-quarter discount. That discount belongs to the original owner, because the low price results from his ownership. Thus, one who buys from the oppressor for thirty gives the owner ten, or gives him one-quarter of the land, and then acquires the whole. Until he does so, that quarter is held as stolen property. Rama: If the owners claim they could have purchased it and the purchaser denies this, the owners bear the burden of proof, because the land is presumed to be in the purchaser's possession. Some say this rule applies only when the owners voluntarily gave the land to save their lives. If the oppressor seized it forcibly, the land returns to the owners, who reimburse the purchaser only for the benefit they received. Jews who imprison fellow Jews and threaten to kill them until they surrender property are not governed by this rule, because it is presumed that Jews do not shed blood and that such threats were not intended to be carried out.
