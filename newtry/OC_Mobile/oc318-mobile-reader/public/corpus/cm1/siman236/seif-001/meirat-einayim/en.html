If the owners have the means to take it from the oppressor, etc. These words are taken from the Rambam in chapter 10 of Hilchot Gezeilah. At first glance they imply that if another person first purchases the land from the oppressor, the original owners can no longer remove him by paying his price. If so, the later phrase "or the land remained with the oppressor for twelve months" would mean that where the owner presently has the money, another may initially precede him and purchase only after twelve months have passed. But that cannot be correct, because in seif 3 the Mechaber rules without qualification that if a person bought from the gentile within twelve months, the original owner may remove him if he wishes. The Tur says so explicitly. In the Derishah I showed from the Rambam's own wording that the right to purchase first and the right to remove a later purchaser are linked. The Rambam says that once the land remained with the oppressor for twelve months, whoever first buys it acquires. This implies that before twelve months, even if another person already purchased it, he has not acquired against the original owner and can be removed even later. Originally, the owner's priority continued indefinitely. Rabbi Yehudah HaNasi enacted that after twelve months the owner loses the power to displace the purchaser. I later saw that the Mishnah in Gittin states: The later beit din said that one who purchases from a violent confiscator gives the owner one quarter. When does this apply? When the owner does not have the means to buy. But if he has the means, he precedes every person. The phrase is not "to buy from the confiscator" but simply "to buy," implying that even after another has bought, if the owner can now buy the land from that purchaser he has priority and need not settle for a quarter. Rabbi Yehudah HaNasi limited that right to twelve months. This appears to be the Tur's understanding as well.

The owners say, etc. This is from the Yerushalmi, cited by the Ran in the fifth chapter of Gittin, but the other poskim omitted it. In the Derishah I suggested that they omitted it because under the Bavli's framework the claim has little practical place. If the purchaser bought within twelve months, the owner can remove him regardless, so why litigate about whether the owner was ready to buy? If the purchase occurred after the land remained with the oppressor for twelve months, then just as the owner can no longer remove the purchaser, so too the rule is that whoever first buys acquires. The Ran and Rama nevertheless cite the Yerushalmi because they do not regard these two rules as necessarily dependent upon one another. They therefore hear the owner's claim that he had the means to buy but the purchaser pushed him aside, whether within the twelve months or afterward.
