When Jews pressured their fellows. At first glance this is difficult. What difference does it make that it was as though they lacked the power to kill them? The Beit Yosef writes in siman 205 that suffering is more severe than death. Since their fellows pressured them through imprisonment until they were compelled to give, and captivity is harsher than all of them, what difference is there whether they sought to kill them or afflicted them with suffering? It appears that the reason is as follows. By strict law, a sikarikon does not acquire, for this is a case of one who was coerced and gave, as the Rivash writes in siman 290. The Sages merely enacted that purchasers should acquire and give the owners one quarter, so that the field should not remain permanently in the hands of the sikarikon because people would otherwise refrain from purchasing it and the owners themselves would not purchase it out of fear. They therefore held that this enactment applies only to gentiles, whereas people are not so fearful of Jews. Consequently, in the latter case the enactment does not apply.
