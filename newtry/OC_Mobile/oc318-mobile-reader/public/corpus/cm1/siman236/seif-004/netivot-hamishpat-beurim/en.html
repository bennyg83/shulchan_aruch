Or he admitted. See Sema, seif katan 9, who says this means that he still admits it, but if he formerly admitted and now says that he admitted only out of fear, we listen to him, as Tur writes in siman 205. This is very difficult. Siman 205 concerns an admission to the robber himself, or an admission to the purchaser that the purchaser paid money to the robber. There one can say that fear of the robber caused the admission. But if the owner admitted to the purchaser that he himself received from the purchaser the one-fourth payment due to him, there is no reasonable claim that this admission was made out of fear. Indeed, even chazakah can help a purchaser from a robber when he claims that he later purchased the property directly from the dispossessed owner, and we do not automatically say that the owner was afraid to protest, as Sema himself writes in siman 149. Here too Sema says that chazakah helps where the purchaser claims he gave the owner the one-fourth payment. All the more so, an explicit admission that the owner received that payment should be effective. The rule that one who first bought from a sikarikon and then bought from the owner has a void purchase because the owner may have acted from fear will be explained below.
