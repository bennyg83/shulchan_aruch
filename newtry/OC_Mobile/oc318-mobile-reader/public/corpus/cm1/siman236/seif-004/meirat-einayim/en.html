But if he first purchased it from the non-Jew, etc. The owner was compelled to consent to the coercer's sale and also to write him a deed, just as at the outset he had been forced to give him the field completely in order to save his life. But if the Jew accepted responsibility toward the purchaser, he certainly made a complete and genuine sale. The coercer would not have demanded such a responsibility clause, just as when the Jew originally redeemed himself with the field the coercer had not demanded continuing responsibility for title.
Or he admitted. In the Perishah I wrote that in my opinion the text should mean that he continues to admit that he received the money, but says he accepted it only out of fear. We do not listen to him, because he should have delivered a moda'ah. But if he had previously admitted receipt of the money and now says, "I never actually received money; I was compelled by fear to make the earlier admission," we do listen to him, as the Tur and Mechaber write above in siman 205.
