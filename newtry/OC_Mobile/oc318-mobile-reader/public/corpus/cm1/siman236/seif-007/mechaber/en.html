If a powerful non-Jew forcibly takes property from a Jew and enters the Jew's field because the owner owed him a debt, or because the Jew caused him a loss or damaged his property, and after taking control of the field the non-Jew sells it to another Jew, the original owner cannot remove the purchaser. This applies when the owners admit that the non-Jew's claim was true. Likewise, if there was a king or official in that place who could have compelled the non-Jew seller to submit to judgment, but the owners did not bring a claim against him, they cannot remove the Jewish purchaser from the non-Jew even if they do not admit the claim and even if there are no witnesses that the non-Jew's claim was true. The purchaser may say to the owners, "If the non-Jew was a robber, why did you not sue him under his law?"
