That he suffered damage, etc. This is how Ramban explains the term anparot mentioned in the Gemara and baraita.
The owners cannot remove it. This is Rambam's view. The Rosh, in the name of Rabban Gamliel, and the Tur write that the owner can remove it from the purchaser even if it remained in the non-Jew's possession for more than twelve months, provided the owner reimburses the purchaser what the purchaser paid on his behalf. Specifically in the case of sikarikon and coercers discussed above, where the Jew is under threat of death and redeemed his life by voluntarily giving the field so the coercer would leave him alone, did the Sages say that after twelve months the purchaser acquires. Here, even if it is true that the Jew owed the non-Jew money, the non-Jew is entitled only to the amount of the debt, and the owner now offers that amount to the purchaser. Their reasoning is compelling, and they are later authorities. I do not know why Rama omitted their view.
Where the owners admitted this. Since there is no fear of a coercer threatening to kill him, the reading "where the owners admitted" is appropriate and need not be emended to the singular form, unlike the case above in seif 4.
