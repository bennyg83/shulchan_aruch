The Rosh, however, in the name of Rabbeinu Gershom, and the Tur, write that the original owner may recover it from the buyer even if it remained in the non-Jew's possession for more than twelve months, provided he reimburses the buyer for what the buyer paid on his behalf. The twelve-month rule applies specifically to a sikrikon or oppressive official, where the Jew was in danger of being killed and voluntarily surrendered the field to save his life. In such a case, after twelve months the purchaser acquires it. Here, by contrast, although it is true that the Jew owed the non-Jew money, the non-Jew had no right beyond the amount of the debt, and the owner now comes to repay that amount to the purchaser. These words are persuasive, and they are from later authorities. I do not know why the Rama did not cite them. This is the Sema's ruling. The Shach writes that Maharshal, chapter 6, siman 14, rules likewise. See Responsa Mabit, part 2, siman 44, and his second collection, siman 128, page 158; and Responsa Shav Yaakov, Choshen Mishpat, siman 16.
