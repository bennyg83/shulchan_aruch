Anything. Although this non-Jew too presumably obtained the field cheaply, because it came into his hands indirectly and without payment, nevertheless once the Jewish owner despaired of recovering it he retained no right in it. This is unlike a violent possessor or a sikarikon, where there is no proof of despair because fear for his life forced the owner to remain silent. Therefore the Sages enacted that the buyer must give the original owner one third, as stated in seif 1. This is clear, contrary to Ir Shushan. Sema.
Under compulsion. See Yam Shel Shlomo regarding these laws.
According to law. Meaning, since the Jew did not absolutely transfer the land to the non-Jew upon failure to pay by the fixed time, but merely gave him authority to sell it, under our law such a person has only the status of an agent. This remains so even if the arrangement was phrased in an effective manner that avoided asmachta, as stated in siman 73. Even if one argues that their law treats it otherwise, the Beit Yosef concludes in siman 150 in the name of the Tashbetz that once such a sale is invalid under our law, even if valid under theirs, we annul it. He brings proof for this. Sema.
