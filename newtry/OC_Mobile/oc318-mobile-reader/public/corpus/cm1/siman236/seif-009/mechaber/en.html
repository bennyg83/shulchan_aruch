The foregoing applies where there are no judges through whom the owner could recover his field from the non-Jew. If there are functioning courts in the land and he could have recovered it by legal action but failed to do so, he is presumed certainly to have despaired of recovery, and the property remains with the purchaser, who owes the original owner nothing. Rama: All this concerns a coercive individual who had no lawful claim to the property. But if a prince or ruler became angry with his servants and officials and confiscated one of their houses under the law of the realm, dina d'malchusa dina applies, and the purchaser owes the former owner nothing. Mordechai, chapters HaUmanin and HaKones. If a Jew mortgaged land to a non-Jew and fixed a deadline for redemption, failed to redeem it, and the non-Jew sold it to another, then if he sold it at fair value the sale stands because it was lawfully sold. If he sold it for less than its value, even by a small amount, the sale is reversible. Beit Yosef, end of siman 150. See below, siman 371, for related laws.
