Wherever they said that a guardian is appointed for them, etc. The Tur in seif 18 cites the Ramban: In every matter where we resort to the orphans' property, whether a specified one hundred or field, or an unspecified one hundred or field, testimony is received in the guardian's presence. The Mechaber writes generally, "wherever they said," and these cases are included. It follows that his concluding statement, "some say that testimony is not received," likewise applies to both specified and unspecified fields or sums, each opinion according to its own position, as I explained with God's help. Although the Mechaber earlier in seif 4 stated the rule without recording a dispute, there he simply followed the Rambam's language in sequence, as explained above. The Tur, following the Rosh, also wrote above concerning specified and unspecified fields and sums that the father's handwriting must be available, meaning handwriting recognized by beit din. Rama's gloss, that some explain the reason as applying to a clear matter free of doubt, such as a will, refers to everything included by the Mechaber's phrase "wherever they said." A specified or unspecified field or sum is included among testamentary matters, as the Rambam and Mechaber wrote in seif 4, "Minors whose predecessor commanded and said, Give a field..." The other cases likewise involve matters that appear clear and that reason indicates remain owing. A guardian is appointed only in testamentary matters of the type described, or in one of the three previously mentioned circumstances where reason indicates continuing liability, or where interest is accruing, for a woman's ketubah, or upon evidence of theft, each of which has its own reason as explained above. Nevertheless, the Rosh and those who follow him hold that even though testamentary matters are clear, testimony is still not received, nor are signatures on a document used to remove property from the orphans authenticated, until they mature, for the reasons explained above. But where beit din or a guardian borrowed for the needs of the orphans, the Rosh agrees that testimony is received before the guardian while they are minors, for the reasons already stated. See the Rosh near the end of chapter Almanah Nizonet, where he discusses this at length, and the Tur's concise presentation there.
Or the witnesses wrote, etc. The Mechaber's conclusion, "or handwriting recognized by beit din," applies specifically according to the Rosh. According to the Rambam, the judges themselves need not recognize the father's handwriting, since it can be authenticated through others. It appears to me that this also refers back to the preceding case of witnesses who signed the will during the father's lifetime. Their signatures too must be recognized by beit din, so that judgment is rendered on the basis of the judges' own recognition.
