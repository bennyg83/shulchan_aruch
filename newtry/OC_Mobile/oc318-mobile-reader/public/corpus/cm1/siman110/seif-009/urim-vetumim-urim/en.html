Likewise, witnesses to a testament, etc. Ramban explains that a testament is a matter belonging to the minors themselves. Just as when a guardian borrows for the minors' needs testimony may be received because it directly concerns their interests, so too testimony concerning a testament may be received.
Recognizable to beit din. See Tumim. The Mechaber holds that a formal note signed by witnesses may be authenticated outside the opposing party's presence, even against minor heirs. A mere handwritten obligation, however, is not authenticated against them, and therefore he requires the handwriting to be independently recognizable to beit din. See Rashba's novellae to Bava Kamma. Rashba holds that according to the opinions permitting testimony outside the opposing party's presence when the claimant or witnesses are ill, the same should apply to minors. This requires study because we do not find other authorities who adopt it. Perhaps even Rashba said this only in cases where we are in any event permitted to proceed against minor heirs but ordinarily appoint a guardian. In an urgent circumstance we need not wait for the guardian's appointment. That is a sound practical distinction. But in cases where the Sages ruled that testimony is not accepted against minors at all, it may not be accepted under any circumstances. See what I wrote above in siman 28.
