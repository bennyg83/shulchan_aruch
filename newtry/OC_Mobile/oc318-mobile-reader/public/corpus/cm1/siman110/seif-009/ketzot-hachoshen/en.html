The Shulchan Aruch says that the father's own handwriting must be recognizable to beit din. Sema explains that according to Rosh this requirement applies both to the father's handwriting and to witnesses who signed a will during his lifetime. According to Rambam, other witnesses can validate the signatures. The Shach rules elsewhere that a debtor's own handwriting requires Torah level validation and therefore cannot be validated outside the litigant's presence. That gives a simple reason why the Shulchan Aruch says it must be handwriting recognizable directly to beit din when the heirs are minors. But as I explained in siman 46, there is no sound basis to distinguish categorically between ordinary document signatures and the debtor's handwriting for this purpose. Sema's reading is therefore preferable: the clause follows Rosh's stricter view and applies likewise to the preceding case of witnesses on the will.
