Wherever they said... As stated in Bava Kamma, testimony is given in the presence of the guardian. Even according to the view that payment is initially made from the guardian's superior property, the guardian later recovers from the orphans when they grow up, without requiring new testimony. Ramban writes this according to his approach that where a loan was taken for the orphans' own needs, beit din may proceed even if the orphans themselves borrowed rather than the guardian. He does not accept Tosafos' explanation that the obstacle is merely inability to accept testimony against minors. Rather, the plain Gemara teaches that one generally does not proceed against a minor's property at all. Since liability here arose automatically, concerns analogous to prior payment or a receipt may exist, such as witnesses later proving proper guarding or that the ox never became forewarned. Damage is different because the Sages specifically allowed appointment of a guardian and collection according to each view.
Likewise witnesses to a will. This follows from the statement in Erchin that in all cases where one proceeds, a guardian is appointed. Whether the decedent specified this field, this maneh, or spoke generally, testimony must therefore be heard before the guardian.
And some say the reason... Nimukei Yosef explains the case of "this maneh" accordingly.
The same applies to any matter... Gittin 40a, in the case of a slave where a guardian is appointed for the minor because once the minor grows up he would certainly be compelled to free the slave. Nimukei Yosef accordingly writes that where a debt document contains interest, beit din proceeds and accepts testimony even outside the litigant's presence. Although documents can generally be validated outside a litigant's presence, with a minor one ordinarily waits because even his father could have requested time to challenge the document. The Rosh's responsum cited by the Tur similarly requires an already validated document. Nimukei Yosef's answer is that a guardian is appointed and testimony is then heard in the guardian's presence.
And there is one who wrote... and how can you find... Meaning, how is the Erchin rule concerning proceedings against minors and an interest-bearing document to be understood? If the document is already validated, why is a guardian needed? The Raavad follows Rambam that even a minor's document may be validated, and therefore explains that the special case is where witnesses wrote and signed the relevant act. Baal HaTerumos and Rashba cite this view. Tosafos in Bava Kamma likewise writes that even with a validated document the concern for a receipt or prior payment remains. Ran in Kesubos says similarly. The proof from an ox that gored may be read the other way, because the Gemara's challenge from the general rule against proceeding against minors indicates that the guardian procedure is exceptional. The Rosh therefore writes that regarding witnesses to a will the Raavad's approach is preferable, consistent with Tosafos' understanding of the goring-ox case.
