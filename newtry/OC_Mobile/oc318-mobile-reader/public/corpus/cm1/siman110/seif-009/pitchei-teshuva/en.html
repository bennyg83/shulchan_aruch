And likewise witnesses to a will, etc. See Responsa Knesset Yechezkel, siman 88, who writes regarding the will about which he was asked: all of the preceding discussion applies if the orphans are adults, against whom testimony can be accepted. Since here they are minors, however, the matter is different. Although the Ramban and Rashba hold that testimony concerning a will may be accepted against minors, two reasons are given for this. One is to prevent the dying person's mind from becoming distressed. The second, cited by the Rama, is that testimony may be accepted in a clear matter such as a will.
According to the first reason, one can say that this applies only to a will made with a formal kinyan, where the gift takes effect on the Torah level and the Sages merely enacted acceptance of testimony against minors to prevent distress. A will without kinyan, however, takes effect only by rabbinic enactment, and we do not make one rabbinic enactment in order to support another. Responsa Maharam Padua, siman 54, writes something similar. According to the second reason as well, perhaps it applies only where the dying person died immediately after giving the instruction, making the matter clear. If his death was delayed for two days, perhaps he retracted in the interim, and when the orphans become adults they may bring witnesses to the retraction. Beyond this, Knesset Yechezkel writes that he doubts there is anyone in that generation broad enough in judgment to extract property from minor orphans against the views of the Ra'avad, Rosh, Ri, and Baal HaTerumot, which form the second view cited in the Shulchan Aruch here. All the more so because many responsa rule uniformly that where there are two views in such a matter, money is not extracted from minors. These are his words. See there. Recognizable to beit din.
