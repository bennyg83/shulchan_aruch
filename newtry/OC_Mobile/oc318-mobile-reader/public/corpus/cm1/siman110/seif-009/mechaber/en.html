Whenever the Sages said a guardian is appointed for minor heirs so that proceedings may be brought against their property, testimony is accepted in the guardian's presence, because the guardian is a litigant. Testimony concerning a will is likewise accepted in the guardian's presence. {Rama: Some say the reason is that, in a clear matter free of concern, such as a will, testimony may be accepted even outside a litigant's presence. Therefore, testimony concerning a will may be accepted in the minor's presence. The same applies to every clearly established obligation of a minor.} Some have written that testimony may not be accepted in the guardian's presence. How, then, can a person who leaves minor orphans make an effective will? He may deliver his instructions to beit din; the witnesses may write the will as a document and sign it during his lifetime; or he may write it himself in handwriting recognized by beit din. See above, siman 108, se'ifim 3 and 4, and note 45.
