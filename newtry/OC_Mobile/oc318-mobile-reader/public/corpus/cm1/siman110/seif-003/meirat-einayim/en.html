We resort to the property of a guarantor, etc. The Tur cites the Rambam: We resort to the property of a guarantor even though the borrower's heirs are minors. We do not say that ultimately the guarantor must turn to the orphans and thus we are indirectly resorting to the property of minors, since the guarantor cannot collect from them until they become adults. Their exemption exists only while they are minors, whereas once they mature they will become liable. They are therefore persons subject to liability, except that there is presently an impediment resting upon them. This is comparable to a lender who sued the borrower, obtained judgment, but the borrower had nothing with which to pay, in which case the lender collects from the guarantor. The Tur concludes, however, that the Rosh holds this only with respect to an unconditional guarantor, but not an ordinary guarantor, who is no stronger than the debtor's property itself, which is also deemed his guarantor, yet cannot be collected from while the heirs are minors. The Mechaber follows the Rambam. The Maggid Mishneh in chapter 27 of Hilchot Malveh VeLoveh likewise decides, based on the majority of opinions, in accordance with the Rambam, and Darkei Moshe cites him, although the Beit Yosef does not appear to agree.
