Seif 9: Or he himself wrote his will in handwriting recognized by beit din. Note: I find it difficult to understand why the judges’ own recognition should help, since a witness cannot become a judge in the same matter. One cannot answer that direct sight is no worse than hearing testimony. According to the Sema in siman 46, who explains that judges’ recognition is effective because “hearing should not be greater than seeing,” the difficulty here is especially clear. Since testimony from others cannot be received to authenticate signatures against minor heirs, why should the judges’ own recognition be effective, when even ordinary testimonial hearing cannot operate in this case? Even according to the Levush’s explanation, cited by the Sema in siman 46, that the Sages were lenient because authentication of documents is rabbinic, the difficulty remains according to the Rosh and those who follow him. They hold that even authentication may not be performed against minor heirs, meaning that the Sages did not apply the ordinary leniency of document authentication in that setting. Why, then, should the judges’ own recognition permit them to function simultaneously as witnesses and judges? This presently requires study.
