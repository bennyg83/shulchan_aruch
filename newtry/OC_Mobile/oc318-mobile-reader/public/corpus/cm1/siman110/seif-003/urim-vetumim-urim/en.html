We proceed against the guarantor's property, etc. We do not say that the guarantor follows the status of the minor heirs and therefore need not pay until they are obligated. Above in siman 108 seif 11 the heirs are entirely exempt, and therefore the guarantor likewise need not pay. Here, however, the heirs will have to pay once they reach majority. Therefore the guarantor pays immediately, and the heirs need not reimburse him until they become adults. One can question this: the reason we wait with minors is that when they grow they may discover proof or witnesses against the debt. How, then, can we force the guarantor to pay now, when the heirs may later find a receipt and the guarantor will lose what he paid? One must say that once payment was made during the heirs' minority, they themselves would not later reopen the matter merely to search for a receipt. But if the guarantor paid and the heirs later actually find a valid receipt, he may return against the creditor and recover what was improperly collected. We do not delay merely from concern that the guarantor may temporarily lose use of his money.
