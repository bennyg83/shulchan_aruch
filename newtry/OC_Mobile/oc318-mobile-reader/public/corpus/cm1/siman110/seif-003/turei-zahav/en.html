The Tur cites a responsum of the Rosh: if the note has been authenticated, the creditor is placed in possession of the estate; if it has not been authenticated, he is not, because a document is not authenticated in the absence of the opposing party. Many have wondered that Rava states in HaGozel Batra, page 112, that a document may be authenticated in the opposing party's absence, and they labored to reconcile the Rosh. It appears to me as follows. The reason authentication may ordinarily be performed in the opposing party's absence is that by Torah law no authentication is required at all, since witnesses signed on a document are treated as though their testimony had already been examined in beit din. The Sages alone required authentication, and therefore it may be done even in the opposing party's absence, as Tosafot write there. Here, however, the opposing party is a minor. Even if the two signatories came and orally gave the same testimony against the minor, we would not accept it. Their written testimony should not have greater force. Therefore the note is not authenticated. This is a sound explanation of the Rosh's responsum. Yet in the chapter Almanah Nizonet the Rosh writes expressly that when claimants come through the minors' father, a document may be accepted, for the Gemara teaches that a document may be authenticated in the opposing party's absence; alternatively, even an authenticated note may not be enforced because of a concern that the father left funds for payment. Thus his rulings plainly contradict the responsum. Beit Yosef cites Rabbi Yehudah, son of the Rosh, that when the Rosh's rulings contradict a responsum, one follows the rulings because they are later. Beit Yosef wonders how this makes the Rosh err in a simple matter. There is no difficulty. The responsum has a valid rationale, as I explained: the principle that signed witnesses are treated as though examined in beit din does not readily apply where testimony against a minor could not be examined now. In his rulings, however, the Rosh understands the phrase to mean that the testimony is regarded as having already been examined before the present court, so no new reception of testimony is needed. With God's help, this explanation is clearer than all the other reconciliations I have seen.
