Tur, seif 15, and in siman 108, seif 6. There it was established that where it is known that particular movables came from a specified business transaction, the creditor collects even from minor orphans. Here he adds that the same applies if the deceased acknowledged this in his handwriting.
There, concerning the baraita taught by Rabbi Oshaya, "One who steals and feeds the stolen property to his children," etc. The Gemara establishes it according to Rav Chisda as speaking after despair. Rif and Rosh write that now that movable property is liened to a creditor, the heirs are obligated to pay, since the obligation is like an oral loan, and we now rule that an oral loan is collected from heirs from either land or movable property. Beit Yosef, Shaar 14, part 1, writes that we rule like the Rabbis who dispute Sumchus in HaGozel, Bava Kamma 112a, and say that both adult and minor heirs are liable, because in a theft case testimony is accepted even outside the presence of the opposing litigant.
