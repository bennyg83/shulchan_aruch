In handwriting. According to most authorities other than Rosh, a handwritten obligation may be authenticated even after the father's death. See Sema. See what I write below concerning handwriting, because the matter contains uncertainty.
Likewise, if their ancestor stole, etc. According to Rambam, Tosafos, and most authorities, testimony concerning the theft may be received even after the father's death. According to Rosh and Tur, the testimony must have been received while the father was alive, or he must have acknowledged it in writing, or the theft must be publicly known and established to the point that it is equivalent to already accepted testimony. See Tumim, where I discussed at length how to reconcile Rosh. According to Rambam, a guardian should be appointed, for there is no difference between stolen land and a stolen movable article. According to the dissenting authorities, no guardian is required. This is simple.
Whether they despaired. We rule that an heir's domain is not like a purchaser's domain. Therefore there is no combination of the owner's despair with a transfer to a new domain. The rule that the creditor may collect from inherited movable property applies after the Geonic enactment. Before that enactment, collection required land specifically.
