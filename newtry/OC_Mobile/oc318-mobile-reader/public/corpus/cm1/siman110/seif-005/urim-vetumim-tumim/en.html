And likewise a minor, etc. To clarify this law, concerning which the Sema writes at some length, it is necessary to set out the Gemara in Bava Kamma. In the final chapter of HaGozel, the son in law's son of Rabbi Yirmiyah, who was a minor, locked the door against Rabbi Yirmiyah. Rabbi Yirmiyah sought to bring witnesses that he had possessed the property during the lifetime of his father in law. Rabbi Avin said that testimony is not accepted outside the presence of the litigant. The Gemara asks from Rabbi Oshaya's ruling that if a minor forcibly enters another person's field with his servants, we do not say that we should wait until he becomes an adult, but remove him immediately. The Gemara answers that in that case the minor had no ancestral chazakah, whereas in Rabbi Yirmiyah's case the minor did have ancestral chazakah. The Hagahot Mordekhai in the final chapter of HaGozel records the view of the Ri that even if Rabbi Yirmiyah had been inside the house, he would have been removed, just as in the ruling concerning the minor who forcibly entered the field, where the Gemara distinguishes only on the basis of ancestral chazakah and not on the basis that the owner of the field there was presently in possession. This demonstrates that in Rabbi Yirmiyah's case one must wait until the minor becomes an adult even if Rabbi Yirmiyah had previously been in possession of the house. The Riva, however, explains that the case was specifically that the minor locked the door and himself took possession, so that Rabbi Yirmiyah was no longer able to remove him except by proving his chazakah through witnesses, and testimony is not accepted outside the presence of the litigant. If Rabbi Yirmiyah had still been dwelling in the house and the minor were demanding that he leave, beit din would not remove Rabbi Yirmiyah. Rabbenu Ephraim likewise rules. The Sema appears to understand that the dispute does not concern a case where Rabbi Yirmiyah was already in possession before the door was locked. According to him, everyone agrees that once the minor has locked the door, testimony is not accepted outside his presence, as the Gemara did not answer by saying that Rabbi Yirmiyah had been in possession. Rather, according to the Ri, even if the minor had never locked the door and Rabbi Yirmiyah was still sitting in the house, since the minor possessed ancestral chazakah, beit din would remove Rabbi Yirmiyah and require him to wait until the orphan became an adult. According to the Riva and Rabbenu Ephraim, however, only once the minor had already locked the door would beit din refrain from ordering him to open it and allow Rabbi Yirmiyah to enter. If Rabbi Yirmiyah were still sitting inside, beit din would not remove him and would accept his testimony. The same may be the meaning of the Hagahot HaRosh in the final chapter of HaGozel, which states that Rabbenu Tam emended the case to say that Rabbi Yirmiyah was not in possession of the house, because if he had been in possession he would not have been removed, as is evident from the incident of Rabbah bar Rav Sharshom in Bava Batra, where a claim that the property was purchased would have been effective even though witnesses would have to be brought. It is possible that Rabbenu Tam too means that although Rabbi Yirmiyah had previously been in possession, at the time in question the minor had locked the door, and he follows the Riva that the decisive point is that Rabbi Yirmiyah was no longer presently in possession. Likewise, Tosafot in Bava Kamma, s.v. aval, who write that the passage implies that even if Rabbi Yirmiyah had been inside the house, may, according to the Sema, mean that he had been inside before the minor locked the door. Despite his previous possession, testimony could not then be accepted because the minor had ancestral chazakah. They could nevertheless agree with the Riva and Rabbenu Tam that if Rabbi Yirmiyah were still inside at the time of litigation he would not be removed. There is no proof at all from Tosafot in Eruvin. The Shach's statement that Tosafot there supports the Sema is incomprehensible to me, because they mention only the distinction between a minor who has ancestral chazakah and one who does not. The Sema and Bach therefore rule like the Riva. If the minor has ancestral chazakah and has already taken control so that the claimant is no longer in the house, then even if the claimant had previously possessed it, testimony is not accepted until the minor becomes an adult. These are the Rama's words upon the Rambam and Mechaber, who state without qualification that where a minor forcibly enters another's field he is removed. The Rama adds that if the minor has ancestral chazakah and has already taken control, we do not remove him. Rabbenu Yerucham, Netiv 26, part 3, likewise appears to distinguish between a case where the minor has removed the claimant and one where the claimant is still sitting in the property. Yet this explanation is difficult. If the Ri and Riva agree that prior possession by Rabbi Yirmiyah does not help once the minor has ancestral chazakah, and they differ only whether the minor must specifically have locked the door or whether Rabbi Yirmiyah is removed even while still sitting in the field, what proof does the Ri bring from the fact that the Gemara does not answer the case of the minor who forcibly entered by saying that the owner there was in possession? In the forcible entry case everyone agrees that prior possession does not help once the minor has taken over. The locking of the door would have no independent legal significance; it would merely describe what happened. The Ri's proof therefore would not address the point of dispute. It therefore appears more correct, as I wrote from the Maharshal in Yam Shel Shlomo to Bava Kamma, that according to all opinions the act of locking the door itself adds nothing. Is the world ownerless, so that one may forcibly expel another from possession and thereby weaken the possessor's legal standing? Such a rule would encourage violent people. Rather, the dispute concerns Rashi's explanation. Rashi writes that the minor locked the door "so that Rabbi Yirmiyah should not take possession of it." This implies that Rabbi Yirmiyah was not yet in possession of the house at all, but merely had witnesses that he had acquired it from the minor's father. The Ri disagrees with Rashi and says that even if Rabbi Yirmiyah had been in possession, he would nevertheless be removed, because otherwise the Gemara could have answered the case of the minor who forcibly entered by saying that there the owner was already in possession. The fact that it instead distinguishes ancestral chazakah proves that current possession is not decisive where the minor has ancestral chazakah. The Riva disagrees with the Ri and says that the case was specifically "he locked the door," meaning precisely as Rashi explains, that the minor prevented Rabbi Yirmiyah from ever taking possession. If Rabbi Yirmiyah had already been in possession, he would not have been removed. The act of locking the door does not itself change legal rights, as the Yam Shel Shlomo explains. Rabbenu Tam likewise says that Rabbi Yirmiyah was not in possession of the house, following Rashi's reading, and brings proof from Rabbah bar Rav Sharshom, as stated at length above. According to this, Tosafot in Bava Kamma, who write that the passage implies that Rabbi Yirmiyah was removed even if he had been in possession, follow the Ri, not the Riva and Rabbenu Tam. This also appears from Piskei Tosafot, which state: "One may not establish possession in the property of a minor who has ancestral chazakah, even where the opposing party is in possession." Their explicit phrase "even where the opposing party is in possession" shows that they mean actual continuing possession, not merely previous possession followed by expulsion. Thus they do not follow the Sema's distinction based on the minor's locking of the door, because that act has no legal significance. The Ramban in Bava Batra likewise supports this. Regarding Rabbah bar Rav Sharshom he explains the statement "you cannot claim that you purchased it, because a public report went out concerning it" by invoking the rule that testimony is not accepted outside the presence of the litigant, as in Rabbi Yirmiyah's case. But Rabbah bar Rav Sharshom was presently in possession. If Rabbi Yirmiyah's case depended on the minor having locked the door and excluded him, there would be no comparison. It follows that the locking of the door is immaterial, as I have written, for force cannot create a legal advantage. The Beit Yosef also appears to understand matters this way. He writes that Rashi's statement that Rabbi Yirmiyah sought to take possession accords with the Riva. Thus he did not understand the Riva as agreeing that Rabbi Yirmiyah had possessed the house and was merely expelled by the minor, for that would not be Rashi's interpretation. Rather, the Riva follows Rashi that Rabbi Yirmiyah was never in possession. We therefore have the Riva, Rabbenu Ephraim, Rabbenu Tam as cited by the Hagahot HaRosh, and Rashi against Tosafot and the Ri. They hold that ancestral chazakah of the minor does not enable beit din to remove a claimant who is presently in possession. I searched further and found in Baal HaTerumot, sha'ar 14, part 1, din 12, that he first discusses whether Rabbi Yirmiyah had been in possession before the door was locked. He cites the Raavad, and finally the view of Rabbi Asher of Lunel that Rabbi Yirmiyah had never been in possession of his father in law's house. His proof is the fact that the minor locked the door against him. If Rabbi Yirmiyah had already been in possession, what legal difference could locking the door make? The mere detail that it happened that way would be forced and unnecessary. Rather, Rashi's explanation is clear: the minor locked the door specifically so Rabbi Yirmiyah could not take possession. He also brings proof from the case of Rabbah bar Rav Sharshom that present possession is effective. The Baal HaTerumot explicitly agrees with him and states that locking the door adds nothing. An elder authority has therefore already ruled like the Riva and Rabbenu Tam: where the opposing claimant is actually in possession, he is not removed even though the minor has ancestral chazakah. Locking the door has no legal effect. It is clear to me that the Sema, Bach, and Rama did not have the cited words of Baal HaTerumot before them, for otherwise they could not have omitted such a major source. The correct law is therefore as follows. The Rambam was right to rule without qualification that if a minor forcibly enters another's field, he is removed, without distinguishing whether the minor has ancestral chazakah. Since the owner of the field is already in possession, testimony is accepted and the minor's subsequent force changes nothing. We are not dealing with fools who permit violence to alter possession. The Gemara's distinction based on ancestral chazakah applies where the claimant is not in possession. If the minor has ancestral chazakah, testimony is not accepted until he grows up, as in Rabbi Yirmiyah's case according to these authorities. If the minor has no ancestral chazakah at all, testimony is accepted. This is the meaning of the rule that where the field is found not to belong to him, testimony is accepted, because the minor has no ancestral chazakah there. Tosafot in Eruvin also imply somewhat as I have written. The matter is clear, and the Ri is a lone view against the foregoing authorities. The Tur's language also points this way. He first writes that land in the possession of an heir, where his father had possessed it, is removed from the heir if it is shown to have been stolen, provided the testimony had been accepted during the father's lifetime. He then writes: "When does this apply? Where the land is in the minor's possession. But if it was not in his possession and the minor forcibly entered with his servants, it is removed from him immediately." This latter statement refers back to the former case and therefore applies even if the minor has ancestral chazakah. It is removed because the other party was already in possession of his field. Likewise above in siman 58 the Tur writes specifically of property "that is in the father's possession and another comes to establish possession in it." This implies that the other party has not yet established possession, exactly as Rashi explains that the minor locked the door so Rabbi Yirmiyah could not take possession. This is the Riva's view. Below in siman 149, letter 27, the Tur cites Rabbi Yehudah of Barcelona that if the claimant had held the property during the father's lifetime, but at the time of the father's death the property was again in the father's possession and he left a minor son, testimony is not accepted. This too shows that only where it was in the father's possession at death do we refrain from accepting testimony. If the claimant remained in possession, then even if the son later expelled him, testimony is accepted. The Bach there likewise writes that their view follows Rabbenu Tam that Rabbi Yirmiyah was not in possession of his father in law's property. It is true that the Rosh's abbreviated rulings state concerning the minor who forcibly entered that he is removed specifically where he lacks ancestral chazakah. One must say that the Rosh reconsidered this, because in his full discussion he cites no opposing view at all, which indicates that he follows Rashi and Rabbenu Tam, as the Pilpula Charifta notes. The Ri's position is therefore a lone view. The Rama's decision to cite it against the majority of the authorities, including the Tosafist authorities Riva, Rabbenu Tam, and Rabbenu Ephraim, requires study. The principal law is as the Mechaber ruled.
