Likewise, a minor who forcibly took possession through his servants. See the Sema, seif katan 13. He writes that Tosafot prove that the property remains with the minor and the adult's testimony is not accepted only when two conditions are present: the minor had ancestral possession, and the property is presently in the minor's possession. Tosafot in the chapter Shum HaYetomim appear to say this. Tosafot in the final chapter of Bava Kamma, however, write the opposite, that ancestral possession is always effective even where the property was in Rabbi Yirmeyah's possession. This requires study. Yam Shel Shlomo rules like the Tosafot cited by the Sema and writes that he saw in Rabbenu Tam's work that the text of Tosafot in Bava Kamma is erroneous. He therefore rejects that version. Nevertheless, one who analyzes Tosafot in Bava Kamma carefully can interpret their words consistently with Tosafot in Shum HaYetomim.
