A minor who took possession through his servants. See Sema, seif katan 14, who derives from Tosafot in Bava Kamma that two conditions are required. At first glance Tosafot do not state this expressly, so their words must be read carefully. Tosafot on Bava Kamma 112, s.v. Aval, write that the ruling apparently applies even if Rabbi Yirmiyah was physically inside the house, because the Gemara does not distinguish by saying that in the other case the landowner was in possession whereas Rabbi Yirmiyah was not. This implies that physical possession is not decisive; what matters is the minor's ancestral chazakah. Sema understands Tosafot's statement that Rabbi Yirmiyah could have been in the house to mean that he had been in possession before the minor's servants seized it. Had Tosafot meant only a case where Rabbi Yirmiyah was never in possession, the Gemara could simply have distinguished the cases on that basis. According to this reading, two requirements follow: the minor must possess an ancestral chazakah, and the minor must already have seized the property before coming to judgment. If ancestral chazakah alone allowed a minor to remove an adult who is presently in possession and prevented acceptance of the adult's witnesses, then whenever a seller died leaving minor heirs, those minors could eject every purchaser merely by pointing to their father's prior ownership. That cannot be correct. Rava bar Sharshom in Bava Batra likewise says that a purchase claim would have helped but for the adverse rumor, implying that such a claim is accepted even against minor heirs.
Shach says Tosafot imply the reverse. He apparently reads them as warning us not to think that Rabbi Yirmiyah's case involved his being outside the house entirely, with the minor merely locking him out, and that testimony would have been accepted had Rabbi Yirmiyah actually been inside. On that reading, Tosafot say that even if Rabbi Yirmiyah was physically in the house at the time of litigation, his evidence would not be accepted because the minor had ancestral chazakah, meaning the property was publicly recognized after the father's death as belonging to the minor's father. Tosafot's proof would then be that in the comparison case, even though the minor had already seized the field in the landowner's presence, that seizure did not destroy the landowner's prior chazakah. If so, Tosafot would indeed imply that ancestral chazakah of the minor can prevent acceptance of testimony even when the opposing adult is presently in physical possession. That he has damage claims. This means damage caused by their ox. If the minors themselves personally caused the damage they are exempt from payment. Rama's word "therefore" means that according to this opinion, whenever the matter is not one that directly concerns the orphans' own present affairs, testimony is not accepted against them. Thus testimony regarding damage is also not accepted even though the practical consequence concerns the orphans. Rosh in Perek Almanah Nizonet explains that because the damage arose automatically from their property, it is treated like a matter inherited from their father rather than a new personal act of the orphans.
