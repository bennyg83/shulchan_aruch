The statement of the scholars of Nehardea, Arakhin 23a. Rava rules according to them, that there is no need to appoint a guardian to argue on behalf of the orphans, because we do not presume witnesses to be lying. Tur writes this explicitly.
There, law 6. Maggid Mishneh writes that since all textual versions include, even in Rava's statement concerning property found not to belong to the deceased, that no guardian is appointed, Rambam's words can be reconciled as follows: he requires appointment of a guardian only at the beginning of the pleadings, when the claimant alleges that the property is stolen and before he produces witnesses. Once witnesses have testified, no guardian is needed. The court may entertain the claim in such cases and we do not apply the rule that testimony is not accepted outside the presence of a litigant, because where the deceased instructed this, or where the property was never in his established possession but was held only through theft, testimony is accepted. Maggid Mishneh writes that this is the principal explanation.
The language of Rambam there, based on Rav Yosef bar Chama in the name of Rav Oshaya, Bava Kamma 112b.
