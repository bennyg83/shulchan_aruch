Specifically, etc. Sema holds that even where the minor has ancestral possession, the minor must presently be physically occupying the field. If another person already occupies it, beit din does not remove him merely because the minor has ancestral possession. See Tumim. I concluded that according to the authority followed by Rama, ancestral possession itself can suffice even where the other person occupies the field. But the principal practical law follows most authorities, including Riva, Rabbeinu Tam, Rabbi Eliezer, Rabbi Shimshon of Lunel, Baal HaTerumot, and others: if the other party is in actual possession, we do not remove him merely because of the minor's ancestral possession, and even the minor's later seizure does not help. The distinction based on ancestral possession matters only where the other party lacks established possession and the minor himself occupies the field.
