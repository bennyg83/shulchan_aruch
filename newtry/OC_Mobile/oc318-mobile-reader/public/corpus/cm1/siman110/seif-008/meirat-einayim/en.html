The position here is that when the Sages said that collection is not made from the property of orphans, they meant debts arising from their father's affairs. A debt incurred for the orphans' own needs is collected from their property. According to the view that the ordinary restriction exists because the father may have left money earmarked for repayment, that concern is absent here. According to the view that minors are not personally obligated in the mitzvah of paying debts, here beit din or the guardian acted for their benefit, and it would be improper for them to remain in the status of one who borrows and does not pay. It also benefits the orphans that people should be willing to lend for their needs. For the same reason the property may be sold without announcement, and payment is made from intermediate quality land, as in the case of any ordinary debtor. The guardian or beit din, being adults, stand in the minors' place regarding transactions undertaken for their benefit.
