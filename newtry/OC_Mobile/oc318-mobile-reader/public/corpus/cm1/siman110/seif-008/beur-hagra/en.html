If they borrowed... Here there is no reason to suspect that the debt was prepaid, and Rav Pappa's concern that debt payment is merely a mitzvah likewise does not prevent collection, because a guardian is charged with the orphans' affairs. The Sages did not impose on someone supervising orphan property the status of a wicked borrower who refuses to pay. All the more so where beit din itself incurred the debt. There is also a protective enactment for the orphans, as Gittin explains regarding minors' commercial dealings being upheld for their livelihood. Kesubos and Gittin likewise distinguish cases with and without a guardian.
And they sell... As explained above, siman 109 seif 4.
And collection is from intermediate land... From Erchin, where the Gemara could have established the exemption for inferior land in a case of the orphans' own borrowing but did not. Kesubos likewise contains a case where Ravina assigned property accordingly.
But if... See Tosafos, fourth chapter of Bava Kamma 39a. The Rosh explains that Tosafos' statement that beit din does not proceed on loans incurred by the minors themselves because testimony cannot be accepted against them refers to loans made by the minors without a guardian. Their borrowing is comparable to their own purchase and sale, which can have validity, but testimony is still restricted. The case of an ox that caused damage is similar. Damage differs because beit din may appoint a guardian and accept testimony for protective reasons. See above siman 96 seif 3 and siman 235 seif 15.
