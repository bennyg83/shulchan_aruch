The Shach argues that after death, even if the creditor may personally retain what he seized and thereby satisfy his moral duty where he knows he was unpaid, if he comes to beit din and admits the seizure, the court should remove the property and place it with a third party. See Tumim. I wrote that this is incorrect. Beit din itself should leave the property in his hands. I also rejected Rabbeinu Yerucham's distinction cited by Sema between cash and other movables. Whenever the creditor has a migo, we do not remove the property from him.
