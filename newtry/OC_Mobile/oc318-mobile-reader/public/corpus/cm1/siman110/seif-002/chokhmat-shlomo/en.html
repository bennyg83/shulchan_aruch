Seif 4: An adult heir who transferred the inherited property to a minor heir. Note: The Mechaber’s words appear difficult, because they seem to contradict what he writes in the laws of inheritance: if the firstborn says, “I do not take a double portion,” etc., one consequence is that if the other brothers are minors beit din cannot collect from their shares, etc. According to the Sema’s reasoning there appears to be no basis to distinguish the cases. According to the Shach’s explanation the matter would be easier, but his own formulation requires study, for why does the Mechaber specifically speak of an adult heir who gave the property to a minor heir? Likewise, according to the Rosh’s distinction between a validated document and an unvalidated document, one could reconcile the rulings by saying that the inheritance law concerns an unvalidated document where witnesses are available to validate it. Even according to the authorities who permit validation after the father’s death, the case there could concern no document at all, but only witnesses who testify that the father owed the debt and that it was still within its term. Yet in truth that cannot be the full explanation, because if there are witnesses establishing the debt as still within the term, collection can be made even from minors under one of the three recognized exceptions stated in siman 108. The Mechaber’s ruling therefore still requires explanation. Perhaps the case is one where the testimony was not received during the father’s lifetime, whereas siman 108 concerns testimony already received in his lifetime. Examine carefully.
