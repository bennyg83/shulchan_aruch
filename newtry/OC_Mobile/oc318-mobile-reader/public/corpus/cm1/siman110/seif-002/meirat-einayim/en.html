Perhaps he gave him security, therefore, etc. This rejects the view that the reason is simply that repayment of a creditor is a mitzvah and minor heirs are not capable of performing mitzvot. According to that reason, seizure by the creditor would not help. I explained additional practical differences; for example, under that reason the creditor could not collect even in one of the three exceptional circumstances stated at the beginning of siman 108.
Therefore, if he seized, etc. Rabbeinu Yerucham writes, as cited by Beit Yosef, that if the creditor seized money the father left without witnesses, we do not remove it and he need not swear. If he seized before witnesses and holds a document, we do not remove it but require him to swear. If he seized existing movable property, it is placed with a third party until the heirs mature, when he swears and collects. This is where he says he seized it himself. If he says their father pledged it to him, we do not remove it; he swears and collects.
Where he can retain it. For example, he seized without witnesses or the object was not later seen in his possession, so he has a migo that he purchased it. He is therefore also believed to say that their father did not repay and did not provide other security, and we do not remove his seizure.
