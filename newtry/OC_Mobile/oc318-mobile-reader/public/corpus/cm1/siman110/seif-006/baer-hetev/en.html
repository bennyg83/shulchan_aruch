Purchased. The Sema explains that the Mechaber follows Rambam, who distinguishes between a claim that the property was stolen, where beit din hears the claimant and accepts testimony even while the possessor is a minor and removes the property from him, and a claim that the property was purchased, where testimony is not received unless the claimant produces a deed. Even if the deed is not yet authenticated, beit din attends to the matter, authenticates it, and removes the minor, because Rambam holds that a note may be authenticated in the presence of a minor just as it may be authenticated in the absence of an adult. The Tur, however, appears to equate a stolen-property claim and a purchased-property claim. In both cases, even where the minor has ancestral possession, the property is removed only if the deed had already been authenticated or testimony had been received during the father's lifetime. Otherwise beit din does not even authenticate the deed while the possessor is a minor. See Responsa Mahari ben Chaviv, siman 102; Mahari ben Lev, part 1 page 150 and part 3 siman 35; and Responsa Maharit at the end of siman 73.
