This is imprecise. No authority, including the Rambam, has a different text here. Rather, the Rambam rules like Rava, as the Maggid Mishneh, Beit Yosef, and Bach all write. Property that his predecessor left to the minor, etc. The Sema writes that a creditor is placed in possession of the property, despite the rule in siman 118 that we do not resort to a minor's property even upon a validated document containing an express clause of credibility. There is no difficulty. Here the document shows that the debt is still within its term, in which case collection is permitted, as explained there. The Mechaber did not specify this because these laws are not his immediate subject and he relied upon the earlier discussion. The Sema further distinguishes this minor, whose predecessor was an adult, and says that had the document been validated during the minor's minority, collection could likewise have been made. According to this, the Tur's later requirement that a document against the minor himself be validated appears to mean validation during the father's lifetime. Although one might force another reading, the Sema himself does not do so. The Beit Yosef's explanation is therefore correct, but it requires slight refinement. A document may ordinarily be validated outside the presence of the adverse party because no collection occurs until that party appears. In the case of a minor, however, validation itself would allow collection from him, and it is therefore not performed. She'erit Yosef objects that the Mordechai explains the refusal to validate against heirs on the ground that minors are not obligated in the mitzvah of repayment. He misunderstood the Mordechai, which actually supports the Beit Yosef. Moreover, this case concerns a minor recipient of a gift, who is legally bound to satisfy the prior lien and not merely by virtue of the mitzvah to repay. The independent answer proposed by She'erit Yosef is also unsatisfactory. See further the Sema, who writes that we do not apply two leniencies together, as Emunat Shmuel also states. The Bach discusses the difficulty at length.
