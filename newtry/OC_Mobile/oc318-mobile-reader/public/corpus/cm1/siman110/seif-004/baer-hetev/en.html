We place him in possession. The Sema writes that although siman 108, seif 3, states that beit din does not proceed against a minor's property even on an authenticated note containing an express credibility clause, there the reason is that had the heir been an adult he might have found a receipt or proven that the creditor had received other security. Here the heir was already an adult and only afterward transferred the property to a minor, so any receipt or proof would presumably already have been found. The Shach answers that here the note states that it is still within its term, in which case beit din does proceed. The Sema further understands the Mechaber, following Rambam, to hold that there is no distinction between an authenticated and unauthenticated note, because a note may be authenticated in the debtor's absence, and a minor is likewise considered absent. The Rosh and Tur nevertheless require authentication here. Although they too allow authentication outside the debtor's presence, ordinarily collection still occurs only in the debtor's presence, where he can still prove forgery even after formal authentication. With this minor, once the note is authenticated they would also remove the property from him because it came to him from an adult heir, and we do not combine two leniencies.
