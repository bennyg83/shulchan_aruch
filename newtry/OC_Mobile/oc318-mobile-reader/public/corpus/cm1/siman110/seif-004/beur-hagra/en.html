Minors... likewise... See Tosafos there and Rashi, who explain the scope of Rabbi Eliezer's statement.
According to Rambam. Rambam does not include the exception "except where it was found..." in the Nehardean statement, but only in Rav Ashi's statement. He understands the Nehardeans to say that in all cases where one proceeds against minors, a guardian is appointed. Rav Ashi then says that ordinarily we do not proceed for debts of the orphans where the ancestor gave no direction, but if a judge does proceed he must appoint a guardian. Where the field is specifically identified or a specified maneh is directed, all agree that a guardian may be unnecessary. The exception of property found not to belong to the ancestor is Rav Ashi's own statement and means that in that case beit din does proceed initially. See Rashba and Maggid Mishneh.
An adult heir... Erchin asks whether one is concerned for a receipt and answers according to the stated rationale. The same applies here. Otherwise every heir could transfer assets to a minor and defeat collection. Even Rav Huna's concern for prior payment applies particularly where the minor has an established ancestral possession. Tosafos and Ran explain that other rationales remain necessary as well. Bava Kamma's rule concerning property left before heirs, whether adults or minors, likewise turns on the existence of established ancestral possession. Here that factor is absent, especially according to Rav Pappa's rationale concerning repayment of debts. See Beer HaGolah.
