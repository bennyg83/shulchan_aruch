The estate inherited by a minor. See Shach seif katan 12. To explain his intent, it appears that he challenges the Sema, who wrote that the reason the document is not authenticated is that this would involve two leniencies. His challenge is from seif 6 and Sema seif katan 17 concerning a deed of sale, where the deed is not authenticated in the presence of minor heirs, even though there removing the property from their possession is not a leniency, since by law the property is removed from them, and nevertheless the deed is not authenticated. Therefore the Shach gives another reason. His meaning is that when a document is authenticated in a litigant's absence, this is because collection is not made from him until he returns. Although when he has gone overseas collection may also be made from him, and nevertheless the document is authenticated, that case is different because when he has gone overseas even oral testimony may be accepted in his absence, by rabbinic enactment, so that every person should not simply go overseas to evade proceedings, as explained in Arachin. Since in such a case testimony may be accepted outside the presence of the litigant, all the more may a document be authenticated. With minor heirs, however, that enactment does not apply, as explained in Arachin, and testimony is not accepted. Therefore the document is likewise not authenticated. The principal question was only from an ordinary absent litigant who has not gone overseas, where that enactment does not apply and testimony is not accepted in his absence, yet a document is nevertheless authenticated in his absence. If so, even regarding minor heirs, although testimony is not accepted, why should the document not be authenticated? To this the Shach answers that an ordinary absent litigant is different. In a case where testimony cannot be accepted because he has not gone overseas, the document may nevertheless be authenticated because actual collection will take place in his presence, and he will know how to pursue any rights or defenses available to him. By contrast, if the document were authenticated against minor heirs, collection would proceed from them immediately, while they do not know how to pursue their rights. Therefore beit din does not act even to authenticate the document. Indeed, for this same reason, even in circumstances where testimony may ordinarily be accepted in a person's absence, such as where the witnesses are about to go overseas or the litigant became ill, testimony is still not accepted against minors, because the poskim explain that they do not know how to pursue their rights. The same reasoning applies to authentication. Although a document is generally authenticated in a person's absence, it is not authenticated against minors for purposes of immediate collection from them, because they do not know how to pursue their rights.
