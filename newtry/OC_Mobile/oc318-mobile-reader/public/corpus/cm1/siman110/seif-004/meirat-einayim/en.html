Give the field a. In need of their possessions the taste d’all, the “el must admit that it is one of the many ways that require them while they are as small as they say several times:
Likewise, if he said, "Give this one hundred," etc. In the first clause the novelty is that even where he simply said "one hundred," we resort to their property after appointing a guardian. In the latter clause the novelty is that even where he said "this one hundred," a guardian must still be appointed, although the subject is definite, because the guardian may be able to invalidate the will or raise other arguments concerning its interpretation or validity. In an unspecified bequest, a guardian is required in any event to clarify the orphans' best portion, and they may also argue that perhaps the deceased meant one hundred provincial coins rather than one hundred Tyrian coins. The Baal HaTerumot writes similarly. His concluding words regarding the Rambam, "in this case there is no...," mean where he says "this one hundred" or "this field." The Maggid Mishneh and Nimukei Yosef write that the Rambam had a different text in the Gemara on this point, and likewise regarding the ruling in the following seif, where property is found not to belong to the orphans and a guardian must be appointed.
The estate of his predecessor to a minor, etc., until, a creditor is placed in possession of his property. Although it was already explained in siman 108, and in the Mechaber there seif 3, that we do not resort to the property of a minor even when a validated document is produced against it containing an explicit clause of trustworthiness, there the reason is that perhaps if the heir were an adult he would produce a receipt showing payment or establish that the debtor had deposited funds for repayment. Here, however, the heir from whom the minor inherited was himself an adult. If there had been a receipt or other proof, that adult heir would have found it. The Mechaber does not distinguish between a validated and unvalidated document, following the Rambam in chapter 12 of Hilchot Malveh VeLoveh. The reason is that it was already explained in siman 46 that a document may be validated outside the presence of the opposing party. By Torah law, witnesses signed on a document are regarded as though their testimony had been examined in beit din. The Sages imposed the additional requirement of validation, and they likewise determined that validation outside the party's presence suffices. A minor is equivalent in this respect to an absent party. See the Perishah. Although the Rosh and Tur also hold that a promissory note may be validated outside the debtor's presence, and even when it is against a minor orphan, they nevertheless write here that the document must already be validated. Ordinarily, although it may be validated outside his presence, collection cannot be made under it except in his presence. When the minor matures, even if the document has already been validated, he can then establish that it is forged if he is able. This case differs because the property came to the minor from an adult heir. If we were to validate the document while he is still a minor, collection could immediately be made from him on the ground that the property reached him through an adult heir. Therefore, although we are lenient in permitting collection from the minor when the document is already validated, we do not combine two leniencies, namely validating it and then immediately collecting from him during minority merely because it came from an adult heir. See the Perishah, where I cite the Rosh's responsum and explain why he carefully wrote, "In this case, if the document is not validated, we do not validate it." See there also my responses to other explanations of this passage, and what I wrote in the Sema at the beginning of simanim 46 and 78.
