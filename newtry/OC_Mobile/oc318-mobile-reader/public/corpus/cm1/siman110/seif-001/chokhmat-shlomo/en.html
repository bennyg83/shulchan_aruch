Seif 1. In the gloss: 'Beit din has authority to collect it for him,' etc. Note: See Shach, who cites Rabbi Avraham ben Chaim that the same applies if a penalty was stipulated for nonpayment. At first glance this is difficult. How can a penalty apply here when death is an unavoidable circumstance, and the Torah exempts one who is prevented by circumstances beyond his control? One cannot say that he expressly stipulated that even if he dies and his minor heirs fail to pay, the penalty applies, because the liability for the penalty arises only upon violation of the condition. At that time the property is already in the heirs' possession, so how can his obligation then take effect? It appears that the case is where he obligated himself in a document that if the due date arrives and payment is not made, even because of any unavoidable circumstance, including death and the property passing to minor heirs, then upon nonpayment at the specified time he undertakes from now an additional sum, in the most effective manner, avoiding asmachta. In such a case the penalty can indeed apply, and since payment produces a benefit for the heirs, beit din acts on their behalf. I later saw that Urim VeTumim sensed some of this difficulty and forced an explanation involving a violent gentile. In my opinion the matter is simple as I have written.
