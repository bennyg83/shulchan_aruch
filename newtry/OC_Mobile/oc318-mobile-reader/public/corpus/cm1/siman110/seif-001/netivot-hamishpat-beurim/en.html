From a non-Jew who accepted this upon himself. See Tumim, who asks that a non-Jew is presumed suspect and is like a suspect person who produces a promissory note, who cannot collect from orphans. He answers that the note contains a clause of credibility. In my opinion, Tumim himself writes in siman 104 that one who borrows from a non-Jew is considered to have subjected himself to their law. Since under their law collection from heirs occurs without an oath, it is as though the borrower expressly granted the creditor credibility against his heirs. Ketzot HaChoshen writes, regarding a fetus, that according to the view that there is no acquisition on behalf of a fetus even in an automatic inheritance, beit din certainly may address the estate, because the fetus has no share and the estate belongs only to the adult heirs, even the deceased's brothers. This is inaccurate. When the fetus is eventually born, the inheritance will also fall before him. Likewise, his comparison to one about whom a report of death was heard is not persuasive. Such a report is merely a doubtful rumor, which all agree is disregarded for terumah, whereas regarding a fetus many hold that we are concerned for a minority, as discussed in Yevamot, Perek Almanah. Moreover, it is possible that even where a report of death was heard, if it later becomes known that the person truly died, the Ran would agree that property is removed from one who seized it, because seizure after death based on witnesses is ineffective, as explained in seif 2. One must therefore say that the other heirs do not acquire the fetus's share. The appreciation attributable to the fetus belongs to the fetus, and in the case of a convert who dies without heirs no one can acquire that share on the fetus's behalf. Rather, the fetus's share remains suspended until the fetus is born. Nevertheless, this does not disqualify the estate from terumah in the case discussed there, because the fetus has brothers who are Kohanim and the slaves may eat by virtue of them. The suspended share does not impair their right to eat, since it is not yet subject to another person, and that share is not considered the property of a non-Kohen. See Sema, seif katan 3, where there is a scribal error. The text should read, until they grow up, just as if the creditor were a Jew.
