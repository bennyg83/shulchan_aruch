A case came before me involving a creditor seeking collection from the estate of a fetus. According to Rif's view in Yevamot, a fetus does not yet possess inheritance even automatically. On that approach the property is not yet the property of a minor heir, and the rule that we do not proceed against a minor's assets would not apply. Several authorities disagree with Rif and hold that a fetus does inherit automatically. Under that view the estate is property of a minor and beit din should not proceed against it. There is, however, an important qualification. We rule like the Sages that a fetus does not disqualify the household from eating terumah, because we combine the minority possibility of miscarriage with the half possibility of a female child, leaving a male fetus as a minority. Rambam codifies this where there are existing brothers. Accordingly, if the fetus has adult brothers, we can treat the estate as presumptively belonging to those adults and proceed for the creditor, even under the view that a fetus can inherit. If there are only sisters, then there is only the minority possibility of miscarriage and the fetus's possible male inheritance remains significant. In that case we should not proceed against the fetus's possible share. Rashba writes elsewhere that when a man leaves adult sons and a pregnant wife, an administrator is appointed for the fetus's possible share until its sex is known. That does not compel a different result here. The prohibition against proceeding against minors applies where the property is definitely theirs. Where adult brothers are presumptive heirs and the possibility that the fetus will displace them is only a minority, there is room to proceed. In the actual case before me there were no adult brothers, and I did not rely on Rif alone to proceed. The creditor ultimately waived part of the debt. But where adult brothers exist, I would permit the court to proceed against the estate.
