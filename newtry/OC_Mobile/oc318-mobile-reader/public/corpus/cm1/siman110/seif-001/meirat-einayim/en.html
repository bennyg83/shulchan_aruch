At the beginning of siman 108. Meaning seif 3, which deals with minor heirs; the beginning of that siman itself discusses adult heirs.
Unless there is an interest-bearing loan against them. To prevent the interest from consuming the principal, it is to the minors' benefit that beit din act and pay from their property while they are still minors. In such a case the property is sold based on appraisal even without waiting thirty days for public announcement, because any increase from announcement is uncertain while the accumulating interest is certain, and we do not set aside a certainty because of a doubt. This is explained at the beginning of chapter Shum HaYetomim. Therefore the case is not identical to collection of a woman's ketubah, where ordinarily they wait thirty days for the sake of facilitating marriage. If the sale is to avoid the cost of her continuing support, however, they also sell without delay for the same reason.
He accepted upon himself, etc., not to demand, etc. Otherwise there would be no need to say that we pay a non-Jewish lender from the minors' assets to avoid interest, because under his law he would simply compel collection from the father's estate when due, without distinction between minors and adults. The case therefore concerns a non-Jewish lender who agrees not to force payment until they mature, but does not waive interest during their minority. Were he also willing to waive the interest, we would certainly wait until they grew, as with a Jewish lender. The Gemara and poskim present the stronger novelty where he expressly agreed to wait, teaching that we nevertheless do not allow the estate to be consumed by interest merely because the father originally entered the interest-bearing arrangement.
