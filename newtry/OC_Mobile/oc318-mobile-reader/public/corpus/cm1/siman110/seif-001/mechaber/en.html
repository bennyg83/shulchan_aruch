We do not proceed against the property of a minor. It contains 11 se'ifim.
It was already explained at the beginning of siman 108 that collection is not made from heirs under thirteen years old, even upon a promissory note, unless the loan falls into one of the three categories explained there. This applies even if the note grants the lender credibility and contains every possible condition. Collection is permitted, however, when their father borrowed from a non-Jew at interest and the non-Jew accepted Jewish law, agreeing not to sue the orphans until they became adults but not agreeing to forgo interest. {Rama: Similarly, if beit din sees another benefit to the orphans in collecting while they are minors, such as when the creditor wishes to waive and forgive part of the debt if he can collect now, beit din may allow collection, as stated in Responsa of the Ran 31. A woman's ketubah is likewise collected from them, as explained in the Tur, Even HaEzer. If some heirs are minors and others adults, the lender collects from the adults the portion attributable to them. To enable the creditor to collect the adults' portion, beit din appoints a guardian for the minors, divides the estate between them and the adults, and collects from the adults the portion they must pay. The rule that we do not proceed against minors' property applies when the property certainly belongs to the minors. If it is uncertain whether their ancestor is still alive, then even if a report was heard that he died, collection is made from the property. See notes 45, 159, and 209.}
