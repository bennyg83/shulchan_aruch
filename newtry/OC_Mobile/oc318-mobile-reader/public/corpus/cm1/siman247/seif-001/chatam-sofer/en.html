Rama, seif 5, "one who says." Note: In Piskei Maharia, siman 73, he writes that although we rule like Rabbi Yonatan that "or" is understood disjunctively unless the verse explicitly joins them together, that works well regarding Aharon and his sons, where the verse includes them and says, "they shall eat it." But in wills and documents this is difficult. He was forced to answer that in such contexts one cannot reasonably say, "either to this one or to that one." See the general principle in the Ritva, cited in Shitah Mekubetzet at the beginning of the chapter HaShoel in the name of Rabbenu Pinchas HaLevi, that Rabbi Yonatan's rule does not apply to an affirmative act. This requires study from Yoma 58a regarding mixing for the corners. The Yad David already wrote this there. See also what the Taz writes in Yoreh Deah, siman 228, seif katan 49. His words require study. See also Responsa Chacham Tzvi, siman 4.
