In this case, the model of girls in general, in particular, is that women are not married or that the girls are not married to the girls who are worthy of the women who are sent by their father, but who are not married or who are not married, and the daughters are not married to their wives, but who are not married to their children We shall return to his house and serve as a messenger to his sons and his children:
They were seen in males and females. It means that there is a good thing for them:
The males take them. In the Perishah and Derishah I proved that this applies specifically where he said, "Give to my sons," because the word sons more strongly indicates males than females. In the next case, where he sent property and said it should be given to his household, it is given to both males and females. The Tur says so explicitly in the Ramah's name. Likewise, where he says "give to my children" and the property is suitable to both, the Rambam says it is given to his children without excluding daughters. The rulings therefore do not conflict.
A daughter receives nothing where there are sons. Although his intention was certainly that the daughters also receive some benefit, for otherwise the condition seems unnecessary since the later born sons would inherit with the earlier sons in any event, nevertheless, because by Torah law daughters do not inherit where there are sons, we do not uproot the Torah's law of inheritance unless he explicitly transfers property to them through the language of gift.
