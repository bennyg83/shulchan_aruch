These shall be given to the sons and daughters. Sema says that if both the sons and daughters are married, daughters in law should receive priority, because their needs ultimately fall upon the donor's sons, whereas the married daughters have husbands responsible for them. He compares the next seif concerning gifts sent to members of one's household. Bach disagrees. Where the donor expressly says, "Give to my sons," daughters can be included within the general language of children, but daughters in law are not his sons. The explicit designation therefore excludes them. Daughters in law are included only when the donor says "members of my household," because they are indeed members of the household. Shach cites this dispute, though the printed text contains an error.
