The assets shall return to their relatives. Ritva writes that one who says, "My property to my sons" or "to my daughters" includes all of them, even a hundred; we do not apply the rule that a plural without specification means two. Likewise, "to my relatives" includes all relatives who are sufficiently close to be disqualified from testimony. Tosafot, however, appears to hold that among relatives the closer relative comes first. In Bava Batra a person died leaving a palm, and two claimants each asserted greater kinship. Tosafot's version explains the case as a direction to give the palm to the relatives. If all testimony disqualified relatives shared equally, it would make no difference which was closer. This indicates that closer kin take precedence. Tosafot's alternate reading, however, says that at the end one claimant merely brought witnesses that he was a relative, without proving he was closer. That can be understood to mean he proved definite kinship of the relevant degree while the other's kinship remained uncertain. Then the rule is simply that a doubtful claimant does not displace a certain one. Thus the precise scope of Ritva's equal inclusion remains open.
