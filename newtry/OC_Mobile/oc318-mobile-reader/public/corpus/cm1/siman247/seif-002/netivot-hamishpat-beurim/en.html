And he stipulated with his first sons. This is difficult. Even without any further issue, his statement that the sons from the second wife should take property is a transfer to persons not yet born, which is ineffective. I later found that the marginal glosses to Mordechai raise this question and answer that the ruling was written according to the opinion that if he explicitly says, "when I die," such a transfer can work. Even according to that view, however, a daughter among sons does not acquire. According to our ruling, therefore, even if the children of the second wife would later be numerous sons, they do not acquire because the transfer was to persons not yet in existence.
