(That which is suitable for the daughters.) The Mordechai there compares this to the ruling in seif 1 concerning a healthy person who said, "To my sons, etc." It appears to me, however, that there is still a distinction. According to the Rambam, we said above that property suitable for both belongs to the males, and there is no distinction there between a case in which all the objects are suitable for both and one in which only some objects are suitable for both. Here, however, it is not so. Only when some items are suitable for both do the males take them, because his statement mentioning his daughters can still be fulfilled with respect to those items suitable only for daughters. But if all the objects are suitable for both, all must be divided between both groups; otherwise his words, "and to my daughters," would not be fulfilled. This appears correct to me.
