Everything goes to the son alone. Only with a healthy person who says, Give to my children, do we say that his intention includes the daughters, so that they will become more beloved to their husbands or more attractive to prospective husbands. A person on his deathbed, however, does not ordinarily divert the inheritance, since the daughters already receive one tenth of the estate. Although he used the plural children when he has only one son, Scripture contains similar usage, as in the phrase, and the sons of Dan, Hushim. One cannot ask why he needed to say that it should be given to his son, since the son would inherit even without his statement. The answer is that he knew he had already given the property to another through a deathbed gift, and by this statement he came to retract the first gift:
Explicitly, to my sons and my daughters. See Beurim: if there are items suitable for both, the sons alone take them. If there is no item at all suitable only for the daughters, the sons take half and the daughters take half:
