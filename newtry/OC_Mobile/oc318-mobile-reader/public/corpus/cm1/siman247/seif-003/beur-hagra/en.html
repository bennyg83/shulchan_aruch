To her and not, etc. As stated in the Gemara there, in accordance with the first Tanna, and in keeping with the rule that wherever Rabban Shimon ben Gamliel taught in our Mishnah the halachah follows him. See Rashbam 138a.
And furthermore, etc. As above; see Mordechai there.
There is an authority who says, etc. See Rashbam there, s.v. "mi karu, etc.," and "this, etc." But otherwise, certainly it refers to the son's son.
Even, etc. In the first question the Gemara asks whether people call him by that term. Once it resolves that they do, that question is settled. Here, although that point is settled, they nevertheless ask whether people use the other term, etc.
Nevertheless, etc. As stated in seif 4 regarding one who divided, etc., for females are called orphans in many places in the Gemara, and so are adults, as stated in Kiddushin 42a: "orphans who came, etc.," according to the Rosh's explanation.
