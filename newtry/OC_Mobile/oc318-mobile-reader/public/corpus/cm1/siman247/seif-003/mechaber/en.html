If a deathly ill person says, "My property to my sons," in the plural, but has only one son and daughters, everything goes to that son alone. Rama: All the more so, if he has several sons, everything goes to the sons. The daughters are not included. Rama: If he expressly says, "to my sons and my daughters," whatever is suitable for sons goes to the sons and whatever is suitable for daughters goes to the daughters. A son's son is also not included. Rama: In one case, a man left everything to his son on condition that he give a fixed sum to each daughter. One daughter died during his lifetime leaving a son. Her son received nothing, because the direction meant her and not her heirs, and a daughter's son is not called a son. Some say that if there is no son but only a son's son, the testator certainly intended the grandson. Rama: This is so even if he has daughters. If he says, "to my orphans," daughters are included. The same applies if he says, "to my descendants."
