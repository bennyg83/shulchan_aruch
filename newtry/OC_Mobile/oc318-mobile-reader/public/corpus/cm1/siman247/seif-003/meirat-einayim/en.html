Everything belongs to the son alone. Specifically with a healthy person who says, "Give to my children," daughters may be included, in order to endear married daughters to their husbands or make unmarried daughters more attractive to prospective husbands. A dangerously ill person does not ordinarily transfer inheritance away from a son merely to endear daughters to their husbands. Even if the daughters are unmarried, after his death they are entitled to one tenth of the estate for marriage expenses, so he is not presumed to transfer more inheritance than that. Responsum of the Rosh explains this. Although he uses a plural expression and has only one son, Scripture itself uses plural language where only one descendant is meant, as in "the sons of Dan, Hushim" and "the sons of Pallu, Eliav." Tosafot ask why he needed to say anything at all, since the son would inherit without instruction. They answer that he may have known that he had already given his property to others through a dangerously ill person's will, and this new statement was intended to retract that prior disposition.

Moreover, a daughter's son is not called a son. Hagahot Mordechai reads "a grandson," and Darkei Moshe likewise. There is no practical difference here. The point of "moreover" is to add another proof that the son need not give anything to the daughter's son. First, the Gemara's rule that when a person says "to me" he means to himself and not to his heirs indicates that the language is limited. Second, the Gemara says that when a dangerously ill person says, "My property to my sons," a grandson is not included when a son exists. So too here his intention was directed to his sister, not automatically to her child.
He certainly means the grandson. In the Perishah I explained the Beit Yosef's language and derived the following. If he made the statement while healthy and had daughters and a grandson through a deceased son, the daughters receive what is appropriate for daughters under the rule concerning the language "my children." If he made the statement while dangerously ill, the entire property goes to the grandson, just as where he had a son and daughter the entire property would go to the son.
