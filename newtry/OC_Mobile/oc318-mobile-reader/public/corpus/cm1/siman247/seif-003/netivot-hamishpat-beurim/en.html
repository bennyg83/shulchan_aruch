If he said explicitly, "to my sons and my daughters." At first glance one should ask why the sons do not take half and the daughters half, as in seifim 4 and 5. The same difficulty arises from Mordechai, chapter Mi Shemet, siman 625, where one says, "to you and your sisters," and they divide equally. Machaneh Efrayim raises this and leaves it unresolved, and also questions Rashba's responsum, siman 1043. Seif 4 can perhaps be distinguished because there he used the word "divide," which implies equal division among them. Rashba's responsum can likewise concern language expressly implying equal division. Mordechai, however, remains difficult. The explanation appears as follows. Where a healthy person says simply, "My property to my sons," and the estate contains items especially suited for daughters, the daughters can take those items even if their value exceeds what would otherwise have been their proportional share, because, as Sema explains, we presume he wished to endear them to their husbands. With a shechiv mera, however, we assume he did not intend to uproot the Torah law of inheritance. Therefore, when he says explicitly "to my sons and daughters," we interpret him as granting to the daughters only those items especially suited for them, so as to endear them to their husbands, while not uprooting inheritance beyond that. Accordingly, if there are items suitable to both sons and daughters, those items go only to the sons, as in seif 1. The expression, "what is suitable for the sons goes to the sons," is therefore not precise. Even property suitable to both goes only to the sons. The daughters take only property especially suited to them and no more. This also fits Mordechai's wording. The estate there included landed estates and jewelry. He writes that what is suitable for the sons goes to the sons and what is suitable for the daughters goes to the daughters. He does not separately discuss the land, implying that the lands and ordinary estate assets are treated as suitable for the sons and go to them, while the daughters receive only the items especially suited to daughters. If, however, there are no items especially suited to daughters and the estate consists only of money and land, then the daughters take half and the sons half, because no other interpretation can give meaning to the express inclusion of the daughters.
