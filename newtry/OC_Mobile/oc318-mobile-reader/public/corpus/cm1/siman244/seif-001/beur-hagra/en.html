The gift, etc. For we rule like Shmuel in monetary law and like Rava there.
How so, etc. For we rule like Rabbi Yose, as stated there 67a: the halachah follows Rabbi Yose.
They said, "Tell so and so, etc." There 66b: "they sent to him, etc."; rather, Rabbi Yose stated two rulings, etc. Although Shmuel and many Amoraim disagree where one says "tell so and so," the Rif and Rosh rule like Rabbi Yose because Rav Ashi establishes the Mishnah there 72a according to Rabbi Yose, and a baraisa supports him.
And if they said, etc. As stated there, "she shall go out, etc." The same applies to a gift.
And certainly, etc. For he did not command you.
And likewise if, etc. This is all the more obvious, for everyone concedes in such a case, even Rabbi Meir, and certainly Shmuel. See there 72a: "it was unnecessary, etc."
Some validate, etc. Ramban asked why "tell them" should be invalid, since we rule according to one view there that a person may appoint an agent even not in his presence. He answers that a get is different because it must be written for her sake and because Scripture says "and he shall write for her," so the witnesses and scribe stand in place of the husband only if they heard from his mouth. The case of a gift resembles a get only regarding mere verbal instructions, but not regarding "tell them." This is evident from Shmuel, who validates "tell them" even for a get, as above. Ra'ah and Ran there have a different approach. The Beit Yosef writes in Even HaEzer 120 that all the poskim reject Ra'ah's view. See Shulchan Aruch there, siman 141, seif 14, "whether, etc." This is Ramban's view as explained in the Ran there.
