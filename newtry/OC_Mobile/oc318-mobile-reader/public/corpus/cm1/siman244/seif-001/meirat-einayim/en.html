A gift is like a get in that a person cannot, etc. The law of a get in this respect is explained in the Tur, Even HaEzer siman 120 and also siman 141, and in Rambam, chapter 2 of Hilchot Gerushin. The Mechaber here copied Rambam's wording from chapter 4 of Hilchot Zechiyah U'Matanah. In the Derishah I reconciled what might appear difficult between the Rosh cited by the Tur in Even HaEzer siman 141 and what the Tur writes here at the end of this siman.
If he said to three. He states three as a greater novelty: even though three have the status of a beit din, words cannot be delegated through them. See the Perishah.
They should tell so-and-so and so-and-so to write and sign. In Hilchot Gerushin, Rambam writes, "Tell the scribe to write and the witnesses to sign," because regarding a get the scribe who writes should not sign. The Sages decreed lest a man occasionally be particular that the scribe not sign the get, but his agents who were instructed to arrange the get, embarrassed before the scribe, might tell the scribe to sign together with one other witness, which would certainly be invalid. With a gift they were not as stringent, and the scribe may also sign as a witness. Rambam equated a gift to a get only with respect to the rule of "say to others," which constitutes delegation of words. This is why Rambam and the Mechaber say that "a gift is like a get in that a person cannot delegate his words," implying that this is the only point of equivalence. See the Derishah.
All the more so if they themselves signed, etc. Meaning, then they actually deviated from the giver's instructions, for he told them to tell other witnesses to sign. Rama placed this "all the more so if they themselves signed" on the first ruling, which is Rambam's view. This supports my emendation in the Tur to place these words in Rambam's position rather than in that of the Raavad, as in our printed editions. See the Derishah for my reconciliation of the Maggid Mishneh in chapter 4 of Hilchot Zechiyah.
Likewise, if he said to two, etc. The Tur does not say "to two," nor does it say there that he told them to write and sign. Rather, it says, "the same applies if he told them to give, etc.," referring back to the three mentioned above. This is an even greater novelty, because one might think that by treating those three as a beit din he empowered them to give a get to his wife in his place, whether by themselves or through others. The Mechaber mentions three in the first clause and two in the latter clause because when he tells them to write and sign, two suffice. In the first clause, where he tells them to instruct others, he teaches that even three who have the status of a beit din cannot delegate the matter and thereby appoint an agent for the giver. If you ask why Rambam and the Mechaber write "likewise if he said to two, write, etc.," when that seems all the more obvious from the first law, the answer is that one might have thought the first case is invalid specifically because he did not authorize them to write, only to tell others, making it mere words, whereas if he instructed them to write and give, they are principals in the matter and could perhaps appoint agents in their place. The ruling therefore teaches otherwise. See the Perishah.
And some validate, etc. The Tur explicitly states that they too agree that a gift is like a get, as stated in the Gemara, but not in every respect. Specifically, if he told the witnesses themselves to write and sign, they may not change his instruction and appoint others because words cannot be delegated to an agent. But if he told them to tell other witnesses, this differs. I explained the reason in the Perishah. There I also reconciled the apparent contradiction between the Tur and Mechaber here, who say that only with a gift document do some validate "say to others," whereas with a get it is invalid, and the Tur in Even HaEzer siman 141, in the Augsburg printing, writes that even with a get we do not invalidate such a case. The latter means only that it is not invalid on the ground that words cannot be delegated to an agent. A get may nevertheless be invalid for another reason, because the verse says "and he shall write and give." See there, where I brought proof for this.
