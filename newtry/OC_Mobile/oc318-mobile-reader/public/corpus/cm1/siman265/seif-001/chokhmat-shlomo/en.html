Seif 1. If he stipulated with the owners or before beit din, etc. Note: It is not stated whether a qualified beit din is required or whether three lay judges suffice. From Bava Metzia 32 it appears that for a sale or division of partnership property, where Rav Safra holds that a formal beit din is not required, three laymen suffice. Our case is more serious and even Rav Safra agrees that beit din is required, which implies qualified judges. The Mishna's wording supports this, for here it says "before beit din," while regarding a widow it says "not before beit din," where the meaning is not before an expert court. Rashi also explains that the advantage of beit din here is its power to declare another person's property ownerless. Three ordinary laymen do not possess that power. The Shulchan Aruch likewise says simply "beit din" here, while above in siman 176 it expressly says that experts are unnecessary for division of partnership property. It therefore appears that a genuine beit din is required. For the same reason a single expert judge does not suffice, since the money belongs to the litigants and the power of hefker beit din is exercised by a court of three, not by one individual expert. Still, one learned and competent judge together with the others suffices for an ordinary beit din, as explained in siman 3 and elsewhere.
