Only like an entirely idle worker. See Sema, seif katan 4, who asks from siman 268, seif 3, and gives a strained answer. In my view Tur follows Nimukei Yosef in Bava Metzia 31. Tosafot ask from the rule that one who rescues another's animal is entitled only to his wages. Nimukei Yosef distinguishes between cases where the owners are present and cases where they are absent. He also distinguishes ordinary labor, which is common and which one normally does not wish to lose, from rescue of an animal swept away, which is uncommon. Nimukei Yosef also holds that even in the unusual rescue case, where the rescuer may otherwise be exempt from acting, the limited payment rule applies specifically when the owners were present. This distinction is necessary because Tosafot's second question, from a custodian who hires shepherds and uses sticks to rescue property, cannot be answered merely by saying the loss was unusual. Thus, when the owners are present, even one who is not obligated to perform the rescue receives at most the value of his lost labor as an entirely idle worker, without an additional rescue wage. Why should he not also receive a rescue wage if he had no duty to return the lost property? The answer follows Shitah Mekubetzet. Since the owners were present and he did not stipulate with them, it is possible he intended to go beyond the letter of the law and perform a mitzvah gratuitously. He therefore cannot later demand a separate rescue fee. If the owners were absent, however, he certainly must be paid his full proper compensation, because he was not obligated to act and is like one who descends into another's property without permission and improves it. He receives the ordinary value of the service.
