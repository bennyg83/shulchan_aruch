Others explain, etc. In my opinion, this is not the primary explanation of the Gemara. According to it, the expression 'like an idle worker from the work from which he was idled' in Perek Elu Metziot would not have the same meaning as the identical expression in Perek Eizehu Neshekh, although Abaye stated both in the same formulation. Tosafot in Perek Eizehu Neshekh and the Semag, positive commandment 82, folio 68, citing Rabbi Shimshon, were forced into this explanation because the Gemara in Perek Elu Metziot asks, 'But he is not idle.' In my opinion, however, their interpretation makes the question less satisfactory. How could the Gemara ask this so simply when the accepted law is that one may not receive payment for returning a lost item? Rather, it appears to me that in Perek Elu Metziot the question also concerns the first occupation and refers back to the Mishna, which states that if he was idled from earning a sela, he is paid like an idle worker. The initial assumption was that he is paid as though he had been idle from his first occupation. The Gemara therefore asks that he was not in fact idle from that first occupation, and answers that he is paid for what he lost from that occupation according to what he would accept to sit entirely idle now. Indeed, this must be the meaning of the Gemara according to the Rosh's version at the end of Perek Ad Kamah, and all the more so here, where the Mishna itself states that he was idled from earning a sela. Examine this carefully. It now follows properly that he receives no payment for the act of returning the lost item, as is evident in Perek HaKones and at the end of Perek Elu Metziot concerning unloading, where returning a lost item is performed without charge. Thus, in Perek Elu Metziot, Perek Eizehu Neshekh, and Perek Ad Kamah of Bekhorot, the phrase 'like an idle worker' has one consistent meaning. Tosafot in Perek Ad Kamah likewise interpret the passages in Elu Metziot, Bekhorot, and Eizehu Neshekh consistently. Torat Chayim in Perek Eizehu Neshekh agrees, although some of his language there is strained. According to what I have written, everything is clear. The Rambam also explains the Mishna in Bekhorot this way. The Beit Yosef, at the beginning of Yoreh De'ah siman 177, did not cite the Rambam's explanation in this manner, and I do not understand his intent. The Rosh himself, at the end of Perek Ad Kamah in Bekhorot, likewise explains it this way. Maadanei Melekh already left his words in Perek Elu Metziot requiring study. See also what I wrote in Nekudot HaKesef, siman 312. The Maggid Mishna further writes in chapter 12 of the Laws of Theft and Lost Property: 'The meaning of like an idle worker from that occupation is as follows. Sometimes work is plentiful and wages are high, while at other times work is scarce, workers are idle, and they hire themselves cheaply rather than remain unemployed. If he returned the lost item at a time when work was plentiful, and the return delayed him from earning a sela, he may not say, Pay me the sela I lost by returning it. Rather, he is paid according to what he would have earned at a time when work was scarce and he would have accepted half a sela rather than remain idle. This is the explanation of Rabbeinu Chananel, Raavad, and the Rif,' end quote from Rashba. Ramban agreed with this, end quote from the Maggid Mishna. This explanation is also sound, for according to it the phrase 'like an idle worker' has the same meaning everywhere. Although Tosafot in Perek Ad Kamah questioned Rabbeinu Chananel's explanation, it appears correct to me. Examine this carefully. The Beit Yosef at the beginning of Yoreh De'ah siman 177 also cites the students of Rashba in accordance with Rabbeinu Chananel, and the Arukh, entry po'el, explains it likewise. Under this interpretation too, he is not paid for the second occupation itself, and the Gemara's need to state both cases in Perek Eizehu Neshekh is also well explained.
