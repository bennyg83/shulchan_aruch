One who sees, etc. Rosh, seif 28, and Tosafos 31b, s.v. "im, etc." It is like unloading, for loading is done for pay because the verse concerning loading is superfluous, as stated 32a, whereas lost property is not, as stated 31a, "both are necessary, etc." The necessity is in comparison with unloading, because there the animal's suffering is present. Loading is further needed because there is no financial loss, as stated in the first explanation there. In chapter HaKones the Gemara asks, "what benefit does he receive, etc.?" See Tosafos to Bava Metzia 68a at the end of s.v. "venoten, etc.," and Bechoros 29b, s.v. "kepoel."
That he would cease, etc. Meaning that he would sit entirely idle, like the "idle worker" in chapter Eizehu Neshech and chapter HaOmanin. Tosafos in Bechoros there explain so.
Some explain, etc. Rashi in Bava Metzia there. The Rosh rules likewise, and Tosafos in Eizehu Neshech there agree.
(Collected note) Like an idle worker, etc. Meaning that he receives compensation for his original labor and not for dealing with the lost object. So explain Tosafos in Bechoros 29b, s.v. "kepoel, etc.," who say the Gemara should have explained, etc. The Rosh likewise interprets it there regarding firstborn animals, and Rambam in his commentary, the Tur in Yoreh Deah siman 312, Tosafos in Bava Metzia 68a regarding interest, Semag in the name of Ri, and the Rosh there all rule so. Regarding the lost object here, however, Tosafos and the Rosh there explain like Rashi, and the Tur rules so here, in accordance with the distinction made by Tosafos and the Rosh. Semag similarly writes that Tosafos initially explained the lost-object case here as they did regarding interest, but later retracted and explained it according to Rashi. See Beer HaGolah, seif katan 4. The Maggid Mishneh wrote, etc. Rabbeinu Chananel is likewise cited in Tosafos to Bechoros, and he explains there, etc. Ramban in Milchamos also interprets the Rif accordingly. Semag, however, explains the Rif according to Rashi and Rabbi Shimon according to Tosafos in the view of Rabbi Meir. It emerges that his explanation reverses Tosafos: Rabbi Meir follows Tosafos's explanation of Rabbi Shimon, and Rabbi Shimon follows their explanation of Rabbi Meir. The Tur, Choshen Mishpat siman 177, explains contrary to Tosafos that he receives compensation for the investment and not for his original labor. Tosafos there then write, "and Rabbeinu Chananel explained like an idle worker, etc.," but this is a scribal error and should read Rabbeinu Tam.
And if the compensation for returning, etc. For the permission was granted only because of his loss.
And that which it is permitted, etc. Tosafos and the Rosh there note that here he is paid his entire loss as an idle worker and loses nothing, whereas in the case where his donkey is swept away he receives only his compensation and loses his donkey. They answer that there the owners are present, as stated 32b, "ordinarily a person, etc.," whereas here the owners are absent, as stated 31a, "if it had taught only lost property, etc." This is the meaning of the Mishnah: "if there is a beit din there, etc.; if there is not, etc." There too it says, "if he said, 'I will save, etc.,' to the owners." Similarly there, if the owners are absent, he does not lose his donkey, as stated in siman 264:3. This requires study, however, for it should have said that here, if the owners are with him, he receives compensation only for returning it and not for his lost labor. Here it states the opposite. These are the words of the Rosh and Tur; see Bach and Sema, whose explanations are strained.
Like a completely idle worker. Meaning according to Rambam and Shulchan Aruch. See Beer HaGolah, seif katan 4. Tosafos in Bechoros likewise cite this in the name of Rabbeinu Chananel and present that explanation without qualification.
And if he stipulated, etc. As in the case of his donkey being swept away.
Or, etc. The Mishnah in Bava Metzia there.
And he must, etc. This follows from the statement there 30a, "or if the work was, etc.," unlike such a case. See Tosafos 31b, s.v. cited above: "but above, etc."
And they too, etc. As stated in Bava Kamma there: "why? Let him say he was jesting, etc.; but, etc." The Rosh writes all this there; see there.
