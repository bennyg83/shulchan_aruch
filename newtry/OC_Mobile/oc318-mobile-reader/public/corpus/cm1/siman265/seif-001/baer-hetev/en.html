Engaged. The Rambam and the Mechaber likewise explain the term 'an idle worker,' mentioned regarding interest between two partners in Yoreh Deah at the beginning of siman 177, in this manner. According to this, the person receives no payment at all for returning the lost object itself; rather, he receives the amount for which this worker would have been willing to stop the work in which he was engaged and sit completely idle. The Tur, however, and the alternative view cited by the Rama hold that because the mitzvah of returning the lost object is not specifically incumbent on this worker at the cost of his employment, he must also be paid for the act of return. Although regarding interest the Tur explains 'idle worker' differently, namely how much a completely idle person would demand to undertake this partnership business, that case is different. The partner who receives the investment is pleased to undertake it because he receives half the profit, and the only concern is the prohibition of interest; therefore the Sages said that this lesser payment suffices. Here, by contrast, the worker does not wish to stop his own work. Sema.

Completely. Here they say that he receives no payment for the return itself, but only compensation for the loss caused by ceasing his work. Yet in siman 264 seif 6, where one person on his own descended to save another's animal and abandoned his own, the law is the reverse: he receives payment for returning the other's donkey but not compensation for the loss of his own donkey. The reason is that strictly speaking he should receive nothing there, because the owner of the lost object was present and no stipulation was made. The owner may say: had you not gone down on your own to save it, I would have gone down myself. Nevertheless, the Sages assessed ordinary human intent and presumed that the owner is generally pleased to pay whoever acts first to save his property in such circumstances. Since the payment is not a strict legal obligation but rests on an assessment of intent, he is always given only the lesser amount. Here the compensation for lost work is less than or equal to the wage for returning the object. In siman 264, however, where his donkey was swept away and his loss exceeds the wage for returning the other donkey, he receives only the lesser amount. Sema.
