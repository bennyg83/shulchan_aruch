And some explain otherwise. The Shach concludes in accordance with the first explanation:
He caused the loss to himself. The ruling in siman 264 seif 3 appears opposite, for there he receives compensation for returning the lost item but not the value of the donkey he lost. The explanation is that by strict law he should receive nothing there, because the owner of the lost item was present and the rescuer did not stipulate compensation. The owner can therefore say, Had you not gone down to rescue it, I myself would have rescued it. The Sages nevertheless assessed that people generally prefer that whoever first saves their lost property receive compensation. Since payment there rests only on this presumed intent, he receives whichever amount is smaller. Here the loss from interrupting his own work is the smaller amount, while there the compensation for returning the item is the smaller amount (Sema):

His own property takes precedence. This is not comparable to siman 264 seif 3, where if the owners are absent it is treated as though they stipulated payment. Here it is different, because ultimately they will not believe his claim, and since every person certainly prefers his own rest and interests, the Sages allowed him to attend to his own work and leave the return of the lost item.
