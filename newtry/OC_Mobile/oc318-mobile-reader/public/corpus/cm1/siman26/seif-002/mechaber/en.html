If the non-Jewish authorities are powerful and the opposing party is violent, so that one cannot recover from him through Jewish judges, he must first summon him before Jewish judges. If the opposing party refuses to come, he obtains authorization from the beit din and recovers from him through the non-Jewish courts. {Rama: The beit din may go before the non-Jewish authorities and testify that this person owes the other (Baal HaTerumos in the name of Rav Sherira Gaon). This applies only when the person refuses to obey a Jewish court. Otherwise, a beit din may not authorize litigation before non-Jews (Maharick, shoresh 1).}
