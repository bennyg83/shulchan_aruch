Seif 3 “Before gentiles it is nothing,” etc. The Sema understood a responsum of the Rosh to mean that an explicit undertaking to litigate before gentiles is binding. Yet the Rosh expressly writes that the customary clause “whether under Jewish law or gentile law” cannot intend to uproot Torah law; it means only that if the debtor is violent or evasive, gentile enforcement may be used. Beit din never permits voluntary submission to gentile jurisdiction merely because the clause was explicit. The same explains the Rosh’s other responsum cited in siman 61. Nor can Jewish judges award a substantive advantage granted only by gentile law, for that is a stipulation against Torah law. The Mechaber therefore correctly made no distinction.
