Permission. Baal HaTerumot writes that beit din does not permit a claimant to proceed before a non-Jewish court unless collection cannot be effected through an adrakhta issued by beit din. See the end of siman 11 regarding one who lodges a public grievance three times in order to suspend prayer, including on which day and in which synagogue the grievance is made. Sema.
And to testify. The Sema writes that the Rama derived this rule from Rav Sherira, where it is explicit that the non-Jewish judge must not be one who takes bribes and that he accepts testimony from Jews. If so, it requires study why the Rama stated the rule without qualification and did not say that the judge must not take bribes. The Shach answers that Rav Sherira certainly agrees that even before a bribe-taking judge Jews may testify if he will accept their testimony. Rav Sherira mentioned that he does not take bribes only to indicate that if he does take bribes their testimony will have no practical effect because he will not accept it. In truth, if he does accept the testimony, there is no distinction whether he takes bribes or not, since the Jewish witnesses know with certainty that the defendant is liable. Therefore the Rama omitted the qualification.
