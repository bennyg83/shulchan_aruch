He obtains permission from beit din, etc. Baal HaTerumot cites Rav Sherira: one who rebels against judgment is first given a public warning. If he still refuses, testimony is taken concerning his conduct and the claimant may collect through non-Jewish courts. Our practice is first to interrupt public prayer three times in the synagogue and only afterward permit him to proceed. Baal HaTerumot, gate 62, further writes that permission to go before non-Jewish courts is granted only if collection cannot be accomplished through an authorization against the defendant's property. I cited his language in Derishah. See Darkei Moshe and Sema at the end of siman 11 concerning interruption of public prayer.
And to testify that this one owes, etc. Baal HaTerumot cites Rav Sherira: if a person was found liable for a debt or deposit and the Jewish court cannot extract payment, and there is a non-Jewish tribunal whose judge does not accept bribes and accepts testimony from Jews, then elders and scholars may appear before that judge and testify that the debt is owed, and it is a mitzvah to do so. Even where the victim is a non-Jew and the robber is a Jew, one may testify before the judge and have him rule accordingly. This excludes places where non-Jewish courts do not accept a Jew's testimony even against another Jew because Jews are not considered credible there. Beit Yosef and Darkei Moshe cite this. It requires study why Rama states the rule without qualification here and omits the requirement that the non-Jewish judge not accept bribes.
