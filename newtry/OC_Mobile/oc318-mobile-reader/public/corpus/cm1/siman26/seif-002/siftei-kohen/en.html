And to testify that this person is liable, etc. Rav Sherira writes, as cited by Baal HaTerumot, that if a person was adjudged liable for a debt or a deposit and the creditor cannot collect from him, and there is in that place a gentile tribunal whose judge does not take bribes and accepts testimony from Jews, the elders and scholars are permitted to go before that judge and testify. In my humble opinion, Rav Sherira certainly agrees that even if the judge does take bribes, Jewish witnesses may testify before him if he in fact accepts their testimony. Rav Sherira's phrase, "who does not take bribes," means only that if he routinely takes bribes he may refuse to accept their testimony, in which case their testimony would accomplish nothing. But if he will accept their testimony, there is no distinction whether he is bribed or not, since the witnesses know truthfully that the defendant is liable. This is why the Rama did not state as a condition that the gentile judge be one who refuses bribes. The Sema's difficulty is thereby resolved. See there.
