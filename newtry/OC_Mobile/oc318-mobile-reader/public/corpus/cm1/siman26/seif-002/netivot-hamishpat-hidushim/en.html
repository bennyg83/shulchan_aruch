See Beurim, seif katan 3, that this applies specifically where beit din itself knows that the defendant is liable according to law, for example because the plaintiff holds a valid document or similar proof. If beit din does not know that the defendant owes him, such as an oral loan, beit din authorizes resort to non Jews only to compel the defendant to appear before a Jewish beit din:
And to testify that this person is liable. The Rash writes that even where a non Jew was robbed by a Jew, two Jewish witnesses may testify in the non Jewish court, since there are two of them and the judgment is truthful, Sema. An exception is where it is known that those courts accept bribes, because then there is concern that they will exceed the proper measure and impose excessive costs.
