If he refuses to come, etc. This means where it is clearly established to us that he refuses to appear. Kenesset HaGedolah writes that a single court messenger is only one witness, and because the matter has monetary consequences one witness is insufficient and two witnesses are required, as we say in Bava Kamma that for monetary matters two witnesses are necessary. I have not seen the practice follow this. Rather, when a court messenger warns him and he states that he refuses to come, beit din ordinarily authorizes the opposing party to complain against him in gentile court. Perhaps because it is not certain that this is itself a monetary matter, since the claimant may prevail under gentile law in any event, and the principal issue is the prohibition of resorting to gentile judges, where one witness is generally believed in matters of prohibition, they were lenient. Nevertheless, the ruling of Kenesset HaGedolah is reasonable and should be taken into account: beit din should not issue such permission except on the report of two court messengers.
He obtains permission from beit din. If beit din has sufficient power to issue an order of execution against the debtor's property, permission to proceed in gentile court is not granted. Sema. This is correct.
And to testify that this person owes, etc. Rav Sherira Gaon writes that even where the victim is a gentile and the robber is a Jew, two Jewish witnesses may testify, since they are two and the resulting judgment is true. Sema. See Tumim, where I concluded that ordinarily they may testify unless it is known that the gentile judges accept bribes. In that circumstance there is concern that they will exceed the proper amount and impose excessive costs, because a bribe blinds, and it is therefore forbidden to testify.
