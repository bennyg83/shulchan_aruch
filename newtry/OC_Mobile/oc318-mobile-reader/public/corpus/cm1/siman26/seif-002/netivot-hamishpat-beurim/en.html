And he saves through gentile law. See Sema. Jewish beit din gives him permission to recover what is already known to be his; it does not authorize the gentile court to create a new substantive obligation. Therefore the case must be one where the debt is already established before Jewish judges, such as a written loan. With an oral claim, Jewish beit din cannot authorize direct extraction through secular court because perhaps the defendant does not owe the claimed amount. Rather, if he refuses to appear, he is placed under ban. Beit Yosef's language about inability to collect through a foreclosure authorization confirms that the debt has already been judicially established.
