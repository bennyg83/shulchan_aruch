It is prohibited to litigate before non-Jewish courts. Regarding an attachment or seizure imposed through non-Jewish authorities, it appears there is less fault if one goes to their court merely to free himself from that attachment. Initially it is certainly proper to obtain permission from a proper beit din. If, according to beit din, he would otherwise be required to swear in order to escape an attachment that has already taken effect, it is appropriate to permit him to approach the authority from which the attachment originated and seek relief. Even if he acted without prior permission from beit din, this is not quite comparable to voluntarily litigating under non-Jewish law, because he is merely going there to rescue himself from their hold. This is where he remains willing to obey a Torah-law judgment in his own city.
