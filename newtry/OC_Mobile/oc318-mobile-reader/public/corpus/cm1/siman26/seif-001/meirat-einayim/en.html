It is prohibited to litigate before non-Jewish judges. Terumat HaDeshen writes that even where a person would be allowed to take the law into his own hands, he may not do so through non-Jewish authorities. Darkei Moshe cites this, and Rama wrote it above in siman 4.
Even where they judge according to Jewish law. This is learned from, 'These are the judgments that you shall place before them,' expounded as before Jewish judges and not before non-Jewish courts. Thus even if the foreign court would apply the same substantive rule, one may not submit to its jurisdiction.
It is as though he reviled and blasphemed. This is derived from the verse, 'for their rock is not like our Rock, and our enemies are judges,' as Rashi and Ramban explain at the beginning of Mishpatim.
And he raised his hand against the Torah of Moshe our teacher. By abandoning Jewish judges and choosing non-Jewish courts, he acts as though the Torah's law and judgments are not true, Heaven forbid.
Only that he coerces him through non-Jews, etc. Even when the defendant is violent and refuses to obey Jewish law, the claimant should first go before Jewish judges and obtain permission. They can then authorize him to recover his property through the foreign system. According to the Rosh and Tur, it is not necessary that the foreign authority merely compel the defendant to do exactly what Jewish judges said; once permission is given, recovery through that forum may be used as needed.
Some say we still attend to his claim. See Hagahot Darkei Moshe.
Unless he caused a loss to his opponent. The phrase is not necessarily limited to direct loss. The same applies if the plaintiff already collected according to the foreign judgment, even if under Jewish law he would have been entitled to more. He is not then heard to demand a completely new adjudication merely to increase his recovery.
