Intercourse promiscuously, not for the sake of kiddushin, has no legal effect. Tur writes that he receives lashes because of the prohibition 'there shall not be a prostitute,' following Rambam at the beginning of Hilchot Ishut. Raavad objects that if so, why does a seducer pay the statutory fine, since he should instead receive lashes? Rather, Raavad holds that the Torah prohibition of kedeshah applies only to a woman who makes herself available to everyone. A woman who designates herself to one man does not incur lashes. Maggid Mishneh writes that intercourse with an unmarried woman may nevertheless violate a positive commandment. In any event, even according to Raavad, intercourse with an unmarried woman is at least rabbinically prohibited. The beit din of David decreed even against seclusion with an unmarried woman, and gazing at her is forbidden by Scripture, as the verse says, 'I made a covenant with my eyes, why should I contemplate a maiden?' See Rivash, siman 425. Terumat HaDeshen, siman 209, writes that by Torah law there is no prohibition of promiscuous relations with an unmarried woman.
