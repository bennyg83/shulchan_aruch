That caused a loss. Nimukei Maharash of Riesenburg explains that the case concerns one who was sued for bodily injury in gentile court and had already been punished there by a fine. Jewish beit din does not then adjudicate him a second time for the same matter. Sema extends this to ordinary monetary matters where payment was already extracted. Once an operative act has already occurred through the gentile tribunal, beit din does not retry the same claim.
The first reasoning appears primary. Urim VeTumim gives two reasons for not rehearing the case: first, it resembles accepting a disqualified judge and being unable to retract after final judgment; second, it is a penalty for having gone before a gentile tribunal. A practical difference arises where the claimant lost only because the opposing party bribed the gentile judge to distort justice. Under the first rationale, an erroneous judgment by an accepted disqualified judge can be corrected in the proper case; under the second rationale, beit din still refuses to entertain the claim as a penalty. Since the decisors state the rule without qualification, the second rationale appears primary. Nevertheless, if the litigant himself knows that he truly owes money under Torah law, he must pay. If he is uncertain, he must submit the matter to Jewish beit din so that doubtful theft does not remain in his possession.
