It is forbidden to adjudicate. Even where one may lawfully enforce his own right, he may not do so through non Jewish authorities, Sema:
And in their courts. The custom is to compel payment of taxes and levies through non Jews, Maharik, responsum 6:
Even where they judge according to Jewish law. The verse states, "And these are the laws that you shall place before them," which the Sages expound as before Jewish judges and not before non Jews. The word "these" indicates that this prohibition applies even where the substantive rule they would apply is the same as Jewish law:
And he raised his hand against the Torah of Moshe. By resorting to them he demonstrates, Heaven forbid, as though the Torah of Moshe were untrue or incapable of bringing the law to light, and therefore he turns instead to non Jews who are skilled in the civil law of the kingdom that rests on human reason:
Unless they compelled him through non Jews. This means without permission of beit din. If he first went to beit din and claimed that the other party is violent and cannot be compelled, it is explained below that beit din may grant permission. This is obvious, Sema:
And the first opinion appears primary. See Beurim, seif katan 2, that even if he incurred liability only because his opponent bribed the foreign judge to distort justice, beit din will still not assist him in recovering what he lost.
