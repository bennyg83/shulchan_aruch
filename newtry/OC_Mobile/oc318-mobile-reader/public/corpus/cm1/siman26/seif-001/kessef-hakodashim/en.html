Seif 1. In the words of the decisors. The Urim VeTumim in siman 25 writes that a ruling explicit in the Shulchan Aruch has the status of a matter stated in the Mishnah. Nevertheless, many rulings were fixed in the Shulchan Aruch because they reflected accepted practice rather than because the underlying issue was conclusively decided by an intrinsic proof or a clearly established majority. Such rulings may still retain the character of judicial discretion in a case of error. By contrast, where the Beit Yosef deliberately fixed the law according to his three principal pillars of halachic authority, that may be comparable to a majority and thus to an explicit settled rule. The same may be true where the Rama rules according to the great authorities of these lands, and where Terumat HaDeshen establishes a general rule that Tosafot are followed in a given class of cases even against the Rif. Those broader and intentionally adopted rules are more appropriately treated like a matter stated in the Mishnah.
