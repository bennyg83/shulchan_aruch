One who accepts upon himself. It is not only in such a case, which involves a prohibition. Even regarding a permitted matter this is merely a kinyan on words, as stated at the beginning of Bava Batra 3a.
And if he accepted, etc., he is obligated. Nedarim 16a: "there is a stringency of vows, etc.; a sukkah that I make, etc.; with vows it is forbidden." The Rif writes in a responsum that this means that if he accepted upon himself to give charity if he performs this mitzvah, then when he performs it the prohibition of the vow takes effect upon him and he is obligated to give the charity.
And some say that there is no. So it appears there, since it says "it is forbidden" and does not say "he is obligated." The Beit Yosef in Bedek HaBayit disagrees with him and with his interpretation. This is why the first view was stated initially, that he is obligated to give, etc.
