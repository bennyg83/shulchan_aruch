There: “Beit din does not extract it from him” The Sema connects this to a dispute about compelling charity, and my revered father-in-law says coercion is impossible because the person may say he will give to the poor whenever he chooses. This is incorrect. Although some hold ordinary charity cannot be physically compelled because its reward is stated alongside it, a verbal pledge to give charity is a vow and is fully enforceable. Rosh Hashana expressly derives judicial coercion from “You shall observe and perform,” and includes charity under “what you uttered.” Sefer HaTerumot’s statement that beit din does not extract the penalty means instead that there may be no complete vow, since the person could theoretically go to gentile court, although beit din would prevent him. He is informed that his undertaking may have taken effect because he presumably intended to avoid the prohibition and accept the penalty; the true intent is known before Heaven. No authority disputes this ruling.
