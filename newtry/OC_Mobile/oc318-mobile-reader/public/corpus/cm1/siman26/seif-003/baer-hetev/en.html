And it is forbidden. The Rosh writes in a responsum that even where a document states that disputes shall be adjudicated under non-Jewish law, the intent is only that if the debtor is violent and refuses to obey Jewish law, he may be compelled through non-Jewish law, not that one may violate Torah law. This implies that if the document explicitly states that a person personally undertakes to litigate before them in all events, then where the creditor gains no substantive right from this undertaking the kinyan does not take effect, for it is merely a kinyan on words. But if the creditor does gain a substantive advantage, the obligor may not retract, because the kinyan takes effect upon his property, which was encumbered to secure the right he accepted to litigate under non-Jewish law. No one disputes this. It is therefore difficult why the Mechaber rules without distinction here and in siman 22 and does not say that where the creditor gains a substantive right the undertaking must be honored. See also siman 61 for the Sema's further discussion, and the responsa cited there.
