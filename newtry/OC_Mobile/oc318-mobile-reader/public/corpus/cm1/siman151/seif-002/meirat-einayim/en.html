This is explained in the Tur, Even HaEzer. See siman 85, and the Mechaber there, seif 9. If a wife purchased land from her husband with concealed money, her purchase is ineffective, because the husband may say: I sold it only in order to induce you to reveal money that you had taken from me. If she purchased with money that was openly hers, the purchase is valid. The Tur states the same distinction here. In the Perishah I explained why the Tur makes this distinction regarding a purchase from the husband but not regarding a loan from the wife, as the Mechaber stated without qualification above in siman 127. It follows that if she has witnesses or a document proving that her husband gave her this field as a gift, she acquires it in every case, because the rationale concerning inducing disclosure of concealed funds does not apply. For this reason the Tur and Mechaber precisely speak here of property that he "sold" to her. In the preceding cases of a robber and a husband dealing with his wife's property, they also mention gifts where appropriate. See the Perishah.
