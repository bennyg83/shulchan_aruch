With property warranty, some say he acquires. The Tur explains: a robber who does not kill over monetary matters but has merely been established as a robber concerning this particular field may establish possession in another field, because people fear protesting only concerning the field for which he is known to risk himself. Likewise, they do not fear protesting merely against an encumbrance or warranty obligation, because such a lien is comparable to another field. Therefore, if he writes a warranty obligation, it is effective. One who is known to kill over money, however, is a robber for all purposes, and a written warranty is ineffective because it too is presumed to have been given under coercion. Rama states simply that some say warranty is effective because his gloss refers to the Mechaber's case of one known to have robbed only this particular field.
They remove the field from the robber, etc. This is the Rambam's view: where a person is already established as a robber concerning this very field, the victim need not have delivered a prior protest. The Tur explains the Rambam's distinction. This differs from a person who coerces another to sell a field before he himself has taken anything. That coercer is seeking a sale rather than outright robbery, and therefore if the victim fails to issue a protest the sale may stand. But one already established as having robbed this field is treated differently, and his purported purchase is void even without a protest. My master the Rosh disagrees. See the Derishah here and the extended discussion in siman 205 concerning the basis of this dispute in the Gemara.
