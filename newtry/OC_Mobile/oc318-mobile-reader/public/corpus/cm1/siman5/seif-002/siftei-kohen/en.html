They do not hold court on Erev Shabbos. Only adjudication is prohibited. The judges may hear the litigants' claims and issue the ruling after Shabbos or Yom Tov. So writes the Ran at the beginning of Ketubot. However, a litigant cannot be compelled to appear then, as the Sema writes in seif katan 3.
They do not penalize him. He may excuse himself by saying that because of preparations for Shabbos he forgot that he had been summoned. These are the Sema's words.
During Nisan and Tishrei. This means throughout Nisan and Tishrei until after the festival, because of the burdens of festival preparation. Afterward, however, a date is set. This is implied by Hagahot Maimoniyot, Mordechai, and Rama's words below, 'I set them a date after the festival.' The Sema writes that this applies specifically to Nisan and Tishrei, but in Sivan a date may be set because Shavuot lasts only one day in Eretz Yisrael and two days outside it. On Erev Shavuot it is obvious that no date is set, since it is Erev Yom Tov. Ir Shushan writes likewise.
They do not adjudicate at night. Responsa Maharam, siman 28, states that if both parties consent to be judged at night, it is permitted, for this is no worse than accepting a relative or otherwise disqualified judge. The Sema and Bach cite this. The Sema further explains the custom to begin a case at night on the ground that lamps are lit. He writes that he found something similar in an ancient compilation, that a symbolic act of acquisition may not be performed at night except by lamp or moonlight, so that the witnesses clearly recognize the person making the acquisition. I do not understand how he brings a proof that contradicts his position. The Gemara in Bava Batra explicitly states that a symbolic act of acquisition may be performed at night, whereas adjudication may not. This is also clear from Rashbam and Tosafot there, cited by Beit Yosef at the beginning of siman 253. Even the Tur, who disputes Rashbam's explanation there, clearly agrees in law that a symbolic act of acquisition may be performed at night while adjudication may not, as proven by the Gemara and by the Bach there. Responsa Masat Binyamin, siman 78, also rules that an acquisition may be effected at night while adjudication may not. Therefore, if the old compilation requires light even for an acquisition, it follows that adjudication remains prohibited even by lamplight. This appears correct and is implied by the unqualified language of the authorities.
Some say that if they transgressed and judged, etc. The Bach rules likewise. Ramban, however, writes that their judgment is invalid, as do the Tur at the beginning of siman 253, the Ran, and the Rosh. Beit Yosef also implies this. This appears to me the primary ruling, that their judgment is invalid.
