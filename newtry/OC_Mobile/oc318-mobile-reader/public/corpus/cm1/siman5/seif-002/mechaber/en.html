Cases are not heard on erev Shabbos or erev Yom Tov. If a litigant was summoned to appear then, he need not appear; even if he was summoned then to appear after Shabbos or Yom Tov and failed to come, he is not penalized. {Rama: Some write that nowadays cases are heard on erev Shabbos and erev Yom Tov because otherwise teachers would lose study time. Leniency applies only to monetary matters, for beit din has authority to declare property ownerless, but not to matters of prohibition (Terumat HaDeshen, siman 224, and Hagahos Semak). It appears to me that this is permitted only occasionally, not as a regular arrangement; a beit din should not schedule regular sessions on erev Shabbos or erev Yom Tov. This is the practice.} During Nisan and Tishrei, a person who is out of town is not given a deadline to return for litigation. If he was summoned during Nisan or Tishrei to appear afterward and fails to come, he is penalized. {Rama: Some say that a public complaint should not be lodged in the synagogue during Nisan or the Days of Awe, even against villagers who came to complete a minyan. Instead, a date is fixed for them after the festival. If delay or deceit is involved, the case is heard immediately (Mordechai, beginning of chapter HaGozel Batra, and Hagahos Maimoniyos). In some places the custom is that if litigation began before Nisan or Tishrei, deadlines are fixed even during those months; where this is the custom, it is followed (Piskei Maharai, siman 207).} The initial proceedings may not be held at night. If a monetary case began by day, it may be concluded at night. {Rama: Some say that if judges transgressed and adjudicated at night, their ruling is valid (Rashbam). See also siman 50.}
