That one may not lodge a complaint. See Baer Heitev, who refers to the end of Orach Chaim, siman 545, where the Mechaber states the rule without qualification. The Rama also writes in Orach Chaim, siman 539, seif 3, that one may lodge a legal complaint in order to collect debts. Magen Avraham there cites the Sema's question: how is this permitted when one does not set court dates in Nisan and Tishrei? He answers that the Sema did not see the Mordechai, from which it is evident that the case concerns litigation in a non-Jewish court, and therefore the Rama carefully wrote “to lodge a complaint in court.” Shevut Yaakov, part III, siman 139, objects that the Sema's question was directed to siman 545, where the ruling simply states that one may lodge a complaint for debts during Chol HaMoed, which implies even before a Jewish beit din. Eliyah Rabbah raises the same objection. Machatzit HaShekel and Pri Megadim, however, defend Magen Avraham, explaining that the Mechaber's ruling at the end of siman 545 likewise comes from that Mordechai and refers to litigation in secular courts. Urim VeTumim offers another resolution: here the case concerns a complaint requiring actual adjudication, which should not be undertaken because summonses are not issued on these days; in Orach Chaim, siman 545, by contrast, there is no dispute to adjudicate, and the creditor merely seeks to compel his debtor to pay. That may be done on the festival, since the conclusion is that cases may be judged on the festival, and if a party refuses to obey he may be placed under communal sanction. Shevut Yaakov further discusses the Rama's ruling that if there is concern of evasion or deceit one may lodge a complaint even in Nisan and Tishrei, and the Sema indicates that this is permitted even on Chol HaMoed. In a case where, once they came to court, the defendant asserted a counterclaim against the plaintiff, the plaintiff argued that the Sages said that the plaintiff's claim is addressed first, and therefore his claim should be heard now while he should need to answer the defendant only after the festival, since he is not suspected of evasion or deceit. Shevut Yaakov responds that this may be compared to the ruling in siman 6 that once beit din has undertaken a case concerning a perutah, it completes the case even regarding less than a perutah, because it is all one case. Although the Sema there follows the Rosh that the court does not complete judgment for less than a perutah, nevertheless in the present case one may be lenient during Nisan and Tishrei because the adjudication has already begun. On Chol HaMoed itself, however, where there is no concern of evasion or deceit, the plaintiff must wait regarding the counterclaim until after the festival, out of honor for the festival.
