What difference is there between a zonah, a divorcee, and a chalalah, since all are written in one verse: 'A woman who is a zonah or chalalah, and a woman divorced from her husband, they shall not take.' A female convert has the status of a zonah because she had relations while a non-Jew, as Rashi explains in Yevamot 76a. Thus a valid Kohen is prohibited from marrying a convert or a freed maidservant because of zonah. Likewise, none of the sanctity of Kehunah applies to him, such as the prohibition against becoming impure through the dead and the priestly blessing, because once you permit him to marry a convert, who is a zonah under the verse, the same applies to the other mitzvot of Kehunah, and he is like an ordinary non-Kohen. This is difficult, however, because Rambam, in chapter 7 of Hilchot Biat HaMikdash, lists the bodily defects that disqualify Kohanim and includes twelve defects of the reproductive organs, among them one with crushed testicles. He writes there that a Kohen with a bodily defect may eat kodshei kodashim, implying that he is not like a non-Kohen. This is also an explicit verse concerning one with crushed testicles, translated as one whose testicles are crushed, followed by, 'from the most sacred offerings he may eat.' Although the Mishnah at the beginning of chapter HaArel states that a Kohen who is a petzua daka may eat terumah, terumah is different, because even slaves may eat terumah, as stated there at the beginning of the chapter.
