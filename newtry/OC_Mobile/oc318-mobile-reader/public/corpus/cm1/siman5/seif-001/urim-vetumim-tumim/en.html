It is forbidden to adjudicate on Shabbat and Yom Tov, etc. In Beitzah 36b the Gemara asks: "One may not judge", but is he not performing a mitzvah? It answers that the case is one where there is another judge more suitable than he. Tosafot explain, according to Rashi, that the Gemara's question is only why judging is listed among activities classified as discretionary rather than mitzvah-acts. Even where there is no better judge available, the act nevertheless remains prohibited on Shabbat. Rabbeinu Tam explains differently. The Gemara's question is substantive: why should judging be prohibited when it is a mitzvah? The answer is that another judge more suitable than he is available. According to Rabbeinu Tam, where there is no one better available, judging is in fact permitted. Rabbeinu Tam gives the same explanation regarding kiddushin. Rama, Orach Chaim siman 339, cites Rabbeinu Tam's opinion and rules that in a pressing situation one may rely upon it. Even entry into marriage can be permitted where the man has no wife and children, because he is thereby fulfilling the mitzvah of procreation. It follows that the same principle applies to judgment. In an urgent situation, for example when witnesses become dangerously ill on Shabbat or Yom Tov and may die, beit din may accept their testimony even though acceptance of testimony has the law of judgment. This should apply particularly where the court involved is the highest beit din in that city, so that there is no more suitable court available. Indeed, Rama in responsum 124 appears to hold that even Rashi agrees with Rabbeinu Tam regarding actual judgment. His version of the Gemara did not apparently read Rashi as explaining the question on "one may not judge" itself, but only on the later question concerning kiddushin. In our printed text, however, Rashi does explain the question concerning judging in the same manner. In any event, there is stronger reason for stringency in kiddushin than in judgment. Kiddushin involves not only concern that one may write, but also the problem of making a kinyan on Shabbat. Judgment does not contain that additional issue.
