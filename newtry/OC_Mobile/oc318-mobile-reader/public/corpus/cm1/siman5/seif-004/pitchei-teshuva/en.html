In Nisan and Tishrei. The Shach writes that this applies only before the festival, but after the festival a date for litigation may be set. Maharshal, in HaGozel, siman 17, writes likewise. Gidulei Terumah, page 23, cited by Knesset HaGedolah, however, writes that the entire month is included. Responsa Shevut Yaakov, part I, siman 139, likewise writes that this is the implication of the Gemara, Rambam, and Tur, that the entirety of Nisan and Tishrei is included. The proof Maharshal and the Shach bring from the Mordechai and Hagahot Maimoniyot, cited in the Rama nearby, that villagers who came to the city for judgment are given a date after the festival, is no proof. There it is different, because they are already in that city for the gathering, and therefore a date is certainly set for them even in Nisan or Tishrei. One does not, however, summon a person to another city during the entire month. Urim VeTumim, seif katan 5, likewise writes that it is reasonable that the entire month is included, since the burdens of the festival still occupy him. Knesset HaGedolah writes that a groom is not summoned for twenty one days, seven days before the wedding, the seven days of the wedding, and seven days after it. Shevut Yaakov, part I, siman 139, writes instead only three days before the wedding and the wedding day itself. Urim VeTumim disagrees and holds that a groom is given the seven days of celebration and three days before the marriage; Netivot HaMishpat agrees. Regarding the father of a child at a brit milah, as well as other celebrations and mitzvah meals, Shevut Yaakov writes that everything depends upon local custom and the extent of their preoccupation, according to the judge's assessment. Ateret Tzvi, seif katan 4, writes that the father of the child is not summoned throughout the eight days until the circumcision and also on the following day, and states that this was his practice.
