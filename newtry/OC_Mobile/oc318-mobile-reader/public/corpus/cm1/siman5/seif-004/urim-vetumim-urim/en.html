From the beginning of the seventh hour, etc. Sema explains that immediately after six hours the sun begins inclining westward and the Torah time of Minchah begins, although the Sages delayed actual prayer until half an hour into the seventh hour to avoid error. This explanation is unnecessary. Even if the Torah time of Minchah itself begins only after half an hour, the Sages can require a person to refrain from entering judgment beforehand as a protective buffer, just as they instituted advance safeguards for Minchah Ketanah even for those familiar with the time.
Even to conclude the judgment, etc. Rama writes in Orach Chaim 232 that because communities now summon people to synagogue through an attendant, the custom permits beginning a meal even close to Minchah Ketanah, since there is little fear that the prayer time will be missed. The same applies to sitting in judgment. This explains the established practice of batei din in these lands to convene close even to Minchah Ketanah, relying on the synagogue summons. The accepted custom therefore has halachic basis.
