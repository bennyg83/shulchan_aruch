A Torah scholar who brings merchandise, etc., to prevent everyone else from selling. Knesset HaGedolah cites Rama, Responsa siman 9, that when the seller competing with the Torah scholar is a non-Jew, the community may enact that Jews not purchase from the non-Jew until the scholar has sold his goods. Rama further derives from the Sifra that, even apart from the scholar's special priority, a Jew should prefer buying from and selling to a fellow Jew, even where the non-Jew offers a cheaper price. Knesset HaGedolah asks how this fits the rule that where non-Jews also sell the merchandise and the scholar gains no practical advantage, others may sell as well. He answers that the latter case concerns merchandise purchased by both Jews and non-Jews. A ban on Jewish buyers would then injure Jewish merchants without materially benefiting the scholar because non-Jewish buyers remain in the market. Rama's case involved books, whose market was essentially Jewish, so a temporary restriction on buying from the non-Jew could effectively protect the scholar. Chatam Sofer makes a similar distinction and indicates that where Jewish and non-Jewish buyers are equally significant, one may need both to grant the scholar priority and to restrict Jewish purchases from the competing non-Jew so that the priority has real value. The matter requires further study.
