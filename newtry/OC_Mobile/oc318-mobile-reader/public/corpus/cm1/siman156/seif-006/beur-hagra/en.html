The same applies to teachers. This is stated in the Gemara. Rambam omitted it because he understood that it refers only to a resident of the same alley, which is why the Gemara says here, 'Rav Huna concedes,' whereas regarding peddlers it says, 'Rav Huna son of Rav Yehoshua concedes.' Since we do not rule like Rav Huna, the rule would have no practical effect. In chapter two of Hilkhot Talmud Torah he expressly speaks of a resident of the same alley. The Tur, however, understands that Rav Huna is mentioned to emphasize the ruling. Regarding peddlers, the Gemara may have mentioned Rav Huna son of Rav Yehoshua because even Rav Huna might not concede there: Ezra's enactment permits peddlers to circulate, but perhaps not to establish a fixed shop even in their own alley. By contrast, regarding teachers the enactment specifically permits appointing a teacher, etc.
