The residents of the alley compel him. See Sema, seif katan 16, who discusses this at length, and see the Bach, who also discusses the Rambam's view at length and disputes the Sema. His words are unconvincing, for he distinguishes between one mill and another without any source for such a distinction. See further in the Sema, where he writes that since the newcomer pays taxes with them, it is as though he lives among them, and one who lives among them has already been defined as their neighbor. This follows the lenient resolution of the Gemara's question. Yet I do not know how to explain what Rav Huna son of Rav Yehoshua was asking. Why should such a person be worse than a resident of another town who shares in the royal tax, whom the townspeople plainly cannot prevent where there is another craftsman? Certainly a resident of an alley in that same city should not be worse. This requires careful study. According to the Beit Yosef's understanding of the Rambam, and the interpretations of Rashi, the Tur, and Tosafot, the question is resolved. See the Beit Yosef in seif 6, where he writes that he examined the Raavad's glosses and did not find this statement. He was mistaken because the abbreviation in his source meant 'and Aviasaf,' but he thought it meant 'and the Raavad says.' The statement does not appear in the Raavad's glosses. It is explicitly cited in the Mordechai and Hagahot Maimoniyot in the name of Aviasaf, and the Beit Yosef himself cites them later. So too in the newer printed Hagahot Maimoniyot. See also the work Ateret Zekenim, page 88, whose discussion appears inaccurate.
