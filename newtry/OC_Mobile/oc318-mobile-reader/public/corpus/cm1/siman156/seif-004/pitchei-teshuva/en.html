They may prevent him. Responsa Nachalat Shivah II, siman 2, explains that residents may exclude an outsider only where no member of the local community derives benefit from his activity. If the outsider forms a genuine partnership with a local resident, the others cannot necessarily prevent it. Even communities that enacted rules against partnership with outsiders did so where the local person could reasonably find a partner within the community. Such a rule cannot be applied so as to deprive a resident of any possibility of earning a living where no local capital is available. In the case discussed there, foreign merchants bought fattened cattle and employed local Jewish brokers, and the community could not prevent the arrangement merely because the merchants were outsiders. If, however, the foreign buyers seek an animal suitable for local slaughter while it is physically within the settlement, the broker should first offer local residents the opportunity to purchase it for slaughter and receive his normal brokerage fee. If the animal is outside the settlement, even that obligation does not apply.
