I heard that Netivot HaMishpat writes concerning the rule in siman 156 that a person must pay the city ruler's tax in order to compete in local trade, that this should mean a payment specifically imposed for the right to conduct commerce. As a matter of law, such a requirement exists only where the city's original civic arrangement imposed a commercial contribution, so that one who pays it has priority over one who does not. Ordinarily, however, there is no inherent separate payment to the city ruler merely because a person conducts business. The Sages list the recognized head taxes and similar civic charges but do not describe a universal commercial payment of this kind. If a person pays the residential city tax customarily imposed upon those who dwell in houses there, he is already a resident of that city and need not establish residency again through a separate trading payment. Only where the ruler or government expressly established a commercial tax can one say that the law of the kingdom is law and that local commerce may be denied to one who refuses to pay it. A person who previously did not engage in commerce may begin to pay the commercial charge and then trade; an established neighbor cannot object merely because he is a newcomer to that line of business. By contrast, if he has never paid the ordinary contribution imposed upon residents of the city, his offer to begin paying now does not necessarily defeat the existing resident's objection. The neighbor may be entitled to prevent him from becoming a resident for the purpose of competing in the local market until he properly acquires resident status under the city's established rules.
