My son is out. It is written that “to the end of the sign, everything is the tongue of the Rambam. He said, “And he shall give him a hand, and he shall be given to him, and he shall speak to him.”
For he established a right, etc., and this damage, etc. The Yerushalmi teaches: regarding one's neighbor, he may retract; once he accepted it upon himself, he may not retract. Rabban Shimon ben Gamliel says that even after accepting it he may retract, because he can say, 'They come and go,' etc., and increase traffic upon us. It relates Rabban Shimon ben Gamliel's view to R. Meir, who holds that a wife may say, 'I thought I could tolerate it,' etc. The law follows the Sages, as it does in Ketubot. From this the ruling in siman 155, seif 36 follows, that if he made a formal acquisition, etc. But absent such an acquisition, mere silence is not waiver, for the Yerushalmi says he may retract. The same is true of defects that existed before marriage and were met with silence. The Yerushalmi also indicates that damage caused by people entering and leaving is among the forms of damage for which no prescriptive right is acquired. See there.
