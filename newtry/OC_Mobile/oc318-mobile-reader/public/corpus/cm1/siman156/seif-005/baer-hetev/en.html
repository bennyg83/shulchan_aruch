The Shach writes that the Bach likewise discusses the matter at length to explain the Rambam's position and challenges the Sema, but his words do not appear correct to one who examines them, for he distinguishes between one type of mill and another without a source for that distinction. See there, and see what the Taz writes on this matter. One who wishes to establish an animal-powered mill to grind wheat for all customers, and whose fellow objects, must heed the objector, even if he has already established the use. Mahari Adari, siman 275, and Maharashach, part 1, siman 33. The compiler adds: if he grinds only for himself and does not increase foot traffic, then according to the Rashba and those who follow him, the neighbor cannot prevent him even if he has not established a chazakah. This likewise emerges from the cited authorities. See Maharik, shoresh 132; Responsa Radach, bayit 31, cheder 3; and Mishpetei Shmuel, simanim 101, 102, and 112. Knesset HaGedolah.
