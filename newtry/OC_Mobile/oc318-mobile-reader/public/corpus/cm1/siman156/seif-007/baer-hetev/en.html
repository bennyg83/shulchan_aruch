Their livelihood. The Tur states that they may sell enough to earn what is necessary for their livelihood. Rambam altered the wording of the Gemara, which says that they may sell "enough for their sustenance." Some infer that this means they may support themselves from the profit, for otherwise, once the principal is exhausted, they would have nothing on which to live. Rambam, however, writes only "enough for their livelihood," from which there is no basis to infer that they are to live from it indefinitely. It therefore appears that in his view they may sell only enough of the principal to provide their livelihood. This may also explain why the Mechaber did not write that it must come from the profit. Rama, however, immediately rules like the Tur with respect to a loan, writing that one may borrow until he earns enough, and there is no reason to distinguish between borrowing and selling merchandise. So writes the Sema. The Shach, however, writes that in his view Rama follows the Beit Yosef, who explains that Rambam's wording may also be understood like that of the Gemara. See there.
Their debt. This wording implies that they owe money to others and wish to pay it, and they may remain there until they make payment. Otherwise it should have said, "until they collect their debt." Accordingly, when the text states, "if they have a loan," it means a loan that is owed by them. The Tur, however, writes, "until they collect their debt." And they may. Mordechai, at the end of chapter HaGozel Batra, rules that if the local ruler says on his own initiative, "I do not want any Jew to live here except with Reuven's permission," Reuven may then prevent anyone from coming to reside there. This implies, however, that Reuven himself is forbidden to seek such an arrangement from the ruler. This also appears from Maharik, shoresh 173. He concludes there that if the ruler's original law was enacted only for the ruler's own benefit and not for Reuven's benefit, so that no other Jew may reside there without the ruler's permission, Reuven cannot object to another person's residence even if it causes him a loss.
