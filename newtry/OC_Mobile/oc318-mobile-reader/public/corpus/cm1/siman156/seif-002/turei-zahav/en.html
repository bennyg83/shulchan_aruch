[Seif 5, even if he was from another alley]. This is astonishing, for in any event he pays the king's tax. Why does the latter clause count it as an advantage that he pays the king's tax together with them, when the first clause also concerns such a person? Necessarily, the principal novelty of the latter clause is that he pays together with the residents of that alley the particular royal tax assessed upon that alley itself. Accordingly, it appears that the many authorities cited by Rama concede that in this situation he may enter even an alley already occupied by members of that trade. Those authorities are Rashi and Tosafot. They explain the Gemara's statement, “if he belongs to the local tax,” as referring to the tax due to the ruler of this city, although he comes from another city. In that case the Gemara takes it as obvious that he may enter the city, though not that particular alley. If he already belongs to another alley in the city, his position is stronger and he may enter even that alley. But if he pays the special tax belonging to that very alley, it appears that they too concede that he has full rights there.
