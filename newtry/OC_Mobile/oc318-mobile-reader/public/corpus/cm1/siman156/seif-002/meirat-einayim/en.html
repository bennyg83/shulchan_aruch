But they cannot protest, etc. This is because he already established the use. It differs from siman 155:15, where one pounds grain in his own premises and neighbors may protest, and even chazakah does not help, as the Tur writes there at the end of the siman, seif 28. There the noise is so great that it shakes the wall and constitutes substantial physical damage.
From the sound of the hammer, etc. In the Perishah and Derishah I explained that because he strikes the hammer inside his own house or shop, neighbors cannot prevent a person from ordinary activity within his own premises. The earlier cases are different, because the entrants, exits, and their noise occur in the courtyard jointly owned by all, and they may protest against damage inflicted in the shared courtyard. See seif 4 below, where the Mechaber repeats these two rules: chazakah helps as to the trade itself, but not as to increased traffic.
Since he has already established it. Maggid Mishneh infers that Rambam allows it specifically after chazakah. This chazakah does not appear to mean three years, but simply that he visibly conducted the activity, the neighbor knew and heard, and did not protest. That is Rambam's rule for chazakah in nuisances, as the Tur and Mechaber cite in simanim 153, 154, and 155.
