For its produce. Since he did not say, 'The produce of such-and-such dovecote,' or, 'The doves that will be born to me this year I sell to you,' we infer that he intended to transfer them in a legally effective manner, as though he had said, 'The dovecote is sold to you for the doves that will be born in it,' and similarly a beehive for its honey. Lest one say that even such wording is ineffective because the object is not yet in existence, since the doves are not born from the substance of the dovecote as fruit grows from a tree, the text compares it to a water channel, where the fish likewise are not born from the body of the water but grow within it, yet they are sold just as a tree may be sold for its fruit. Sema. Taz writes that in truth the case is where he says explicitly 'that will be born,' and nevertheless it is not considered an acquisition of something not yet in existence because he mentioned the beehive and the dovecote.
