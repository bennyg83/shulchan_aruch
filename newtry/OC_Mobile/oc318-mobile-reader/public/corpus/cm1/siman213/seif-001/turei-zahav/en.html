(One who sells the produce of a dovecote, and so forth.) The Sema explains that this is effective because the seller did not say, “the produce of a particular dovecote,” or, “the doves that will be born,” and so forth. This is difficult, for in the chapter HaSefinah Rashbam explains the case as one who says, “the produce of this year, whatever will be born each month.” Thus, the law indeed concerns one who uses such language. It appears to me that the case is certainly one where he says, “the doves that will be born,” yet it is still not regarded as something not yet in existence because he mentioned the beehive or dovecote, which encompasses everything within it. He has thus transferred all the doves within it, just as a water channel encompasses whatever will be trapped in it. Likewise, when one says, “the produce of the dovecote that will be born,” it is as though he says, “I sell you the doves for their produce.” Alternatively, he says, “I sell you the dovecote for its produce,” like the law of a tree for its fruit above. The novelty is that although the doves are not part of the dovecote, and one might have thought that the expression “the dovecote for its produce” is ineffective, it teaches that it is effective just like a tree.
