Rather, he sells a dovecote for its offspring, etc. The Beit Yosef explains that the dovecote is subordinate to its offspring and is therefore presumed to be sold together with them, unlike a tree, which is not subordinate to the fruit growing from it. In the Perishah I distinguished differently. Since he did not say, “I sell you the offspring of such and such dovecote,” or “the pigeons that will be born to me this year,” his language indicates that he intended to transfer the future pigeons in an effective manner, as though he had said, “The dovecote is sold to you for the pigeons that will be born in it,” and likewise a beehive for its honey. Lest one say that even an express sale of the dovecote for future pigeons is ineffective because the pigeons are not born from the substance of the dovecote, unlike fruit that grows from a tree, the Rambam compares it to selling a water channel for its fish. The fish too are not born from the water itself, but grow within it, yet they are sold just as one sells a tree for its fruit. This is why the Rambam and Mechaber first compare it to the law of selling a water channel and then conclude, “like one who sells a tree for its fruit,” without adding “and like.” Their meaning is that both cases share the same principle.
