So long as they have not flown. Meaning, so long as the chicks have not yet flown. If they have flown, the prohibition of "do not take the mother upon the young" no longer applies, even if the mother has not risen from over them. In chapter Shiluach HaKen we derive that chicks are analogous to eggs: just as eggs do not fly, so the verse concerns chicks that have not yet flown. Rambam writes this in chapter 13 of Hilchot Shechitah, and the Tur in Yoreh Deah siman 292. See there.
He taps upon the dovecote, etc. Darkei Moshe writes that Rashi's explanation implies that even if the mother rose from them once, he acquires the brood, unlike what appears from the Maggid Mishneh in chapter 23 of Hilchot Mechirah. See the Derishah.
Until the mothers fly away. The same law applies if the chicks themselves fly. The case here concerns small chicks incapable of flight even if one taps on the nest, similar to eggs in the nest. For them there is no solution until the mother flies away. As soon as the mothers fly off, his courtyard acquires the chicks and eggs for him. Thereafter, even if the mother returns and sits upon them, he may take the mother together with the young. Although Scripture says "you shall surely send," and Chazal expound this to require sending even one hundred times, that applies where the nest is not in his courtyard. There, even if the mother flies off, so long as he has not taken the nest into his hand and acquired the young, they are not considered prepared for him, and the prohibition returns when the mother comes back. In his own courtyard, however, the instant the mother flies away, his courtyard acquires the nest for him and it becomes prepared property. The verse says "if a bird's nest happens before you," excluding that which is already prepared. See the Perishah for further discussion.
