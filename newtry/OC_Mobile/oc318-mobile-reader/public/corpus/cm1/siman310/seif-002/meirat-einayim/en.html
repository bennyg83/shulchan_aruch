The Beit Yosef nevertheless rules like the Rambam, while Darkei Moshe questions it. This is Rama's later "some disagree." The Rambam's formulation is indeed difficult. Why should saying "this donkey" create one law for riding and another for burden? In the Derishah I suggested that the Rambam's version of the Gemara read: "Rabbah bar Rav Huna said in Rav's name, one who rents it to ride upon it and it dies," and the Gemara later establishes the case where the owner said "this donkey." The phrase "to ride upon it" therefore seems deliberate. The expression "this donkey" can be understood in two opposite ways. It can benefit the owner, meaning, "I undertake only this particular animal and no replacement," or benefit the renter, meaning, "this very animal and its value are pledged to you as long as they can be used to procure a substitute." When the rental is merely for carrying a burden, if the owner says "this donkey," we ordinarily interpret it in his own favor. Death is uncommon, and if the animal becomes blind or is seized it may still remain fit to carry a burden, so there is little reason to assume he intended an added obligation. But where it is rented for riding, events such as blindness or seizure make the animal unusable for the agreed purpose. Those are realistic possibilities. We therefore interpret "this donkey" as language benefiting the renter, namely that this donkey's value is pledged so long as it can be sold or rented out to supply another. Although the owner himself stated the phrase, it is common for the owner to define the rental terms in a way that justifies the rent he charges. Thus, according to the Rambam, even though he does not explicitly state this distinction in every clause, saying "this donkey" in a riding rental can serve the renter, whereas in a burden rental it ordinarily limits the owner's obligation. This resolves the principal difficulties. See the Derishah for the full argument.
