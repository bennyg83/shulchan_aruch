(Supplement) One who rents, etc.; and in seif 2, he said to him, etc. All this is Rambam's wording. Whether an unspecified or specified donkey was rented, the owner must provide another donkey or rent one if he wishes to retain the rent. Thus he writes, 'If he did not provide another, etc.' The same applies when the animal dies; he relies on the prior wording. The distinction is that in the first case, if no replacement is provided, rent is paid for the distance traveled, while in the latter case no rent is paid. Rashi likewise explains the Mishna: 'he must provide another,' or return the rent. Rambam further understands the Gemara's mention of renting for riding to teach that with this particular donkey rented for carrying a load, the owner need neither sell the carcass nor rent another. With an unspecified donkey there is no distinction, since the Mishna itself concerns an unspecified donkey and includes carrying a load. If the owner refuses to provide another, he must return the rent. The renter may sell the carcass without beit din and act for himself, all the more so with an unspecified donkey. He may also retain other property even where the carcass is insufficient. Therefore Rambam does not repeat 'if its value is insufficient,' because the main novelty is that the principal may be consumed despite the availability of other property. Raavad, Rashba, and the other authorities disagree in three respects: they make no distinction between riding and carrying in the case of death or breakage; they hold that returning the rent is insufficient because the owner's property is obligated; and they dispute the application of 'riding' to all cases. Raavad applies it only to insanity, and Maggid Mishneh extends it to blindness, but not to seizure for royal service. Rosh's presentation is difficult because he first cites Rashi's option of returning the rent, yet later states that pulling the donkey obligates all the owner's property. Rambam's own commentary is also difficult, because there he makes no distinction between riding and carrying when the donkey dies, while he distinguishes between specified and unspecified animals in the first clause. The Gemara and Tosefta oppose that presentation, and the later commentator did not notice that Rambam retracted it in his code. (End of supplement.) See 6. See 40. See 81.
