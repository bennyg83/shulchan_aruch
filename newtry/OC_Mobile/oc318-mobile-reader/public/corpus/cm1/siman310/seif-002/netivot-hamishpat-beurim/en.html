If he rented it for a load. Sema, seif katan 9, gives a strained reconciliation of Rambam. The simpler explanation is this. Saying "this donkey" ordinarily excludes another animal. But the effect of that exclusion depends on what liability would otherwise exist. If the renter hired it for riding, then if the donkey merely becomes ill, a generic-donkey rental would require the owner, where he has another donkey, to provide a replacement. Therefore the word "this" is naturally understood to exclude that obligation in the illness case. If the donkey dies, however, the carcass is itself something that ordinarily gets sold to fund a replacement. The proceeds come directly from "this donkey." It is unreasonable to interpret the word "this" as excluding even value that derives directly from the identified animal when common practice is to use that value for replacement. We do not extend restrictive language more than necessary. By contrast, when the animal was rented for a load, even a generic-donkey rental does not require the owner to provide another animal merely because this one became ill. Therefore the word "this" has no work to do in the illness case. Its function must be to limit liability upon death, meaning the owner need not provide another animal at all and need not sell the carcass to fund one. One cannot say that "this" merely excludes an obligation to provide another donkey that the owner already owns while still requiring him to use the carcass proceeds to hire one. If the renter could sell the carcass and use the proceeds for another donkey, it would usually be more sensible for the owner simply to provide his other donkey rather than consume the capital represented by the carcass. Thus such a reading would not meaningfully limit anything. Therefore, in the load case, "this donkey" excludes replacement liability entirely upon death, while in the riding case its narrower function is to exclude replacement during illness.
