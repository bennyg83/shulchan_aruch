And if it was accustomed, etc., the loss falls upon the renter. See Noda BiYehuda, second edition, siman 56. A rabbi asked how this ruling can be reconciled with the rule at the end of siman 307 that there is no payment for mere loss of an animal's labor. Noda BiYehuda first answers that here the renter is liable only for one additional day, the third day, because on that day he actually rode the animal. If the river detained him many days, the days when the animal stood idle beyond the first two would not be charged. He later gives a second answer and retracts in effect: where the animal initially entered the renter's possession by rental, even if rented only for a particular destination, it remains rented until returned to its owner. Because the renter knew that the river commonly rises, he should have stipulated concerning that possibility. This differs from one who locks another's animal in a room and prevents its work, who never received it by rental. It also differs from siman 307, seif 3, where after the rental term the renter returns the animal in a weakened condition from which it will recover. Once it is returned, the rental has ended. In my opinion the second answer is the principal one. The responsum printed as siman 57, which gives only the first answer, was likely written earlier, before the second explanation occurred to him.
