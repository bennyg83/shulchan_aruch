To cast it off. Meaning, for this reason there is no concern. Although the animal may fall beneath its load, the load itself will not thereby be ruined; there is only added effort and delay in reloading it. This is unlike where he rented it for riding, where one must be concerned that it may fall while on a bridge and fall into the water or into a pit, or where the load consists of glassware or similar items that will be ruined if they fall from the animal. Therefore he must provide another animal. Sema.
Another. The Tur writes that the Raavad explains this specifically where the lessor obligated himself by a kinyan; otherwise, how was he obligated to provide another animal? The Rosh writes that by drawing the donkey into his possession, the lessor's property becomes obligated to provide him with a donkey to the destination. The Rambam and Mechaber conclude that if the lessor does not provide one, the renter may sell the animal, etc. This implies that they follow neither view, for according to those views the kinyan or meshichah creates a lien upon all the lessor's property to provide another animal. According to the Rambam, however, only this animal appears to be subject to the renter's right, even where he rented "an animal" without specifying which one. The law follows the Rosh that no formal kinyan is needed, since the Rif, Rashba, Rabbeinu Yerucham, and Tur agree with the Rosh. See Responsa Ra'anach, beginning of siman 66. Ibid.
