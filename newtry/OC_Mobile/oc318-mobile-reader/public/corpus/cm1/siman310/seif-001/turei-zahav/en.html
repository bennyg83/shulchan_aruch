(Seif 2) To ride upon it. The Sema’s resolution of the Rambam’s words is difficult. Certainly, where it is not evident whether the stipulation was intended for the benefit of the owner or the renter, reason dictates that a person intended his own benefit, as is evident in siman 312, seif 7, concerning one who says, “I rent you this house.” To resolve the Tur’s objection, it appears that when one rents an animal without qualification and does not mention riding or carrying, but merely says, “Rent me this donkey,” he certainly intends to use it for every ordinary purpose, including riding, in accordance with common practice. Therefore, in the first clause, where he expressly says, “Rent me this donkey to ride upon,” the riding condition is superfluous language. It must therefore mean that this particular donkey remains obligated for that purpose even after it dies. In the latter clause, however, where he says “for carrying,” there is no superfluous language. Since he stipulated carrying specifically, we say that he intended to exclude other uses. This appears correct.
