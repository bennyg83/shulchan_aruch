a) The language of the Rambam at the beginning of chapter 5. The Maggid Mishneh writes that this is the Mishnah in the sixth chapter of Bava Metzia 78a, concerning one who hires an animal and it becomes afflicted, as explained in the Gemara there on 78b. The phrase there means that its legs became worm-eaten, which is the illness mentioned by the Rambam.
b) Explicit in the baraita there.
c) Ibid., in the Mishnah and baraita.
d) According to Shmuel's interpretation there.
e) Ibid., according to Shmuel. The Maggid Mishneh writes that Rabbeinu Chananel explains: if the royal service encountered the animal on its route of travel, so that had it not encountered them they would not have searched for it. Tosafot also write this there in the name of Rabbeinu Chananel.
f) For the lessor may say to him, 'Your fortune caused this, for had it remained at home it would not have been seized,' as Tosafot write there.
g) The Kesef Mishneh writes that in the Rambam's view, if it became ill or deranged, since it can still be used with difficulty, the renter must pay the hire. Likewise, where it is seized for royal service and will not return, if it was seized on its route of travel the lessor may say, 'Your fortune caused this.'
h) The Maggid Mishneh writes that this refers to illness or derangement, but regarding royal service the law is the same whether the animal was hired for riding or for carrying. He writes that this wording is found in the Tosefta. In my humble opinion, it is explicit in the Gemara, Bava Metzia 79a, in the name of Rabbi Shimon ben Elazar, as Rabba bar Rav Huna explains it there.
i) The Maggid Mishneh writes that this is the Rambam's explanation of the baraita's statement, 'he must provide him another donkey': this applies if the lessor wishes to receive the full hire, but if he does not seek the full hire, he need not provide another. This follows the Rambam's approach that where the animals were hired for carrying, the renter pays the full hire.
j) Ibid., law 2.
k) The Maggid Mishneh writes that the Rambam thus explains the Mishnah there, 78a: the lessor did not obligate any property other than that specific donkey, and therefore the carcass and hide of that donkey are pledged to the renter.
