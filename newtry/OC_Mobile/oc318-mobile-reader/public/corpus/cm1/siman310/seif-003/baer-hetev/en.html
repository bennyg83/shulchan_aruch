Customary. The general rule is that whenever both parties should have known of the circumstance and stipulated regarding it, or neither party should have known, the loss falls upon the lessor, because he seeks to extract the rent from the renter. The burden to stipulate therefore lies upon him, and the burden of proof rests upon the claimant. His position is inferior unless the matter was known to the renter and unknown to the lessor. In that case the renter certainly had to stipulate, and if he failed to do so he bears the loss and must pay. The same rule appears in the Gemara, chapter HaOmanin, regarding workers and employers, as the Tur and Shulchan Aruch write in siman 334. The same applies to all analogous cases. Sema.
