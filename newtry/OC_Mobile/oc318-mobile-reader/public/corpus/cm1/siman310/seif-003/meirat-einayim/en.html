To pay him rental for each and every day. At first glance it appears that he must pay the full daily rental, rather than the reduced amount paid to an idle worker, because the owner prefers that his donkey become weakened rather than that he himself become weakened by labor. Nevertheless, everything depends on the nature of the load, as the Tur writes at the beginning of siman 334 concerning a donkey driver who rented out his donkey, where he writes that he pays as an idle worker. The Rosh likewise writes this in a responsum at the end of klal 92.
If it was not ordinarily expected to rise, etc. The general rule is as follows. Whenever both parties should have known and stipulated, such as where the water ordinarily rises, or neither should have known, such as where it was not expected to rise, the loss falls on the owner, because he seeks to extract rent from the renter and therefore bears the burden of stipulating. The burden of proof rests on the claimant and his hand is on the lower side. Only where the renter knew and the owner had no reason to know is the renter clearly obligated to stipulate; if he failed to do so he bears the loss and must pay. The Gemara in chapter HaOmanin applies this principle throughout the laws of workers and employers: the worker who seeks to extract payment has the weaker hand, as the Tur and Shulchan Aruch explain below in siman 334. The same applies in all similar cases.
And the animal's food, etc. Meaning, the food for the third day during which the animal was delayed. Since the delay is the owner's loss because he failed to stipulate, as explained above, the owner must also reimburse the renter for the animal's food for that day. So writes the Tur.
