And if he intended, etc. This is the Gemara's ruling. Rif and Rabbenu Yitzchak disagree. Rif understands the two disputes to depend upon each other, following Rabbi Yochanan's explanation. Accordingly, after a negligent fall all agree that he is liable. Rabbenu Yitzchak holds that Rabbi Yehuda disputes even a case following negligent falling. In practice there is no difference, because the law does not follow Rabbi Yehuda on this point, as Rabbi Yochanan states, and as shown by the ordinary pit in the public domain, for which liability is imposed. The text therefore says, 'and if he was negligent, etc.' The term 'Rabbi Yehuda' is used because throughout the chapter the opposing view is called the Sages. See siman 410 and siman 413, seif 2.
