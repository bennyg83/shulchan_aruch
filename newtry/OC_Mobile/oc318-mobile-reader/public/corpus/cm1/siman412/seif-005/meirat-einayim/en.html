It fell to the ground and was injured. In Derishah I cited Tosafos, who establish the case as one where it fell on the ground onto which the water had spilled, so that the ground is treated like the bottom of the pit. Otherwise it would be difficult to distinguish this from one who trips in a pit and falls outside the pit, where the owner is exempt, as explained in siman 410.
The water was absorbed into the ground. Meaning, the ground was softened by the water until it became mud and mire.
And it caused injury, for now, etc. Specifically injury. If an ox died from this, the owner is exempt, because there is no harmful air and there is not an impact from a depth of ten handbreadths. As explained in siman 410, a ten-handbreadth fall is required for liability for death.
