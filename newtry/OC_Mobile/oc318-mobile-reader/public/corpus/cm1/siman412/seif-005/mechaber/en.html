If one pours water into the public domain and another person's utensils become soiled, he is exempt because the water is treated as a pit, and a pit does not impose liability for utensils, whether or not the water was abandoned. If a person slips on the water, falls, and is injured, the one who poured it is liable. Certainly, if the water was absorbed into the ground and became mud that caused damage, it is an actual pit. Liability applies even during the rainy season, when people are permitted to open drains that discharge into the public domain, because permission to discharge does not exempt one from paying for damage. All the more so in summer, when the discharge itself is unauthorized.
