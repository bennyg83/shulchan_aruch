d) Tur, seif 2, in the name of a responsum of the Rosh, klal 1, siman 2.
e) He explains there that a person may object to accepting various kinds of people as a co-occupant: some because of their importance, before whom one is embarrassed; some because of their lowly conduct, with whom one is embarrassed to live; some because they are quarrelsome; and some because they are untrustworthy. Therefore one who enters a joint residence is careful from the outset concerning with whom he will live.
f) See there. The Mechaber rules there anonymously in accordance with the Rambam, who writes that each partner may compel the other by saying, 'Either rent it from me,' and that if the property is divisible they divide it. Here, however, he rules anonymously like the Rosh that one cannot compel the other to divide. In my humble opinion, the Rambam's words, 'each may compel his fellow,' do not refer to the case in which the property is divisible, but rather to the rule of gud o agud, or 'rent it from me.' He then adds that if it is divisible, they divide it, and if one refuses to divide, he is compelled through the rule of gud o agud or 'rent it from me,' as written there in the name of the Rashba. The Sema explains differently; the reader may choose.
