He cannot compel him to divide the courtyard, etc. See the Sema, seif katan 6, who explains that the Mechaber wrote the feminine form ‘in it’ rather than the masculine form because the former accords with a field, whereas the latter accords with a courtyard. This proof is not decisive. Scripture itself states, ‘the field and the cave that is in it,’ using the masculine form with a field, while the Mishna and Gemara state, ‘A courtyard is not divided unless there is sufficient area in it for division,’ using the feminine form with a courtyard. Tosafot in Bava Kamma 38b likewise write that, if the reference were to a beit se’ah, the masculine form should have been used, and therefore explain that it refers to the field. Their intent is merely that a field is also found in the feminine, as in, ‘the field shall yield much seed.’ Likewise, in Yechezkel a courtyard is found several times in the masculine and several times in the feminine. It therefore appears that both ‘field’ and ‘courtyard’ are like ‘camp’ and similar nouns, which may be used in either gender.
