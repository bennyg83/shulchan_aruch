The landlord may. See Sema, seif katan 3, concerning a case where the landlord already received the rent. Mordechai indicates that in that circumstance the landlord cannot even rent the property to others. Later commentators ask about an apparent contradiction in Mordechai and cite Maharik's answer that the restrictive case is where the landlord lives together with the tenant. Another answer is that after the landlord has already received the money he may no longer rent to someone else. In my view the first answer is primary. If the tenant had no right to sublet, as where the landlord lives with him, then any rent the landlord later receives from a replacement tenant belongs to the landlord. The original tenant had no proprietary right to obtain that replacement rent. This resembles siman 313, where if the tenant has no right to sublet, the surplus belongs to the owner. But where the original tenant had the right to sublet, he can say, "I would have rented it to another." The replacement rent therefore belongs to him, and the landlord must credit or return what he receives from others. Mordechai in HaOmanin likewise says that even where the entire rent was paid, the tenants may have power to place others in their stead. Thus the practical rule is: Where the tenant could not sublet, such as where the landlord lives with him, replacement rent collected by the landlord belongs to the landlord, even if the tenant had not prepaid. Where the tenant could sublet, replacement rent belongs to the tenant and must be credited to him, even if he had prepaid. There is no basis for inventing a distinction based solely on prepayment. If the landlord cannot find replacement tenants, he must still reduce the rent by the amount a person would accept for the benefit of leaving the premises unused, analogous to the deduction for an idle worker. This follows Mordechai. Even where replacement tenants are merely readily available, the original tenant need not necessarily pay future rent if the landlord can mitigate the loss, as appears from the law of the ship and from Mordechai in Chezkas HaBatim.
