(Provided that there are not, etc.) The Rama's ruling is astonishing. The limitation appears in Rashba responsum 1:145 only because the deed expressly imposed it. Indeed, throughout that responsum the Rashba otherwise states simply that the tenant may sublet, without limiting the number of household members, which accords with Maharam's view that one may fill his house with residents. The responsum itself says that the father may bring in only those who serve him and may not combine others' belongings with his own, because that was the express reservation. It then permits substitute residents equal in number to his own household and servants, and substitute goods equal to his own. The wording of the condition compelled these limitations. Where a father merely reserves a dwelling generally, there is no such restriction. Another responsum in the same collection, 1:53, permits a father who reserved residence to rent it to whomever he wishes and does not mention household size. Moreover, had the Rama understood the restriction as an ordinary rule rather than a condition, he should also have prohibited introducing other people's goods while the holder remained there, as that responsum does, yet no authority generally imposes such a restriction. Mahari Weil likewise rules explicitly that one who reserves a right for himself may rent even to a larger household. Even according to the Rambam's stringency regarding a tenant, a person who reserves a proprietary right in the house is different. He may sell that right even to someone with a large household, and therefore may also rent it. The final distinction follows the Ramban, cited in siman 209: an undefined reservation, such as “the first tithe is mine,” is not inherited unless he expressly includes his heirs, because it is not a defined object. A defined reservation, such as half of a particular house, remains outside the sale and belongs to him and his heirs, and he may sell it. Although the Rashbam appears otherwise, the Rivash in responsum 257 rules like the Ramban, and no later authority should lightly overrule him. An undefined right of personal use therefore cannot be sold, whereas a defined portion can.
