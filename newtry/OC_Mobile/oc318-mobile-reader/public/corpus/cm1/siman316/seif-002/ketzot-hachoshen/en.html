The landlord may rent it to others. The Sema reads Mordechai as allowing the landlord to keep the prepaid rent and also rent the house to someone else. That cannot be correct. The first tenant acquired the rental use. Mordechai only says the tenant cannot insist that the house remain empty, because an empty house deteriorates, and the landlord may prevent harmful vacancy. It does not permit double rent. Moreover, Maharam of Rothenburg holds that even a prepaid tenant may sublet to a suitable person, comparing it to a Jubilee purchaser who can transfer his temporary interest.
For practical halachah, Maharam's view should govern. Beit Yosef says most of Mordechai's reasoning is rejected, retaining only the point that the tenant cannot insist that the house remain vacant. Darkei Moshe agrees. Rama's omission of the distinction between prepaid and unpaid rent likewise shows that he did not accept it. The tenant may therefore sublet to a suitable substitute even after prepayment.
I am therefore surprised that the Sema adopts Mordechai's rejected distinction. Mahara Sasson suggested that Rambam might also allow the landlord who already received payment to refuse another occupant. But Beit Yosef and Rama have already ruled in accordance with Rambam and Maharam that a proper substitute may be placed in the house.
