And he cannot compel him to divide, etc. This is difficult. Here the Mechaber rules simply like the Rosh in responsum, as the Tur cites him here, while in siman 171:9 the Mechaber rules simply like the Rambam, whom the Tur cites at the end of that siman: if one rents part of a field or courtyard from another where there is no legal right of division, or if two people jointly rent one place, either can compel the other to say, "Either rent my share from me or let me rent your share," and if it is divisible they divide. One can reconcile the rulings. Here, where the Mechaber says one cannot compel division, he speaks specifically of a house, whose use is intensive and involves visual privacy. If they are to dwell together in a house large enough to divide, they would need to build an actual partition to eliminate visual intrusion. They cannot impose such a permanent structural division because the house is not theirs. In siman 171 the Mechaber mentions only a field and courtyard, and deliberately places field before courtyard. The courtyard there may therefore mean a courtyard analogous to a field, one without fixed domestic use and without a problem of visual intrusion, like a field, as explained in siman 158. Such property can be divided by a simple marker indicating each person's boundary, even though they do not own the land. The Tur's objection to the Rambam, "How can they divide by a partition something that is not theirs but merely rented?" arose because the Rambam's own order, in chapter 1 of Hilchot Shekhenim, lists courtyard before field, naturally implying even an ordinary residential courtyard involving visual intrusion. The Tur therefore properly challenged that wording. But when the Mechaber copied the Rambam he reversed the order, putting field first, not without reason, in order to indicate that he means a courtyard comparable to a field. There is also a hint in the singular ending, "if it is divisible, they divide," although two types of property were mentioned. This suggests the operative reference is the field, and by extension only a comparable courtyard. According to this explanation, Rama's statement here and in siman 171 that some disagree with the Mechaber is unnecessary.
