Or he rented it and retained a right for himself. Maharit discusses a landlord who angrily told the tenant to empty the house, and the tenant later claimed release from future rent. Maharit rules that mere words do not discharge him. "Empty the house" can mean, "Leave and still pay," and even if it means, "Leave and be exempt," the release takes effect only when the house is actually returned and accepted. Before then either side can retract. There is also an acquisition problem: a rental right already acquired by the tenant does not return to the landlord through verbal waiver alone. A fresh kinyan is needed. The transaction can work if the landlord releases the rent and the tenant transfers the rental right back in exchange for that benefit, but mere speech is insufficient.
