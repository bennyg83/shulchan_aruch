(One who granted a dwelling, etc.) The Sema already disagreed with this ruling, and I find it difficult for an additional reason. The case appears to involve an undefined gift of residence, meaning one house among the donor's houses. The Rama's own responsum, cited in Maharshal responsum 17, describes a father who gave his daughter the right to live in his house after his death for her lifetime. Yet the Rama himself rules in accordance with the Rivash that an undefined right cannot be sold. If, instead, the case concerns a defined part of a particular house, it is even more difficult to understand why the recipient may sell only to one whose household is no larger than his own. That defined portion never entered the donor's later sale or gift and is the recipient's property like any other asset; he may place there whomever he wishes. The correct distinction is therefore this: when a person grants residence in a specifically defined portion, the recipient may do whatever he wishes with that portion, including renting or selling it to a large household. When he grants generally the right to live in one of his houses, the recipient has only a personal right of residence and may neither sell nor rent it.
