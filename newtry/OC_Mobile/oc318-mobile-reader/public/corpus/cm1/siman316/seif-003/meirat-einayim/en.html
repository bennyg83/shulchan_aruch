And he stipulated that after the term the tenant would have permission. The word "tenant" is not precise. It should have said, "the person holding the mortgage." But an ordinary mortgage is generally arranged with deduction from the produce. Otherwise it would involve rabbinic interest. Since a fixed amount is deducted annually in exchange for the produce, the arrangement resembles rental, so the mortgagee is described here as a tenant.
For even without the condition he may rent it, etc. The source is a responsum of Rashba cited by the Beit Yosef. Reuven mortgaged the upper storage floor of his building to Shimon for a term under a deduction arrangement and stipulated that after the term Shimon would be entitled either to demand his debt, to live there with his household, to mortgage it, or to rent it to whomever he wished. After the mortgage term expired Reuven died, leaving minor sons. Shimon could not demand payment from the minors until they grew up, and he wished meanwhile to mortgage or rent the property to others. Rashba ruled in Shimon's favor. By strict law, even without the special condition, Shimon could have rented or mortgaged it even during the original term. If so, what does the added condition accomplish? Necessarily it adds something, namely permission to rent it even to a household larger than his own. We do not say that language was inserted merely to beautify the document where it can reasonably be understood as adding a substantive right. From this responsum we also learn that even if the mortgagor dies leaving minor sons, and payment cannot be demanded from them during minority, the mortgagee's existing rights are not removed. From the time the property was mortgaged to him he already had authority to rent it even during the term, and the stipulation is treated as though the mortgagor expressly promised that after the term he could rent it even to a larger household. That right was fully vested during the mortgagor's lifetime and is not displaced by the heirs' minority. The Mechaber abbreviated the source and omitted both the point that he could rent even during the term and the case of the mortgagor dying and leaving minor children, apparently because Rashba himself treated those points as obvious.
