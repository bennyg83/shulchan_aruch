(Seif 1: He rents it until the end of his term) The Tur quotes the Rambam's conclusion: the Sages said that a renter may not sublet only concerning movable property, because the owner may say, “I do not want my deposit in another person's hands.” With land, or with a ship whose owner remains with it, this concern does not apply. Some ruled that he may never sublet and must pay until the end of the term, but the Rambam rejects that ruling. The Beit Yosef, in the name of the Maggid Mishneh, explains that even a grievance exists only where the shipowner must live with the substitute tenant and endure his temperament; with a house, even a grievance does not exist. This plainly concerns a house in which the landlord does not live, as is also evident from the Tur, who afterward cites the Rosh's responsum forbidding substitution in a case where the landlord lives with the tenant, without presenting it as a dispute with the Rambam. The Rambam's phrase “for its owner is with it” means that with movable property the second renter might conceal or remove it where the owner's eyes do not reach, whereas land remains visible and the owner can inspect it at any time, even if he does not live in the same house.
