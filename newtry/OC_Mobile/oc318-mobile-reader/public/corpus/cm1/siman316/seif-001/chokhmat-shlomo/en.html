Seif 2. 'He cannot compel him to divide, because the house does not belong to them.' Note: It appears to me that there is proof for this view from Kiddushin 53a. Rabbi Yochanan says that they voted and concluded that one who betroths a woman with his share of sacrificial meat, whether kodshei kodashim or kodashim kalim, does not effect kiddushin. Abaye says Rabbi Yochanan's view is reasonable, and the Gemara explains that what is called 'division' there is actually grabbing. Tosafot make clear that the two issues depend upon one another: if the property is theirs, the law of division applies; if it is not theirs, because they receive it from the table of Heaven and cannot use it for kiddushin, there is no law of division. The same should apply to property rented for a fixed period. Since rental for a day is not a sale, the corpus does not belong to the renters, and if one betrothed a woman with the body of the rented object she would not be betrothed. Therefore there is no right to compel division. Although with one sacrificial animal they do divide it, that is because there is no other practical way for all of them to eat from it. But one sacrifice cannot be exchanged against another because, where each can take from both, no one has ownership enabling him to divide one against the other. So too with rental property, since both can use the entire house together, there is no compelled division. This appears to me a clear proof.
