One who rents, etc. This is derived from Bava Metzia 79b, which asks: if he can find another tenant, etc. The Gemara does not say that the landlord has a grievance merely because his preference was altered. The restriction that a renter may not sublet applies to movable property, because the owner may say, 'I do not want my deposit in another person's possession,' as Gittin 29a states. That concern does not apply to land, since movable property can be stolen and hidden. See the end of siman 342. Hagahot Maimoniyot adds proof from Arakhin 30a regarding a field sold to a first buyer, where the sale is evidently without the original owner's consent. Otherwise the case would be obvious. Every sale while the Jubilee applies is effectively a rental for produce. Additional proof comes from Maaser Sheni, where Rabban Gamliel rented the place of the produce, and from the rule that one may lend another a place. If subletting were forbidden, collection through such a place and writing a prosbul against it would be impossible. The Jerusalem Talmud concerning a widow's residence says that when she asks for money so that she may rent it out, she is not heard, implying that ordinarily such a request is heard. Rashba. Some of these proofs may be rejected in light of the rules stated in siman 212. See 146. See 315.
Provided that he does not, etc. This follows Rambam's approach, as stated in siman 154:2. He derives it from Bava Batra 1a concerning partners, where a change makes the arrangement as though, etc. Even the gloss in seif 2 disagrees only regarding partners, not a landlord, as Sema explains. Mordechai and Hagahot Maimoniyot disagree even in this case, following Rashbam. Rashba agrees with Rambam.
And if the landlord said to him, etc. This is required by the principle of what is good and upright, like the rule in the chapter HaMekabel that with respect to the original owners the law of the adjoining property owner does not apply.
"And if you are like," he said. P. B. D. A.D.: This is a reason to raise wages:

And some distinguish, etc. As I wrote above in siman 209, seif 7, regarding an heir, and all the more so regarding a sale. See also siman 312, seif 1, in the gloss: if he stipulated with him, etc.
One who gives, etc., for one who gives, etc. This is learned from the words of Rashba cited above. As I explained, it is no less effective, etc. The phrase 'for one who gives' means as stated in Bava Batra 64a regarding a pit and cistern according to Rabbi Akiva, whose view we follow there, as explained there. This applies all the more to one who gives a gift, as stated there on 65a. See Sema, who objects that in several places the opposite rule is stated, including Bava Batra 36a and 148a, and as I wrote above in siman 209, seif 7, siman 212, seif 3, and siman 216, seifim 11 and 12.
