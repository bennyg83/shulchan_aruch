And if the landlord said to him, etc. Sema, based on the Perishah, explains that if another person offers the landlord a higher rent, the landlord cannot tell the present tenant to leave unless the tenant refuses to match that additional profit. Rama in siman 363 and Radbaz I:139 state similarly. Radbaz adds that this applies before the landlord has already rented the premises to the second person. Once the second tenancy has validly vested and the second tenant has no readily available alternative, his right is not displaced merely to benefit the first landlord, for the principle 'do not withhold good from its owner' does not apply where it causes loss to another. If the second tenant has not yet entered and can readily rent elsewhere, the first landlord may be heard, and the same principle is addressed to the second tenant. Shaar Mishpat agrees.
To rent it to others. Sema cites the Mordechai that if the landlord already received the rent he need not return to the first tenant what he later receives from another. Ketzot HaChoshen strongly questions this: since the tenant acquired the house for the rental term, why should the landlord profit from the tenant's right? Even if in some cases the tenant may not sublet, if the landlord himself rents it out the second rent should ordinarily belong to the first tenant. Netivot HaMishpat distinguishes cases. Where the first tenant had no right at all to sublet, such as where he lived together with the landlord, the landlord may keep rent received from another, though the first tenant is entitled to an allowance comparable to an idle worker. Where the tenant was entitled to sublet, the substitute rent belongs to him even if he prepaid. Responsa Brit Avraham discusses the issue at length and concludes that even where the tenant could not himself sublet a particular replacement, if the landlord voluntarily accepted that replacement without objection the basic rent should generally reduce the first tenant's liability. Prepayment may be different because it can be understood as acceptance that the rent becomes the landlord's despite later nonuse. A pledge seized by the landlord is not equivalent to voluntary prepayment. See there for the extensive analysis and the remaining dispute with Netivot HaMishpat.
