To rent it out. The Mordechai there states explicitly that if the lessor has already received the rent, he cannot return it to the renter even if the renter says, "Then leave the house unoccupied." The lessor need not heed him. But if the lessor has not yet received the money and rents the house to others, he deducts from the original renter's rent what he receives from the others. Even if the renter says, "I will pay the entire amount, but let no others live there," and the law would otherwise allow the lessor to refuse, one may say the opposite: the renter has revealed that he will pay the full rent only if no others live there, and since others do live there, how can money be extracted from him? Sema.
To sell. This requires study in light of what the Tur and Mechaber write below in seif 2 regarding two people who rented a house in partnership. The same should seemingly apply here. Although the Gemara explicitly rules regarding a ship that one renter may place another in his stead and the shipowner has only a complaint, there it is different because it is customary that the renter disembarks when he sells his merchandise and the purchaser takes his place. From the outset the shipowner contemplated that possibility. Even so, he may resent the change of person. One must distinguish that a renter of a house initially accepts that the lessor may place another person there when he wishes, since every person controls his own house and may do with it as he wishes; the lessor has retained for himself the right to live there, and likewise to place another there. Consider this carefully. Ibid.
