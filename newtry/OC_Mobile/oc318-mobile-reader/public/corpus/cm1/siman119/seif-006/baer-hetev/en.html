The Tur concludes that the reason is that the purchaser does not have both average-quality and inferior land in his possession, so that he can say, 'I do not desire the benefit of the Sages' enactment.' Nevertheless, if the creditor prefers to collect the average-quality land in the hands of the second purchaser, he may do so, because that is the principal property subject to his lien. Had it remained with the first purchaser, the latter would ordinarily have paid him from the average-quality land rather than the superior land. Thus the creditor's lien is present there. Sema.
