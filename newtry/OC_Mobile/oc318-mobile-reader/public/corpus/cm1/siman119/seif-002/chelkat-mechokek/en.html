It is not proper for him to hasten to send her away. This is Rambam's wording, but it is awkward. At first it implies that there is an actual prohibition against divorcing one's first wife when he has not found in her a matter of immorality, since the prophet laments this, 'And this second thing you do, covering the altar with tears, etc., the wife of your youth against whom you have acted treacherously,' in Malachi. How then can Rambam say merely that 'it is not proper to hasten to send her away,' which implies no prohibition, only improper conduct? Perhaps even with the wife's consent, when there is no prohibition, it is nevertheless improper to do so. Alternatively, even if he found a matter of immorality, such as where one witness testified against her and there is a mitzvah to divorce her, he should not say, 'I believe him,' and divorce her immediately. Rather, he should proceed deliberately, investigate thoroughly, and perhaps discover that divorce is unnecessary.
