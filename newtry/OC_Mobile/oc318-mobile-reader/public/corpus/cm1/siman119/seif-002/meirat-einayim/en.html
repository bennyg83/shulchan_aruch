The creditor does not collect from superior land. The Tur concludes that this is analogous to a tort victim, whose Torah lien is upon superior land. If the tortfeasor sells the superior land, the injured party collects only from the inferior land remaining unencumbered with the tortfeasor.
From the superior land that he sold. But the creditor does collect from intermediate land remaining in the debtor's possession. Although ordinarily, if a debtor possesses both intermediate and inferior land, the creditor collects only from the inferior land because land quality is assessed according to the debtor's own holdings, as explained in siman 102, that applies when the debtor had no superior land at the time of the loan. In such a case the creditor's lien never attached to the intermediate land as his ordinary grade. Here, however, the debtor did possess superior land at the time of the loan. Therefore, even absent an express stipulation that collection be from the superior land, once the superior land is sold the creditor collects from the intermediate land still in the debtor's possession. This is contrary to the implication of the Ir Shushan, who ties collection from the intermediate land to an express stipulation concerning superior land.
