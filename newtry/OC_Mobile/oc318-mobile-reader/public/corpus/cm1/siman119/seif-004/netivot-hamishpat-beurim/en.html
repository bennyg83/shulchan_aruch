And he appeased the purchasers. See Sema seif katan 25 through his statement that the creditor then takes average-quality land. This applies specifically to average-quality land. The injured party cannot appease a purchaser so as to take superior-quality land beyond his legal entitlement. When a purchaser waives the rabbinic enactment protecting him, the situation is treated as though all the parcels had been acquired simultaneously by separate purchasers. Likewise, if a ketubah has earlier priority and the superior-quality land was sold last, so that by law the injured party would have to go to the superior land, the injured party may appease the purchaser of the inferior land to waive the rabbinic enactment and thereby push the ketubah claimant toward the inferior land. This appears clear.
