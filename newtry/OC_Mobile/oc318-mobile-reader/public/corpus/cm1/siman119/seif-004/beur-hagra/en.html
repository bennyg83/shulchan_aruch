And they wrote the hours. Kesubos 93b.
And if his document... Tosafos there and all the poskim. See Sema. Kesubos 95a, concerning a wife who signed away rights to the first purchaser but not the second, shows that her lien can shift according to the order and terms of sale. Rashi explains that she loses even against the first because of the argument that other property had been left available. See above, siman 118.
And if the injured party appeases... The parties may agree to alter the ordinary collection order because that order exists for the purchasers' protection. Bava Kamma gives the rationale that the Sages' rule is an enactment, and the Rosh emphasizes this.
(Collected notes) And if the injured party... The ordinary order is only a purchaser-protection enactment, as the Rosh explains. (End of collected notes.)
