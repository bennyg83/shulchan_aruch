Where the debtor sold inferior land last, Rosh explains that a tort claimant may nevertheless be forced to collect that inferior land if it remained free in the debtor's estate. The apparent contradiction with the ordinary right of an injured party to superior land is resolved by the general rule that one does not disturb encumbered property while free property remains. If only inferior free land remains, the claimant must take it. The difficult case is where the inferior land itself was sold last. The purchaser can sometimes say, "If you do not accept the land from me on the terms I propose, I will return its deed to the seller and you will then have to collect that inferior land from him." Rosh's reasoning follows the purchaser's power to restore an asset that had already been part of the debtor's original estate and thereby recreate the debtor's free property. This does not contradict the priority rules for after acquired property, because the inferior field itself was part of the original lien when the debt arose.
