When he purchased superior land last. The Rambam explains: This applies where superior land was purchased last. If inferior land was purchased last, all of them collect from the inferior land, because the purchaser can say to the injured party and the creditor, 'I left you the inferior land available for collection.' But where superior land was purchased last, the purchaser may say, 'The rule that claimants collect from the last property acquired is an enactment for the purchaser's benefit. I do not wish to use this enactment. Rather, each of you shall collect the grade legally appropriate to him.' The Maggid Mishneh writes that according to the Rambam, if intermediate land was purchased last, the tort claimant and creditor collect from the intermediate land and the ketubah claimant from the inferior land. He further writes that one resolution in the Gemara limits this law to where the original owner died, but the Rashba rules that as a matter of law we do not use the argument 'if you remain silent' to reduce an earlier legal entitlement. Thus Rama's cited alternative opinion disagrees with the Rashba and permits that argument even to reduce the original entitlement. Rama's phrase 'or he purchased them simultaneously' is his own addition. His subsequent phrase concerning purchasing inferior land last belongs to the Rambam's text and should be read as part of the Mechaber's ruling.
Specifically where the seller died. When the seller has died, the purchaser cannot say, 'If you do not accept the inferior land from me, I will return the deed of that inferior field by writing and delivery to the heirs and force you to collect inferior land from them.' Even if he returns it to them, the heirs are not obligated to pay their father's debt from property that they themselves newly acquire or receive as a gift after his death. Since the purchaser cannot thereby remove the seller's debt from himself while the lien is in his hands, each claimant takes from him according to the applicable grade. The creditor takes intermediate land even if the purchaser acquired it before the inferior land. Once the purchaser repels the creditor from superior land by saying that he declines the rabbinic enactment and will give him what the law ordinarily assigns, he cannot then turn around when the creditor seeks intermediate land and invoke the enactment to direct him to inferior land. We do not push the same person in opposite directions by inconsistent positions. That concern differs where there are two distinct parties.
And Rama's statement, 'the same applies if he acquired inferior land,' etc. Through the end of the gloss, this is repetitive, because even according to the Rambam, whom the Mechaber follows, the same law applies for the reason I explained. Know also that the Mechaber abbreviated and omitted the Tur's reasoning and the derivative laws concerning simultaneous purchase or purchase of inferior land last. The Tur writes that if the seller is still alive, all claimants must collect from the inferior land, because we view the purchaser as though he had returned the deed of the inferior land to the seller by writing and delivery. This does not require an actual return of that field. Even if he returns a different field, gives the seller a gift, or even gives movable property, so long as the seller then has some property from which collection can be made, the creditors cannot collect from the purchasers. They also cannot say, 'When you actually return it, then we will respond,' because anything a person can presently do is viewed for this purpose as though already done. The Rosh writes that even if the purchaser does not make this argument, beit din advances it for him.
