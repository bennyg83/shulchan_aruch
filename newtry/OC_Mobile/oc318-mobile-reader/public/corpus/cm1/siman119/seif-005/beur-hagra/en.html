Some say that even... The Gemara asks, "If so, damages too..." Rosh, Rashba, Rabbeinu Chananel, Ramban, and Tosafos in Kesubos discuss whether the same rule applies to creditors. According to one approach, where the seller is alive, all claimants may be directed to inferior land, and the more favorable collection rules stated in the Shulchan Aruch apply particularly where the seller died. Rif and Rambam omit the Gemara's orphan qualification and apply the rule even while the seller lives, which many commentators questioned. Ramban explains that the first rationale based on "If you remain silent..." is not a fully operative claim unless the purchaser himself raises it. The court does not raise it automatically. Therefore, absent that plea, claimants collect according to their ordinary rights even if the seller lives. Rashba gives a related explanation: the "If you remain silent" argument may preserve the purchaser's existing legal position but should not reduce another person's substantive right. He links this to the dispute of Admon and the Rabbis and the later interpretation in Yevamos. Thus Rif's omission can be understood as focusing on what beit din itself pleads, while the Gemara's orphan formulation discusses a case where the purchaser affirmatively raises the argument. Other Rishonim maintain that the distinction really does depend on whether the seller is alive.
