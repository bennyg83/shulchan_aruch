An injured party. Although an injured party has no promissory note, damage claims generate publicity comparable to a written document, and the purchaser should not have bought the property. Sema. The Shach notes that this requires study in light of what he wrote in siman 66, seif 35. As a matter of law, however, there are dissenting authorities. The Beit Yosef records them, and Hagahot Ashri in the first chapter of Bava Kamma cites Or Zarua that an injured party cannot collect from encumbered property unless the matter was first adjudicated. The Shach leaves practical ruling requiring study.
Because of uncertainty. The case is where the property of each purchaser, or at least of two of them, is sufficient to satisfy all three debts. Otherwise the creditors could authorize one of themselves to act for the others, and he could collect from all three purchasers. The Tur concludes, "until they bring proof who acquired last." This means that if their documents contain kinyanim, the witnesses are asked for whom the first kinyan was made. If the documents contain no kinyan, then one asks whose deed first came into his possession, and that person acquired first. Sema.
As one. Maharshal rules likewise in Yam Shel Shlomo, siman 14. Shach.
