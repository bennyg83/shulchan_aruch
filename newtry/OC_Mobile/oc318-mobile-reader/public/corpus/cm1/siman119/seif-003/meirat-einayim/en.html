It follows that where the documents state that they were all written on one day and no hours are recorded, all agree that beit din does not advance for the purchasers the argument that one purchaser may have acquired first. Although courts ordinarily advance claims for heirs and purchasers, we do not advance this claim to injure others whose liens unquestionably rest upon these properties. Even where three deeds were written and signed by the same scribe and witnesses, we can say that the seller retained all three after writing them and delivered them simultaneously. The Ramban and Ri disagree only where the purchasers themselves make definite claims, each saying, 'Mine was delivered to me early in the morning and yours only afterward.' According to the Ramban, because the first purchaser claims this with certainty, we heed him and the burden of proof lies upon the other party. The Baal HaTerumot's language appears to limit the Ramban's position to documents with no date at all, but in the Derishah I offered an interpretation that reconciles the Tur and Mechaber, who present the matter as a dispute.
