Only superior land. The creditor can say, 'Why did you distance my lien by selling the intermediate land to the second purchaser? Why should I trouble myself collecting from him?' All the more so if only inferior land remains in the first purchaser's hands. But if he retains both superior and inferior land, then if the original seller is still alive he can plainly say, 'If you remain silent, fine; otherwise I will return the deed to the owner of the inferior land.' Even if the seller died and he cannot say that, the rule remains that just as the borrower himself could have pushed the creditor from superior land to inferior land, so too can the purchaser who stands in his place. Sema. Maharshal disagrees where only superior land remains and holds that Shimon can push Levi away. See Tumim. The principal law follows Rosh. Even if the second purchaser bought without responsibility and therefore could not recover from the first purchaser if foreclosed upon, the creditor may nevertheless foreclose the superior land from the first purchaser.
