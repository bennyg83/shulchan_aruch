Reuven sold all his fields. See the responsa of Menahem Azariah, simanim 46 and 69.
If he wishes, he collects from intermediate-quality land. See the Sema. He explains that according to the opinion cited by the Rama in seif 5, this case may concern a seller who has died. Maharshal rejects that opinion for this reason. See also Bach.
Only superior-quality land. Maharshal disagrees with the Rosh and is surprised that the Rosh understood Rashi this way. He overlooked Rashi’s explicit statement in the chapter Mi SheHayah Nasui: the creditor may collect superior-quality land from Shimon, but if he collects from Levi, such as where only inferior property of Reuven remained with Shimon, he collects there. Maharshal also says that Ran supports him, but Ran in Bava Kamma proves nothing, while Ran in Mi SheHayah Nasui explicitly follows Rashi. Baal HaTerumot cites Rav Hai Gaon in agreement with the Rosh. That is the primary ruling.
And he purchased inferior-quality land last. The rule specifically speaks of inferior land purchased last. If Shimon purchased superior land last, the creditor may collect intermediate land from Levi if he wishes. The Sema and Tosafot state this explicitly. Levi cannot say, as Rashi suggests, that Shimon left unencumbered property available, because Shimon may waive the rabbinic protection against collecting encumbered property where unencumbered property exists. Although the Gemara says that Shimon cannot make this waiver where he sold the intermediate land and retained superior land, that is only when the creditor seeks to collect from Shimon. If the creditor seeks to collect from Levi, Shimon may certainly waive the protection. Maharshal objects that the first purchaser sold the second purchaser every right he possessed. Since Shimon could waive the protection and direct the creditor to the inferior property purchased last, Levi should inherit that right. Baal HaIttur, a student of Rabbenu Yonah, the Maggid Mishneh, and the Tur accordingly limit the rule to fields bought under one deed. In my view, this objection is not persuasive. The rule that a seller transfers all his rights does not revive Rashi’s argument that Shimon left unencumbered property. The practical difference is where the last-acquired property is superior: under Rashi, the creditor could not collect from Levi, while under Rabbenu Yonah, because Shimon could waive the protection, the creditor may collect Levi’s intermediate land. The student of Rabbenu Yonah indicates this expressly. The Rosh also appears to agree. His limitation to one deed was needed only where Shimon bought inferior land last, because then the creditor cannot collect Levi’s intermediate land. Baal HaTerumot, the Maggid Mishneh, and the Tur likewise mention only inferior land purchased last. Their reason, that Shimon could direct the creditor to the inferior land, confirms that where the last purchase was superior, the creditor may collect intermediate land from Levi.
