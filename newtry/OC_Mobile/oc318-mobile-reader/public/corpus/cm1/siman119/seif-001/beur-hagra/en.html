At one time. If the parcels were purchased sequentially and the purchaser acquired inferior land last, the creditor may be restricted to that inferior land. The Gemara says, "What the first sold to the second..." and the buyer stands in the seller's place. Maggid Mishneh, Baal HaTerumos, and Tur explain this. Thus below the text distinguishes sequential acquisition. See Tosafos, Kesubos 92a, Sema, and seif 5.
Even if he did not... See Sema and Rashi in Kesubos. The expression "if he wishes" implies that there is some practical advantage in choosing one source over another, which indicates that he may collect superior land from Shimon.

And some say that he collects... See Sema. It is presented as a distinct opinion because the Gemara can imply the opposite. Where the purchaser bought superior and inferior land, the rationale of his effort in purchasing a particular parcel appears necessary only if no suitable intermediate land remained with the first purchaser. Rashba, however, establishes the case even where such land remained, explaining that without the special rationale the creditor could say that the inferior land itself was available to him because the purchaser entered the seller's position. Where equivalent intermediate land remained, the first purchaser could have directed the creditor there. According to the Rosh and the gloss elsewhere, however, even inferior land cannot always be forced on the creditor, creating difficulty. Hence the Shulchan Aruch's first clause and its later qualification reflect differing approaches.
