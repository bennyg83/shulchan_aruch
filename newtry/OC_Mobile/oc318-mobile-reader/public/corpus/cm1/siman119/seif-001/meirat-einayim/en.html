Reuven sold all his fields, etc. If Reuven, the borrower, had retained any field, even inferior land, his creditor could not collect from Shimon or Levi because collection must first come from unencumbered property.
At one time. Meaning in one transfer. Otherwise, the field remaining last in Reuven's hands at the time of the earlier transfer would have been the one initially subject to the creditor, even if Reuven later sold it too to Shimon.
And it is intermediate land. A creditor ordinarily collects from intermediate land.
A creditor came. Meaning Reuven's creditor.
He may collect from the intermediate land, etc. Had it remained in Shimon's hands, Shimon could have pushed the creditor from the intermediate field to the inferior field also purchased from Reuven, by saying: if you do not take the inferior land from me, I will return it to Reuven by sale or gift and you will then be required to collect from that unencumbered field. Levi, however, cannot use that argument after purchasing the intermediate field, because the inferior land is not in Levi's hands to return to Reuven. Since the creditor's proper lien, the intermediate field, is in Levi's possession, the creditor may collect it. According to the view cited by Rama in seif 5, this must sometimes be understood where the original seller has died.
This is not comparable to Levi buying the intermediate field directly from Reuven while leaving inferior land with Reuven, because Reuven is the borrower and personally obligated to pay his debt; ordinary market-protection rules apply around his assets. Shimon, by contrast, is himself only a purchaser and owes the creditor nothing personally. Levi therefore cannot always push the creditor back against Shimon.
Even if only superior land remained in his hands. Shimon can say, "You caused my proper lien, the intermediate land, to move away when you sold it to Levi, and I do not wish to trouble myself with Levi; I will collect from you." The word "even" teaches all the more so where only inferior land remained with Shimon. But if Shimon retained both superior and inferior land, the creditor can collect only the inferior, because Shimon stands in Reuven's place with respect to the available grading.
He cannot collect. Levi can say to the creditor: "I was careful not to purchase the intermediate field that remained in Shimon's hands because I intended to leave your proper lien there."
He collects from the inferior land, etc. Meaning if the creditor wishes. Since Shimon held only superior and inferior land, the creditor's effective lien rested on the inferior land relative to the superior alongside it, so in that setting the inferior land functions like intermediate land for the creditor's choice.
Intermediate land remained in Shimon's hands. If both intermediate fields had remained with Shimon, he could have chosen which one to give the creditor. Levi purchased that right together with the field, so he may direct the creditor to the intermediate field left with Shimon. And he purchased the inferior land last, etc. In that case even if Levi purchased intermediate land from Shimon, the creditor cannot collect from Levi, because the inferior land was still unencumbered in Reuven's hands when Shimon made the earlier purchases, so Shimon could have directed the creditor to it. Levi purchased all Shimon's rights. By contrast, if Shimon purchased the superior field last and then sold to Levi the intermediate field bought earlier, the creditor may collect from either one. Likewise, if Shimon purchased the intermediate field last and sold it to Levi, the creditor may, if he wishes, collect the superior field remaining with Shimon because Shimon moved away the creditor's ordinary lien.
