Levi. Although if the properties were still in Shimon's hands he could have deferred the creditor to the inferior land by saying, 'If you do not take the inferior land, I will return it to Reuven by sale or gift, and you will then have to collect from him, for one does not collect from encumbered property when unencumbered property is available,' Levi cannot make that argument. The inferior land is not in Levi's possession for him to return to Reuven, while the property subject to the creditor's lien, namely average-quality land, is in Levi's possession, and the creditor may seize it. According to the opinion cited by Rama in seif 5, one may explain that this case is where the seller died, and the same explanation is necessary in many subsequent cases. Nor is this comparable to a case where Levi bought average-quality land from Reuven while leaving inferior land in Reuven's possession, in which case the creditor collects only from the inferior land because it is unencumbered. Reuven is the debtor and is personally obligated to pay his own debts, and the market-protection enactment applies to him. Shimon, by contrast, is himself merely a purchaser and owes this creditor nothing. Therefore Levi cannot redirect the creditor to Shimon, and the creditor may choose from whom to collect. Sema.
Superior land. The reason is that the creditor can say to Shimon, 'You removed the average-quality land that was subject to my lien by selling it to Levi, and I do not wish to trouble myself with Levi.' If, however, superior and inferior land remained in Shimon's possession, the creditor may seize from him only the inferior land. This is so even if Reuven has already died, so that Shimon can no longer say that he will return the property to Reuven, as stated by the Tur and Shulchan Aruch at the end of seif 4. Nevertheless, Shimon stands in Reuven's place, and just as a creditor collecting from Reuven would be given inferior land rather than superior land, as stated in siman 102, Shimon likewise has that right.
