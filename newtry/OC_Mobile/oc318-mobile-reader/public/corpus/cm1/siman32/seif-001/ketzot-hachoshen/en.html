He did not hire them. The Shach asks from Kiddushin 43, which implies that even though there is no agency for transgression, the sender is at least punished by Heaven. The Gemara asks whether the first Tanna really exempts even under Heavenly law and answers by distinguishing greater and lesser Heavenly punishment. In my view our law here concerns a monetary obligation to pay under the laws of Heaven, like the cases where one says, "I do not know whether I became liable," and is exempt in human court but obligated to pay in Heaven. Where he actually hires false witnesses, he can be monetarily obligated in Heaven to repair the loss. Where he merely instructs others to sin without hiring them, he can certainly still deserve punishment in Heaven because there is no agency for transgression, but that is punishment, not necessarily a monetary obligation to reimburse. Many forms of indirect causation carry spiritual punishment without a Heavenly monetary duty. Thus the Gemara in Kiddushin and our ruling address different consequences.
