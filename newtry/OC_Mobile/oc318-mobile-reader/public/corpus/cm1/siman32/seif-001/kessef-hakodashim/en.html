See Shach, seif katan 3. The Shach in siman 32 writes that one who sends an agent to commit a transgression may be liable under the laws of Heaven. Perhaps this heavenly liability concerns primarily the wrong between the person and Heaven. Although an evil intention is generally not combined with the agent's act to impose the full major liability, a lesser heavenly liability can still remain. With respect to the interpersonal monetary wrong itself, however, perhaps the sender remains exempt even in Heaven under the rule that there is no agency for transgression, whereas an ordinary indirect causer can be liable in Heaven toward the injured party. This may be included in "do not place a stumbling block before the blind" and "one who misleads the blind on the road." If so, the difficulty the Shach raises against Tosafot may be resolved. The ancient serpent, by contrast, was punished even in an interpersonal form, "I will place enmity between you and the woman," meaning that Heaven imposed consequences for the harm caused between creatures as well. The matter requires careful study.
