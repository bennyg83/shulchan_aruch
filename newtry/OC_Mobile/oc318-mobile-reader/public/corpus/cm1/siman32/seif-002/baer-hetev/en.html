The one who hired. This concerns one who hired false witnesses for another person's benefit, as the gloss explains. Even if the hirer admits what he did, he is exempt under human law because the stolen property never entered his possession. Although we follow the opinion that dina d'garmi is actionable, here he did not himself testify but merely hired witnesses, and he can say that where the master's instruction conflicts with the student's obligation, the student should not have obeyed. Sema.
Heaven. He does not discharge his obligation before Heaven until he compensates the victim for the loss. See Responsa Maharam Alshich, siman 73. Shach.
Enticed. This is the view of Tosafot at the beginning of HaKones. The Shach disagrees and holds that one is always liable before Heaven even if he did not hire the false witnesses. In his glosses he nevertheless brings support for Tosafot from the Gemara in Sanhedrin: from where do we know that no argument is advanced in defense of an enticer? From the primordial serpent, etc. See there.
For himself. Meaning that he himself was one of the claimants and admits this. The Shach writes in the name of Maharshal that where one person has a claim against three people, and one of the three acts as litigant while the other two testify, if they later admit what they did they must return the money, even if they maintain that they took it lawfully because the claimant truly owed them. See Responsa Chacham Tzvi, siman 138.
