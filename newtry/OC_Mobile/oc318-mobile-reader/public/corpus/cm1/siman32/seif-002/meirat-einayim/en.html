He is exempt under human law. The case is where he hired false witnesses for another person, as Rama explains. Therefore even if the hirer admits it, he is exempt under human law because the stolen money never came into his possession. Although we rule that direct causation can create liability, the hirer did not himself testify; he can say that when the master's command conflicts with the student's obligation, the student should obey the Master. The witnesses themselves are the primary cause. The case assumes that the witnesses and the person who obtained the money do not admit false testimony, or that the witnesses have no funds or went overseas.
He does not admit. Meaning the person for whose benefit they were hired does not admit that the money was wrongfully taken, whereas the hirer does admit hiring them.
But if he hired, etc. This clause contrasts both points with the opening: if he hired them for himself rather than another, or where the person who received the money admits the wrongful extraction, the law changes accordingly.
And what it says, 'and he extracted money unlawfully.' Meaning he admits that the extraction was not according to law. The text did not need to say this expressly because it follows the Mechaber's case, which assumes the hirer admits the conduct.
Or the litigant admits. Meaning that even if he hired the witnesses for another, that other person admits that he wrongfully extracted the money through their testimony.
The witnesses are obligated to pay. But the person who received the money is not made liable merely on the witnesses' retraction when he himself denies wrongdoing. Once witnesses have testified, they cannot retract and give a different account against another party. Regarding themselves, however, they are believed to impose liability upon themselves. The Tur writes this explicitly in the name of the Rosh, and the Mechaber rules similarly in simanim 46 and 29.
