One who hires, etc. Meaning that he admits having done so. If there are witnesses, then either the litigant or the false witnesses may already be liable to pay. The case is where he hired false witnesses for another person's litigation. If he hired them for his own case and admits that the testimony was false, he is liable even under human law. Gemara.
And he is liable under the laws of Heaven. He does not discharge his heavenly obligation until he reimburses his fellow for the entire loss. Shach. Under human law he is exempt, because he may say that the witnesses should have obeyed the Master rather than the student.
But he violates "Distance yourself from a false matter," etc. See Tumim concerning why the Mishnah does not instead invoke the prohibition of placing a stumbling block before the blind.
Likewise if he did not hire them, etc. See Tumim, where I explain that "hiring" means that he fixed a specific payment for this false testimony. If the witness is generally employed by him and receives a salary, and he persuades him to testify falsely, that is not included in the category of hiring false witnesses. I also concluded that even if he did not use language of agency but merely said, "If you testify, you will receive such and such payment," he is nevertheless liable under the laws of Heaven. If he hired gentile witnesses to testify falsely in a gentile court in a case where resort to such a court is permitted, as described above in siman 26, it appears that he is still exempt under human law.
And he does not admit. Meaning that the person who received the money does not admit wrongdoing. That person would be liable to repay if he admitted, while the one who hired the witnesses remains exempt.
And he extracted, etc. Meaning that the recipient of the money admits, or the opposing litigant admits, as explained above, so he is obligated to return the money. In that case the one who hired the witnesses is exempt as stated.
They are obligated to pay. See what I wrote above in siman 29. Maharshal writes in Yam Shel Shlomo, Bava Kamma, chapter 4, siman 18, concerning three creditors of one hundred against one debtor who arrange that one will appear as litigant and the other two as witnesses, violating "Distance yourself from a false matter." If they later admit before beit din what they did, he rules that they must return the hundred they extracted. He says there is no migo because it is a migo against witnesses: their admission that they extracted one hundred unlawfully is like one hundred witnesses, and their prior presumption of truthfulness has also been impaired. His ruling requires great study. What does "against witnesses" mean here, when the very mouth that creates the problem also provides the explanation? They say both that they testified deceptively and that the debt was in fact owed to them. Nor do I know the source for saying that an impaired presumption of truthfulness alone allows extraction of money. I am unworthy to disagree, but it is difficult to remove money without clear proof. The Gemara says only that such conduct is forbidden, not that afterward they lose credibility when supported by a migo. This requires study. See below in siman 37, seif 3, where I wrote that the law appears explicitly contrary to his conclusion.
