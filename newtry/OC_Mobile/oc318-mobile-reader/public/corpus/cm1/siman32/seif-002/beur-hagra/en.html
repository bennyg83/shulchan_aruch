(Collected note) Likewise if he did not, etc. Proof is from the law of one who sends a fire, etc., Tosafot there. Another proof is from Sanhedrin 29a: "from where do we know that we do not plead, etc." Shach's gloss. The Mishneh LaMelech, chapter 2 of Hilchot Rotzeach, writes similarly, and so does the Rambam: "but one who hires, etc." Likewise Kiddushin 43a: "one who says to his agent, etc." According to the first Tanna he is exempt even under the laws of Heaven, except that there he is nevertheless liable under a lesser heavenly judgment. That applies specifically to bloodshed, where even if he could have saved the victim he would be liable and violates "you shall not stand idly by your fellow's blood," as written in siman 426, even to the extent of hiring assistance; all the more so where he tells another to kill. That is unlike the present case. (End of collected note.)
