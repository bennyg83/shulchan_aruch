An identifying mark. Meaning, even if the depositor was not accustomed to frequent the custodian's house. Only with heirs, who cannot assert definitively, "Our father bought this from you," do we remove the item from them by combining three factors: the depositor provides an identifying mark, he was not accustomed to be in the custodian's house, and the custodian was not financially capable of owning such an item. But if the heirs assert definitely that it belonged to their father, or if the custodian is alive and says, "It is mine; I bought it from you," or "You gave it to me as a gift," we do not remove it from his possession even where all three factors are present.
