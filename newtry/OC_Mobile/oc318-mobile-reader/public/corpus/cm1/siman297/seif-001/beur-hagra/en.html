And it is unnecessary to say if, etc. Bava Batra 52a asks whether we advance such a claim, and Bava Metzia 116a gives the same principle. And if they said, etc. Meaning that the heirs refuse to display the object. Tur explains: while the father was alive he could claim that he purchased it, through the alternative claim that he returned it, so long as witnesses had not seen and identified it in his possession. With heirs, however, beit din does not advance this uncommon claim for them. They must display the object, and if it is identified, that identification functions like testimony and it is removed from them. If the heirs themselves claim with certainty that their father told them it was his, they are believed with an oath until witnesses have actually seen and identified it. A clear identifying mark defeats both the father and the heirs; an uncertain resemblance does not. The rule that heirs do not swear to exempt themselves concerns doubtful claims, but where they advance a definite claim they swear like any other litigant. See the related discussions in simanim 69, 75, and 134. See 33, 108, 140, 150, 153.
