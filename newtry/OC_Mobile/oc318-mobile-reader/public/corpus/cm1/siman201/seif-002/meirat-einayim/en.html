For example, by the purchaser giving the seller a perutah. In such customary forms of acquisition the Sages were not concerned that the seller might say, "Your wheat burned in the attic," because, as I have already written, they were concerned about this only where the seller had already received the entire value of the sale. See the Derishah.
Or by striking palms. This does not mean giving a handshake in the sense of an oath. The discussion here is not about an oath but about modes of acquisition. Rather, in some places merchants customarily conclude a transaction after both have resolved verbally and mentally, one to sell and one to buy, by striking their forearms or hands against each other.
They give the purchaser the key. In siman 192 it was explained that land is not acquired merely through delivery of a key. Therefore this cannot mean that the room itself is acquired through transfer of the key and then the room acquires the merchandise for him. Rather, the merchants' custom is to transfer ownership of the merchandise itself through delivering the key to the room in which the goods are stored. Beit Yosef writes this. Alternatively, there the discussion concerns the strict law, while here it concerns commercial custom. Examine this carefully.
