If the custom of the locale is that it acquires. The law of situmta requires study: is it an acquisition by Torah law or only rabbinic, and does it apply to non-Jews as well? The relevant custom appears to be determined not by formal civil law but by commercial practice. Whenever merchants use a particular act with intent to conclude and finalize the transaction, that act constitutes situmta. The custom of drinking together after a transaction is not itself a situmta. It is rather a practice intended to foster goodwill between the parties, the one toward his money and the other toward his purchase, comparable to the teaching that one who received a coin from Iyov was blessed because Iyov gave generously and with a good eye. So too in a sale, when there is goodwill between buyer and seller there is blessing. Among the Jewish people this arouses blessing all the more, because they bless Hashem over the benefit, and one who blesses is himself blessed.
