If it is the custom of the country. See Shach, seif katan 1. In my view situmta does not acquire coined money. Rambam and all the poskim write that coin cannot be acquired by any ordinary mode except agav with land. This implies that merchant custom does not create an exception for coin. Ran, cited by Beit Yosef in siman 195, compares the form of coin to documentary rights that lack a physical acquisition through ordinary chalipin. We do not find that situmta alone acquires documentary rights even in places where delivery is customary; full writing and delivery are required. All the more so, where there is no explicit custom concerning coin, one certainly cannot infer from a merchant custom used for ordinary movable property that the same custom acquires money. Shach himself writes regarding land that situmta works only where that very custom is explicitly used for land. The same principle applies even more strongly to coin. Ketzot HaChoshen cites Mordechai that situmta does not acquire a davar shelo ba la'olam. Maharshal, responsum 34, writes that with a lease or concession a document can be effective even regarding future rights because commercial custom treats the document as binding, like situmta. It is possible that there the custom also rests on dina d'malchuta. Situmta itself is only a rabbinic acquisition. A rabbinic mode of acquisition is certainly no weaker than a merchant custom, yet the poskim still classify its effect as rabbinic for questions such as kiddushin. The same should therefore be true of situmta.
