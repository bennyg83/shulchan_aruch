Netivot HaMishpat writes that situmta is only a rabbinic acquisition, for a rabbinic mode of acquisition is certainly no weaker than a commercial custom. Chatam Sofer, Choshen Mishpat, siman 12, disagrees. He writes that he explained elsewhere that a voluntarily adopted commercial custom is a full Torah acquisition, stronger than acquisitions instituted by the Sages independently of commercial consent, whose force rests on the power of beit din to declare property ownerless. Some therefore hold that kiddushin effected by such a rabbinic acquisition are only rabbinic kiddushin. Situmta, however, and a commercial practice accepted voluntarily by the parties are, in his opinion, Torah acquisitions even for chametz, a firstborn animal, and kiddushin. A responsum attributed to Ramban explicitly states that acquisition by custom is biblical and exempts an animal from the laws of the firstborn. The parallel responsum is Chatam Sofer, Yoreh Deah, siman 314. There he cites Netivot's view that situmta is merely rabbinic, but rejects the analogy. At minimum, in the case there concerning a firstborn animal there was a double doubt: perhaps situmta acquires by Torah law, and even if rabbinic, perhaps a rabbinic acquisition is effective for a Torah matter. Moreover, the question indicated that the customary hand-shake was binding under the law of the kingdom in that place, and dina de-malkhuta in such a matter is unquestionably effective by Torah law. See there and Pitchei Teshuva, Yoreh Deah 320:4.
