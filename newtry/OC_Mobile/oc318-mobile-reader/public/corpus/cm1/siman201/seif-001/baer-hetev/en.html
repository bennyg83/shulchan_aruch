A mark. The Bach rules that situmta and a handshake effect acquisition only for movable property and not for land, and he infers this from the Tur's wording. The practical law requires study. Yet according to what the Sema writes at the end of this siman, "here we are dealing with custom," it would appear otherwise. Maharshal writes in the first chapter of Bava Kamma, siman 36, that presumably the same applies to renting or acquiring a house: in a place where the custom is to acquire by handing over the key, the acquisition is effective because we follow commercial custom. It appears that where there is an explicit custom even for land, perhaps the Bach would agree. The Bach merely records the custom in our place. See Responsa Rama, which appears to support this, Be'er Sheva 96b, Responsa Mabit part 1, second series siman 153, page 171, and part 2 siman 310. Shach.
