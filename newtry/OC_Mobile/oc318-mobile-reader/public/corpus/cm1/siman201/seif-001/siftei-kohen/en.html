A mark upon the merchandise. The Bach rules that situmta and a handshake operate as acquisitions only for movable property, not for land, and he infers this from the Tur. This requires study as a matter of practical law. Moreover, according to the Sema's explanation that the rule here depends upon commercial custom, the conclusion appears contrary to the Bach. Maharshal writes that the same principle presumably applies to renting or acquiring a house: in a place where the custom is to acquire through delivery of the key, the acquisition is effective, because commercial custom governs. It therefore appears that where an explicit custom exists even with respect to land, the Bach may agree, and the Bach merely records the current custom in his locale. See Responsa Rama, which appears to support this, and the additional cited responsa.
