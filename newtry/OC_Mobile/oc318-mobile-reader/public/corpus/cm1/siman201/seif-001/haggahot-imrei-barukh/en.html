(Shach, seif katan 1) The Bach ruled that this applies specifically to acquisition of movable property, etc. Note: See Responsa Beit Yosef, siman 90, which rules decisively like the Bach. It appears simple that with respect to a personal undertaking, the handshake custom among merchants does not strengthen the obligation. It is merely a commercial custom signaling that one has completed a purchase and the other a sale. The beit din agreed with me in an actual case. Afterward I reviewed the matter and found that the Rama, siman 129:5, and especially the Sema there, seif katan 15, appear to indicate that in a place where this custom is used as an acquisition, it can even create a personal obligation, such as liability as a guarantor. See also the Shach there.
