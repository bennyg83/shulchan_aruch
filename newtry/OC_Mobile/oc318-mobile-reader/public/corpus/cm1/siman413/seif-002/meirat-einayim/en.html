Whether he was injured by his load. This refers to potters and glassworkers carrying their wares who fell on the road.
Some disagree, etc. According to the way this gloss is printed and arranged before us, I initially thought the text should read: "Some disagree and hold that even if he was injured by the first person's load, the body, etc." The word "even" would then refer to the statement that if he was injured by the second person's load he is exempt, while these authorities would hold that even with the first person's load he is exempt. I later found in Rama's handwritten glosses that this gloss was actually placed earlier, on the Mechaber's statement that the first person is liable for bodily injury to the second, whether the second was injured by the first person's body or by his load. The gloss therefore means that some disagree and hold that the first is liable specifically if the second person's body was injured by the first person's body, but not if he was injured by the first person's load. This is Rosh's view. His reason is that since we rule that tripping is not itself negligence, it is sufficient that the first person's body be given the status of a pit, but not his load.
I am not the one who dug it, etc. But for damage caused by his own body he is liable, because in any event he was able to stand together with his load. See Derishah.
