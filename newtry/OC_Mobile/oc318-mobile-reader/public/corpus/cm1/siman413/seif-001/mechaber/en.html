The law of potters walking one behind another who stumble and fall. It contains three seifim.
If two potters walk one behind another, the first stumbles and falls, and the second then stumbles over him, the first is liable for the second's bodily injury if he had time to rise and failed to do so. Although his initial fall was unavoidable, remaining in the roadway when he could rise was not unavoidable. If he had no time to rise, he is exempt even if he did not warn the second, because he was occupied with his own condition. Some say he is liable if he had time to warn and failed to do so. The first is liable only for bodily injury to the second. If the second's utensils were damaged, the first is exempt, because this obstacle has the law of a pit, and a pit does not impose liability for utensils.
