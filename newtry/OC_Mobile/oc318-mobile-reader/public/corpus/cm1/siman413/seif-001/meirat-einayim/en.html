Some say he is liable if he had time to warn him and did not warn him. This is the Tur's view in the name of the Rosh at the beginning of this siman. In the Derishah here and in siman 379 I explained that the Rosh compares it to the Mishnah's case where one person walks carrying a beam and another follows carrying a barrel. If the first person stops and the barrel breaks against the beam, the first is liable if he failed to warn. The Rosh understands this even where the first stopped in order to adjust his load, and does not excuse him merely because he was occupied with the load. The Rif, by contrast, interprets the Mishnah specifically where he stopped to rest. This creates an apparent difficulty. The Tur and Rama in siman 379 rule without qualification like the Rif that if he stopped only to adjust the load he is exempt, while here they cite the Rosh's stricter view. One must say that they distinguish between the greater distraction of a beam carrier actively adjusting a heavy load and the lesser distraction of a potter or similar person who has fallen. It is possible that even the Rosh agrees to this distinction in the final law. See the Derishah to siman 379.
As explained. Meaning, in simanim 411 and 412. This is the view of the Rambam and the Rosh. The Tur, however, writes that according to the Rif, the body of the first person, who could have stood up and did not, is treated like a person who negligently causes damage. One who is negligent is liable even for damage to utensils. This creates a practical difference also for the law stated in the following seif. In the Derishah there I discussed these interpretations at length according to the Gemara.
