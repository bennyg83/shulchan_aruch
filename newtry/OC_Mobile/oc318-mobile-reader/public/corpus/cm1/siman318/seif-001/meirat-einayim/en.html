And if you do not need it, sell to others. The Gemara says, "Grind and sell, grind and store." Rashi and the Rosh explain: since you are wealthy, purchase wheat, grind it, sell the flour to others, and whatever you need for your household grind at my mill and store at home. The Mechaber copied the Rambam's abbreviated formulation. Ri, however, holds that if the landlord cannot practically find wheat to buy, grind, and resell, then although the landlord cannot compel the tenant to pay cash for grinding because the tenant can say, "I will fulfill my condition by grinding your grain," nevertheless the landlord may rescind the rental and take back the mill even during the rental term. The Rosh and Tur disagree. Since rental for a day is treated as a sale for that day, the landlord cannot revoke the rental before the term ends. The Rosh writes that the Gemara's suggestion to grind and sell is merely an added practical possibility, not the legal basis of the tenant's right. The Beit Yosef cites this dispute. It is therefore surprising that the Mechaber and Rama omit it, and especially that their reason, "if you do not need it, sell to others," might imply that if such resale is impossible the tenant must pay cash, which all agree is incorrect. In my view the Rambam and Mechaber can be understood differently. Perhaps their version concerned not a tenant who became wealthy and bought another mill, but one who simply became wealthy and no longer needed to grind his own wheat because he purchased bread in the market. In that case, if he has no wheat to grind for the landlord, the landlord may say, "Fulfill your undertaking to grind with my mill, or sell your rental right to another person," meaning place another tenant in your position whom I can serve. This is why the Rambam says only, "if you do not need it, sell to others," and does not say, "buy wheat, grind it, and sell the flour." He means sell the rental right, not the flour. Such a practical alternative is generally available, so there is no basis for the landlord to revoke the original rental.
