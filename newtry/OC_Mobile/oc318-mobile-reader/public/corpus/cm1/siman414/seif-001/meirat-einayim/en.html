The Gemara says that the Sages penalized the body of the straw and treated it as ownerless because of the benefit its owner sought to obtain by leaving it in the public domain so that people would trample it and turn it into manure. The Gemara asks whether they declared the straw ownerless only after it had already improved, when the connection between the body and the improvement is stronger, or whether they penalized it even before it became manure, in order to prevent people from leaving straw in the public domain. The question is unresolved. The Mechaber rules like the Rambam, who holds, as explained many times above, that seizure is effective in a doubtful monetary matter. Therefore he writes afterward that if another person first took possession, it is not removed from him. Rama cites the alternative view, that of Ri and the Rosh, that seizure is ineffective in such a doubt.
