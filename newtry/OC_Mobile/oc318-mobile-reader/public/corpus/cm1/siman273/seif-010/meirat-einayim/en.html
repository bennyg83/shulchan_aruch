For one day, for one month, etc. Meaning that he did not abandon it absolutely, but only for a limited period, even a long period such as a sabbatical cycle. Since he did not abandon it permanently, even after three days he may retract. It remains subject to tithes before he retracts as well, until either he or another person makes a full acquisition from its ownerless status. The Rosh explains from the Gemara: Since people do not ordinarily declare property ownerless only temporarily, and this person acted unusually, the Sages said it should not have the status of ownerless property for exemption from tithes until it actually comes into another possessor's domain. Once someone acquires it, however, the temporary abandonment is valid. We learn this from Shemittah itself, where the produce is ownerless only for the year.
