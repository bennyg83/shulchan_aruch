There, law 4. The Maggid Mishneh explains that one person spread a trap and an animal or bird was caught in it. After it was trapped but before the trapper came and took it, the landowner said, 'My field has acquired it for me.' Since the owner stands within his field, he acquires it. One who spreads a trap in another person's field without permission does not receive the rabbinic protection against taking the catch that was enacted for the sake of peace. Even if the trap has an interior space, the owner of the ground acquires if it is a guarded courtyard, as the Rama writes in the gloss. If it is not guarded, the owner acquires when he stands in the field and says, 'My field has acquired it for me.' This appears to be the Mechaber's view, and the principle is sound.
