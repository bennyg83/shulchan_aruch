And he said, "My field has acquired it for me," etc. These are the Rambam's words, cited by the Tur. The Tur comments that this is specifically where he can reach and take the animal from within the field. Then his field acquires it for him even if he says nothing. The Tur means that this case parallels the law of a found object in siman 268. There, if a deer is wounded or standing close enough that the owner can reach it while it remains inside his field, the deer is considered retained within the field and the field acquires it even without the owner saying, "My field should acquire it for me." The Rambam holds that even there such a statement is required, as the Tur cites in his name, but he agrees that the deer must be wounded or close enough to reach. The Mishnah says so explicitly, and the Mechaber rules that way at the end of siman 268. Here, however, the Mechaber does not distinguish. The Maggid Mishneh explains that according to the Rambam this case is different because once the animal is caught in the trap, the trap makes it like a wounded deer that cannot run away. It is possible that for this reason Rama does not add here the dissenting view that the statement "my field has acquired it for me" is unnecessary. More likely, Rama holds that even with a wounded deer that can be reached we rule like the Rambam that the statement is required, and only with abandoned utensils or garments do we rule like the Rosh that no statement is necessary, as Nimukei Yosef writes and as I explained in the Sema to siman 268. Certainly, if the owner of the trap first removes the animal from his trap, it belongs to him. Here the owner of the field preceded him and acquired it, because the trap had been spread in his field without permission. If the trap was spread in ownerless property, even the Rambam agrees that one who takes the animal from another's trap is considered a robber, as the Mechaber states in the preceding seif.
