Shin) There, halachah 2. The Maggid Mishneh writes that this too is straightforward.
Tav) The Maggid Mishneh writes that because this is an unguarded courtyard, the owner must be standing within the field, as explained in siman 14. Nevertheless, he may not initially hunt there, because he has no permission to enter another person's property without consent.
Alef) The Maggid Mishneh writes that a properly enclosed preserve is a guarded courtyard, and its owner acquires what enters it. A similar rule is stated regarding sending away the mother bird in Chullin 141b. Whenever an animal enters a guarded courtyard, the owner's courtyard acquires it even without his knowledge, all the more so than acquisition through a utensil, as explained below.
Bet) There, halachah 3, from the Mishnah in the fifth chapter of Gittin 59b, as the Gemara explains at the beginning of 61a concerning a line and beam. Rashi explains that these are traps and devices made by placing a long cord in the river and attaching hooks along it. Rambam rules like the first Tanna that the claim is not judicially enforceable.
Gimel) This is straightforward according to all views in the Gemara. The Rif likewise rules so, and it is agreed.
