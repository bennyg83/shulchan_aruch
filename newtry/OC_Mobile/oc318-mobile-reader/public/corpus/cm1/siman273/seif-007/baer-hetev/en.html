One. He holds that at least one person is required, so that this person hears the owner declare the property ownerless and can acquire it for himself. Without this, the property does not attain the status of ownerless property. The alternative view cited by the Rama addresses this point and holds that by Torah law it is sufficient even if one declares it ownerless privately, between himself and himself. By rabbinic law, however, all agree that three people are required. See the words of the Tur and Mechaber in Orach Chaim, siman 246, seif 3, in the gloss. See there. Ibid.
