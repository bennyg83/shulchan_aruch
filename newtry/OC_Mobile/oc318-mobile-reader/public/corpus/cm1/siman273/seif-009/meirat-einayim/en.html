During the first three days, etc. The Rambam writes this in chapter 2 of Hilchot Nedarim, based on a baraita in Nedarim. The Rosh explains that one may retract within three days because of deceivers who were accustomed to declare their produce ownerless and then return and eat it in order to exempt it from tithes. The Sages therefore enacted that for three days the owner can retract and say, "I retract my abandonment," after which no one may acquire it. Consequently, even before actual retraction, the object does not yet have the full status of ownerless property. If either the owner or another person eats from it before someone has intentionally acquired it as ownerless property, it remains subject to tithes. One may ask what the enactment accomplished, since a deceiver could still abandon the produce and immediately precede everyone else in acquiring it from ownerless status, thereby exempting it from tithes. The answer is that so long as mere speech remains retractable and acquisition requires a full act of possession, deceivers will fear that someone else may precede them and acquire it. Once the Sages enacted this rule for abandoned land because of deceivers, they applied it to all abandonment, allowing retraction within three days so long as neither the owner nor another person has actually acquired it. This is the Mechaber's conclusion, "unless he first acquired it," etc. See the Derishah for further discussion.
