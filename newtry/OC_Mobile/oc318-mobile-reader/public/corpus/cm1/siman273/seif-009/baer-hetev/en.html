Three. The Rosh explains that this enactment was made because of deceivers who would commonly declare their produce ownerless and then return and eat it in order to exempt it from maaser. Therefore the Sages ordained that for three days a person may retract and say, 'I retract my declaration of ownerlessness,' after which no one else can acquire it. Consequently, even before he retracts, it does not yet have the full status of ownerless property, and if either he or another eats from it, it remains liable for maaser so long as the person did not intend to acquire it from ownerlessness. One might ask what the Sages accomplished, since a deceiver could still declare it ownerless and immediately be first to acquire it from ownerlessness, thereby exempting it from maaser. The answer is that once he cannot retract merely by speech but must perform a complete act of acquisition, he will fear that others may acquire it before him. Once the Sages enacted this regarding ownerless land, they likewise enacted it for every declaration of ownerlessness, allowing retraction within three days so long as neither he nor another has acquired it. This is why the Mechaber concludes, 'unless he preceded and acquired it.' Ibid.
