One twenty-fourth of another species, etc. This is one quarter of a kav per se'ah, since a se'ah is six kav. At that proportion the field is prohibited because of kilayim, as stated in the laws of kilayim.
Beit din declares the entire field ownerless. The Mechaber follows his view in the Beit Yosef that the field does not become ownerless until beit din expressly announces it as hefker. The Tur, however, appears to hold that it becomes ownerless automatically. In the Derishah I explained the Tur's reason: once transgressors became numerous and people were not careful about kilayim in fields, the Sages enacted that any field found to contain one twenty-fourth of another species would itself become hefker. See there for my proof.
