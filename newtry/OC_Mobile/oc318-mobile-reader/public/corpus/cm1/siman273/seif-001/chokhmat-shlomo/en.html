Seif 7. 'But by rabbinic law it is not ownerless until he declares it ownerless before three.' Note: See Yoreh Deah siman 221, seif 9, where the Shach cites Tosafot's view that this requirement applies specifically to land, but movable property does not require three. The Bach holds that before three the owner can retract, but if he did not retract it is fully ownerless. According to both views, the same logic should apply to seif 9 regarding the period before three days, each according to his approach, for otherwise the Shach's difficulty from the law before three days remains. Yet one who studies the Ran in the first chapter of Pesachim regarding bitul will see that the Ran disagrees with Tosafot and the Bach. He asks why bitul is effective when hefker before three people is required before three days. This proves that in his view the requirement applies even to movable property and that without the rabbinically prescribed procedure there is no status of hefker at all. According to the Ran one must use the Bach and Shach's first answer in Yoreh Deah. In any event there is a dispute among the authorities, unlike the Shach and Bach in Yoreh Deah, who omit the opposing view and make it appear agreed upon. I later saw that Ketzot HaChoshen noticed this. See also my novellae to Orach Chayyim, Hilchot Pesach, siman 460, seif 3, where I wrote that common practice follows Tosafot and that the accepted halachic practice is in accordance with them.
