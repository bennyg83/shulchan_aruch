It is not hefker. See Noda BiYehuda, second edition, Yoreh Deah, siman 154, who writes that although we rule in accordance with Beit Hillel that an abandonment is not effective unless it is open to everyone, like produce in the Sabbatical year, that applies where one abandons it to all poor people but not to the wealthy, in which case no poor person can acquire it. But if a person renounces a right specifically as against one or two identified persons, it is effective. See there.
