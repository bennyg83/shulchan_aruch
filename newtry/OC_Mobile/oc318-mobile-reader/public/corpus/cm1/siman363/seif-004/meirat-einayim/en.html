And he did not prevent him from other work. Meaning, from the slave's work for his master.
If he prevented him from other work, he pays, etc. I already explained in seif 1 that this is because slaves have the law of land and are not legally stolen. Therefore we do not say that he pays their value as of the time of robbery. In the Perishah I wrote that even Rif, who disagrees regarding one who actually robs a slave and treats him like movable property, as the Tur cites here, agrees in the present case where the person did not take the slave for the purpose of robbery but merely used him for labor. He must therefore pay the value of the lost labor.
