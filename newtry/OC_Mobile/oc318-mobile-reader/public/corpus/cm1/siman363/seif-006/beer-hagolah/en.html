He explains there that this is merely indirect causation. If the homeowner needed to rent another dwelling for himself, it appears to me that even though one who lives in another's house ordinarily need not pay where the owner suffers no loss, here he must pay. Rosh writes in his rulings on the following law that the Gemara's expression, 'What did he deprive him of and what damage did he cause him?' implies that where there is actual deprivation and damage, liability is obvious. If liability is imposed, it is because the occupant consumed the other's loss. Beit Yosef cites this in seif 6. Mordechai writes in the name of Raavan that nowadays all houses are ordinarily rented, and Rama cites this below.
The Maggid Mishneh writes that this follows Rif in the Halakhot, since this measure is not mentioned in the Gemara at all. Tur likewise cites Rif, and writes that Rosh agreed for the reason stated above.
