For if he wishes to benefit, etc. This implies that where the owner cannot profit from the property, the other person may compel him even initially. See what I wrote above in siman 170, seif 1, in the name of Noda BiYehuda, second edition, siman 54, that this follows only Rambam, whereas according to Rosh the law differs. Beit Ephraim, Choshen Mishpat, siman 49, likewise writes that Rama's reason that the owner could profit but does not wish to is not supported by the other decisors. Even where that reason is absent, one cannot ordinarily compel the owner initially against his protest. See Nimukei Yosef in the name of Raah, Tosafos, Bava Batra 12b, and Shitah Mekubetzet, Bava Kamma 20. See also Biur HaGra.
