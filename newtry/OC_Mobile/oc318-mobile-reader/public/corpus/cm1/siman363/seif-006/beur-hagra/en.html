Even though he incurred expenses, etc. See Tosafot there, who states that this applies even so, and Nimukei Yosef.
(Supplement) Even though he incurred expenses, etc. All robbers pay according to the value at the time of robbery, as Rosh writes there. This applies even to land when it is not normally rented. See Tosafot, Bava Kamma 57a, and the Gemara's explanation there.
Even if it was, etc. As explained there on 97a, where the Gemara establishes the case as one in which it was not normally rented.
(Supplement) This one benefits and the other, etc. The reason is not merely that the owner benefits because an inhabited house is preserved from desolation. Rosh writes that this was stated only for added force and is not essential, in accordance with Rabbi Ami's question, 'What loss did he cause?' Yet the passage on 97a appears otherwise. It may mean that there the occupant caused no loss and also benefited the owner, whereas here he caused deterioration. Maharshal explains similarly, though this is strained. Rashba's view is preferable: ordinary residence causes some wear, but the benefit of preserving the house offsets that loss. Where there is no loss at all, the owner's benefit is unnecessary. This is also clear from the case of the ship on 97a. However, the statement that even if he diminished it he pays only the diminution requires study in light of seif 7.
And specifically, etc. See Tosafot there.
(Supplement) And specifically, etc. Bava Kamma 21a cites the verse concerning desolation. Rosh brings this and means that even initially it is permitted for this reason, as indicated by the case of Rav Yosef's house on 97a. Yet Rosh's words require study, for the Gemara implies that without the benefit of preventing desolation he would be liable.
(Supplement) And specifically, etc. Since the Gemara asks whether he is liable because he benefited, it follows that the owner cannot compel him initially to occupy it. See Tosafot.
(Supplement) Siman 369, seif 6, in the gloss, 'and specifically, etc.' See Tosafot, Bava Batra 12b, citing Ritzba.
But if the courtyard, etc. This follows the Gemara's statement, 'What did he do to him?' If he caused a loss, he is liable. The same applies throughout the law of damages where one party suffers a loss even though the other receives no benefit. Nimukei Yosef and Be'er HaGolah agree. Tosafot and Rashba disagree. The Mishna did not impose liability for loss without benefit, yet where there is benefit it does. Rava's objection means that even where the property is normally rented but the occupant does not normally rent, he is exempt, so loss alone is insufficient. The Mishna's case is therefore not identical to ours.
Nevertheless, etc. As stated above in the gloss, even though he incurred expenses, liability here arises only because he consumed the owner's loss.
(Supplement) Nevertheless, if, etc. We rule that indirect damage is exempt. Likewise, one who accepted a field and left it fallow is liable only because ordinary contractual language is interpreted accordingly. The Yerushalmi states that one who prevents another from using his money is exempt. Residence is different because the occupant directly consumed the owner's loss, as Rosh writes.
