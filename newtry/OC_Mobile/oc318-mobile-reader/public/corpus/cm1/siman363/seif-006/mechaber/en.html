One who resides in another's courtyard without permission, after being told to leave and refusing, must pay the full rent. If not told to leave and the courtyard is not ordinarily rented, he pays no rent, even if he forcibly excluded the owner, and even if it had previously been rented but was not available for rent at that time, because one benefits while the other suffers no loss. Rama: This applies only after the fact. He cannot compel the owner initially to allow free residence. The rule against conduct like Sodom applies where the owner could not otherwise benefit, but here the owner could rent it for profit and merely chooses not to, so he cannot be compelled to provide it free. If the courtyard is ordinarily rented, even one who does not usually rent must pay because he caused monetary loss. Rama: If he did not live there but merely dispossessed the owner, he is exempt from rent. Ordinary houses today are presumed intended for rental even if never previously rented.
