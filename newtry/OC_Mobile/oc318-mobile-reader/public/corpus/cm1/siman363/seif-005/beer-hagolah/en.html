The Maggid Mishneh writes that Rambam holds that where the object is not normally used for hire, even if the robber took it intending to rent it out, he must pay only its depreciation, because he is a robber. This is primary. Rambam's ruling applies even where the rental value exceeds the depreciation.

See Beit Yosef, who questions this: since we rule that all robbers pay according to the value at the time of the robbery, why should it matter whether the robber used the object himself or rented it to another? Bach writes that Mordechai at the beginning of HaGozel Batra cites Rabbi Eliezer of Metz, who ruled, with the agreement of all the leading authorities, that any property not legally acquired by the person holding it remains in the owner's domain. Thus the rent belongs to the owner as though the owner himself had rented it out. Bach brings extensive proofs and concludes that we rule like the Ramah, Mordechai, and the consensus of the leading authorities. See also siman 307, seif 5, for a similar case.
