We assess how much it diminished. Rashba writes that this person still does not have the full law of a robber. A full robber who causes a permanent deterioration does not necessarily have the broken-pieces appraisal in his favor in the same manner. If the remnants fall in value after the robbery-law payment point, that market loss can fall on the robber. Here, by contrast, the person did not intend to steal the object but only to use it. Although an unauthorized borrower is compared to a robber for accident liability, Chazal did not impose the full robber-law stringency in every respect. Therefore we assess the diminution in the object itself. If the remaining damaged object later becomes cheaper in the market, that later decline does not automatically remain on the user. But if the remnants are themselves completely lost through accident while still in his responsibility, he is certainly liable under the ordinary law of an unauthorized borrower.
