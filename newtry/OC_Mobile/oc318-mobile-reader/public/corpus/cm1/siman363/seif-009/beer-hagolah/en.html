There, seif 7, from the statement on 21a that one who rents a house from Reuven pays rent to Shimon, etc., established there as a courtyard normally rented. Nimukei Yosef writes in the name of the Ramah that since the Gemara does not distinguish between one who entered intending to pay rent and one who did not, we learn that whenever the property is not normally rented by the owner, even if this occupant entered intending to pay another person, he need not pay the true owner. Just as the supposed lessor's rental is a mistaken transaction because it turns out the property was not his, so too the occupant's agreement is mistaken. Even money already paid is considered a mistaken waiver. This is also implied by the incident of Rav Anan moving a boundary marker in Bava Batra chapter 3, 41a. Raavad and Ritva likewise agree.
The wording of Nimukei Yosef, cited by Beit Yosef.
