Even if he paid the one from whom he rented it, etc. Nimukei Yosef explains: Just as the rental from the supposed landlord is a mistaken transaction because it is now established that the house was not his, so too from the renter's perspective the lease was a mistaken transaction. He rented from someone who had no right in the property. The renter therefore became obligated to neither claimant by virtue of that lease, and it is as though he never rented from him. The supposed landlord must return the rent because waiver based on such a mistake is not a valid waiver. For the same reason, paying rent does not constitute an effective revelation of intent as in the preceding seif. Here the lease itself was void from the beginning. In the Perishah I further wrote that a revelation of intent can help only where he revealed his intent to pay the true owner, not where he merely paid another person.
