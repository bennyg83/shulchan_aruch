There. Tosafot Rosh writes that Rabbenu Yonah inferred from the wording in Bava Kamma, 'the house is found to belong to Shimon, he pays rent to Shimon,' rather than 'he pays Shimon's rent.' Nimukei Yosef cites this and writes that Ritva likewise holds so.

He explains there that the rental agreement made with Reuven was based on mistake and has no effect.
Bach writes, however, that Terumat HaDeshen, siman 317, holds that where one benefits and the other suffers loss, even if the owner knowingly allowed it, the loss is not waived. Maharik similarly rules in the name of Rabbenu Ephraim that if one says to his fellow, 'eat with me,' and he eats, he must pay, as above at the end of siman 246. Beit Yosef in Yoreh Deah siman 166 likewise cites the students of Rashba that where a courtyard is normally rented and the lender lives there, although the matter is only rabbinic interest and is not recoverable in court as interest, he nevertheless owes rent because he caused monetary loss. It appears to me that since the authorities dispute, the burden of proof rests on the claimant. If the occupant is in possession, he may say that he relies on Tashbetz. If the homeowner is in possession, he may say that he relies on Maharik and Terumat HaDeshen. This is Bach's conclusion in responsum.
