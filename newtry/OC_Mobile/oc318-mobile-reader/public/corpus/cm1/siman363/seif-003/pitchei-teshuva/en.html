For if he wishes to benefit, etc. This wording implies that where the owner cannot profit from the property, the other person may compel him even initially. See what I wrote above in siman 170, seif 1, under the words "all of them," in the name of Noda BiYehuda, second edition, siman 54, that this follows only Rambam at the beginning of siman 174, whereas according to Rosh the law differs. Responsa Beit Ephraim, Choshen Mishpat, siman 49, likewise questions Rama's explanation here that the owner could profit but chooses not to, which comes from Mordechai. The other decisors imply that even where that reason does not apply, one cannot initially compel the owner. See Nimukei Yosef, Bava Kamma, in the name of Raah. This is also explicit in Tosafos, Bava Batra 12b, in the name of Ritzba and Rabbi Yeshayah, cited in Shitah Mekubetzet, Bava Kamma 20, who explain that the Torah needed a special verse concerning a firstborn receiving adjacent portions because ordinarily one does not compel another to avoid the trait of Sodom when he affirmatively protests and prevents the arrangement. See there. See also Biur HaGra.
