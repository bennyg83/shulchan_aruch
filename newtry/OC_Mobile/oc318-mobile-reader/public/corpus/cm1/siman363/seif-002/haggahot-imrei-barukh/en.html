(Shach, seif katan 7) All his words are not convincing. Note: See Responsa Sha'ar Ephraim, siman 28, and Chok Yaakov, siman 443, seif katan 7. See also Rashba's novellae to Gittin 54b, s.v. 'Abaye said,' where he explains that the bailee claims the produce became impure through his negligence. Abaye therefore holds that he is not believed merely on his own word to say that he was negligent in his guarding, and is believed only while the matter is still within his control, through a migo; see there. It appears clear from this that even if the bailee was negligent he is exempt from payment, apparently because the bailee too can say, 'Here is your property before you.' This is even more explicit from Yoreh Deah, siman 127, seif 2: 'When does this apply, etc., where he loses his entire wage, such as when he says it became prohibited wine through his negligence.' One cannot object that, if so, why is he believed according to Abaye through the migo that he could have rendered it impure directly? If he actively rendered it impure, he would have to pay if it were recognizable that he did so intentionally. See the Rosh there, who rejects the migo explanation for this reason. Perhaps according to the authorities who explain his credibility through migo, their view is that even if he actively rendered it impure he could exempt himself by making a definite claim that it had already become impure through negligent guarding. Even if the depositor would not otherwise have to believe him, once the bailee himself knows it had already become impure, he cannot be charged for subsequently making it impure. It is even possible that according to strict law he should do so in front of the depositor in order to save him from eating impure produce. This is a novel ruling and requires great study, especially since it is not stated in the Rosh cited above.
