One who robbed chametz and Pesach passed over it. Pri Megadim, Orach Chaim, Mishbetzot Zahav seif katan 1, writes that if one stole a very valuable etrog and returned it after the festival, perhaps he can discharge himself by saying, "Here is your property before you," just as with chametz after Pesach. On the other hand, the case of chametz after Pesach is unusual and the Sages may not have extended liability for indirect damage to it, whereas the loss in the value of an etrog after the festival may be no less than legally cognizable indirect damage. Similarly, if one steals merchandise worth one thousand on market day and returns it after the market when it is worth very little, can he simply return the same merchandise? Pri Megadim leaves the matter unresolved. Responsa Beit Shmuel Acharon, Choshen Mishpat siman 5, includes a responsum of Rabbi Moshe Shlomo Zalman, av beit din of Krakow, who rules that with an etrog the thief cannot say, "Here is your property before you," because everyone recognizes that after Sukkot it has lost its mitzvah value and is worth essentially only its food value. He compares this to a stolen coin invalidated in every country, where the visible loss prevents the thief from returning the same coin as full restitution. Chametz after Pesach differs because a person unaware of the prohibition sees no physical defect in the chametz itself. Rabbi Tzvi Hirsch of Hamburg agrees that a thief who stole an etrog must pay its festival value after Sukkot because the loss is publicly recognizable. Pitchei Teshuva questions whether this conclusion can be accepted as law. Their reasoning fits Rambam and Shulchan Aruch, who follow Rav Yehudah that a coin invalidated by the government is treated like a physically cracked coin. But Rosh, Tur, Rama, and several authorities cited by Shach follow Rav Huna that even if a coin is invalidated in every country the thief may still say, "Here is your property before you." That view appears to reject the distinction based on visible and universally recognized loss. The matter therefore requires analysis.
