Or he robbed fruit and all of it rotted. "All" is not literal, because the Mechaber immediately afterward says that where only part rotted he can say, "Here is yours before you." Rather, a majority is treated as the whole, and an exact half is treated like a majority for this law.
Like one who robbed a utensil and broke it. The Tur and Mechaber wrote this law in the preceding siman, seif 11. There it was explained that if the item increased in value after the robbery, there is a distinction between a utensil that broke on its own and one the robber broke intentionally: in the latter case he pays according to its value when he destroyed it. Here the Mechaber should technically have compared the case to a utensil that broke on its own, because the loss was not caused directly by the robber. He instead says "and broke it" because the present discussion does not concern appreciation but rather depreciation. His point is simply that just as it is obvious that one who breaks a utensil with his hands cannot say, "Here is yours before you," so too in these cases where the object has undergone a substantial irreversible loss he cannot return it as is.
Or he robbed slaves and they aged. Although aged slaves may become nearly worthless, similar to an animal that aged, he can nevertheless say, "Here is yours before you." The reason is that a slave is compared to land, at least according to Rashbam in Torah-law matters such as robbery, ona'ah, and oaths, based on the verse, "You shall bequeath them to your children after you, to inherit a possession," and "possession" refers to land. Land cannot be stolen in the legal sense; it always remains in the victim's domain. This operates both for benefit, such as appreciation or fruits, and for loss, such as deterioration that occurs on its own. Movable property and animals, by contrast, enter the robber's legal domain upon robbery for purposes of later appreciation and deterioration, so that he must pay according to the value at the time of robbery, unless the loss is reversible and therefore not treated as legal damage.
Or terumah became impure, or he robbed chametz, etc. The reason is that even though such property became completely unusable to the victim in its special status, the physical produce or bread itself did not change from other produce or bread. It is physically the same type of object as at the time of the robbery. Therefore the robber can say, "Here is yours before you." Currency invalidated by the government is different. Once the image of that coin no longer circulates anywhere in the kingdom and a different coin has replaced it, the loss is physically apparent and the coin's commercial identity has changed. See the Perishah.
And a transgression was committed with it. For example, an animal used for bestiality, worshipped as idolatry, or designated for idolatrous use.
