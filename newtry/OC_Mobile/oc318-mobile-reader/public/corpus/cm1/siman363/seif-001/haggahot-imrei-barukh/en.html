(Shach, seif katan 2) Regarding a creditor it is different because he does not rely upon it, etc. Note: It is true that Rashbam writes this in Bava Batra 128a, and Tosafot in Yevamot 39a likewise. But the Shach's statement that the same rationale of lack of reliance applies to a prozbul requires study. A prozbul may be written even on the basis of a perforated flowerpot and certainly on even a minimal interest in land. With prozbul one must therefore use Tosafot's reason that the rule is rabbinic. Since Reish Lakish brings proof from prozbul that a creditor does not collect from slaves, saying, "I know only a baraita," and treats the judges of Nehardea and the others as having erred in an explicit mishnah, we must say as Tosafot there: he holds that liens are not Torah law. Therefore collection from slaves is comparable to the rabbinic rules of prozbul and agav.
