And the festival passed over it. Chok Yaakov rules that during the festival the robber cannot say, "Here is yours before you," because everyone is commanded to destroy the chametz and it is therefore already like something destined to be burned. This is difficult. If its prohibited status is treated as a visible physical change, the robber should already acquire it through change. If it is only nonapparent damage, why can he not return it? One possible defense is that during the festival the robber himself violates the prohibition of possessing chametz and carries responsibility for unavoidable accidents. His command is to destroy it, and under the stricter view destruction specifically requires burning. Therefore, while the obligation to burn rests upon him, simply handing it back is not the proper mode of restitution. Still, if he actually hands it back and the owner accepts it, the mitzvah of returning the stolen object can be fulfilled even though the robber separately violated the Torah's command concerning chametz. The issue is therefore not simple, especially because we generally do not rule that every item destined for burning is legally treated as already burned.
