The thrower is liable. The Gemara asks whether we follow the beginning of the act or the moment the vessel actually breaks. It compares this to Rabbah's ruling that if one throws a vessel from a roof and another breaks it with a stick before it reaches the ground, the second is exempt because he broke an already doomed vessel. Rif, Rambam, and Rosh understand Rabbah to make the thrower liable and the second person exempt. Ramban, in Milchamot, develops a different reading. Rabbah stated only that the second person is exempt. The original thrower can also be exempt if liability follows the moment of actual breakage, because after being thrown the vessel has no market value. Under Ramban's reading, both can be exempt. Ramban maintains that this is actually Rif's approach. The conventional reading of Rif, however, follows Rambam and Rosh: Rabbah resolves the matter by following the beginning of the act, so the original thrower is liable and the later breaker exempt.
