And some say, etc. This follows Tosafos in Bava Kamma, who distinguish dina d'garmi from ordinary grama, since ordinary grama is exempt on 60a and in many other places. See Tosafos Bava Batra 22b and the Rosh. Tosafos themselves write that many of the proposed distinctions are difficult, and Riva ultimately explains from the Yerushalmi that liability for garmi is a rabbinic fine. The Mordechai cites that Yerushalmi and says he could not maintain many of the earlier distinctions; Rabbeinu Yitzchak also hesitated about them. The Rama therefore does not list all the distinctions but cites Riva's broader approach below, and the Shach agrees.

A scribe, etc. See siman 95 seif 1 in the gloss.
Some say that every, etc. According to Riva, all such garmi liability is a rabbinic fine. The Rama states this briefly. The Ramban has another extensive approach, holding that liability is fundamentally Biblical, though he explains the Yerushalmi differently. See the Shach, who also disputes aspects of that position.
(Collected note) Some say that in every matter, etc. One cannot object that a fine could not be adjudicated in Bavel, because where the matter is common and involves financial loss the courts do adjudicate it. The Rashba writes this, though he also notes that the Gemara's language may indicate that dina d'garmi is true compensatory law rather than a discretionary fine. (End of note.)
(Collected note) Some say that every grama, etc. According to this opinion dina d'garmi is a fine. This raises the question how later Babylonian courts imposed it, as in the incidents involving Rafram and Rav Ashi. See siman 1 seif 4 and the Ramban's lengthy discussion. It can be answered that a common case involving financial loss is adjudicated even if the liability is rabbinic. (End of note.)
(Collected note) Some say, etc. The Rashba explains the Gemara's question "law or fine" as asking whether the judge acted from established law or imposed an emergency fine. The answer "it is a Mishna" means the ruling came from a source rather than personal discretion, not necessarily that the liability is Biblical. Even if it is a rabbinic fine, common financially harmful cases were adjudicated in Bavel. (End of note.)
