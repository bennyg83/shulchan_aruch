The distinction between indirect causation in damages and dina d'garmi, and the law of one who throws a vessel from a roof. It contains four seifim:
We rule like Rabbi Meir, who imposes liability for dina d'garmi. Liability for dina d'garmi applies specifically to a person; for damage caused by an animal, all agree that there is no such liability. Nimukei Yosef, chapter HaParah. Therefore, one who pushes another person's coin so that it rolls into the sea is liable even though he never lifted it. Likewise, one who defaces another person's coin and removes its stamped form is liable even though he did not reduce its physical substance. Similarly, one who sells a debt document to another and then forgives the underlying debt, where the forgiveness is effective, must reimburse the purchaser, as explained in siman 66.
