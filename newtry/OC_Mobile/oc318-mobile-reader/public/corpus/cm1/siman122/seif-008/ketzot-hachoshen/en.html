A husband generally does not need an authorization document to litigate concerning his wife's land because husband and wife have overlapping interests in the property. Rashba and Ran explain that this is so particularly where the wife is present in the city and does not object, because the husband has his own fruit rights and may litigate concerning the property. If the wife is absent, or if she is present and expressly objects, the husband's independent interest may not be enough to litigate the whole corpus on her behalf without authorization. The rule therefore does not mean that marriage automatically makes the husband unrestricted owner of all litigation rights. It rests on the combination of his own legal interest and the wife's presumed consent where circumstances support it.
