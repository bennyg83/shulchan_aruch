That Shimon will give him his money. See Shach, who rules that if the money is still in the borrower's possession, the depositor can retract. Ketzot HaChoshen nevertheless writes that he must pay the value of the promised benefit, measured by how much the use of the money for the promised period was worth, because the other person exerted himself and traveled in order to collect the funds. This is also difficult. According to that approach, the arrangement would be actual ribbit, for he is working without pay because the other will later lend him money. The permissibility must therefore rest on the reasoning Taz gives in Yoreh Deah siman 143 seif katan 24: the purchaser exerts himself for his own benefit, so that the money will eventually come into his possession. If so, he cannot later demand wages for that effort.
