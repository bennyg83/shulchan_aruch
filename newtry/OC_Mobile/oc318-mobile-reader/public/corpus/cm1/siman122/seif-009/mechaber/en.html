If one of several brothers whose estate has not been divided, or one of several partners, comes to bring a claim, he claims the entire amount. Since he owns a share of the money, he does not need a power of attorney from the other partners. If judgment is entered against him, the other partner cannot tell the partner who litigated, 'Had I been there, I would have made other claims and caused the opposing party to be held liable,' because the litigating partner may answer, 'Why did you not come and bring the claim as well?' Therefore, if the other partner was in another city, he may proceed again against the opposing party and say, 'I do not accept everything my partner claimed.' For this reason, the defendant may insist, 'Either litigate with me only regarding your share or bring a power of attorney. I hold money belonging to both of you, both of you are my opposing parties, and tomorrow your brother or partner may come and sue me again.' This is explained further in siman 176, se'ifim 25 and 26.
