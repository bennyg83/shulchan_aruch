His share. This refers to their inheritance, through which they automatically became partners. Regarding ordinary partners the text need not say that they have not divided, because once they divide they are no longer partners. See Responsa Maharashdam 68, 136, and 162. Shach.
The whole. Siman 77, seif 9, explicitly implies that if it is not known that the absent partner heard of the proceedings, then even if he is in the city the defendant need not give the entire amount to the partner who appeared until the other is notified. The distinction is that there the issue is actual payment. We therefore require certainty that the other partner heard and chose not to come, because otherwise perhaps he never heard, may later sue separately, and the defendant might be unable to recover what he already paid. Here, however, for purposes of litigation alone, we assume in an ordinary case that the partner in town heard, and the defendant must litigate with the partner who appears. If it is later established that the other did not hear, he can litigate again, and the defendant suffers no irretrievable loss. But if it is definitely known that the other partner did not hear, even if he is in the city the defendant need litigate only regarding the appearing partner's share. The Mechaber himself distinguishes this way there: even though the defendant must litigate regarding the whole, he need pay only half to the appearing partner and deposit the other half with beit din. The same applies here. Shach.
Other. One might ask that if the absent partner has other claims, then even if he was in the city he should not be bound by the other partner's litigation, as the Tur says here and in siman 176, and as the Mechaber states there. The Sema answers that when the partner was not in the city, even if we do not know that he has any other defense, the judgment rendered against the appearing partner does not bind him because had he been present he might have advanced some argument that would have prevented liability. A practical consequence is that once he later appears, he receives a fresh beit din payment period from the time he litigates, and also time to prepare for the litigation. Tur. If he was in the city, however, he should have come. The Shach disagrees. The Mechaber follows the Rambam, who disagrees with the Tur's rule that an absent partner with other defenses is not bound. Bedek HaBayit says this explicitly. Thus when the Mechaber writes that the matter will be explained further in siman 176, he means that a dissenting view will be recorded there, namely that of the Rosh and Tur. As a matter of law, the Shach holds like the Rambam: when the partner was in the city, even if he has other arguments he cannot reopen the judgment. Several authorities agree.
To delay. The Sema writes that if the other partner is in the city, both must appear. The Bach adds that sometimes the defendant may say he will not litigate with only one partner until the other comes, because the appearing partner may be more willing to advance false claims than he would in the other's presence. The Bach says the law is true and uncontested. The Shach writes that he does not understand this. The Rambam and Tur clearly disagree with such a limitation, for their words imply that when the partner is in the city the defendant cannot postpone litigation on any ground and must litigate with the appearing partner regarding the entire claim. The Ran and other authorities likewise imply this. The Shach rules accordingly.
