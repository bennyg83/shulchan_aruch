One half or one third. See Shach, seif katan 21, who distinguishes between a debt evidenced by a note and land or movable property. What an authorized representative succeeds in extracting from a debt is comparable to a seller who says, "I sell you the proceeds that will be received when the wine is sold," which is a transfer of something not yet in existence, as explained in siman 209. Before collection, the money of the loan is not the creditor's property, since a loan is given to be spent. Land and movable property, by contrast, already belong to the claimant. Ketzot HaChoshen writes that where the representative is to receive wages, since the principal becomes monetarily liable to him, the rule of "since he can litigate the case" applies. This is astonishing. The worker has no proprietary right in the object in dispute; he has only a monetary claim that arises after collection. There is therefore no basis for the principle of migo de mishtai dina.
