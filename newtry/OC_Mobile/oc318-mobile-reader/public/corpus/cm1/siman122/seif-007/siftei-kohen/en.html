A deed of authorization. Maharshal gives a concise proper form in Bava Kamma, siman 15. Printed formularies contain a fuller version. Maharshal adds that instead of writing, “Before us, the undersigned witnesses,” the principal may write, “I, the undersigned,” provided the acquisition occurred before witnesses and the document states that the transfer and declarations were made before them. I do not understand why the acquisition itself must be witnessed. An acquisition is valid even privately, for witnesses were created only to prevent falsehood, as all authorities rule. Perhaps Maharshal recommended witnesses only initially, as an added precaution for Raavad’s minority view that not everyone accepts an unwitnessed acquisition. After the fact, it is certainly valid, since the authorities disagree with Raavad. Moreover, even Raavad concedes an incidental transfer through land, and authorization forms expressly use that method. In my view, Raavad’s concern need not be followed even initially; he himself states it only as a possible disagreement, not a definitive ruling.
