That it is written. If the authorization does not contain the words permitting litigation, and also lacks the clause binding the principal to every result of the litigation, it is invalid. When the text later says, "and if the clause binding him to every result is not written," it refers to an authorization that does contain the express litigation clause. However, where the representative actually acquires the underlying property, such as movable property transferred by a kinyan sudar, deposited money transferred together with land, or a document transferred by writing and delivery, the authorization is valid even without the standard agency clauses. Although he does not acquire by virtue of agency, he acquires by virtue of the actual transfer, and the defendant must litigate with him. This is explicit in the Ir Shushan and is correct. Shach.
Authorization. See Maharshal, chapter Merubah, siman 15, for a concise and accurate form. A full form also appears in printed collections of legal documents, though at somewhat greater length. Shach.
Date. It appears proper to include a date because it can matter regarding revocation, as stated in siman 123, seif 3. Responsa Rama 44 writes that initially one certainly should neither antedate nor postdate an authorization, although if this was done the document remains valid. See also simanim 42 and 43 there.
I was not coerced. This too is only customary language. Maharshal does not include it in his form and implies that it was not his custom to write it. He likewise writes that one does not include revocation of prior declarations in a monetary authorization, nor the clause that it is not an asmachta. Printed document forms, however, include "I was not coerced," revocation of declarations, and the clause excluding asmachta, and that is the common practice. Shach.
