Responsa Chacham Tzvi, siman 169, discusses three congregations in one city. One congregation sued another and demanded that each side select a dayan and that those dayanim select a third. The defendant congregation answered that because the dispute concerned communal affairs, the third congregation also had to appear, since the matter affected all three. The plaintiff congregation refused. Chacham Tzvi rules for the defendants. According to the Semag, where two partners are in the city both must appear, and the Bach writes that all authorities agree. On that view it is obvious that the defendants need not answer the claim of one congregation unless the third, their partner in the matter, also appears. Even according to the Shach, who maintains that the Rambam, Tur, and other authorities disagree, they would concede here. Their dispute concerns property capable of division, such as land or movable property. Communal affairs, however, cannot be divided into one partner's separate share: if they prevail, both prevail, and if they lose, both lose. In such a matter even those authorities agree that the defendants need not answer one partner until the other appears. One might object that this would allow one absent partner to defeat the plaintiff's rights. Chacham Tzvi answers that the responsum of Maimonidean authorities, siman 13 in the laws of partners, speaks of a partner who is overseas and may never return. There the defendant must answer the partner who is present so that the plaintiff not lose his rights indefinitely. But where both are present in the city, before compelling the defendant to litigate against one, beit din can compel the other partner to appear as well. If, however, the absent plaintiff agrees in advance to be bound by whatever judgment is entered regarding the plaintiff who appears, and not to alter that judgment later, there is no reason to prevent adjudication, for the defendant suffers no prejudice. This is except according to the Bach's explanation of the Sema, that the defendant may say, "Your partner would not dare make this claim against me, and therefore I will not answer you alone." According to that rationale, even if the absent partner agrees to accept the result, the defendant cannot be forced to answer until both appear. Accordingly, in the case before him the defendants can say, "We rely on the Semag, as explained and ruled by the Bach," and they need not answer the plaintiff congregation until the third congregation, their partner, also appears.
