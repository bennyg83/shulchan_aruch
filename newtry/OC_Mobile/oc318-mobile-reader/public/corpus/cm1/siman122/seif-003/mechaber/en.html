If a person granted one person a power of attorney and then wishes to revoke the agency and authorize another, he may do so. {Rama: If he wishes, he may state that the first person's agency also remains in force, and each of them will be an authorized agent in the matter, as stated in Responsa of the Rosh, rule 62, responsum 4.} The defendant cannot repel the authorized agent by saying, 'Perhaps the principal revoked your agency and authorized another person.' The agent may answer, 'Give me what you hold, and keep this power of attorney. If the principal revoked my agency, he caused his own loss.' The defendant is exempt, because he paid under the power of attorney. Similarly, if the authorized agent litigated with the defendant and judgment was entered against the agent, the principal cannot overturn the judgment by claiming that he had already revoked the agency when the litigation occurred. {Rama: If the authorized agent knew his agency had been revoked, however, even though the defendant did not know, the judgment is void and the case must be retried.}
