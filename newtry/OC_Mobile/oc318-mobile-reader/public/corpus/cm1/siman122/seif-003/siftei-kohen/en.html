However, if he knew. Beit Yosef attributes this limitation to Rosh. In my view, Rosh agrees with Rambam. The principal may revoke by telling the attorney, “I no longer wish you to act for me,” and taking back the authorization. But if he leaves the authorization in the attorney’s hands, he cannot secretly revoke it as against a defendant who pays or litigates on the strength of that document, for the defendant retains the authorization. Rosh’s discussion in Bava Kamma concerns the former case; his discussion elsewhere concerns the latter. Tur understood Rosh differently, but the better reading is as stated. Accordingly, even if the attorney knew of the revocation, where the defendant did not know, litigated on the basis of the authorization, and kept it, the matter is not relitigated. Mechaber may also agree. Beit Yosef mentioned the attorney’s knowledge only while explaining Rosh, whereas Rambam makes the defendant’s reliance on the document decisive. This is why Mechaber omitted the limitation.
