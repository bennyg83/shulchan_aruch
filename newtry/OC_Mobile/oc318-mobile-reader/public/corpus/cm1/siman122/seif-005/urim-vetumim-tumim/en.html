And acquire. The Sema wonders why the Tur omitted the word "acquire" from the standard wording of an authorization, since according to the Rif the omission matters. See there. The Shach cites Maharshal that this is a scribal error. It seems to me that according to the Rambam, who requires the principal to transfer ownership to the authorized agent by a fully effective kinyan, the word "acquire" is certainly unnecessary. What additional purpose could that word serve after a completed and legally effective acquisition? Therefore, when the Tur cites the Rambam, he properly omits the word "and acquire." But according to the Rama's ultimate position, which appears to be that an intrinsically effective kinyan is not necessary, and even regarding coins a kinyan sudar is sufficient for authorization, the word "acquire" is certainly required to express that the principal is conferring upon him the necessary right, because the kinyan itself does not actually transfer the coins. The Rif's responsum concerns that form of authorization. This resolves the matter precisely.
Half, etc. Maharshal holds that granting the authorized agent a partial share suffices only when that share is transferred by a fully effective kinyan. If the principal uses merely a kinyan sudar, which does not effect acquisition of a loan or coins, granting half does not suffice and the authorization must cover the entire claim. The Shach agrees. I do not understand this. The Raavan and Mordechai explain that the Nehardeans' requirement to write "litigate and acquire" arose because under strict law one would need transfer of the underlying property, as is clear in the chapter HaGozel. The Nehardeans instituted that a mere kinyan suffices, provided the instrument says, "Go, litigate." Regarding this Abaye says that if he writes an authorization for half, then because the agent may litigate regarding half he may litigate regarding all. This plainly shows that even where the kinyan itself is not intrinsically effective to transfer the underlying property, granting a half interest suffices. Even if this is not expressly demonstrated from the views of all the other authorities, why create an unnecessary dispute among them? The Shulchan Aruch therefore rules without qualification, and this is the primary ruling.
