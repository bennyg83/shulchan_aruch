The phrase "only in such a case" concerns an agreement giving the agent a share of whatever he recovers from the debt. Sema cites Yam Shel Shlomo that if the principal gives the agent one third of whatever may later be collected, the gift resembles transfer of the future fruits of a palm tree and does not presently take effect. The Shach adds that the rabbinic authorization rules do not automatically validate such a substantive transfer merely because they recognize the agent for litigation. The distinction is between agency and ownership. The Sages can authorize a representative to sue and collect on another's behalf even where the underlying claim cannot presently be transferred as property. But if the parties intend the representative himself to own a percentage of future recovery, ordinary acquisition rules apply. A gift of future, uncollected proceeds is therefore ineffective without a legally valid structure. Once the agent has actually collected and the principal has not retracted, later possession can sometimes validate the arrangement, but that is a separate rule from the initial authorization.
