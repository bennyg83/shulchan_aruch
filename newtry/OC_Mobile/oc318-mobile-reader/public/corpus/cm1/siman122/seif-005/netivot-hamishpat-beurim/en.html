And he wished to revoke the agency. See Shach seif katan 9. Although death does not itself revoke a power of attorney where the claim was transferred by agav, the principal can nevertheless revoke it. The reason seems to be Ramban's explanation of authorization. Although it involves a form of acquisition, the agent may not retain the money for himself, because the transfer is comparable to a gift on condition that it be returned. It must therefore be understood as a gift on condition that it be returned whenever the principal demands it. Consequently, even after the principal dies, so long as the heirs do not demand return, the gift remains effective. Death alone does not cancel it, but an express revocation by the heirs does.
