One who appoints an agent, etc. See Beer HaGolah. Proof that we are not concerned that the principal retracted may be brought from the end of the first chapter of Gittin, 14a: "Take," etc., and Shmuel says, etc., "he may retract," while regarding a deposit all agree otherwise. Nevertheless, the agent gives it to the recipient and we are not concerned that the principal retracted. As stated there regarding "Take a maneh," etc., "Shall we say that they dispute this," etc. Even according to the authority who holds that "take" is not equivalent to "acquire," only if the agent did not find the recipient does he return it to the sender; otherwise he gives it to the recipient. Likewise in Bava Kamma 104a, the Mishnah states, "He shall not give," etc., and the Gemara concludes otherwise; were the concern valid, even an appointment before witnesses should not suffice. See Milchamot there.
And some say that he is liable, etc. This is inferred from the fact that formal authorization is required, for without it the debtor may say, "You are not my litigant," etc. The Rosh writes in the chapter HaGozel that this is because we are concerned that the principal may have revoked the agency, as above. He does not write that we are concerned for the principal's death, as mentioned in the Gemara, because he holds like the Ramban in Milchamot there that we do not suspect death. As stated in chapter 3 of Gittin, even regarding a get we do not suspect that a sick or elderly person has died, except one who was already dying; likewise, those aboard a ship being battered at sea retain the presumption that they are alive for all matters, all the more so here. When the Gemara says, "Perhaps he died," its meaning is: perhaps he died and Rav Yosef bar Chama will hear of it and will not pay you, so your effort will have been for nothing; therefore let him convey it to you, etc., so that there will be no concern. However, according to the Rosh's responsum and the gloss at the end of seif 1, that even with an authorization made by acquisition together with land the debtor should not pay if he heard that the principal died, see Shach, seif katan 6, who disputes this and writes that the Rosh agrees where there was acquisition together with land. Alternatively, the Rosh may hold as the Nimukei Yosef writes there, that the Gemara is not concerned for death as such, but because, once he came to arrange the matter, he wished to make the representative's position fully secure. Accordingly, one still needs the reason that perhaps the principal revoked the agency.
