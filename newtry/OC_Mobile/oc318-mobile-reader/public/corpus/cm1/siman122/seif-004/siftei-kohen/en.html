He must make an acquisition with him, Tur. This is Rambam’s ruling at the beginning of chapter 3 of the laws of agency. Beit Yosef struggled to identify its source and suggested that an ordinary symbolic acquisition suffices for authorization, while transfer through land is needed only to convey money itself. He therefore thought Tur should have written, “Ramah writes,” rather than “Rambam writes.” In my view, Tur was exact. Rambam first says generally that one who authorizes another must make an effective acquisition. He then explains that authorization cannot concern mere words but only a monetary claim. If one has money on deposit and wishes to authorize another to collect it, ordinary symbolic acquisition does not work because coin is not acquired by exchange. He must transfer a small parcel of land and convey the money incidentally. A loan cannot be assigned by authorization under strict law because it has been given for expenditure and is treated as property not presently in existence. Rambam thus clearly requires the same acquisition that would transfer the underlying property itself. This follows from the Gemara’s cases where the money was conveyed incidentally with land. Had ordinary symbolic acquisition sufficed merely for authorization, those transactions would have been unnecessary. Ramah therefore disagrees and says that authorization is not intended to transfer the corpus of the property but only to grant standing to sue; any valid symbolic acquisition suffices, without transfer through land. Tur’s wording deliberately contrasts Ramah with Rambam. Bach’s attempt to attach Ramah’s statement to a later case is unnecessary and incorrect.
He must write, “Litigate and acquire.” Mechaber likewise requires the word “acquire” below, seif 6. Tur’s sample form in seif 8 omits it. Sema notes the issue. Maharshal writes that Tur contains a scribal error, for Tur’s abridged rulings do include the word.
Or other similar language. Rashba explains that the wording must indicate a transfer of rights. This follows Rambam’s view that authorization requires acquisition of the underlying property. According to our ruling below, siman 123, that one may appoint an agent even where the property itself cannot be transferred, any similar wording that clearly appoints him as agent is sufficient.
He need not answer him. Sema explains that the defendant may fear that the principal died or revoked the agency. That follows Sema’s earlier view. According to what I have explained, those concerns alone do not justify refusal. Rather, the defendant claims that he has defenses or counterclaims against the principal and that the attorney is not his proper litigant, as explained above.
For he is no worse than a messenger appointed before witnesses. Mabit discusses a validated debt instrument issued by Shimon to Reuven through Reuven’s attorney. Shimon claimed the document was false because the attorney knew the obligation was originally a deposit, against which deductions could be asserted, but the witnesses mistakenly wrote it as a loan. Reuven said he knew only that his attorney delivered a validated instrument; perhaps the deposit was later converted into a loan. Mabit ruled that the matter should await the attorney, who is believed like a stakeholder. In my view, even if the attorney arrives and says the instrument was improperly written, he is not believed to invalidate it. The witnesses wrote a loan document, and witnesses are presumed not to sign a false instrument. If the attorney merely told them to write that Shimon owed Reuven one hundred without identifying it as a deposit, the omission itself converted the obligation into a loan for documentary purposes. Mabit’s analogy to a creditor who says he does not know whether a validated instrument was paid is also inapposite. There the instrument is designed for eventual payment, and the creditor’s uncertainty cannot extract money against a definite defense. Here two witnesses attest in the instrument to a loan. Their testimony is equivalent to a definite claim. Just as one witness, or certainly two witnesses, who testify that another took property can obligate payment even where the owner himself does not know, so too this validated instrument establishes the debt.
