With witnesses. As stated in siman 121, seif 2, and at the beginning of the present siman. See Responsa Mabit, part II, siman 213, concerning Reuven, who produced a validated promissory note against Shimon. The note had been arranged through an authorized representative. Shimon claimed that it had been created falsely, and the representative knew that the obligation had not been incurred as a loan but arose from a deposit belonging to Reuven, against which there were possible deductions. The witnesses mistakenly understood it to be a debt and wrote it that way. Reuven said that he did not know, because his agent had given him a validated note; it was true that the matter began as a deposit, but afterward it had been converted into a loan as written in the note. The ruling there was that the money should remain deposited until the representative appears, because the representative is believed when Reuven himself says he does not know, just as a third-party custodian is believed. The responsum discusses this at length but does not explain what happens if the representative never appears, for example because he died or went overseas. The Shach writes that even if the representative appears and says that the note was fabricated, he is not believed to say so. There is a presumption that witnesses do not sign a false document, and we cannot assume that they mistakenly wrote 'loan' instead of 'deposit.' If they were simply instructed to write that a particular person owed one hundred without specifying that it was a deposit, that itself is equivalent to converting the deposit into a loan. Shach.
