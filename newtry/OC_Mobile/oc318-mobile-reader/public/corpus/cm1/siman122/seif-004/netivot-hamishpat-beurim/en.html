However, if it is known that he died. See Shach seif katan 6, who objects that no authority says this. In my view, Shach himself writes in seif katan 9 that death does not by itself revoke a power of attorney where the claim was transferred by agav, but express revocation does cancel it even then. Therefore, when seif 3 does not fear that the principal may have died and the heirs may then have revoked the authorization, the reason must be that we do not entertain two successive uncertainties, perhaps he died and perhaps the heirs revoked. But once we know that he died, we do fear that the heirs may have revoked it. Shach's point that the agent already acquired by agav is not decisive, for seif 4 makes clear that if the agent wishes to keep the money for himself he cannot do so.
