And give it to an agent who shall bring it to her. In the Gemara, chapter HaTekabel, two questions are raised. The first is without the words "and he shall bring it to her"; the second includes those words. Rashi explains the second as an if-you-say argument: even if in the first case they have completed their agency because they did everything the principal instructed, perhaps the second case differs because he added "and he shall bring it to her." The Tur rules according to the Geonim's approach to if-you-say formulations. Therefore, in the first case he writes that they certainly were not appointed further agents. It is possible Rambam also agrees, which is why he omitted the first question and records only the case of "and he shall bring it to her," where doubt remains. Lechem Mishneh on Rambam explains the opposite: Rambam does not follow Rashi, and the if-you-say assumption is that without "and he shall bring it" they have not completed their agency and may write even one hundred times. See Beit Yosef, who understands both questions to remain unresolved and does not rule by the if-you-say principle. Bach says there is a scribal error in Beit Yosef and Kesef Mishneh. According to his view, Beit Yosef's earlier phrase "even if he did not say, and he shall bring it" would likewise require emendation. In light of Lechem Mishneh, however, perhaps there is no error at all.
