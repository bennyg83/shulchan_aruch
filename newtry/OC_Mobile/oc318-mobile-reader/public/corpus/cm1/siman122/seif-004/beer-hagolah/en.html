Beit Yosef and Kesef Mishneh write that, in their opinion, this follows from the dispute in Bava Kamma 70a whether the authorization makes him merely an agent or a partner. This shows that the case involves an actual transfer to him, for otherwise why would one think he was made a partner? Moreover, the Gemara says that if the principal transferred to him one third or one quarter, he may litigate concerning the whole; without such a transfer he may not litigate. This proves that an act of acquisition is required. The acquisition is a kinyan sudar, by which the principal transfers through exchange of the cloth the money he has in another's possession. Beit Yosef cites Baal HaTerumot that authorization to sell movable property or land does not require such an acquisition, from section 3.
