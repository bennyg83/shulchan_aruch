Agent. This applies even if the lender told the borrower or bailee to send the property through this person. Sema and Shach.
Die. Although regarding gittin we generally do not fear that a person may die, that concerns ritual law between a person and Heaven. In monetary law, between one person and another, no one can prevent a litigant from protecting himself against possible loss. Some authorities nevertheless hold that even in monetary matters we do not fear death except where the principal is elderly. Sema. See the Shach, Responsa Pleitat Beit Yosef 21, and Responsa Divrei Rivot 221.
Known. This implies that the same rule applies even where the principal transferred the property to the agent by acquisition together with land, and Darkei Moshe cites a Responsum Rosh accordingly. The Sema therefore explains the Tur's statement that if the principal transferred the money together with land the agent already acquired it and the principal's heirs do not acquire it upon his death. The meaning is only that the bailee need not fear giving the money to the agent lest the principal die and the heirs acquire it, causing the agency to lapse and making the bailee liable if the money is later lost. For that purpose the heirs do not acquire it. But if the bailee actually knows that the depositor died, he should not deliver the money to the agent. Although the depositor transferred the money together with land, his intent was only to appoint the agent for himself, not for his children. The Shach rejects this. In his view no authority makes such a distinction, and there is no reason to distinguish between knowledge and lack of knowledge according to the Mechaber. The Rama too appears to hold that if the principal transferred the property together with land, and the bailee knew of the death yet delivered it to the agent, he is exempt from responsibility. The Rama says only, "nevertheless, they should not give it to the agent," not that if they do so they are liable. It is practical advice not to deliver it, because ordinarily the property now belongs to the heirs. Maharshal similarly writes that even if the authorization did not automatically lapse upon death, the defendant may say, "I will not give it to you, because the heirs may revoke your authority." Darkei Moshe can also be read this way. See the Shach.
