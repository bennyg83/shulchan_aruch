There is concern that the principal may have died before the messenger acted. At first this is difficult because we ordinarily preserve a person in the presumption of life, even if he was ill or elderly. Maharshal explains that the specific principal in the source was very old, but that alone does not fully answer the ordinary presumption. The better understanding is that the concern here is tied to agency and property rights after death, where acting on behalf of a possibly deceased principal can create direct prejudice to heirs or other parties. In such settings the Sages sometimes required stronger assurance than the ordinary presumption of life. The exact scope depends on the particular transaction and on whether third party rights would be changed if the principal had died.
