According to the view that there is no retroactive clarification, one cannot say retroactively that the husband had instructed the writing of the particular get that the rabbi now rules valid. Darkei Moshe writes in Seder Gittin, siman 9, that after the rabbi has examined the get the scribe must be careful that if it contains some defect, such as a letter needing to be extended or letters that became blurred and whose trace is no longer recognizable, he must not repair it without the rabbi's permission, for perhaps the rabbi considers the get valid and the scribe has already completed his agency. In some get forms, the husband initially tells the scribe to write a get that will be valid in the eyes of the rabbi and of anyone to whom it is shown. End of his words.
