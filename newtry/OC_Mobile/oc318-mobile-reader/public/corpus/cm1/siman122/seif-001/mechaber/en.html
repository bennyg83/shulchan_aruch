If the lender died or revoked the agency or power of attorney. It contains 9 se'ifim.
Although a borrower who sends the debt to the lender at the lender's instruction through his agent is exempt if it is lost, as explained in the preceding siman, the borrower may nevertheless refuse to send it through that agent, even if witnesses attest that the lender appointed him to retrieve it, until the agent produces a power of attorney. {Rama: Today, however, the practice is that if the claimant provides a guarantor for the power of attorney, the defendant must litigate with him even if he is not an agent at all, when beit din sees that this benefits the lender, because a benefit may be acquired for a person in his absence.} The reason for requiring a power of attorney is the concern that the lender may die before the borrower gives the money to the agent. If the money is then lost through unavoidable accident on the way, the borrower might be liable to pay the heirs, because he did not give it to the agent until after the lender's death. It is therefore prudent for a borrower or custodian not to give the money to an agent unless the agent brings a power of attorney under which the creditor conveyed by kinyan, together with land, whatever he has in so-and-so's possession. {Rama: If it is known that the lender died, the money must not be given to the agent. See note 450.}
