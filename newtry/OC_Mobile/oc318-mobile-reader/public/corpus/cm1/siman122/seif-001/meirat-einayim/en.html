Even if there are witnesses that he appointed him as an agent to bring it to him. The text could have stated an even greater novelty: even if witnesses heard the lender instruct the borrower to send the money to him through this particular agent, which, as I wrote in the preceding siman, exempts the borrower according to all opinions. The Tur and Mechaber simply adopted the language of the Gemara, stated there according to Rav Chisda, who held that the two formulations have the same effect.
Because one may acquire a benefit for a person in his absence. In the writings of Mahari, he concludes that if beit din sees that the person who seeks to act under authorization is not acting for the benefit of the lender or depositor, his words have no effect. In such matters the judge must decide according to what he sees. This is Rama's intent when he writes, 'if it appears to beit din,' etc.
Lest the lender die. The Tur gives another reason: perhaps the principal will revoke the agency. If the money were then lost from the agent through unavoidable circumstances, the borrower or custodian might remain liable to the lender or depositor. The Mechaber omitted this reason because he follows the Baal HaItur's view, cited by the Tur at the beginning of this siman, that the borrower or custodian is discharged from liability in such a case even without a formal authorization, as the Mechaber rules in seif 2. Rama there cites an opinion that authorization is required, but he does not add this reason because he is speaking according to the Rosh, who holds that the lender cannot revoke a formal authorization. The Mechaber here requires the claim to be transferred through land as part of the authorization, while fear of revocation alone would not require such a transfer. In the Perishah I also explained that although with gittin we generally do not fear death, that concerns prohibitions between a person and God. In monetary matters between one person and another, no one can prevent a person from taking precautions against possible loss. Still, some hold that even in monetary law death is not ordinarily feared except in the case of an elderly person.
However, if he knows that the lender died, he should not give it to the agent. This appears to apply even where the claim was transferred to the agent together with land, because Rama's statement comments directly on the Mechaber. Darkei Moshe likewise cites this in the name of a responsum of the Rosh. We must therefore explain the Tur's citation from the Rosh, that if the money was transferred together with land the agent has already acquired it and even if the principal dies his heirs do not acquire it, as addressing only the custodian's fear that the principal might die and that the heirs would thereby acquire the money, cancel the agency, and create liability if the money were lost en route. With respect to that concern, the heirs do not acquire it. But if the custodian actually knows that the depositor has died, he should not hand it to the agent. Even though the depositor transferred the money to the agent together with land, his intention was only to appoint the agent for himself, not on behalf of his children.
