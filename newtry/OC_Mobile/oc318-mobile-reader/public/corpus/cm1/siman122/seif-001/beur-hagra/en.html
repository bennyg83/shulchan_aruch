Although, etc., if he wishes, etc. As stated in the chapter Merubah: an authorization that does not contain the words "Go, litigate," etc., is ineffective, for the defendant can say to him, etc.
Nevertheless, etc. They asked why such an authorization has no force, since we hold that a person's agent is as himself. They answered that it is because of the debtor's potential loss, as explained above in the Shulchan Aruch, "The reason is because," etc., "therefore," etc. That concern does not apply in this case.
For one may confer a benefit upon a person, etc. This reason applies even though he is not actually his appointed agent. As stated there, we hold that acquisition on another's behalf operates through agency. Just as one may acquire a benefit for a person in his absence, so too one may become his agent in his absence for his benefit. See Beer HaGolah.
And the reason, etc. So writes the Mordechai in the chapter Merubah. He asks, as above, that we hold that a person's agent is as himself. The Rosh writes that we are concerned that the principal may have revoked the agency, unlike an authorized representative, whose authorization cannot be revoked. The Tur gives both reasons. The Shulchan Aruch omits the Rosh's reason because it maintains that even an authorization may be revoked, as explained above in seif 3. Even according to the Beit Yosef's explanation that the Rosh also holds that it may be revoked, but that with a formal authorization the debtor is discharged even if the principal revoked it, as the Rosh writes in the chapter Merubah, unlike an agent appointed before witnesses, nevertheless, according to the ruling in seif 2 following the Baal HaItur that even payment to such an agent discharges the debtor, the Rosh's reason no longer applies.
Unless he comes, etc. This explains what is stated in Bava Kamma 104b regarding the incident of Rabbi Abba, where the representative came with an authorization. This is unlike the Mordechai in the chapter Merubah, who writes that an authorization does not require acquisition together with land. See there. The Gemara required acquisition together with land because coin cannot be acquired by barter, as the Rambam writes in chapter 3 of Hilchot Sheluchin, and as stated in Bava Metzia 46a and Bava Batra 77b. See also siman 203, seif 9, and above, seif 4, "One who authorizes," etc. There acquisition together with land is not mentioned because the case there does not concern coin. See Rambam at the beginning of chapter 3 of Hilchot Sheluchin and there, halachah 7. So too the Ramban writes in Milchamot in the chapter HaGozel there, in the name of a responsum of the Rif, that Rabbi Abba's document was an authorization and contained the words "Go, litigate," etc.; nevertheless, when the principal died the agency terminated. See there.
Nevertheless, etc. Since we hold there, 70a, that he made him merely an agent, when the principal dies the agency terminates. What is stated there, 104b, "Rather, go and acquire them," etc., concerns liability, meaning that the debtor is discharged even if the principal dies. But if it is known that he died, the debtor should not pay the agent. When there is uncertainty, the principal is presumed alive, as stated in chapter 3 of Gittin, 28a. Still, because of the debtor's potential loss, he need not pay the agent so long as the principal did not convey the claim to him by acquisition together with land.
