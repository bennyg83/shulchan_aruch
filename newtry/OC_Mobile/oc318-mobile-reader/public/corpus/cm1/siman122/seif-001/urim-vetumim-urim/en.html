Whom he appointed as an agent. Even if he merely said, 'Send it to me through so and so.' See Sema.
For one may acquire a benefit for a person, etc. But if beit din sees that the authorized representative does not intend the lender's benefit and is acting improperly, the authorization is ineffective. The judge must decide such matters according to what he sees. See Terumat HaDeshen, Sema, and Shach.
Perhaps he will die. Although ordinarily we do not suspect imminent death, that rule applies in the laws of gittin. In monetary law, who can prevent a person from saying, 'I am nevertheless concerned that he may die'? See Sema. This is consistent with the rule that in monetary matters we do not follow a majority.
Before he gives, etc. The Rama writes in seif 2 that the principal can revoke the agency, in which case the borrower would be liable. If so, one might have expected the reason here to be concern that he will revoke the agency. Instead, however, the reason given is that the authorization must specifically be made through acquisition together with land. For concern of revocation, ordinary authorization would suffice. See Sema.
And if an unavoidable accident occurs, etc. The Shach holds that if the borrower has no dispute with the lender, he is obligated to pay the representative. See Tumim, where I resolved the words of the Baal HaTerumot in several sound ways, leaving no basis for the Shach to disagree with the earlier authorities.
He is obligated to pay. The Shach cites several authorities who exempt him. See Tumim, where I wrote that the majority of authorities obligate him.
To pay the heirs. See Tumim. In the case of a deposit, where the messenger did not travel through a dangerous place, there is room to doubt whether the custodian is liable. These are the same principles discussed below at the end of siman 340 in the Rama.
However, if it is known, etc. Although the Rosh writes that where he transferred the claim through acquisition together with land, payment should be made even if the principal died, that means only that there is no need to suspect that he may have died. If it is known that he died, acquisition together with land does not help. See Sema. The Shach disagrees. See Tumim, where I wrote that although the Shach's proof from the responsum of the Rosh is not conclusive, his ruling nevertheless appears reasonable. The practical law requires study.
