Terumat HaDeshen discusses a case where Reuven directed a custodian to pay money to Shimon and accepted a kinyan on the instruction. If the money was still in the custodian's hand, Reuven could retract because coins are not acquired by sudar kinyan. Once Shimon actually received the money, however, Reuven could no longer recover it merely because the original kinyan was ineffective. The principle is that even where the original attempted acquisition could not operate on money, later actual possession taken under the giver's direction can complete the transfer so long as the giver had not revoked the instruction beforehand. The recipient's seizure is effective because it occurs pursuant to the giver's standing authorization. It is not necessary that the giver know the exact moment of seizure. This supports Terumat HaDeshen's broader rule that where an attempted transfer of a debt or future property was ineffective initially, actual collection by the intended recipient can nevertheless become effective if the transferor had not retracted. The debtor is not necessarily discharged merely because the recipient collected under such a defective assignment. Where the formal assignment did not transfer the debt itself, the original creditor may still retain a personal claim against the debtor, even though the recipient keeps what he collected. Thus acquisition by later possession and discharge of the underlying debtor are distinct questions.
