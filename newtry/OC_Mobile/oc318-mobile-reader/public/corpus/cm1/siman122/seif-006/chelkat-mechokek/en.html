All this applies where he said to them, etc. Meaning, the Gemara's unresolved question concerns only a case where he said, 'Give it to so-and-so and he shall take it,' without appointing him as agent for a particular get, but rather instructed them to write a get and give it to the agent. If the get is then found invalid, we say that the husband did not instruct them to give such a get to the agent. But if the husband himself took a particular get from the scribe and told the agent, 'Take this get,' and it was found invalid, the scribe may not write another get and give it to the agent, because they may not appoint an agent without the husband's knowledge. Even if the husband instructed the scribe to write up to one hundred gittin, the agent was appointed by the husband only for the one get actually placed in his hand, unless the husband explicitly also told the agent to carry as many gittin as necessary until one of them is valid in the rabbi's opinion. This is as explained above. Darkei Moshe likewise writes that the law of the agent is the same as that of the scribe and witnesses.
