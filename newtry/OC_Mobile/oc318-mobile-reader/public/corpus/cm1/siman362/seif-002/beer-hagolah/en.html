dalet) Ibid., law 2. The Maggid Mishneh writes that in the Rambam's view a robber acquires neither the stolen object nor its increase before the owner's despair. He wrote these laws on that basis, and the Raavad likewise held this. The Maggid Mishneh adds that there is some support from Rabba's statement in Bava Kamma 66a: 'The Rabbis said that despair acquires, and I do not know whether this is from the Torah,' and he discusses this at length.
heh) It appears to me that the Rambam holds that since we rule like Rabba whenever he disputes Rav Yosef except in the three specified cases, despair alone effects an acquisition. Yet the sugya at the beginning of chapter HaGozel Etzim establishes that acquisition requires an irreversible change, as written above in siman 360, seif 6. The Rambam therefore understands Rabba's statement that despair alone acquires as referring to the increase produced by the stolen item, while the requirement of a complete change concerns acquisition of the stolen object itself.
vav) The Maggid Mishneh writes that when the Gemara there, 95b, asks according to Rabbi Shimon whether the robber is removed by paying money or by returning the actual improvement, it is because according to Rabbi Yehudah it is obvious that his obligation is monetary, due to the enactment for those who repent.
