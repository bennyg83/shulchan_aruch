If he stole a pregnant cow and the owners despaired before it gave birth, or a wool-bearing ewe and they despaired before it was shorn, he pays the value of a cow ready to give birth and a ewe ready to be shorn. If they gave birth or were shorn before despair and before any change, the offspring and wool belong to the owners. Even if pregnancy or wool growth occurred in the robber's possession, without despair or change the property remains in the owners' domain, though the robber is liable for accidents. Rama: These are Rambam's rulings, but others disagree, as explained at the beginning of simanim 354 and 356. Even a reversible change, such as making bricks from earth, does not acquire the principal but does acquire the improvement, and the owner must reimburse it.
