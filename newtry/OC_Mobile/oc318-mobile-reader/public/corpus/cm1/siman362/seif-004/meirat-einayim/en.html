And the enhancement that it gained was sold. Meaning, in this respect a purchaser or heir has greater power than the robber himself. They acquire the enhancement even though the victim did not yet despair. According to Rambam, the robber himself does not acquire that enhancement while it remains in his possession. The reason is that the Sages instituted a special enactment for a purchaser and an heir. Rashi and the Rosh, by contrast, hold that the purchaser and heir acquire the enhancement because the robber himself would also have acquired such enhancement, even if it arose before despair, by virtue of the enactment encouraging robbers to repent. See the Perishah.
The value of the enhancement. I already wrote in the preceding seif that this means even the monetary value of the enhancement.
And what the Mechaber writes, "for he did not despair." This refers to the preceding statement that the stolen object itself is returned. The reason is that the owner did not despair. Had he despaired, the purchaser would retain even the stolen object itself.
Likewise, if the purchaser improved it, etc. The Ir Shushan writes that where the heir or purchaser improved the property, the victim does not then recover the value of the enhancement from the robber, because the enhancement did not arise in the robber's possession. I do not know a sound reason for that distinction. Here we are speaking of enhancement that came on its own, as explained below in seif 9. The victim can say: had you not robbed me, the property would have improved in my own possession as well. Although the Sages enacted that the victim must reimburse the purchaser or heir for the enhancement, Rambam's approach would still seem to require the robber to restore the amount as though the enhancement had arisen in his own possession. One must say that the Ir Shushan holds that only where the stolen property improved while in the robber's possession and he then sold it, so that the robber actually received the enhanced value from the purchaser, does the victim recover that value from him. Where it did not improve until after the sale, the victim cannot recover the enhancement from the robber. If so, the Mechaber's phrase, "likewise, if the purchaser improved it," teaches that although in such a case the victim does not recover the enhancement from the robber, he must nevertheless reimburse the purchaser for the enhancement out of his own pocket. Still, none of this is compelled. The word "likewise" may simply teach that even though the special enactment encouraging repentance is less applicable when the enhancement arose only in the purchaser's possession, the victim must nevertheless reimburse the purchaser for it.
