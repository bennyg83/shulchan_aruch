Sema suggests that when appreciation occurs only in the purchaser's hands, the original owner cannot recover that appreciation from the robber because it did not arise while the property was with him. That is correct. A robber ordinarily pays the value at the time of theft, or the value at the later moment when he personally consumes or disposes of the object. If he sells the object before it appreciates, his own liability is fixed by that disposal. Later appreciation in the purchaser's hands is not treated as though it occurred in the robber's hands merely because the owner could say, "Had you not stolen it, it would have appreciated with me." Otherwise every robber would be liable for all later hypothetical growth even after the property passed beyond his control. Therefore the later purchaser's appreciation does not increase the robber's liability.
