We do not say that he gives the broken pieces, etc. This was already explained at the end of siman 354. The Ran writes in the first chapter of Bava Kamma in the Ramah's name that this applies specifically where the robber has money. If he has no money to pay, then even if he owns land or other movables he need not first pay from those. We appraise the broken pieces and he completes the remainder from other movables. In the Perishah I explained that this resolves the Rambam and Tur's statement that if the owners prefer the broken pieces, we listen to them and the robber must give them. The Maggid Mishneh asks why the robber must hand over the pieces if he already acquired them through change. The answer is that the case concerns a robber who has no cash and comes to pay with movables. The victim can say: Since you are paying me with movables even though my proper right is to money, at least give me my own broken pieces, which I prefer to other movables.
