We do not remove it from him. Beit Yosef says that seizure generally helps in an unresolved monetary question. Yet where the doubt concerns a rabbinic enactment, Maggid Mishneh explains Rambam as leaving the parties under the underlying Torah law rather than allowing later seizure to create rights. The distinction is between prior possession and later seizure. If one party was already in possession before the rabbinic doubt arose, that possession can remain controlling. But a later opportunistic seizure does not improve his position merely because the enactment is uncertain. Accordingly, in an uncertain enactment concerning appreciation, the underlying Torah ownership remains with the original owner. A later seizure by the other party should not prevail merely on account of the unresolved rabbinic rule.
