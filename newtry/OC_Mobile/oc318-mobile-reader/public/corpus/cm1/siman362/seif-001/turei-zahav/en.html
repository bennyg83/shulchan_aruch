The Gemara leaves unresolved whether the market enactment applies because both the initial and final possessors are Jews, or does not apply because a gentile intervened. Therefore, if the original owner seized the property, it is not removed from him. The Sema writes that everyone agrees possession helps in an unresolved Talmudic question. This is very difficult, since at the beginning of siman 388 he rules concerning an unresolved question that possession does not help. I searched for the Rosh's supposed statement and did not find it. The Rosh merely records that the question remained unresolved, which ordinarily means possession is ineffective. Maharshal likewise writes that Rabbeinu Yitzchak and the Rosh hold that if the original owner seized, it is removed from him, and that the Tur wrote otherwise here only in haste. I say, however, that the Tur frequently records the Rambam's position that possession helps in a legal doubt, as at the end of siman 164 and in siman 383. It appears that practical law follows this view, and money should not be removed once the person has acquired possession in reliance on the Rambam.
