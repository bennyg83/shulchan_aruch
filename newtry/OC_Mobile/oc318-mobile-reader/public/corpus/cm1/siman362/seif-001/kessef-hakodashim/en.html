I heard from my father-in-law that although repayment of stolen goods is ordinarily valued like a bulk sale rather than according to the higher price obtainable by selling each item separately, if the robber himself actually sold the items separately at the higher price, then because despair alone did not transfer ownership, at the moment of each sale he was selling property that still belonged to the victim. The proceeds therefore belong to the victim according to the actual retail sales. The poskim similarly rule regarding one who rents out another person's house where land cannot be stolen: the rent belongs to the true owner because the property remains in his possession in law. According to this reasoning, even if the robber privately intended not to receive the money as rent for the owner's house, the tenant's payment may still be subject to Rabbi Nathan's rule, since the tenant owes the robber and the robber in turn owes the owner. Likewise, if the stolen goods were sold in a distant place, despite the general rule that value is assessed by local time and place, the robber may have to pay according to the value at the time and place where he actually removed the goods from the owner's ownership through sale. This requires further study.
