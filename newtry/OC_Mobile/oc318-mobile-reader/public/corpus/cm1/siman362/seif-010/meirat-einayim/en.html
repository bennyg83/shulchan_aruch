One who robbed a barrel of wine, etc. The Tur introduces this with "therefore," deriving the law from the principle above that a robber does not acquire later market appreciation.
For if he had left it, etc. Meaning, since the barrel could have been returned intact, the rise in value occurred in the victim's domain. Although robbers ordinarily pay according to the value at the time of the robbery, the essential act creating the final monetary loss here occurred when the robber drank, sold, or broke the barrel. Until that time, the item remained legally destined to be returned intact and is therefore regarded as standing in the victim's domain. When the robber actively destroyed or disposed of it, he prevented its return and must pay the then-current value. This differs from where it broke on its own, as the Mechaber concludes. This is straightforward.
If it broke on its own or was lost. The phrase "was lost" likewise means through circumstances not caused by him. An ordinary loss is negligence, as explained above in siman 354, seif 3. See there.
