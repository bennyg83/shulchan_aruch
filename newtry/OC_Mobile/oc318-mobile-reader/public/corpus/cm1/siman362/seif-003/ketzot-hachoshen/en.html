Or he gave it as a gift. I was uncertain whether stolen property given after despair to a minor can be acquired by the minor through despair plus change of possession. According to Tosafot, Rosh, and Ramban, the purchaser's acquisition after despair is not because change of possession is itself an acquisition. Rather, once it reaches the purchaser permissibly, the purchaser acquires through despair. If so, the minor lacks Torah acquisition, because there is no transferor presently conveying title. The robber himself never acquired through despair. Rambam, however, holds that even change of possession followed by despair can acquire. That implies change of possession is itself part of the acquisitive mechanism. When the robber gives the object to the minor, the giving and the minor's acquisition can come together, providing the "other person's transferring intent" required for a minor's acquisition. Therefore the result can depend upon the competing theories of despair plus change of possession. Under Tosafot, Rosh, and Ramban, the minor should not acquire by Torah law. Under Rambam, there is stronger basis to say that he does.
