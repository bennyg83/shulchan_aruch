He pays according to the value at the time of the robbery. Meaning, at minimum the victim does not receive the later enhancement but only the value the property had when robbed. The Mechaber then explains that sometimes even the stolen object itself remains with the robber and he pays its value, namely where the animal already gave birth or was shorn. Since he acquired the wool or offspring, he also acquires the animal itself together with them. At other times the victim takes everything, while reimbursing the robber for the enhancement, namely where it has not yet been shorn or given birth. See siman 354, where I gave a more detailed proof and explanation of these distinctions.
If it has not yet given birth, etc. Rambam holds that pregnancy or carrying wool does not constitute a change. Even childbirth itself, in his view, does not create a complete irreversible change but is like a change that can recur, as explained in the following seif.
