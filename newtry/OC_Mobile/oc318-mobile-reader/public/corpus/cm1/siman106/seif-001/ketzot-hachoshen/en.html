That collection in the debtor's absence applies only from the debtor's own property and not from heirs. See the Sema, who cites Rosh that this means specifically property belonging to the debtor himself. If the debtor died and property belonging to the heirs is found in that place, although the heirs are obligated to pay their father's debt because they inherited other property from him, this particular property was never subjected to the debt and cannot be collected in their absence. The wording implies that if the heirs are present, collection from this property is possible. Why? They should be able to say, 'These assets were never subject to your lien, because they did not come from our father.' If the inherited assets themselves still exist here or elsewhere, the creditor should collect those assets on which his lien rested. If they no longer exist because the heirs sold them or otherwise disposed of them, then either one who damages another's lien is exempt, in which case what they did is done and they owe nothing from their personal assets, or one who damages a lien is liable, in which case they have incurred a direct damages debt against themselves. If that is so, collection should be possible even in their absence because it is now their own debt. See Mishneh LaMelech, chapter 13 of Hilchot Malveh.
