Tur there, for this is how Rosh explains Rif's words, 'if he can send to him and inform him.'
There in his name. Rosh writes this in the cited chapter and in Responsa, rule 73, siman 3. The reason is that it benefits the borrower, since strictly speaking the creditor could descend to his property immediately because of the concern of closing the door before lenders. This differs from the creditor's expenses for obtaining a writ of authorization and a final collection order, which are for the creditor's own benefit and must be paid by him unless there was an express stipulation. See what is written in the name of Rabbenu Yerucham, Netiv 3, part 4, in the name of Darkei Moshe. If the defendant becomes exempt after the claimant summoned him to court, the claimant must pay the messenger's fee. Beit Yosef, seif 4, in the name of Responsa Rosh; Sema.
In accordance with Rava in the name of Rav Nachman, even regarding a creditor, so that each person should not take another's money and travel overseas, thereby closing the door before borrowers. Rif rules likewise, since we follow Rav Nachman in monetary law. He brings additional proofs, and Rosh writes that this is the accepted practice.
