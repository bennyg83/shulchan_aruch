Authenticated. See Responsa Raanach 15 regarding why the ruling specifies an authenticated document. See also Responsa Rashach II, 180, part 3, siman 49, and Maharashdam 43. Shach.
And they notify. Because the debtor may possess a receipt showing that he already paid the debt. Sema.
The agency. One who sends a summons requiring another person to appear with him before beit din is not entitled to require the debtor to pay the messenger's fee. So rule Maharshal and the Bach. See siman 14, seif 5. Shach.
That he swear. This applies even if the debtor had exempted him from taking an oath. See siman 71, seif 20. Shach.
From movable property. We do not fear that the creditor may hide the property, leaving the debtor unable to recover from him if the debtor later arrives with a receipt. In siman 98 the Tur and Shulchan Aruch do entertain such a concern because the debtor had already claimed, "Give me time and I will bring evidence invalidating the document." We do not delay collection beyond the travel time of thirty days merely because of an unsupported possibility. So writes the Rashba. Sema.
From his heirs. The Rivash writes that this applies specifically to the debtor's own assets. If the debtor died and the creditor finds property belonging to the heirs in the debtor's locality, then even though the heirs may have to pay their father's debt because they inherited other assets from him, the creditor's lien does not attach to these particular assets belonging independently to the orphans, and he does not collect them in their absence. The Rama must be understood accordingly. Property actually inherited from the father, however, which was subject to the creditor's lien, may be collected even in the heirs' absence. Sema.
