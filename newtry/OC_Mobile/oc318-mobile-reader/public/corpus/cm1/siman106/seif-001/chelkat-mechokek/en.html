One who writes all his property. The Tur says, "one who distributes his property," implying that actual writing is not essential. If he is a dying person, oral instruction suffices. If he is healthy, mere writing does not suffice, and the case must involve a valid transfer. This is also the plain Gemara. Once he made his wife a partner among the sons, her status parallels theirs. Wherever the sons acquire by speech, such as a deathbed gift, she too becomes a partner by speech. Wherever they require a formal acquisition, she requires the same. Although the mishnah says "writes," that word is not literal. As at the beginning of chapter HaKotev, "writes" can mean "says." The Gemara explicitly discusses one who said, "a third to my daughter," etc., showing that mere speech can make her a partner without writing. Bach says specifically writing is required, but his words are not clear.
