To authenticate. If the note has not been authenticated, collection is not made in the debtor's absence. It follows that collection is likewise not made from purchasers or orphans, because we plead for them that the note may be forged. This appears to be the view of the Maggid Mishneh, and Tosafot, the Rashba, the Ran, Rabbeinu Yerucham, and the Nimukei Yosef agree, unlike Rabbeinu Yitzchak. The Shach therefore rules accordingly. He further cites a responsum of the Mabit concerning two creditors holding notes against Reuven, where the earlier note was not authenticated but the borrower admitted having written it. The Mabit held that the later creditor, whose note is authenticated, cannot claim forgery, trust, or collusion, because the borrower's admission is equivalent to authentication. The Shach rejects this and rules that collusion remains a concern, supporting this from the Gemara and the poskim.
