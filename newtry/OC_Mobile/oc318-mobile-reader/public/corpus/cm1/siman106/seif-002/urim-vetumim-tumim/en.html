The Beit Yosef cites the Rivash that collection in the debtor's absence applies specifically where the debt was created in this country and the debtor afterward traveled far away. If, however, the creditor and debtor were together in another country, the loan was made there, and the creditor later returned here and seeks to collect from property belonging to the debtor while the debtor remains in the original place, we do not collect in his absence. The Beit Yosef writes that the unqualified language of the authorities does not appear to support this distinction. I do not understand his objection, because the Ritva in Ketubot on this very passage writes the distinction explicitly. The Rivash therefore certainly has authority on whom to rely. The rationale is also compelling. The entire enactment permitting collection in a debtor's absence was made so that a person should not take another's money and then flee to a distant country. That concern is absent here. The loan was made in the country where the debtor remains, and the debtor has not gone farther away than he was when the creditor chose to lend to him. Moreover, many early authorities and Geonim disagree with the Rif altogether and hold that one never collects in a debtor's absence. We should therefore certainly not extend the Rif's exceptional enactment to a case where its underlying reason is absent. The Rivash's ruling is consequently clear, and this is also the view of the Mabit and the Rashach.
