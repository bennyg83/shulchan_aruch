a) The language of the Rambam, chapter 2 of Hilkhot Nizkei Mamon, law 19.
bet) Straightforward from Bava Kamma 24b. Rashi explains that it is indirect causation in damages. The Beit Yosef writes that according to the Rambam he nevertheless remains liable under the laws of Heaven, as explained above in simanim 378 and 386.
gimel) In my humble opinion the Rambam learned this from Rava's statement there introduced by 'if you say,' regarding one who incites another's dog against a person. This implies that the question whether the dog's owner is liable was resolved, following the Rambam's and the Geonim's general practice of ruling according to an 'if you say' formulation.
dalet) The Maggid Mishneh writes that even if the dog was forewarned, since it did not act on its own but only because it was incited, it is like an arena ox, concerning which the verse teaches 'if it gores,' but not if others cause it to gore; such an ox is exempt from death. Here too its status is that of a tam.
heh) Ibid., in Rava's statement. The meaning is that the dog bit the person who incited it.
