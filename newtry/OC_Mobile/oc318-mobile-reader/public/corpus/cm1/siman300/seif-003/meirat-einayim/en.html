Likewise, if they claimed from him, etc., he pays both. Meaning after both swear. One may ask why this differs from one who bought from one of five people and does not know from whom. There, if all five claim payment, he leaves the money with beit din and withdraws until they identify the seller, as stated in siman 222. Nimukei Yosef answers that an ordinary purchase is normally paid immediately, so there is no negligence in failing to record the seller's identity. A deposit remains for later return. The custodian should have written the depositor's name, and failure to do so is negligence.
He is exempt even from satisfying Heaven. Only when two people actually deposited with him, one one hundred and the other two hundred, was it strongly incumbent upon him to be precise and record the owner of the larger amount. He knew both would later return and claim their deposits. Here only one person deposited. The custodian can say, "I assumed the person who deposited with me would be the one to return. I had no reason to anticipate a stranger who never deposited anything with me would appear and claim." For this reason, in a case where he says, "The father of one of you deposited with me," his failure to identify which father can still amount to negligence, creating an obligation to satisfy Heaven.
