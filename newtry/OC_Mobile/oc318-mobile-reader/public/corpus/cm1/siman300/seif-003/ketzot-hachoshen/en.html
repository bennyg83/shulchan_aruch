If they themselves do not demand it from him. Rosh explains that the Mishnah says, "The father of one of you deposited a maneh with me," rather than, "One of you deposited with me," because where the depositors themselves made the deposit they should have been careful to remember whose money it was. If they now do not demand it, the custodian is not obligated even to satisfy Heaven. Tosafot appears to understand differently. They say that Rabbi Tarfon's version using "your father" teaches a novelty even where both sides are uncertain. According to Rabbi Akiva, if the depositors themselves had deposited, they would ordinarily demand it and the case would become one of definite claim against uncertain custodian. Their wording suggests that where both sides are uncertain but the claimants do make a demand, there can still be an obligation to satisfy Heaven. See siman 365.
