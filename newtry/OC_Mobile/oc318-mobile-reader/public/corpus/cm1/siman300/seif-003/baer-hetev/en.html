To both. Meaning, after both swear. One may ask how this differs from siman 222, seif 2, where the Tur and Mechaber rule that if one purchased from one of five people and does not know from whom, and all five come and demand payment, he deposits the purchase money with beit din and withdraws until they identify the proper person. The answer is that an ordinary purchase is expected to be paid immediately, so the buyer was not required to be precise. A deposit, however, is for a later time, and failure to write the depositor's name on the purse is deemed negligence. So writes the Nimukei Yosef. Sema.
To satisfy. The reason is that only where two people deposited, one hundred and two hundred, was there a strong duty upon the custodian to be precise and write the owner's name on the two-hundred deposit, since he knew both had deposited with him and would return to claim their deposits, and he might confuse one with the other. Here, however, only one person deposited with him, and he may explain: I assumed that the person who deposited with me would return, not another person from the marketplace who never deposited anything. For the same reason, where he says, "the father of one of you deposited," the failure to remember is negligence and he is liable to satisfy Heaven. See the end of siman 76. Ibid.
