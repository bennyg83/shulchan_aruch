He shall swear, etc. Like the shopkeeper and workers, and two lenders, in chapter HaNishba'in, even though each one alone would be obligated by Torah law to swear, as stated in siman 91.
For he should have, etc. Rif and Rambam.
And if they themselves, etc. So write Tosafos and the Rosh. Since the Gemara says there only, "there, where they demand, etc.," this shows that it did not retract the first answer. Furthermore, there 103b it says, "and did Rabbi, etc., say with two, etc.?" The Rosh writes that this proves that with one bundle, even to fulfill one's obligation toward Heaven, payment is unnecessary, whereas with two bundles he is liable even in human law if they demand it. The latter clause is therefore established as one bundle; with two bundles he would be liable. This is the meaning of the ruling above, "he shall swear, and if they do not demand, etc.," as stated here, "where he comes, etc., specifically, etc." The same applies to the latter clause in such a case with two bundles.
Therefore, if, etc. The Gemara's reading in the latter clause is that they deposited with him in one bundle, so he was not required to distinguish between them, for he can say to them, "you yourselves, etc."
It remains with him. Rambam. See Rashi there 103b, s.v. "if he took, etc." The words of Maggid Mishneh and Shulchan Aruch in siman 222:2 require study, for they write that it is deposited with beit din.
Others say, etc. Tosafos 103a, s.v. "aval, etc.," likewise write so. See there.
Even where he comes, etc. Maggid Mishneh, according to Tosafos there, s.v. "hatam, etc."
Others say, etc. The Rosh holds that the Mishnah concerns human law, while only the first clause concerns liability toward Heaven. In the latter clause he is likewise liable toward Heaven because his claim is uncertain, as in the case where one says, "you have a maneh of mine," and the other says, "I do not know," in Bava Kamma 118a.
