Who deposited. See above, siman 76; siman 222, seif 2; and Even HaEzer, siman 49, seif 1.
Shall swear. The Rambam's language is, "as in every case of one who swears and collects." This implies that he takes an oath while holding a sacred object, as the Sema writes in siman 365. Shach.
His. It does not suffice for the owner of the two hundred alone to mark his purse, because negligence still remains: perhaps a third person will come and claim the purse containing one hundred. This is like the case where one person deposited one hundred and two people later each claim the hundred, in which case the custodian must give each one hundred for this reason, as stated in seif 3. Sema.
Exempt. He can say, "I carefully identified which one owned the two hundred, but I forgot," and the proof is that they too forgot, since they did not demand it from me. So writes the Sema. The Shach writes: rather, he gives one hundred to this one and one hundred to that one, and the third hundred remains deposited until Eliyahu comes, since one claimant demanded part of it. If neither claimant demanded it at all, the remaining hundred is divided, and we do not say it must remain deposited, because where they do not demand it there is no certain liar, as below, siman 365, seif 2.
In one bundle. This is the Rif and Rambam's reading. According to them, whenever they did not deposit in one bundle, even if they deposited in one another's presence, it is as though each deposited outside the other's presence and the custodian should have been precise. Rashi, Tosafot, Rosh, and Tur, however, read: if they deposited in one another's presence. This is the Sema's language, and so too in the Beit Yosef and Nimukei Yosef, chapter HaMafkid. Rabbeinu Yerucham likewise writes that the Rif and Rashi disagree. Hagahot Maimoniyot, chapter 5 of Hilkhot She'elah, likewise states that Rashi and the Rambam disagree. This is unlike what the Sema wrote above in siman 76, seif katan 1, regarding the Mechaber's view. Shach.
And demanded. Even if they demanded it, and certainly if they did not, where the deposits were in one bundle he gives one hundred to each and the third hundred is divided. Ibid.
Remains deposited. We do not say it is divided, because where it is demanded there is certainly a liar. Ibid.
Beit din. Tosafot, chapter HaGozel, Bava Kamma 103a, likewise writes this. See what I wrote in siman 222, seif 2, that many leading authorities hold the money remains with the custodian. The Mordechai there writes that Or Zarua cited Maimoniyot as saying that the Mishnah's phrase, "it shall remain until Eliyahu comes," means not in his hand but in the hand of beit din, for otherwise a person could always scheme regarding a deposit and say, "I do not know." This is surprising, for Maimoniyot does not say so. As to the concern that a person might scheme, one can answer that the depositors caused their own loss by depositing with him in one bundle at the outset. Indeed, they themselves are acting deceptively, since each claims two hundred. Since one of them is certainly lying, we are no longer concerned about deception by the custodian. Nevertheless, he must take a shevuat heset that he does not know, because each definitely claims two hundred from him. This is not a partial admission that would make him one who must swear but cannot do so, for the reason stated by the authorities cited by the Sema at the beginning of this siman. Even if he wishes to place the third hundred with beit din, he must swear that he does not know. Ibid.
Forever. Meaning, until Eliyahu comes, or until they agree to divide the third hundred, as stated at the end of this siman regarding animals. Sema.
Exempt. So writes the Maggid Mishneh, and so too the Nimukei Yosef, Semag, and Tosafot. I wrote above in siman 222, seif 2, that the Rambam and Rashba also hold so. The Mechaber's words there require study. Shach.
And some say. So writes the Tur and Rosh. The view of Tosafot, Semag, and most authorities is not so. Ibid.
Heaven. But if they did not demand it from him, he is exempt even from the obligation to satisfy Heaven. Ibid.
