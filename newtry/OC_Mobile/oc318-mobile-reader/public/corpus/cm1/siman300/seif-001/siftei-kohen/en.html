[For years, they have been appointed. “And I shall come to thee.”
Each one shall swear, etc. This is the Rambam’s language, following the law of one who swears and collects. It implies that a sacred-object oath is required, as stated below in the Sema, siman 365.
And if they do not claim from him. So write the Maggid Mishneh, Tur, Rosh, and Nimukei Yosef. See Sema, seif katan 1; his explanation accords with the Maggid Mishneh, Nimukei Yosef, and the other authorities.
"And thou shalt give unto each one."
But under human law he is exempt, etc. He gives one hundred to each claimant, while the third hundred remains deposited until Eliyahu comes, since one claimant demands only part of it. If neither claimant made a definite demand, the applicable distinctions follow the rule stated here.
In one bundle. This is the reading of the Rif and Rambam. According to them, whenever the deposits were not placed in one bundle, even if each deposited in the other’s presence, it is considered as though each deposited separately, and the custodian should have recorded which sum belonged to whom.
And they came and demanded, etc. This applies even when they demanded payment, and certainly when they did not. If the deposits were in one bundle, he gives one hundred to each and they divide the third hundred.
[“Dia is called ‘.’ And he did not say that he was not afraid of Hashem’s mercy and mercy
In the custody of the beit din. Tosafot in Bava Kamma 103a likewise state this.
The beit din, etc. I wrote above in seif 2 that many great authorities hold that the money should remain with the custodian. The Mordechai cites Or Zarua in the name of Maimonides that when two deposited in one bundle, the remainder remains until Eliyahu comes. The custodian must swear a hesset oath that he does not know which claimant owns it. We are not concerned that he will deliberately forget, because the depositors caused their own loss by placing the sums together. Indeed, each claimant may be attempting deception by demanding two hundred. Since one claimant is necessarily false, we do not alter the law because of a possible stratagem by the custodian. Although each claimant makes a definite demand of two hundred, this is not an ordinary partial admission creating the rule that one unable to swear must pay, for the reasons cited by the authorities and Sema. Even if he wishes to place the third hundred with the beit din, he must swear that he does not know.
And even one who seeks to satisfy the demands of Heaven, etc. So write the Maggid Mishneh, Nimukei Yosef, Semag, and Tosafot. I wrote above in siman 252, seif 2, that the Rambam and Rashba also hold this way; the Mechaber’s words there require study.
