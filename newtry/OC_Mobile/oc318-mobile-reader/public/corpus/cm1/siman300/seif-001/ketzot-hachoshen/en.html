Even in order to satisfy his heavenly obligation, he is exempt. The Shulchan Aruch rules differently in an uncertain purchase from five sellers, where the buyer may have to pay all claimants in order to satisfy Heaven. The distinction is that an intact deposit remains legally in the domain of its owner wherever it is. The custodian has no independent Torah duty of "return" comparable to a robber, and a loan was given to be spent and therefore creates a debt that must be repaid to its owner. So long as the deposited object itself remains intact and the custodian was not negligent, the owner must come and take his own property. The custodian can say, "What is yours is before you." If two owners deposited together and the custodian cannot distinguish their objects without fault, he does not have to pay both even to satisfy Heaven. A buyer or debtor is different. He owes a monetary debt and must restore that debt to its true owner. Where he caused or accepted the uncertainty and cannot identify the creditor, satisfying Heaven can require payment to each claimant. In an intact deposit there is no comparable debt of repayment. This is why even when the claimants demand payment, the custodian can remain exempt in the bundled deposit case.
