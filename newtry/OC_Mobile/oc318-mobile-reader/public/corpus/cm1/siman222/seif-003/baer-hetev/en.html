Transgression. Meaning that he does not complete his repentance until he returns the stolen property into the hand of the victim, and merely depositing it with beit din is insufficient. Since he must give it directly to the victim, which one can we compel him to pay rather than the other, perhaps the other is the true victim? So writes Sema. This wording is not precise, because the same law applies even if he persists in his wrongdoing and does not wish to repent, if beit din learns that he swore falsely, for example through his admission before others or the like. In such a case we penalize him and obligate him to pay each claimant, as is clear from the Gemara and the authorities. See Even HaEzer 40:1. When Rambam and the Mechaber write at the beginning, 'and he repented,' they use the ordinary circumstance, because otherwise it is uncommon that his false oath would become established. This is why they do not repeat that phrase at the end. Shach. Taz writes similarly. See there.
