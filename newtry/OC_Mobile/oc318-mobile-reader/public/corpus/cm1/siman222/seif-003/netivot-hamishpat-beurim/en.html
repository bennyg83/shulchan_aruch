He is not an interested witness. See Sema, seif katan 2, concerning a case where both parties hold the purchased item. Ketzot HaChoshen writes that where each says "all of it is mine," and the seller is a single witness, the defendant swears only concerning his half. A single witness concerning a point on which the defendant does not swear is treated like two witnesses, so it is as though two witnesses testify concerning half the item. The other party would therefore receive only one quarter. According to this reasoning, once he is awarded only a quarter, he would then swear only that the quarter is his, leaving the seller as though two witnesses testified concerning the remaining three quarters. He should then receive only one eighth, and this regress would continue until he receives nothing. The premise is incorrect for two reasons. First, once the party swears to contradict the witness regarding part of the witness's testimony, and that testimony is nullified in part, testimony that is invalidated in part is invalidated entirely. The seller is no longer treated as a witness against him at all. Second, the reason two competing possessors are treated as equally in possession is that, from the perspective of beit din, the uncertainty is equally balanced between them. In the case of one saying "all of it is mine" and the other saying "half is mine," the uncertainty is not equal as to the whole item, but only as to one half. When two actual witnesses establish one part, the adverse claimant has no viable claim to that part at all, because even without the other's possession the witnesses would award it. His possession therefore operates only upon the remaining half. A single witness is different. The other party does not receive the portion supported by that witness merely because of the testimony, for absent possession the opposing claimant could still take upon oath. The reason he retains that portion and prevents his opponent from taking it by oath is precisely his own status as possessor. Thus his possession must operate also on the half about which the single witness testified. Once his possession is required on that half, the opposing party must likewise be considered in possession of the other half. There is no basis to treat one possessor as stronger than the other.
