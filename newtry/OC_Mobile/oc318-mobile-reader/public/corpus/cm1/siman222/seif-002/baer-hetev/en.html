He leaves it. Bach asks why he should not be required by law to pay each claimant, since he was negligent in failing to be precise, as we say regarding a deposit. Bach answers that even with a deposit he is liable by law only where the deposits were made in two separate bundles, meaning that each deposited without the other's presence, as stated in siman 300. Here, however, the expression 'he purchased from one of five' implies that all were present together when he purchased from one of them, making it like a single bundle, where he may say to them, 'You yourselves were present.' This answer does not appear correct to me, for the question would still remain. In truth, however, there is no question at all. Bach overlooked the words of Nimukei Yosef in HaMafkid, who writes explicitly that only with a deposit is one liable because he should have been precise, since an item is deposited with him for a long period. In a purchase, where the seller expects payment immediately, the purchaser had no reason to be especially precise, for he assumed that the seller would immediately come for his money. Maadanei Melech and Sema below, siman 300, write similarly. Accordingly, even if he purchased from five people who were not together, the law is the same. Shach.
With beit din. The Tur simply writes that the money should remain set aside. Rambam likewise writes this in Hilchot Mechirah, chapter 20. Maggid Mishneh explains in the name of earlier authorities that he deposits it with beit din, and that this is Rambam's intent. The Mechaber followed this explanation. This is surprising, however, because in Hilchot She'eilah, chapter 5, Rambam explicitly writes that it remains with him, and the Mechaber likewise writes at the beginning of siman 300. It therefore appears that the words should be understood literally, that the money remains in his possession. Rashi explains this way in HaMafkid, and so does Baal HaMaor. Ramban in Milchamot also agrees. Nevertheless, it appears that the purchaser must swear a shevuat heset that he does not know which claimant is entitled. See what I write below in siman 300. Shach.
Each one. This applies specifically when each one claims from him. But if they are not claiming from him and he himself says, 'I purchased from the father of one of you and I do not know from which one,' he need not, even to satisfy the requirements of Heaven, give payment to each. See above siman 75 and below siman 365. Sema. Shach writes that the Mechaber is difficult: here he cites Rambam without qualification, whereas at the end of siman 300:1 he rules without qualification that one is exempt even from the obligation to satisfy the requirements of Heaven. That follows Maggid Mishneh there. It appears clear that Maggid Mishneh is consistent with his own view, namely that wherever there was no negligence, such as deposits made in one bundle or a purchase where negligence is not applicable, one is exempt even according to the requirements of Heaven. Perhaps the Mechaber distinguishes between a purchase and a deposit, but such a distinction is strained, since in both cases there was no negligence, as explained above. Nimukei Yosef in HaMafkid explicitly derives from this purchase case that one who received deposits in a single bundle is exempt even according to the requirements of Heaven, even when each claimant asserts certainty, because he was not negligent. The Mechaber's words therefore require study. Shach.
