And if he is pious, he gives the money to each one. Rambam appears to hold that even to fulfill one's duty toward Heaven this is only a measure of piety. An ordinary person has no obligation even toward Heaven. Ordinarily, whenever the Gemara says that one is liable toward Heaven, that applies to every Jew, resident and convert alike. Here, however, the requirement is presented specifically as pious conduct. This follows from the episode in Yevamot in which Rabbi Akiva told a certain pious man that he would not be free of responsibility until he returned the money to each and every claimant. The language implies a demand specific to the pious man. This also explains Rambam's ruling in seif 1 that in the case of a purchase one is exempt even toward Heaven because he had no reason to investigate carefully. The duty described here is not an ordinary heavenly liability but only exceptional piety. The later authorities struggled with Rambam's ruling in siman 300; see Shach there. According to this distinction, there is no difficulty.
