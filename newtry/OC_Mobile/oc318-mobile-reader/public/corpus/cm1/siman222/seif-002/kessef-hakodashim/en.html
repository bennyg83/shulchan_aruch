Seif 4. You sold it to me. If one party claims that the money was paid as the purchase price of a house, while the owner claims that the money was a loan, and the person who paid or his tenant occupied the house throughout the year, and neither party claims that they used a heter iska or other lawful loan arrangement, there is reason to conclude that the transaction was a sale. We should not invent a third factual possibility that neither side alleges. Since a person does not ordinarily render himself wicked, the absence of a permitted loan structure supports the sale theory. Where the negotiations were for a sale, the purchaser also acquired through chazakah, because during a year of residence it is virtually impossible that he did not perform an act such as locking. Since this occurred in the seller's presence, no separate instruction of 'perform chazakah and acquire' was necessary. An agent can perform chazakah as well. Delivery of a key is not indispensable; even what is commonly called a latch or locking device can function comparably because it prevents entry. The fact that some devices can be opened from outside through special means does not negate their function, just as ordinary locks can sometimes be opened with tools. A valid mortgage arrangement ordinarily requires a written instrument, so where no such instrument exists the circumstances again incline toward a sale. This still requires study.
