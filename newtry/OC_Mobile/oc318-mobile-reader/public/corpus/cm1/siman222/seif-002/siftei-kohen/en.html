"And every one of them is succed to him, and he has not only been cursed, nor is he inscribed to him," he said
He deposits the purchase money between them and withdraws, etc. The Bach asks why the purchaser should not be required by law to pay each claimant, since he was negligent and should have taken care to remember, just as is stated regarding a deposit. He answers that even regarding a deposit one is liable by law only where the deposits were made in two separate bundles, such as where each deposited without the other's presence, as explained below in siman 300. Here, however, the formulation, 'He purchased from one of five people,' implies that all five were together when he bought from one of them. It is therefore comparable to their depositing in one bundle, where he may say, 'You yourselves were not careful concerning one another.' In my opinion this is incorrect. It remains difficult according to the Rif, Rambam, Mechaber, and those who follow them, who rule below in siman 300 that only an actual single bundle exempts him, whereas separate deposits made in one another's presence still impose liability. Here too it is comparable to deposits made in one another's presence. Moreover, only regarding two deposits, one of one hundred and one of two hundred, is it reasonable to say that the depositors were not careful concerning one another, since the two deposits could be confused. Here only one of them sold the item, so there was no reason to suspect that another would falsely claim to have made a sale that never occurred. In truth, there was never any difficulty. The Bach overlooked the explicit words of Nimukei Yosef in the chapter HaMafkid: only with a deposit is the custodian liable because he should have been exact, since money is deposited with him for a long period. In a sale, the purchase money is held only temporarily, so he need not have taken special care, for he reasonably expected the seller to return immediately for the money. The Maadaney Yom Tov and the Sema below, siman 300, seif katan 11, write likewise. Accordingly, the same law applies even if he purchased from five people who were not together. Examine carefully.
They remain deposited with beit din, etc. The Tur writes without qualification that the money remains deposited. Concerning the Rambam's language in chapter 20 of Hilchot Mechirah, the Maggid Mishneh writes that the authorities explain that the money is deposited with beit din, and that this is the meaning of the Rambam's words, 'the money shall remain deposited.' The Mechaber follows him. This is surprising, because in chapter 5 of Hilchot She'eilah the Rambam explicitly writes that the money remains with the holder, and the Mechaber likewise rules below at the beginning of siman 300. It therefore appears that the words should be understood literally: the money remains in the purchaser's possession. Rashi explains similarly in the chapter HaMafkid, Bava Metzia 37b: he does not remove it from his possession; rather, it remains with him and he withdraws from the litigation, while stolen property remains in his possession until Eliyahu comes. Baal HaMaor likewise writes there that only one who seeks to fulfill his duty before Heaven must pay everyone. If they bring legal claims against him, he does not pay each one. He merely leaves the stolen property among them in an effort to clarify the matter, and if he cannot clarify it, he leaves it in his own possession until Eliyahu comes and removes nothing from his possession. The Ramban in Milchamot Hashem agrees with him on this point. It is obvious that according to Rabbi Akiva the law of a sale is no more stringent than the law of stolen property according to Rabbi Tarfon. This also appears from the Gemara in HaMafkid. Nevertheless, the purchaser must apparently take a hesset oath that he does not know. See what I write below in siman 300.
He gives the purchase money to each one, etc. The Mechaber's ruling is difficult. Here he cites the Rambam without qualification, but below, at the end of siman 300, seif 1, he rules without qualification that one is exempt even from the obligation to satisfy Heaven. This follows the Maggid Mishneh there. It appears clear to me that the Maggid Mishneh follows his own position: wherever there was no negligence, such as deposits placed in one bundle or a sale where negligence is inapplicable, the holder is exempt even from the duty to satisfy Heaven. Therefore the Maggid Mishneh writes here, in chapter 20 of Hilchot Mechirah, concerning these words of the Rambam, that the Ramban and Rashba disagree and maintain that in a sale, even to satisfy Heaven, he leaves the money among them and withdraws. His point is that in a sale the purchaser committed no prohibition and was not negligent, so he is exempt even before Heaven, even if each claimant asserts his claim with certainty. His statement that they wrote similarly in the chapter HaMafkid means that there they compared the law of a sale to the law of deposits placed in one bundle. It is possible that the Mechaber distinguishes between a sale and a deposit, but such a distinction is strained, since neither involves negligence, as explained above. Nimukei Yosef in HaMafkid explicitly derives from the law of a sale that a custodian who received deposits in one bundle is exempt even before Heaven, even if each claimant asserts a certain claim. This indicates that in a sale too he is exempt even before Heaven, despite certain claims, because he was not negligent. The same applies to deposits in one bundle, where he was not negligent. See there. The Mechaber's words therefore require study.
