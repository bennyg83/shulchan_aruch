He is like one witness. Shach, seif katan 2, discusses a case where the seller says he drew the object and deposited it with another. The point matters only where the value also differs, such as a bargain sale. Otherwise, since the object is already under another person’s control and cannot be returned, the seller owes only its monetary value, no worse than a robber, and if he returns that value there is no separate oath. Ketzot HaChoshen says similarly. Ketzot’s further suggestion that, because the seller says he previously gave the object to another, he was originally believed concerning what he did, is very difficult. The special credibility of a third-party holder applies where the parties entrusted the matter to him and accepted his determination. Here the defendant says only that he left the object as a deposit. If the depositary says, “You did not deposit it with me; your fellow deposited it,” the first person certainly must swear. Even a genuine third-party holder who faces a grievance concerning return of the entrusted object may have to swear and is treated as a witness who requires an oath, as Shach writes in siman 56.
