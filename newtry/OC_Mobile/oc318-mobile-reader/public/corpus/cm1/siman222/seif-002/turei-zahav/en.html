(Seif 3.) He is obligated to pay. The Sema in seif katan 7 writes that he has not discharged his duty of repentance, implying that beit din does not compel him. One who studies Yevamot 118 will see that according to Rabbi Akiva this is a fine imposed by the Sages. The same appears in the chapter HaMafkid, page 36, where Rabbi Akiva's ruling does not concern one who merely wishes to satisfy his obligation before Heaven. Beit din therefore certainly compels him to pay. The statement at the outset that he wishes to repent teaches a novelty: although the obligation arises through his own admission, and one might have thought that the Sages did not impose their fine in such a case, we are taught that they did. This appears correct in my humble opinion.
