Claims it from him. All the more so where they do not make a claim. The text speaks of their making claims because of the concluding clause, where if they do not claim he is exempt even from the duty to satisfy Heaven. “They claim” means specifically that each presents a definite claim, even if he did not initially demand payment; a mere possibility is considered as though they are not claiming (Shach).
He leaves the purchase money. This applies even if he purchased from five people who were not together. It is unlike a deposit in siman 300, because a deposit, which is held for an extended period, is something about which he should have been precise, whereas a purchase is momentary and he need not have been precise, since he assumed that the seller would immediately return for the money. According to this, a purchase on credit is comparable to a deposit.
The purchase money between them. This means with beit din. The Shach writes that it should remain in his own possession rather than with beit din, and the buyer must swear that he does not know.
And if he is especially pious. This is difficult, for the Mechaber himself ruled in siman 300 that he is exempt even from the duty to satisfy Heaven. This requires study (Shach).
