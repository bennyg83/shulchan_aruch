If one claims, "You sold this to me," and the other says, "I did not sell it," or, "I sold it but you did not pay"; or the purchaser claims that he paid but has not yet performed meshichah, or that he performed meshichah without seeing this particular object, while the seller says he disclosed it; or one party claims there was a condition and the other denies any condition, then in all such claims the burden of proof rests upon the party seeking to extract property. In the absence of proof, the party denying the claim and presently holding the property takes a heset oath. If he admits part of the claim, or one witness supports the claim against him, he takes a Torah oath like any other litigant. Rama: Some write that if a person says, "I sold a field but do not know to whom," and another says, "I am the purchaser," that person is believed.
