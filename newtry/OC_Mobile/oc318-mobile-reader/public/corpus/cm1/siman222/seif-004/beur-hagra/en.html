If one claimed, etc., the general rule, etc. Shevuos 48a: Rabbi Yehudah taught, etc. Bava Kamma 46a calls this a great principle in monetary law, and the same rule appears in Bava Metzia 100a and elsewhere.
There is an opinion, etc. As stated in Kiddushin 63b regarding one who says, “I betrothed, etc.” We rule like Rav Asi, who believes him even to consummate the marriage. The Yerushalmi, however, limits the inference: if a person says, “I sold one of my fields but do not know to whom,” and someone says “I bought it,” his word alone is not sufficient. The Hagahos Maimoniyos’ formulation is therefore not clear.
(Collected note) There is an opinion, etc. Hagahos Maimoniyos, Hilchos Ishus chapter 9, cites the Yerushalmi’s analogous case of one who says, “I sold a field, etc.” (End of note.)
