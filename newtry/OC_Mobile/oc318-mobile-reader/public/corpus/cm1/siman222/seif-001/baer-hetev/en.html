Credible. There are several explanations of this matter in the Gemara. See Sema, Shach, and Taz, who discuss it at sufficient length. Study their words and your thirst will be satisfied.
Witness. Shach writes in the name of Hagahot Ashri that nowadays, when there is a shevuat heset, the seller is not considered a witness, because the opposing party can require him to swear and say, 'Give me the item I purchased from you,' and any witness who requires an oath is not regarded as a witness (as stated in siman 121:9). Maadanei Melech writes, however, that from the Rosh (and the other authorities), who state the rule without qualification, it clearly appears that even nowadays the law remains the same, because there is no obligation of oath upon this witness. The case of two persons holding a garment is applied even to a purchase and sale. If each claims that the seller delivered the item to him alone, neither has any claim against the seller, for each says that the seller gave it to him, so how could they impose an oath upon the seller? If each claims that the seller did not deliver it to him but delivered it to the other, he thereby admits that he himself never took possession, and the seller may retract, although he is subject to mi shepara. Rashi similarly explains in Kiddushin, regarding a case where the purchased object remains in the seller's hand, that the seller must be meticulous not to return it to the wrong person, refund that person's money, and give the item to the one to whom it was actually sold, because otherwise he incurs mi shepara. Therefore he will investigate carefully. Since his only consequence is mi shepara, we have never heard that this prevents him from being considered a witness. See there.
