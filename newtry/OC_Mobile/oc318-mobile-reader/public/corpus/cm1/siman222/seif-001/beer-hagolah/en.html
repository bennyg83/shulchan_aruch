Alef) This is the language of the Rambam, chapter 20 of Hilkhot Mekhirah, halachah 4, from the baraita at the end of Kiddushin 73b, as explained there at the beginning of 74a and in Bava Metzia 2b.
Meaning, the case is only where both parties are holding the item.
Bet) Tosafot likewise write this there.
Gimel) The Maggid Mishneh cites the Ran's explanation: if the seller received the money from only one of them, then even if the merchandise is no longer in his possession he is no more credible than any other person. Nevertheless, the person against whom he testifies becomes obligated in a Torah oath through his testimony, because he is a single witness. The person whom he supports should be completely exempt, because his oath is only rabbinic and one witness supports him, so he should collect without an oath. The Maggid Mishneh writes that this was Rambam's intent. The same applies where a single witness supports the claimant: he collects without an oath. The Kesef Mishneh writes that in his view Rambam means that where the seller received money from only one party, even while the merchandise is still in his possession he is not believed to say that he agreed to sell specifically to the other, because it is evident that he is lying. The Tur cites this view in the name of Rabbenu Chananel. If neither party is in possession, see siman 139.
