Deeds of sale, etc. The Tur also includes deeds of gift, writing as follows: If one sold property in Nisan and dated the deed from Tishrei, there is reason for concern that before Tishrei the seller will obtain money, repurchase the land from the purchaser, and execute for him a deed dated from that earlier time, etc. Yet the purchaser may fail to take back the first deed, assuming that it is no longer needed, because even if the deed is returned, the sale or gift is not thereby rescinded, in accordance with the Sages who disagree with Rabban Shimon ben Gamliel in Bava Batra 169a, and as explained below, siman 243, seif 9. With an antedated promissory note, however, the borrower must take back the note itself, and the debt is thereby extinguished. If the creditor does not give him the note because it was lost, the borrower writes an unspecified receipt. This appears correct to me, unlike the strained explanation of Rashbam on Bava Batra 171b concerning a lost deed of sale, where he writes that a receipt should be issued. See there, because the Gemara states afterward that where a promissory note was lost, a receipt is written, not where a deed of sale was lost. See there.
