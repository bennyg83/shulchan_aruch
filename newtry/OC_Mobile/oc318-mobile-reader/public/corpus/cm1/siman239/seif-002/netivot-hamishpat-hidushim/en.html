Later dated sale deeds are invalid. The concern is that perhaps the seller later repurchased the property from the purchaser between Nisan and Tishrei. The purchaser could afterward produce this later dated deed and say, "You sold it back to me after my purchase from you," and thereby prevail improperly. The Tur writes that if witnesses delayed writing the deed and no longer remember the date of the kinyan, they may write the present date and we do not fear this concern. The reason is that if the seller had indeed repurchased the very field he had sold, that event would generate publicity, and the witnesses would not return later and write a deed contradicting what had become publicly known. With an intentionally later dated sale deed, however, we do fear that the repurchase may later become known, because the purchaser can still say, "It is true that I sold it back to you, but afterward you sold it to me again," relying on the intentionally later dated deed. See Beurim.
