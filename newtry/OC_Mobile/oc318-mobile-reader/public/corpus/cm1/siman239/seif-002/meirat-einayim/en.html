In the words of the Late Debt, which is written by C. M.C. in the words of the column and in the words of the author, it is written in his book, Dasni Choo, and in order to speak:
Postdated sale deeds are invalid. This is a separate law and does not concern a person who lost his deed. The Tur explains that if a field was sold in Nisan but the deed is dated Tishrei, we fear that before Tishrei the seller may acquire enough money to buy the field back from the purchaser and receive a deed for that repurchase. Later the original purchaser could produce his deed dated Tishrei and claim, "After I sold it back to you, I bought it again from you." The Tur concludes that if the witnesses delayed writing for a long time after the original kinyan but still remember the date of the kinyan, they may write the deed with the original date. If they do not remember, they should date it with the date of writing. In the Perishah I explained why we do not fear that before the writing date the seller had already repurchased the field. Had such a repurchase occurred, it would ordinarily have become known. The witnesses' lack of knowledge therefore allows them to write as of the current date. This differs from initially choosing a later future date, where there is a real possibility that between the sale and that future date the seller will repurchase, and afterward the purchaser will use the postdated deed to claim another purchase. The Ir Shushan says there is still some concern even when using the actual writing date but that it is preferable to writing a false date. That is incorrect. In that case there is no meaningful concern, because a prior repurchase would ordinarily have become known to the witnesses.
