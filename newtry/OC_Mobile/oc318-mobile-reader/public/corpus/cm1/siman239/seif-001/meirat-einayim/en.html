We heed him and write another. Although regarding a loan deed siman 41 rules that a lender who says, "I lost my deed, write me another," is not believed, the cases differ. A lender seeks a deed in order to collect. We therefore fear that the first deed was not lost or that it will later be found and he will collect twice. With a deed of sale, safeguards can be written into the replacement so that it cannot be used to collect from the seller or seize property from later purchasers. The purchaser needs it primarily as proof that he bought the field.
I later bought it back from you. Although the replacement deed states that it was written only because the first deed was lost and cannot be used for collection, which itself reveals that an earlier deed existed, we still fear misuse. The purchaser might later claim: I never bought the property based on this later date. When I originally bought it I asked the witnesses to prepare two deeds, one ordinary deed and one backup deed marked not for collection, because I feared one might be lost. The ordinary deed was lost, and this one remained. Since multiple deeds may indeed be written for the same field in this fashion, the date on the surviving deed could support his claim that he acquired at that later time.
