A true judgment according to its truth. Sema explains this as judging truthfully according to the needs of the time, sometimes by strict Torah law and sometimes through extraordinary measures needed to preserve public order. Tosafos and Maharsha in the first chapter of Bava Batra explain differently. If witnesses testify and the judge knows they are lying, he may not say, "What concern is it of mine? I will rule according to the witnesses and the responsibility is on them." Such a ruling may be formally "true" because it follows ordinary evidentiary law, but it is not "true according to its truth," because the judge knows that the factual result is false. A judge who seeks truth according to its truth must withdraw from such a case and constantly ensure not only that the ruling follows Torah law but also, insofar as he can discern, that the underlying result is free of deception.
