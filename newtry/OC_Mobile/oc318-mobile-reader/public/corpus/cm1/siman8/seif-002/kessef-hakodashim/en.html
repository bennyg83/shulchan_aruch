Seif 4. It is forbidden to perform work. The prohibition against a judge performing labor in the presence of three people does not appear to include labor done for a mitzvah. The holy Amoraim personally salted fish, chopped wood, and performed similar preparations for Shabbat every Friday. It is inconceivable that they could always hide these activities from other people, and people certainly sometimes entered their homes while they were engaged in them. Likewise, the Sages record scholars carrying burdens for a mitzvah in public. Therefore labor undertaken for the sake of Heaven in connection with an immediate mitzvah does not carry the same concern for judicial dignity. David HaMelech's dancing and leaping before the Ark, and the public activities at the Simchat Beit HaShoevah, likewise included forms of exertion resembling labor. Although where possible it is still preferable to avoid unnecessary public display if no mitzvah benefit is lost, after the fact there is no objection.
