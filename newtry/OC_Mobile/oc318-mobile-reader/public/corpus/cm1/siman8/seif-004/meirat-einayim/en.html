He will not see a son who is a Torah scholar. The Sages derive this from Iyov: "Therefore men fear him; he does not see any of the wise of heart." Meaning, one who imposes excessive fear upon people will not see descendants who are wise of heart.
And they should not step over the heads of the holy people. In their times the people sat on the ground during the public discourse. The judge is therefore told to come early and sit in his place, rather than arrive after the people are seated and step over their heads to reach his seat. This is derived from "You shall not ascend by steps" placed next to "These are the judgments." It appears the same principle applies nowadays where people sit tightly together on a long bench with a table before them. A judge should not step across the bench behind those already seated in order to reach his place.
It must be tolerated by the public to learn from the PLO in P.R. and to be condemned to the Israelites and not to write about the people of Israel, but that they will always take care of them to suffer their troubles:
To honor a judge. As Scripture says, "And I commanded you," which the Sages interpret as a warning to the public that the awe of the judge should be upon them.
To eat, drink, and become intoxicated in public. Tur and Rambam conclude: "and at the gathering of the ignorant and at drinking parties." The vav in "and at the gathering" can mean "or": even at a mitzvah meal it is forbidden for a judge to become drunk, while at gatherings of the ignorant or drinking parties he should not eat and drink at all, even without intoxication. Alternatively, the phrase continues the a fortiori: it is certainly forbidden for him to eat and drink publicly, and all the more so at gatherings of the ignorant and drinking parties. If so, it is understandable why the Mechaber and Rama omitted this additional point because it is self-evident.
