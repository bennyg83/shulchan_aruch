A judge may not conduct himself with domination and arrogance toward the community, but only with humility and reverence. Any communal leader who imposes excessive fear upon the community not for the sake of Heaven will never see a son who is a Torah scholar. It is likewise forbidden to treat the people lightly even if they are unlearned, and the judges must not step over the heads of the holy people. A judge must bear the burden and difficulties of the community. The community, in turn, is commanded to honor the judge, and his authority should be upon them. He too must not demean himself or behave frivolously before them. Once a person has been appointed as a communal leader, he may not perform labor in the presence of three people lest he become demeaned before them, and certainly he may not eat, drink, or become intoxicated, in public. Any judge who has no one to attend to him may not accept appointment as a judge.
