To eat and drink. Concerning the rule that a judge may not eat in the presence of three, it appears that this applies only before adults. Minors are as though absent for this purpose. His wife is also like himself and does not count toward the number. Women would otherwise appear to be like men for this matter, since the reason is that the precious children of Zion should not, Heaven forbid, be regarded as earthenware vessels in the eyes of the unlearned through the familiarity created by eating together. Therefore Chazal did not distinguish, and a judge should not eat even before three Torah scholars. Perhaps this is similar to the statement that among three people it is unlikely that there is not one who has learned, meaning that ordinarily among three there is one of lower standing. Since women are more numerous than men, one should be careful also before women. Nevertheless, one's wife is like one's own person and does not count. Perhaps the same applies to members of his household who are completely familiar with him, though being stringent before three adult household members is proper. It also appears that this restriction applies only in a city where he is appointed as a judge, or elsewhere when he has actually been called upon to act as a judge. It further appears that there is no concern with eating less than a kezayit, for there is no concept of half a measure here. The prohibition is expressed in the language of eating, which ordinarily means a kezayit. Perhaps the concern applies only to a fixed meal, just as the pious practice associated with 'do not eat upon the blood' before burial applies only to a fixed meal. Chazal call nonfixed eating 'tasting.' This requires further study. It also appears that when those seated with him are eating too, there is no concern. The same applies where all are participating in one meal, even if he happens to eat at a moment when the others are singing or otherwise occupied, because throughout the meal everyone remains connected to the eating until birkat hamazon. There is therefore no degradation in his eating in their presence. Perhaps the common practice of inviting those who enter during a meal to eat also lessens the concern, since they are not entirely removed from the meal. Still, one should not rely on this alone initially. If they are seated together at the same meal, even if they eat at different moments, it appears permissible.
