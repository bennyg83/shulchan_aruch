He violates a negative commandment. The Tur quotes Rambam that one violates the prohibition, "You shall not erect for yourself a pillar." Maharik and the Kesef Mishneh ask why Rambam did not quote the verse cited by the Gemara, "You shall not plant for yourself an asherah." It appears that the Gemara in Avodah Zarah 52 teaches that Rabbi Akiva holds that an idol belonging to a Jew is not prohibited until it is worshiped, based on the verse, "and he places it in secret," meaning until he performs acts done in secret. Rabbi Yishmael, who holds that it is prohibited immediately, explains that verse as teaching that the idol requires burial. Rabbi Akiva derives the requirement of burial from the verse juxtaposing, "You shall not plant for yourself an asherah," to the altar: just as the altar requires burial, so does idolatry. The other opinion requires that verse for the prohibition against appointing an unfit judge. Rambam rules in Hilchot Avodah Zarah like Rabbi Akiva, that an idol belonging to a Jew is not prohibited until it is worshiped. He therefore requires the verse, "and he places it in secret," for that rule, while the verse of the asherah is needed to teach burial. There is consequently no derivation left there for the prohibition against appointing an unfit judge, because that interpretation depends upon the dispute between Rabbi Yishmael and Rabbi Akiva. Rambam therefore quotes the Sifrei's derivation from the verse, "You shall not erect for yourself a pillar." This is straightforward.
