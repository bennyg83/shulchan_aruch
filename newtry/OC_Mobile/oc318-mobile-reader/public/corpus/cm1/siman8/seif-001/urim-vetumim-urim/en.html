And he is not learned, etc. The conjunction divides the alternatives: either he is unfit in conduct, or he is proper in conduct but not learned. Sema. Since above at the beginning of siman 3 we ruled like the Rosh that among three judges it is sufficient if one is learned, this seif must either concern a panel where none of the three is learned, or, more plausibly, a permanent judicial appointment. If unlearned men happen to sit with one qualified judge in an isolated case, the Rosh's rule may suffice. But it is forbidden knowingly to appoint an unlearned person as a permanent judge who will repeatedly sit on panels. Those formally appointed to judge disputes should all be as learned in Torah as circumstances allow. "God stands in the congregation of judges," and the office itself requires competence.
He violates a negative commandment. "You shall not show favoritism in judgment," meaning one may not recognize the face of a wealthy man or relative and appoint him to judge despite his lack of qualification. Sema. It is a mitzvah to treat him lightly. Scripture calls such a judge "gods of silver and gods of gold," comparing him to idolatry in order to teach that he deserves contempt. Mockery is ordinarily forbidden, but mockery directed at such corruption is permitted.
