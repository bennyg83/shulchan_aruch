Seif 5. In the gloss: “The agent of beit din may report the matter to beit din, and this does not constitute lashon hara.” Note: See the Sema for the source of this law. In truth, that source does not prove the general proposition. According to the Hagahot Maimoniyot cited by the Magen Avraham in Orach Chaim siman 156, it is permitted to speak negatively about habitual instigators of quarrels, based on the verse, “I will come after you and confirm your words.” Datan and Aviram were quintessential men of strife, as described in the Midrash and Gemara. It was therefore permissible to speak of them in this way. But how does that prove that an agent of beit din may similarly report lashon hara about ordinary people who are not habitual quarrelers? This requires study. Seif 5. “One who takes payment to judge, all judgments that he judged are void.” It appears to me that this applies specifically where he adjudicated by coercive authority. If the litigants came to him voluntarily, knew that his practice was to take payment, and especially if they paid him before he announced the ruling, they were free to leave and refuse to accept his decision. If they nevertheless remained before him for judgment and paid his fee, they accepted him upon themselves. His judgment should therefore be valid, no worse than where litigants accept upon themselves one who is otherwise disqualified. Here the defect is not a Torah disqualification but a penalty, so their acceptance should certainly be effective and they cannot later retract. Moreover, the underlying prohibition against taking payment would seem to apply chiefly when one acts as a coercive judge and demands compensation for performing a judicial obligation imposed upon him. Where the parties voluntarily choose a person and agree to compensate him, the matter is closer to acceptance of an arbitrator, though each case requires its own conditions. I have discussed this elsewhere and cannot elaborate here.
