Not to appoint an unfit judge, and the greatness of a judge's reward and punishment. This siman contains 5 seifim.
Whoever appoints an unfit judge who is not learned in Torah wisdom and is not fit to serve as a judge, even if he is otherwise wholly admirable and possesses other good qualities, violates a negative commandment. Rama: It is forbidden to appoint an ignoramus as a judge on the assumption that he will ask a scholar whenever necessary. Towns that have no scholars fit to serve as judges, or whose residents are all unlearned, but which need judges to adjudicate disputes among them so that they will not resort to non Jewish courts, may appoint the best and wisest among them, according to the judgment of the townspeople, even though they are not otherwise fit to serve as judges. Once the townspeople accept them, no individual may disqualify them. Likewise, a community may accept upon itself a beit din whose members are not fit according to Torah law. Any judge appointed in exchange for silver or gold is forbidden to be treated with the honor due a judge. Moreover, it is a mitzvah to treat him lightly and with contempt. See also the end of siman 3.
