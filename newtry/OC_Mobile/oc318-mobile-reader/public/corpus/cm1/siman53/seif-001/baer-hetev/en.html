They may not. The reason is that each alteration may disadvantage the borrower. If the lender holds one document for one hundred and the borrower later repays part, even if the lender gives him a receipt the debt document is now impaired unless the payment is written directly on the document itself. If the borrower later says he paid the balance, the lender may collect only after swearing, absent a credibility clause, as explained at the beginning of siman 84. Conversely, if the lender holds two documents of fifty each, the borrower may prefer that arrangement to one document for one hundred. If he pays part of the debt and receives a receipt, he must preserve the receipt carefully from mice or loss, lest the lender later claim the entire debt. These reasons are stated in the Gemara. They are needed only when it is known that the borrower has not yet paid. Otherwise, independent of these concerns, one may not alter the documents because perhaps the borrower already paid and received a receipt, as the Mechaber concludes. Thus the Sema.

The Shach notes, however, that one might still fear collusion. Perhaps the borrower also owes other creditors and lacks enough land to pay everyone, and he conspires with this lender to create two documents so that this lender will collect two proportional shares, as discussed in siman 104, seif 6. The Shach answers that the documents can be written with an express clause: if the borrower also owes others such that this creditor's proper share would be only fifty, then the holder of this particular document may collect nothing. Alternatively, since ordinarily a lender does not know of the borrower's other debts, as stated in siman 37, seif 13, we do not suspect that the borrower revealed them and arranged collusion. We generally fear collusion only where there is an independent defect such as a lost document. Shach.
