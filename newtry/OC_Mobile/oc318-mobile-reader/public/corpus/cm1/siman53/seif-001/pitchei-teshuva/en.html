And he said: Make two for me. See Responsa Radbaz, part 1, siman 115, concerning a creditor who asks that the debtor execute an additional instrument in the non-Jewish courts so that payment at the agreed time will be more secure. He records that the Rambam was asked this and answered that the debtor should strengthen his obligation through another valid device and deposit the secular document with a trustworthy person. The Radbaz questions this and concludes that in our times one should not rule in that manner across the board. Rather, if the creditor follows upright ways and the debtor is violent or recalcitrant, so that there is reason to fear that he will not obey a din Torah, he agrees that one may write an additional document under secular law, explicitly identifying it as the very same debt already recorded under Jewish law. The creditor must be warned that if the debtor obeys beit din and the creditor nevertheless sues in the secular courts, beit din will stand against him. If the situation is reversed and the creditor is the violent party, his request is not heard at all. See there. Shaar Mishpat cites this responsum and agrees with it.
