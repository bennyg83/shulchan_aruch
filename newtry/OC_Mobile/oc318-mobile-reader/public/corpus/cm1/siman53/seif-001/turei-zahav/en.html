(Gloss to seif 1: Even if we know, etc.) Tosafot add this so that the Gemara’s earlier rationale is still needed. One cannot simply say that perhaps the debtor already paid and took a receipt, as in the following seif. Even if we know he has not yet paid, he may later pay in full and take a receipt without knowing that the document was altered. The case may be where the creditor asks that the document be exchanged and that the debtor be informed. In the following case, however, the concern is that payment already occurred, and later notice cannot repair consumed theft. One may ask why at the beginning of siman 54 the creditor may choose either to issue a partial receipt or exchange the document. Why is the absent debtor stronger than one who is present and protests? The answer is that when the debtor actually pays part, he compels the creditor to choose one of the two lawful methods, and the creditor may select between them because the burden of proof rests on the claimant. Before any payment, the debtor seeks nothing from the creditor and benefits from leaving matters unchanged. Since one proposed alteration may disadvantage him, the burden of proof operates in the opposite direction.
