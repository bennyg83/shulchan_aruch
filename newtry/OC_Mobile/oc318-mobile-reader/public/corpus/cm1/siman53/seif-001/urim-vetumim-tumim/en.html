Except with the borrower's consent. If both parties consent, the document may be rewritten. Rabbenu Yerucham and Shach say this. One must ask: from what date may the new document be written? If from the original date, how can the witnesses write it after they already completed their agency by writing a valid first document, as explained above in siman 49? If from the new date, then it is obvious that with both parties' consent a new note may be written; even if the first note never existed, if both now want to create ten documents for one thousand, the witnesses may do so. There is also a difficulty in the Gemara's formulation: "Would the Sages do something that benefits the lender and harms the borrower," or the reverse? Why is it necessary to say that the change benefits one side? Surely if the lender already holds a note for one hundred, the borrower cannot compel him to exchange it for two notes of fifty each. Whether or not the change benefits the lender, the borrower has no authority to force the holder of a valid document to restructure it. Indeed, Sema and Rambam, Hilchot Malveh chapter 23, focus only on the fact that the proposed change disadvantages the opposing party, not on the benefit to the party requesting it. The better explanation is that the case concerns a loan note that was erased or damaged, where beit din is in any event authorized to issue a replacement from the original date. The lender then says: "Since you are already writing a replacement, write me two notes of fifty instead of one of one hundred." Or conversely, the borrower asks that the replacement be structured differently. The Gemara teaches that beit din may not alter the form because the change can disadvantage the other party. Rabbenu Yerucham's statement now has a meaningful novelty. If both agree, beit din may write the replacement from the original date even though its form differs from the first note. One might have thought that beit din has authority to recreate from the original date only an exact copy of the first document and that any changed formulation would appear false. Rabbenu Yerucham teaches that with both parties' consent, beit din may preserve the original date while dividing or consolidating the obligation as agreed. There remains a further question whether such a restructuring can alter the order of collection among creditors because the number of documents may affect priority or distribution. I discuss that below in siman 104.
