If the lender holds one document for one hundred and the borrower later pays part, then even if the lender gives him a receipt for the payment, the remaining document is considered impaired unless the payment is written on the face of the document itself. If the borrower later claims additional payment, the lender cannot collect the balance without an oath where the document lacks a credibility clause. Conversely, if the lender holds two separate documents for fifty each, the borrower may prefer that arrangement because if he pays one part he can receive and destroy the corresponding document rather than preserve a receipt and fear its loss while the lender still holds one document for the full amount. These two considerations are also stated in the Gemara. They are needed when it is known that the borrower has not yet paid. But if payment is uncertain, the lender's document may not be altered in any event, even by replacing one large document with a smaller one and tearing the large document, because perhaps the borrower already paid the large document, as the Mechaber concludes. The Tur and Mechaber's final clause speaks where we do not know whether payment was made. In such a case, if it is certain there was no payment, it would ordinarily be permitted to replace a document for one hundred with one for fifty. Rama comes to teach that even when nonpayment is known, the alteration is forbidden for the reasons stated.
