To rent her dwelling to others. Rashba, responsum 1, netiv 3, writes this law in the name of the Yerushalmi. In general, one who possesses a right of use in another person's property may lend or rent it to others. But the widow's residence exists only by virtue of the beit din condition, so that she not be disgraced. The Sages granted it to her alone, not so that she should rent it out. Ran, in chapter HaNose, cites a Tosefta: if one says, "Give my daughter a widow's residence," they give it only if she accepts upon herself to live in it; the heirs may prevent her from renting it. The Tosefta concludes that when she dies, they inherit it, meaning the heirs of the donor, not the daughter's heirs, because the donor's intent was only to prevent her humiliation in widowhood, as Rashba explains.
