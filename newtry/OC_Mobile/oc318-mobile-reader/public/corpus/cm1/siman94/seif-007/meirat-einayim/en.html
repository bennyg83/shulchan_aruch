There is an opinion that he must swear, etc., and there is an opinion, etc. The Mechaber follows here the approach he adopted in Beit Yosef, where he wrote in the name of the Baal HaTerumot that the Baal HaTerumot and the Rosh disagree in this law. According to the Baal HaTerumot, he must swear regarding the matters added by oath extension, or else reverse the oath; according to the Rosh, there is no law of oath extension here at all. In the Perishah, however, I demonstrated that this presentation is contradicted by what the Tur writes in siman 75:18, where he cites the view that we do not apply the rule that one who cannot swear must pay in such a case, and those are the very words of the Baal HaTerumot here. The Tur concludes there that the Rosh agreed with him. I also proved there that even the Ramah, whom the Tur cites as disagreeing with the Baal HaTerumot, disagrees only with the second case there, which is the case stated by the Mechaber here in seif 9. But where the defendant says concerning part of the original claim, “I do not know,” everyone agrees that there is no oath extension that creates the rule that one who cannot swear must pay. Rather, the law is as follows: if the matters added by oath extension are matters concerning which, had they been claimed independently, the defendant would have been obligated to take an oath of hesset or another oath, then he must swear regarding them now as well. This is what is meant there by “either he swears or he reverses it.” If he would not have been obligated to swear regarding them independently, he likewise does not become obligated merely because they were added by oath extension. The Baal HaTerumot cited by the Beit Yosef discusses a case where the defendant would have been obligated to take an oath of hesset on those additional claims even without oath extension. The Rosh cited there addresses both situations and says only that there is no law of oath extension here that would trigger the rule that one who cannot swear must pay; each claim is governed by its own law. See there. This is clear. The Mechaber himself rules without qualification in siman 75:15 in accordance with the Baal HaTerumot on this point, as I wrote there. The Ir Shushan followed the Mechaber’s wording here and presented the matter as a dispute, but that is not precise.
