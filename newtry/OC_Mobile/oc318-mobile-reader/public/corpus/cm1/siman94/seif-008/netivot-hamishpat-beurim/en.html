And on the matters added by gilgul shevuah she swears and collects. See Shach, seif katan 12. There is a textual error. The correct reading is: this is a different oath, and if the defendant denies everything, it is now only a rabbinic oath and he may shift it. Nevertheless, the ruling teaches that even though he jumped and swore after the other party sought to add the matter through gilgul, and even though that new matter is now being added, once the original oath matter has been concluded the legal status changes. Tumim asks that we rule that where a matter is added through gilgul and the person says, "I do not know," he is not treated as one who is obligated to swear and cannot swear, and is therefore exempt. Why then would refusal to swear because his conscience troubles him and he is uncertain be worse than expressly saying, "I do not know"? In my view the practical case is where the added matter concerns the borrower's claim that he paid. If he says, "I do not know," he becomes liable because that is an admission of uncertainty whether he repaid. He must therefore make a definite claim and swear on it. What Ketzot HaChoshen writes throughout this siman is difficult in my eyes. His uncertainty whether a Torah oath can be shifted onto one who otherwise swears and collects has no real basis. Gilgul applies to all oaths unless the poskim distinguish them, and Tosafot in Bava Metzia 14, s.v. Dina hu, explicitly write that one can compel the holder of a note through gilgul to swear that the debt was not paid. Their intent must concern a Torah level gilgul, because without that the note holder would in any event take the ordinary oath. Ketzot's explanation in seif katan 20 of Rashi is likewise very difficult. The Mishnah itself states there that when one says, "I do not know," he acquires the larger item. Whatever the rationale there, he cannot simply say that he does not know. Moreover, even if he could, he chooses to make a definite claim and swear on it, and Sumchus never said otherwise. Ketzot's discussion in seif katan 5 answering Tumim's question is likewise very difficult.
