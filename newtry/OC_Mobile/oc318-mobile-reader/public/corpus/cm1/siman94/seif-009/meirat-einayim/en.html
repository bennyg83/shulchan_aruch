He must swear regarding the matters introduced through gilgul. This too is from Baal HaTerumot. It concerns a case where, had these matters been claimed against the defendant independently, he would not have been obligated even in a hesset oath, such as the oath of partners after they have divided. The Beit Yosef explicitly explains Baal HaTerumot this way; see there. The Tur likewise proves this, for in siman 75, seif 18, in the ruling of the cited "some say," which is from Baal HaTerumot, he writes that the defendant takes a hesset oath on the matters introduced through gilgul that he does not know, and Rama likewise rules there in seif 15. The reason is that there the case concerns a matter on which the defendant would have been obligated to take a hesset oath that he does not know even if it had been claimed independently. The ruling teaches that we do not say that through gilgul all the claims become one unified claim, so that since he says "I do not know" regarding part of it he would be subject to the rule that one who is obligated to swear but cannot swear must pay, or at least that he must take a Torah oath regarding the gilgul just as on the principal oath. Rather, this is not so. Rama did not state his view here because he relied on what he wrote there in siman 75.
