If this one... they say to him... Meaning, when the principal oath is reversed, the gilgul oath cannot be reversed independently. If the claimant does not wish to swear the reversed oath, we tell the defendant to swear on everything. Although a heses oath can ordinarily be reversed onto the claimant against the claimant's will, here the defendant still owes the original oath and therefore the claimant may roll the second matter onto it. Even though the second matter would not independently warrant an oath, gilgul can attach it, as the Yerushalmi at the end of the first chapter of Kiddushin derives from sotah. There the oath includes matters that themselves would not have generated an oath, such as her status while betrothed or awaiting yibbum. The same applies here. But if the defendant says regarding the original claim that the claimant should swear, the result differs. This is the meaning of the continuation, "And if he says..." unlike Sema, seif katan 12. This also appears from the Raavad's gloss to Rambam, Hilchos To'en, chapter 1: even after reversal, the defendant remains bound by the original heses oath and therefore the gilgul may still attach.
