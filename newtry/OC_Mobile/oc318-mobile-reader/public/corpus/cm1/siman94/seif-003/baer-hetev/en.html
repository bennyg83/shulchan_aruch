The definite claims. The Shach writes that one should not ask why definite claims require gilgul at all, since even without gilgul the claimant can impose a hesset oath. One can find cases where this matters, such as where the claimant exempted the defendant from an oath, as stated in siman 71, seif 5; where he claims, 'I had a prozbul and it was lost,' as in siman 67, seif 33; or where he claims regarding interest, 'I took it permissibly,' as discussed above in siman 82 by the Sema as stated there, seif katan 19 and in Yoreh Deah at the end of siman 169. The same applies to similar cases. Lechem Mishneh answers that the case is one in which the defendant already accepted before beit din that he would take the principal oath and not reverse it. The Taz resolves the matter differently.
