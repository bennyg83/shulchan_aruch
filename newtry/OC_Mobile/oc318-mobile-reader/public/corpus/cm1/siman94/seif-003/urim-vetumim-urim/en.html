Shach and Lechem Mishneh ask: if with definite claims he must swear through rolled oath, why is rolled oath needed at all? On a definite claim he would independently owe hesset. Shach answers with cases such as one who says, "I had a prozbul and lost it," or, "I took it permissibly," where no hesset is imposed, yet the matter can still be rolled into another oath. This requires study as practical law. If the presumption that a person does not abandon what is permitted and eat what is forbidden is strong enough to remove hesset, why should it not also remove a rolled oath? See Tumim. A better case is where the person was contractually exempted from an independent oath, but the exemption did not expressly include rolled oaths. Then the claim may be rolled into another oath even though it could not generate an independent oath.
