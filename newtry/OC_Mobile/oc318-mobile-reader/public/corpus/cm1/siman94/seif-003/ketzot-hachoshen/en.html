The Shach asks what practical case exists for the rule that one already obligated in an oath can have another matter added, since a person making a definite denial is ordinarily already subject to hesset. He answers with cases such as claiming that a prozbul existed and was lost, or claiming that prohibited interest was taken, where no ordinary hesset may be imposed but an oath can nevertheless enter through extension. This is correct. A legal presumption that removes hesset does not necessarily remove oath extension. We find similarly that Rashba exempts a person who has held land for three years from hesset, yet the Mishnah says movables can attach land to an oath by extension without distinguishing whether the land had already been possessed for three years. Thus even where a presumption removes an ordinary hesset oath, it does not necessarily defeat an attached oath.
