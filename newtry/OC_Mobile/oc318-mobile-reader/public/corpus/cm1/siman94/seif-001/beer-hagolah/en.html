So writes the Rif there. The Rambam likewise explains this at the beginning of chapter 10 of Hilkhot Sheluchin and in chapter 1 of Hilkhot To'en, law 12. Rashi likewise writes so there.

Tur, seif 6. Rashi likewise explains there regarding the Gemara's statement that one practical difference is whether beit din opens the matter for him. The Ran similarly writes at the beginning of Bava Metzia in the name of Tosafot regarding deriving a gilgul shevuah from the oath imposed by one witness, there 4a, and from the gilgul shevuah of a sotah in the first chapter of Kiddushin, 26b, even though her husband accuses her only concerning one particular man. Rabbi Yosef ibn Migash writes that this applies where beit din knows that the claimant has a basis to impose an additional oath. Otherwise, the judges may not raise it on their own, because they would be acting like advocates for litigants.
