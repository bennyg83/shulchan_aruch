I was uncertain whether one may extend a Torah oath to a matter on which the claimant would otherwise swear and collect. For example, a lender holds a document and the borrower claims payment. Separately, the borrower brings an oral claim against the lender supported by one witness, requiring the lender to take a Torah oath. Can the borrower attach by oath extension a demand that the lender also swear that the written debt was not paid? On the one hand, land, slaves, and documents themselves are excluded from direct oaths yet can enter through oath extension. Perhaps the same should apply to an oath of one who collects. On the other hand, Scripture says that the owner takes the oath "and shall not pay," from which we derive that Torah oath is fundamentally for one who swears and is exempt, not for one who swears and collects. Perhaps this exclusion remains even through oath extension. The matter presently requires study.
