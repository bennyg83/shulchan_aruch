Every... See Rashi there. This is also the plain meaning of the Gemara.
For if he were... and if... This is derived from Kiddushin 28, "Money, regarding which..." as explained in the preceding siman.
And there is an authority who says... See Tosafos, Kiddushin 49a, who writes in the name of Ritzba that even there gilgul applies. All the more so here. Even according to Rabbeinu Yitzchak's interpretation, here he concedes, because this resembles a worker's oath, which is not imposed by strict law. A Torah oath cannot be reversed because the Torah itself obligated him.
Even... The Gemara there says that beit din itself may open the matter for him. See Rashi there. Even according to Rabbeinu Chananel, his qualification applies only to a rabbinic oath; with a Torah oath everyone agrees that gilgul is applied. At the beginning of Bava Metzia the rule is derived from the oath imposed by one witness and extended to two witnesses, where beit din administers the oath even if the claimant did not expressly raise the point. There is also proof from sotah. See Beer HaGolah.
