The condition that the defendant make a certain plea means only that we do not apply the rule, "Since he cannot swear on the matter added by oath extension, he must pay." He must nevertheless swear through oath extension that he does not know. This is Taz's explanation and it fits the rulings in siman 93 and siman 344, where a party can swear by oath extension that he does not know the amount of the loss or does not know which animal died. The Shach's broader position that no oath of uncertainty can ever be imposed by oath extension cannot be reconciled with these rulings. Although a Torah oath ordinarily requires a definite responsive plea, oath extension is different. Just as matters such as land and documents, which are excluded from direct Torah oath, can enter through extension, so too an uncertainty can be included and the party swears that he truly does not know. What does not follow from oath extension is the payment rule of one unable to swear. This also explains Rashi in Perek HaShoel. The Gemara says Sumchus agrees where a Torah oath exists. Rashi initially explains the case as a direct oath rather than the later conclusion involving clothing or produce attached by oath extension. Ramban explains that where the principal Torah oath requires the defendant either to swear or pay, the credibility created by the oath can defeat Sumchus's division rule. If the only attached matter could be answered merely, "I do not know," through oath extension, that would not produce the same effect. Rashi therefore had reason to identify a true principal Torah oath requiring a definite oath.
