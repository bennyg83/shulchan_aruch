Definite. The Tur likewise uses this wording. The Sema explains that the defendant makes a definite denial regarding the claim that the plaintiff wishes to roll into the oath. The phrase "and became obligated in an oath" means that he became obligated in an oath on another matter, and this additional claim is then rolled into that oath even though it would not independently require an oath, such as the partners' oath after the partnership was divided. The Shach writes that the Bach disagrees and explains the word "definite" as referring to the primary oath. The Bach says this is clear from Tosafot and the Rosh in the chapter HaShoel. The Shach responds that the sources there actually state explicitly like the Sema. Moreover, the primary claim cannot be an uncertain one, because then the defendant would not initially incur an oath at all, and the law would instead be that one who is obligated to swear but cannot swear must pay. See there. See the Taz.
Uncertain. The Sema writes that here and at the end of this siman the Mechaber rules without qualification, whereas in siman 75, seif 15, he records an opinion that gilgul applies in such a case and that the rule of one who is obligated to swear but cannot swear must pay is then applied. The Shach asks that at the end of siman 344 the Mechaber also rules regarding produce that gilgul applies where the defendant says he does not know, and leaves the matter requiring study. See what is written on this in siman 82, seif katan 28.
Basis. The Sema explains that the claim to be rolled in must have some factual basis that gives reason to suspect the defendant, as in the case of partners, who may rationalize taking for themselves. The Shach writes that this too is difficult, because at the end of siman 344 the Mechaber rules that gilgul applies even without such a basis. He leaves the matter requiring study.
