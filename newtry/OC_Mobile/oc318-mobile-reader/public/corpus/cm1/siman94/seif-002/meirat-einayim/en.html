Only where the defendant makes a certain claim, etc. The Tur likewise writes this. It refers to the claim that the plaintiff wishes to add by extension: the defendant asserts with certainty that he is not liable on that claim, analogous to the case of the sotah. The words, 'and he became obligated to take an oath,' mean that he became obligated to take an oath because of some other matter, and we then add this additional claim to that oath even though, standing alone, it would not have required an oath. An example is a partners' oath after division, as explained above. This is why the Mechaber concludes, 'But regarding an uncertain claim of the claimant,' etc. Meaning, one may add an uncertain claim to an oath already required for another reason when there is some basis for that uncertain claim, as in the case of partners where there is concern that a person may rationalize retaining money. One should not interpret the Tur and Mechaber's words, 'where the defendant makes a certain claim and became obligated to take an oath,' as referring entirely to the principal oath, meaning that only where the defendant certainly denies the principal claim and is thereby obligated to swear may another claim be added. If so, the conclusion, 'But regarding an uncertain claim of the claimant,' would likewise refer to the principal oath, and the requirement that there be a basis for that claim would then be peculiar. Even without extension, one does not administer an oath on a claimant's uncertain allegation unless there is a basis for suspicion, as in the cases listed by the Tur and Mechaber at the beginning of siman 93.
