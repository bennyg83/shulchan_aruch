Only where the defendant makes a definite claim. The Sema explains that this refers to the additional claim: the defendant states with certainty that he incurred no liability regarding it. The Bach disagrees and says it refers to the principal oath, claiming support from Tosafot and the Rosh in the chapter HaShoel. His words are surprising, because Tosafot and the Rosh there explicitly support the Sema. It is also impossible to interpret an uncertain plea as referring to the principal Torah oath, because such a plea would not create an oath obligation at all. Rather, one who is obligated to swear but cannot do so must pay. One could perhaps find a case after Rav Nachman's enactment where a person says, “I do not know whether you lent me,” and owes a hesset oath, but this is not the meaning of the Tur or Mechaber, and Tosafot and the Rosh discuss the Mishna itself. As a matter of law, whether an additional claim can be imposed when the principal hesset oath itself concerns uncertainty requires study. Even according to the view that an uncertain response to the additional claim cannot be extended, the principal uncertain claim may differ. The rule of the sotah teaches that extension is used where the person can actually swear. If he says of the additional claim, “I do not know,” a Torah oath cannot be taken in that form, and the Sages did not go so far as to make him pay through the rule that one who cannot swear must pay. This is implied by the Rosh. But where the principal oath itself is that he does not know, it may still be extended in a form that he can swear. This must be accepted according to the Tur and Mechaber, as explained in siman 72, seif katan 47, and at the end of this siman. Cited cross-references in the source include: 70.
But if the defendant makes an uncertain claim regarding the additional matter. This is as the Sema explains in seif katan 7, and it is explicit in Tosafot and the Rosh in the chapter HaShoel. They prove it from the Mishna where the lender says that the borrowed animal died and the renter says, “I do not know.” He is exempt unless another oath obligation exists, such as where three cows are claimed and he admits one. Tosafot and the Rosh ask that he must in any event swear that the animal died naturally, and the claim that the rented animal died should be added by extension. They answer that an uncertain claim is not imposed by extension. Yet below, at the end of siman 344, the Mechaber rules concerning produce that an uncertain claim is imposed by extension.
