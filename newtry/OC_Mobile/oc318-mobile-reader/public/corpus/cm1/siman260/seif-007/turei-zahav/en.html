(Seif 8, beyond fifty cubits.) At that distance the fledgling no longer has the status of one that merely hops from its dovecote, but of one that flies, and we may follow the general majority. Rashi explains this way. One cannot object that in siman 232, seif 21, we rule that monetary law does not follow the majority. Tosafot answer in Bava Batra, chapter Lo Yachpor, that this limitation applies where there is no possessory presumption, but where there is a monetary presumption we do follow the majority. See what I wrote at the beginning of siman 259 concerning a lost article. Beit Yosef writes that within fifty cubits the bird belongs to the dovecote owner, because we cannot follow the general majority and say that it fell from travelers, since the Mishna speaks of a path through vineyards. Beyond fifty cubits it belongs to the finder, because it must have fallen from travelers and cannot be attributed to the dovecotes; since it bears no identifying mark, the owner despairs. This is astonishing. If the Mishna speaks of a path through vineyards, even beyond fifty cubits it belongs to the dovecote owner, as the Tur and Rambam state explicitly. Moreover, there is no difficulty requiring us to follow the majority of the world, since a fledgling does not ordinarily hop more than fifty cubits, as Rashi explains. Nor did the Sages concern themselves with travelers, because travelers do not ordinarily carry fledglings, just as they did not take travelers into account regarding large wine flasks in siman 259, seif 9. Beit Yosef’s statement that beyond fifty cubits the bird belongs to the finder because it fell from travelers is likewise no more than Rashi’s explanation that at that distance we see it has left the category of a hopping fledgling. Nimukei Yosef’s view requires study. The Sema too writes that beyond fifty cubits, since the bird cannot be attributed to a dovecote, we say it fell from travelers. He appears to follow Nimukei Yosef. His explanation is even more difficult, because within fifty cubits, why do we not follow the majority of the world, namely travelers? It is clear that the case does not concern a path through vineyards. The reason cannot therefore be travelers, but only Rashi’s explanation.
