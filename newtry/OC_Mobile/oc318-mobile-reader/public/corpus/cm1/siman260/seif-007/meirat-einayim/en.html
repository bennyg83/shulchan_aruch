At the place of the threshing floors. The Tur likewise writes this. In the Perishah I explained that the law depends on the place where the produce is found. Since it is found at the threshing floors, even if it is found after the threshing season, we attribute it to the owners. When they threshed, they did not want to trouble themselves to gather a small amount of fruit scattered over four cubits. This excludes opinions that make the law depend on whether it was found specifically during the threshing season.
A kav within four cubits. The Tur says, "within four by four cubits or more." In the Derishah I wrote that according to both approaches in Tosafos this should be understood as a stringency: only where a kav is spread over an area of at least four by four cubits does it belong to the finder. If it is spread over four by two, he should not touch it. Likewise, the unresolved question of half a kav within two cubits means that even where it is spread over an area of two by two, or two by four, because of the doubt he should not touch it. The Mechaber follows Rambam and quotes the Gemara's wording simply.
Or over more than four cubits. Rambam, chapter 15 of Hilchot Gezeilah, law 12, likewise says this, meaning the kav is scattered over an area larger than four cubits. The expression is somewhat awkward; it should have said "within more than four cubits." It also appears unnecessary, because if within four cubits it belongs to the finder, all the more so if spread over a larger area. This difficulty also exists in the Tur. It cannot mean that more than a kav, up to two kav, spread over four cubits belongs to the finder, because that is not so. Such a case is one of the unresolved questions, as Rama immediately glosses and as the Tur explicitly states. Perhaps Rambam, Tur, and Mechaber include this wording to teach by implication that specifically a kav spread over more than four cubits belongs to the finder, whereas more than a kav, even if spread over more than four cubits and the labor per unit is similar, does not belong to the finder because it falls within the unresolved question, like two kav within eight cubits. The Mechaber therefore concludes that the case is doubtful.
Even with dates alone, etc. Meaning, if a kav of dates is found within four cubits. The textual versions of the Gemara differ. Rama follows the Tur's version. The Maggid Mishneh writes on Rambam that according to our Gemara's version this is not correct. Rather, each species poses an independent question: because dates and pomegranates are coarse and involve little effort to gather, perhaps the owner does not despair but will gather them; sesame is more valuable than other produce, and the question is whether because of its value the owner will exert himself to collect even a kav within four cubits.
