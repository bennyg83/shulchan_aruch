But some disagree with all this and hold that there are three distinctions, etc. The three distinctions do not all represent explicit disputes with the Rambam, but there are two principal disagreements. First, where the Rambam says that if an object is found in a place only partially guarded and has no identifying sign, the finder may take it and keep it, the other authorities hold that it must remain in his possession until Eliyahu comes, because it came into his hand before lawful despair and therefore involved despair without knowledge. Second, in a case of doubtful intentional placement in a place not fully guarded, where the object does have an identifying sign, the Maggid Mishneh and Kesef Mishneh understand the Rambam to hold that one should initially leave it in place. Other authorities disagree and hold that the rule "doubtful placement, do not touch it" was said only where the object has no sign. If it has an identifying sign, one takes it and announces it. The Gemara strongly supports their view. The Maggid Mishneh says the Rambam had a different textual version. According to their explanation, the Rambam's words, "and every object that has a sign must be announced," refer specifically to a case where the finder already transgressed and took it. This is supported by the fact that he says merely "must announce it," not "must take it and announce it." I gave further proof in the Derishah. The Tur records explicitly only the first disagreement, but I brought support there for the second as well.
