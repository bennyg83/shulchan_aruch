By their wings. The Gemara and the Tur state explicitly that such a knot is not an identifying mark, because everyone ties them this way. Nor can the location serve as an identifying mark, because since the fledglings move about we say that they wandered there from elsewhere. The Sema explains that the case is where no dovecote is within fifty cubits, so there is doubt whether they were intentionally placed there or fell there unintentionally. The Shach writes that apparently the Sema was troubled that otherwise they would belong to the owner of the dovecote, as stated in seif 8, and as the Tosafot Yom Tov writes in Elu Metziot. But this difficulty is unnecessary. On the contrary, if there were no dovecote within fifty cubits and the doubt were merely whether they were intentionally placed or accidentally dropped, why would it matter that they move about? Rather, the case must be where there is a dovecote nearby within fifty cubits. We therefore do not suspect that they fell from a passerby, as Tosafot in Bava Batra 23b explain: where it is possible to say that they came from a dovecote, we do not attribute them to an accidental fall. See there, where the Shach brings proof from the Gemara and the authorities.

At all. Here too the Rama abbreviated his wording. It is obvious that where an object was certainly placed there and has no identifying mark, even if the location is entirely unguarded it does not belong to the finder; rather, it must remain until Elijah comes, because this is despair without knowledge. The Tur and the Rosh therefore had to explain that small bundles without an identifying mark in the public domain are different because they are rolled about by people's feet. Their owners originally set them down there to lighten their load, then forgot them and despaired, since there is no identifying mark either on the object or in its location. Meaning, only in such a case, where we may presume that the owners despaired immediately before the finder found them, did the object come into his possession permissibly. But ordinarily, where the case is one of despair without knowledge, it must remain until Elijah comes according to the authorities and the Rama. See below, siman 262 seif katan 12. Shach.
