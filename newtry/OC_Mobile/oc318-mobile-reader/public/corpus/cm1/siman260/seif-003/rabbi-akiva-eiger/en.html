[Sema, letter 42] For in this case everyone agrees that he should retain it. Note: It appears to me that the rationale can be explained as follows. Since he took it with intent to return it, and the owners later despaired, if after that despair he then intended to steal it, he violates only the positive commandment of returning a lost object, as stated in our sugya and above at the beginning of siman 260. See Tosafot. The reason is that through despair the object has partially left the owner's domain, so only the positive commandment to return it remains. In a case of doubtful placement, he is not obligated where there is doubt concerning fulfillment of a positive commandment. It is possible that even where it was certainly placed there intentionally, since it has no identifying mark and the owner cannot be ascertained, the positive commandment no longer rests upon him. I later saw the Derishah, whose language clearly implies as I have written. See there. According to this, the law would be that he may not use it or keep it for himself until we estimate that the owners have learned of the loss and despaired.
