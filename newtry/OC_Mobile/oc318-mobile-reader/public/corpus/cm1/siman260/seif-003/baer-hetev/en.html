The last. Meaning, if the last tenant is a Jew. The earlier Jewish tenants who left certainly searched and checked and removed from the house everything belonging to them when they departed. So writes the Sema. The Shach writes that if the last tenant is a gentile, the item belongs to the finder, as the Tur writes and as is evident from the Gemara according to Rashi and the Rosh. He further writes that this refers to a case where the owner of the house does not live with them, for otherwise it belongs to the owner, who is the last occupant, as Tosafot write. This applies specifically to an item without an identifying sign. An item with an identifying sign must be taken and announced, unless it has accumulated rust. So states the Hagahot Ashri in the name of the Or Zarua. It appears that if the owner rented the house to others while continuing to live there with them, he takes and announces an item bearing an identifying sign even if it has accumulated rust. To three. This is the Gemara's wording. The Tur's wording is: 'If two gentiles and one Jew lived there together...' He follows the Rosh's approach, and this is also the intent of Tosafot. It is possible that this is likewise the Rambam's and Mechaber's view, and when they wrote 'three gentiles' they did not mean precisely three. Alternatively, they understood the Gemara as referring to a case where the Jew did not live with them, in which case we would have to say that three is precise, though there is no apparent reason for that distinction. Thus writes the Sema. See the Shach, who disputes him on this point and writes that this certainly cannot be correct. See there.
