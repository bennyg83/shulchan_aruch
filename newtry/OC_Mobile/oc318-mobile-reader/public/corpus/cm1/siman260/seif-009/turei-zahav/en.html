(Seif 10, tied by their wings and hopping.) The Sema appears to explain “hopping” as referring to fledglings that cannot yet fly and only hop. This is astonishing. Why would the Gemara need to establish the case specifically that way? It needs only to exclude the location from serving as an identifying mark, and if the birds can fly, the explanation is even easier. It also appears as a matter of law that even if a dovecote lies within fifty cubits and the fledgling cannot fly but only hops, we do not attribute it to that dovecote. Here we see that its wings are tied and that some person prepared it for himself. It therefore could not have come directly from the dovecote. The only uncertainty is whether someone placed it there or whether it was lost by another person and reached the place behind the fence through hopping and movement. Since it bears no identifying mark, the owner despairs. By contrast, in seif 8, where the fledglings are not tied, we certainly attribute them to the dovecote. The word “hopping” here teaches only that they were not fixed and lying in a single spot, for then the location would serve as an identifying mark, as the text later says, “and likewise if he found them fixed in place.” This appears correct and clear.
