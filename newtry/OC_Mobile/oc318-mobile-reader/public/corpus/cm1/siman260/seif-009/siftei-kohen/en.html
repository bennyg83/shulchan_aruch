He acquires it and need not return it, etc. The Raavad and Tur object that it should remain deposited until Eliyahu comes, as Rama rules below in seif 10. The Maggid Mishna writes that Ramban and Rashba agree. Their reason is that this is despair without knowledge. In my view, there is no objection to the Rambam. The plain wording of the Gemara, 'he does not return it,' implies that it is his; otherwise it should have said explicitly that it remains until Eliyahu comes. Although despair without knowledge is ineffective, he need not leave the object untouched forever. Since he cannot return it, he may use it as he wishes until the owner becomes known. The rule that despair without knowledge is ineffective means that if the owner is later identified, he must return it; we do not say that subsequent despair vested title in the finder. This may also be the intent of the Beit Yosef and Kesef Mishna, though their wording is somewhat unclear. The Sema's explanation that the Rambam speaks only where the finder took it intending to return it is unsupported and legally unpersuasive. The Bach's explanation, that such money has no definite owner, is also difficult, for it certainly has an owner, merely an unidentified one. The correct explanation is as stated. Migdal Oz's discussion is confused and requires no response.
