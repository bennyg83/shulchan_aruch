One who finds an object, whether or not it bears a distinguishing mark, may not touch it if it appears deliberately placed, because its owner may have left it there intending to return. If it has no mark, taking it would cause the owner's property to be lost, since the finder cannot identify the owner for return. If it has a mark, taking it needlessly forces the owner to search and provide identifying marks. He therefore may not touch it unless it appears lost by falling. Even when uncertain whether it was lost or placed, he must leave it. If he transgressed and took it, he may not return it to that place. If it has no mark, he acquires it and need not return it. Any object bearing a mark, whether doubtfully placed or lost by falling, and whether found in private or public property, must be announced.
