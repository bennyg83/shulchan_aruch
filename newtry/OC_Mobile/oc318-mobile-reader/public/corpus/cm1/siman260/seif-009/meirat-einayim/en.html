And what he writes, "whether it is doubtfully placed," etc. The Rambam does not use the word "doubt." He writes, "whether in a manner of placement." The Mechaber deliberately writes "doubtful placement" because when it is certainly placed in a guarded location, we rule that even if one picked it up, so long as he did not carry it home he returns it to the place from which he took it, even when it has no identifying sign. If the Rambam's text reads simply "in a manner of placement," it can be reconciled by saying that he speaks of a place only partially guarded, though witnesses know it was intentionally placed there. Even then he says that one takes it and announces it, because we assume the owner intended to return and retrieve it after a short time but forgot. Since the place is not fully guarded, the owner presumably prefers that a proper Jew take it and announce it before a gentile or dishonest Jew takes it for himself.
