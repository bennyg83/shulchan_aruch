Its finder. This is the wording of the Tur. The Mechaber copied the Rambam, who did not write this qualification. It appears that the Rambam follows his own view regarding a cow grazing among vineyards belonging to non-Jews after its owner was warned, where he likewise says that it is intentionally abandoned property. Although one is not obligated to attend to it, he still may not take it for himself, as explained in the following siman. The reason, as I wrote there, is that although the owner of the cow was careless with his property, another person still may not appropriate it. The same applies here. The Rambam therefore did not distinguish this case and included it within the law of refuse that is not ordinarily cleared away, where one may not touch the object. According to this, it is somewhat difficult that the Rama did not introduce this gloss with 'some say.' So writes the Sema. The Shach writes that this is not compelling. The Rambam may simply have abbreviated the matter and recorded only what the Gemara states according to the actual conclusion, while in law he may agree with the Tur. Although in siman 261 seif 4 the Rambam does not hold this way, there is no necessary implication from his wording here, and the Rama therefore copied the Tur's words according to their plain meaning, as he often does. Alternatively, one can distinguish between intentionally abandoned property here and there, as one is in any event forced to distinguish according to the Rambam's position there. As a matter of practical law, the Tur and Rama are clearly primary. Shach.
