When is this said, with refuse, etc., until "it is an intentional loss and belongs to the finder." This is the Tur's language. The Mechaber copied the Rambam, who omits the conclusion that it belongs to the finder. It appears the Rambam follows his own view concerning a cow grazing among gentile vineyards after warning the owner. He calls that an intentional loss in the sense that the finder is not obligated to tend to it, but does not allow another person simply to take it for himself. Even though the owner failed to guard his property, that does not automatically give it to another. The same applies here. Therefore the Rambam leaves it within the general rule of refuse not intended to be cleared and says not to touch it. According to this, Rama should have introduced the Tur's conclusion as "some say," rather than blending the two views as though they were undisputed. He does so correctly in the following siman.
