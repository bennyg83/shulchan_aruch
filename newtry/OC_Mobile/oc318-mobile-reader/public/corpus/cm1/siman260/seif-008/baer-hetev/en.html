Its finder. Since we cannot attribute the fledgling's arrival there to its having wandered from a nearby dovecote, we say that travelers passed there and it fell from one of them, and most travelers and passersby are non-Jews. So writes the Sema. The Tosafot Yom Tov, in the chapter Lo Yachpor, questions this, because the Mishnah in Elu Metziot proves that fledglings have no identifying mark; accordingly, even if most travelers were Jews, it should be permitted. In my view this is no difficulty, for who says that the finder found them after the owner had despaired? Perhaps the owner still did not know that they had fallen, in which case this is despair without knowledge, which is not valid despair. Tosafot in Lo Yachpor, Bava Batra 23b, also appears at first glance to agree with the Sema. However, the Ramban in his novellae there, in the name of Rabbeinu Tam, and the Nimukei Yosef write like the Tosafot Yom Tov, and so does the Ir Shushan. We must therefore say that the owner becomes aware immediately when fledglings fall, making this comparable to the cases in siman 262 seif 6. Shach.
