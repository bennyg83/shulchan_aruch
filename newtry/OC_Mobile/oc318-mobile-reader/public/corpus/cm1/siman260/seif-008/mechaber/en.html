A fledgling that hops about but cannot fly, found within fifty cubits of a dovecote, belongs to the dovecote's owner. Beyond fifty cubits it belongs to the finder, because such a fledgling does not ordinarily travel farther. On a path through vineyards, however, it belongs to the dovecote's owner even beyond fifty cubits. If found between two dovecotes, it belongs to the nearer one; if equidistant, they divide it. This applies when the two dovecotes contain equal numbers of pigeons. If one contains more, follow the majority even though it is farther away. All this concerns fledglings unable to fly; those able to fly belong to the finder in every case.
