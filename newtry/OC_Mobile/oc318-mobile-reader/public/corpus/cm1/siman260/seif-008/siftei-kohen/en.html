It belongs to its finder, etc. The Sema explains that since the fledglings cannot be attributed to having strayed from a dovecote, we assume they fell from travelers, and most travelers are non Jews. Tosafot Yom Tov questions this because fledglings have no identifying mark, so even if most travelers were Jews the finder could keep them. This is not conclusive, because perhaps the owner had not yet learned of the loss, making it despair without knowledge, which is ineffective. Tosafot in Bava Batra 23b appears to support the Sema. Ramban, citing Rabbeinu Chananel, the Nimukei Yosef, and the Ir Shushan, however, rule like Tosafot Yom Tov. They must hold that one ordinarily notices when fledglings fall, comparable to the cases below in siman 262, seifim 6 through 9.
