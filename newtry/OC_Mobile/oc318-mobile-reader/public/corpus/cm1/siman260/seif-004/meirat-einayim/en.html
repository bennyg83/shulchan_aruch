If one of them acquired a found object, etc. The Tur says "one of them." At first glance the same law should apply to another person who enters and acquires the found object there, because the reason in the Rosh's responsum applies equally. The Tur may have stated the ordinary case because outsiders do not commonly enter and acquire things there, and because the actual incident concerned one of the tenants. The matter is not entirely conclusive.
The other has no right in it. The Rosh explains that this is not ownerless property in a form acquired automatically by the courtyard. If the original owner despaired, then according to the Sages who hold that ordinary theft produces despair, the thief already acquired it. If ordinary theft does not produce despair, the property remains the original owner's. Either way, it is not ownerless. Even if it were ownerless, so long as it remained in the thief's hand the courtyard would not acquire it until it came to rest on the ground.
