The Shach further writes that, in his opinion, the principal ruling is that the entire find belongs to the landlord even though the tenant alone resides in the premises. See Responsa Rabbi Akiva Eiger 133, some of which is cited below in siman 313:3:1; Responsa VeShav HaKohen 47; and Responsa Beit Ephraim, Choshen Mishpat 44. The latter discusses Reuven, who rented his house to Shimon, and Shimon found there a treasure that had plainly lain there for many years, so that its owners had certainly despaired of recovering it. Reuven claimed that his courtyard had acquired it for him even though it was rented to Shimon. Beit Ephraim concludes that in such a case the burden of proof rests upon the party seeking to extract the property from the one presently holding it.
