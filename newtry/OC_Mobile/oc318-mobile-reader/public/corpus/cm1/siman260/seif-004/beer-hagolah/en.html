K. K. S.C. on behalf of the Rashi answer:
Because rental for a day is considered a sale. See siman 313, seif 3, where the ruling follows Rambam that the landlord acquires it.
He explains there that this is not ownerless property such that we could say the courtyard acquires it. He further writes that even if it were ownerless, so long as it remains in the thief's hand the courtyard does not acquire it until it comes to rest on the ground. The Sema writes that for this reason, if another person entering there acquires it, one can say that he has validly acquired it. This requires study.
