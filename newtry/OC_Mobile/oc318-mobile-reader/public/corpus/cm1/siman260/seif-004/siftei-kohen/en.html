Both of them acquire them, etc. From Tosafos at the beginning of Bava Metzia, page 11a, it appears explicitly that the entire find belongs to Shimon, the lessor. The Agudah in Perek HaShoel indicates likewise.
For they are like two partners, etc. The Bach rules this way. In my humble opinion, however, the primary ruling is that it all belongs to the lessor even though the lessee alone lives there, as stated below in siman 313, seif 3, and as I explain there.
A found object from a thief, etc. Ramban writes in Perek Eilu Metziot that if a thief entered Reuven's house intending to transfer a found object to him, but Shimon was sitting there and the thief mistakenly thought Shimon was Reuven, the homeowner, and sold the object to him, Reuven's house acquires it for Reuven whether Reuven is then at home or not. This is so even if many people pass through the house and it is not guarded, because when another person's intent is to transfer ownership, a guarded courtyard is unnecessary. Here too the thief entered in order to transfer it to Reuven. Shimon must therefore return it to him. See there, and see below, siman 268, seif 3, in the Rama.
