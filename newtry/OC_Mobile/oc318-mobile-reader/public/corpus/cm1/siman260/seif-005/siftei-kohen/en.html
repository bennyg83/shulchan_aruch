Rama does not follow the Rosh here, but the Rambam, as does the Semag, who rules that only in a non Jewish majority may the finder keep it. Rama therefore first adds 'even if it has an identifying mark,' teaching that the case of a shop likewise concerns an item with a mark and a non Jewish majority, like the money changer in the latter clause. This is the principal ruling. Rashi, Bartenura, Tosafot, the Nimukei Yosef, the Raavad, and the Maggid Mishna likewise support it. The Tur's statement that Rabbeinu Yitzchak disagrees with Rashi is difficult, for Tosafot, who generally represent Rabbeinu Yitzchak, appear to disagree only over the textual reading, while conceding the law. The Rosh's reasoning can be rejected: where many people enter and leave, the owner does not despair in a Jewish majority, because he assumes that another person may have found it and will return it upon identification. The Maggid Mishna records the view of Tosafot and Raavad in accord with the Rambam, and calls the Rashba's contrary explanation forced.
