[Sema, letter 2] Rather, it is completely ownerless. Note: In my humble opinion, this is an isolated view, for it appears that most authorities disagree. This is evident from the fact that they required other explanations: that an object hidden away is lost from him and from everyone; that in a wall, from its midpoint outward, the area is an unguarded courtyard; and similarly in a shop or at a moneychanger's table. Alternatively, the reason is that it is not destined to be found, not because it never occurs to the owner that his courtyard should acquire it, since he does not ordinarily come to that place. See below, siman 268, in the gloss. Or, with respect to a shop and a moneychanger, the reason is that it came into his possession in a prohibited manner. This implies that none of them accept Rabbeinu Baruch's reasoning.
