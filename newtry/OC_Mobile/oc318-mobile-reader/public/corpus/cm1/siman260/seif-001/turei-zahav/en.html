(Seif 1, it belongs to the finder.) Rambam writes: “Why does the owner of the courtyard not acquire this hidden treasure by virtue of his courtyard, so that the find should belong to the owner of the house? Because it is unknown to him and to others, and is like a treasure lost from him and from every person.” Tosafot answer that a courtyard does not acquire an article that is not destined to be found, and they say the same regarding lost money. Mordechai records many answers. He also writes in the name of Rabbeinu Baruch that a courtyard acquires only property that is fully ownerless, but not a lost article. Even after the owner despairs, since the courtyard owner does not know of it, it is not considered ownerless as to him. It appears to me that Rabbeinu Baruch did not intend to answer Rambam’s question, for if so he answered nothing. Property left there by the ancient inhabitants is fully ownerless. Rather, Rabbeinu Baruch teaches an independent rule. I am therefore astonished that the Sema cites Rabbeinu Baruch here as an answer to the question.
