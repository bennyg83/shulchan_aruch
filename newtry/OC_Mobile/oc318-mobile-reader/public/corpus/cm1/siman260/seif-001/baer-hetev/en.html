As for why we do not say that the courtyard of the present homeowner acquires it for him, the Mordechai writes in the name of Rabbeinu Baruch that the rule that a person's courtyard acquires for him without his knowledge applies only to complete ownerless property, but not to a lost object, even where there has been despair, because had the owner known where it was, he would not have abandoned it. Rust. This applies where this hole is a guarded place, for otherwise, even if the object had not developed rust it would be considered intentionally abandoned property. So writes the Hagahot Ashri in the name of the Or Zarua. Meaning, even in a place that is entirely unguarded it is intentionally abandoned property and belongs to the finder; regarding a place that is somewhat guarded, see below, seif 10 in the gloss. Shach.
Deep below. The words of the Mechaber require study. The Tur states: 'provided that it developed rust,' whereas he writes that the Rambam requires that 'he find it deep below.' It appears that the Rambam interprets the Gemara's phrase 'very rusted' to mean that it is found deep below, as is also explicit from the Maggid Mishneh. Otherwise, from where did the Rambam derive this requirement, and why did he omit the requirement that it be very rusted? The Tur therefore records the Rambam's words because it is possible that Rashi's explanation and the Rambam's explanation are both legally correct: whether it developed rust or was found deep below in a manner indicating that it had been there a long time, it belongs to the finder. But the Mechaber, who requires both, is difficult, for why are both needed? Perhaps he was uncertain about the interpretation of the Gemara and therefore ruled stringently that both are required. Shach.
The old ones. One may say that when the object was placed there, it was put into a hole in the wall or within a handbreadth of the ground; afterward the wall sank and the ground around the wall was also raised, as commonly occurs, and therefore the object became buried so deeply beneath the ground. So writes the Sema. The Shach writes that his words imply that he means the object is now situated in the portion of the wall below ground level. This is also explicit in the Rambam's Commentary to the Mishnah: 'When they said that one who finds something in a ruin or an old wall may keep it, this is only when that object is within the ground in a manner that allows one to say that it was hidden there a long time ago. But if he found it near the ground surface or near the face of the wall, its law is like that of an object found in a refuse heap.'
