Provided that he finds it very deep, etc. The Mechaber's words require study. The Tur writes that the item must have accumulated rust, and then cites the Rambam as requiring that it be found very deep. It appears that the Rambam interprets the Gemara's phrase 'greatly rusted' as meaning that it was found very deep. This is explicit in the Maggid Mishna; otherwise, what source would the Rambam have for this requirement, and why would he omit the requirement of extensive rust? The Tur therefore cites the Rambam separately because it is possible that Rashi's and the Rambam's explanations are both legally correct. Whether it accumulated rust or was found very deep, if the circumstances show that it has been there for a long time, it belongs to the finder. The Mechaber, however, requires both conditions. Why are both necessary? Perhaps he was uncertain how to interpret the Gemara and therefore ruled stringently that both are required.
"As a way of all the meteors," he says, "that is, in his words, that he shall speak to him, and that he shall speak to him, and that he shall be the same in the ground, and that he may say that he has long been subjected to the land, but that he may have been exposed to the ground."

From its midpoint outward, etc. The Yerushalmi teaches: If one found an item between the doorposts, from the inner edge inward it is treated as inside, and from the inner edge outward it is treated as outside. If the opening is exposed inward, even an item found from the midpoint outward belongs to the homeowner; if exposed outward, even an item found from the midpoint inward belongs to the finder. The Tosefta states the same rule. 'Exposed inward' means that the hole opens and is visible from inside while sealed on the outside. Therefore, even what lies beyond the midpoint belongs to the homeowner, since it certainly came from within, and conversely in the opposite case. The authorities did not cite this because it is self evident. The Sema writes that the rule applies even if the item was found very deep, but it appears that his text should read: even if it was not found very deep. Still, his source is difficult. On the contrary, Tosafot, the Rosh, and the Tur require extensive rust here because otherwise it is certainly, or at least possibly, an intentional placement and one may not touch it; and if it bears an identifying mark, one must take it and proclaim it. They therefore explain that this case too concerns an item heavily rusted, as in the first clause concerning an old wall. Since the Rambam interprets heavy rust as being found very deep, he must interpret the latter clause likewise, unless he has another answer to their questions. The Maggid Mishna records answers that the circumstances show the objects have lain there for many days, or that a hole open to the public domain is like a lost object found in a city whose majority are non Jews. The Rambam states the law generally according to the plain sense of the passage. If so, no rust is required. The case must therefore concern a Jewish majority, and according to the Rambam and Mechaber the object must be found very deep. The Sema apparently understood that in a new wall it is uncommon to find the object very deep, and that if it were on the ground the phrase 'from its midpoint outward' would not fit. The Mechaber's requirement in an old wall that the object be deep and rusted applies so that even what lies inward of the midpoint belongs to the finder, because there he was uncertain. For what lies outward of the midpoint, he evidently holds that rust alone suffices. This is the principal explanation of the Mechaber's view.
