Seif 10. He found tied fledglings, etc. Note: See Shach, seif katan 28, and Netivot HaMishpat's challenge. In my opinion the Shach's intent can be explained as follows. If the issue were merely that the location itself might be an identifying mark, the Gemara could have established the case as fledglings that are capable of hopping or flying, where location would not be a mark. Although Rama above rules that such fledglings belong to the finder, that is only where there is no reason to suspect deliberate placement. Near a wall or branch, where people do place them, the case remains a doubtful placement and one should not take them. One might object that free-moving fledglings are deliberately abandoned, but according to Rambam and the Mechaber in siman 261 even property deliberately abandoned should not initially be taken. Thus the Gemara could still have used that case. This, I believe, is the Shach's intent.
