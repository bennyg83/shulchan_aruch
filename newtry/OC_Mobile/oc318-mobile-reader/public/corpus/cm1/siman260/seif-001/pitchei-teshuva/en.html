See also Shach, who writes that in his opinion the principal ruling is that it belongs entirely to the landlord even though the tenant alone lives there. See Responsa Rabbi Akiva Eiger, siman 133, on this matter, partially cited below in siman 313, seif 3, seif katan 1. See also Responsa VeShav HaKohen, siman 47, and Beit Ephraim, Choshen Mishpat, siman 44, who discusses at length a case in which Reuven rented his house to Shimon and Shimon found there a treasure that had clearly lain there for many years, so that its owners had certainly despaired of recovering it. Reuven claimed that his courtyard acquired it for him even though it was rented to Shimon. He concludes that in such a case the burden of proof rests on the one seeking to extract from the other.
