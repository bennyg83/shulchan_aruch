This division was made by document, etc. Meaning, they claim that they purchased the field jointly from the protester and had a deed from him, which was lost. The deed of division between themselves, providing that one would consume the produce in one year and the other in the next, remains before beit din. They establish chazakah immediately after the first three years have elapsed. See Sema.
A deed of sale. Meaning, the deed is presently in their possession before beit din, but it is not established whether the intermediate seller originally acquired the land from the protester or came into possession through robbery and then sold it to them. Nevertheless, their chazakah becomes effective immediately, because the fact that this person sold it to them by a formal deed created publicity, and the protester should have protested. However, it must be known that the intermediate seller lived in the property for at least one day. The Tur writes this, and the law is explained in siman 146. See Sema, and see below for further discussion.
