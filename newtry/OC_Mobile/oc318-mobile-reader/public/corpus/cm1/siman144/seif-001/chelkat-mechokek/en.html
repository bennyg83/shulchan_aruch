A responsum from my father and teacher, the author, to the eminent scholar Rabbi Hirsch Segal, known as Rabbi Hirsch son of Rabbi Meir of the holy community of Vilna. You asked about the Rama's ruling in Even HaEzer 144 that captivity is considered a common type of unavoidable circumstance. The marginal note says that this was copied from Mordechai in chapter Mi SheAchazo, yet Mordechai there says the opposite, that captivity is an uncommon circumstance. There is also a clear proof from the beginning of chapter HaShoel, Bava Metzia 94, where captivity is described as an occurrence a person does not ordinarily contemplate. Moreover, Rama appears to contradict himself, because in Choshen Mishpat 115:48 he rules that one seized on the road suffered an uncommon accident, based on Agudah in chapter Mi SheAchazo. The answer is that the Rama's rulings are correct. The authorities rule according to the first version in Ketubot 2b: in monetary law there is a claim of unavoidable circumstance even for an uncommon event, but with gittin we do not recognize an unavoidable-circumstance claim even where the event is uncommon, unless it is virtually unheard of, such as being eaten by a lion or bitten by a snake. Captivity or imprisonment, although uncommon, still occurs often enough that the Sages were concerned for problems of modesty and promiscuity, as explained in the Gemara. I also saw in Hagahot HaRif the language of Ri'az: whether he became ill or was imprisoned, etc., he cannot cancel his get by claiming unavoidable circumstances. Therefore, Rama correctly ruled in monetary law that seizure and captivity are uncommon, consistent with the plain Gemara in HaShoel. But regarding gittin, there is no claim of unavoidable circumstances even for an uncommon event so long as it is not comparable to being eaten by a lion. This is exactly the position of Hagahot HaRif. All the more so according to those who distinguish that being eaten by a lion is an act of Heaven, where an unavoidable-circumstance claim may apply, whereas captivity is caused by human beings and therefore does not. This is sufficient for a scholar such as yourself.
