And the son inherited it, etc. Sema writes that between father and son no deed is needed because everyone knows the son comes through inheritance. The challenger cannot plausibly say he assumed the father was a trespasser. But where a purchaser buys from the son, Mechaber requires a deed. Maggid Mishneh cites Ramban and Raavad that even if the son possessed as a minor, the possession can count because it began with his adult father and the son comes through him. Some authorities require that the son too be an adult. In the later case where possession is partly before the father and partly before the son, everyone agrees the son must be adult at that time because one does not establish possession against a minor's property. See Tumim. I explained that Beit Yosef's presentation of a Tosafot versus Rashbam dispute on whether the son had to be adult during the father's possession is inaccurate; they did not disagree on that point.
Provided he bought by document. See Tumim. Tur writes that where the purchaser bought this field together with other fields, even if this field was specifically mentioned, the sale may lack sufficient public notice. I considered the reverse case: the possessor bought from the son all the son's fields including this one, the field was expressly identified, and the possessor then ate the third year. Can the challenger say, 'Because such a bundled purchase lacked publicity, I did not know or care to protest'? I initially thought this might depend on the two opinions in seif 4 whether a deed is required. After analysis I concluded that according to all views the possession is effective. See there for the reasoning and for my resolution of why Tur follows the view that a bundled sale lacks sufficient publicity.
