The son inherited it and consumed its produce, etc. With a father and son no deed is required, because everyone knows that the son comes by virtue of inheritance from his father. The claimant therefore has no excuse to say, 'I thought he was eating it by theft.' This differs from a purchaser who bought it from the son, and therefore the Mechaber concludes specifically regarding the purchaser, 'provided that he purchased it by deed.' Maggid Mishneh, chapter 13 of Hilchot To'en, writes in the name of Ramban and Rashba that even if the son established possession while still a minor, the chazakah is effective, because his father began the possession while an adult and the son comes through his father. He also writes in chapter 12 of Hilchot To'en that this appears from Rambam. Some commentators cited in chapter 13, however, require the son also to be an adult. In the next clause, where the possession was established in the father's presence and afterward in the son's presence, all agree that the son must then be an adult, because one cannot establish chazakah against a minor's property. Rashbam, moreover, requires the son to have been an adult even when the initial possession began during the father's lifetime. See there and Hagahot Darkei Moshe.
