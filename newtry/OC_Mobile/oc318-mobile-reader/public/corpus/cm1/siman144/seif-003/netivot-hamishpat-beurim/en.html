Provided that he purchased by document. Tumim is uncertain whether, if the son sold all of his fields without specifying them individually, the purchaser can combine the periods for chazakah, since an unspecified sale has no publicity, as explained in seif 4. In my view it is clear that chazakah is effective. The purchaser sits in the field and possesses it in the same manner as his seller, and he bought all of the seller's fields by document, which creates publicity. This is exactly analogous to an heir. An heir likewise cannot inherit something that did not belong to his father, yet if the decedent possessed the field for one year and the heir for two, the periods combine to establish chazakah. The reason, as Sema writes in seif katan 3, is that everyone knows the son comes by virtue of his father's inheritance, so the challenger has no excuse for failing to protest. The same is true here. Since it is publicly known that the purchaser bought all the seller's fields, everyone knows that he comes by virtue of that seller, and the challenger has no excuse. Indeed, the very source from which Tumim sought a proof is a clear proof for this conclusion. In Bava Batra 41 the Gemara places in tension the baraita where the father consumed produce for one year with the baraita where the possession occurred in the father's presence. Tosafot and Ramban ask why the Gemara did not answer simply that one baraita concerns a sale by document and the other a sale without a document. Ramban answers that the two baraitot were taught adjacent to one another, so they must be read consistently. When the Gemara then establishes the baraita concerning possession in the father's presence as a case where one sold all his fields without specifying them, it necessarily follows that the adjacent baraita concerning one year by the father and one year by the purchaser also concerns an unspecified sale of all the fields. Nevertheless, the purchaser's possession combines to establish chazakah. This proves the rule stated above.
