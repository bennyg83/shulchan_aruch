You have no greater protest than this. The Tur writes that the possessor should therefore preserve his deed forever. Meaning, the son's sale is stronger than an ordinary protest. With an ordinary protest, if the protester remains silent for three years afterward, it appears that he retracted the protest, as the Tur and Mechaber write in siman 146, seif 5. Here, however, once he sold the property to another person, he cannot simply retract. Even if he later realizes that his protest was legally mistaken and that the field truly belonged to the possessor, his change of position cannot prejudice the purchaser. So writes the Rosh. Although the Mechaber does not expressly write the word 'forever' here, he does use that wording in siman 146. See the Sema, seif katan 4, who discusses this at length. See Tumim, where I wrote that the Sema's explanation does not appear correct and offered a sound reason to distinguish between the present case and siman 146. The Mechaber therefore properly omitted the word 'forever' here while writing it there. See Tumim for the reasoning.
