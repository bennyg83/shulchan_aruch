And he sold at the bar and did not pay tribute to the DDT protest in which he protested:
He sold it by deed. If the challenger consecrated it, chazakah is defeated even without a document, because the years of possession then elapsed while the person entitled to protest was someone unable to protest. Sema. See Beurim: if he sold it orally and the purchaser protested, the chazakah is defeated. This differs from a secret sale in siman 146:4, where the sale was by private handwriting and could not have become known even through investigation, so the possessor can say, "I disregarded your protest because I did not know of the sale." But a sale before witnesses, though not publicly announced, can be learned through inquiry. Once the purchaser protests, the possessor should investigate. The same would apply if he sold to someone in a place from which protest could not be heard. The absence of the original challenger's protest then proves nothing, just as with consecrated property. Chazakah fundamentally depends on the original owner's failure to object, and if he was unable to protest there is no chazakah even though there is no defect in the possessor's own conduct.
Without a deed. A purchaser who wishes to establish chazakah needs a deed because the challenger is not expected to investigate so extensively. The possessor, however, is expected to investigate. Sema.
