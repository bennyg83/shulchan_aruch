Even if he gave it to him against his will, etc. See above siman 120 in the Tur and Mechaber concerning repayment of a loan against the creditor's will, where there are several distinctions.
But if he told him to bring it to him, etc. Meaning, the custodian says to the depositor, "Wait here and I will bring you your deposit." The depositor does not wait, and afterward the deposit suffers an unavoidable accident in the custodian's hand. The custodian nevertheless remains liable. Mordechai and Darkei Moshe state this. It is unlike the Ir Shushan, who understood the words to mean that the depositor told the custodian, "Bring it to me," and then left. That is not the source's case. Rama concludes, "and the depositor went away," showing that the earlier instruction came from the custodian. Moreover, the law would not make sense according to the Ir Shushan. Here the deposit was not given for a fixed term. Once the depositor reveals that he wants the deposit returned and says, "Bring me my deposit," the custodian should be no worse than an agent appointed to receive it. If an unavoidable accident then occurs in the receiving agent's hand, the responsibility is upon the depositor.
