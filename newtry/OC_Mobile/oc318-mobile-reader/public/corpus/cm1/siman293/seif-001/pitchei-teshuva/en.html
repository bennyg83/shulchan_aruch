But if he said. Beer HaGolah, following Sema, explains that the custodian said to the depositor, "I will bring you the deposit." This follows the Mordechai and Darchei Moshe, unlike Levush, who understands that the depositor told the custodian to bring it. Sema also argues from the law itself: here the deposit was not for a fixed term, and once the depositor has shown that he wants it returned, the situation changes. Ketzot HaChoshen objects from the law of one who sends a borrowed item back through the borrower's slave. Even with the lender's consent the borrower remains liable because the slave is legally an extension of his master, and all the more so where the item remains literally in the borrower's domain. Responsa Rabbi Moshe Rothenburg, Orach Chaim, siman 11, defends Sema. There the borrower accepted custody for a fixed term and remains obligated until that term ends and the item is actually returned. Here the custodianship is not fixed in that way. See also below, siman 342, seif katan 1.
To compel him to accept it. Responsa Gilya Masechta, siman 13, writes that only the custodian cannot force the depositor to accept the deposit before the agreed term. The depositor, however, may demand his deposit even during the term. Sema above, siman 292, seif katan 16, likewise implies this; see Baer Heitev there.
