Where the owner of the deposit was accustomed to deposit with that person. Ran writes that if the owner normally entrusts the second custodian, or specifically sent a trustworthy person to receive the deposit, the first custodian may transfer it and may not afterward retract the transfer. Yet the Shulchan Aruch here still says that the first remains liable for responsibility. This is difficult according to Rambam and Beit Yosef, who hold that when the first custodian transfers to a person whom the owner customarily trusts, the first is discharged even if the second later acts negligently. Maggid Mishneh states this expressly: because the owner has already shown trust in the second custodian, the first cannot say he wants the property back and has already been released from responsibility. The same should apply where the owner sends a person whom he identifies as trustworthy. Tosafot compares that case with one in which the owner regularly deposits with the second person. Shach attempted to distinguish by saying that when the owner merely sends a trustworthy person, he has not necessarily appointed that person his agent or accepted him as custodian. But Ran and the Shulchan Aruch expressly say that because the transfer is beneficial, the first cannot retract after giving it to that person. If the owner could still say, "I do not want my deposit in his hands," there would be no clear benefit and no basis to prevent retraction. One might say that the first remains liable only for special road risks if he returns the deposit in a dangerous place. But the wording "liable for responsibility" is general, and if the place were truly dangerous the transfer would not be beneficial in the first place. Thus the ruling is difficult to reconcile with Beit Yosef's own position that transfer to a person whom the owner customarily trusts fully discharges the first custodian. The matter requires study.
