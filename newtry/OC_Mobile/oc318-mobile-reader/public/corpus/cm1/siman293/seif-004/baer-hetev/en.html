Take. Meaning, although with a gift and other matters the expression "take" is not equivalent to "acquire on his behalf," as the Tur and Shulchan Aruch write in simanim 125 and 243, with a loan or deposit "take" is equivalent to language of acquisition. The reason is that the money already belongs to the lender or depositor; therefore, with any language by which the borrower or custodian tells a third party to take possession of the money delivered to him, it leaves their domain completely and that third party acquires it for the depositor or lender. Thus, where the custodian had been established as a denier, he may no longer retract; and regarding a loan he may not retract even if he had not been established as a denier, as stated at the beginning of siman 125. Sema.
I say. And for the sake of C.C. as such in raids and negotiations:
Accustomed. For certainly the depositor is satisfied that the deposit should now remain with this person as well. The Sema writes that at first glance this should apply only if the depositor was not accustomed to deposit with the original custodian, for otherwise why should one be preferable to the other? In truth, however, this is not so. Even if the depositor was also accustomed to deposit with the original custodian, once the sender cannot now say that the depositor does not want his deposit in another's hands, he may not retract. This follows from the Gemara and poskim. A further proof is that in the case of a loan he may never retract, even though the one is no better than the other. Shach.
He sent. Since the depositor sent through him, this indicates that he is content for his deposit to be sent by that person, and delivery into that person's hand is therefore a benefit to him. Consequently, the custodian may not retract. Nevertheless, because the depositor did not appoint that person as his agent, responsibility remains upon the custodian. Sema.
