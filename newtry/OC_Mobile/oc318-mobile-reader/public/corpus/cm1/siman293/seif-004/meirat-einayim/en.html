Whether he said "Take it and bring it," etc. Although regarding gifts and other matters "bring it" does not ordinarily mean "acquire it," as explained in simanim 125 and 243, with a loan or deposit it does. The money already belongs to the creditor or depositor. Therefore even a minimal expression by the borrower or custodian when he gives it to a messenger removes it from his own control and gives the recipient rights on behalf of the owner. If the original custodian is a known denier, he cannot retract the delivery. With a loan the rule applies even where he is not known as a denier, as explained at the beginning of siman 125.
Only that he was accustomed, etc. Since the depositor was accustomed to entrust property to this person, he presumably is pleased that it remain with him now as well. At first glance this should apply specifically where the present custodian was not himself one with whom the depositor regularly deposited. Otherwise, why is the other person's status stronger than his?
Likewise, if he sent to him, etc. By sending for the deposit he reveals that he wants the property sent into his possession, and delivery to that person is therefore a benefit for him. Consequently the custodian cannot retract after delivering it. Nevertheless, because the recipient was not formally appointed as the depositor's agent, responsibility remains on the original custodian until actual receipt.
