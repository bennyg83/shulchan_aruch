Rather, he should say to him, "It is at your risk," etc. This is the Rambam's language. The Tur writes that if the custodian accepts responsibility for transport, the depositor must accept the deposit even in the wilderness. The Rambam certainly agrees. In that case the wilderness becomes for him like an inhabited place. The Beit Yosef also indicates this.
He told him, "I want to depart," etc., he may return it, etc. In the Perishah I explained why the Tur and Mechaber here use a formulation different from siman 74:1 regarding a loan. There, the Tur and Rama speak of the borrower saying, "I wish to depart into the wilderness," because ordinarily one borrows money for a long journey. Here, with a deposit, one who plans a distant trip ordinarily leaves his possessions with people remaining in the city. Therefore the case begins with the depositor saying that he wishes to depart. If the custodian responds that he too wishes to leave, that is effectively a notice that he wants the deposit returned. Thus when the ruling says the custodian may return it, the depositor must receive it. Otherwise the statement would have no practical significance.
