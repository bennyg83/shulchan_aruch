From the sharecropper. Tosafot to Bava Metzia 22a, the Hagahot Ashri, and the Gemara there imply that if a person gives his fellow food belonging not to himself but to another, it is forbidden to eat it even if he knows that the owner will consent. We rule like Abaye that despair without knowledge is not legally effective: although the owner is pleased now, he was not pleased initially. Were I not afraid to say so, I would argue that here it is permitted. Despair without knowledge is different, because even later the owner does not consciously despair; he merely does not know where the object is, and is compelled to despair. We therefore say that it originally came into the finder's possession unlawfully. By what can he acquire it? Not through despair, for the owner is not presently despairing, and if he knew the object was with this person he would not despair. Here, however, since it is known that the owner will consent, the item presently comes into his possession permissibly, because presumably the owner does not object. Although the Gemara there compares terumah and impurity to despair without knowledge, there is a different reason in those cases: actual knowledge is required for terumah and impurity. See there, Sefer Imrei Binah, page 110, and Responsa Maharit, siman 150. Shach.
