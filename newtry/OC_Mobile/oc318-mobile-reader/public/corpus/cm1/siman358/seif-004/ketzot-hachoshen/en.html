It is permitted to purchase from the sharecropper. Tosafos in chapter Eilu Metziot, Bava Metzia 22a, write that if a person gives his fellow food belonging to another, even where it is known that the owner will certainly be pleased once he learns of it, the recipient may not eat it. We rule like Abaye that despair without knowledge is not valid despair. Although the owner will be pleased later, he was not presently pleased because he did not know. Hagahot Ashri and Mordechai write similarly. This appears difficult from Ketubot 48a. If a person becomes mentally incapacitated, beit din descends to his property and provides food and support for his wife, sons, daughters, and another matter. Ravina asks Rav Ashi why this differs from one who traveled overseas, where beit din supports his wife but not his sons, daughters, or the other matter. Rav Ashi answers that there is a difference between one who left knowingly and one who left without awareness, namely through incapacity. Rashi explains that where he became incapacitated and departed from normal awareness involuntarily, we presume he is pleased that his sons and daughters be supported from his property. Yet if even known future consent is comparable to despair without knowledge and ineffective because he presently does not know, how can his children be supported from his property? Perhaps support of his sons and daughters, even beyond age six, is at least a mitzvah, although coercion is not imposed, and consent connected with a mitzvah is different. Even without present awareness, it can be treated as effective, as Beit Yosef writes in Yoreh Deah, siman 331. See what I wrote in siman 262, seif katan 1. Yet the Gemara there also includes the 'other matter,' meaning ornaments for his wife, because it is assumed he does not want her to become repulsive. Why should that help if he is presently unaware?
