Whatever the shepherd sells, if the householder would notice its absence it is permitted to buy from him, but if not, it is forbidden. The Mechaber abbreviated the Rambam's final wording and thereby created room for error. One might think that the phrase means: if the householder were physically standing there and watching, the shepherd would still be permitted to sell it. That is not the Rambam's intent. The Rambam concludes: if it is something whose absence the householder would notice, it is permitted; if the householder would not notice its absence, it is forbidden. Thus the meaning is that where the item is significant enough that the owner would notice it missing and the shepherd would be exposed as a thief if he sold it without permission, one may assume he is selling with authorization. If the owner would not notice the loss, purchasing from the shepherd is forbidden.
