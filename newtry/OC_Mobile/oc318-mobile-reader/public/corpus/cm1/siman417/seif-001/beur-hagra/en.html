And all of them may waive, etc. Although silence by the public is ordinarily not a waiver, as the Gemara says regarding the public domain, explicit waiver by the seven trustees of the city in the presence of the townspeople is effective, as stated there 23a. Alternatively, the Gemara's phrase "they waived," etc., means that they can waive. See Tosafos there. The statement concerning Rav Ami is because mere silence does not constitute public waiver. The Rashba suggests instead that the public cannot waive at all, and the Gemara's case can be explained because perhaps the structure was originally set back within private property. The Rashbam at the end of chapter 3 likewise explains. He adds that where everyone has become accustomed to the arrangement and no one refrains, collective acquiescence permits it, as in Bava Metzia 12b, where one who does not acquire is treated as though he acquires because the poor are content with the arrangement. Here too everyone benefits because all require such use.
And furthermore, etc. See Sema and siman 369, Sema seif katan 12.
And one who digs a pit, etc. Bava Kamma 20a, as stated in siman 410 seif 7. So too the Tosefta, chapter 6: one who digs a pit with permission and opens it to the public domain, even if it belongs to the public, is liable until he delivers it to the public. The Gemara there recounts that Nechunya did so; see my comments there.
