Into the public domain. The Tur writes: but into an alley he may extend them with the consent of the residents of the alley, unlike the public domain, which has no owners whose consent can be obtained. Rabbeinu Yerucham writes that this refers to a closed alley. If it is open from end to end and the public regularly passes through it, it has the law of the public domain. The Beit Yosef cites this, and it is surprising that the Mechaber omitted it. Thus writes the Sema. See Responsa Maharit, siman 108.
