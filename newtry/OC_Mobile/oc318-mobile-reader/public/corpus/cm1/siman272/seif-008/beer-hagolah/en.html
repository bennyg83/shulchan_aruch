The Kesef Mishneh writes that from what the Gemara says there concerning the Sages and Rabbi Shimon, 'from the words of both we learn that suffering of animals is d'oraisa,' even though the Gemara challenges this at length, the conclusion remains so. The Rif and Geonim likewise explain. Rambam's phrase 'he need not attend to it' means specifically loading, but he must unload because preventing animal suffering is d'oraisa. This is why Rambam writes, 'if not, he must unload and load,' implying that in the first case he is exempt from both only in one respect, namely loading. He relies on what he writes at the end of the chapter, that when one encounters two animals the mitzvah is first to unload because of animal suffering. The Kesef Mishneh further proves at length that one cannot say Rambam holds animal suffering is only rabbinic; rather, he necessarily rules like the Sages that it is d'oraisa.
