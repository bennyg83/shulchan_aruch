The "enemy" mentioned in the Torah is not a non-Jew but a Jew. How can a Jew properly hate another Jew when the verse says, "You shall not hate your brother in your heart"? The Sages explain that it refers to one whom he personally saw committing a transgression, whom he warned, and who did not desist. It is then a mitzvah to hate him until he repents and abandons his wickedness. Nevertheless, even before he repents, if one finds him struggling under his load, it is a mitzvah to help load and unload with him and not leave him in danger, lest he delay there because of concern for his property and come to physical danger. The Torah is concerned for the lives of Jews, whether wicked or righteous, so long as they remain attached to Hashem and believe in the fundamentals of the faith, as it is stated: "Say to them: As I live, says Hashem God, I do not desire the death of the wicked, but that the wicked return from his way and live."
