Likewise if the animal, etc. The general rule is that the mitzvah of unloading for free applies only where both the animal and the load belong to Jews. Therefore Rambam gives the obligation here as arising from the Jew's distress, meaning that payment may be accepted. The Tur, however, challenges this and writes that where the animal belongs to a Jew, one must unload for free because of the mitzvah of unloading. See the Derishah. All this follows the Tur's understanding of Rambam. From Rambam's conclusion, which the Mechaber likewise cites in seif 11, it appears that Rambam understands the mitzvot of unloading and loading as grounded in the Jew's distress. Examine this carefully.
Some say that regarding unloading he is obligated even if the non-Jew is not present. This appears to refer to Rambam's conclusion that if both animal and load belong to a non-Jew, he need attend to it only because of hostility. Hostility exists only where the non-Jew sees the Jew standing there and refusing assistance. Rama therefore writes that he must unload even when the non-Jew is absent, because even without hostility there is a Torah obligation based on animal suffering. According to what I wrote above, however, Rambam himself also holds animal suffering is by Torah law and his ruling there concerns loading rather than unloading, so there is no difficulty.
Likewise, wherever he is exempt from unloading, etc. Meaning, where the animal is habitually crouching or habitually standing, as explained above in seif 1, and similar cases. An elder for whom the task is beneath his dignity is not included in this, as stated above in seif 7.
The practical difference is that he may accept payment. The Rosh and Tur likewise use this wording. In the Perishah I explained that they carefully say "he may accept payment" to exclude a task he is obligated to perform specifically as a mitzvah, such as unloading, judging, teaching Torah, and the like, for which it is prohibited to take payment even if the other person offers. I also wrote that even in the present case he may not condition the unloading on payment by saying, "If you do not pay me, I will not unload." Even if the owner refuses payment, he must unload because of animal suffering.
