Likewise, if the animal belongs to a Jew and the load belongs to a non-Jew, one must unload and reload because of concern for the Jew's animal. But when both the animal and the load belong to a non-Jew, one is not obligated to attend to it except because of possible hostility. Rama: Some say that unloading is obligatory even when the non-Jew is absent, because preventing animal suffering is a Torah obligation. Accordingly, whenever one is otherwise exempt from unloading, one is nevertheless obligated because of tza'ar ba'alei chaim. The practical difference is that in such a case he may accept payment. Tur, seif 14.
