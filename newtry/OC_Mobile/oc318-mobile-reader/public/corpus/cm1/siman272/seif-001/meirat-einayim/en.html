Whether it was carrying more than its proper load, etc. This excludes the opinion that expounds the verse, "If you see the donkey of your enemy crouching under its burden, you shall surely help with him," to mean specifically under a burden appropriate for it.
The animal ordinarily crouches, etc. The verse is expounded: "crouching," but not an animal that habitually crouches; "crouching," but not standing.
Rather, he gets up with him and reloads it. Meaning, where the animal lies under its load because the load shifted from the place where it was originally positioned, he unloads it from that misplaced position and then reloads it onto the proper part of the animal where loads are ordinarily placed.
He has neglected a positive commandment. Meaning, the positive commandments "you shall surely help" with unloading and "you shall surely raise" with loading.
