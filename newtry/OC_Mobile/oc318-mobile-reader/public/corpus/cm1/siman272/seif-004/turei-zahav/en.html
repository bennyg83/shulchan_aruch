(Seif 13, they give precedence.) The Sema, seif katan 11, explains that the two travelers are walking toward one another. In my view the legal reasoning indicates the reverse. If they can walk one behind the other, why burden the unloaded traveler to delay and walk slowly behind the loaded one? In truth, it is more advantageous for the person who walks behind his fellow. The baraita certainly concerns two who meet face to face, as Levush explains. This is proved by the latter clause in the Gemara: “Likewise, ships that meet one another,” as cited in the next seif. The fact that the present clause says “they give precedence,” whereas the later one says “is pushed aside,” and the repetition in seif 14, present no difficulty. The Gemara contains the language of one baraita regarding ships and camels ascending steep inclines, and Tosafot’s language regarding donkeys whose legs are weak. The authorities combined them and each preserved the wording of its source. They were not taught by a single Tanna whose differing language would require explanation. Likewise, one Tanna states generally, “impose a compromise between them,” while the other explains that one pays compensation. The authorities followed both formulations. The Sema himself uses this approach in siman 168.
