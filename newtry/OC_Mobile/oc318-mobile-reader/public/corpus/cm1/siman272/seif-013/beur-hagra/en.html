One who encounters, etc.; when does this apply, etc. There: "come and hear, one who loves, etc.; and if you would think, etc." This applies even if animal suffering is only rabbinic. It was not necessary to pose the question specifically according to the view that it is d'oraisa. Rather, because the other person is his enemy, the questioner knew that assisting the enemy has priority, but initially thought that preventing animal suffering should have even greater priority. This is the meaning of "nevertheless, etc." This explanation follows Rambam. We, however, rule that preventing animal suffering is d'oraisa, so all this is unnecessary.
