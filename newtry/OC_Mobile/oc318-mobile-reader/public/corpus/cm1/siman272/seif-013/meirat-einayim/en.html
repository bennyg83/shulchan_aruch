And the narrow road, they allow, etc. See the Perishah, where I proved that this does not speak of two parties meeting each other head on. That case is discussed in the following seif. Therefore the language "they meet" is not used here, nor does the ruling say that one is pushed back, but rather "they allow to pass." Nor does it mention one paying the other, as in the following seif. Rather, the case is where the rider and the loaded animal travel along the same road side by side, like the preceding law. When they come to a narrow road where they cannot continue side by side, the rider, and certainly an unloaded traveler, yields to the loaded animal. The expression is similar to "one lets the bride pass before the dead," meaning that one gives her precedence. Since no one here is driven backward, but one merely stands aside until the other passes first, no payment is required. They compromise over precedence, for example this one first on one occasion and the other on another. This is unlike the Ir Shushan, who explains even this clause as involving two parties meeting from opposite directions and requiring an equalizing payment.
