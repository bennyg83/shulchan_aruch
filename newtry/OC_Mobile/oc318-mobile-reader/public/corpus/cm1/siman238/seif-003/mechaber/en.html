One authority says that the rule allowing a deed to be written for the seller even when the purchaser is absent applies only when the seller acknowledges in the deed that he has already received the purchase money from the purchaser. If the deed does not say this, then when the seller later sues the purchaser for payment, he must prove that the sale was entered into with the purchaser's consent. Another authority says that even if the seller acknowledges receipt of the money, if the wording of the deed implies that the sale requires the consent of both parties, it may not be written in the purchaser's absence. A third authority says that the deed may be written even without an acknowledgment that payment was received. Rama: This is because the deed does not force the purchaser to buy; it serves only as evidence. Tur in the name of Ramban. Therefore, the purchaser is also believed to say that he paid the money. Beit Yosef in the name of Responsa Rashba 1136. A deed of sale that states the subject of the sale but not the purchase price is valid. Responsa Rashba 1981.
