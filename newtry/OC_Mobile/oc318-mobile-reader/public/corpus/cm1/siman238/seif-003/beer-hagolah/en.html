Kaf) There in the name of the Ramban.
Lamed) The text there states: if the seller claims that the buyer has not yet paid the purchase price, while the buyer says that he paid, the buyer is believed because the purchase price has become like an oral loan. This follows Bava Batra 154b, where the rule is that if not, one follows the deed, meaning that once the deed is authenticated by its signatures, the buyer needs no further proof. The same applies here. This is also indicated at the beginning of Bava Batra 5b concerning one who built another wall beside his neighbor's wall, where the Gemara discusses a claim, "I paid you on time." The Yerushalmi is also cited there: Reuven stole a field from Shimon and sold it to Levi. Levi then gave it to Yehudah, and Shimon recovered it from Yehudah. Yehudah does not proceed against Levi because he received it as a gift, but Levi proceeds against Reuven and collects from him. The cited authority writes that Yehudah does not acquire Levi's warranty claim against Reuven unless Levi expressly transfers to Yehudah, together with land, all deeds and documents relating to that field together with all of their liens.
