That one does not. Because a benefit may be acquired for a person in his absence. Since the deed does not state that the purchaser agreed to the acquisition, but only records that the seller said such-and-such, there is no possible disadvantage to the purchaser. Sema.
That immediately. The Tur explains: Otherwise we must fear that the seller ordered the deed written in Nisan but did not deliver it until Tishrei, meanwhile sold the land to someone else, and then the first person would produce the deed dated in Nisan and wrongfully collect from the later purchaser. If, however, the land was immediately transferred to him by kinyan, his collection is lawful because he acquired from that moment. This is the Rif's view. Rabbeinu Yitzchak rules that the witnesses' signatures themselves acquire on the recipient's behalf, so even without a kinyan one may write the deed. See there and the end of siman 39. Ibid.
The field. This is contrary to the Rambam in chapter 24 of Hilchot Malveh, who distinguishes between a loan document and a sale document. Rather, the law is as the Tur writes, that according to Rav Asi even with a sale document it may be written only where a kinyan has already occurred. The Maggid Mishneh writes likewise there in the names of Rashi, Ramban, Rashba, and other commentators, stating that there is no clear reason to distinguish between a sale and a loan for this law. Bedek HaBayit cites this. Shach.
The names. The Sema writes that it is difficult to understand why the witnesses must recognize the buyer. Recognition of the seller is understandable, because otherwise the seller might falsely assume another person's name and thereby obligate that other person to surrender the field under this sale deed. But once the seller's identity is known, what concern remains? I can explain the need to recognize both only where the buyer also appears before the witnesses when they are asked to write the sale deed. Then the witnesses write that both parties completed the transaction, which may harm the buyer because at times he does not want publicity. Therefore we must also fear that the purported buyer may assume another person's name. See there.
The seller. The Maggid Mishneh writes that this teaches that even if the buyer says, 'Write the deed for me and keep it with you until the seller comes and admits before you that it is true, or makes a kinyan before you,' we do not listen, because the document appears false at the time it is written, when the witnesses do not yet know whether the matter is true. Sema.
In his presence. Nimukei Yosef in chapter Get Pashut writes that the Rashba and Raah disagree. The Beit Yosef cites this in siman 39, seif 6, and the Rama there in seif 4 cites that some disagree. Shach.
