The purchaser pays the fee. Mishkenot Yaakov, siman 27, notes that this wording might suggest there is no need for the sale deed itself to belong first to the seller, since the purchaser pays the scribe and seemingly acquires the paper with his money. Ramban, however, writes explicitly that the rule that the purchaser pays is a rabbinic allocation of expense: the party who benefits pays the cost, but the deed must nevertheless be legally transferred to the seller first. The buyer's payment is treated as though he gave the money to the seller so that the seller could acquire the document and then deliver it back as the operative deed. This is explained from Gittin 20. Mishkenot Yaakov concludes that a deed of sale or other conveyance of property must first be owned by the transferor and then delivered by him to the purchaser. If this does not occur, the deed is mere pottery and effects no acquisition. He therefore warns against the contemporary practice in which a purchaser writes a deed on his own paper, or instructs a scribe to write and witnesses to sign it with the seller's knowledge, but the paper never comes into the seller's legal ownership before being handed to the buyer. Such documents may fail as instruments of acquisition, and scribes should be warned because serious monetary error can result.
