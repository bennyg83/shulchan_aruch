(In the Shulchan Aruch, seif 1: and similarly, one who deposited with another, etc.) This applies even according to the first opinion cited by the Rama immediately afterward, that once the agent becomes obligated to pay, the creditor may not retain the money, etc. There, the creditor would contradict the agent's own account if he retained it, because the agent says that he gave him the money for this debt and the creditor now wishes to apply it differently. Therefore, the creditor must say that he is not changing the agent's designation at all, but that the agent expressly gave it to him for another debt. Here, by contrast, in the case of a deposit, whenever the custodian claims that the depositor, who acted as agent, gave the property to him in the name of a particular person for safekeeping, the custodian is not changing the agent's instruction at all according to his own account. This is parallel to the earlier case where the creditor says that the money was given for another debt. The Sema writes that this ruling follows only the dissenting opinion cited above, and that according to the first opinion it is ineffective unless the creditor says that it was given for another debt. This is not precise.
