And I accepted them on account of that debt. Meaning, even if he admits that the debtor gave him the money intending to pay this document, so long as he does not admit that he himself accepted it toward this particular debt, he may say, 'I applied it to a different debt,' as the Mechaber writes at the end of this siman and in siman 83.
And he collects with it without an oath if it contains a credibility clause. This teaches that we do not say that since there is a defect before us, for he admits having received payment, he must swear notwithstanding the credibility clause. See Perishah.
If it contains no credibility clause, he collects only with an oath. So writes the Tur. In Perishah I wrote that the novelty is that this applies even if the borrower did not demand, 'Swear to me.' Beit din raises the oath for him because there is an evident defect created by the lender's admission, analogous to one who admits partial payment of his document in siman 84. This differs from where the lender admits receiving no payment, as the Tur and Mechaber write at the beginning of siman 82.
Whether he told him, 'Give him the money,' etc. Even though the principal trusted the agent to give the lender the money first, once he also instructed him to take the document from the lender, the agent should have been mindful not to deliver the money unless he was certain the lender would hand over the document.
The agent must repay the principal. A similar rule is stated by the Tur and Mechaber in siman 130, seif 5, concerning a guarantor.
The lender cannot retain them. The agent can say: 'What relationship do I have with you that you should expose me to a loss? Return what I delivered into your hand.'
Likewise, one who deposited with another and said, etc. This follows the dissenting view above, which holds that even though the agent will suffer a loss, the lender may still say, 'I applied the payment to another debt,' because the money was delivered to him in the principal's name. According to the first view, merely giving the money in the principal's name is not enough, for that case also involved payment in the principal's name, yet the creditor is required to have received it specifically toward the other debt.
For he was negligent. Meaning, negligent in his guarding. Had he guarded properly, the second person could not have seized the item. Ritva stated this law regarding a borrower. Beit Yosef notes that even without negligence a borrower is liable for accidents. Rama therefore states the law in the context of a depositor and custodian, where liability to the depositor arises only through negligence. The law of a borrower follows automatically: he is liable to the first owner even without negligence.
Since he did not tell him to give it in the presence of witnesses. Meaning, the principal apparently regarded the creditor as trustworthy. Had he instructed the agent to give the money in the presence of witnesses, even if he did not also instruct him to take the document, the agent would have been liable for failing to do so. Had payment been delivered before witnesses, the creditor could not have said, 'I applied it to another debt,' through a migo that he could have said, 'I returned it to you,' or 'It never happened.'
