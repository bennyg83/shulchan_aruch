(Collected note) And some disagree, etc. This is analogous to the case of the cattle keeper discussed above. According to the first opinion one must distinguish that case from one in which money was entrusted to him, as stated later under "and some say specifically," etc. Accordingly, the later dispute concerning "likewise one who deposited," etc., and "some say," etc., is within the first opinion. It initially appears to me that there is actually no dispute there, because the Rivash did not discuss a case where he seized the money, and perhaps he agrees with the Ritva, as is indicated by the case of the cattle keeper and by the Rivash's own words. The gloss writes "and some say specifically," etc., only because this is not explicit in the Rivash. All of this is according to the first opinion. According to the latter opinion, however, one may always seize. On further examination, however, it appears that the Rivash disagrees with the Ritva, and the wording "and some say," etc., is appropriate. The Rivash explains that one may seize in the case of the cattle keeper even though this causes the cattle keeper liability, because according to the person seizing, the keeper owes nothing and is lying. This implies that if it is known that the keeper's account is true, seizure is ineffective. According to the Ritva, however, he may seize even then, as stated in "and some say specifically," etc. They therefore disagree about why seizure is effective in the case of the cattle keeper, all according to the first opinion above. The Shach's words require study, for he writes that the Ritva agrees with the dissenting opinion. If so, why does the Ritva agree with the first opinion where the property came to him as a deposit? Rather, he agrees with the first opinion and distinguishes a case of seizure, as stated there: specifically where it came as a deposit, he must make a complete return. See the Shach, seif katan 9, who writes that Ir Shushan holds similarly. The principal view appears to me to be that the Ritva represents a third opinion: according to him there is no distinction based on whether the agent becomes liable, as the dissenting opinion holds. See Beit Yosef, siman 83, who cites the Ritva's responsum, etc. It appears there that these issues are not dependent upon one another. His distinction is instead between money given with the authorization of the litigant, or seized directly from him, where seizure is always effective even if the intermediary must pay, and money received as a deposit, where seizure is never effective even if the intermediary is exempt. This is why the Rama writes here "and some say specifically," etc., without attaching it to the first opinion. The Ritva agrees that seizure is effective in every case there, like the dissenting opinion, except where it was received as a deposit. The Rama did not accept his view concerning a deposit, since all disagree with him there, and therefore wrote "and some say specifically," etc., as though no one disagrees with him concerning the deposit, without noting that there is disagreement concerning seizure. See the Shach.
