Seif 1. The agent must repay the principal to the sender. I was uncertain about a case in which the sender specifically told the agent about the promissory note, but the agent paid the creditor before witnesses without taking back the note. The creditor subsequently claims that the payment was for a different debt. According to the Mechaber there is little doubt, because the document is completely nullified and is taken from the creditor; the agent therefore caused no loss. According to the Rama, however, the document is not wholly void, and if the creditor seizes property we do not remove it from him. The question then is whether the agent must pay. Since the creditor retains the ability to seize and the document is not taken from him, perhaps the agent was negligent in failing to obtain the document, for had he taken it the creditor could not later seize on its strength. On the other hand, perhaps there is no certain damage because without seizure the creditor cannot collect, and the debtor can protect himself against seizure. Only where the creditor is believed even to extract money would the agent’s omission clearly be negligence. One might classify this as a case whose beginning was negligent and whose end was an unavoidable accident, which can impose liability. At first glance Tosafot in the episode of Avimi provides a proof. Tosafot explain that the case took place after Rav Nachman instituted the oath of hesset; before that enactment the agent himself would function as a witness. Pnei Yehoshua asks why the agent could have been a witness, since if his conduct made him financially liable he would be interested testimony. He answers that before the enactment of hesset the agent was not negligent because he could rely on the creditor not being brazen enough to deny receipt. Yet according to Tosafot in Shevuot, even where two witnesses testify to payment, if the creditor seizes under a claim that the payment was for another debt, the seized property is not removed. If so, even before the enactment, the agent left the note in the creditor’s hands and thereby enabled a seizure that might stand. If that omission itself were negligence, the agent would be liable and thus disqualified as an interested witness. The fact that Tosafot still treat him as a witness suggests that exposure merely to a possible seizure is not considered actionable negligence. However, elsewhere I explained that Tosafot understand the Avimi case as one where the agent expressly told the creditor that the payment was being made on this particular document. In such a case the Rama rules that if the creditor later seizes, the property is removed from him. That removes the proof. It may therefore be that where seizure would stand, the agent is indeed liable for failing to take the document. I have not presently found a decisive source for this question. See also my discussion in Mishpat Tzedek on this siman.
