Authenticated. The same applies if the document is not yet authenticated. If the creditor later authenticates it, he remains believed regarding his earlier claim that the payment he received was for another debt, through the migo that he could initially have denied receiving the payment at all, as explained at the beginning of siman 82. Shach.
I received them. Even if the creditor admits that the debtor gave him the money as payment toward this very document, so long as he does not admit that he himself accepted it in satisfaction of this debt he may say that he applied it to another debt, as explained at the end of this siman and in siman 83. Thus the Sema. See Responsa Rashdam, simanim 73 and 112.
With an oath. The Sema writes that the novelty is that even if the borrower did not demand, "Swear to me," the court imposes an oath on behalf of the borrower because the creditor's admission that he received money creates a defect in the document, analogous to a creditor who admits partial payment in siman 84. This differs from a normal document where the creditor admits no payment, as discussed at the beginning of siman 82. The Shach disagrees and holds that the court does not raise the oath on its own. See there.
Liable. If the principal did not tell the messenger to obtain the document, then even if he informed the messenger that the debt was evidenced by a document, the messenger is exempt. He can say, "I thought you were relying on the creditor's trustworthiness." So writes the Beit Yosef in the name of Baal HaTerumot. See Responsa Rashdam at the end of simanim 116 and 126. Shach.
And likewise. The Shach writes that Rama's "and likewise" follows the first "some say" opinion. The reason the creditor cannot retain the money is that because the messenger is liable to reimburse the principal, retention would effectively be taking the messenger's money rather than the principal's. That is exactly the reasoning of the first opinion Rama cited. This is unlike the Sema, who says Rama's "and likewise" follows the dissenting opinion. The Bach already objected to the Sema, and the Shach concludes that the first opinion is primary for practical halacha. The Taz likewise criticizes the Sema.
Was negligent. The Ir Shushan writes that if the messenger claims the creditor forcibly seized the money and that he was not negligent, the messenger swears and is exempt. The Shach considers this correct. Since a custodian could exempt himself truthfully by claiming the money was taken from him by force, his failure to prevent the seizure does not itself cause actionable damage.
Exempt. Responsa Mabit, vol. 2, siman 232, rules that if the messenger is paid and the creditor is a non-Jew, the messenger is liable even though he was not instructed to take back the document. Mabit gives two reasons: as a paid messenger he should have taken the trouble to make payment before witnesses, and because the creditor was a non-Jew. The Shach writes that these reasons are not compelling and that the practical law requires study.
