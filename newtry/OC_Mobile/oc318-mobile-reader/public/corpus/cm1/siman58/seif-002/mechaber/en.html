If the borrower says, 'I paid you before so-and-so and so-and-so,' and those witnesses testify that he paid but did not mention the document, while the creditor says payment was for another debt, the document is canceled. {Rama: Some say it is not entirely canceled and merely cannot be used for collection; if the creditor has seized property, it is not removed from him. This appears to me to be the primary view.} This applies when the witnesses testify that the money was given as payment. If they merely saw money delivered and do not know whether it was payment, a deposit, or a gift, then if the creditor denies receiving anything, he is established as a liar and the document is canceled. If he says it paid another debt, he is believed, takes an oath, and collects the document, because no witnessed payment of this debt occurred; since he could have said the money was a gift, he is believed to say it paid another debt. {Rama: Others disagree and hold that even then the document's force is impaired. This appears to me primary, because the burden of proof rests upon one who seeks to remove money. All this concerns payment before two witnesses; payment before one witness does not impair the document. See siman 70.}
