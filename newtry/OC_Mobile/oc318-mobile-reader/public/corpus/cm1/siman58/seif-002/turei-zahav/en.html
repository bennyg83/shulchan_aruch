(Seif 2.) The two seifim beginning with the borrower’s claim through seif 4 require explanation. There are two forms of “other-account payment.” The first is an ordinary unexplained payment; the second is payment said to be proceeds of the ox, which is weaker for the creditor. Rabbi Yosef HaLevi and the Rambam hold that in an ordinary case, if the debtor gave money before witnesses merely as a transfer and not expressly as repayment, the document is not impaired because the creditor may say it was a gift. The Rosh rejects this because gifts are uncommon. They also disagree whether the document is entirely cancelled or merely impaired. In the ox-proceeds case, the Rambam is especially lenient to the debtor: even without witnesses to the transfer, the creditor’s admission that he received the ox proceeds can cancel the document, because the circumstances establish that those funds were for the debt. Rabbi Yosef HaLevi requires witnesses to the transfer. The Tur follows the Rosh for an ordinary unexplained payment, treating the document as impaired but not cancelled, and treats the ox-proceeds case as fully cancelling the document when there are witnesses, following Rabbi Yosef HaLevi rather than the Rambam’s broader leniency.
