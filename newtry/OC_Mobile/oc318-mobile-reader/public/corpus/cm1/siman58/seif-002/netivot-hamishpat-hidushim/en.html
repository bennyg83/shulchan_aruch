The document is void. There is no distinction in a claim of "I applied it to another debt" between claiming a separate oral loan and claiming that there had been another documented loan which the creditor later returned. The borrower swears a hesset oath. This applies even if the present document contains an ordinary credibility clause. If it contains credibility equal to two witnesses, the creditor may claim that the payment was for another debt through a migo that he could have contradicted the witnesses. If the borrower has a handwritten acknowledgment from the creditor concerning the payment, the creditor cannot claim that it was for another debt, Shach. Even if the witnesses do not remember when the payment occurred and cannot say whether it was before or after the date of the document, the document is nevertheless void. If two witnesses say that the payment was for this document and two say it was for another debt, the borrower is exempt, just as where two say he paid and two say he did not. Even if another oral or written loan is independently known by witnesses, the creditor cannot claim that the payment was for that other debt. The borrower is believed to say that the payment witnessed by the witnesses was for the present document and that he later paid the oral debt separately. If the other written loan was still within its fixed term, the creditor may claim that the payment was for the other debt. See Beurim. If the present document is being collected through an authorized agent, while the money received was collected by that agent in his own personal capacity, the document is not void because the agent could return the document to its owner. The reason is that the borrower had no authority to pay the agent without taking the document from him. If the borrower expressly mentioned the document when paying the agent, then the agent must return the money to the borrower. This applies specifically where the borrower demanded the document back immediately at the time of payment before the witnesses, while the agent claimed that the payment was for an oral debt. If the witnesses already left and only afterward the borrower demanded the document, and the agent says, "I returned to you the document you paid, and this is a different document," the agent swears and is exempt. But before one witness. Even where the document contains a credibility clause, the borrower need not swear, Sema. The Shach writes that even without a credibility clause, he need not swear unless the creditor specifically demands an oath, as with any other document. This is not a case where one who cannot swear must pay, as in the case of Rabbi Abba's silver ingot, because the oath here is only a rabbinic enactment. The rule that one who cannot swear must pay is not applied to a rabbinic oath. Many decisors hold that if he had denied the event entirely he would have been required to swear the rabbinic oath patterned after a Torah oath, with all its consequences. The Tumim, however, agrees with the Rama's ruling. If the creditor produces the single witness only after the second payment, the law is as follows. If that witness had not previously appeared in beit din, then had the borrower denied the first payment he would have been obligated in a Torah oath. Therefore, if the borrower admits that at the first payment he expressly mentioned the document, after the second payment he becomes one who cannot swear and must pay even if the second collection was movable property, because once he identified the document seizure no longer helps. If he did not mention the document at the first payment, and the second payment was collected from land while the first witnessed payment was money, he likewise becomes one who cannot swear and must pay. But if the second payment was collected from movable property, then even if the first payment had been made before two witnesses without mention of the document, and only afterward those witnesses appeared, he need not return the movable property under the Rama's ruling in seif 2 that seizure is effective. Once he already collected, it is treated like seizure. All the more so with only one witness. Likewise, if even the first collection was from land, there is no rule of one who cannot swear and must pay because no Torah oath applies to land. If the witness appeared in beit din before the second payment and the borrower then paid pursuant to beit din's ruling, there is likewise no later application of one who cannot swear and must pay. These distinctions do not depend on whether the witness also says that the borrower failed to mention the document, whether he says that the borrower did mention it and the borrower contradicts him, or even whether the witness says that the creditor himself mentioned the document and the borrower contradicts him.
