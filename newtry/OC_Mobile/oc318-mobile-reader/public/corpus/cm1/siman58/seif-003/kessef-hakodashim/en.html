If a lender says he intended to apply a payment to an oral debt or to another weaker claim, perhaps no general acceptance of a ban is required unless people commonly make such mistakes, in which case it may be required. Likewise, if he received merchandise or money without specification and says he did not intend it as payment, where the law is that each party collects separately, perhaps a formal declaration is required. Through gilgul, however, such a declaration can apply even where there is a strong presumption. According to Urim VeTumim, a declaration is not required for matters added through gilgul, and perhaps the same applies where there is a strong estimate. If a married woman owes a debt that her husband is not obligated to pay, and another debt is owed by her husband, and the husband sends payment through her, while the lender claims he takes it for her personal debt, the situation requires distinction. Even if it is known that she owns separate property in which her husband has no rights, if she says the money belongs to her husband she may be believed, especially if when she handed it over she stated that she was paying her husband's debt, since she then had a migo. If the lender claims with certainty that the money was hers, perhaps he is believed, all the more so if he took it without witnesses and has a migo. This requires further study.
