Witnesses. The Sema says the rule applies even if the lender did not admit that the documented debt was the price of the ox. The Shach explains that the witnesses must have seen that the payment itself was given as repayment. Where the money came from purchasers of the meat, the lender's admission is needed. The reason is that the ox was originally presumed to belong to the lender, and both stood at the slaughterhouse while the meat was sold. The lender can otherwise say, "I did not sell you the ox on credit at all. The meat was mine and I hired you only to cut it." The ordinary presumption that a person does not leave a documented debt unpaid while accepting an oral payment does not apply. Therefore his admission is required. If witnesses establish that he sold the ox on credit, then even if they do not know that this document represents the ox's price and the lender does not admit it, the document is void. Alternatively, if the witnesses do not know the date and the meat sale may have preceded the document, his admission is needed.
The price of the ox. According to the Mechaber, the document is entirely void. According to Rama, it is neither torn up nor collected upon, but if the lender seized property it is not removed from him. This is unlike the Sema, who holds the document is entirely void here according to everyone. The Shach further writes about a lender who appointed an agent to collect money. The borrower told the agent not to pay the lender until the lender wrote a receipt on the document, but the agent failed to do so, and the lender then claimed the payment was for another debt. If the lender appointed the agent before witnesses, he cannot deny the agency and therefore cannot claim another debt. If he did not appoint him before witnesses, he can claim another debt, provided he says the agent too paid him toward another obligation. Otherwise, since the agent would suffer loss, he cannot seize the money for another debt.
If it is known. This applies specifically where the payment occurred before witnesses and all the money was given together. See the Shach, who questions this law and leaves it unresolved.
