The phrase 'since he admitted' appears only because the Mechaber copied Rambam's wording. Rambam holds that the lender's admission that he received the ox's value is enough even without witnesses to invalidate the document. The Mechaber did not alter Rambam's wording although he had already cited the stricter view requiring witnesses.
If there are witnesses that he paid him the value of the ox. This is also the Tur's language. In Perishah I proved that it is not literal. Even if the witnesses do not know the payment was specifically the ox's value but testify merely that the debtor gave the creditor money as payment, this opinion holds the document is entirely canceled. Rabbeinu Tam and the Rosh, cited in the previous seif, hold that even if witnesses merely saw money pass from debtor to creditor, the document is weakened, meaning it is neither torn up nor collected upon. In the present case, where the lender admits that what he received was the ox's value, they would hold the document is entirely canceled. The dissenting opinion holds that even if there are no witnesses at all to the payment, the lender cannot be believed once he himself admitted receiving the ox's value, because that migo stands against a presumption.
