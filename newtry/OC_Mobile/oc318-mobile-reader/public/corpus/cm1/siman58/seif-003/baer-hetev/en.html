Witnesses. The Sema writes that this is obvious: if there are witnesses, even if the creditor never admitted that he was paid from the proceeds of the ox, the document is void, as stated in seif 2. The Mechaber therefore need not have used the expression 'since he admitted'; he merely did not wish to alter the Rambam's wording. The Shach writes that this is strained, for the Tur also writes 'since he admitted,' although the Tur explicitly rules differently from the Rambam. He therefore explains that the witnesses do not testify that the creditor received the proceeds of the ox as payment of this debt. Rather, they testify only that he received meat or proceeds from the purchasers of the ox. The wording 'if there are witnesses that he paid him' is therefore not exact, or alternatively it refers to the purchasers. This also resolves the wording of the Tur and Shulchan Aruch, 'if there are witnesses that he paid him the value of the ox,' without resorting to the Sema and Bach's forced explanation that 'the value of the ox' is not literal. Thus concludes the Shach.
The ox. The Sema writes that according to Rabbenu Tam and the Rosh, who hold that even if witnesses testify generally that they saw the borrower give the creditor money the document is impaired, as stated in the gloss to the preceding seif, so that it is neither torn up nor collected upon, in this case, where the creditor admits that the money was the proceeds of the ox, the document becomes entirely void. The other cited opinion holds that even where there are no witnesses at all to the payment, the creditor is not believed, because by voluntarily admitting that he received the proceeds of the ox he is asserting a migo against an established presumption. The Shach disagrees and writes that here too the document is neither collected upon nor torn up; see there. See also the Taz. The Shach further cites Responsa Maharam of Lublin, siman 114, concerning one who sent an agent to receive money; see there.
