Since he admitted, etc. Sema writes that admission is unnecessary because there are witnesses, and that the Mechaber mentioned admission only incidentally. Shach writes that the case is one where the witnesses did not know that the money was accepted from the borrower as repayment. See Tumim, where I wrote that in such a case witnesses alone do not suffice and admission is also required. See there for the reason. The case may even be where the witnesses testify that he accepted the money as repayment.
If there are witnesses, etc. Without witnesses he is believed through a migo that he could deny having received the proceeds of the ox at all. We cannot say that even now he has a migo to claim that the document was unrelated to the ox, because such a migo is invalid when it requires a prior falsehood. See Tumim.
Who paid him the price of the ox. Sema writes that this is not precise and the same law applies if the witnesses say merely that he paid him. According to what I wrote in Tumim, seif katan 10, the Mechaber may be teaching a stronger case, because where the witnesses specifically identify the proceeds of the ox one might have thought there was more room for the lender to say, "I applied it elsewhere." See there.
The price of the ox. Sema writes that in this case even Tur and those who agree with him, who ordinarily hold that we do not tear up the document, agree that the document is entirely void. Shach objects. See Tumim, where I defended Sema.
Some write, etc. This is a migo against a presumption. Therefore witnesses are unnecessary. He has no migo to say, "I never received it," because the presumption is that payment relating to this transaction was made toward this very document, since everything concerns one matter.
If it is known, etc. Shach questions this ruling. See Tumim, where I wrote that if his admission that he received one thousand gold coins was made as one total statement, and part was toward this debt, he may still say that the remainder was toward another debt because the actual payments were not one simultaneous payment. But if the payment itself was made all at once, Maharik holds that one cannot reasonably divide it, saying that part was for this debt and part for another. Although I found no other authority who states this expressly, in Tumim, seif katan 13, I established a proof for Maharik from Rosh.
