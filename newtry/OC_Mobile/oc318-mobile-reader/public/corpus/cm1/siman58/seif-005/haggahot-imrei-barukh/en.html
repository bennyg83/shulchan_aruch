Note: See the Rosh's responsum, which explicitly states that payment was made before witnesses. Independently, there is no basis for the Rosh's question, because Maharik's reasoning depends upon an assessment of circumstances. In the Rosh's case there was no such assessment. The claimant, acting under his daughter's authorization, admitted that a certain sum from his father-in-law's estate had reached his hand, but he also had a debt to collect from that estate. He made an overall accounting with the guardian, who treated 276 gold coins from the money already received as payment toward his wife's debt. There is therefore no basis to infer that because those 276 coins were allocated to his wife's claim, the entire remaining sum must likewise have been allocated to her claim rather than to the husband's own debt.
