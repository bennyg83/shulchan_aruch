Whether it was recorded in a document, etc. See the Sema, seif katan 1, who distinguishes a lease document, which may be written initially so that the tenant will not acquire a presumption of ownership. This is the second explanation in the Gemara in Bava Metzia, as cited by Tosafot in the name of Rabbeinu Tam, and it is also the view of Rabbeinu Chananel, Tosafot, Baal HaMaor, Nimukei Yosef in the name of the Raavad, Baal HaIttur, Behag, Hagahot Ashri in the name of Or Zarua, and the Tur. Even according to those who explain the passage differently, the ruling itself is correct, as stated by the Rivash, the Bach, and Hagahot Ashri. The Sema further writes that, unless a separate document was written for the rent and it contains a clause of credibility, the tenant may say that he paid. This is difficult, for why should both conditions be necessary? Nimukei Yosef states expressly that either one suffices: if the tenant wrote proof that he entered as a tenant and then wrote a separate obligation for the rent, it is like an ordinary promissory note and he is not believed to say that he paid; likewise, if the lease contains a clause of credibility, the monetary stipulation is valid. It appears that the wording of Darkei Moshe misled the copyist. There it states that either a separate document was written for the rent, or the lease contains a clause of credibility. The second ‘if’ should be ‘or.’ Alternatively, the letter vav in ‘and it is written’ is disjunctive. Or perhaps the intent is that, where credibility was expressly granted, the tenant cannot even require the landlord to take an oath, as in the law of an ordinary promissory note above, siman 82, seif 2. See Responsa Maharam Padua, siman 39.
Within the term of the lease, etc. It appears that the Rama suspected an omission in the Mechaber’s words, for otherwise the reference to thirty days would be out of place, and therefore inserted that the property had been rented for thirty days. Yet the plain wording of the Mechaber does not support a lease expressly fixed for thirty days. Why then mention specifically thirty days? Moreover, at the end of the seif he writes, ‘Likewise, if he rented from him on condition that the rent be paid annually,’ which should have been combined with this case. Rather, the Mechaber is reproducing the Rambam. The Rambam’s phrase ‘if he claimed from him within thirty days’ means, as the Maggid Mishneh explains, a tenancy of unspecified duration during the thirty-day notice period discussed above in siman 312, seif 5. It may also refer to a landlord who rented without specifying a term, whose minimum period is thirty days. Nevertheless, a difficulty remains. The Rambam and Maggid Mishneh may follow the Ran, who holds that even an ordinary loan is presumed not to be repaid within thirty days. The Mechaber, however, ruled above at the end of siman 78 that this presumption applies only where a term was fixed, not to an ordinary loan, and he cited no dissenting view. The same should therefore apply to an ordinary tenancy, so that the tenant should be believed to say that he paid within thirty days. One may perhaps distinguish an ordinary tenancy from an ordinary loan in light of the laws stated above in siman 312, but the matter requires study.
