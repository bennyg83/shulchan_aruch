He may claim. The Tur's language is: "or he may make the claim against him after paying him," and then administer a hesset oath. This is also the Mechaber's intent. One should not object from siman 78, where the Tur states that before paying a creditor one may require him to swear that he has not already been paid. The Sema distinguishes rent from a loan. People do not ordinarily prepay rent during the term, because the lessor may perhaps remove the tenant before the term ends and, after having paid, the tenant may have no practical means to hold him to the lease. Since reason indicates that he did not prepay, he cannot demand an oath before paying. A borrower, by contrast, is under an obligation to repay the debt. Even before the due date he may fear that he will spend the money and later lack funds, and therefore may repay early. This is the Sema's explanation in somewhat fuller form. The Shach writes that this reasoning is remote and found in no posek. Although Tosafot and Nimukei Yosef at the beginning of Bava Batra distinguish between rent and a loan, that is according to Rava, not Reish Lakish, whose opinion we follow. Even according to Rava, the distinction concerns credibility, not whether the oath is taken before or after payment. In truth, there is no difficulty. The Tur in siman 78 also means that the oath is taken after payment, since he cites the Rosh, who explicitly says it is a hesset oath, and there is no hesset oath before payment. The Bach there also explains the Rosh this way. See Responsa Maharam Padua, siman 39.
During the day. The Tur and Mechaber write at the end of siman 78 that a person is believed only if he makes his claim at the end of the day. That case is where he says on the day the debt falls due, "I paid you before the due date." His credibility depends on a migo that he could have said, "I paid you now." We recognize that migo only when he makes the claim at the end of the day. At the beginning of the day it would be a brazen migo, because the creditor is standing before him and denying receipt. At the end of the day there is less brazenness, for he can say, "I paid you last night at its beginning and, because you were occupied throughout the night and today, you forgot." Tosafot in Bava Kamma write similarly. Here, however, he says, "I paid you today," and since people sometimes pay during the day, he is believed even at its beginning without relying upon a migo. Sema.
The tenant. This is unlike the law in siman 312, seif 15, where the tenant mentioned both a month and a year, and even after he has already lived there he must pay for the intercalated month. As the Sema explained there, the uncertainty between the two expressions already existed at the beginning of the month. Here, by contrast, there was no uncertainty until now. The debt was unquestionably due, and the tenant now claims that he paid it. We therefore leave the money where it presently is. The Shach adds that the cases differ for another reason: there the tenant does not make a certain claim, while here he does, as the Rosh explicitly states in the first chapter of Bava Metzia and chapter 2 of Ketubot.
