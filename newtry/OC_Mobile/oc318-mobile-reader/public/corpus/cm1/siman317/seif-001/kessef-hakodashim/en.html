Where landlord and tenant made no stipulation concerning a tax imposed by the authorities upon houses, the landlord may be considered in possession if the lease is formulated as a monthly rental, while in a lease for a fixed year the tenant may be considered in possession if the authorities pursue the landlord. In a mortgage arrangement, the mortgagor remains in possession for these purposes because land stands in the possession of its owner. In a place where officials do not enforce Jewish monetary law and a forceful tenant refuses to vacate until he receives what he believes is due, he may perhaps not thereby violate Torah law if he knows that his own understanding of the agreement was that the tax fell upon the landlord, though this requires study. In our town the authorities collect an annual tax from persons residing in a homeowner's premises, including occupants of a smaller rear house. A dispute arose between the owner of the larger house and the holder of the smaller house under a mortgage, where their agreement was to divide equally all taxes assessed on the two houses. There was a doubt concerning a particular annual gold coin that the occupant would have had to pay wherever he lived in the city. Since it is virtually certain that he would not leave the city, there is reason to say that this personal tax is not included in the parties' dispute at all. On the other hand, if no one lived in the smaller house the tax might otherwise have fallen elsewhere, so some doubt remains. On balance, because the property would certainly not have stood vacant and this occupant would in any event have remained in the city, I incline not to include that personal tax in the disputed house charge. If a landlord rents space in his own home to a person for a year and midway the tenant travels far away, while the tenant's wife may be unable to pay, the landlord may perhaps require security or even refuse her continued occupancy. Although rent may be payable only at the end, ordinary rental presumes that payment is reasonably secure. The husband's obligation to house his wife does not make her a co-obligor on his rental debt, and the landlord cannot simply collect from her separate movables.
