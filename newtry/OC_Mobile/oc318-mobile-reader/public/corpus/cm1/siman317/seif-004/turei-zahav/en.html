(Seif 3: in the possession of those who consumed the produce.) The Gemara asks: do we not rule that land remains in the possession of its owner? It answers that this applies only to a matter that is not expected ever to become clarified. Rashi explains that ordinarily we follow the landowner where the doubt cannot ultimately be resolved, such as the uncertainty whether to follow the first or last clause of a contradictory formulation. Here, however, the matter is expected to become clarified, because the witnesses to the document may eventually come and testify. Beit din does not trouble itself twice, since perhaps the lender's version will later be established. The Sema, seif katan 9, writes that the same should apply in the preceding seif where a document states a number of years but it is unknown when the term began, because this too may be clarified. He compares Perek Chezkas HaBatim 35, where one says, "It belonged to my ancestors," and the other says the same, and the stronger party prevails because witnesses may later appear and contradict the present ruling. Therefore, whenever clarification is possible, beit din should not decide definitively. The difference is only that there neither party is presently in possession, so the stronger party prevails, while here we leave the matter with the present possessor. This is unlike the Sema's distinction that the present case cannot be clarified. There is no basis for that distinction. The phrase, "so that the tenant or mortgage holder cannot," etc., refers to the lender holding the property as a mortgagee, as in the following seif. The Tur says "tenant" and the Rosh says "mortgagee"; both refer to the same need for the present possessor to clarify his claim. The Sema's interpretation of "mortgage holder" as referring to the landlord is far-fetched and legally incorrect. The landlord has no burden to clarify. If the party who must clarify remains uncertain, the rule follows the landlord because land remains in its owner's possession.
