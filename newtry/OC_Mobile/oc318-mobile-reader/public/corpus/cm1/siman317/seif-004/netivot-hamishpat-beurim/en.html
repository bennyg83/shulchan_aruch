Or the mortgagee. Sema, seif katan 13, understood "the mortgager" to mean the borrower who mortgaged his land. He was therefore troubled: why should we wait for the landlord to bring proof when any proof he brings would only weaken his own present position? Sema answers that whenever the landlord is capable of clarifying his right, beit din does not extract from the present possessor until he does so. Shach disagrees and reads the phrase as referring to the lender holding the mortgage, who stands in the position analogous to the tenant. That reading avoids the difficulty.
