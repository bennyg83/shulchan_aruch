And the tenant acted first, etc. This implies that he acted first in the presence of witnesses, so that his consumption of the produce is known. Otherwise, even where the matter can no longer be clarified, we do not extract it from him, because he has a migo that he could have said, "I did not consume it." The case also concerns circumstances in which he has no migo that he purchased it, for otherwise he would be believed by virtue of that migo, as explained below at the end of this siman. Even though he showed the document in beit din, had he wished he could have concealed it. Rivash writes likewise at the end of simanim 336 and 343. Nor can one argue that since the document says merely "years," that wording supports the borrower because it implies two, as we say above in siman 43, seif 2, regarding "sela'im" and "dinars." In my Sefer Tekfo Kohen I wrote, citing Nimukei Yosef in Perek HaMekabel and the Ritva's novellae at the beginning of Bava Metzia, that since the borrower allowed the lender to consume the fruits of the third year, the evidentiary force of the document was weakened. See there. Moreover, even in siman 43, if he has a migo he is believed, as the Sema writes there, seif katan 32. See what I wrote there.
The produce remains with the one who consumed it. The reason is that in a matter that is expected to become clarified, we do not trouble beit din twice. The Ritva writes at the beginning of Bava Metzia that this applies specifically where he initially took possession to some extent with permission. Even when he consumed more than two years, the borrower did not object, and because he did not object he is considered to have possession with permission for purposes of not extracting it from him. Even so, if the other party later retakes it, we likewise do not extract it from him, even if he seized it in the presence of witnesses, and the stronger party prevails. But where he seized entirely without permission, we remove it from him even in a matter expected to be clarified. See there. The Sema, seif katan 11, writes that if the document states a three year rental but contains no date, since the witnesses were not required to record when the rental term began, this is considered a matter that cannot be clarified, and this appears correct. The Bach writes in seif 3 that in the case of a document saying three years, even when clarification is impossible, such as where the witnesses died and beit din sees that he cannot clarify it, we do not extract from him because the tenant is believed together with his document. He distinguishes this from our case, where the document merely says "years," which supports the landlord because it implies only two. I conclude that the law is the same as here: where the matter can be clarified we do not extract from him, and where it cannot be clarified we do extract from him. Everything depends upon beit din's assessment whether clarification is still possible. This also appears from the Rif, Rosh, and Nimukei Yosef in Perek HaShoel, who write concerning Rav Nachman that this applies specifically to a matter not expected to be clarified, as stated later in Perek HaMekabel. Why did they need to write this if it is already explained in Perek HaMekabel? Rather, they mean that the same principle applies everywhere: wherever clarification is possible, beit din does not trouble itself twice. Examine carefully.
And there is an opinion that if beit din sees, etc. The language of the Rosh and Tur is: "If the witnesses died and beit din sees that the tenant will no longer be able to clarify his claim." This implies that even after the witnesses died, beit din must still determine that he cannot clarify it. The Hagahot Alfasi in Perek HaMekabel writes similarly in the name of Rabbi Avraham of Zolkiew: although the witnesses died, the matter may still be clarified, because perhaps the witnesses or the litigants publicized the facts and the matter is likely to become known.
Or the mortgage holder. The Tur does not contain this wording. The Sema suggests that perhaps the Mechaber holds that whenever the matter can be clarified, whether by the tenant or by the landlord, clarification is required. This is an error. "Mortgage holder" here stands in place of "tenant," as stated in the following seif, "the tenant or mortgage holder consumed it." The tenant holds it through rental, while the mortgage holder holds it through a loan. The Tur has "tenant" and the Rosh has "mortgage holder"; they are the same for this purpose. Obviously everything depends upon whether the tenant or mortgage holder can clarify his claim.
We extract it from him. It appears that here we extract it from him even if he consumed the produce for three years before being summoned to judgment, because there is no reason to distinguish. It resembles the rule above, siman 312, seif 15, where even if he comes at the end of the month the entire month belongs to the landlord, for the reason explained there by Rashi and Maharsha that the uncertainty arose at the beginning of the month. The same applies here. Although there the claimant's assertion is uncertain while here it is definite, I already explained there that the definite claim here is equivalent to the uncertain claim there. This also appears from the language of the Ritva's novellae cited above. The Tur and Mechaber say at the beginning, "the tenant acted first and consumed it, and afterward the owner says three," merely because they follow the language of the Gemara and that was the factual case there. Alternatively, they state it to teach a greater novelty in the first ruling: even if he consumed it after being summoned to judgment, we do not extract from him where the matter is expected to become clarified. Where we do extract it, this is in a case where the tenant has no migo, such as where he cannot claim that he paid or that there was no transaction. The Ir Shushan writes this, and I already wrote likewise above.
