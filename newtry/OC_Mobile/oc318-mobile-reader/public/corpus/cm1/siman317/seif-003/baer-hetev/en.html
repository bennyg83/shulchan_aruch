And he preceded. This implies that he established his prior consumption through witnesses; otherwise, even where clarification is impossible, we would not extract the produce from him because he has a migo that he could have said, "I did not consume it." The case also must be one where he has no migo that the property was purchased, for otherwise he would be believed through that migo. Even though he showed the document in beit din, he could have concealed it. So writes the Rivash at the end of simanim 336 and 343. Nor can one say that the document's unspecified word "years" assists the borrower by implying two years, as in siman 42, seif 12, regarding "sela'im" or "dinars," because I wrote in Sefer Tekfo Kohen in the name of the Nimukei Yosef and Chiddushei Ritva that since the borrower allowed the lender to consume the produce of a third year, the evidentiary force of the document is weakened. Moreover, in siman 42 he is likewise believed through a migo, as the Sema writes there. See what I wrote there.
He consumes them. Chiddushei Ritva, chapter 1 of Bava Metzia, writes that this applies specifically here, where he initially took possession with permission. Even after he consumed more than two years, the borrower did not protest, so his possession is treated as having begun with permission for purposes of not extracting from him. Nevertheless, if the borrower later seized from him, we do not extract from the borrower either, even if the seizure was witnessed; whoever is stronger prevails. But where one seized entirely without permission, even in a matter expected to become clarified, we remove it from him. See there. The Sema writes that if the document says "for three years" but bears no date, the witnesses were not required to record when the rental began; this is therefore considered incapable of clarification. This appears primary. The Bach writes that where the document says "for ten years," even if clarification is impossible, for example the witnesses died and beit din sees no way to establish the truth, we do not remove the property from the renter because the renter is believed alongside his document. This is unlike our case, where the document merely says "years" and thereby supports the lessor's claim that only two years were rented. I conclude that the law is the same in both cases: where clarification is possible, we do not extract until it is clarified; where it is not possible, we extract. Everything depends on the judge's assessment whether the matter can be clarified. This also appears from the Rif, Rosh, and Nimukei Yosef in chapter HaSho'el, who write regarding Rav Nachman's rule that it applies only to a matter not expected to be revealed, as stated later in chapter HaMekabel. Their purpose is to teach that in every place where clarification is possible, beit din does not trouble itself to decide until clarification is attempted. Ibid.
It appears. The Rosh and Tur state: if the witnesses died and it appears to beit din that the renter cannot further clarify his words. This implies that even after the witnesses died, beit din must still determine that clarification is impossible. Hagahot Alfasi, chapter HaMekabel, cites Riaz likewise: even if the witnesses died, the matter may still be clarified because perhaps the witnesses or the litigants publicized it and it is capable of becoming known. Ibid.
We remove. It appears that here we remove the produce from his possession even if he consumed three years before he was sued. There is no reason to distinguish. It is also comparable to siman 312, seif 15, where even if the lessor comes at the end of the month, the entire month belongs to the lessor, because, as Rashi and Rashba explain there, the doubt arose at the beginning of the month. The same applies here. Although there the claim is uncertain and here definite, I wrote there that the present claim is treated like the uncertain claim there. This also appears from the language of Chiddushei Ritva cited above. The Tur and Mechaber state in the first clause, "he preceded and consumed," merely because that was the language and fact pattern of the Gemara, or to teach a stronger point, that even if he consumed after suit was brought, we do not remove it from him in a matter expected to become clarified. Ibid.
