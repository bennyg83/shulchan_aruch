And eat the fruits. Meaning of the third year:
The produce remains in the possession of those who consumed it, etc. The Tur writes that we do not remove it from the tenant until the landlord brings proof, because perhaps today or tomorrow the tenant will produce witnesses supporting his claim, in which case beit din would have to return the money or produce to him. Beit din does not impose needless procedural burden. Therefore, my father the Rosh writes, if the witnesses have died and the matter has been forgotten by others, and beit din sees that the tenant will no longer be able to clarify his claim, we remove from the tenant the value of what he occupied. This explains the two views cited by the Mechaber. At first glance the rule should not be limited to a dispute over a document that says merely "years," one party claiming two years and the other three. The same should apply if the document explicitly says three years, but they dispute whether those three years have already ended, and the tenant has already occupied the third disputed year. So long as the matter can still be clarified through the witnesses to the document, we should not extract from the tenant. Likewise, even with no document, if the rental was made before witnesses for three years and the parties dispute when those three years ended, we should not extract from a tenant who already occupied the disputed period while the facts can still be clarified. The Tur's phrase that the matter was forgotten by others implies that if there remains a reasonable possibility of clarification, we do not extract. The Tur states this law separately in the case of a document saying "years" simply because he follows the Gemara's actual case. Still, one can distinguish. Where the document says only "years," the witnesses were expected to remember and explain how many years the kinyan concerned, and sometimes witnesses travel away and communicate those details to others. That is specifically a matter likely to be clarified. But if the document says ten years yet has no starting date, the witnesses were not necessarily expected to remember when the term began. That case may be treated as incapable of clarification. This latter distinction appears primary.
