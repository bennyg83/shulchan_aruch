That the scribe signs at the direction of the witness, etc. The same applies to a borrower who obligates himself in his own handwriting, or by a mamram stating, 'So-and-so son of So-and-so signed by handing over the pen,' or, 'So-and-so son of So-and-so instructed that it be signed.' This is effective, as explained above in seif katan 4. Rashba likewise writes in a responsum regarding a ketubah that if someone else signed for the groom it is effective, concluding that he is a witness to the matter. Nevertheless, for practical purposes the matter is unseemly because it may lead to mishap. See Darkei Moshe, seif 4.
If the townspeople accepted them upon themselves, etc. This is like the rule of one who says, 'My father is acceptable to me as judge,' discussed above in siman 22.
And they know how to sign. Although the case concerns witnesses who testify about the signatures of the original witnesses, this still teaches that we do not suspect that the witnesses signed by having the outline of letters cut into paper and then filling the cutout with ink, which would invalidate the document. Rather, we maintain them in their presumption that they know how to sign on their own.
If they said, etc. In Perishah I wrote in the name of Beit Yosef that this refers to where they said so on their own initiative before the judges questioned them.
If their reading corresponds to the document, etc. In Perishah I wrote that they are also asked whether, at the outset, they had it read to them by someone who knows how to read. If they say they did not have it read, their signatures were initially invalid and are not rehabilitated merely because they can now state the content correctly. Perhaps they know what happened but nevertheless should not have signed, because the document itself could contain a forgery. But when, before the beit din asks them, they themselves say that it was read to them, we maintain them in their presumption of truthfulness that it was indeed read, and therefore there is no need to scrutinize whether their account corresponds to the document.
