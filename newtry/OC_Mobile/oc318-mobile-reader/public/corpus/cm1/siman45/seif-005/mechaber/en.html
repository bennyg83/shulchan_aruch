In a place where the custom is for the scribe to sign on behalf of a witness, writing that so and so signed at the witness's instruction, or where the witness handed him the pen, or where the document states that so and so instructed him to sign, then if the townspeople accepted the practice of treating the scribe's attestation as the testimony of the witness, it is valid. If they did not accept this practice, it has no effect. Any document presented before us whose witness signatures are authenticated by testimony concerning those signatures is upheld, because there is a presumption that witnesses do not sign a document unless they have read it and know how to sign. If the witnesses themselves are before us and we know that they cannot read, but they say, "We had someone who knows how to read read it to us," we rely upon them. If they do not say this, we examine their oral testimony, and if it corresponds to the contents of the document, we authenticate it.
