(In the Tur, seif 19: Nevertheless, nothing is learned from half a line.) Maharshal writes that “nevertheless” refers to the first case, where the witnesses sign one after another. I do not agree. It refers to the latter case. The Tur means to answer the question why a document is valid when the witnesses sign one beneath the other, since the holder might forge text in the line and a half that remain. Although the document is valid, no legal inference is drawn from that line and a half, so there is no concern that forged writing there will be accepted by beit din. This is also stated in the Tur’s Remazim. In the first case, however, where the signatures follow one another, one certainly may rely on the text. When a written document with signatures comes before us, we do not suspect that a line and a half had originally been left blank, for that would have invalidated it, and witnesses are presumed not to act unlawfully. The Beit Yosef cites the Ramban expressly to this effect, and the Mechaber rules so in siman 44, seif 3.
