And there is blank space. The Darkei Moshe records a dispute whether the deed is invalid if the witnesses signed while such a margin remained. The two views appear in Tosafot. The point is that a deed is invalid because of a margin only where cutting away the margin would itself invalidate the instrument. Here the document remains valid, and if any concern arises the blank margin can still be cut away. It is therefore not necessarily invalid.
Lest an obligation against him had been written there. The Sema says that although the final line is ordinarily disregarded, that rule concerns only a benefit to the holder, not a liability against him. This is difficult. If so, even where ‘firm and established’ appears above, one should fear that an adverse clause was written afterward. The Bach’s reliance on the rarity of writing the validating phrase twice is also insufficient. The Rosh, Tur, and Mechaber appear to speak under the current practice in which the final line is read because the phrase is written. Under the Talmudic rule even an adverse clause in the last line was not read. Accordingly, under our ruling that an adverse clause is read from the final line, this entire concern should no longer apply. The Rama and Ir Shushan therefore require study. In my view, this law does not apply under present practice.
Lest the deed had been there, he cut it, and blank space remained. The Sema and Bach infer that one should never prepare a deed with its witnesses on one line while leaving blank parchment above, unless the blank area is inked over or cut away. I disagree. The Talmud and the authorities validate a deed and its witnesses on one line. We should not suspect the witnesses of having acted improperly. Such a deed is valid because an ordinary deed has side margins. If the holder cuts from the side in order to write above, the absence of a margin will reveal the alteration. The witnesses should nevertheless leave no space at all between the text and their signatures, and a judge who sees an irregular arrangement must investigate carefully.
