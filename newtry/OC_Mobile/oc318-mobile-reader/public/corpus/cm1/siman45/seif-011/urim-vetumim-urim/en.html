We scrutinize the testimony by their oral knowledge. Sema and Perishah explain that in all cases the witnesses must have had the document read by someone capable of reading it. The difference is only whether the judges ask them about that reading. If they know the matter orally, beit din asks whether they had it read; if they do not know it orally, the court does not itself raise the issue and invalidate the deed unless they say on their own that they relied on another person's reading. The Shach disagrees and says that if they know the contents orally, the deed can be valid even if no one read it to them. See Tumim. I conclude that if it is clearly established that they did not have the deed read to them, their oral knowledge alone does not validate it. But absent such proof, the fact that they know the contents creates a presumption that they did have it read. They are not believed to say otherwise where their handwriting is independently authenticated. If they do not know the contents orally, however, they are believed to say that no one read it to them even where their handwriting is independently authenticated. See there.
