When witnesses sign and blank space remains before their signatures, they must take care not to sign one after another on the same line. Otherwise, someone could write whatever he wishes in the blank space, cut off the original upper portion, and produce a document whose text and witnesses occupy one line. They should fill the blank with ink. We do not say that they signed only over the ink blot unless the blot stands between the document and the signatures. If they began signing in the middle of a line and the blank before their signatures was cut away, the document is invalid, because an obligation or condition may have been written there and then removed. If the words 'valid and established' appear above at the end of the preceding line, it is valid, provided the witnesses signed one beneath the other. If they signed side by side, it is invalid, because someone may have cut away a one-line document and its witnesses, leaving blank space above, and then written this document there.
