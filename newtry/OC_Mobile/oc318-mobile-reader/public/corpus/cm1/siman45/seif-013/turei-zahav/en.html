(There: and the Geonim did not distinguish, etc.) It is difficult. Since the reason depends upon the principle that the valid witnesses left space, etc., how do we know that the Geonim disqualify even where the invalid witnesses signed at the end, where that reasoning does not apply? One may answer that our master inferred this from the Geonim's explanation of the case where invalid witnesses signed first. They explain it through the rule that the valid witnesses left space, rather than because the invalid witnesses merely signed as fillers. Necessarily, their formulation is one of "not only." Not only where the invalid witnesses signed at the end is the document clearly valid, because perhaps they signed without the knowledge of the earlier valid witnesses; even where they signed first, one may still say that this occurred without the knowledge of the valid witnesses, because the valid witnesses left space. This is the meaning.
