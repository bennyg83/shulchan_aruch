They remember the testimony. The Shach writes that if disqualified persons were already present at the time of seeing the loan, all the testimony is void even if they never later testified, because joinder at the seeing stage itself can nullify the qualified witnesses. In my view this is not necessarily so for a loan document. Even if all saw the transfer of money together, if the borrower later instructed only the qualified witnesses to sign, the document can rest upon the new obligation created through his formal written undertaking. Disregard the original transfer of money. By instructing qualified witnesses to sign, he creates a valid written obligation even apart from the prior loan, as explained in siman 40. The qualified witnesses then witness his new admission or undertaking, not the earlier viewing shared with disqualified people. Only if he instructed both qualified and disqualified witnesses together to sign do they join at the relevant seeing stage, because his command to sign is itself the witnessing event and their signatures are the testimony. Perhaps this is the Shach's intended case. But where he instructed only the qualified witnesses, the earlier joint observation of the loan does not invalidate the later document.
