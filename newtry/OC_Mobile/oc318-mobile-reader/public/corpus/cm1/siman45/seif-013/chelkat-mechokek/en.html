Only the groom's father sent them. If the groom's father gave the gifts to the messenger in the groom's presence and the groom remained silent, or said, "Take these to the bride in the groom's name," then the father certainly conferred the gifts upon his son for this purpose. Above in siman 35:4 it was explained that where a man revealed to the matchmaker that he desired a certain woman and the matchmaker then betrothed her for him, there is concern. Likewise, in siman 35:5, if the father proposed the matter before the son and the son remained silent, we say that he may have been silent from embarrassment and the father's kiddushin are at least doubtful. Therefore, in a place where most people betroth before gifts, there is plainly concern for kiddushin arranged by the father. Even according to a minority practice there may be concern under the readings of Rabbeinu Chananel. Rivash ruled only that we follow Ravina and do not invent an agency or prior consent without evidence. But where the father actually proposed the matter to the son, we certainly take the gifts seriously.
