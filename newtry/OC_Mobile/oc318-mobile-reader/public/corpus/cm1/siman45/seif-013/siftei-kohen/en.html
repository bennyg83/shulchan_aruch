If the valid witnesses remember. This is from a responsum of the Rosh. The Sema sees a contradiction between two responsa, but there is none. The present ruling concerns a case in which the disqualified witnesses were not present when the loan was seen; they joined only later in signing. If they had also joined in seeing the event with intent to testify, the testimony would be void according to all views, since signatures upon a deed are equivalent to testimony received in beit din. As explained in siman 36, joining in the observation itself may invalidate the testimony even without later testimony. If the disqualified persons were absent from the event but later signed the deed, the document is invalid because all signed together upon one instrument and the instrument is internally defective. Nevertheless, the valid witnesses may testify orally to the underlying transaction, because witnessing the event and signing the defective deed are distinct matters. This is contrary to Maharam Alshakar, who infers from Rambam that the original observation itself was necessarily void.
