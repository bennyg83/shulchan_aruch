And there are witnesses that he signed before reading it, etc. Ramban writes in that responsum: nevertheless he becomes obligated by everything written in it, since he did not trouble to read it and relied on the scribe. Anyone who relies on another person's trustworthiness resolves to bind himself by whatever the person he trusted will say. This is the reason for the law of a third-party custodian as well. Even where the husband says that the get was deposited merely for safekeeping and the custodian says it was deposited for divorce, the custodian is believed. Since the husband knew, when he entrusted the get, that the custodian could deliver it to the woman, he resolved that it should operate as a get if the custodian would say it had been entrusted for divorce. All the more so here, where a person relied on another and did not care to read the document, but signed with his own hand. He thereby resolves to obligate himself by everything written there, or even to create an obligation although he had not previously borrowed, following Rabbi Yochanan's rule that one who writes, 'I owe you a maneh in a document,' becomes obligated even though he had not previously owed it, and we rule like him. End of Ramban's words. From here there is support for our custom and the communal regulation concerning mamranot. There is another proof from what the Tur and Mechaber write above at the end of siman 42: language customarily written in documents, even if it is not an enactment of the Sages but merely language used by ordinary people in that locale, is followed, and even if omitted it is treated as though written. Understand this well.
