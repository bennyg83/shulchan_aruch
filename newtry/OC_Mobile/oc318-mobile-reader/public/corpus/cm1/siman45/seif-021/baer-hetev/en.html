The Sema writes that the same result applies even if they did not explicitly write this, because if there is a visible space between one witness and the other there is no concern that the document will be erased. If it were erased, it would become invalid, because people would assume that the space between the witnesses originally contained a statement that the document was written on ordinary paper. The Sema nevertheless concludes that initially the matter should be clarified by writing the statement explicitly. The Taz also writes that the document is not validated unless they explicitly wrote this. Otherwise we fear that the holder may have witnesses who saw that nothing was originally written between the document and the signatures, and that he himself later created the erasure there. Knowing that, he would not be afraid to falsify the upper portion and convert the document itself into one written on an erased surface.
