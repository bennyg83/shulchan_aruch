Urim VeTumim says that if the witnesses write, 'We witnesses,' they must specify that they testify concerning this particular document, giving its amount, date, lender, and borrower; otherwise perhaps they signed only the notation, just as witnesses can sign only a blot or erasure. In my view this is unnecessary. If witnesses sign directly adjacent to a statement, ordinary practice treats their signatures as referring to the entire document. Otherwise even the common practice of validating erased lines in the line before the final line would fail, because one could always say they signed only that validation sentence. The authorities and universal practice show otherwise. The special rule that witnesses may be deemed to sign only a blot applies where the signatures are physically separated from the deed in an abnormal manner. When they sign immediately after the text, they attest the whole matter. This is especially clear from Tosafot in Bava Batra concerning an 'I, the witnesses' clause placed between signatures. If the wording occupies a line between one witness at the beginning and another at the end, the document can still be protected by arranging the continuation below so that cutting it would reveal the forgery. The ordinary presumption remains that adjacent signatures refer to the entire document.
