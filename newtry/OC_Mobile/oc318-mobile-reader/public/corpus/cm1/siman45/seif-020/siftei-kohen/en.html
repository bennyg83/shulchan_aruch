Therefore they should not sign. The blank area may have been erased once, while the deed itself was erased and rewritten a second time. The witnesses may not notice the difference when they sign, because the difference between a surface erased once and twice becomes clear only after writing over it has dried, by which time they have left. Tosafot explain that according to Riva. According to Rivam the difference is immediately visible to a careful beit din, but not necessarily to witnesses who do not examine it closely.
If there is space between one witness’s signature and the other. Tosafot and Hagahot Ashri invalidate according to Riva. Rivam, however, validates when the area does not visibly differ from the rest, because even before new writing is added one can distinguish an erasure made once from one made twice. Rivash cites Ramah similarly: the blank area between the signatures must match the deed and signatures in the number of erasures. Otherwise one fears that the deed was originally on paper, the signatures on an erasure, and the holder erased both the deed and the intervening declaration. The plain language of the Gemara appears to support this view, and it is surprising that the Mechaber records only the Tur’s stricter position without noting the major dissenters. Their view appears primary, though such documents are uncommon.
