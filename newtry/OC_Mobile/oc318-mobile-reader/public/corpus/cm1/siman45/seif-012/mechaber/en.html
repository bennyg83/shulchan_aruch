If many witnesses signed a document and one is found related or otherwise disqualified, or two are related to one another, and the witnesses are no longer available to be questioned, then if clear testimony establishes that all assembled to sign as witnesses and intended to testify, the document is void. If not, the testimony may be established through the remaining witnesses, because the valid witnesses may have signed while leaving space for a distinguished person, after which the related or disqualified person signed without their knowledge. The document is valid even if the first signature is that of the disqualified witness. {Rama: Ideally, no related or disqualified person should sign a document (Tur). Some say a document containing disqualified signatures is valid only if its final two witnesses are fit (Tur in the name of his father, the Rosh).}
