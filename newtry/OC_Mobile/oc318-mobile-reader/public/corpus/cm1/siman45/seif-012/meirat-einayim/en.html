And the witnesses are no longer alive so that we can ask them, etc. Beit Yosef infers from Rambam's wording that if the witnesses are alive, they must be asked, and the witnesses themselves are believed to say that all of them sat together to sign, even if their handwriting is authenticated from another source. This is not considered retracting from prior testimony. If they say they did not all sit together to sign, while other witnesses testify that they did, there are two witnesses against two and the money remains in the possession of its current owner. Darkei Moshe cites this in seif 18.
For it is possible that they signed, etc. This applies where they signed one beneath another. But if all signed on one line, we certainly say that they intended full testimony. So writes Nimukei Yosef in chapter Get Pashut, cited by Darkei Moshe, seif 16.
Even though the witness signed on the document first, etc. Meaning, one might have thought that the first signer is certainly among those who originally came to sign and therefore joined with the valid witnesses. The ruling teaches otherwise: we say that the valid witnesses were the principal signers and left space, after which the disqualified witness came and signed there. All the more so if the disqualified witnesses signed after the valid ones, the document is valid. This is the view of the Geonim cited by the Tur in seif 18.
Where disqualified witnesses are signed on it. See Darkei Moshe, seif 4, and Beit Yosef, seif 19, who cite many opinions. Where disqualified witnesses signed in the middle, with valid witnesses before and after them, if we do not know the order in which the signatures were affixed the document is invalid. If we know that they signed in the same order in which the signatures appear, the document is valid, since the first witnesses are valid. By contrast, if all the first witnesses are disqualified, the document is ordinarily presumed valid because we say the disqualified witnesses signed merely to fill space after the valid witnesses had already signed. But if it is known that they signed in the order appearing, the document is invalid. See Beit Yosef.
Unless the last two witnesses are valid. We then attribute the matter to the possibility that the valid witnesses deliberately left space before their signatures so that an elder among them could sign there, and the lender subsequently filled the space with these relatives or disqualified witnesses, as I write nearby in seif 14. This excludes Ramban and Mordechai in chapter Get Pashut in the name of Raavyah, who require witnesses to establish that the disqualified signatures were added merely to fill space. The Tur concludes from this: therefore, when a document has many witnesses, one authenticates it only from its lower witnesses. See there. Nimukei Yosef in chapter Get Pashut writes that even if the final two valid witnesses signed side by side, the document is valid. So cites Darkei Moshe, seif 14.
