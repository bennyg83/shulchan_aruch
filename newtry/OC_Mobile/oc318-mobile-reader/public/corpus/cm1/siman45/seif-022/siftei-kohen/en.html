Even if we, the witnesses, wrote, etc. Meaning, even if they wrote that statement on the paper before signing, and such a document now comes before us, so that there certainly was no actual forgery in this document, it is nevertheless invalid because ultimately the holder could erase it, etc. This cannot mean that the witnesses wrote the statement between the signature of one witness and the other, because in that case the document would be invalid regardless. Perhaps the document was originally written entirely on plain paper, the first witness signed, and afterward the entire document was erased, while the second witness wrote the statement after the first witness’s signature. The first witness would then not be assenting to it, and the document would be validated only by one witness, which is nothing. This differs from the preceding seif, where what was written between the witnesses was for the borrower’s protection, so that the lender could not forge the document. Here it is for the lender’s benefit, and it is plainly ineffective when supported by only one witness. Otherwise, every lender could, after one witness signs, forge whatever he wishes in the document, certify it between the two witnesses, and then collude with the second witness. Rather, it must be as I have written. On this basis I rejected above, at the end of siman 44, the proofs of Rashba. See there.
