The Sema writes that when the witnesses signed one beneath the other, there is no concern that someone will write before each witness, “So-and-so borrowed from so-and-so,” because two witnesses appearing in two separate documents, each on his own document, or in two documents of this form, do not combine. One might ask that there is still concern that he will forge a separate admission above each witness's signature, writing before each one, “I owe so-and-so.” The answer is that for this reason we do not invalidate the document, because the witnesses caused their own loss by beginning their signatures in the middle of the line. We invalidate a document only where there is concern that a forgery will enable him to extract money from others. The Shach disputes the Sema's statement that two witnesses on two documents do not combine, since in siman 51 we rule the opposite, that they combine at least to collect from unencumbered property. Rather, the reason is that since each document contains only one line, the forgery will be recognizable; two documents of this sort, each containing only one line, do not combine. This is the principal explanation.
