If the deed ends in the middle of a line, it is invalid because one can write in the remainder. The Bach says that this applies only under Talmudic practice, but today, when ‘firm and established’ is written, ending in midline is harmless. This is incorrect. We rule that two occurrences of that phrase are valid when written before the final line. A holder could therefore add material in the unused half-line and then write the phrase again. This explains why the Tur and Mechaber declare the deed invalid without distinguishing whether the phrase appears. The Bach’s citation from Hagahot Ashri concerns a different case, where witnesses begin signing in the middle of the line one beside the other. Since witnesses ordinarily sign only after the validating phrase and only one occurrence is present, the half-line and the preceding full line may then be used. The Semag states this explicitly. The Bach’s interpretation is therefore untenable.
It is invalid if they sign one after the other, but valid if they sign one beneath the other. Even in the valid case, no inference is drawn from the final half-line or from the complete line above it. The Darkei Moshe and Bach understood the Rosh and Tur to disagree with Rashbatz and Ramban, who validate reliance on those lines when the witnesses signed side by side, because witnesses would not intentionally sign in midline and create a deed consisting of its text and witnesses on one line. Their words are astonishing. The Rosh and Tur discuss signatures one beneath the other, which witnesses can properly make, so the preceding lines remain unusable. Rashbatz and Ramban expressly discuss signatures side by side. In that case the deed would otherwise be invalid, and we therefore know that the upper half-line already contained text when they signed. Thus the authorities agree, and the ruling of Rashbatz and Ramban is correct.
If they sign one after the other. If they sign one beneath the other, the deed is valid. The Sema and Ir Shushan explain that two witnesses appearing in two separate deeds do not combine. That is not fully correct, for below, siman 51, we rule that they may combine at least to collect from unencumbered property. Rather, because each resulting instrument would contain only one line, any alteration would be recognizable, and two such one-line documents do not combine. The words of the Rosh and Tur can also be understood this way. This appears to be the correct explanation.
