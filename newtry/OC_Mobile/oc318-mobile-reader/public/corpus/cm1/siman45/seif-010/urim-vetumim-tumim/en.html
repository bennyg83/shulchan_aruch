If they sign one after another, etc. Sema says that if the witnesses sign one beneath the other, there is no concern that the holder will create two separate one-witness documents, because two documents each bearing one witness do not combine. Shach objects that below in siman 51 we rule they can combine. He answers that in this particular form, where each supposed document contains only one witness in a visibly manipulated one-line structure, the forgery is evident and the two documents do not combine. This reading is supported by Tosafot. In Bava Batra 164b, Tosafot appear to accept that two separate documents each containing one witness can in some cases combine. Yet at the beginning of Perek Get Pashut they say that where witnesses sign one beneath another there is no concern the holder will make a one-line document with one witness. The only way to reconcile these statements is Shach's distinction: where the very form reveals the manipulation, the one-witness papers do not combine. There is another possible route. As I explain below, Tosafot may hold that a one-witness document can combine only when one additional witness later appears or when the single written witness is properly established. If the available space here is too small to create a complete second attestation, the concern does not arise. There is also support from Tosafot in Bava Batra 161a. They say, in one answer, that the first witnesses may smear ink in the lower margin outside the document so that no one can later write a new document there and turn a witness into the apparent borrower through his handwriting. That discussion assumes the witnesses signed vertically, one under another, rather than side by side. Otherwise a same-line document concern would look different. This also helps resolve Maharsha's question there. Still, Rama below in siman 46 rules that a single witness signed in a document, even when his handwriting is independently authenticated, may need to remember his testimony orally. If so, there is less reason to fear forged one-witness documents. A witness to a fabricated paper will not remember an event that never happened. Likewise, Rama later cites Rivash that one witness signed in a document can sometimes retract even after his handwriting is authenticated. A forger should therefore be afraid to create a false one-witness document because the witness can deny its contents. Tosafot themselves suggest in another context that a forger fears fabricating a whole new document lest the witnesses contradict him. The later authorities reject that concern for a two-witness document because once the signatures are fixed the witnesses cannot simply retract. But with one witness, where retraction remains possible, the deterrent is stronger. Thus the practical fraud concern is weaker than it first appears. In my view, however, the simplest meaning of "one beneath another" is not that the signatures are perfectly aligned vertically. It means they occupy two separate lines. The first witness may sign toward the middle of the second line, while the second signs below toward the beginning of the next line. There is then no usable horizontal strip in which a forged text can be written above both signatures as though they attested to it. This physical layout eliminates the concern entirely. The law should therefore be understood according to the actual placement and available blank space, not merely the phrase "one beneath another" in an abstract geometric sense.
