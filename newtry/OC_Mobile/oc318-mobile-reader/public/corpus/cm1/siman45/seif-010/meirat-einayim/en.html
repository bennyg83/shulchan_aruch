And they began signing on the third line, etc. In this case it makes no difference whether they signed one under the other or side by side, since they began signing at the beginning of the line.
It is invalid, because he can write, etc. It appears that in such a case the entire document is invalid even if it comes before beit din for collection while nothing has yet been written in the blank line and a half. It was capable of forgery in a manner that would not be detectable. This is analogous to the invalidity in the next clause, where they left one full line blank and on the following line began signing in the middle, side by side. There too the entire document is invalid when presented for collection, because one could cut it and fabricate a document with its witnesses on one line, as the conclusion states and as Rama writes in siman 44, end of seif 3. This differs from what the Mechaber and Rama write where they left a blank line and a half and then the witnesses began signing in the second half-line one beneath the other. There, the entire document is not invalid. Only anything written in the final blank line and a half is disregarded. The difference is that in that configuration it is impossible to fabricate a document with its witnesses on one line, since the witnesses signed one beneath the other. Nor would anything useful be gained by adding text in the blank space, because the rule that we do not learn from the bottom line refers to one entire continuous line, and therefore in such a case we would likewise disregard the entire line above the witnesses, all the more so the half-line below it before the signatures. But where a half-line is blank and after it there is another complete blank line, a forgery would not be detectable and we would improperly rely on the first half-line. See what I write further in Sema to siman 44, seif 3.
If they sign one after another, etc. But if they signed one beneath the other, there is no concern that he will write before each witness, 'So-and-so borrowed from So-and-so,' because two witnesses appearing on two separate instruments, each by himself, or on two lines in such a manner, do not combine. One might ask that there is still concern that he will fabricate a claim against each individual witness by writing before each signature, 'I owe So-and-so a maneh.' The answer is that for this reason we do not invalidate the document, because the witnesses caused that risk to themselves by beginning to sign in the middle of a line. A document is invalidated only where there is concern that a forgery could be used to extract money from others. So write Tosafot and the Rosh in chapter Get Pashut. See Perishah.
And then we learn from the half-line, etc. The reason is that we maintain the witnesses in their presumption of proper conduct. Had they not seen that the document text also occupied the half-line preceding their signatures, they would not have signed in the middle of the line, since doing so would enable someone to fabricate a document with its witnesses on one line.
