Seif 7. “But he cannot claim that the matter never occurred.” See Shach, seif katan 14, who explains in the name of Rabbeinu Yonah that this testimony was capable of being rendered valid, for example by filling the blank with signatures of relatives or with repeated wording. At first glance this is difficult. That corrective measure is effective only before the document's defect has been seen by beit din; afterward it does not help. Yet regarding a predated document Shach himself rules above that it may be validated if, before its defect is seen by beit din, an endorsement is written limiting collection to the proper later date. The distinction appears to be that in the predated document, the testimony written in the document that the transaction occurred on the earlier date can never itself be made valid. Even if an endorsement is added before the document reaches beit din, stating that collection may begin only from the later date, that changes the substance of the original testimony and effectively creates a new document from the later time. The original testimony that the event occurred on the earlier date remains false and cannot be cured. Therefore one cannot collect even from the debtor on the strength of that defective testimony. Here, however, there is no change in the substance of the testimony. The testimony appearing in the document can be rendered valid exactly as it stands, without any alteration to the witnesses' substantive statement, by filling the blank in a permissible manner. Therefore the testimony itself is valid enough to collect from the debtor even before the blank has actually been filled. This is a sound and clear distinction.
