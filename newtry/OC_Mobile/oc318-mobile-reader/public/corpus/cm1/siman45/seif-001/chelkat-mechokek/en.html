Even though he did not send the gifts in the presence of witnesses. At first glance this means there were no witnesses and the parties merely admit that he sent bridal gifts. One should then ask: if they admit only the gifts but deny prior kiddushin, why do we not apply the rule that the same mouth that creates the prohibition removes it? It appears that this would be a migo against witnesses, because established local practice itself testifies that kiddushin ordinarily precede such gifts. One cannot explain the case as lacking witnesses to delivery but having witnesses to the sending, because even according to Rashi that would generate concern, as stated nearby. See Terumat HaDeshen, siman 207: where the local custom is to betroth before sending gifts, according to Rabbeinu Tam a party who claims contrary to the custom is not believed even by a migo.
