The witnesses sign below. If witnesses first sign their names on blank paper and only afterward the document is written above their signatures, the document is invalid because the writing must precede the signatures. Tosafot in Gittin says that one reason not to permit signatures written without proper intent would be concern that people might first sign and afterward write a get over their signatures, where there is no testimony at all. Tosafot adds that the same concern would exist for ordinary documents. Thus when the witnesses signed blank paper, their signatures related to nothing. Later writing does not revive those signatures. The same should apply to a person's own signature. If he first signs blank paper and someone later writes above it, "My field is acquired to you," the signature does not effect that conveyance because the signature preceded the writing. The Gemara's case of a document reading, "I, so and so son of so and so, borrowed from you a maneh," does not prove otherwise. There the entire text can be in the borrower's handwriting, and his handwritten statement is independently effective even without a separate signature, as explained at the beginning of siman 69. Indeed, this supports the view that a handwritten obligation need not contain a separate name signature. There remains a difficulty from the institution of a folded get, where witnesses sign on the reverse after each line is folded, apparently before the entire operative text, including names, has been written. According to the opinion that validation may rely on the upper signatures, how can those signatures be effective if they preceded later portions of the text? The matter requires resolution.
