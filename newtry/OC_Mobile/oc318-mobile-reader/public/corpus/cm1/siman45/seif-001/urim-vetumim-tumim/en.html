And not on the sides. Sema explains that signatures on the side are invalid because they create an opportunity for forgery. Shach objects from Tosafot at the beginning of Perek Get Pashut. Tosafot indicate that even where no realistic forgery is possible, a signature placed on the side is not regarded as a signature upon the document at all. Shach is correct regarding the immediate halachic effect. Nevertheless, Sema can be understood as explaining why Chazal created the form requirement in the first place. Ordinarily, side signatures do create greater possibilities of alteration and forgery. For that reason Chazal established that witnesses must sign in the recognized position. Once they enacted that formal rule, they did not distinguish between a case where actual forgery is possible and one where it is not. A side signature then lacks documentary status because it violates the established rabbinic form. Without the underlying forgery concern, Chazal may never have enacted the location requirement. This is comparable to many document rules where the original concern is fraud but, after enactment, the formal rule applies uniformly. Thus Sema and Shach can largely be reconciled. The operative law follows Shach: a side signature is invalid even without present forgery risk. Sema explains the reason Chazal established that rule.
