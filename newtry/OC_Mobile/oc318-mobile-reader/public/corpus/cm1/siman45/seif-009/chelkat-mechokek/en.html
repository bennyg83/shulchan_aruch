And some are stringent if he sent them in the presence of witnesses, even though, etc. The reason is that witnesses need not actually see the precise moment the kiddushin take effect. The Gemara says that witnesses to seclusion are witnesses to relations, even though the relations themselves effect the kiddushin and the witnesses do not see them, because seeing the seclusion from which relations will follow is sufficient. Similarly, where messengers are sent with bridal gifts in a context where they signify kiddushin, we may assume they carried out their mission and delivered the gifts as instructed. Terumat HaDeshen cites Ri and Rabbi Eliezer of Metz as holding that in a place with a fixed practice of sending gifts in this manner, no witnesses are needed at all. The public custom itself makes it certain that the gifts were sent as kiddushin. In this respect bridal gifts can be stronger evidence than an ordinary act of kiddushin without witnesses, because here the communal practice supplies the equivalent of witnessing.
