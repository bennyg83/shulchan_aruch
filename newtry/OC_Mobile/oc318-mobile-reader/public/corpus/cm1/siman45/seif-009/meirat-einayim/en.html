A lamed on the upper line and a final kaf on the lower line. This is the Gemara's example according to the opinion requiring three spaces. The Tur rules likewise. The third space need not have a special marker, because whenever two written lines appear one beneath the other there is naturally a space between them. Ir Shushan writes an unnecessarily elaborate example, with a lamed and final kaf on the upper line and another final kaf on the lower line directly beneath the upper final kaf. Perhaps he did so because the Gemara, according to the opinion requiring two lines and four spaces, gives the example 'lekh lekh,' one above the other, where the lower lamed lies under the upper final kaf. Under our ruling that three spaces suffice, however, there is no need for the lower lamed to be beneath the upper final kaf. A final kaf beneath a final kaf is enough. The expression is not exact.
