(Collected note) And if they do not understand, etc. So writes the Tur, Even HaEzer, siman 130. The Beit Yosef there writes that the Rambam and Rashba disagree and that this appears from Gittin 11a and 19b: "but they do not know how to read," where the Gemara answers that they do know how to read and does not answer that it was translated before them. If one says the anonymous Gemara follows Rava, consistent with the Rif and Rambam, that is fine. But according to the Tur and Rav, who rule like Rav Gamda, this is impossible, for then Ameimar and the anonymous Gemara would follow Rava and the halacha should follow them. The Beit Yosef also brings proof from the Yerushalmi at the end of Gittin concerning one witness in Hebrew and one in Greek: Rav said judges may sign even though they cannot read, but witnesses may not sign unless they can read. The Mishnah apparently contradicts Rav: "a get written in Hebrew with witnesses in Greek, etc." The Yerushalmi resolves that they knew how to read but not how to sign, or knew both and chose whichever language they wished. At first glance this is strong proof even for the Rif's approach that follows Rava, because regarding a get all agree the witnesses need not be able to read, yet the Yerushalmi challenges Rav from a get. But this is not so, because the Yerushalmi must hold that Rav's rule applies even to a get; otherwise why challenge Rav? On the contrary, there is proof from there for the Tur's law and the Rav here: the challenge to Rav implies that without Rav's rule the Mishnah would be understood readily, and we do not rule like Rav. The Babylonian Gemara cited above, however, follows Rava and supports the Rif and Shulchan Aruch's ruling, which is the principal view. Still, this particular law is clear: wherever they may read through translation, translation is equivalent to reading. So writes the Shach. This has practical relevance for a get. (End of collected note.)
