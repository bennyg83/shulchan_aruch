If the head of a beit din knows the subject of a document and his own scribe reads it before him, he may sign it without personally reading it, because he trusts the scribe and the scribe is in awe of him. Other people, including a head of beit din using another scribe, may not do this; the witness must read the document word for word. {Rama: Some say two people should read it before the witnesses, who then sign. If the witnesses do not understand the document's language, those reading it translate it for them and they sign; this appears to me to be the primary view. Some say it may be read before a congregation and then signed, because people fear lying before the public, which is comparable to the head of a beit din and his scribe.}
