Even though he himself did not read it, etc. This is not testimony from one witness through another. The case is where the head of the beit din saw the testimony, and this scribe merely informs him what is written in the document. Since this is a matter that is readily discoverable, concerning which people do not lie, the scribe was trusted even though the judge did not read it himself, because the judge's authority over him inspires fear. So explains Rashi.
Until the witness reads it, etc. Beit Yosef writes that according to Rambam and those who follow him, even if two people read it before the witness casually, that is ineffective because the witness has no fear of them. The Rosh and those who follow him, however, hold that two are believed even if they are not speaking casually. They are the 'some say' cited by Rama. See Derishah for what I wrote further about this.
