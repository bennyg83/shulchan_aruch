Seif 13. If the document became invalid because of signatures of disqualified witnesses, but the valid witnesses remember the testimony by looking at the document. See Sema and Shach. In my sixth edition to Even HaEzer, siman 98, in a responsum to the community of Rimalov, I defended the position of the authorities. I concluded that where the document was executed with the lender's or beneficiary's consent, their voluntary joining of all the witnesses together causes the testimony to be entirely void, and the valid witnesses cannot later return and testify separately. But where the witnesses signed without the lender's or beneficiary's knowledge, their joining cannot be treated as an intentional combination on his behalf. The invalid signatures are therefore regarded as nonexistent for purposes of the document, but the underlying testimony itself is not void, since there was no valid joint seeing and testifying arranged by the beneficiary. See there. The distinction is logically sound. I also considered there whether, in matters for which testimony is required only rabbinically, the rule that discovery of one relative or disqualified witness invalidates the entire set applies at all; see there.
