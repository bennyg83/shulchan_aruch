We rule above that when the deed ends in the middle of a line and the remaining half-line and the line below are left blank, the deed is invalid. How can we suspect that the witnesses acted in precisely this invalid manner? The Ir Shushan mistakenly says that the earlier case was declared valid. The Sema answers that the irregular beginning before us creates the suspicion, but an irregularity does not justify assuming that witnesses signed an invalid deed. The source is Avi HaEzri, who validates a line and a half of space only where ‘firm and established’ ended in the middle of the upper line. He relies upon the view that the phrase may not be written twice, even before the final line, so no forgery can be added after it. Under our ruling that the phrase may be repeated before the last line, the concern does not arise in that way. We should presume that the witnesses did not leave forgeable blank space. Accordingly, the deed should be valid, and the Rama’s invalidation requires study.
