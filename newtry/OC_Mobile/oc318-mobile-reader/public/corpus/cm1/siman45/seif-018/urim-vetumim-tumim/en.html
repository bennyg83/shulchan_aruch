That the first line does not begin, etc. Shach, seif katan 29, leaves Rama's ruling difficult because of Sema's objection. Witnesses should not be allowed to leave half a line and the following line blank. Even if "valid and confirmed" already appears, the holder could write whatever he wishes in the blank space and then write "valid and confirmed" a second time. Rama can be defended through a basic rule of document measurement. All blank-space calculations are made according to the handwriting actually used in the document. If the document is written in large, thick letters, the measure of two lines is assessed with that same writing style. We do not assume a forger will switch to unusually small script, because the alteration would be visibly apparent. Tosafot say this explicitly in Bava Batra 163b, and I explained the principle above in siman 44. Suppose the document uses especially broad handwriting. The remaining half-line may be too small, according to that writing style, to contain both a favorable addition and another full "valid and confirmed." Then the witnesses may safely leave it blank once the original closing formula has already been written. If the holder later inserts favorable words without another closing formula, they accomplish nothing. If he tries to include a second closing formula, there is simply not enough room in the established script. If he switches to much smaller handwriting in order to squeeze both into the space, the forgery is obvious. Now suppose we see a document where the upper half-line has been cut away. One might say the holder merely trimmed the page. But in fact the abnormal cut itself is a defect. Documents are not ordinarily shaped with an unexplained half-line missing. Since the writing throughout the instrument is broad, while the surviving space could accommodate only unusually small script, the physical irregularity creates suspicion that the holder altered the paper and compressed new writing into the remaining area. Thus Rama's rule is understandable. It does not authorize witnesses to leave a dangerous ordinary blank. It speaks of a gap that is harmless relative to the actual script of the document but becomes suspicious if later cut or rewritten in a different script size. Accordingly, Rama's words are sound.
