And they cut out for them a blank paper. This is the Gemara's wording in Gittin. Rabbeinu Chananel explains that they take a sheet of paper and cut through it the shapes of the witnesses' names, place it over the parchment on which the get is written, and the witnesses fill the cutouts with ink so that the ink passes through the openings and forms their signatures on the get. This was permitted for a get because of concern for agunah. With other documents it is forbidden. But merely tracing the shape of the witnesses' signatures with guide marks and having the witnesses fill those traced lines with ink is forbidden even for a get, because it is writing over writing. So writes the Tur in Even HaEzer 130. See Derishah for more.
He is given disciplinary lashes. In Even HaEzer 130 the Tur writes, 'the judge who causes witnesses to sign this way in other documents is lashed,' and the Gemara also appears to refer to the judge, as I explained in Perishah. The Mechaber's language somewhat supports this, because he begins in the plural, 'they signed,' and concludes in the singular, 'he is beaten.' This is also logical, because the witnesses may be simple people who do not know the practice is forbidden. Why should they be lashed? Yet in some editions of Rambam and in a large edition of the Shulchan Aruch the text reads 'they are beaten,' in the plural, and the plain language of the Tur and Shulchan Aruch can also be understood as referring to the witnesses.
