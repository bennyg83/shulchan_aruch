They cut out paper for them. The Sema follows Rabbeinu Chananel: one cuts the witnesses’ names through a sheet, places it over the parchment, and the ink passes through the openings to form the signatures. This was permitted for a get because of the danger of leaving a woman unable to remarry, but prohibited for other deeds. Merely tracing the shape of the signatures and having the witnesses write over the tracing is invalid even for a get, because it is writing over writing. This is also the Ran’s objection to Rashi’s explanation and is cited by the Tur in Even HaEzer 130. Even so, Rambam and the Mechaber need not be read exclusively this way, for their wording can include both methods. Since they rule here that the deed is invalid after the fact, they may refer to any method in which the witness does not independently form his own signature.
They strike them. This refers to the witnesses. Some texts read ‘him,’ leading the Sema to explain it as referring to the judge who authenticated the document. That cannot be correct, since the Tur and Mechaber have mentioned no judge here. The proper reading is ‘them,’ meaning the witnesses.
