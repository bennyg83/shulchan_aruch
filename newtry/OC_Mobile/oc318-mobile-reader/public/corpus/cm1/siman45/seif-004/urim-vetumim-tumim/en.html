And they signed by means of the tracing, etc. Sema explains the case as follows. They cut the shapes of the letters out of another sheet, place that sheet over the document, and the witnesses fill the cut-out letter shapes with ink. After the covering sheet is removed, the witnesses' signatures remain on the document. This can be valid for gittin but invalid for ordinary documents. If, however, the letters were merely traced directly on the document and the witnesses then wrote over that tracing, Sema says it is invalid even in a get because it is writing over writing. According to Sema, even the authorities cited in Rama who validate certain traced signatures would agree that such direct over-writing is invalid. See Shach, seif katan 6, who discusses several distinctions at length. In my view that discussion can be shortened. Even if we say that in a get the direct tracing is invalid because the Torah requires actual writing and writing over writing may not count as writing, that concern is specific to gittin. For ordinary monetary documents, the Torah does not require the witnesses to create liability through a formal act of "writing." The witnesses are needed so that falsehood will not prevail. If their markings objectively establish that they attested to the transaction, what difference does it make whether the technical act is classified as fresh writing? Moreover, the Gemara itself says that for Shabbat such over-writing can be considered writing. The Gemara is merely reluctant to rely upon analogy to permit a get because of the severity of marital law. In monetary law, where that special Torah requirement is absent, we can rely on the ordinary legal effect of the mark. Accordingly, according to the lenient authorities, even this direct tracing can be valid in monetary documents.
