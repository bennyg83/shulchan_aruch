If they sign one after another, etc. Then the entire deed is invalid, because one can cut away everything above and produce a new document with a witness and supporting witness in the final line. But if they sign one beneath the other, the entire deed is not invalid. The only concern is the half-line before the signatures and the full line immediately above, from which we do not derive, because half a line may have been left for a senior witness and a full line may have been left as ordinary spacing. If, however, the closing phrase is written, we may derive from that line, because witnesses normally write the closing phrase and here it did not appear until the end of the half-line before their signatures. That shows the document text continued to the signatures and there is no forgery concern. Shach, seif katan 15. See Tumim for why one need not fear that, although the signatures are one beneath the other, someone could use each as a separate one-witness document and later combine the two one-witness documents.
