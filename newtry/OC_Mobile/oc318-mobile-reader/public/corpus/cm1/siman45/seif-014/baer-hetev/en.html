On his own initiative. The Sema writes: It appears obvious that according to this view the creditor may, on his own initiative, fill the remaining signature spaces with relatives even after the date on which the document was signed, so long as the document has not yet appeared before beit din. This is also evident from the Rosh. If so, it is difficult that in seif 15 the Mechaber writes without qualification contrary to the Rosh. It therefore appears that the two paragraphs are really one seif, and a conjunction should be inserted so that it reads, “And that which we validate...” The Shach writes that this is unconvincing. Rather, all authorities agree to the distinction: although the creditor may fill the spaces without the borrower's express consent, because delivery of the document to him is equivalent to authorization to do so, the document does not become valid until the time the spaces are actually filled. The Taz likewise writes that the rule in seif 15 follows all opinions, and this is the ruling we adopt.
