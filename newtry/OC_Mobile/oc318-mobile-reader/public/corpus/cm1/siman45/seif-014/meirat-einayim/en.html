Without the borrower's knowledge, it is valid. No inherent invalidity exists merely because the lender has no authority to convert it into a document without the borrower's knowledge. For this reason it appears clear that the lender may fill the space with relatives on his own initiative even after the day of signing, so long as the document has not yet appeared before beit din. This is implied by the Rosh, as I explained in Perishah. The Mechaber's later statement in seif 15 that if they filled it the next day it is invalid was written only according to the 'some say' cited at the end of the present seif, who require the filling to be with the borrower's knowledge and who hold that the document remains in a state of invalidity until that is done. According to that view, if it is filled without the borrower's knowledge he was never bound by it. That is Rabbi Yonah's view, followed by Maggid Mishneh. Beit Yosef, however, writes that the Rosh's contrary view is primary. It is therefore difficult that the Mechaber later rules without qualification against the Rosh. It seems rather that all those words form one continuous clause connected to the preceding 'some say,' and the text should read, 'And the rule that we validate it...,' with a connecting vav.
