He has the same standing to collect, etc. The wording implies that the lender who comes to collect derives his right from the non-Jew, even though that gives him an advantage. Yet in siman 154:18 the Mechaber rules that one who comes through a non-Jew has the non-Jew's status only to his detriment. One must say the present case differs because a debt instrument can involve dina demalchuta, as Rama writes at the end of siman 369 and similarly in siman 154. Perhaps the Mechaber also agrees where dina demalchuta applies. An even better distinction is this. Here the non-Jews commonly collect on such a document, and the document itself proves the Jew owes the money. Even between Jews collection would essentially be possible, since we see there is no actual forgery; the document is invalidated among Jews only because it is susceptible to forgery. With a non-Jew's document the Sages did not institute that technical invalidity to deprive the person who comes through the non-Jew. In siman 154, by contrast, a person had possessed a window for many years and one who purchased from a non-Jew seeks actively to harm him by building opposite it and darkening it. Preventing the construction may cause the purchaser a loss, but that is only indirect. This distinction also reconciles the adjacent rulings there. For the same reason the Mechaber writes below in siman 66:25 that when a non-Jew sells a debt document to a Jew, some say that because under their law he sold all his rights, we adjudicate the purchaser according to their law and the non-Jew can no longer forgive the debt. Once he sold the debt document, he cannot directly harm the purchaser by forgiving it. Likewise, Rama in siman 67:17 writes that one who comes through a non-Jew has the non-Jew's status, and therefore shemittah does not cancel a debt document purchased from a non-Jew. There too, the Jewish borrower was already obligated to pay the non-Jew, and the borrower's position is not worsened by the Jew's purchase of the document. Thus everyone can agree in cases of this type that the purchaser receives the non-Jew's strength.
