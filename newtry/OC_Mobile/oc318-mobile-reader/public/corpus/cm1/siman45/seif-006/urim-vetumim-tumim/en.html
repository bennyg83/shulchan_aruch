And if they left a space of two lines, etc. The Sema and Beit Yosef explain that the problem is that the holder could cut off the entire document above and write a new document in the blank space. The Shach says this reason is unnecessary, because even where the document ends with "valid and established," the holder could erase that phrase, add whatever he wishes in the second blank line, validate the erasure, and write a new "valid and established" at the end of the last line. This difficulty is not only against the Sema and Beit Yosef, but also against Tosafos in Bava Batra 162, who explain that the practice of writing "valid and established" and relying on the final line was instituted because sometimes two blank lines are left. Maharsha asks how the phrase helps, since the holder could erase it, add material in the second line, and then write a new concluding phrase. Maharsha answers that according to that approach, if an erasure appears in the place where "valid and established" should have been, the document is invalid even if the erasure is in the third line. This resolves the point. Tosafos and the earlier authorities were explaining why the Sages chose to invalidate a two line gap instead of adopting the opposite arrangement, namely validating such a document but invalidating any erasure in the place of the concluding formula, even where it occurs in the third line, and certainly invalidating a document containing two concluding formulas, one in the third line and another in the first. Such a rule might have seemed preferable because witnesses would not need to be so exact about leaving no more than one blank line. Tosafos therefore explain that there remains a different concern: the holder may cut off the entire upper document and create a new document, as Tosafos themselves state at the end of the discussion. For that reason a two line gap must itself invalidate the document.
And even if he covered it with ink, etc. The Sema explains that the witnesses testify that the area was covered with ink in their presence, even if it is merely random ink scratches. The Shach asks how such covering can help, since perhaps the area originally contained language detrimental to the holder and he erased it. This difficulty also applies to Rashbam, who explicitly explains the case this way in Bava Batra 163. According to Rashbam, however, we rule that nothing is learned from the final line, even if it is detrimental to the holder. There is therefore no difficulty. No one needs to erase both blank lines. It is enough to erase the last line adjacent to the document while leaving the line adjacent to the witnesses blank. What concern remains that the erased line originally contained detrimental language? Nothing would have been learned from the final line in any event. One might still object that the document is susceptible to forgery because the holder could write in the erased line and then validate that writing in the final line before adding "valid and established." But if the erasure is in the line before the last line and is large enough to contain the concluding phrase, nothing written there is relied upon, so there is no concern. Even according to those who hold that detrimental material may be learned from the final line, the problem can be resolved. According to Rashba and those who hold that an erasure in the body of the standard form does not create a presumption that detrimental language was removed, there is no difficulty. Even according to the opposing authorities, one can reverse the arrangement: erase the line adjacent to the witnesses and leave the line adjacent to the document blank. It is then apparent that nothing was written in the last line of the original document, because the preceding line was blank, and people do not ordinarily leave a blank line in the middle of a document. If one fears that the holder may later write in the blank line, nothing can be learned from the final line according to these authorities unless the concluding formula is present, and if he writes one the document will then reveal the suspicious erasure. The only remaining remote concern is that the erased line adjacent to the witnesses originally contained an entire one line document and signatures, with the present witnesses added merely as extra witnesses, after which the holder erased that entire prior document and wrote the current one above it. Tosafos in Bava Batra 165 indicate that witnesses do not sign a one line document while leaving blank space above in a way that would enable such a forgery. See below, seif 11. The matter is therefore resolved.
