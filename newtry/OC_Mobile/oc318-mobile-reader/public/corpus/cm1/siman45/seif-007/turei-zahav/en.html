(Seif 19: If the document and its witnesses are on one line, it is valid.) Maharshal asked why the Sages validated this form and therefore forbade witnesses to sign consecutively in the middle of a line. Why did they not enact the reverse, that a document whose text and witnesses appear on one line is invalid, thereby allowing witnesses to sign in the middle of a line, just as they enacted invalidity when one line separates the witnesses from the text? There is no difficulty. Neither rule is an enactment. When the witnesses sign on a separate line, they cannot ordinarily place their signatures exactly against the end of the writing; leaving one line therefore involves no unusual conduct, and the document is invalid because that space permits forgery. But when a document comes before us with its text and witnesses on one line, it is valid by law. Why should we create a new invalidity and suspect that the witnesses departed from normal practice by signing in the middle of a line? The matter is plain.
