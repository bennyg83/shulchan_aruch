And every concern arising from bridal gifts, etc. This wording implies that even according to Rashi, where concern exists only in a place where most people betroth before sending gifts, if she later marries another she need not leave him. But one who studies the responsum will see that this rule was derived from Tosafot, who compare concern for a minority who betroth first according to Rabbeinu Chananel with the minority of survivors in the law of waters without a visible end. Therefore, in both cases she need not leave a second husband after the fact. Even though the minority of survivors is extremely uncommon while the minority who betroth before gifts is somewhat more common, the woman's prior presumption of being unmarried remains. But where most people betroth first, and even where the practice is evenly divided, if she marries another she must leave him; in such a doubt we do not simply rule leniently.
