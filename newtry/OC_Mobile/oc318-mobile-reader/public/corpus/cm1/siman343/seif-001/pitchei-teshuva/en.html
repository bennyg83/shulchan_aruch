When that period ends, the custodianship ends automatically. If, however, one accepted a deposit without a fixed term, although each day may be treated as its time and he may return the deposit to its owner, even against the owner's will, he does not cease to be a custodian until he actually returns it to the owner's possession. Even if the custodian tells the depositor that he no longer wishes to guard it, he remains responsible until he returns it. Mordechai, chapter Mi SheAchazo, cited by Rama in siman 293 seif 1, likewise rules that if the custodian told the depositor to come take the deposit and the depositor left, the custodian remains liable, despite having disclosed that he no longer wishes to guard it. Sema explains there that if the depositor instructed the custodian to bring the deposit and he brought it, the custodian is released even before it actually reaches the depositor's hand. That is where the original deposit was accepted without a term. If it was accepted for a fixed term, not only can the custodian not withdraw during the term, but even if both agree to terminate the arrangement, and even if the depositor tells the custodian that the item should no longer be under his responsibility or instructs him to send it through the custodian's Canaanite slave, the custodian remains responsible until the deposit enters the depositor's possession, because the hand of the slave is legally the hand of his master. The reason is that just as the object does not enter the custodian's responsibility until an act of acquisition occurs, so too it does not leave his responsibility until it is returned to the depositor's domain when the custodianship was accepted for a known term. By contrast, where no term was fixed, once the depositor agrees that the custodianship should end, it is treated as though a fixed term had expired, and the custodian is exempt even while the object is still physically in his possession. This is the meaning of Sema's statement there that the case concerns a deposit made without a fixed term. See there, and see what I wrote in siman 293, seif katan 1.
