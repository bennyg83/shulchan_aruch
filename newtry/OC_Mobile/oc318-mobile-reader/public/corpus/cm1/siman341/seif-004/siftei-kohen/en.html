If he left them property, etc. The Sema writes that the Mechaber uses the unqualified term property, which includes movable property. Indeed, the Mechaber's wording is precisely that of the Rambam in the editions printed with the Kesef Mishneh and in other editions. Although the Beit Yosef quotes a version reading encumbered property, that follows only certain manuscripts. The Maggid Mishneh, cited by the Beit Yosef, plainly had the reading property alone. He therefore explains that although the Gemara appears to speak specifically of land, the Rambam wrote property generally because today the ordinance of the Geonim permits collection also from movable property.
And they slaughtered it, etc. See the Sema, seif katan 13. The Rosh follows the second version, as do the Ramban, Rashba, Mordechai, Nimukei Yosef, and Rabbeinu Yerucham, and Maharshal rules accordingly. The Semag, however, follows the Rambam, and this also appears to be the Rif's view. Their reason is that according to the second version the failure to instruct them must itself be treated as negligence, whereas in the chapter HaMokher Et HaBayit we rule that one may say that the Angel of Death caused the animal's death, and Rava himself holds so there. The Shiltei Giborim's claim that the Rambam agrees with the other authorities is untenable. The Maggid Mishneh asks why the Rambam, in chapter 3 of the laws of theft, himself rules like Rav Pappa that the borrower is exempt. The later authorities struggled greatly with this, especially the Kesef Mishneh's suggestion that the Rambam lacked the readings, 'and this disagrees with Rav Pappa' and 'this accords with Rav Pappa.' That suggestion has no basis. The correct answer is that the Gemara says the first version disagrees with Rav Pappa only regarding Rav Pappa's ruling that the borrower is exempt even from the principal until the accident occurs. No one disputes Rav Pappa regarding double payment. Even if liability for the principal begins at the time of borrowing, liability for double payment can begin only at the time of the theft. The Rambam therefore carefully wrote that he is exempt from double payment. Although Rashi explains that double payment does not apply to a borrower because a borrower is liable for theft and loss, the Rambam holds that where he slaughtered it in the manner of a thief he is treated as a thief. This resolves the matter clearly.
