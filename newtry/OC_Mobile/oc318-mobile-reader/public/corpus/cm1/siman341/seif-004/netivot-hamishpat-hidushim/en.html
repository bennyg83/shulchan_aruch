And if they slaughtered it, they pay its value. According to Rambam, if it merely died, the heirs are liable when their father left assets, because Rambam holds that the borrower's lien attaches to his assets during his lifetime. The Rosh holds that if it died they are exempt, because the lien does not attach until the unavoidable accident occurs. If they slaughtered it, however, they are liable because their father was negligent in failing to inform them. Rambam holds that this failure to inform them is not itself negligence. See Beurim: with an unpaid or paid custodian, the heirs are exempt even if they ate the deposited item, even where the father left assets.
