See Sema, seif katan 20, who explains that the borrower can be liable if the animal dies through the work after he used it for a different task, because the owner's stated preference may have created a causal basis for the loss. This is difficult. Siman 342 rules that even if the borrower lent the object to another, he is exempt where witnesses establish that it died through its ordinary permitted work. To answer, Sema earlier required at least some slight change capable of connecting the accident to the deviation. Here, however, Mechaber says the substitute work is "like it," implying there may be no relevant difference at all, and yet the borrower can still be liable. The better distinction is between rental and borrowing. Siman 309 shows that if a renter was told to use an animal on a mountain and used it in a valley, the renter can be exempt when the actual cause of death is unrelated to the difference. A paid rental is normally made because the owner wishes to earn rent and can be presumed indifferent among equivalent ordinary uses. A gratuitous lender is different. Because he receives no payment, when he specifies a particular use we may treat that specification as meaningful insistence. One who departs from the owner's express instruction can become like a wrongdoer even if the substitute use is objectively equivalent. This fits the Gemara's rule concerning a lessor who says "this": the designation can indicate insistence. Accordingly, if the lender merely says generally, "lend me an axe to dig," without specifying a particular place or project, no special restriction exists and use for another equivalent digging project can be permitted. But if he specifies a particular use or place, a borrower should not deviate even to something equivalent without permission.
Many vineyards. See Sema, seif katan 21, who notes that Tur appears to rule similarly even if the borrower said simply "vineyards," without specifying many. If so, Tur must follow Tosafot's view that in doubts concerning the duration or scope of a borrowing of movable property, the borrower is treated as the possessor. The same would apply generally to doubts about the time or extent of rental or borrowing of movable property. Shach, seif katan 14, rules similarly.
