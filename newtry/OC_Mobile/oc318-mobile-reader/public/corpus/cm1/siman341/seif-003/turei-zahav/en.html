(He borrowed it to dig, etc.) Tosafot ask from this passage, where an indefinite plural is interpreted broadly, to the law of one who sells “my lands,” where it means only two. Several answers are offered. It appears that the result depends on who used the ambiguous phrase. Here the borrower says, “Lend me a spade to dig with,” and naturally chooses language favorable to himself, intending broad permission. There the seller uses the ambiguous phrase and naturally intends the minimum favorable to himself. The Gemara applies the same principle at the beginning of HaMekabel, and the Tur and Rama codify it in siman 325. This explanation appears fully correct, and eminent rabbis have agreed with it.
