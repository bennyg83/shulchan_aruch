And concerning the remainder he takes a hesset oath. It is proper to consider the case of one who robbed his fellow before witnesses and then claims, 'I returned it.' He is believed in this claim, as the Shach writes in siman 361. Must he take a hesset oath that he indeed returned it, or do we say that because he robbed before witnesses he is established as one suspected of dishonesty and disqualified from testimony and oaths, and an oath may not be entrusted to him? Although a robber who took before witnesses need not return before witnesses and is believed regarding the money itself when he says, 'I returned it,' he is not thereby restored to personal qualification. He does not regain status as a valid witness or oath-taker until witnesses establish that he has rehabilitated his conduct, as Rashba writes in Responsa, part 3, siman 110, cited in siman 350, seif katan 5. Since he remains under the presumption of being suspected, perhaps no oath should be administered to him. This requires study.
