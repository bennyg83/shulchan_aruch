He must pay the twenty, etc. This requires study. Even with two witnesses, why should he not be believed to say that the object was his, through the claim that he could have denied taking more than one? Perisha answers that this is not a strong migo, following his general approach, but Shach repeatedly rejects it. He also answers that the migo opposes an established presumption, as Darkei Moshe writes. Their words are difficult, for Ran asks in the case of Rabbi Abba's ingot why the defendant is not believed through a migo of having returned it, and answers that the case involves witnesses who saw it. Maggid Mishneh infers from Rambam's omission of such witnesses that one cannot claim to have returned it, as discussed above at the end of siman 361. It therefore appears that Rambam distinguishes between no witnesses at all, where the defendant is believed, and testimony by witnesses or one witness whom he does not contradict, which is treated like two witnesses and defeats the migo because it opposes the presumption. This differs from Maggid Mishneh and Ran. See simanim 3 and 75.
