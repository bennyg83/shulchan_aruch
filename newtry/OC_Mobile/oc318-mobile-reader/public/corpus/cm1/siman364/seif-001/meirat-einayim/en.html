Its laws were explained in siman 90. See the Tur at the beginning of that siman and seif 20. There, on the statement that all these distinctions apply likewise to a victim of robbery where the other entered his house to take collateral, I explained that there is a difference between a case where witnesses heard him say he was entering to seize collateral without authority, thereby giving him the status of a robber, and he later claims, "I took only what was mine," or, "I took only these items," in which case the victim swears and collects, and a case where a person merely suspected as a robber entered and removed utensils and claims he bought them from the householder. That second case has somewhat different rules. All this applies specifically where witnesses saw him enter in the owner's presence and remove utensils. If witnesses saw him enter his fellow's house in the owner's absence, another law applies, stated here in the next seif and also in siman 90:21. Since this siman is the principal source for robbery law, the Tur and Mechaber repeat the rule here.
