And it is rabbinic robbery. This is the Rambam's view, as the Tur also cites in simanim 34 and 207 and here. His reason is not that he rules like Rami bar Chama that gambling involves an asmakhta and therefore constitutes complete robbery. Rather, he too follows Rav Sheshet that the core problem is that the gambler does not occupy himself with productive social activity. The Rambam explains that ordinarily such a person has no money other than what he earns from gambling. The Sages prohibited those winnings as a form of rabbinic robbery because the loser does not part with the money wholeheartedly, although this is not full Torah robbery. A person disqualified because of rabbinic robbery is not disqualified merely by the act unless he regularly consumes and lives from those gains, as I wrote in siman 34. Therefore, even if he has another occupation, if it does not provide enough for his needs and he must also live from gambling winnings, he is disqualified. This is specifically when gambling with a Jew. When gambling with a gentile the Sages did not impose this robbery based disqualification, because the underlying transaction is not Torah robbery and the gentile himself does not regard it as theft, but intends to confer the winnings upon the winner. The authorities who disagree with the Rambam hold that the disqualification of a gambler and similar persons does not stem from robbery. Rather, one who does not engage in productive social activity does not appreciate how difficult it is for people to earn money, and testimony becomes cheap in his eyes. According to that approach, even if he has independent money and does not live from gambling, he is disqualified if he has no other occupation. Conversely, if he has even a small occupation, although it does not fully support him, he understands the difficulty of earning money and is not disqualified for this reason. This is contrary to the Beit Yosef in the two cited places, followed by the Ir Shushan. From this explanation one can see that when the Mechaber says in the next seif, "and they disagreed with him," the disagreement refers to the end of this seif, where he says that the gambling is prohibited as rabbinic robbery. They disagree specifically with that characterization.
