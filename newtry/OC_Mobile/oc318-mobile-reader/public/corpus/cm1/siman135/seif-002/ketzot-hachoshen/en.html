Rava says that if a small child is lying in the cradle, possession of a slave can be established immediately. The Gemara says the novelty is even though the child has a mother. One might ask: slaves are normally transferred by document, so why do we not say, "Show your document," just as with land? Ramban explains that three year land possession should logically prove ownership immediately because the owner's silence strongly supports the possessor, but during the first three years we still say, "Show your document." Only afterward, when people no longer preserve documents, do we rely on possession. The answer is that where there is exceptionally strong evidence, we do not require the document. If the prior owner actually brings the possessor a basket of fruit from the field, immediate possession works because that act itself is strong evidence of ownership. A small child lying in a cradle is similar. The circumstance is so powerful that it supplies a stronger immediate presumption than ordinary possession, and therefore we do not require production of a document.
