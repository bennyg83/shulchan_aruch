And some say that if, etc. In every circumstance possession for three years creates a chazakah. The Shach discusses at length that many authorities follow this view, but he does not mention that Tosafot in chapter 2 of Gittin and Ramban in his novellae to Bava Batra likewise clearly support it. They ask what Reish Lakish adds by saying that wandering livestock do not have an immediate possessory presumption, since the Mishnah already teaches that slaves acquire a chazakah only after three years. If wandering livestock never acquired a chazakah even after three years, Reish Lakish would teach a substantial novelty and Tosafot's question would not arise. Necessarily, they hold that after three years such livestock can indeed establish a chazakah. Their question is therefore proper. It appears, however, that this applies to livestock that produce ongoing benefit, such as animals used for work or whose produce is consumed. Calves and foals, which Bava Metzia chapter 2 classifies as property that does not work and eat, do not fit the usual three year chazakah model because a chazakah requires consumption of produce, and there is no comparable fruit consumption from such animals. This also explains why the Mishnah at the beginning of Chezkas HaBatim lists categories whose chazakah is three years and mentions slaves but not wandering livestock. One might ask that there are circumstances where such animals are regularly entrusted to shepherds, just as a minor slave can have an immediate chazakah. According to what I wrote, the Mishnah lists categories that characteristically produce continuing benefit. Wandering livestock are not uniform, for many are held merely for slaughter and do not produce ongoing fruits. There is therefore no difficulty.
