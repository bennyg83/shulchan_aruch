And if he claims that it caused damage, etc. He must swear with a Torah type oath while holding a sacred object. The Sema in seif katan 2 writes that even the Raavad, who disputes the Geonim's rule regarding a pledge and holds that the claimant need not take such an oath there, agrees here because the migo is weak, being a migo involving brazenness. The Sema, however, did not examine Baal HaTerumot, gate 49, part 2, law 1, where the Raavad himself comments on the Geonim's requirement of a Torah type oath for a pledge. The Raavad suggests that perhaps they learned it from the incident involving the owner of the animal that ate fodder, where certainly an oath was required because the owner of the animal did not know how much damage the animal had caused, and any amount impossible for the opposing party to know cannot be collected without an oath. The Geonim extended that rule to every pledge, and the Raavad questions whether they erred. Thus the Raavad does not require a Torah type oath in the animal damage case because the claimant's migo is brazen, as the Sema says, but because the amount of damage is inherently unknown to the defendant. If the amount of damage were known, no Torah type oath would be required even though the migo involved brazenness. The Sema's explanation is therefore incorrect. This is simple.
