The possessor takes a hesset oath. See below, siman 140:1. The word "gloss" appearing here is a scribal error. The reference indicates that specifically where the challenger asserts with certainty, "You stole it from me," as in siman 140, must the possessor swear. Otherwise, for example if the challenger asks only, "Why is my property in your possession?" and the possessor responds, "I purchased it from so and so, and perhaps it is not yours," he does not swear, because the challenge amounts only to an uncertain claim that he knows the item belongs to the challenger. If he maintained possession for three years. This differs from items ordinarily lent or rented, where even three years of possession does not help. Those items may commonly be lent for more than three years, or the owner may have forgotten to whom he lent them. That rationale does not apply here. The Mechaber's view is that the reason ordinary movables do not require three years of possession is that they are not normally documented. Accordingly, the same rule should apply to livestock that wander independently, and three years should not help. A slave is different because transactions involving slaves are documented. Sema. The Shach rules like Rama that three years of possession is effective even for wandering livestock.
