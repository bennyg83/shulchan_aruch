And some disagree. Rambam and Shulchan Aruch understand the rule as applying only to slaves, as stated in seif 2, because the mishna mentions slaves and not roaming livestock.
And some say, etc. The Jerusalem Talmud, Kiddushin 1:2, records three teachings: slaves are like land, slaves are like movable property, and slaves are unlike either. It proves the first from the mishna listing houses, pits, caves, bathhouses, dovecotes, olive presses, irrigated fields, and slaves among properties acquired by possession. One cannot say that the Jerusalem Talmud rejects the rule concerning roaming livestock, for it expressly states in the beginning of chapter 3 that calves and foals that move from place to place do not create a presumption of ownership.
