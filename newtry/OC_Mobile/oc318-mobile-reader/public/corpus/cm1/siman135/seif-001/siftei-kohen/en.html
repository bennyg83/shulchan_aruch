Others say that if, etc. It appears to me that this is proved by the Gemara and that no one disputes it. The Mishna at the beginning of Chazakat HaBatim states that slaves and all property producing constant fruits acquire a presumption after three complete years. The Gemara asks: do slaves have such a presumption? Reish Lakish said that roaming livestock have no presumption. Rava answers that they have no immediate presumption, but they do acquire one after three years. This appears to imply that roaming livestock too acquire a presumption after three years. Moreover, the term 'roaming livestock' does not include slaves, since it derives from the language of a sheepfold, as the Rashbam explains. The Gemara's comparison must therefore be based on the shared reason that both move independently, and the Rashbam explains this explicitly. If livestock never acquired a presumption while slaves did after three years, the Gemara should have left Reish Lakish's statement in its simple sense and distinguished livestock from slaves. Its reinterpretation proves that there is no such distinction. The Rashbam accordingly states explicitly, as the Tur records, that animals and beasts also acquire a presumption after three years. This is also the view of the Nimukei Yosef, Rabbenu Yerucham, and apparently the Semag. Nothing contrary is proved from the Rambam. The Maggid Mishneh's understanding of him does not appear correct. Since the Rambam writes that slaves acquire a presumption after three years, the same should apply to animals, which he treats similarly. The Bach also writes this, and it appears from the Rambam's Commentary to the Mishna. The Bartenura should likewise be explained this way, contrary to the Tosafot Yom Tov's reading. It therefore appears that the Mechaber too holds that animals acquire a presumption after three years, as he states explicitly above in siman 72, seif 21. There is no need for the Sema's forced explanation that in siman 72 the Mechaber merely copied the Tur and relied upon his present ruling. To the contrary, since the Mechaber rules here that slaves acquire a presumption after three years, the same follows for animals. Nor is anything proved from the Baal HaIttur. The Maggid Mishneh apparently inferred his view only from the statement that movables, not being documented by deed, never acquire a presumption even after three years. Even if the Baal HaIttur held otherwise, the Gemara and the cited authorities prove that animals and beasts do acquire a presumption after three years. This is therefore the halacha. See Responsa Rabbi Shlomo Kohen, part 2, siman 93.
