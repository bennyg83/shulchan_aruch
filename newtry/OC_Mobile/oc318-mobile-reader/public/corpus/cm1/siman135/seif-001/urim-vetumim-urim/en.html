I swear to Hashem, and I swear to him, and he will be blessed with him, and he will be blessed with him
He is liable. This means even if the animal did not originally come into his hands as collateral. If he says the owner owed him because the animal caused him damage, he may still assert the corresponding claim, as Tur and Mechaber state in siman 133. Sema.
He swears while holding a sacred object. Although the owner has witnesses that the animal belongs to him, the possessor is believed through a migo of purchase, because this type of animal is ordinarily entrusted to a keeper. Sema says that even Raavad, who above in siman 72 holds that one who claims ordinary movables are collateral may need only hesset, agrees here that if he claims the animal caused damage he must swear while holding a sacred object, because the purchase migo is one involving greater brazenness. See Tumim. I rejected this distinction. Raavad disputes the stronger oath here as well. The reason for the sacred-object oath in the majority view is that the owner does not know whether the alleged damage occurred. I brought proof from Baal HaTerumot. See there.
Some say that if, etc. Sema distinguishes these animals from ordinary movables, for which three years of possession ordinarily do not create ownership because people may lend them for long periods or forget where they placed them. He also tries to reconcile Mechaber's earlier language with Rambam's distinction between slaves and animals. The Shach argues at length from the Gemara and authorities that no such distinction should be made and concludes that Mechaber relies on his earlier rule. See Tumim. I brought additional support from Tosafot and Ramban, though I suggested that the category of 'animals that wander' may mean working or productive animals, not every animal. See there at length.
