Although the other party has witnesses that the animal is his, the possessor is believed through a migo that he could have claimed, “I purchased it,” because this is an animal that is ordinarily delivered to a custodian. It appears that even according to the Tur's citation in siman 72 that Raavad holds that one who claims regarding other movables, “I hold it as collateral,” is believed with a hesset oath, Raavad would agree here that when the possessor claims the animal caused him damage he must swear while holding a sacred object. The migo that he could have claimed purchase is not a strong migo because such a claim is brazen. He prefers to claim damage, which the animal's owner may not know about. By contrast, a claim that the item is collateral is also a brazen claim, so the two alternatives are equivalent. Sema.
And some say. This is not comparable to ordinary movables, where three years of possession do not establish ownership under siman 133 seif 5. There an owner may lend such an article for more than three years, or may forget to whom he lent it. This is not so with these cases. The Sema notes a difficulty: here the Mechaber rules anonymously like Rambam, distinguishing between a slave and an animal, while in siman 72, at the end of seif 21, he appears to hold that three years of possession establishes ownership for both. The Sema answers that siman 72 is not the principal location of this law, so the Mechaber did not formulate it with full precision there, whereas here he did and followed Rambam. The Shach, however, writes that the Gemara and decisors clearly show that three years of possession also establishes ownership of animals and beasts just as with slaves, with no distinction between them, and this is the practical ruling.
