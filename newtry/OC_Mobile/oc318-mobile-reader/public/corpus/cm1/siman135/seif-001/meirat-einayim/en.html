Some say that if he possessed it for three years, etc. This differs from other movable property, regarding which siman 133:5 explains that three years of possession does not create a presumption. Articles ordinarily lent may be lent for more than three years, or the owner may forget to whom he lent them, and therefore they can remain for so long without protest. Animals are different. The Rambam, whose view the Mechaber follows, explains the general rule concerning movable property differently: three years do not help because movable property is not ordinarily transferred by written deed. Under that rationale the same rule applies to animals. With a slave, however, the Rambam agrees that three years establishes possession, because slaves are transferred by deed. The Mechaber therefore distinguishes in the following seif. It is somewhat difficult that here the Mechaber follows the Rambam's distinction between a slave and an animal, while above at the end of siman 72:21 he appears to treat them alike and to hold that three years helps for both. It may be that siman 72 was not the principal place of this law, so the Mechaber did not formulate it with full precision there. His point there was merely to distinguish immediate possession of ordinary movable property from possession of living creatures, and to say generally that with living creatures three years may matter, each authority according to his own view. Here, where the law belongs, he states the Rambam's position precisely.
