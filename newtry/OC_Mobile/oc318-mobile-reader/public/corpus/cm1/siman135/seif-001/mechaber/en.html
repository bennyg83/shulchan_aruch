The law of one who possesses living creatures, such as an animal or a slave. It contains 2 se'ifim.
Although a person possessing movable property is believed to say that it belongs to him, he is not believed regarding a domesticated or wild animal. Since it moves about, we are concerned that it may have entered his property by itself, or that he may have taken it on the road and retained it. See above, siman 72, at the end of seif 21. Therefore, if the claimant has witnesses that the animal belongs to him, he takes a heses oath and takes it. If he has no witnesses, the possessor takes a heses oath and retains it. In a place where animals are customarily delivered to a shepherd in the morning and retrieved in the evening in a manner that prevents them from wandering alone, an animal has the law of other movable property. The possessor is believed upon taking a heses oath that he acquired it. If he claims that it caused him damage equal to its value, or that the owner owes him such-and-such amount, he swears while holding a sacred object. {Rama: Some say that possession for three years establishes a presumption of ownership in every case.}
