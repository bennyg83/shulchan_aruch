(Seif 6, gloss) Some say even if he brings witnesses. I find the Rosh's view astonishing. First, in siman 126 the Rosh himself rules that even after an express, complete admission a person may say that he erred when witnesses prove the mistake. All the more so here, where silence is not a complete admission, he should be able to say that he was mistaken when he remained silent. Second, the Gemara leaves unresolved whether silence constitutes an admission. Because of the doubt, property is not extracted from the one who seized it. If witnesses are now present and the opposing party seeks to disqualify them because of the earlier silence, we should reason the other way: the silence is not a definite admission and the witnesses tell the truth. Even witnesses' own statements are reconciled wherever possible rather than treated as contradictory, as Rama writes in siman 30, seif 2. Certainly this should apply where the party says there is no contradiction. Moreover, Rav Nahman sought to resolve the question from a baraita saying that the burden of proof rests upon the claimant, in a case where one seized before us. That formulation implies that proof would be effective. If even under the proposed resolution silence was treated as a complete admission, yet proof could still overcome it, then all the more so after the question remained unresolved should proof be effective. The Tur's own following words, that one cannot seize without proof, likewise imply that proof helps. The Gemara in HaMadir similarly asks what proof remains where proof would be ineffective, showing that one does not speak of a burden of proof when no proof can avail. Although I am unworthy to decide, this ruling is not agreed upon and Rama cites it only as “some say.” Earlier silence should therefore not disqualify actual witnesses.
