Or they sit beside a heap of wheat, etc. The Tur in siman 139 cites this rule in the name of the Rambam and explains that because the heap lies in a side alley, a place fit for acquisition, and both parties sit beside it, they are regarded as though both possess it. The Tur's wording is precise. Only where both sit beside it are they deemed jointly in possession. If not, its presence in a side alley alone does not suffice. This is proved in Bava Batra 35a, where the Gemara compares the case of one who exchanges a cow for a donkey and the case of a maidservant with the case of a ship. With a ship, neither party is deemed in possession, as explained below at the beginning of siman 139, while the cow and maidservant necessarily stand in a marsh or side alley, as stated in Bava Metzia 100a. Thus location alone does not create joint possession. See Responsa Rabbi Shlomo Kohen, part 3, beginning of siman 122.
