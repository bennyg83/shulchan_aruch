See below, siman 271. It appears the reference should be siman 197, because there in seif 5 it is explained that riding does not always effect acquisition. The Tur also writes so there. Nothing relevant is stated in siman 271. Know also that the Mechaber's ruling that riding effects acquisition follows Rif and Rambam. Rosh and Tur rule like the opinion that riding alone does not acquire unless he also directs the animal with his feet, as stated here in seif 10 and in siman 197:7. Rama agrees with the Mechaber's ruling according to Rif and Rambam, and therefore writes without qualification here, in siman 197:3, and also in siman 271:3, where he explicitly writes, 'two who were riding,' without mentioning any requirement of directing with the feet.
Or they are sitting beside a heap of wheat. The Tur likewise cites this in Rambam's name in siman 139, seif 6, and writes: Rambam appears to hold that since it lies in a side alley, a place fit for acquisition, and both are sitting beside it, it is regarded as though both are in possession of it.
And brought his fellow into it with him. But if both exercise equal control over the house, they divide, as stated immediately afterward in the gloss concerning a roof over their houses.
And they dispute the property. Specifically regarding property already in the house, the householder is considered to have superior possession. With respect to acquiring ownerless property that entered the house on its own, or a found item there, both are equal, as explained below in siman 260, seif 4.
That he has a share in this thing, and that he has no less, etc. This is also the language of the Gemara. In the Perishah I explained the reason. If he swore only, 'I do not have in it less than half,' there would be room for deceit: perhaps he has nothing at all, and it would technically be true that he does not have less than half, since he has nothing. Therefore he first swears that he has a share in it. Once he then swears that he does not have less than half, it can only mean that he has at least half. The Sages did not institute that he swear, 'I have half,' because that would weaken his original assertion that the entire item is his. Nor did they institute that he swear that the entire item is his when he will receive only half. Rambam and Tur do not write that he must first swear that he has a share in it. In the Derishah I wrote that the reason appears to be that we administer the oath according to the understanding of God and beit din, and therefore there is no concern for such verbal trickery. See there for further discussion.
And they divide it. See Tur, seif 1, who writes that Rashi and Ramban hold that division applies only where it is possible that both acquired it, such as where they found and lifted it simultaneously or purchased it together. But if they are holding an item where one of them is certainly lying, such as each claiming, 'I wove it,' it remains deposited until Eliyahu comes. Rabbeinu Yitzchak and Rosh hold that even there they divide. From the Mechaber's unqualified wording it appears that he follows Rabbeinu Yitzchak and Rosh and does not distinguish.
On a roof over their houses. Rivash there discusses a roof supported on one person's wall on one side and on the other's wall on the other side, with the public domain underneath the middle of the roof, and rules that they divide.
