Sitting beside a heap. This is seemingly difficult. Tosafot in Bava Metzia, in the sugya of one riding and one leading, write that if riding does not effect acquisition, the rider is likewise not considered a possessor. Maharam Schiff explains there that even if both are riding, they are not considered possessors. Why then should merely sitting beside a heap make these people possessors, when sitting itself effects no acquisition? We must say that the case is in a side alley, where a person's four cubits acquire for him, and they are therefore properly regarded as possessors. But if so, the Gemara's question there concerning one riding alongside one leading should be resolved by checking which of them first acquired the object within his four cubits. And when they come before us while both are equally within four cubits, both should be considered equally in possession. I have discussed this at length in the sugya of riding and leading. It appears, however, that in a public domain, where four cubits do not acquire, merely sitting nearby does not make one a possessor.
