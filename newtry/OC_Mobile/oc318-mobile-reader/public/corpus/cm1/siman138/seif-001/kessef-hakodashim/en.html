Seif 1 in the gloss. (It is presumed to belong to the person established in the house.) Although siman 138 states that movable property found in a house can be presumed to belong to the person established as the householder or principal tenant, that presumption does not override known prior ownership. If it is known, even through one witness, that a particular movable item belonged to a person living in the house, the fact that the householder presently possesses it does not remove the prior owner's presumption, all the more so because a dwelling is the natural place for the resident's belongings. The householder is therefore not automatically believed to claim that he purchased it.
