See below siman 197. The reference should read siman 197, not 271, because there it is explained that sometimes riding does not effect acquisition. The Tur likewise writes this there. In siman 271, however, he says nothing of the matter. See Sema.
Beside it, etc. The Tur, siman 139, cites this law in the name of the Rambam and writes that the Rambam apparently holds that since the object lies in a simta, a place fit for acquisition, and both parties are sitting beside it, it is as though both are in possession. The phrase 'both are sitting beside it' is precise. Otherwise, merely because it is in a simta, they are not treated as joint possessors. This is also evident from chapter Chezkas HaBatim. See Responsa Rashach, part 3, siman 123, cited by the Shach.
With him. Meaning, to live with the homeowner in his house. Specifically in a dispute over property already inside the homeowner's house, the homeowner is considered the stronger possessor. But when an ownerless item enters the house on its own, or regarding acquisition of a found object there, both have equal standing, as explained in siman 260. See Sema.
That he has. This is also the Gemara's wording. The reason he must swear affirmatively that he has a share in it, rather than merely swear that he does not have less than half, is that otherwise there is concern of deceit, perhaps he has no share at all. The Rambam and Tur do not require the additional affirmative wording. Apparently their reason is that the oath is administered according to the intent of Heaven and beit din, eliminating the concern of deceptive wording. See Sema. See Tumim, where I brought proof that the Rosh too agrees with the Rif, Rambam, and Tur that one swears only that he does not have less than half, and we do not fear deceptive wording because the language cannot bear such a manipulation. I also explained the intent of the Beit Yosef and Bartenura. See there.
And they divide, etc. In seif 1 the Tur cites Rashi and Ramban, who hold that division applies only where it is possible that both acquired the object, such as where they found and lifted it together or purchased it together. If they both seize an object in a case where one of them is certainly lying, such as each claiming, 'I wove it,' the object remains deposited until Eliyahu comes. Ri and the Rosh, however, hold that even then they divide it. Since the Mechaber rules without making this distinction, the Sema concludes that he follows Ri and the Rosh. See Tumim, seif katan 2, where I gave a correct answer, according to Rashi and Ramban, to Tosafot's difficulty from two parties holding a document, why it should not remain deposited until Eliyahu comes.
On a roof over their houses. In the Rivash's case, the roof rested on the wall of one party on one side and the wall of the other party on the other side, with the public domain underneath between them. He ruled that they divide it. See Sema.
