Before us. The Shach explains that this means before beit din. If the seizure occurred only before witnesses, authorities disagree, and he ruled elsewhere that witnesses are equivalent to beit din. See Tumim. I found no early authority who equates witnesses with beit din in this matter, and I do not accept the Shach's conclusion.
Even if witnesses later come, etc. Bach disagrees where the person was initially silent but later protested. He holds that even though the initial silence can be viewed as admission, later witnesses showing ownership may prove that the silence was not an admission and that the later protest was truthful. The Shach rejects this. Since the initial silence itself legally counts as admission, he asks, what can later witnesses change? See Tumim. I rejected the Shach from the Gemara and agreed with Bach as practical law.
