Witnesses. Bach disagrees with Rama, arguing that the Rosh said witnesses do not help only where the original possessor remained silent from beginning to end, not where he was initially silent and later protested. This is not a decisive objection, since Rama may also be referring only to the first case. In truth, however, the plain language of the Tur and Rama appears to mean that witnesses do not help even in the latter case. The reason is that the person who seized the object can say, “He admitted it to me.” Witnesses cannot overcome the litigant's own admission, for an admission by a litigant is equivalent to one hundred witnesses. See Responsa Maharam Padua, siman 40. Shach.
