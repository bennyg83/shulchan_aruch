Rabbi Zeira asks what happens if one party forcibly seizes the whole garment in front of beit din. Rashi explains that one party took it from the other before the oaths were administered. Rashi's emphasis on "before they swore" implies that silence can function as admission only at that stage, when ownership is still unresolved and each side must yet swear. If the other party remains silent, it can be treated like saying, "I will not swear," a position from which he cannot ordinarily retract after judgment. See siman 22, seif 2. After the party has already sworn and thereby established his half, however, subsequent silence does not count as an admission of the underlying money. Even if he remained silent throughout, the acquired half is not lost merely by that silence. See what I wrote in siman 81, seif katan 6. Accordingly, if after the seizure the silent party later brings witnesses that half belonged to him, the witnesses prevail even if he had remained silent from beginning to end. The silence was not a substantive admission that the property belonged to the seizer. At most it was an abandonment of the oath while the dispute remained unresolved. Once witnesses remove the doubt, that basis disappears. This is even stronger than the case in which he had already sworn, where we likewise do not treat later silence as admission.
