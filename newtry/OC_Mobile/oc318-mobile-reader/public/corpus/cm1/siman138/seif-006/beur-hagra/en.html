If both came... This follows the ordinary rule of monetary doubt, as in Bava Metzia 102b regarding the bathhouse and in Bava Kama regarding the slaughter defect.
Collected note: Even though... This is also the rule for a monetary doubt. The sugya implies that we do not remove the property from the possessor, for the Gemara asks what the final law is regarding the bathhouse. If property were removed, that question would already have been resolved by the discussion of one who seizes it.
Some say that even... This statement refers only to the first case, where he remained entirely silent. In the latter case, where he later protested, proof certainly helps, as indicated by the Gemara's conclusion that the burden of proof is on the claimant. The Ritva explains that if he remained silent throughout, later proof cannot help because his silence constitutes an admission.
