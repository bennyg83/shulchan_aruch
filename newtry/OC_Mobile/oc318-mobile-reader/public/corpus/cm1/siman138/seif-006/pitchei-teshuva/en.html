Responsa Shevut Yaakov, part 3, siman 151, discusses Reuven and Shimon who deposited property with a third party on condition that it not be delivered to either without the consent of both. Later both came, and Shimon stated that they had compromised and that the deposited property now belonged entirely to him, while Reuven remained silent. The responsum rules that this is comparable not to the law of a bare oral claim, where silence is not necessarily an admission, but to the case in siman 138 where both parties are physically holding the object and one seizes it while the other remains silent. Since both had entrusted the property to the stakeholder, who is regarded as reliable as two witnesses, the property in his hands is considered as though both parties are holding it. When Shimon asserted in Reuven's presence that it belonged to him alone, it was as though he seized it from Reuven while Reuven remained silent. That silence is therefore an admission, and Reuven has no further claim.
