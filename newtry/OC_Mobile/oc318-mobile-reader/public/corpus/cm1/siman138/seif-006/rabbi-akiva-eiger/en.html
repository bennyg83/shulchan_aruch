[Rama] He is believed through a migo. Note: See Shach above, siman 133, in the passage beginning "And it further appears," and Sema, siman 134, seif katan 2. If he says, "I sold it to him in the presence of so-and-so and so-and-so, and they went overseas," one may say that he is not believed because it is a migo used to extract money, since he admits that the object is no longer his and claims only money. See Shitah Mekubetzet. One can perhaps distinguish: if he claims, "I sold it to him, and he owes me money from another matter," this is a migo to extract, because the object is not in his possession; but if he claims that the purchase price itself is due to him, it is not as strongly classified as extracting. See Ketzot HaChoshen, siman 72, seif katan 38. However, Responsa Raanach, siman 32, writes plainly that in such a case it is a migo to extract. See there. See also Shach, siman 91, seif katan 33, s.v. "And it further appears, even the shopkeeper."
