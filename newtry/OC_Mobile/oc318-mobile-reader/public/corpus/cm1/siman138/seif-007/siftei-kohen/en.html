They shall divide it as at the outset. The Hagahot Mordechai to Bava Metzia writes that even according to this view, if the first party consecrated it before the other seized it from him, the consecration is effective and does not thereafter lapse, even if the second party later seizes it from him. This is quoted by the Sema in Darkei Moshe 7. The same appears in Tosafot to Bava Metzia 6a, at the end of the page, and in the Hagahot Ashiri and Agudah there in the name of Ri. Nevertheless, one may distinguish. The Hagahot Mordechai and the authorities cited there address a disputed object governed by the rule that the stronger party prevails. They therefore write that if one party first seized it and consecrated it while it was in his possession, the consecration does not lapse even if the other later seizes it from him. This may apply specifically there, because beit din itself rules that the stronger party prevails, so the first seizure was legally authorized. That differs from a case in which one seized without authorization of beit din. Consider this carefully.
Some say, etc. The Bach challenges the Rama and writes that the Raavad and Tur said this only where the party remained silent from beginning to end. In my humble opinion, however, if the Raavad and Tur are interpreted that way, the Rama's words must likewise be so interpreted, for they too wrote their ruling in this context, just as the Rama did. This also appears correct as a matter of law. If he remained silent from beginning to end, he cannot later seize it back. If he was initially silent but ultimately protested, he may seize it back.
