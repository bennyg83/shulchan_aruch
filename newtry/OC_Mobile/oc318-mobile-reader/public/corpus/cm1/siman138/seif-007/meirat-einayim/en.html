As the first time, Dr. Dovach wrote that he was the first person to attack him and that he had not been expropriated from him, even if he had attacked his second hand
Since the first one acquired it. The Mordechai in the second chapter of Ketubot, Maharik, and Terumat HaDeshen write that if the person who seized the object makes a definite claim, possession can be effective even after the doubt arose. The present case is different, because we treat his prior conduct as an admission to the other party. See Nimukei Yosef at the beginning of Bava Metzia, Rabbeinu Yerucham netiv 3, part 5, concerning the various rules of seizure, and Darkei Moshe.
