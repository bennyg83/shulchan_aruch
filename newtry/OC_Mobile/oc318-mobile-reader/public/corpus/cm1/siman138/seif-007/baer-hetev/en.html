That he acquired it. The Shach writes that the practical rule is that where the original possessor was silent from beginning to end, he cannot later seize the item back. Where he was initially silent but afterward protested, he may seize it back. When his silence can reasonably be attributed to some other cause, silence is not treated as admission. Binyamin Ze'ev, siman 37; Mishpetei Shmuel, siman 102. If two or three partners are present and one says, “We did this,” and the others remain silent, their silence is treated as admission and it is as though they too said they did it. Radbaz, part II, siman 283. Knesset HaGedolah. See the Taz on this rule.
