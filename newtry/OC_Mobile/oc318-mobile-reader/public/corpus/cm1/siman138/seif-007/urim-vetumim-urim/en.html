They divide as at first. Sema cites Hagahot Mordechai: even according to this view, if the first party consecrated the object before the other seized it, the consecration takes effect and is not undone when the second later seizes it. The Shach questions this because Tosafot say such a thing in the bathhouse case, where the rule is 'whoever is stronger prevails' and seizure is recognized by beit din. See Tumim. The Shach follows his later position that in a legal doubt we do not apply 'whoever is stronger prevails.' According to what I establish below against him, Sema's ruling here is correct and supported by Bava Metzia.
