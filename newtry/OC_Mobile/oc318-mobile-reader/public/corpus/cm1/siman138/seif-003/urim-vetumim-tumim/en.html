And they divide the remainder with an oath. See Shach, who rules like those who require an oath even concerning the portion already physically held. Still, the question raised against the opposing view can be answered. They ask: if no oath is required on the part already held, what novelty did Rabbi Abahu add when he said that the baraita's division is with an oath? Is that not already taught by the Mishnah? The answer is that the baraita simply says they divide and does not mention an oath. Earlier the Gemara asks whether the Mishnah must disagree with Sumchus, since according to Sumchus disputed property is divided without an oath. The Gemara answers that even Sumchus can agree because this oath is a special rabbinic enactment. Rabbi Abahu teaches precisely that point concerning the baraita. One might otherwise say, as in the Gemara's initial question, that the baraita follows Sumchus and therefore its unqualified word divide means without an oath. Rabbi Abahu therefore states that the division is with an oath, teaching that no authority exempts from this oath because it is a rabbinic enactment. This resolves the difficulty. Rav Chiyya's inference concerning heilech from the Mishnah, that whatever each litigant already holds is treated as his and the case is therefore like a partial admission, gives some support to Rambam's view that no oath is imposed on the already held portion. Otherwise, how can this be a case of partial admission if even what he holds remains subject to oath? One may answer that according to Rav Chiyya, who obligates in heilech, this indeed follows. But according to Rav Sheshet, who exempts heilech and explains the Mishnah's oath solely as the enactment preventing people from attacking one another's garments, the enacted oath properly includes even what each already holds. This itself is part of the dispute between Rav Chiyya and Rav Sheshet, and we rule like Rav Sheshet.
