They divide the remainder equally and swear, etc. The Bach and Torat Chaim rule that only the remainder is divided under oath, while each takes without an oath the portion physically held. It appears to me that the primary reading of the Gemara follows the majority of authorities, who require an oath even for what each physically holds. Tosafot and the other authorities explain that the oath prevents each person from attacking another's property and seizing it. The Rosh and Rashba add that otherwise Rabbi Abbahu's teaching would add nothing beyond the Mishna. Torat Chaim asks why the Gemara's question, 'How can this case be found?' is not answered by saying that both grasp equal portions. This is not difficult. If the decisive factor is what each physically holds, the Mishna should not say simply, 'They divide,' language implying that property in doubt is always divided equally. It should say, 'Each takes up to where his hand reaches.' That wording would automatically teach the rule even where their grips are unequal. Indeed, the Rambam's approach is more difficult, for the Gemara infers from the Mishna's language, 'They divide,' rather than, 'Each takes up to where his hand reaches,' that the parties physically hold the garment. Yet even if the Mishna had used the latter wording, one would still ask why its oath is required. The proper explanation is that even if both hold equal portions, because the essential rule depends upon physical possession the Mishna should have formulated the law in terms of where each hand reaches, which would include unequal possession as well. Furthermore, the Mishna cannot be limited to equal possession, because in its latter clause, where one claims the whole and the other claims half, the latter receives only one quarter. Were both holding equally, he should receive half. Nor can one say that the half-claimant holds only one quarter while the other holds three quarters, for then the first claimant's assertion that the whole is his would be unnecessary. Even if he claimed only the portion he holds, he would receive it. The Gemara therefore means that their verbal claims determine the division only within the area not already controlled by physical possession. The Maggid Mishneh's citation of Rav Hai Gaon is also surprising. Rav Hai explicitly writes in Mishpetei Shevuot that where one holds most of a garment and the other a smaller part, nothing is removed from either. Each swears that he has a right in it and no less than the part in his hand, each receives what he holds, and the remainder is divided. He later states that the third method is that each takes what he holds and they divide the remainder under oath. Rav Hai's phrase that Rabbi Abbahu demonstrated that 'the remainder is divided under oath' must therefore not mean that the oath applies only to the remainder. Rather, Rabbi Abbahu demonstrated that the entire rule of taking what one holds and dividing the remainder is performed under oath. Likewise, the version of Halachot Gedolot before us contains only, 'Rabbi Abbahu demonstrated, under oath,' exactly as in the Gemara.
