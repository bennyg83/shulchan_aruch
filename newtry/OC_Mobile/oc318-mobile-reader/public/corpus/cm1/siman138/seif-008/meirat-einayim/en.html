I rented it to him, or he overpowered me, etc. The Gemara says: we tell him, 'Until now you suspected him of robbery, and now you rented it to him without witnesses?' The Tur, Ramban, and Rashba understand from this reason that specifically the claim 'I rented it to him' is not believed, but a claim that he seized it from him is believed. This is the basis of the first opinion Rama brings in the gloss. Maggid Mishneh writes that Rambam holds the same rule applies to a claim that he seized it from him: since he knew that the other was a robber, he should have protected himself and taken witnesses. This is the Mechaber's position.
Through a migo that he could have said, 'He seized it from me.' But if he does not say, 'I rented it to him before so-and-so,' he is not believed through a migo that he could have said, 'He seized it from me,' because that is a migo against a chazakah.
