This one shall swear, etc. This means a rabbinic oath of denial. Rabbeinu Yerucham writes in Meisharim, Netiv 23, end of part 10: Two men came from overseas accompanied by a woman and a bundle. Each man says, 'She is my wife and the bundle is mine.' She requires two bills of divorce and collects one ketubah from the bundle. If the woman says, 'Everything is mine and they are my slaves,' there is a dispute whether she receives anything from the surplus beyond the ketubah after they divorce her, and the principal ruling is that she receives nothing from the surplus. These words require study and appear corrupt. The conclusion of Kiddushin 65b and Rashi there is as follows: Two men came from overseas with a woman and a bundle. Each says, 'She is my wife, the other is my slave, and the bundle is mine,' while she says, 'Both are my slaves and the bundle is mine.' The bundle remains deposited until Eliyahu comes, unless both voluntarily give her bills of divorce. Their divorces then enable her to collect one ketubah in any event, while the surplus remains in doubt among all three until Eliyahu comes. Ritva explains that if she seeks collection strictly as a ketubah claim, she receives nothing, because in such a case we follow her own admission, as where one says to another, 'I owe you one hundred,' and the other says, 'You owe me nothing,' in which case he is exempt because there is no claimant. Moreover, once she admits that one is her husband by claiming a ketubah, she has effectively admitted that she has no right in the bundle except through the ketubah, leaving her without a claim from either direction. Rather, the case is that she proceeds against them in any event: according to her, the entire bundle is hers; according to them, she is at least entitled to one ketubah from it. She therefore collects that amount in any event, and as to the balance her position is equal to theirs, under the rules governing money in doubt, whether division, deposit until Eliyahu comes, or the stronger party prevails.
