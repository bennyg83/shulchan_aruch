The burden of proof rests upon the claimant. Even if these are objects ordinarily lent or rented out, here they are treated, against this particular claimant, as though they were not ordinarily lent or rented. We say: until now you suspected him of theft, and now you claim that you rented the item to him without witnesses. But if he says, "I rented it to him before so and so and so and so, and they have gone overseas," that reasoning no longer applies. He is plainly believed regarding objects ordinarily lent or rented out. Tumim writes this. Ketzot HaChoshen says that even then it is considered against this person like an object not ordinarily lent out, because he should have witnesses, but that is strained. Tumim's ruling is primary.
