This one shall swear, etc. The Shach cites Rabbeinu Yerucham in Meisharim: two men came from overseas with a woman and a bundle. Each man says, 'She is my wife, and the bundle is mine.' She requires two gittin and collects one ketubah, etc. If the woman says, 'Everything is mine and these two are my slaves,' there is a dispute whether she receives anything beyond the ketubah, and the primary view is that she does not. The Shach writes that Rabbeinu Yerucham's words require study and appear corrupt, and cites the sugya in Kiddushin and the Ritva. The substance of his argument is that the woman should collect the amount of her ketubah from the bundle either way: if her claim is true, the bundle is hers; if it belongs to the men, she collects her ketubah from their property. See there. In Tumim I wrote that Rabbeinu Yerucham's words are correct, because he follows Rabbi Yehudah Albargeloni as cited by the Rashba, and Rabbeinu Yerucham disagrees with the Rashba. I also derived another law: if two people are holding a garment and one says, 'It is entirely mine,' while the other says, 'It is entirely mine, but you have a lien on it because I owe you money,' then the first party takes it either way, because either the whole garment is his, or according to the other party's own words his lien rests on it. This follows from the case of the two men, woman, and bundle arriving from overseas. I asked whether this is like one who claims wheat and is answered with barley, and explained, albeit with some difficulty, why it is not. See there.
And some say, etc., before so and so and so and so, etc. He is believed through a migo. The Mechaber's view is the Rambam's in chapter 9 of Hilchot To'en. The Rama follows the Rashba and Ramban cited by the Maggid Mishneh. In Tumim I concluded that regarding items ordinarily lent or rented, no migo is required at all, and even the Mechaber and Rambam agree that if one says, 'I rented it to you before so and so and so and so,' he is believed. Regarding items not ordinarily lent or rented, however, the Rambam and Mechaber hold that he is not believed, whereas the Maggid Mishneh, in the name of the Ramban and Rashba, holds that he is believed even then. I proved this from the Gemara. As practical law, with items ordinarily lent or rented, everyone agrees with the Rama's ruling. See there.
