Since he remained silent, etc. See Baer Heitev, seif katan 14, concerning silence as admission. See Responsa Shevut Yaakov, part 3, siman 151, concerning Reuven and Shimon, who deposited an item with a third party and instructed him not to give it to either one without the consent of both. Later they both appeared, and Shimon said that they had compromised and agreed that the deposited property belonged to him. Reuven remained silent. The question was whether his silence constitutes admission. Shevut Yaakov answers that at first glance one might compare this to Choshen Mishpat siman 81, where Reuven says to Shimon and Levi, "You owe me one hundred," Shimon answers yes, and Levi remains silent. There the silence of Levi does not constitute admission. In truth, however, the present case is comparable instead to siman 138, seif 6, where two people are both holding an object and one forcibly takes it while the other remains silent. There the initial silence is treated as admission. The Shach agrees with the Rosh that this is so even if the seizure occurred before witnesses. Here, both parties entrusted the object to a third party, who is believed like two witnesses. So long as it remains in his hands, it is legally as though both are holding it. When Shimon says in Reuven's presence that the object belongs exclusively to him, that is equivalent to Shimon seizing it from Reuven while Reuven remains silent. Reuven's silence therefore certainly constitutes admission, and he has no further claim. See there.
