Some say he collects from the proceeds. There are two rationales. Rav Hai Gaon treats sale proceeds in the heirs' hands as substitutes for the inherited movables, and therefore subject to the Geonic enactment. A second rationale is that even if the proceeds are considered the heirs' own movables, they may be liable because they damaged the creditor's lien. A practical difference arises in gifts and similar transfers.
And some say he does not collect. Sema explains that when heirs sell property, they sell their own property, because a creditor does not own the body of the land before collection. But if a later creditor sells land to a gentile and thereby defeats an earlier creditor, he damages the earlier lien and is liable. I further wrote that according to the view above in siman 103 that after the due date a creditor may descend into the land without formal appraisal, an heir who sells may likewise be liable because he sold property the creditor could already have taken. Also, if an oral creditor had a lien on unencumbered land and the heirs sold it so that strict law prevents collection from the purchaser, everyone agrees that this clearly damages the creditor's lien and creates liability.
