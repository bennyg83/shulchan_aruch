Dagul MeRevavah comments: "I do not know what difficulty the Shach sees. If the heirs explicitly said that they were giving the movable property in exchange for the land, this is like selling the movable property. Even after the enactment of the Geonim, according to the Rosh they would not be required to pay, and according to the Sema, in a case where they sold the movable property even Rav Hai agrees. What difference is there whether they sold it for cash or accepted this land from the creditor in exchange for the movable property? A later creditor could then no longer collect from them. It appears, however, that the Shach follows his own view above, seif katan 8, that where the heirs received money everyone agrees they are liable, not because of the law of indirect damage but because they profited from it. Here too they profited by receiving the land." See Tumim and Netivot HaMishpat concerning this.
