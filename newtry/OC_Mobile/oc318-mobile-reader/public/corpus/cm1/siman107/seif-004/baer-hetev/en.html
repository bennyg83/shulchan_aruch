And some say. The Tur writes that if heirs sold movables, all agree that the creditor cannot collect from the proceeds, because the heirs' movables were not subject to the creditor under strict Talmudic law, and the Geonic enactment extended collection only while those movables remained in the heirs' hands. If they sold land, the Tur initially thought they should be liable from the proceeds because they had damaged the creditor's lien by placing the property beyond his reach. The Rosh, however, ruled that this is not considered damaging another's lien because they did not damage the land itself. This does not contradict siman 104, where a later creditor who sold land subject to an earlier creditor to a non-Jew must compensate the earlier creditor. The Rosh expressly distinguishes heirs: when heirs sell, they sell property that is presently theirs, because a creditor acquires only from the time of collection onward. A later creditor, by contrast, has only a lien on property that is already subject to the earlier creditor. Sema. See the Shach's discussion.
