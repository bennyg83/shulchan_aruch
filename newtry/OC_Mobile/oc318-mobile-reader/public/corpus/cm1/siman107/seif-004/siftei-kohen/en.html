Some say that he does not collect. The Sema writes that regarding movable property all agree that the creditor does not collect from the proceeds. This is imprecise, for the Tur implies that according to Rav Hai he does collect from the proceeds. See the Ramban's novellae at the beginning of Mi Shemet, where he disputes Rav Hai; the Rosh and Nimukei Yosef likewise disagree. In my view, however, Rav Hai's opinion is primary. The Rosh's two objections do not refute him, because Rav Hai speaks after the enactment of the Geonim, under which movable property is treated like land. Moreover, the heirs are liable for damaging another's lien, as explained at the beginning of siman 104. The Tur's statement that he does not respond to Rav Hai's words is merely out of respect for the Rosh, but as a matter of law the Tur accepts the rule stated at the beginning and end of siman 104 in the name of the Raavad. The Shiltei Giborim leaves the apparent contradiction in the Tur unresolved, which indicates that the two cases share one rule, unlike the distinctions proposed by the Sema and Bach. See Even HaEzer, simanim 100 and 112, siman 235:1 and the second gloss there, Responsa Ran, siman 89, and the Beit Yosef at the beginning of siman 108 in the name of the Ri. Whenever the authorities say that one must pay under the law of indirect damage, this applies even if the seller did not receive the proceeds; if he did receive them, he is liable in any event because he benefited, as the Tur writes at the end of siman 104 in the name of the Raavad. Furthermore, today, when reliance is placed upon movable property as upon land, as the Rosh, Rashbam, Tur in siman 419, and Rabbeinu Yerucham state, movable property and land are legally equivalent even without the enactment. Therefore, according to the Tur's ruling that heirs who sold land are liable, the same follows for movable property. This requires study. See siman 252:2 in the gloss and siman 251:6. The Rosh's responsum, however, concerns only impairment of a lien, not destruction or transfer of another person's actual property, for which one is liable as a tortfeasor, as stated in Bava Kamma 116 to 117 and codified in siman 388.
