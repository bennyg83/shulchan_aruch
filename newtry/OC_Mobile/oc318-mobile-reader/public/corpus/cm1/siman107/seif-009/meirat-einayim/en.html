They inherited lands and divided them. The Rosh writes in klal 80, responsum 8: Two brothers compromised with a person who claimed that their father owed him money. A third brother then appeared and demanded restoration of his share. He may collect his share from whichever party he chooses, either from the creditor or from his two brothers. Darkei Moshe at the end of this siman. See above, siman 175:3.
He seizes half the debt from the purchaser, etc. The Tur explains that the rule that one does not collect from encumbered property where unencumbered property is available applies only where the debtor himself encumbered his property. It does not apply where an heir or purchaser later encumbered property. The reason is that where the debtor himself left unencumbered property, since the entire debt rests upon him alone the purchaser can say, "I left you property from which to collect," even if it is inferior land, because under Torah law a creditor collects from inferior land and with respect to purchasers we revert to Torah law. With heirs, by contrast, their father's debt falls equally upon both of them. One heir cannot sell his own share and thereby cast the entire debt upon the other heir.
