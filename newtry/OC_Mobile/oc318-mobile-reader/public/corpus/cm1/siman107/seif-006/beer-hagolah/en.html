Beit Yosef writes so from the incident before Abaye in Ketubot 91a, according to the other commentators: specifically where the father left them movable property. If he did not, the creditor does not return and collect from them, because where their father left no movables there is no mitzvah upon them to repay their father's debt, as stated at the end of seif 1. Maggid Mishneh likewise infers this from Rambam's language at the end of chapter 18 of Hilkhot Malveh, and the rule is straightforward.
Maggid Mishneh writes there that Ramban and Rashba proved this from the Yerushalmi. Beit Yosef likewise writes that Ran derives it from the Yerushalmi there, and that the Rosh writes likewise. It is explained there that the same applies if their father left movables and they explicitly said that they paid money in exchange for them: a later creditor cannot collect from them for this reason.
