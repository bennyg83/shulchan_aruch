Even if they did not tell him that it was in exchange for, etc. This applies specifically where their father left them no movable property, for then they are not obligated even as a mitzvah to pay their father's debt. But if he left them movable property, we say that whatever they paid was paid on their father's account. Therefore the Rosh and Tur write that only if the heirs expressly stated, when giving the creditor the fifty gold coins, that they were paying them in exchange for the land, is it considered as though the creditor first awarded them the land and they then purchased it back from him. If they paid without specification, that effect does not arise. The Maggid Mishneh writes likewise, and the Beit Yosef says this is the view of most commentators and the primary ruling.
Likewise, a later creditor cannot, etc. The same applies where the father left movable property and the heirs expressly said that the money they paid was in exchange for it. A later creditor cannot then collect the same amount for this reason. This is explicit in the Ran and Maggid Mishneh. See Darkei Moshe.
