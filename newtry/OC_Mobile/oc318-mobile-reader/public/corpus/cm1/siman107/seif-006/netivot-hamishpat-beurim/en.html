Likewise, a later creditor does not. See Sema, seif katan 19, who writes that the same applies where the deceased left movable property and the heirs expressly said that they gave the money in exchange for it. He discusses the law as it stood under the Gemara, where the deceased left land and movable property and the heirs gave movable property or money in exchange for the land. In the first clause there is one creditor, and in the latter clause there are two creditors. It appears, however, that the Sema's words can be understood even after the Geonic ordinance. Suppose the debtor owed fifty to Reuven and fifty to Shimon, the later creditor, and left land worth fifty and movable property worth fifty. The earlier creditor collected the land, and the heirs then gave him the movable property. If they expressly said that in exchange for the movable property they were acquiring the land, this is like selling the movable property to another person. According to the Sema in seif katan 15, they would be exempt from paying from the proceeds, and here too they are exempt from paying from the land they received as consideration for the movable property. But if they gave the movable property without specifying, we assume it was given to discharge the debt and pursuant to the rule that an appraisal can be returned. We do not treat it as a sale of the movable property unless they expressly said that they gave it by way of sale. This appears to be the best way to interpret the Sema so that his words are not so difficult.
