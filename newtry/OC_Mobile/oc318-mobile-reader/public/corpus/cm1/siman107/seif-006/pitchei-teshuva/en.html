Dagul MeRevavah answers that if the heirs expressly transferred the movable property in exchange for land, the transaction is equivalent to selling the movables. Even after the enactment of the Geonim, according to the Rosh they would not have to use the proceeds to pay the creditor, and according to the Sema even Rav Hai agrees where movable property was sold. What difference is there whether they sold for cash or received land from a creditor in exchange for those movables? A later creditor therefore cannot collect the exchanged property. Dagul MeRevavah suggests that the Shach follows his own view above, seif katan 8, that where heirs received money everyone agrees they are liable, not because of tort liability but because they derived a direct benefit. Here too they benefited by receiving the land. See Tumim and Netivot HaMishpat for further discussion.
