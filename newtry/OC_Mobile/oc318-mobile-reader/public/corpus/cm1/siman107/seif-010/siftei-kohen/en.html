Since they made an unqualified admission. It appears that this applies only where they have no migo to claim payment, such as where the debt is still within its term. One cannot say that their unqualified admission creates so strong a presumption that they admitted their own debt, and that no migo is accepted against this presumption. Were that so, the Baal HaTerumot and the Sema, seif katan 25, would not have needed, in the case of one witness, to compare it to the case of Rabbi Abba's piece of silver. Examine this carefully.
See above, siman 75, etc. Meaning, it was explained there that the rule 'one who cannot swear must pay' applies only where he lacks a migo. The same is true here: they must pay only where they cannot claim that the debt was paid, such as where the single witness testifies that the debt is still within its term, or that he never left the creditor's presence, and similar cases.
