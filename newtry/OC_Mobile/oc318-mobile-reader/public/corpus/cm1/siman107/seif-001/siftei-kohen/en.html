They are not compelled, etc. The Sema writes that Tosafot and the Rosh disagree with Ramban, but I found no such statement in the Rosh. Tosafot in Bava Batra ultimately cites Ritzba in agreement with Ramban, as do Mordechai and Hagahot Ashri. Tosafot elsewhere offer other explanations and omit Ritzba, but that need not mean they disagree in law. They may have declined to explain the incident of Rava and Rav Natan as an optional coercion, since Rav Natan was himself a scholar who knew his duty. The Rama appears to regard Ramban's rule as straightforward and therefore did not repeat it here, relying on what he wrote above in siman 97. His principal purpose there was to teach that return of collateral is a positive commandment whose reward is stated in the Torah, and beit din is not obligated to coerce its performance.
Even from movable property left by their father, etc. The Rosh writes that nowadays movable property is treated like land even under Talmudic law. Elsewhere he says that a creditor does not collect movable property that the debtor or heirs gave away. There is no contradiction. With respect to purchasers or donees, movable property has no priority and no effective public lien, so a donee is treated like a purchaser. The creditor did not rely on such property because it lacks publicity and can easily be removed. Thus the enactment subjecting movable property of heirs does not create a right to pursue it from purchasers or recipients of gifts.
From a loan that others owed their father, etc. The creditor may invoke Rabbi Natan's lien against the debtor of his debtor and collect movable property that would otherwise belong to the heirs. The Rosh and Tosafot rule so. Even Raavad, who rejects combining two stringencies in the law of a levir, may agree here, because his objection concerns an heir who is himself the creditor, not an outside creditor. Alternatively, authorities who are lenient regarding a ketubah may agree regarding an ordinary creditor, since a ketubah is rabbinic and an ordinary creditor is stronger in several respects. Baal HaItur, Ramban, the Rif, and Rivash expressly support collection in this case. See also what I wrote above at the end of siman 104.
