(Collected notes) Likewise they are obligated... Although a loan may be considered merely prospective property for purposes of a firstborn or husband, with respect to a creditor it is treated as property already held. See Kesubos, Bava Basra, Bechoros, Ramban, and Even HaEzer siman 100. The Rosh notes that this distinction was especially significant when heirs' movables were not generally encumbered; nowadays the practical law is broader. (End of collected notes.)
Whether they collected money. The Gemara's limitation to land reflected the old rule that heirs' movables were not encumbered. Without that limitation there is no distinction. This is also clear from Kesubos 84a concerning one who died leaving property, and from the Yerushalmi cited by the Rosh. Although Rabbi Akiva there rejects seizure under the old law, the Rosh explains that nowadays movable property is treated differently. The distinction in Bava Basra between heirs who collected land and those who collected money follows Rabbi's view, but not necessarily the Rabbis'.
And if they inherited... See Sema. The Geonic enactment was made to prevent lenders from refusing credit, not for every unrelated obligation. Thus if the property was expressly encumbered, collection follows even against purchasers, all the more against heirs. See Bava Basra and Tosafos in Bava Kamma.
