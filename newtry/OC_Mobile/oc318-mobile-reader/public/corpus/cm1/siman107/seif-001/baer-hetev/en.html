They enacted. The Rosh in the chapter HaIshah Shenaflu writes that nowadays movable property is treated like land even under Talmudic law, while in Kiddushin, chapter HaOmer, he writes that a creditor does not collect movable property that the debtor gave away. There is no contradiction. Plainly, for property transferred to purchasers we do not treat movable property like land, because movable property has no law of priority, as stated in siman 104. The same applies to a gift, which is treated like a sale in this respect, as stated in siman 111, seif 8, and at the beginning of siman 113. See Responsa Maharam Mintz 32, Responsa Maharam Alshich, beginning of siman 14, Maharit 72, and Responsa Mahara Sasson 218. Shach.
Collected. The Sema writes that "collected" is not literal. Even if the heirs did not actually collect the debt but the deceased left promissory notes against his own debtors, his creditor may take those notes from the heirs and collect them, because documents are treated as movable property. The Shach adds that under the rule of Rabbi Natan, the creditor may likewise collect movable property of the orphans from the debtor of his own debtor. See there.
The heirs. Even if the creditor seized movable property, that seizure is ineffective if the heirs wish to give him land instead. So it appears, and this is proven from Baal HaTerumot and Tur, seif 17, contrary to the Bach in siman 108, seif 4. Shach.
