Because the enactment of the later authorities does not have power to obligate orphans in such matters. The Tur likewise cites this in his name in Even HaEzer siman 100 regarding collection of a ketubah from movable property. I write this to teach that it is proper even nowadays to state such a lien explicitly in a debt instrument for this reason.
However, according to the Tur in the name of the Rosh at the end of siman 419, regarding the rule that an injured party does not collect from the orphans' movable property under Torah law according to Rif, and according to Rabbeinu Ephraim not even by the enactment of the Geonim because that enactment was made only for lenders to avoid closing the door before borrowers, the Tur concludes that according to his father the Rosh, in these generations movable property has the status of land for every type of lien by the law of the Gemara itself, without need for the enactment of the Geonim, just as a woman's ketubah is collected from Arab camels.
According to that view, even for an ordinary loan there is no need to write the lien expressly and there is no concern for Rambam's objection.

Whether they collected land or money. Under the law of the Gemara, only if the heirs collected land could the creditor in turn collect from them. The wording "collected" is not exclusive. Even if the heirs have not yet collected the debt and their father left them promissory notes against his own borrowers, the creditor may take those notes from the heirs and collect through them, because documents are treated as movable property. The Tur states this here and at the end of siman 64.
