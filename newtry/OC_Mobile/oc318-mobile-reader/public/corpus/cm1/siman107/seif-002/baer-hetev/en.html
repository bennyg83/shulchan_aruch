The Sema initially explains that the lender and the heirs disagree: the lender claims with certainty that the deceased left money and refuses to rely on the family's oath that nothing was left. This would explain why the Rama placed this gloss here. Otherwise, why should the deceased be treated more harshly after death than during life, when one does not imprison or restrain a debtor who has nothing, as stated in siman 97, seif 15? The Shach offers another explanation. The heirs wish to spend the estate on burial, while the creditor objects and demands payment first, comparable to a living debtor who is dissipating assets, as in siman 73, seif 10. This is also indicated by the Rama's concluding words. The Shach notes that even the deceased's wife can sometimes object, as stated in Even HaEzer siman 118, seif 18. He distinguishes between blood relatives and relatives by marriage, or between a case where communal charity funds are available for burial and one where they are not. See also Responsa Chacham Tzvi, siman 48.
