And if he advances an uncertain claim, etc. This is stated by the Tur in the name of the Rif, and appears in the Baal HaTerumot and in the Ittur, p. 67; see there, in the name of a responsum of the Rif.
Reuven owed Shimon, etc. See the Sema, seif katan 10, through his explanation of why the Rama placed this gloss here. Were I not apprehensive, I would explain the Hagahot Ashri as referring to minor orphans, where the testimony had not yet been formally received, in accordance with siman 110 and siman 108:3. The Rama, however, plainly does not understand it this way, as the Sema writes. According to the Rama, it appears that the heirs wished to spend the estate on burial expenses, while the creditor restrained them and demanded payment first. The same applies during a person's lifetime: if he is seen dissipating his assets, the creditors may restrain him, as stated in siman 73:10. This is also implied by the conclusion, 'and likewise if he comes, etc.' These are the words of my father and teacher, the Gaon, of blessed memory, and they appear correct. A proof is that even a wife may act this way, as stated in Even HaEzer 118:18. Although here the relatives may prevent him, one may distinguish between blood relatives and relatives by marriage, such as a wife or brother-in-law. Alternatively, Even HaEzer may concern a case where a charity fund will provide burial, whereas here there is no such fund, and nevertheless another creditor may restrain the expenditure. See Even HaEzer there, the Bach at length, Piskei Rekanati, simanim 917 and 556, and Maadanei Melech, chapter Mi Shemet. The Bach's ruling there also appears difficult, especially his statement that 'such is the common practice,' for custom is inapplicable to an uncommon event, as the Rambam writes in the laws of forbidden foods. This requires study. See also siman 275 and Even HaEzer, p. 84.
