To delay his burial. See Sema, seif katan 10, through his statement that he does not wish to trust the family members even with an oath. At first glance this is astonishing, for then the deceased would always remain unburied until the relatives paid from their own funds, which is unreasonable. It therefore appears that he means the heirs do not wish to swear, while the other family members offer to swear but the creditor does not trust them, and the creditor delays the burial until the heirs swear. Alternatively there is a scribal error and the text should read that he does not wish to trust the family members except with an oath. This too is strained. The principal explanation is that of the Shach in the name of Avi Mori. The creditor seeks to prevent burial expenses from being paid out of the estate until his debt is first satisfied, because if the estate funds are spent on burial there may not remain enough to pay the debt. Accordingly, the distinction regarding money in the charity fund is also clear. If the charity fund has enough money for burial, even a relative may delay payment from the estate, because he can argue either way: if estate funds remain after the creditor is paid, the charity fund can afterward recover its burial expense from the estate; and if nothing remains, the charity fund is in any event obligated to bury the deceased. But where the charity fund lacks money for burial, if burial is delayed until the debt is paid the deceased may come to disgrace. A relative upon whom burial is incumbent may not permit that.
