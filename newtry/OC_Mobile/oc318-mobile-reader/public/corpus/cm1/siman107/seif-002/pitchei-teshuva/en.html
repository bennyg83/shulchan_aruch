To delay his burial. See Baer Heitev and the responsum of Chacham Tzvi. Chacham Tzvi explains that the case concerns powerful heirs who, once the deceased is buried, will prevent the creditor from recovering what is rightfully his. Because the deceased left assets and the heirs are legally obligated to pay, but refuse, the creditor may delay burial until his claim is secured. Tumim rejects this explanation sharply, stating that it is inconceivable to leave the deceased unburied merely because his sons are violent. What can the dead man, lying helpless like a stone, do about their conduct? Tumim therefore says this interpretation should not be accepted. The correct explanation, he says, is that of the Shach in the name of his teacher and of Maadanei Yom Tov in the chapter Mi SheMet, letter 100: the estate contains only enough for burial expenses and nothing beyond that. Netivot HaMishpat likewise explains the law this way.
