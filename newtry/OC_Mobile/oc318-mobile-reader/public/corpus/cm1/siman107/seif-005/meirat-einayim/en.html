If the lender seized movable property. The Tur and Mechaber already explained in siman 64 the law of seizure under Talmudic law, which is effective only during the debtor's lifetime, and that after the enactment of the Geonim it is effective even after death. They repeat it here in its proper place among the laws of collection from heirs. If the debt is known, seizure is unnecessary because the lender can recover through court. The practical case is where the debt is not established and the lender seized without witnesses, so that he has a migo to say that he never seized the property. The same applies if he seized before witnesses but they do not now see the property in his hands, because he is believed to say, up to its value, "I seized it and later returned it to them," since even one who stole in the presence of witnesses need not return in the presence of witnesses, as explained in simanim 45, 72, and 133.
