An earlier creditor does not have priority. See Baer Heitev in the name of the Sema, who writes that the novelty here is that the heirs are also exempt and this is not considered, etc., as he wrote in seif 4. See Responsa Shevut Yaakov, part 2, end of siman 163, who writes that although the Shach in seif 4, seif katan 8, challenges the Sema concerning heirs who sold their father's land to a place where the creditor cannot collect it, and holds that they are liable because they damaged another's lien, nevertheless the primary ruling here follows the Sema. First, even there the Shach's words are not sufficiently compelling to extract money from a party in possession against major authorities who disagree, and the heirs can say that they rely on those authorities, as Knesset HaGedolah writes. Second, even the Shach may have ruled that way only where the orphans received the sale proceeds. Where they paid a later creditor and no proceeds ever reached the heirs, the Shach certainly agrees that they are exempt. This explains why he rules without qualification in accordance with this view in seif 11. See Ketzot HaChoshen and Netivot HaMishpat, who distinguish differently. Shevut Yaakov further rules for this reason that heirs who spent estate funds on necessary burial expenses, whether in proper order or not, to save the deceased from disgrace, are exempt when afterward the widow and creditor find the estate insufficient. Since burial expenses are also a lien on the estate, their payment is like paying a later creditor, for which the heirs are exempt. See there at length.
