To his wife and to his son, etc. Relative to his son, his wife is treated like an outsider. Ordinarily, one assumes that he appointed his son only as guardian, while giving his wife half the estate as a true gift. If he wrote, "half my property to my son and half my property to my wife," the son's half is also a gift, because it resembles a gift of part of the estate first, with the remainder reserved. Choshen Mishpat 246:10 states this law regarding a son and an outsider. The same appears to apply to a son and wife. One might distinguish slightly because an outsider would not ordinarily be made guardian, whereas a wife could be. If he mentioned the son first, perhaps the son receives a gift because of the remainder, while the wife was appointed guardian. But since the Rosh explicitly says that with respect to the son the wife is like an outsider, there is no basis to distinguish. If he wrote two-thirds to his son and one-third to his wife, the wife's third is a gift and the son is guardian over the two-thirds. See the cited Choshen Mishpat seif.
