An earlier creditor has no claim. See Baer Heitev citing the Sema, who explains that the novelty is that the heirs themselves are also exempt and are not treated as having damaged the earlier creditor's lien. Responsa Shevut Yaakov, volume 2, end of siman 163, writes that although the Shach in seif 4, seif katan 8, disagrees with the Sema regarding heirs who sell their father's land to a place from which the creditor cannot collect and holds them liable as one who damages another's lien, the primary law here follows the Sema. First, the Shach's ruling there is not sufficiently established to extract money from one in possession against major authorities who disagree, so the heirs may plead that the law follows those authorities. Second, even the Shach may have imposed liability only where the heirs received the sale proceeds. Here, where they paid a later creditor and no money came to the heirs, the Shach would agree they are exempt. Shevut Yaakov therefore rules that heirs who spent estate assets on burial needs, whether in strict priority or even out of the usual order in order to save the deceased from disgrace, are not personally liable when the remaining estate is insufficient for the widow and creditors. Burial itself is a charge upon the estate, and paying it is analogous to paying a later creditor, for which the heirs are exempt.
