To delay his burial. See Baer Heitev, who cites Responsa Chacham Tzvi. Chacham Tzvi writes that in his view the case concerns violent heirs. If the deceased is buried first, the creditor will no longer be able to recover what is his from them. Therefore he may delay the burial, since the deceased left assets in circumstances that obligate the heirs to pay, but they refuse to do so. See there. Urim VeTumim rejects his words and writes that there is no basis at all to delay the burial of a dead person because his sons are violent. What can the deceased, who lies like a stone with no one to turn him, have done? Therefore this view should be disregarded. The primary explanation is that of the Shach in the name of Avi Ezri, and likewise Ma'adanei Yom Tov, chapter Mi SheMet, letter 100, that the case is where there are assets sufficient for burial needs and no more. Netivot HaMishpat likewise writes so. See there.
