But movable property that he sold, etc. The Tur says the lender does not rely on movable property because the borrower can hide it. That reason alone is insufficient, for even if the borrower made the movable property an explicit apotiki, so that the lender clearly relied on it, the Tur and Mechaber below in siman 117 rule that it cannot be collected from purchasers. The Gemara explains the reason: a lien on movable property has no publicity, so a purchaser cannot know to avoid it. The Tur's reason teaches a different point: even before the market-protection enactment, or in a case of successive lenders where that enactment does not apply, there is no rule of priority in movable property because the lender does not rely on it. The second reason teaches that even when he definitely relied on the movable property by making it an explicit apotiki, he still cannot collect it from purchasers because the lien lacks publicity.
If he gave it as a deathbed gift. A deathbed gift takes effect only after death, whereas the creditor's lien already operates during the debtor's lifetime. See also siman 292.
If he encumbered movable property together with land, etc. This rule was explained at the beginning of siman 60. The acquisition of movable property through agav is learned from the verse, "Their father gave them silver and gold and precious things together with fortified cities." The source rules are explained below at the beginning of siman 202.
He can collect the movable property, etc. The Tur concludes that this applies if he wrote d'akni, as Baal HaTerumot does. Rambam does not mention it, and where the movable property was already in the debtor's possession at the time of the loan and was later sold there is no logical need for d'akni. The Tur likely speaks generally because it is often difficult to prove when particular movable items entered the debtor's possession, so customary forms include both agav and d'akni.
Even if he sold the land first, etc. Maggid Mishneh explains that this is specifically where the debtor owned that land at the time of the original lien, so the lien took effect from the writing. I added that since the creditor could, if necessary, collect that land from its purchaser, it is treated for his benefit as though still available to support the agav lien over the movable property.
Provided that he wrote, etc. This refers back to every lien of movable property through agav: the document must strengthen it so that it not be treated as asmachta.
Not as asmachta and not as a standard-form clause. The Tur explains that the lender's primary reliance is on land. The additional lien on movable property resembles a guaranty that operates if land does not suffice, and therefore the document must state that it is not an asmachta, to show genuine reliance on the movable property. It must also state that it is not merely copied from a standard form but intended as an actual binding lien.
Or he writes that he encumbers them from now. Rambam and the Mechaber hold that "from now" alone removes the asmachta concern. The Tur's wording mentioning both "from now" and an important beit din should be read disjunctively: either one suffices, not that both are required. Rama rules likewise.
