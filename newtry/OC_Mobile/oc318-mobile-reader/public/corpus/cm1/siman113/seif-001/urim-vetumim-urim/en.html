But movable property that he sold. The lender did not rely upon movable property because it lacks publicity. Even if the debtor designated it as an apotiki, the designation has no public notice. See Tumim for my reconciliation of Tur and why Sema's question does not arise.
Even if he warned, etc. The Sages did not distinguish in this lien rule. Even if the purchaser personally knew of the creditor's claim, no lien capable of recovery from the purchaser takes effect.
And if he gave them as a deathbed gift. This is after the Geonic enactment allowing creditors to collect inherited movable property. Therefore movable property given by deathbed gift may likewise be collected. Under Talmudic law, when movable property was not collected from heirs, certainly it could not be collected from a deathbed donee.
And if he encumbered, etc. If the note did not expressly include after acquired property, the creditor may recover only movable property the debtor already owned at the time of the loan, not property acquired later. For disputes concerning this, see above simanim 112 and 104. If the note contains an after acquired property clause, subsequently acquired movable property is also encumbered. See above, siman 112, where all agree that in this setting we do not presume the after acquired clause merely as a scribal omission.
Even if he sold the land, etc. Since the debtor owned land when he created the lien, the lien on the movable property already took effect. The later sale of the land therefore does not remove the lien from the movables.
Provided that he wrote to him. The document must state that the arrangement is not an asmachta. The apparent structure is that the primary lien rests on land, while movable property is only secondary security if land is unavailable. A conditional obligation of that kind resembles an asmachta, and therefore the document should state that it is not one. This is not a complete asmachta, however. If it were, merely writing 'not an asmachta' would not cure it, because a true asmachta remains ineffective despite such language. Since this is only an asmachta like concern, the formula is effective. See Tumim concerning a note that mentions movable property and land without agav, which under Talmudic law permits collection from heirs, and whether it likewise requires the anti asmachta clause. The matter is uncertain.
And not like standard boilerplate forms. This prevents us from saying that the language was inserted merely to beautify the document and was never intended to create a real lien. See Tur and what I wrote above in siman 42.
From now, etc. According to the Mechaber, who holds that 'from now' cures any asmachta, this plainly works here. Even according to Tur, who holds that a full asmachta requires both 'from now' and an important beit din, here the obligation is one degree less severe because it is not a true asmachta, and 'from now' alone suffices. Sema writes that an important beit din is also sufficient as an alternative, but this is incorrect. Rosh does not hold that 'from now' alone cures a complete asmachta, as Rosh writes in the chapter Eizehu Neshech. Only here is it effective because the asmachta is incomplete. See Tumim concerning whether a formal kinyan has the same effect as 'from now,' and whether a dated note, where below in siman 258 we say that the date proves present effect, is sufficient. I wrote there that this may depend upon the dispute whether 'from now' cures a complete asmachta. I also concluded that 'from now' concerning movable property together with land can operate only on movable property already owned at the time of the lien. It cannot presently attach to movable property acquired later, because at that earlier time such property was not yet in the debtor's possession and there is nothing upon which 'from now' can operate.
