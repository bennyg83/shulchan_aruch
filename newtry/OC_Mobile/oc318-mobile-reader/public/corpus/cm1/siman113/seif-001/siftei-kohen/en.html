But movable property that he sold. The Tur explains that a creditor does not rely upon movable property because the debtor can conceal it. The Sema objects that this reason is insufficient, since even if the debtor made the movable property an express apotiki, so that the creditor certainly relied upon it, the Tur and Mechaber rule in siman 117 that it still cannot be recovered from purchasers. The Bach's ruling that an express apotiki may be recovered when the purchaser was warned is not correct. It appears that the Tur means that even after the Geonic enactment permitting collection from the movable property of heirs, and even though movable property in our times is generally relied upon like land, a creditor still does not rely upon it against purchasers or gift recipients because it can be concealed. The Rosh rules accordingly in Kiddushin. A deathbed gift is different. The Tur's language here is based upon that ruling of the Rosh. See what I wrote at the beginning of siman 107.
