He encumbers them to him from now. See Tumim, who writes that the expression "from now" is ineffective with respect to movable property that the debtor did not own at the time of the encumbrance. This requires examination in light of the Rashba in the third chapter of Kiddushin, who writes that according to Rabbi Meir, although one who transfers an item not yet in existence may retract, if he wrote "from now" he cannot retract. If so, this should certainly apply to an encumbrance, which according to Rabbi Meir is stronger than a transfer, as the Shach writes in siman 112. Tumim further expresses doubt concerning a kinyan, which we rule takes effect from now, and likewise where the date of a document demonstrates that it is effective from now, whether these also suffice here. In my view, however, we should not extend this novelty beyond what was expressly stated. Even the effectiveness of "from now" is not mentioned in the Gemara and at first glance is contrary to reason; rather, the poskim, in the breadth of their understanding, introduced this rule. We should therefore not add to it. Only an explicit "from now" is effective, and not other indications, as stated above. See Sema, seif katan 10, where there is a textual error. It should read: one who transfers slaves, they are not acquired; the word meaning "become encumbered" is a textual error and should read "are acquired."
