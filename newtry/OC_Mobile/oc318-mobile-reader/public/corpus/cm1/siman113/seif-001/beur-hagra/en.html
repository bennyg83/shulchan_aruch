Even if he warned... This appears from Bava Basra, where testimony concerning the property is accepted and remote suspicions are not entertained. See above, siman 37 seif 21, where even distant concerns are discussed.
And if he gave them... Bava Basra 133a says that a deathbed gift is weaker than Torah inheritance in this regard. Rabbi Yosef Migash derives that an oral creditor may collect from a deathbed gift. Likewise, if movables are given by deathbed gift, a creditor may collect from them whether his loan is written or oral, because the Sages treated a deathbed gift like inheritance, and in some respects even weaker than inheritance. See Bava Basra 149a.
Even if he sold... From the Gemara's statement that the recipient never had land, which, in context, means at the relevant time. Ramban explains this.
Or that he writes... Baal HaTerumos, following Ramban, explains that the clause must be drafted to avoid the character of an asmachta, because a lien on movables resembles reliance on uncertain future assets. Land is different because ordinary liens are relied upon. A present-effect clause removes asmachta, as stated in Bava Metzia and ruled in siman 207. Even according to the dissenting gloss there, here a present-effect clause alone can suffice because this is not a full asmachta. Merely writing "not as an asmachta" would not cure a genuine asmachta. See Tosafos and Beit Yosef.
