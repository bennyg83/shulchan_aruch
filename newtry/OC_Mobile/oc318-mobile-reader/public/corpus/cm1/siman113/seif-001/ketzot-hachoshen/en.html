Maggid Mishneh and Nimukei Yosef rule that even if the debtor sold the land before he later acquired the movables, the original clause subjecting movables together with land can attach to the movables once they are acquired. Ramban explains that once the debtor wrote an after acquired property clause he cannot retract, so the lien is regarded as rooted from the first moment. Rashba allows retraction before acquisition but agrees that if no retraction occurred, the lien relates back. It is not clear why the authorities emphasize that the land was sold before the movables were acquired. Even if the land remained in the debtor's hands, if its lien had already vested before the movables arrived one might think the incidental movable lien could not arise later because the property did not come together at one time. Conversely, if the ongoing land lien can serve as the basis for later incidental lien, that should remain so after the land is sold, because the creditor's lien in the land itself survives the sale. The necessary conclusion is that the movable lien is regarded as having been rooted from the original undertaking, so long as the debtor did not revoke it where revocation is permitted. It does not depend on the land and movables arriving simultaneously in the debtor's hands. Therefore sale of the land before later acquisition of the movables does not prevent the movable lien.
