They need not be piled upon it. Below in siman 202 the Mechaber, following Rambam, distinguishes: if the movable property is physically on the land, acquisition of the land itself acquires the movables even without an explicit statement "acquire them through agav." If they are not on the land, the grantor must say "acquire the movables through agav" or "together with the land." The Tur and Rama require an explicit agav formulation in all cases.
Slaves can become encumbered. Although below in siman 202 the Mechaber rules that slaves cannot be acquired through agav unless they are physically on the land, that concerns complete acquisition. For purposes of a creditor's lien, agav can create a lien even when they are not there, because the lien becomes public. Similar distinctions appear regarding things not yet in existence, which can sometimes become subject to a lien though they cannot yet be fully acquired.
Even through any amount of land, etc. Baal HaTerumot writes that the customary phrase "four cubits in my courtyard" is not literal; even the smallest amount of land suffices.
He should write, "I transferred to him four cubits in my courtyard." Where we have no established knowledge that the grantor owns no land, a generic written statement that the movables were transferred through agav is enough, and we presume he owns some land. But where we positively know of no land in his possession, a vague agav clause may reflect an error by someone who thinks he can transfer movables through land he does not own. In such a case he must expressly transfer land before us, the witnesses must ascertain that he has it, or the document itself must state, "I transferred to him four cubits of land." Then we say that one who knows an actual land transfer is required would not falsely write it, and we presume such land exists even if we do not know where.
