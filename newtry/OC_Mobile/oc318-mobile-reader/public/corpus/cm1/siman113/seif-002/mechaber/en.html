When movable property is encumbered together with land, it need not be piled upon the land. Even movable property that moves by itself, such as animals and slaves, becomes encumbered. Any amount of movable property may be encumbered through even a minute parcel of land. If the borrower owns no land, the lender may convey to him a minute parcel in the lender's field by any method used to acquire land, or lend or rent him such a parcel, and the borrower may then convey through it any movable property he wishes. The precise place through which the movable property is acquired need not be specified. If the lender also owns no land, the borrower writes, 'I conveyed to him four cubits of land in my courtyard, and through it I conveyed the movable property.' Although we do not know that he owns land, a litigant's admission is equivalent to one hundred witnesses against himself. See note 50.
