Even movable property that moves... Although Bava Kamma 12b says that movable property acquired through agav must sometimes be situated on the land, for purposes of a lien physical presence is unnecessary. A lien can be stronger than an acquisition, as explained in siman 112 seif 1.
For example livestock and slaves. Not only livestock, which can be acquired in a sale without being physically piled on the land, as Tosafos proves from Kiddushin and Bava Basra, but even slaves may be included in the lien. This is why Tosafos did not rely solely on the case of a cow; a lien differs from ordinary acquisition.
And if there is no... or he lent it to him or... Gittin 37a: if he owns no land, another may grant him rights in land, even by loaning it.
Or rented it to him. As in Kiddushin, where a place is rented to him.
And if there is no... admission... Bava Basra 149a says that a deathbed person's admission can effect the result. Tosafos explains that an admission document may be used even where the factual premise is strategically supplied. The cases in Bava Metzia and Bava Basra where the person had never owned land show only that people did not ordinarily write an agav transfer unless some land, owned or temporarily granted, existed.
