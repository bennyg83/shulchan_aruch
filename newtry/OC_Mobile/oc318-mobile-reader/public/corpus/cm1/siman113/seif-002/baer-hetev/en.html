And slaves. Although siman 202, seif 8, explains that one who transfers slaves together with land does not acquire them unless they are physically upon the land, that concerns a full acquisition. For a lender's lien to attach through an acquisition of movable property together with land, the transaction has publicity, and the lien takes effect even if the slaves are not on the land. See simanim 60, 112, and 209, where the Tur writes similarly regarding property not yet in existence: a lien may attach even where a full acquisition could not. Sema.

To his detriment. The Sema writes that when we have no established knowledge that he owns no land, or even if the document merely states generally that he transferred movable property together with land, this suffices and we presume he owns some land. But when we know and are established that he owns no land, general wording does not suffice, because perhaps he mistakenly believes one may transfer movable property together with land that is not his. He cannot do so unless he expressly transfers land before us and the witnesses ask him whether he owns land, or the authorization document expressly states, "I transferred to him four cubits of land." In that case, because he knows a land transfer is necessary, we say he would not lie and he certainly owns some land even if we do not know where. See Taz to Yoreh Deah 173, seif katan 9, that regarding the prohibition of interest, a standard clause permitting payment by way of certain coinage does not help if he does not actually possess the necessary property.
