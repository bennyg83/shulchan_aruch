They need not be gathered upon the land. See below, siman 202. Here it is even clearer that physical concentration is unnecessary, because even after acquired property may be encumbered, and certainly presently owned movables need not be gathered on the land.
And slaves may be encumbered. Although for purposes of acquisition we rule that because slaves are movable and move independently they must be physically situated on the land for an agav acquisition, with respect to liens the matter depends upon publicity. Ownership of slaves is publicly known, and therefore they may be encumbered. Rosh writes this in Responsa, rule 80. See Tumim, where I wrote that this ruling requires study.
Even with any amount of land. The four cubits customarily written in deeds is not literal. It is merely standard document language. See Sema.
He should write, 'I have transferred, etc.' A party's admission is equivalent to one hundred witnesses. See Tumim concerning a case where witnesses contradict his admission and say that he never owned land, and whether the admission still helps. If he has a share in a public street, synagogue, or cemetery, whether acquired personally or inherited from ancestors, he may use that share as the land basis for an agav transaction, as Ramban writes in his novellae to Bava Batra.
