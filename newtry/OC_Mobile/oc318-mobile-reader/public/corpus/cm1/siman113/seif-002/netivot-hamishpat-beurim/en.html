The admission of a litigant. Although a contractual stipulation of credibility does not operate against purchasers, an admission does operate here. At the time of the admission the debtor did not thereby prejudice any existing third party, and his admission was legally effective. Once made before witnesses, those witnesses publicized it, creating a kol. Therefore the creditor may collect from property subsequently sold, just as one who admits before witnesses that he owes money may collect from later purchasers because the matter became public and they caused their own loss by purchasing after the kol. This is unlike a contractual credibility clause, where purchasers may have heard that payment was made and then bought in reliance on that fact, so applying the clause against them would unfairly cause them loss. This distinction is straightforward.
