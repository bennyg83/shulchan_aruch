And he gathered its produce. Sema writes that this applies even if he did not first announce, 'I am going to gather,' because once he actually gathered and acquired possession of the produce we do not remove it from him. The next siman concerns produce already gathered but still lying in the owner's field, where he is not considered possessor unless he first announced that he would gather it, relying on the presumption that a person does not ordinarily brazenly gather another's produce. If the owner arrives before he gathers, he may stop him. Sema also cites Ramban and Nimukei Yosef that where the produce was gathered long ago, this can function like such an announcement even if the produce is no longer in his possession. See Taz for disagreement.
Even if there are witnesses, etc. Tosafot in Bava Batra hold that this rule can apply even where he is not currently in possession. Bach compares the mortgage case in siman 317, where a dispute whether a mortgage was for two or three years leaves the land with its owner because the claim ultimately concerns land. The Shach gives two answers, but see Tumim. I rejected both and distinguished instead: here the acquisition concerns produce alone, which is not itself treated as a land-document claim in the same sense. This also accords with Rambam's language.
