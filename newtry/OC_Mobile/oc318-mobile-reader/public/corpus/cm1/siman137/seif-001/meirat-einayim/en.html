One who descends into another person's field, etc. In the Derishah I proved that this applies even if he did not first announce, "I am going to pick the fruit." In that case there is no presumption based on the rule that a person does not brazenly assert such a thing, yet once he has already picked the fruit and physically possesses it, we do not remove it from him. The following seif concerns a case where he has picked the fruit but does not yet possess it, because it remains in the owner's field. There he would not be believed without having first said, "I am going to pick it." When he made that declaration, however, he is believed against the owner who objects before he removes the fruit into his own domain, because a person ordinarily does not brazenly announce that he is going to pick fruit when he has no permission to do so. This applies only after he has already picked the fruit, even though he has not yet taken possession of it. If the owner objects before the fruit is picked, he can prevent the person from picking. The mere presumption created by the declaration, "I am going to pick," is not sufficient, because otherwise any brazen person could announce this knowing that the owner would thereafter be unable to stop him. I further wrote, based on Ramban's novellae and Nimukei Yosef, that if he had already been picking the produce of the field for an extended period, that itself is equivalent to having announced, "I am going to pick," for this purpose. Even if the fruit is not yet in his domain or possession, the owner cannot recover what has already been picked, because people ordinarily do not act with such brazenness without permission. See there for additional reconciliation of the Tur and resulting practical differences. The Tur and Mechaber write in seif 2, "if the owner of the field and tree is not here," because if he were present, it would be obvious that beit din does not intervene to stop the picking. The owner himself is there and could object if he had not sold or otherwise granted the right. See the Derishah for a fuller explanation. Regarding the declaration, "I am going to pick," the Tur records two opinions whether it must be made publicly before an assembled group or whether a private declaration before a few people suffices. According to both opinions, however, some declaration is required, contrary to Tosafot. I discussed all this at length in the Derishah and filled in matters that the Beit Yosef stated only briefly.
