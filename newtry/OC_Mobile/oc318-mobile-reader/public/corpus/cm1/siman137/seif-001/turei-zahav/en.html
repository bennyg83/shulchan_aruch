(Seif 2) I will go and gather. Sema writes that according to Ramah an express statement is at least required. This does not appear correct. The Tur writes that Rabbi Hizkiyah says it is not necessary that he publicly announce, “I will go gather,” and that acting privately is also sufficient; Ramah says specifically in public, but not in the manner of theft or robbery. If Ramah referred only to the statement, how could he describe the alternative as theft? Necessarily he refers to the conduct itself. “Publicly” means either an advance declaration or the act itself performed openly, even without a declaration. Rabbi Hizkiyah holds that neither an express declaration nor public conduct is essential, and that the act itself may even be private. Ramah requires publicity either in the statement or in the act; if everything is concealed like theft, it is ineffective. Nevertheless, if he acts as an actual thief, waiting until all people leave before entering, Rabbi Hizkiyah would also agree that he gains no credibility. Tosafot likewise agree. Ramah's phrase “in the manner of theft” therefore means private conduct as the opposite of public conduct, not necessarily every degree of concealment.
