One who enters his fellow's field, etc. Even if there are witnesses that he gathered the produce, Tosafot on Bava Batra 33b, s.v. I ta'in, prove that even if he is no longer physically holding it he is believed because a person would not be so brazen. If the rule applied only where he still held the produce, the Gemara would not need the reason that he would not be brazen, for his possession itself would suffice, just as with movable property not ordinarily lent or rented. The Bach disagrees and holds that he is believed only while still in possession, arguing from the law of a mortgage where one says the term was three years and the other says two. Were it not that the truth will ordinarily become known, possession would not help because the dispute derives from land and we say land remains in the owner's possession. So too here. The Shach in seif katan 1 answers that there the document supports the owner because it merely states two years, but this is insufficient. The Gemara itself explains the rule by saying that produce is presumed in the possession of the one who consumed it, and then challenges from Rav Nachman concerning an intercalated month where the entire amount goes to the landlord because land remains in its owner's possession. There no document supports either side. Moreover, the Shach himself wrote above in siman 42 in the name of the Nimukei Yosef that the document does not support the owner there, since we see that he allowed the claimant to consume the produce. Nor is the Shach's further distinction, that in the mortgage case the claimant originally entered with permission and is therefore not brazen whereas here he is, understandable. Tosafot's very question is why the Gemara needed the reason that he is not brazen at all if simple possession suffices. The Shach's words therefore require study. It appears that Tosafot understand the plea, I entered for the produce, not as a mortgage or lease of the field, for if so how could the Gemara say that documents are not ordinarily written for produce? Mortgages and leases are acquired by document like the land itself, as stated below in siman 195, and documents are certainly written for them. Rather, as Rashbam explains, he claims that the owner sold him the produce for ten years. This is not a sale of something not yet in existence, for the case can be established through an oath or similar mechanism, as Kesef Mishneh explains on Rambam, chapter 23 of Hilchot Malveh. Thus the Gemara says documents are not ordinarily made for produce, because produce itself is not acquired by a land document, just as the Tur writes in siman 133 in the name of Baal HaIttur that movables do not acquire a three year chazakah because they are not subject to documentation. If so, the difficulty disappears. Here he acquired only produce, which is governed entirely by the law of movables, and the principle that land remains in its owner's possession does not apply. In the mortgage or lease case, however, the dispute concerns the duration of a temporary property interest in the land itself. A mortgage or lease is a land acquisition, a sale for a time, and therefore the uncertainty concerns ownership of land. We then properly say that land remains in the owner's possession. This is simple and clear, and the Rambam's language in chapter 9 of Hilchot To'en points the same way.
