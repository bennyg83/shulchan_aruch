(See there: Even if he gathered them). The Tur says that even if the owners came and found him in the field before he brought the produce home, he is believed. Beit Yosef says the Tur learned this from Tosafot, who validate the claim while the produce is in a field belonging to neither party, since they did not say that he left it in the field. This is difficult, because neutral premises are plainly no worse than the field owner's domain in many laws, and Tosafot should have stated the greater novelty. It appears that the Tur means that after gathering, the produce is in the gatherer's hands or vessels, not lying on the ground. Produce lying on the ground is still part of the act of gathering. There is no difference between produce still attached and produce detached but not yet taken. In either case the rationale that a person would not brazenly gather another's produce does not yet establish possession. Certainly one cannot distinguish between fruit detached on the ground and fruit that a person then picks up from the ground. The Tur therefore speaks where he gathered it and holds it in his hands or vessels. He is not yet treated as fully established in possession because he still stands in the field and everyone sees that it came from that field. This is why the Tur adds the otherwise unnecessary phrase “before he brought it home.”
