Beit din prevents him. The reason is that where a person says, "I am going to pick the fruit," the fruit is naturally ready to be picked and the presumption that a person does not brazenly announce an unauthorized act supports him. We therefore say that the owner probably allowed him into the field as a sharecropper or renter for the produce. If he says instead, "I am going to cut down the tree," and the tree is not ordinarily ready for cutting, we assume he entered without permission and intends to damage the owner. Beit din therefore restrains him. It follows that if the tree is in fact ready to be cut down, beit din does not restrain him. The Mordechai writes this in chapter Kol HaNishba'in. See Darkei Moshe.
