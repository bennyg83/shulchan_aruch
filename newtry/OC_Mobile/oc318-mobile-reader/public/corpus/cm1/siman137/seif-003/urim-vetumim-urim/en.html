Beit din stops him. The presumption that a person does not brazenly take another's produce applies where he says he will gather produce that stands ready for gathering. We can plausibly say the owner allowed him to enter as tenant or sharecropper. It does not apply where he says he will cut down a tree not intended for cutting. That would be destructive and presumptively unauthorized. From this Mordechai derives that if the tree is in fact ready to be cut, beit din need not stop him. Sema. Shach cites Raavad and Ramban as holding that beit din never stops him in this setting.
