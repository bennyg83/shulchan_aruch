In Rabbeinu's words. A purchase is not completed for the buyer unless the owner conveys it to him. Thus, if a deposit is already in the depositary's possession and he privately decides to acquire it for a known price that the owner had said he would accept, he does not acquire. Many ask why his premises do not acquire for him. The straightforward meaning is that the owner merely disclosed willingness to sell for that price but had not yet found a buyer and therefore left the object as a deposit. The depositary cannot, without the owner's knowledge, decide to keep it and later pay the stated price, because no one conveyed it to him. In addition, the depositary's premises are treated as the depositor's premises with respect to the deposited object, as the Rashbam explains.
