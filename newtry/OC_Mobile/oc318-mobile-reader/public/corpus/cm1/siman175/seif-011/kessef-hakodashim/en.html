To the son of the one who bought it. The seller's son has a status similar to an adjoining owner. An unmarried daughter who is not under her father's name, or a divorced or widowed daughter, perhaps has the same status. Still, perhaps this preference is limited specifically to a son because inheritance naturally devolves upon him. If the daughter is married, the inclination is stronger that she does not have this status, because even if she conducts business, her property is generally under her husband's domain. Although a son-in-law is sometimes called a son, he may not have the law of a neighboring owner. A father or brother, where it is known there is no other heir and the only uncertainty is who will die first, perhaps has a status similar to a son. This requires further study.
