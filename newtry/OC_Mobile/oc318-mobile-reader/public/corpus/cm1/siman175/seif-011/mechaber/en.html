If the sold field has four adjoining owners, one on each side, all have equal rights in it. They divide it diagonally so that each receives a portion extending along the face of his own field. If there are five adjoining owners, three on three sides and two on the fourth, the two on the fourth side are counted as one and together receive one share, equal to each share received by the other three. This applies when they all come at once. If one preceded the others and removed the purchaser, he alone acquires it. Even if there are many adjoining owners and one adjoins only a very small portion, if he preceded the others and removed the purchaser, or purchased it first from the seller, he acquires it. Rama: Initially, however, the seller should sell to all of them together. Some disagree. If one bought first and the others claim that they too wished to buy, but he threatened to harm them and had the power to do so, and they have witnesses, then when the coercion ceases they may remove him from their respective shares. See below, seif 33. If only some adjoining owners are present and others are in another country, those present may remove the purchaser and the property belongs to them alone.
