And they are divided in such a dyslexon:
Nevertheless, initially, etc., and some disagree. From the Beit Yosef and Darkei Moshe it is clear that according to the students of the Rashba, whenever several neighboring owners come simultaneously, even if the seller wishes to sell to only one of them, that one may not purchase the whole for himself. He must allow the others to share in the purchase, even against the seller's preference, in the same manner Rama wrote immediately above where the seller does not wish to sell to a neighboring owner yet the neighboring owner purchases against his will. Thus Rama's phrase, "nevertheless, initially," excludes only a case where one already purchased before the others. "Some disagree" refers to the alternative explanation cited in Darkei Moshe. Examine the sources.
