Seif 22: Except where he conveyed it to him. This presents a dilemma. If the adjoining owner conveyed his field before the purchaser bought, it is obvious that the grantee can remove the purchaser. If he conveyed it afterward, seif 15 rules that a purchaser who later buys the adjoining owner's field cannot remove the original purchaser. It appears that when one buys from the adjoining owner merely in order to remove the first purchaser, he cannot do so, because the first purchaser acquired from the landowner before him. Here, however, the adjoining owner expressly conveys the field on condition that the grantee stand in his place regarding the right of adjoining ownership. Although an adjoining owner cannot sell the bare right itself, because such an acquisition has no object upon which to take effect, that is the case discussed by Rabbeinu in seif 29. If he sells the body of the field and says, “I sell you my field and place you in my stead regarding the adjoining right to this parcel,” the transfer is effective. This is clear from the Rema's wording, “except where he conveyed it to him,” which refers back to the attempted sale of the bare right.
