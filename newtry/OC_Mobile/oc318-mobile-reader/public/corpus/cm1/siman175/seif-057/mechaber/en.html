If a person mortgaged his field and later sold it to the mortgagee, an adjoining owner cannot remove the mortgagee. If the mortgagor sold it to another person, even one who is not an adjoining owner, the lender cannot take it from him. Rama: Some say that even if the adjoining owner bought first, the lender may remove him. This is the ruling to follow, provided beit din sees that the loan was not a device arranged with the seller to defeat the adjoining owner's right. A Jew who lends a non-Jew money on the security of the non-Jew's field or house may remove a later purchaser. If the loan was secured by a house but not by the cellar beneath it, the lender has no right in the cellar; if he buys the cellar, the adjoining owner may remove him.
