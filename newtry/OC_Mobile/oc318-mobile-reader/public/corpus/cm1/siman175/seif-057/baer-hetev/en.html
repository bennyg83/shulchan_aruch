The Nimukei Yosef writes that even according to the view that a lender holding a mortgaged house may remove an adjoining owner who purchased land next to that house, as the Rama rules at the end of the following seif, that view nevertheless agrees here. There the mortgaged house stands on the land adjoining the purchased property, so they are regarded almost as one unit. A cellar under the house, however, or another house standing on land not mortgaged to the lender, has no connection to the lender at all. The Darkei Moshe likewise records this rule concerning a cellar after first ruling that a lender has the status of an adjoining owner. So writes the Sema. The Taz writes that this is extremely difficult. Why should a lender be more of an adjoining owner with respect to land beside the mortgaged property than with respect to land directly beneath it? Moreover, the Beit Yosef writes that if there is another unmortgaged house beside the mortgaged one, the lender has no preemption right to it, and for that reason he also rules that the lender has no right to the cellar. Since the Rama rules in seif 58 that the lender has a right with respect to adjoining land, certainly he should have such a right to the cellar beneath it. I can only say that the Rama wrote the present ruling according to the opinion that a lender has no adjoining-owner status with respect to a neighboring field. According to the Rama's own ruling that he does have that status, all the more so should it apply here. Taz.
