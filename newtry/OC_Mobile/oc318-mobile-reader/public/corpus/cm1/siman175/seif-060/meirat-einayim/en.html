For rental rights are subject to the law of a neighboring owner. From what the Mechaber writes in seif 45, that if the purchaser says to the neighboring owner, "The field on whose strength you seek to remove me is merely rented or mortgaged in your hands," etc., it is evident that the Mechaber holds, and Rama too, since he wrote nothing there in disagreement, and the Tur likewise states the rule without qualification at the end of seif 65, that a renter or a lender holding a mortgage cannot remove the purchaser of the old field adjoining that property. What Rama writes here, that rental rights are subject to the law of a neighboring owner, means only that if the renter or mortgagee himself first purchases the property, another neighboring owner cannot remove him. The Mechaber follows his own view: just as a renter or mortgagee does not have the status of a neighboring owner so as to remove someone else, so too he does not have such status in the opposite direction, and an ordinary neighboring owner may remove the renter even if the renter purchased first. The Beit Yosef states this explicitly. See what I wrote above at the end of seif 58.
