The seller. The Sema writes that this applies specifically where the seller suffers no loss by selling to the adjoining owner, because the adjoining owner is prepared to purchase on the same terms as the purchaser. Otherwise, compelling the sale would not be included in the mandate to do what is good and upright. Even where the seller suffers no loss, the law of the adjoining owner was not enacted against the seller himself, but against the purchaser, who must withdraw from the purchase, thereby leaving the seller to sell to the adjoining owner. This is as the Tur and Shulchan Aruch explain from seif 23 onward. See also Responsa Maharam Alshakar, siman 89.
