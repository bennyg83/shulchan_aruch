When a person sells his land to another, whether he sold it himself, through an agent, or through beit din, the owner of the adjoining property may pay the purchaser the purchase price and remove him. Even if the purchaser is a Torah scholar, neighbor, or relative of the seller, while the adjoining owner is an unlearned person and distant from the seller, the adjoining owner takes precedence and may remove the purchaser. Rama: Even if the seller declares that he would rather not sell at all than sell to the adjoining owner, nevertheless the adjoining owner acquires it against the seller's will. The outside purchaser is regarded as the adjoining owner's agent in every respect. The witnesses to the purchaser's acquisition may therefore write the deed in the adjoining owner's name, and no new act of acquisition is required. If the property appreciated, the purchaser must transfer it at the price he paid; if it depreciated, the adjoining owner must take it at that price if he still desires it. If the purchaser repaired and improved it before the adjoining owner demanded it, he is treated as one who entered with permission, the improvement is appraised for him, and he receives the more favorable measure. If he improved it after the demand, he is treated as one who entered without permission. If he uprooted plantings or caused damage, the amount of the damage is deducted from the price, even if the damage occurred before the adjoining owner appeared. Fruits consumed after the adjoining owner came with the money and demanded removal must be paid for; fruits consumed beforehand need not be paid for. If the purchaser borrowed money before the adjoining owner appeared, the creditor's lien does not attach to the property and the creditor may not seize it from the adjoining owner.
