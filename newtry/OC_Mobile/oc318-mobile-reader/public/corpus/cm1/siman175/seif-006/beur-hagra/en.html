Whether his agent sold it or... There, regarding payment of the king's tax and provisions, etc. Only beit din or a widow may sell, and a widow has the status of an agent, as stated in Kesubos 58. Since the ruling here is stated without qualification, it applies in every case.
And even. Since they said "take precedence" rather than "the law does not apply."
And even if he says. This also appears there from the discussion of subterfuge. If the seller had the power to do this, there would be no need for him to resort to subterfuge. See the Rosh there and Rav Hai Gaon's explanation. The same applies to a gift with a warranty; see below, seif 26.
He is considered his agent. There, "I sent you..."
And no kinyan is required. As stated above, siman 190 seif 4.
And if he improved it. There, "I sent you to improve, not to impair."
With permission, and they appraise... Bava Batra 42b: like one who descends into property with permission.
And if after. After he removed him from his agency.
And if he uprooted. There, "and not to distort."
And if he consumed... he need not pay. For he is no worse than a mortgage in a place where the debtor may remove the creditor. But after the adjoining owner comes, he must pay, as in the law of a mortgage there on 67b: "He said, I will go and bring..."
And if he borrowed. As above, since he is only an agent. Kesubos 97a, regarding a widow who sold, etc., and she is like an agent as above. Since the Gemara speaks simply of liability, it implies that liability is only against the orphans, even if she herself makes the claim.
