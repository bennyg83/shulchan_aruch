Seif 4: Likewise, if a creditor came. Rav and Shmuel dispute the case of brothers who divided an estate and a creditor later collected one brother's portion. Rav says the division is void, while Shmuel says the other brother need not compensate him because divided brothers are like purchasers without warranty. The halachah follows Rav. The Gemara and authorities formulate this only regarding brothers, not two purchasers who bought separate fields from one seller and then divided them, where the seller's creditor collected one purchaser's share. This indicates that ordinary purchasers do not compensate one another. The rule for brothers rests on their status as heirs standing in their father's place and jointly responsible for his debt. The Rashbam explains that Shmuel treats them as purchasers without warranty because they paid no money to one another. Although the halachah does not follow Shmuel regarding brothers, his reasoning shows that true purchasers who divided have no mutual warranty. The Rosh likewise says that brothers are treated as heirs here because children stand in their father's place to pay his debt equally. Maharshal also bases the rule upon the son's standing in the father's place. Thus, partners who purchased from one seller and later divided have no claim against one another if a creditor takes one share. Even brothers ordinarily have no mutual warranty after division, as stated in siman 173. Only their preexisting obligation to pay their father's creditor equally creates this exception. The Sema's ruling that ordinary purchasing partners are like brothers because their division was mistaken is therefore difficult. If that were the reason, Rashi and the Rosh would not need the principle that both brothers must pay their father's debt, nor the phrase that a son stands in his father's place. The Gemara itself likewise explains the brothers' liability through their shared filial obligation, not through mistaken division. It is therefore clear that ordinary partners are not comparable to brothers in this matter.
