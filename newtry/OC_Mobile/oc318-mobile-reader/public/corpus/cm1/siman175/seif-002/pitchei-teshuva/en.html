The partners remove the purchaser. See Baer Heitev and Shevut Yaakov, part II, siman 161. A widespread popular assumption held that whenever a person sells his house, any relative, near or distant, can displace the purchaser. Shevut Yaakov rejects this custom where the relative is not actually an adjoining owner. The case before him involved a widow who sold a synagogue seat that had belonged to her husband and had been assigned to her for payment of her ketubah. Members of the husband's family sought to remove the purchaser. He rules that kinship alone creates no right of bar metzra. He proves this from the law that judicial appraisal of property for a creditor may sometimes be reversed, yet if the creditor sold, inherited onward, or gave the property away, it does not revert even to the original debtor, all the more so not merely to relatives. If the synagogue seat were still in the widow's hands and beit din had assigned it to her against the heirs' will, there could be room to discuss the rule of returning judicially appraised property, and the same principle may apply to a wife's ketubah. If, however, the heirs consented to the assignment, the rule does not apply. And once the widow has sold the place to another, even an involuntary original appraisal does not reopen. The settled rule that a sold or transferred appraisal does not return applies equally here. See also the later glosses in this siman.
