The law of the adjoining owner does not apply. This remains true even if the adjoining owner is also a woman or an orphan. So writes the Mordechai in the name of Maharam. See Responsa Rabbi Moshe Alshich, siman 84.
"The buyer of the woman is the buyer of the orphans by the woman, and he is not like an enemy, and he shall see his name as a buyer of the woman and not as an enemy."

A partner has priority over an adjoining owner. The same applies to a tenant according to the Rosh and the Rama there. See Responsa Rabbi Eliyahu ibn Chaim there, who writes that the Rivash, siman 69, disagrees. He concludes that since the authorities dispute the matter, the burden of proof rests on the claimant and the purchaser is regarded as the party in possession. See also Responsa Rabbi Shlomo Kohen associated with book 2, siman 27, and Responsa Maharashdam ibn Sasson, siman 126, page 138b.
