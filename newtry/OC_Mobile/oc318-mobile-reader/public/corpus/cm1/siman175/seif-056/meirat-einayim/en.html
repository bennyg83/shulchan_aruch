It is possible that the manufacturer removes him from the mouth of his name, but rather gives the mask to the owner of the breast as he has been given to him:
And what he wrote, "and if not, he cannot," etc. Meaning, without paying its fair value he cannot displace him. But by paying its fair value it appears that he can displace him. The present possessor is no better than an ordinary purchaser, to whom we say: Take your money and buy elsewhere. The preceding seif 54, which exempts a gift from bar metzra, rests on a reason that does not apply here. See the Perishah. Still, the matter is not entirely certain. One can argue that since the present possessor originally took possession lawfully, because there was no equivalent property available for him to obtain elsewhere without payment, and had he not taken possession this neighboring owner would not have given him its value, the neighbor can no longer remove him against his will even by paying fair value.
