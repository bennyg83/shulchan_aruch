Responsa Ibn Lev, part 2, siman 41, concludes that with respect to a recognized tenancy or possession right in a shop or houses, all opinions agree that the law of the adjoining owner does not apply when one wishes to sell that right to another. See there at length. Responsa Divrei Rivot, siman 59, likewise states that all the great earlier sages and later authorities agreed that no form of such possession right is subject to the law of the adjoining owner. The same appears in Responsa Maharam Alshakar, beginning of siman 81 and simanim 126 and 127, and Responsa Maharashdam, siman 228; see also there simanim 277, 297, and 299. See Responsa Maharshal, siman 43, and Yam Shel Shlomo, chapter Shor Shenagach Et HaParah, siman 33, and chapter HaGozel Batra, siman 24, where he likewise rules that the law of the adjoining owner does not apply to rentals, because in this context we do not say that a rental for its term is like ownership. See the Bach and what I wrote in siman 313, seif 3, siman 310, and siman 334. Shach.
