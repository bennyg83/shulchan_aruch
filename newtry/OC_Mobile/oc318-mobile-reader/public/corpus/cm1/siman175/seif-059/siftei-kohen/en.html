We do not heed him. Responsa Mahari ibn Lev, part 2, siman 41, concludes that with respect to a possessory right in a shop or houses, all authorities agree that the law of the adjoining owner does not apply when the holder wishes to sell that right to another. See there at length. Responsa Divrei Rivot, siman 59, likewise records that all the great earlier sages, and all later authorities as well, agreed that no form of possessory right is subject to the law of the adjoining owner. The same appears in Responsa Rabbi Aharon Sasson, beginning of siman 81 and simanim 126 and 127, and Responsa Maharashdam, siman 228; see also there, simanim 277, 296, and 299. See Responsa Maharshal, siman 43, and Yam Shel Shlomo, chapter HaSocher et HaParah, siman 33, and chapter HaGozel Batra, siman 24. He too rules that the law of the adjoining owner does not apply to a lease, because in this context we do not say that a lease for its term is equivalent to a sale. See the Bach and what I write in simanim 313:3, 310, and 334.
