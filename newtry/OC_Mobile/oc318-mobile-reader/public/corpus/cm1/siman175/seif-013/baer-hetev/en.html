Two. It seems obvious to me that everything depends on there being two fields. Even if he bought both from one seller, the adjoining owner may remove him from one of them. The Mechaber's wording, 'one who purchased from two,' teaches the greater novelty that even then he cannot remove him from only half of what he purchased. What is written in seif 36, 'for he purchased it and the other one together,' means all the other properties, as shown by the fact that the passage begins, 'he sold all his properties,' and concludes, 'for he purchased it and the other one.' Shach.
The seller. The phrase 'the seller of land' is written to teach the greater novelty. It is obvious that if two people sold to two purchasers the adjoining owner can remove one purchaser, but even where one person sold land to two purchasers he may remove one of them. Ibid.
