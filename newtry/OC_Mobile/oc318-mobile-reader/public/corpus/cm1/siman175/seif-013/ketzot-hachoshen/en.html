Sema says that where one buyer purchased two adjacent fields from two different sellers, a neighboring owner can exercise preemption over both. He initially wonders whether the same should be true where the two fields came from one seller, since later law says that when a seller sells all his fields together, the neighboring owner cannot remove the buyer. The distinction is straightforward. In the later case the neighboring owner borders only one of the fields. The seller sold that field together with another field to which the claimant has no neighboring right. The seller need not delay the package sale merely to ask whether the neighbor wants to buy both. Therefore the neighbor cannot undo even the adjacent field. Here, however, the claimant is a neighbor to both fields. He can therefore remove the buyer from both and acquire the whole package. The two rulings concern different configurations.
