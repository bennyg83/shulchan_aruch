Seif 26: Because of settling the land. The Sema infers from the Tur's statement that planting is more important than building that if the adjoining owner wishes to build while the purchaser wishes to plant, the law of adjoining ownership does not apply. In my humble opinion this is astonishing. Rabbeinu would not have omitted such a significant ruling. The Sages preferred an activity that settles the land over one that does not, but they did not abolish adjoining rights whenever both uses promote settlement merely because one is superior. Otherwise every purchaser could say that he intends to plant while the adjoining owner wishes to build, and the entire principle of what is right and good would be nullified. Rabbeinu's words follow the Rosh, who writes that planting trees appears more important than houses, as is evident from the rule that one may change rented premises from a house to a field but not from a field to a house because of settlement of the world. His purpose is only to prove that planting is not classified with ordinary sowing, since elsewhere it is even more important than building. Where the purchaser wants to plant and the adjoining owner wants to build, there is no basis to cancel adjoining rights, since the adjoining owner also contributes to settlement.
