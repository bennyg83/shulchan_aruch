One of. According to Rav Hai Gaon's explanation and according to Rav Nachman; see the Rosh.
And some say. They hold likewise there, and they also bring proof from Rav Nachman that between brothers and partners one may remove the purchaser, whereas by reason of bar metzra one may not. See Beer HaGolah. This follows Rav Hai Gaon's explanation, though at first glance there is no proof. According to Rambam's explanation, which I shall write in seif 12, it is understandable: Rav Nachman holds that a partner may remove the purchaser while an adjoining owner may not, for the Rosh explains in his name, "and if because..." that they are adjoining owners. See the Rosh. It must be that they explain "and if because..." like Rashi, as referring to any adjoining owner. This also appears from the plain meaning of the Gemara, and all the commentators write that the law of an adjoining owner is based on "you shall do what is right and good." According to the Rosh's explanation, however, there is a difficulty: if the law of bar metzra was obvious to them, then according to Rav an adjoining owner must be preferable to a partner. How, then, could the Rosh write the reverse at the end of the seif?
