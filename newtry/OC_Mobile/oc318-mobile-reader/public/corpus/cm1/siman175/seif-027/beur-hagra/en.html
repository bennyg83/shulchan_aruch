(Supplement) Or a structure, etc. The Rif writes: 'Mashukhita means a reed bed, as Scripture states, 'Can a reed grow?' Some explain it as a high and strong structure built to restrain the water. This is what the final chapter of Ohalot teaches: What is the shonit? A place upon which the sea rises in its fury. Others say it is a structure called masua in Arabic.' This explains the phrase, 'and similar things.' The Arukh likewise records three explanations. His first is similar to the first explanation, except that he calls it a high place, based on the mishna, 'What is the shonit?' His second explanation is a reed bed. As a third explanation he writes that some say it means barren trees. Rashi, however, explains it there as a rocky projection. See Bava Batra 109a, Eruvin 100a, and Taanit 23a. This requires study. In our text of Ohalot the reading is, 'Every place upon which the sea,' etc. (End of supplement.)
