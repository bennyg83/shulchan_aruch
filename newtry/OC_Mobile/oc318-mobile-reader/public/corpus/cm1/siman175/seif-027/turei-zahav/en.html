(Seif 58: and this appears primary.) It appears to me that the Rama rules this way only to the extent that if the mortgagee purchases the field adjacent to the mortgaged field, the neighboring landowner cannot displace him. He does not mean that the mortgagee can displace another buyer, because many great authorities in wisdom and number hold otherwise, and it was already ruled above that where the matter is disputed we do not remove property from a purchaser. Our master's wording also implies this. He writes that if the mortgagee already purchased it, the neighboring owner cannot remove him, specifically limiting the ruling to that direction. Therefore, there is no difficulty from our master's citation of Rambam earlier regarding rented or mortgaged land, which implies only that neither type of holder can displace the purchaser. Nimukei Yosef discusses this further. Regarding a cellar beneath the lender's property, however, it appears to me that he can remove the purchaser, as I write nearby.
