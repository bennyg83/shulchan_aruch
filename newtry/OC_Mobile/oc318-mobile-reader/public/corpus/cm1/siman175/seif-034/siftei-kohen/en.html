If the adjoining owner was, etc. See Responsa Maharam Alashkar, siman 118; Responsa Rabbi Levi ibn Chaviv, simanim 60 and 61, at length; Responsa Mabit, siman 26, concerning these laws, and part 2, siman 168; Responsa Maharan Sasson, siman 205; and Responsa Maharashdam, siman 297.
He should inform the adjoining owner's friends, etc., whether he wishes to purchase. The meaning is that the relatives may know with certainty that the adjoining owner wishes to purchase. Therefore the seller must inform close friends of the adjoining owner and ask them whether the adjoining owner wishes to buy. For this reason the text uses the singular, 'wishes.' This is also the Levush's intention. When he writes, 'and they shall inform him,' he means that they shall inform the seller. The Sema did not understand the words of Mahari and the Levush this way and therefore strained to explain why 'wishes' is singular. This too was written in my youth.
