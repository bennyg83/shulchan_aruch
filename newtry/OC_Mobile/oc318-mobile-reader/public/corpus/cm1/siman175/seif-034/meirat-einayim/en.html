That they notify his friends, etc., if he wishes to buy. The responsum states: The seller should go before beit din and say, "So and so wishes to buy it for such and such a price. If the neighboring owner, or another person in his place from among his relatives or friends, wishes to buy it at that price, good. If not, we do not delay the purchaser further in order to notify him, and the purchaser acquires his purchase." This implies that if the neighboring owner is not in the city, there is no need to wait while a messenger is sent to inform him. If none of his relatives or friends in the seller's city wishes to buy on his behalf, the neighboring owner immediately loses his right. According to this, Rama should have written, "if they wish to buy," in the plural, because the subject is the relatives. His language can be explained to mean, "if one of them wishes to buy it on behalf of the neighboring owner." The Ir Shushan, however, writes that the seller should go to beit din, who should notify the neighboring owner's friends or relatives, and they in turn should notify him whether he wishes to buy. That is certainly incorrect, for the responsum says explicitly that we do not delay the sale any further.
