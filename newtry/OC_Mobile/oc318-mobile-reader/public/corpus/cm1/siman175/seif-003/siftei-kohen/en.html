Two brothers who divided, etc. Hagahot Ashri at the end of the chapter HaKones records the following case. Bar Ziza's sharecropper deposited a litra of gold with a certain person. Bar Ziza died, and the sharecropper also died. Bar Ziza left adult and minor sons. Rabbi Yishmael son of Rabbi Yose ruled that half the gold should be given to Bar Ziza's adult sons, while the other half should remain deposited until the minors grew up. After Rabbi Yishmael died, the case came before Rabbi Chiya, who ruled that the remaining half should be given to the sharecropper's sons, while the adult sons of Bar Ziza need not return the half they had received, because they acquired it by order of a beit din. Nor would they give anything to the minors. This is from the Or Zarua. The incident appears in the Yerushalmi, cited by the Rif at the end of the chapter HaKones: 'May the minors say to the adults, Let us divide with you? They may answer, We found a lost object. Rabbi Yitzchak said: There is no difference between the minors and adults except that it is as though it had been given to him as a gift.' It appears to me that Rabbi Yitzchak disagrees, for the Yerushalmi frequently introduces a dispute with the words, 'Rabbi so-and-so said.' His meaning is that there is no distinction between the minors and adults, because the money is regarded as though it had been given as a gift to Bar Ziza, in which case all his heirs must divide it equally. It is possible that Hagahot Ashri understood it this way as well, but ruled that the adults need not give anything because the Yerushalmi presents a dispute and the adults are in possession. According to this, if the minors seized half in a manner in which seizure is effective in a dispute among authorities, it is not removed from them. Maharshal, in the chapter HaKones, siman 32, writes that Bar Ziza's minor sons have no claim against the adults regarding their half, because it is as though that half was given to the adults as a gift or found by them. He apparently understood Rabbi Yitzchak also to deny the minors any share, reading 'there is no claim between the minors and adults,' or emending the text from 'difference' to 'claim,' as appears in recently printed editions of the Rif. Yet all old Rif editions read 'there is no difference'; Hagahot Maimoniyot, chapter 14 of Hilchot Nizkei Mamon, and the Baal HaItur, laws of deposits, page 48a, likewise cite the Yerushalmi with the word 'difference,' and that is also the text of the Yerushalmi itself in the chapter HaKones. The same expression appears in the Yerushalmi, chapter Kol HaNishba'in. This supports my explanation. Moreover, if Rabbi Yitzchak also meant that the minors did not acquire anything, what did he add? He should simply have said, 'It is as though it was given to them as a gift.' Why state that there is no difference between the minors and adults, when the Yerushalmi had already said that the minors have no right to the adults' money? Furthermore, the wording in all Rif editions, 'as though it had been given to him as a gift,' supports my explanation, meaning that it had been given to Bar Ziza, so that his heirs divide equally. Otherwise it should say, 'as though it had been given to them as a gift.' Although the Yerushalmi itself reads 'to them,' this may refer to the heirs as a group: it is not a found object, but as though it had been given as a gift to the adult heirs, in which the other brothers also have a share. The singular wording 'to him,' however, can refer only to the father of the heirs. Rabbi Yitzchak's meaning is that since the adults received the money through inheritance from their father, it is as though the gift had been given to the father, and the minors therefore acquired half. Accordingly, whenever a similar case occurs in which one judge awards half to the adult heirs and a second judge later rules otherwise, there is an unresolved legal doubt whether the adults must give a share to the minors. If the minors seize from the adults, it is not removed from them. See what I wrote above at the end of siman 62. The same principle applies in every comparable case.
