If land was sold in order to pay the king's tax, to pay burial expenses, or to provide food for a wife and daughters, the law of adjoining ownership does not apply. Rama: Some say the same applies to the seller's own sustenance. Even if the seller borrowed for burial expenses, for the support of his wife and daughters, or to pay the tax, when he sells in order to repay the lender, the law of adjoining ownership does not apply. Rama: Some say that even initially, when several people seek to buy, whoever acts first acquires. Others say the adjoining owner takes precedence.
