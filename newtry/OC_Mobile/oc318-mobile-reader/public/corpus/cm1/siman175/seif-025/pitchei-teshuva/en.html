One who mortgages his field. See Chatam Sofer, Choshen Mishpat, siman 11. The mortgagee is treated as one whose interest is already situated beside the property even if he does not live there and does not consume its produce. It is enough that the loan is secured specifically by that land. This is also apparent from Kesef Mishneh, Laws of Neighbors 12:8. Chatam Sofer adds that there is no difference whether the land was mortgaged at the time of the loan or afterward. Certainly, if beit din later placed land in a creditor's hands as security and that arrangement remained for some time, then when the land is eventually transferred to him in satisfaction of the debt an adjoining owner cannot remove him, because his secured interest was already attached to the property. This follows all the more from the rule concerning a mortgage created by the debtor after the loan. He further suggests that the modern registration system recognized by the law of the kingdom, under which the first registered creditor has priority and the owner cannot sell or further encumber the land free of that creditor's claim, is even stronger than an ordinary mortgage interest. When such a creditor later reaches the stage of foreclosure or final transfer, an adjoining owner cannot then invoke bar metzra to displace him, especially where additional supporting considerations are present.
