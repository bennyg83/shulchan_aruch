He is not permitted to purchase. The Tur concludes that this resembles the rule regarding a guardian who sells to others but may not sell to himself. The reason is suspicion. See below siman 185:2, where the Tur cites the Rashba that even where there is no suspicion, an agent cannot purchase for himself because his hand is like the owner's hand and the property cannot pass from one person's control to that same person's control. That reason applies specifically there when the seller seeks to retract. Here the seller has no practical concern. In siman 185 the seller had also fixed a price of four dinars, so suspicion did not apply and the other rationale was needed. See the Perishah.
He cannot displace the purchaser. The Rambam concludes: because he himself sold it, and there is no greater waiver than that. Meaning, he should have refused to act as agent for the outsider's purchase if he wished to preserve his neighboring right. Since he himself sold it to the outsider, we treat his conduct as waiver. Although the Tur cites a Gaon who disagrees with the Rambam, many authorities accepted the Rambam's view, as the Beit Yosef notes, and therefore the Mechaber rules accordingly without qualification.
Likewise, one who signed the deed, etc. The reason is again waiver. He knew of the sale and consented. This differs from siman 111, where the Tur and Mechaber rule that if a creditor signs as a witness on a deed by which his debtor sells property, he may still later collect from the purchaser. There the creditor has only a lien and can say: I assumed that I would be paid in money or from other property. The present case resembles one who challenges title to a field but had signed as a witness on the deed concerning that same field, whose claim is rejected under siman 147, because his conduct directly contradicts his later challenge.
