Permitted. The reason is suspicion. See below, siman 185, where the Tur writes in the name of the Rashba that even where there is no suspicion, an agent may not buy for himself because his hand is like the hand of the principal and the object has not left the principal's domain for another. It appears that this applies specifically there, where the seller seeks to retract. Here the seller is unaffected. Also, there the seller fixed the price at four dinarim, so suspicion does not apply, and therefore the Rashba gives the reason that the object never left the principal's hand. Thus writes the Sema. See what the Taz writes on this.
To remove. The Rambam concludes: 'For he sold it, and there is no greater waiver than this.' Meaning, he should have refrained from acting as agent, lest it be said that he waived his adjoining-owner right by personally selling it to the purchaser. Although the Tur cites a Gaon who disagrees with the Rambam, the leading authorities accepted the Rambam's view, as the Beit Yosef writes. The Mechaber therefore rules accordingly without qualification. Sema.
As a witness. This is unlike siman 111, seif 17, where if a creditor signed as a witness on a deed by which a purchaser bought from the debtor, he may nevertheless later collect the land from him. There the creditor has only a lien on the property, and he may have assumed the debt would be paid in cash or other assets. Here, however, it is like one who contests title to a field but previously signed as a witness to its sale, whose claim is not heard, as explained at the beginning of siman 147. Ibid.
