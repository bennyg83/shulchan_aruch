Seif 57, in the gloss: He has no connection to the cellar. I do not understand this ruling. Since the Rama rules in seif 58 that a lender secured by a field is regarded as an adjoining owner with respect to the neighboring field, all the more so should he have such a right in the cellar directly beneath his property. The Sema's claim that the present case is weaker is astonishing. How can land at the side create a stronger adjoining right than land directly beneath? The Beit Yosef writes that if there is another house not mortgaged to this lender adjoining the mortgaged house, the lender has no adjoining right to it, and on that basis likewise denies a right in the cellar. But once the Rama rules in seif 58 that the lender does have a right in the adjacent field, he certainly has one in the cellar beneath. The Rama's present statement can only have been written according to those authorities who deny a lender adjoining rights in a neighboring field. According to the Rama's own later ruling, the right certainly applies here.
