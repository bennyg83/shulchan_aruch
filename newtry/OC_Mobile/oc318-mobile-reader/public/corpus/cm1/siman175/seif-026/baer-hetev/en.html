To remove him. Meaning, even if the purchaser wishes to build houses on the property. The Tur writes that planting is more significant than houses. The Sema explains that this may be because trees are rooted and remain in the land more firmly than houses. It would follow that if the purchaser wishes to plant and the adjoining owner wishes to build houses, the law of the adjoining owner does not apply, just as between sowing and building. The Taz writes that this is very difficult. The Sages only distinguished between development of inhabited land and nondevelopment, not between two forms of development merely because one is preferable. Otherwise, the Gemara should have taught the greater novelty that even among forms of development there is a distinction between planting and houses. Moreover, the law of the adjoining owner would effectively be abolished, since every purchaser could say he intends to plant. The Tur's statement that planting is more significant than houses is merely a proof that planting is not included in sowing, since we find it regarded as more significant even than houses. But when the purchaser wishes to plant and the adjoining owner wishes to build, there is no basis to cancel the right of preemption, for the adjoining owner too is engaged in developing the world. Taz. See Responsa Raanach, siman 82 from page 125 onward, and siman 125.
