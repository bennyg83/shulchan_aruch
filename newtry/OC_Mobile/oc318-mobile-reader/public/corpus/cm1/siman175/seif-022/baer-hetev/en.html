In his place. Meaning, even to send another person and give him authorization to stand in his place, remove the purchaser, and have the field acquired for the adjoining owner, he cannot appoint him unless he transfers to him a share in that very adjoining field by virtue of which the right of preemption arises. Although in simanim 122 and 123 authorization is effective when granted together with any land, there the particular land is no better than other land. The Mechaber then concludes, "but to stand in his place," meaning that if the adjoining owner is occupied and unable immediately to obtain money or travel to remove the purchaser, and fears losing his right when the period fixed by the Sages expires, he may appoint another person to act in his place and deposit the money with beit din, after which he himself will come and remove the purchaser. In such a case, even if he transfers no land to the agent, appointment as an agent before witnesses is sufficient, in accordance with what is explained at the beginning of siman 121. So writes the Sema. The Shach agrees with this interpretation, against the Bach, who explained that the land must actually become the authorized representative's, whereas if it remains the adjoining owner's he may authorize another. The language of the Gemara does not support the Bach. The Shach further writes that this does not conflict with siman 122, because the Ramah may hold that authorization is itself a rabbinic enactment treating the representative as an agent, and the adjoining owner's right is also rabbinic. Combining one enactment upon another is generally not done. See the Taz, who explains why this also does not conflict with seif 15.
