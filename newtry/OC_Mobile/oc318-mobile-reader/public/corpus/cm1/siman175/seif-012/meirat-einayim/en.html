The same applies if an unrelated person purchased it, etc. The Tur writes, "and it goes without saying if an unrelated person purchased it." The Mechaber writes instead "the same applies," following his explanation in the Beit Yosef that "it goes without saying" is not literal. The first clause merely says that an ordinary neighboring owner cannot displace a partner who has already purchased, but it does not directly teach that if an outsider bought first, one partner may displace him. Rather, the same reasoning applies: just as a partner is regarded as a neighboring owner in the first case, so too he is regarded as a neighboring owner and can displace an unrelated purchaser, like any full neighboring owner. In the Perishah I reconciled the Tur's expression "it goes without saying.".
