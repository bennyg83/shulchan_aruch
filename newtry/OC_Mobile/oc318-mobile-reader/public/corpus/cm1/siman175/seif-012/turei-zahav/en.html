Seif 23: Only with money of the same kind. Rabbeinu disagrees and holds that this resembles exchanging land for movable property, where the adjoining owner pays the movable property's value. The Beit Yosef rejects this, arguing that different forms or weights of money are like exchanging one parcel of land for another, because the seller may care which form he receives, whereas ordinary movable property can readily be purchased and is not unique. The principal view follows Rabbeinu. The distinction between land and movable property depends upon the seller's ordinary intent: a person generally does not sell land unless he receives other land, but he does not usually sell it merely for movable property. Different coinages are not comparable. Although a seller who has both forms before him may prefer one, a person selling land ordinarily does not refrain from selling solely because the buyer offers one valid coinage rather than another. This is included in the Rivash's words cited above, and the Sema likewise rules that the Tur is correct. This is the practical halachah.
