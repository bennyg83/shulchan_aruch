One who sells to a gentile, etc. The Beit Yosef cites Rav Natronai concerning one who sold his house in an alley to a gentile. The residents of the alley claimed that the gentile subsequently harmed and coerced them, but they had no witnesses. He ruled that since the seller cannot swear that he knows nothing of the matter, and an ordinary gentile is presumed to act coercively, their claim has substance. Since all the residents of the alley sue him together, we do not say that all of them joined in order to rob him. They may swear to what the gentile took from them and recover it from the seller.

Specifically where they first placed him under a ban, etc. Examine the Raavad and those who follow him, cited by the Beit Yosef and Darkei Moshe. Their words show that the decisive factor is not the ban itself, but whether the seller first accepted responsibility to remove the harm caused by the gentile. Since ordinarily the ban induces him to accept that responsibility, Rama writes simply that they first placed him under a ban. See siman 55:1 and below siman 386:3, where Rama addresses this rule.
