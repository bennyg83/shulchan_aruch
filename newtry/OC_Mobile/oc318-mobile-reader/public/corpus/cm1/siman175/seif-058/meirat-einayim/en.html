Some say that a creditor has the status of an adjoining owner. See Beit Yosef, who equates a creditor's right to remove a later purchaser with the law where the creditor himself purchased first, in which case an adjoining owner cannot remove him. According to this, the cited opinion also applies to the preceding case: if the debtor does not wish to purchase the property and another person buys it, the creditor can remove that purchaser under the law of an adjoining owner. Although the Tur says, 'If the creditor bought first, he acquired it,' which sounds limited to a creditor who actually purchased first, that is because the Tur did not previously discuss another purchaser and took it as obvious that the creditor has stronger rights than an ordinary third party. In the Derishah, however, I proved from the Tur's later wording in seif 65 and the Mechaber's seif 45, as I write nearby in seif katan 60, that if another person purchases first, the creditor who holds the property as a mortgage cannot remove him.
