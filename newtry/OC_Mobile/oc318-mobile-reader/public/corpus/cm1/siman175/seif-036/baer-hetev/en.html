And the other. In the gloss to seif 13 I wrote that it appears that if a person owns only two fields and sells both to another, the law of the adjoining owner applies, because it is common for the adjoining owner also to purchase both. Accordingly, the Mechaber's wording here, "it and the other," means all the other fields. This is also the Shach's view, as cited in seif katan 22. The wording, however, does not readily imply this, as the Sema notes. The Taz maintains that there is no distinction between two fields and more than two. Even with two, there is concern that in the meantime the purchaser may change his mind and the seller will suffer a loss. He writes that this also appears to be the Sema's conclusion. One cannot object from the Beit Yosef at the end of this siman, who writes that if each wishes to retain his own property, they purchase the property jointly and appraise between themselves how much the house is worth. That case concerns parties who had not yet purchased anything and now wish to purchase, whereas when one has already purchased both properties, an adjoining owner with respect to only one cannot remove him. This appears clear. There is no distinction whether all the properties were in one place and one arrangement or not. So ruled my teacher, of blessed memory, and Bnei Aharon, siman 27, reached the same conclusion from Responsa Maharashach, part 1, siman 43. Bnei Chayei.
