The language of Rambam, chapter 12, law 6, explicit in the Gemara on Bava Metzia 108b.
Tur, seif 54, who writes that his father the Rosh ruled this way because it is uncommon to find someone willing to purchase all of a seller's properties together. If such a purchaser appears, the seller need not notify the neighboring owner, because in the meantime the purchaser may change his mind and refuse to buy. The enactment of uprightness was not made for the neighboring owner at the seller's expense. Maggid Mishneh writes likewise in the name of Ramban, Baal HaIttur, and Rabbi Asher. Nimukei Yosef writes the same in the name of Rashba and Ran. Hagahot Ashri writes that according to Rashi, if all the neighboring owners come and protest, they may remove the purchaser. If the seller agrees, even if not all adjoining owners come but only one, he may remove the purchaser, because since the matter is for the seller's benefit and the seller consents, the objection falls away.
