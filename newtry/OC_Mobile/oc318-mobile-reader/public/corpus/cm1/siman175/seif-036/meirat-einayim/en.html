Even if the neighboring owner also wishes, etc. The Tur gives the reason: It is uncommon for someone to buy all of another person's properties together. Therefore, when such a buyer appears, the seller need not notify the neighboring owner, because in the interim the buyer may change his mind. The enactment of what is good and upright for the neighbor was not made at the seller's expense. Were it not for this reason, the seller would indeed ask the neighboring owner whether he wishes to buy. Although the law of bar metzra was not enacted as an obligation upon the seller, it is in the seller's own interest to ask, because otherwise an unrelated purchaser may be afraid to buy lest the neighboring owner later displace him. See the Perishah. Similar reasoning is needed several times in the Mechaber's preceding rulings.
