And the same applies if. The reason that the original owners do not have this right is that for him too this is "right and good," as stated regarding restoration after appraisal, Bava Metzia 35. There it appears that it is restored even to his son; see Rashi there, s.v. "and we do not restore."
There is one who says. This is not comparable to that case, as stated in the gloss, "nevertheless, initially..." and "if he came..." because an adjoining owner is certainly preferable, and the rule applies specifically to the original owners themselves.
Nevertheless, initially. Since it says "the law does not apply to it," this implies only that he cannot remove him.
And if he came. As the Nimukei Yosef writes: wherever the Gemara says "the law does not apply to it," there is no such law at all, even with respect to his own subsequent transfer.
He sold to suitable persons. For this is no better than appraisal, regarding which it is stated there, "if he sold it...".
