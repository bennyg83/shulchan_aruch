To a son. The practical law requires study where a person sold his own land to his son and the adjoining owner seeks to remove the son. Perhaps the son may say that what is good and upright is that he retain his ancestral estate. This appears persuasive at first glance, but requires study. See Responsa Maharam Alshakar, siman 126. Shach. This is surprising to me. Why should this be worse than where one sells the property to the son of the purchaser, and certainly here? One cannot say that the Shach wrote only according to the dissenting opinion, because that makes no practical difference: whenever there is a dispute among the authorities, the law follows the purchaser, who is in possession. Examine carefully.
