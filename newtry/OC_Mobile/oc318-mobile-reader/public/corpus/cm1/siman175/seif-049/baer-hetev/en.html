They disagree. Mahari writes that since the leading authorities dispute this issue, the law favors the purchaser, who is considered in possession because his acquisition is valid by Torah law. He further writes that even according to the dissenting authorities, their rule may apply only when the purchaser already has a dwelling or field and seeks to purchase this property merely because his circumstances are strained and he wishes to support himself from it. In such a case one may say that nowadays we are not sufficiently expert at determining whether his circumstances are truly strained. But if the purchaser has no house at all, perhaps all agree that the law of the adjoining owner does not apply. He further concludes that if it is known that the purchaser can buy another house elsewhere, even if that house is small and cramped, and he seeks this spacious house because it better meets his needs, the law of the adjoining owner does apply.
