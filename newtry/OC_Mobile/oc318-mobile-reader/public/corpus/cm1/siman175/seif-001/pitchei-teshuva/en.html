The division is void. See Chavot Yair, siman 61. Twelve men cast lots for a silver cup, each contributing one Reichsthaler. Twelve slips bearing the participants' names were placed in one box. In another box were to be eleven blank slips and one slip marked "mazal tov." A child drew one slip from each box, and the person whose name emerged together with the marked slip won. The marked slip appeared unusually quickly. On examination they discovered that the second box had only ten blank slips and two marked slips. Chavot Yair rules that the lot was plainly void, on the principle that where a division was conducted on an erroneous basis it is canceled. They cast lots again and one participant won on the sixth drawing. Then someone casually examined the remaining name slips and discovered that one contributor's name had never been placed in the box, so only eleven names had participated. The group again sought to cancel the lot. The winner argued that the omission only improved everyone's odds and that he was prepared to settle separately with the omitted participant. Chavot Yair nevertheless ruled that this lot too was void. A lot must be conducted exactly among all those entitled to participate. He compares it to brothers who divided and later discovered another brother; even if the shares were equal and the newly arrived brother could be satisfied with one particular share, the original division may still be reopened because the lot was not conducted with all rightful participants. He goes further. If a person fraudulently inserts two slips bearing his own name and another person nevertheless wins the prize, once the fraud is discovered the others may cancel the lot, and even the cheater himself may do so. VeShav HaKohen, siman 73, agrees in a similar case, though he notes that the final formulation of Chavot Yair is imprecise. The cheater's own right to cancel is obvious, because with two name slips in the box he was not participating on the same terms as everyone else and the valid lot had effectively omitted him. The greater novelty is that even if he does not seek cancellation, the others may void the lot because one participant was not properly included in the common lot.
