(Mechaber, seif 44) And if he brought no proof, the purchaser takes a shevuat heset. Note: See Shitah Mekubetzet to Ketubot 44a, in the discussion beginning "it is obvious, the first," where it states that since the right of a neighboring owner is based only on the principle of what is right and good, there should be no claim against the purchaser unless the matter is clarified by witnesses or a document. Where there is no proof, one should neither require an oath nor impose a ban in his presence, for that itself is not the path of what is right and good. The author's teacher said this in the name of the Ramban. The Rambam, however, appears to hold that an oath is administered. See there.
