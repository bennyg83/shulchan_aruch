A non-Jew is not subject to this judicial rule, etc. Meaning, the Jewish seller is not subject to the enactment of bar metzra, as explained above, because the enactment was not imposed on the seller. Nor does the law of bar metzra apply directly to this purchaser, since he is a non-Jew. Therefore the adjoining owner has no remedy against him except to impose a ban upon the Jewish seller until he accepts responsibility, as the Mechaber concludes in the following seif.
One who purchases next to a non-Jew, etc. Terumat HaDeshen discusses a case where Reuven purchased a house from Levi. Shimon's house adjoined that house on the south, while on the north side of the purchased house lived a non-Jew. Thus the house Reuven bought stood between Shimon's house and the non-Jew's house. Shimon sought to remove Reuven under bar metzra, and Reuven responded: 'I am protecting you from the non-Jew.'
