In kind. This language implies that even if changing the currency would cause him no actual loss, but only some additional inconvenience because the money is less readily accepted, the adjoining owner still cannot remove him unless he gives him precisely the same kind of money. The Tur disagrees and writes that this does not appear from the Rambam and Rosh. The Beit Yosef, however, writes that this was not the Rambam's intent. In the Derishah there I proved that the Tur's proofs are sound and that the law follows him. This requirement was stated specifically for the seller, because the enactment of 'good and upright' was not imposed upon the seller himself, who can say, 'If I wish, I will not sell at all.' Thus far the Sema. The Taz also understands the Tur this way and rules that this is the primary halachah. See there.
