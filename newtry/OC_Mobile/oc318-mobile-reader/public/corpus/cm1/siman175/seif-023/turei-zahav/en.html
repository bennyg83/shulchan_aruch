(Seif 52: in land.) "As explained." This implies that even a temporary proprietary right suffices, as the Mechaber writes in seif 58, following Rambam, and as the Sema writes in seif katan 94. This requires study, because such a person is no better than a mortgagee, and a mortgagee has no neighboring-land right in land adjacent to the mortgaged property, as the Mechaber writes below in seif 56. The gloss notes that an even greater difficulty could have been raised above in seif 51, which implies that even if the land was sold to an actual neighbor, a person who owns a building with a temporary right in the land can displace him, whereas below in seif 56 even if the mortgagor sells the field to an outsider who is not a neighboring owner, the mortgagee cannot displace the buyer. It appears, however, that there is no real difficulty. A mortgage is unlike the present case. Here the person has an actual proprietary right and acquisition in the body of the land for the duration of his term and can use it as his own. A mortgage, by contrast, is merely collateral. This is clear.
