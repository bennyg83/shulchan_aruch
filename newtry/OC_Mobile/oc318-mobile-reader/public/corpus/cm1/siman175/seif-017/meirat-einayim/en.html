He cannot remove an heir. Follow the reason for the enactment allowing an adjoining owner to remove a purchaser: because this purchaser bought here, he can instead buy elsewhere, so we require him to act uprightly toward the adjoining owner. That reasoning does not apply to an heir or to one who received the property as a gift from the purchaser. Neither came down to purchase the field, and Chazal did not require him to accept money from the adjoining owner, sell property that came to him from Heaven through inheritance or gift, and trouble himself to purchase elsewhere. This is the reasoning of the opinion that an adjoining owner cannot remove an heir or gift recipient in the two seifim. The Tur cites this in Ramah's name and writes: I do not understand why the adjoining owner cannot remove him, because the purchaser can transmit by inheritance or gift only whatever right he himself has. This is the reasoning of the dissenters in both seifim, and Rama writes that this appears to him the primary view.
