If the purchaser bought the property for two hundred although it is worth only one hundred, and the adjoining landowner wishes to exercise his right of first refusal, he must pay the purchaser two hundred. If the adjoining owner claims that the purchaser and seller conspired to inflate the price, the purchaser must take an oath while holding a sacred object and then receives two hundred. If witnesses testify that he paid two hundred, but the adjoining owner claims that this was only a fictitious arrangement between buyer and seller and he knows with certainty that the true price was only one hundred, he must pay the amount established by the witnesses and thereby remove the purchaser, and he may then require the purchaser to swear that he truly bought it for two hundred; after that the purchaser is discharged. Rama: If the purchaser received the property in payment of a debt, the adjoining owner need pay him only according to the beit din appraisal of the land, even if the debt was greater. Rivash, siman 803.
