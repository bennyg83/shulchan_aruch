Collection: And if he claimed... Since he is like an agent, we administer an oath to him on a claim of uncertainty, as with every agent, as stated in the chapter Kol HaNishba'in. See the Rif. This excludes a case where there are witnesses, for he cannot say, "Perhaps it was merely entrusted to him," etc. The oath of partners and agents applies only to the concern that something remains in his possession, as stated in siman 93. But if the claimant says, "I know," it is like every definite claim, and the defendant takes a heses oath. First he must pay, and afterward the claimant may assert his claim against him and require a heses oath. This is the meaning of "he removes him and administers an oath to him," unlike the first case, where it is a rabbinic oath. The same law applies to every agent and partner. See below, siman 93 seif 12. End of collection.
