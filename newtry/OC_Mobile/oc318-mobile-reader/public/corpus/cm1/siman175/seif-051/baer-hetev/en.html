The house. See the Sema, who argues at length against the Rama's ruling here. The Rama states as though it were clear in the name of the Rosh that the owner of the upper story cannot remove the adjoining owner, but this is not stated in the Rosh's responsum. On the contrary, the plain meaning there implies that he can remove him, and the matter requires study. The Shach agrees with the Sema and notes that Responsa Rabbi Aharon Sasson, siman 128, raises the same question. The Taz likewise writes that the primary ruling appears to be that the owner of the upper story may remove him, since he is a full partner, having one-third of the land, as stated in siman 164, seif 5.
