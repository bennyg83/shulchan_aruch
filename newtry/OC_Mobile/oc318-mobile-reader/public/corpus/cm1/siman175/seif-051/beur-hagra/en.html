The house and the upper story. The land is encumbered to the upper story, and the owner of the upper story has a share in the substance of the land, as stated in Bava Metzia 117b. Therefore, the owner of the upper story has the status of a partner. So writes the Rosh in a responsum. Beit Yosef writes that according to this, the owner of the upper story may remove an adjoining owner, though the wording of the responsum does not appear to imply this. Rather, the question there was whether the adjoining owner could remove the owner of the upper story. His words require study, because that was the actual case, and there is no clearer share in the land than this. This is also consistent with the statement above, 'even,' etc. See Sema.
