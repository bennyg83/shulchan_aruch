If the land belongs to one person and the building or trees belong to another, and the owner of the building or trees has a permanent right in the land, each is regarded as an adjoining owner with respect to the other. Therefore, if either sells his interest, the other may remove the purchaser, even if the purchaser himself owns adjoining land. If the owner of the trees or building has no right in the land, and the landowner may at any time tell him to uproot his trees or demolish his building, then if the landowner sells, the purchaser acquires and the owner of the trees or building may not remove him. If the owner of the building or trees sells his interest, however, the landowner may remove the purchaser. Rama: Where one person owns the house and another the upper story, and one sells his portion to an adjoining owner, the other cannot remove him, because they are not considered partners in one property; they have only the status of adjoining owners.
