Good. The Beit Yosef cites the Baal HaIttur that the terms "bad" and "good" here mean something objectively recognizable to everyone as bad or good. If it is bad for one person and good for another, or the reverse, this rule does not apply. It appears that no one disagrees. So writes the Sema. The Shach writes that this indicates that if a person moved his residence to another city and sold the house he owned in the city he left to another resident of that city, the adjoining owner of that house cannot remove the purchaser. The Tur and Shulchan Aruch state in siman 190, seif 12, that this is considered one who sells his field because it has become undesirable to him. Shach.
