If it appears to be so to beit din. See Baer Heitev, which notes the Shach's disagreement, and Shevut Yaakov, part III, siman 164, who sides with Rama. In his case, if the first partner was wealthier than the second, there was reason to say that the owner preferred the first, in keeping with the advice in Pesachim 112 to associate commercially with a person whose fortune is favorable. He further explains that Beit Yosef's principal objection to Maharik was that the adjoining owner is the one seeking to extract property and therefore bears the burden of proof. Where, however, the second partner is himself in possession of the movable property, as in the case before Shevut Yaakov, one should not remove it from him, consistent with Maharik. See also what I wrote above in siman 66:23, seif katan 16, regarding the Mabit and a creditor's claim that he prefers to leave the property to a particular person.
