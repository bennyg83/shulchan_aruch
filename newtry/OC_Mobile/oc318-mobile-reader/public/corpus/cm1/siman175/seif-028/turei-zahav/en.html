Seif 59: One who rents a house from another. This is the Rambam's meaning when he says that the law of adjoining ownership does not apply to rental, as Rabbeinu understood him according to the Beit Yosef. Rabbeinu cites the Rosh as disagreeing because a rental for its term is considered a sale. On that view, the Rosh also disagrees with the Mechaber's ruling at the beginning of seif 60, for a tenant can displace a later purchaser of an adjoining house. The Rama's statement there that some disagree therefore refers to the opening clause as well. Yet Rabbeinu's ruling concerning a lender secured by an adjacent field is difficult. He says that if the lender first bought the adjacent field, the ordinary adjoining owner cannot remove him. He should have stated the stronger ruling that even after another purchased it, the lender could remove that purchaser. Moreover, the Rosh himself writes that one who has only a temporary interest in land is like a tenant and has no adjoining right. The Beit Yosef leaves this unresolved. It therefore appears that a lender or tenant has no superior power beyond this: if he first purchases the adjacent field, the ordinary adjoining owner cannot remove him, because at that moment he too acted in accordance with what is right and good. But he cannot remove a purchaser who obtained permanent title, since his own adjoining status is temporary. The Rama's dissent at the end of the seif therefore refers only to the latter clause, that if the lender first bought the adjacent field he cannot be removed. Rabbeinu's statement that the Rosh applies adjoining law to rentals concerns renting an adjacent property that is itself offered for rent. A tenant may claim priority to rent it. But where the adjoining property is sold, the tenant has no power to remove the purchaser. The Rosh's responsum means that for purposes of a loan or rental, the tenant and lender are full adjoining owners regarding another rental, but for a purchase their right extends only to resisting removal after they themselves bought first, not to removing another purchaser. The Rosh also disagrees with seif 59. As for rental of the same house, the first tenant certainly displaces a later tenant who seeks to take possession after the first term expires, and it appears that even the Rambam agrees. Rabbeinu later writes that even though the dispossessed owner learned of the sale immediately after the robber was removed, he does not lose his adjoining right by first recovering his own field. He may say that he waited until he recovered his property.
