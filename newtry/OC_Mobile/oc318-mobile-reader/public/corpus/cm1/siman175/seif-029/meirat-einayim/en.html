Because he does not become his agent against his will. The Mordechai concludes that it is like a man saying before another person's agent, "I do not desire this woman," after which the agent betroths her for him. She is not betrothed. So too here, the neighboring owner said, "I do not want it." This requires study. The Tur and Mechaber write below in seif 31 that where the seller consulted the neighboring owner and he answered, "Go sell to another, I do not want it," no kinyan is required. That implies that specifically where the seller consulted him does this statement itself waive the right. If the purchaser consulted him, even an answer, "Go buy it, I do not want it," should not constitute waiver without a kinyan. The Mordechai also writes immediately before that a kinyan does not help for relinquishment of the neighboring right because it is merely a kinyan on words, and we do not accept that ruling. Darkei Moshe indicates that we likewise do not follow the Mordechai in this second ruling. It is therefore surprising that Rama states the rule here without qualification. It appears that Rama means a case where the purchaser says, "I will buy it and then give it to you," and the neighboring owner responds, "I do not want it, buy it for yourself." Then the purchaser acquires. The neighboring owner cannot later claim that he said this only so that the purchaser would negotiate the price downward for him, because the purchaser had expressly offered to buy in his own name and afterward transfer it to the neighbor. Perhaps this is why Rama altered the Mordechai's wording and wrote "to purchase," meaning to purchase and afterward hand it to the neighbor. The Mordechai may also be understood this way.
