Because he does not become his agent against his will. See Sema, seif katan 53, through his citation of the Mordechai that an act of acquisition does not help to waive the adjoining owner's right because it is an acquisition concerning mere words. When the Gemara says that an acquisition must be made from him, it refers to a case where the purchaser already acquired the land and the adjoining owner presently has a right in it; an acquisition then takes effect and is not merely an acquisition concerning words. Before the purchaser buys, however, if the adjoining owner says, 'Go buy it for yourself, for I do not want it,' he may waive his right even without an acquisition. Since he said this, the purchaser does not become his agent against his will. The Gemara's statement that saying, 'Go buy it,' does not forfeit his right applies where he did not add, 'I do not want it,' and the language does not imply cancellation of the agency. Rabbi Eliyahu ibn Chaim explains the Mordechai this way in responsum 125. On this basis he proves that the Mordechai's ruling is inconsistent with the authorities cited in seif 32, who hold that after the purchaser bought while the adjoining owner remained silent, the adjoining owner lost his right. According to them, why would an acquisition be needed? Silence and waiver suffice. This proves that they do not accept the Mordechai's view. The Rama's ruling here is therefore astonishing, because he was concerned for a lone opinion.
