Against his will. The Sema writes that this requires study. In seif 31, regarding a seller who consulted the adjoining owner, the implication is that no kinyan is required only where the seller consulted him. If the purchaser consulted him, even if the adjoining owner said, "Go purchase it, I do not want it," this should not constitute waiver without a kinyan. The Darkei Moshe likewise implies that the law does not follow the Mordechai on this point. It is therefore surprising that the Rama wrote the ruling here without qualification. The Shach likewise writes that the Rama's words are certainly difficult, since he took account of a lone opinion. It appears that the Rama means a case in which the purchaser said, "I will buy it and then give it to you," and the adjoining owner replied, "I do not want it; buy it for yourself." In that case the purchaser acquires the right, because the adjoining owner cannot later claim that he said this only so that the purchaser would acquire it at its fair value. The purchaser had offered to buy in his own name and then transfer it to the adjoining owner. Perhaps this was also the Mordechai's intent, and the ruling is then understandable.
