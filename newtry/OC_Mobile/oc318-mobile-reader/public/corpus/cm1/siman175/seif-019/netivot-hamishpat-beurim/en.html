The first purchaser sold it. See Sema, seif katan 31, who explains that because the buyer gains nothing by returning it, he cannot use the argument, "If you remain silent, well and good." This is not the principal reason. Even according to those who allow that argument without profit, it does not apply here. The purchaser is no better than if the field had always been his and he then sold it, in which case the adjoining owner could remove him under the law of bar metzra. What claim is there in saying, "I will return the deed to the original seller"? If the original seller needs to sell, and no one else can safely purchase because of the adjoining owner's priority, then the adjoining owner should buy it. That is the very purpose of the equitable enactment of bar metzra: outsiders should withdraw and leave the property available for the adjoining owner. Otherwise every buyer could defeat bar metzra by saying, "If you remain silent, well and good; otherwise I will return the deed to the first owner." Nor can it be said that if the buyer resells to the first owner, the buyer himself would then become an adjoining owner and gain priority. Any waiver of bar metzra rights concerning the first sale does not waive rights concerning a later sale, which is a matter not yet in existence and cannot be waived in advance, especially where no perpetual waiver was stated. There is an additional reason: every purchaser in a bar metzra case is treated as the adjoining owner's agent for purposes of the equitable rule, and therefore cannot use return of the deed to undermine the very right under which his purchase is judged.
