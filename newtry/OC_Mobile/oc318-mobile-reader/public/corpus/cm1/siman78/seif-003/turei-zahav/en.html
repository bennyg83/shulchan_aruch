(Ibid., seif 4) “When he demanded payment at the end of the day.” The Sema distinguishes three stages of the due date: beginning, middle, and end. At the beginning the debt is wholly within its term. In the middle the debtor may say, “I paid you,” but not, “I paid before the term,” because he lacks a migo. At the end he may say, “I paid today.” I do not know what counts as beginning or end; a definite boundary should have been given. Moreover, in seif 5, where the Sema summarizes four rules, he omits this category of being believed to say, “I paid today, but not before maturity.” His proof from Tosafot at the beginning of Bava Batra does not establish a division between middle and end. Tosafot say only that on the due date there may be no migo. The Tur and other authorities hold that there is a migo. It appears that Tosafot wrote their limitation only at the sugya’s opening stage, when the Gemara was uncertain whether a migo prevails against a presumption. They needed to explain why the case did not resolve the question through a migo of payment that day. Once the conclusion leaves the issue doubtful, the ordinary migo may be used. In practice, whenever according to the judge’s assessment there was not yet a reasonable opportunity to pay, it is the beginning of the day; afterward it is the end. Rashi’s phrase in HaShoel, “on the day the term ended he says, ‘I paid you in the morning,’” can be understood similarly, meaning at a time when morning payment was realistically possible.
