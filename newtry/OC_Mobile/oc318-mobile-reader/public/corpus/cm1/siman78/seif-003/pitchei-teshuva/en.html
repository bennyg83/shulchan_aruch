Deposited money. See Baer Heitev. See Responsa Chatam Sofer, Choshen Mishpat, end of siman 144. One rabbi wished to say that where a loan was made under a heter iska, the borrower is believed to say that he repaid before maturity, because a person may repay within the term in order to avoid further profit payments. The Chatam Sofer writes that this is not persuasive, and that here even Abaye and Rava would agree that he is not believed. He concludes that this is the strict law. Nevertheless, if in the judge's assessment the investor fixed the term for the recipient's benefit and would in fact have been pleased had the money been returned earlier, the borrower may perhaps be believed on the basis of a compelling assessment of circumstances. Such a ruling requires a worthy, God fearing judge and a clear, established inference. See there. His words plainly follow the view of Shevut Yaakov II, siman 184, cited below, siman 176, seif 23, seif katan 27. According to the Tumim, siman 74, seif katan 6, and Shaarei Deah, laws of interest, siman 177, seif 36, cited there, who hold that if the borrower comes during the term to repay the principal together with the profit accrued until that time he may do so, the first rabbi's ruling is correct, and this is indeed written in Urim VeTumim here, seif katan 8. According to Netivot HaMishpat, however, cited there, he may still not be believed because he was obligated to make an accounting and swear. This requires study. See also Responsa Maharit, Choshen Mishpat, siman 124; Knesset HaGedolah, Hagahot Beit Yosef, letter 4; and Ateret Tzvi, seif katan 6.
