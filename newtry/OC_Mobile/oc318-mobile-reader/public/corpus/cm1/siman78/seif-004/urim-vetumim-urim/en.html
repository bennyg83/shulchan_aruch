These words apply, etc. Sema and Shach dispute Tur's wording at length, but as practical law they agree that throughout the day on which the term ends the borrower may say, 'I paid you today.' The exception is when the lender sues him at the very first moment of daylight. He cannot then say, 'I paid you today,' because the day has barely begun; any earlier payment would have been before the due date. If later in the day he says, 'I paid you before the due date,' Tosafot holds he is not believed because he lacks a migo that he could have said, 'I paid today.' Saying, 'I paid today,' is more brazen and therefore not an equivalent migo. Ramban holds that the migo does work. Sema says that by the end of the day even Tosafot would accept the migo, because he could easily have said he paid sometime after daylight. Shach rejects this distinction: what meaningful difference is there between the middle and end of the day, and who can measure moments so finely? According to Tosafot the migo does not operate at any point. Maharatz likewise agrees. Their view is correct. See Tumim for reconciliation of the various approaches and whether, under Tosafot, the lender can sue in beit din on the day the term ends.
