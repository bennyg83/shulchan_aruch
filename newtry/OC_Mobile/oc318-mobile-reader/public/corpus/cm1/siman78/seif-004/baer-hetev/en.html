Today. The words of the Mechaber require study. Although the Tur wrote 'at the end of his term,' he did not write this with respect to payment on the due date itself. If he had, the Tur would contradict himself, for at the beginning of this siman he writes that if the borrower says at the beginning of the day that he already paid, he is not believed, implying that after the beginning of the day he is believed to say so. Rather, the Tur mentions the end of the day because he then writes that the borrower is believed to say, 'I paid you within the term,' by virtue of a migo that he could have said, 'I paid you today.' This migo exists only at the end of the day, for then he could have said, 'At the beginning of the day I began counting out the money to you, and you forgot that I paid you.' In the middle of the due date, however, it is more convenient for him to say, 'I paid you within the term,' than to say, 'I paid you today,' because people do not ordinarily forget a payment made only a short time earlier, and this would be a migo involving brazenness. Tosafot and Baal HaTerumot write this explicitly. Thus concludes the Sema. The Bach likewise writes at length to this effect. See there. Their words do not appear correct to me, for if the reason is that the migo involves brazenness, then even at the end of the day, when he claims that he paid within the term, he should not be believed, because it would still be brazen to say, 'I paid you today and you forgot.' There is no reason to distinguish between part of the day and the end of the day with respect to brazenness. The Taz likewise challenges him on this point. See there. Rather, the Tur and Baal HaTerumot's reference to the end of the day also concerns payment on that very day, and they maintain that even if he claims, 'I paid you today,' he is believed only if he makes that claim at the end of the day. Nevertheless, it appears to me that the Mechaber's words are not compelled. The principal practical ruling is that throughout the day he is believed to say, 'I paid you today.' As to the migo, however, from the beginning of the day until its end it appears to remain an unresolved question of law whether, when he says, 'I paid you within my term,' he is believed by virtue of the migo that he could have said, 'I paid you today.' Accordingly, the burden of proof rests on the one who seeks to extract money. Thus concludes the Shach. See there.

Nevertheless, it appears to me that according to the view in siman 108, seif 4, that we plead for heirs even a claim that is uncommon, the same applies here. It appears that for this reason the Rama wrote this law in the form, 'There are those who say,' and so forth. Although one can distinguish between a claim that property was lost by accident and the present case, nevertheless, as a matter of practical law it appears to me that we plead for heirs every possible defense, as I wrote at the end of siman 361.
