When is this said. The Sema and Shach disagree at length over the Tur's wording, but practically both agree that throughout the due date the borrower may say, "I paid you just now," except at the very first instant when the day begins. At that moment he cannot claim, "I paid you now," because daylight has only just arrived. If he says he paid earlier, that would have been within the due date and is not believed. If later in the day he says, "I paid you before the due date," according to Tosafot he is not believed because he lacks a migo of "I paid now": claiming current payment would be too brazen, and it is easier to say he paid yesterday within the term. Ramban holds that even here he has such a migo. At the end of the day, if he claims earlier payment, the Sema holds that even Tosafot would agree he has a migo that he paid after daybreak. The Shach rejects this distinction: there is no meaningful difference between the middle and end of the day, because no one can precisely distinguish the hours and moments. According to Tosafot there is therefore never such a migo. Maharan writes likewise. This is the correct view.
But for heirs. The Shach writes that according to the opinion in siman 108 that we advance uncommon claims for heirs, we would also advance this claim. See Beurim.
