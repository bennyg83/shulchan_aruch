This applies when he demands payment, etc. The Mechaber's words require study. Although the Tur speaks of the end of the term, he does not mean specifically with respect to repayment on that very day. If so, the Tur would contradict himself, for at the beginning of this siman he writes that if the borrower says at the beginning of the day that he already paid, he is not believed, which implies that later in the day he is believed to say so. Rather, the Tur mentions the end of the day because he then writes that the borrower is believed to say, "I repaid you within the term," through a migo that he could have said, "I repaid you today." Such a migo exists only at the end of the day, because only then could he plausibly say, "I began counting out the money to you early today and paid you, and you forgot." If he is sued at the beginning of the day and says that he paid before then, that necessarily means he claims repayment within the term. Similarly, if the matter stands in the middle of the day after the term expired, he prefers to say, "I paid within the term," rather than, "I paid you today," because people do not ordinarily forget a repayment made only a short time earlier. His alternative claim would therefore be a brazenness migo. Tosafot and the Baal HaTerumot explicitly write this, as I cited in the Derishah. See there carefully.
