If he wishes to divorce her, he may divorce her. This requires explanation. What is this coming to teach us? Do we not already know that a man has the authority to divorce his wife, and why would we think that he need not pay her ketubah when he divorces her? It therefore appears that Rambam's view is that although he is not compelled to redeem her a second time, she may say to him, 'Either redeem me or divorce me and pay my ketubah,' and he must fulfill one of those two alternatives. The Beit Yosef does not explain it this way, and Maharamah in his gloss likewise does not. In my opinion, however, the explanation I have given is correct, and I later saw that the Bach also writes this way.
