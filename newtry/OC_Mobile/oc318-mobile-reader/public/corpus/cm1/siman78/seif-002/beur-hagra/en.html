If... according to Rambam... This is like the law regarding orphans, as indicated by the statement, "and even though the Master said..."
(Collected note) According to Rambam... As stated in siman 114, seif 4. (End of note.)
And some say that he must. They infer this because the Gemara mentions orphans and does not state the greater novelty of purchasers. We maintain that an oral loan may be collected from orphans but not from purchasers. See the Sema and Shach.
(Collected note) And some say... This is like the law where the debtor admits, in which case collection may be made from orphans but not from purchasers. Maggid Mishneh rejects their proof, because there the concern is collusion. See there. (End of note.)
(Collected note) And some say that he must. With purchasers we are concerned that money may have been set aside for payment even where we would not have that concern regarding the debtor himself. See Tosafos Kesubos 102b, s.v. "say..." and Rebbe's answer there, and Gittin 51a, s.v. "say..." Although the Gemara there is not concerned about money set aside in the case of heirs and uses the expression "from the debtor," meaning from orphans and not purchasers, this shows that the concern exists regarding purchasers even where it does not exist regarding orphans. (End of note.)
And if the borrower... some say... Since the borrower makes a definite claim. With orphans we do not impose an oath upon the creditor merely because of uncertainty. So writes the Rosh there, as stated there on 33a in the incident of Rabbah bar Sharshom. See Tosafos there, s.v. "migo..." This means a shevuas hesses after he has been paid, as the Shach explains and as stated above in siman 71 concerning contractual credibility.
(Collected note) Some say that he must... This is also Rambam's opinion, as stated at the beginning of siman 317. Likewise, what the Tur and Shulchan Aruch write in siman 84, seifim 1 and 4, "unless..." refers to a shevuas hesses after payment. Otherwise there is a difficulty, because we plead on behalf of orphans. In this manner it is correct, as Tosafos 33a, s.v. "migo," write: "and one may say..." So rules the Shach. (End of note.)
And some say... They hold that the chazakah is like witnesses that he did not repay, as stated above in seif 1 regarding one established as a denier. According to their view, one must say that the Gemara's unresolved question concerns only whether a migo may overcome the chazakah, but not other matters. This requires study.
