Reuven claimed a debt from Shimon before its due date, and Shimon answered, 'I repaid you little by little so that you would not press me and collect the entire debt at once.' One authority ruled that the presumption that a person does not repay before the due date does not apply, because it benefits the borrower to repay in installments and therefore he sometimes pays before maturity. Several sages questioned this ruling. The author cites Be'er Yaakov, siman 78, in the name of Berit Shalom, folio 49, which expressly adopts that view, while Be'er Yaakov disputes it and seeks proof from Bava Batra 5b that no distinction may be made. Shemen Rokeach responds that the Gemara there does not disprove the distinction. Rather, the question depends on the dispute among the poskim whether beit din advances on behalf of heirs even an uncommon claim. If even an uncommon claim is advanced for heirs, then this ruling would indeed appear untenable, for otherwise one must ask how a creditor may collect from heirs before maturity even without an oath. Yet the Yerushalmi at the beginning of the second chapter of Ketubot indicates that an uncommon claim is not advanced for heirs. One must therefore distinguish and say that where a contention is wholly uncommon, all agree that it is not advanced, contrary to what appears from the Shach, siman 69, seif katan 26. On this basis the ruling of Berit Shalom may be sustained according to all opinions: installment repayment before maturity is considered wholly uncommon, especially since the creditor ordinarily would not accept such payments, and therefore beit din does not advance that contention for heirs.
