If he died within the term, etc. The Tur also cites this dispute in siman 114 regarding collection from purchasers during the seller's lifetime within the term. Here the Tur and Mechaber mention death because the Tur cites only Rav Hai and the Rosh, who require an oath, and death teaches the greater novelty: although collection from heirs ordinarily requires no oath within the term, collection from purchasers does.
There is also a novelty in the opposing view: if the borrower died within the term, some hold that even if collection from the purchasers occurs only after the term has expired, no oath is required, since the heirs do not claim that their father paid and he died within the term. In siman 114, by contrast, the case concerns collection during the term. If collection were after the term, everyone would agree an oath is required while the borrower is still alive, because perhaps he paid.And he comes to collect.

The Levush explains that the father would not concern himself with the purchasers' benefit, but the first explanation is preferable.The lender must swear. The reason is that the borrower himself makes a definite claim that he repaid, unlike heirs, where no one makes a definite repayment claim and beit din does not advance the unusual claim that payment was made within the term.
