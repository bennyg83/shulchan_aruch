The principal view. The Shach writes that the Rama's words appear to mean that no oath is required at all, even after payment, and this also appears from the Tur and is explicit in Baal HaTerumot, contrary to the Bach, who improperly challenged the Tur on this point. Moreover, otherwise, according to the first view he would necessarily have to swear before payment, and this is not found in any posek. On the contrary, the Rosh states that he must take a heset oath, which necessarily means after payment, since one never finds a heset oath before payment. According to this, however, there is a difficulty with the Rama, for here he rules that no oath at all is required, whereas at the beginning of siman 317 he follows the Mechaber without qualification that an oath is required after payment. The Taz raises the same difficulty and leaves it requiring study. See there. It is strained to distinguish between rental and a loan; indeed, Tosafot and Nimukei Yosef explicitly imply that rental gives the claimant an even stronger position. Nor can one say that here the case is specifically a written note, because after payment there is certainly no distinction between a loan evidenced by a note and an oral loan. This requires study. As a matter of practical law, the principal ruling appears to be as the Bach decided: before payment no oath is required, even on an oral loan, although a general ban is pronounced; after payment, a heset oath is required even on a loan evidenced by a note. See there, where he brings proof for this.
