A term. This applies specifically where he fixed a time for repayment, for the borrower thereby revealed that he needs the money until the end of that period. This is unlike an ordinary loan without a fixed term, as the Tur and Shulchan Aruch write at the end of this siman. Sema.
And he claimed from him. The Sema explains that this is when the claimant asserts the matter with certainty, but not when he is uncertain, citing Mordechai at the beginning of Bava Batra and Darkei Moshe, paragraph 1. In truth, Mordechai there is uncertain about the matter, and it also appears that he did not rule that the defendant is exempt on this ground alone, but only together with other clear grounds. His language is: 'Furthermore, there is the presumption that a person does not repay within the term. Perhaps that presumption is effective only where each side makes a definite claim, whereas here the heirs claim only doubt. Furthermore, it is obvious that this case resembles one in which he was claimed from after the term,' and so forth. See there. It appears to me that the principal rule is that even when the heirs plead uncertainty, the presumption that a person does not repay within the term still applies. This is also demonstrated by the responsum of the Rosh cited by the Tur in siman 71, seif 21, concerning the heirs of Reuven who sued Shimon on a note where Reuven had died within the term. See there. Shach.
Minors. This is the consensus of the poskim and is the principal ruling, contrary to Maharshal's view in volume 2, siman 113, that because there is a dispute among the authorities we do not extract payment from minor orphans. See there. Mahari ibn Lev likewise ruled in volume 3, siman 35, like the Mechaber, that minor orphans cannot say, 'We hold like the opposing view'; rather, we rule that beit din attends to their property. See there. Maharam Alshich ruled likewise in siman 137, as did the Bach and Maharshal in simanim 196 and 454. However, Maharshal further wrote in siman 208 that this applies only when the repayment date was fixed this way from the outset, but if the due date arrived during the borrower's lifetime and the lender acted kindly toward him, extended the term, and yielded the debt to him for a time, payment is not collected from minor orphans. See there. His words do not appear correct in this respect, for so long as the debt is still within its term, payment is collected from them in every case. Shach.
By a document. The Sema writes that this implies that with a written note it is unnecessary to have testimony accepted during the borrower's lifetime. Although the Tur, in the name of the Rosh, rules in siman 110 that even a document is not authenticated while the heir is a minor, that applies specifically to matters of a will or when the time for collection has arrived. See what I wrote there and in siman 46. The Shach writes that this is incorrect. According to the Rosh there, here too the document must have been authenticated before the debtor died, as written in siman 110, seif katan 12. See there.
Believed. If there is one witness concerning the fixed term and the debtor says, 'I repaid you within the term,' this is a case of one who is obligated to swear but is unable to do so, and therefore must pay. So too the Sema writes in siman 75, as stated there in seif katan 27. Shach.

Deposited. At first glance, the responsum of the Rosh and the Tur and Shulchan Aruch in siman 108, seif 4, disagree with this. One can distinguish, however, because there, since he is not believed to say regarding the half that is a loan that he returned it, he is likewise not believed concerning the other half, which has the law of a deposit, for who can divide the two? Alternatively, this ruling may apply specifically here, where he derives no benefit from the money, as Mahari Weil writes in the responsum cited there. That case is different, because he does derive benefit from the money, since the deposit portion is pledged to the loan portion to make up any deficiency. See there, and the responsum of Mahari ibn Lev, volume 1, general rule 17, siman 94. Shach.
That he fixed. Mahara Sasson writes in responsum 74 that with payment for merchandise we do not apply the presumption that a person does not pay within the term. See there. This requires study as a matter of practical law, because the words of Hagahot Ashri at the end of HaShoel and the responsum of Mahari Weil do not imply this. Rather, it is specifically with a deposit, which one may not use, and likewise where one is handling money for the depositor's benefit and derives no benefit from it, that we say a person may repay within the term. But where he derives benefit, we do not say so. If so, the same should apply to payment for merchandise. Shach.
