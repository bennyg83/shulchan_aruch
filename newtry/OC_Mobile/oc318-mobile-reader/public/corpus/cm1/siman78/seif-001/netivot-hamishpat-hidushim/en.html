And he claimed from him within the due date. This applies specifically when the claimant makes a definite claim. If he does not speak with certainty, the presumption that a person does not pay before a debt falls due is ineffective. Sema. See the Beurim. Even if both parties are uncertain, the presumption regarding payment within the due date does not apply. This is specifically where the claimant himself is uncertain. If his heirs claim and they could not have known, then where the obligation might never have arisen at all, such as redemption of a firstborn son or a wall that fell, perhaps all agree that the presumption applies. Even minors. This is the consensus of the authorities, and the debtor cannot say, "I follow the authorities who hold that one cannot collect from minor heirs." He is established as a denier. Why should we assume he paid it? Even if he knew the law and therefore paid a second time, perhaps he would not dare deny the first payment in beit din, for fear of beit din rests upon him. See the Beurim. If he claimed in beit din that he paid within the due date and beit din obligated him to pay, but enforcement continued until after the due date, it is not only that he may say, "I paid after the due date"; even if he now says, "I paid you within the due date," exactly as he originally claimed, he is believed through a migo that he could have said that he paid after the due date. The Mechaber refers to one who previously claimed outside beit din that he paid before witnesses:
Deposited. See the Beurim. With a deposit he is otherwise believed through a migo that he could have claimed unavoidable loss; the practical difference is whether he must take a stringent Torah oath. See the Shach, who writes that even where the obligation was to pay with merchandise, the presumption against payment before the due date applies:
Each pamphlet. See the Beurim. This refers to a fixed amount of gold payable for each pamphlet, so every pamphlet constitutes an independent job. See the Shach, who writes that even concerning pamphlets not yet completed, the employer may say, "I already paid you," in order not to be harassed for payment whenever a pamphlet is finished. Nevertheless, if he lent money on an installment schedule requiring a small payment each week, after each week the borrower is believed to say that he paid the installment for the previous week.
