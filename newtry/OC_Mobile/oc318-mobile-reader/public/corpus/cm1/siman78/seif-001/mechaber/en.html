One who claims that he paid within the term. It contains 8 se'ifim.
If a person fixed a time for payment to another, and the creditor demands payment within the term, the debtor is not believed when he says, 'I paid you,' because there is a presumption that a person does not pay within the term. {Rama: Similarly, he cannot claim, 'You forgave the debt.'} Even from orphans whose father died within the term, including minor orphans, collection is made without an oath if the loan was recorded in a document. The same applies even to an oral loan if the father was brought to judgment and the testimony was accepted in his presence, for this law applies to an oral loan as well, provided there are witnesses both to the loan and to the fixed term. If there are no witnesses, he is believed upon taking a heses oath through a migo that he could have claimed, 'This never occurred,' 'You did not fix a term for me,' or, 'The term already passed and I paid you when it fell due.' If the creditor demanded payment within the term and the debtor said, 'I paid you,' and then demanded payment again after the term and the debtor said, 'I have now paid you,' the debtor has been established as a denier and is not believed. {Rama: Some say this presumption applies only to a loan. If money was deposited with him and he uses it for the needs of others, it has the law of any other deposit, and he may say that he returned it even within the fixed term, as stated in Mahariv 89. The law of rent is like that of a loan; since it is not payable until the end, one is not believed to say that he paid it within the term. See the beginning of siman 317. However, if he hired a scribe to write several booklets, the completion of each booklet is immediately its due date. The same applies to every type of work divisible in this manner. See note 40.}
