The Gemara leaves unresolved whether we apply a migo against a legal presumption, and the poskim rule leniently for the defendant. Accordingly, if the lender seized property, even in the presence of witnesses, we do not remove it from him where the seizure is of a type that is effective in an unresolved Talmudic question. Another practical difference is that if he seized without witnesses, he need not swear, because of the presumption that a person does not pay within his term. Even according to what I wrote in seif 2, as stated in seif katan 13, that an oath is required after payment, there is still a difference in an ordinary case of seizure: although the person in possession has a migo, he must ordinarily take an oath while holding a sacred object before receiving payment, as written in siman 72. Here, however, where he claims that the debtor paid him within the term, it appears to me that he takes only a heset oath after the payment. Ramban writes that where beit din fixed a time for payment, the debtor may say, 'I paid,' because a person sometimes pays within a term fixed by beit din. He derives this from the chapter Shnayim Ochazin, where the Gemara says that if a lender comes to have a document written, it is not written and given to him because we are concerned that perhaps the debtor already paid. This implies that Ramban holds that where beit din fixed the time, we do not apply the presumption that a person does not pay within his term. I do not know his basis for this, and what he cites from Shnayim Ochazin is no proof at all, because there beit din did not fix a future time. Rather, they told him, 'You are obligated to give it to him,' or, 'Go and give it to him immediately.' This requires study. Thus concludes the Shach.
