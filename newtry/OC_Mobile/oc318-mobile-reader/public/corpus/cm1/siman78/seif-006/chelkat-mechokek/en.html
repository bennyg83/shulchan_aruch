And she was taken captive after he had imposed the vow upon her. But if she was first taken captive and only afterward he imposed the vow upon her, there is concern for collusion, meaning that he may impose a vow upon her in order to exempt himself from the obligation to redeem her, as stated in Ketubot 52a. Even if she herself made the vow after she was taken captive and he then upheld it, since we rule that he is considered to have placed the finger between her teeth, there is likewise room for collusion. So writes the Bach.
