Specifically when he fixed a time. See Shach, seif katan 19, through his discussion of a time fixed by beit din. Tumim explains that beit din grants time only to one who presently lacks money, whereas one who has money must pay immediately. According to this, where arbitrators set a payment date as part of a compromise, it is like the case the Shach describes where the creditor voluntarily granted additional time, and the period is treated as before the due date. The requirement that a time be fixed applies specifically to a loan, where the obligation arose immediately. But where the obligation itself does not arise until a later time, such as redemption of a firstborn son, rent for a house, or rebuilding a courtyard wall that fell, the period before liability matures is treated as before the due date even without an expressly fixed date. This is straightforward.
