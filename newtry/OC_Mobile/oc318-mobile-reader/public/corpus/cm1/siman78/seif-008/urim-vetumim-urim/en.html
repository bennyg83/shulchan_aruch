This applies specifically where he fixed a term, etc. See Tumim. I gave two possible reasons. One is that fixing a term reveals that the borrower is not expected to have funds before that date, unlike an ordinary unspecified loan. Another is that in an unspecified loan, if the lender seizes property before the thirty-day period we may not necessarily remove it, unlike a fixed term. I also concluded like Ramban that if beit din itself grants the debtor time, even though the debtor requested the judicial extension, he can still be believed to say he paid during that court-granted period. See there at length. With a written document, however, even during the first thirty days a borrower ordinarily does not pay without obtaining a receipt. Bach writes this explicitly.
