In the manner explained in siman 7. Above, in seif 11 in the gloss, we state regarding a woman imprisoned in a capital matter that some say she becomes prohibited even to an Israelite husband. The Gemara, Ketubot 51b, likewise states that women captured by bandits are not treated like ordinary captives, and establishes the case as involving ben Netzer. Rashi explains that he was a bandit who conquered towns, ruled over them, and became the leader of brigands. Concerning him the Mishnah states that women taken by a king's authority are not treated as ordinary captives and are prohibited to their husbands, because she assumes he will marry her and therefore submits willingly. The Gemara similarly discusses the wives of thieves where the thieves allowed them to return to their husbands but they went back to the thieves voluntarily, and they are certainly prohibited. Since the husband cannot fulfill the ketubah condition, 'I will restore you to me as a wife,' he is not obligated to redeem her. It appears that this applies specifically to the wife of an Israelite. But the wife of a Kohen who, after being captured, willingly had prohibited relations, since she had already become prohibited to him at the time of captivity and he can still fulfill the ketubah condition, 'I will return you to your country,' he remains obligated to redeem her.
