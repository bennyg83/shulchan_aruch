Or, “I paid,” by virtue of a migo that he could have said, “I returned it.” Without such a migo he is not believed to say that he paid, because the lender already took the collateral into his possession precisely to secure his money and collect from it. Moreover, if he would not be believed to say, “I returned it to you,” he would likewise not be believed to say, “I paid,” because where the lender returned the collateral in the presence of witnesses it is treated as though the collateral still remained in the lender’s possession, and the lender would be believed to claim against it up to its value.
