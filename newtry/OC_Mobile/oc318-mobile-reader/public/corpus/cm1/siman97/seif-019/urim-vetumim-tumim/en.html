There is a difficulty for the authorities who prove that a creditor acquires a pledge even with liability for unavoidable accidents from the Gemara's discussion of returning a pledge. The Gemara asks how the positive commandment can produce the categories of fulfilling or nullifying it and answers that because monetary payment remains possible, the prohibition is not treated in the ordinary way. From this they prove liability even for unavoidable accidents. The Shach answered above that where the creditor seized the pledge unlawfully, everyone agrees he bears liability for unavoidable accidents. A difficulty remains. Suppose the borrower dies. The creditor can no longer fulfill the commandment to return the pledge to him, and the prohibition remains. The Gemara's answer concerning monetary payment does not apply as it does to a convert who dies without heirs, because here the creditor failed to perform the positive commandment while the pledge itself still exists and no monetary payment is due. One cannot say that since the obstacle arose independently of the creditor the case is not classified as a failure to fulfill the positive commandment. The same is true where the pledge is lost by unavoidable accident or, in the law of a violated woman, where she dies. Yet those cases are still analyzed under the categories of fulfilling or nullifying the positive commandment even though the obstacle came from elsewhere. The matter requires study, though a distinction may be possible upon careful analysis.
