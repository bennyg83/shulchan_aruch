When the due date arrives and the creditor comes to collect his debt, the debtor is provided with his essential needs. How is this done? We tell the debtor, 'Bring all the movable property you own, leaving not even a single needle, including implements needed for preparing food.' From all the property, he is given food for thirty days at the standard of an average resident of the city, even if he previously ate at a poor person's standard, and clothing for twelve months. He is not permitted to wear silk garments or a golden turban; these are removed from him, and he is given clothing appropriate for him for twelve months. He is given a bed on which to sit, a bed, bedding, and the other necessities appropriate for him to sleep upon. If he is poor, he is given a bed and a mat upon which to sleep. Nothing from these allowances is given for his wife or children, even if they are minors whose support he is obligated to provide. He is given his sandals and tefillin. If he is a craftsman, he is given two tools of every type used in his craft. For example, if he is a carpenter, he is given two adzes and two saws. If he owns many tools of one type and few of another, he is given two of the numerous type and all he owns of the scarce type; tools of the scarce type are not purchased for him with the proceeds of the numerous type. If he is a farmer or donkey driver, he is not given his team or donkey. Similarly, if he is a sailor, he is not given his ship, even if these provide his only livelihood. These are not tools but property, and they are sold with his other movable property by beit din and given to his creditor. Even if he is a talmid chacham whose Torah study is his occupation, his sefarim are not left with him, including a Sefer Torah, because they are like other property from which a creditor collects. The same applies to seats in the synagogue when they are included among his assets; the creditor collects his debt from them. See note 40.
