From the baraita in Bava Metzia, end of 113b. Although Rabbi Yishmael and Rabbi Akiva disagree, the Gemara establishes their views as a rejected line of opinion, and the halachah does not follow them, as in Shabbat 28a.
There in the baraita, as explained there in the name of Shmuel, that it is dangerous for him if he does not walk four cubits after the meal. Rashi explains that therefore we set up a second bed four cubits away from the place where he ate, so that he will become accustomed to rise and walk to his sleeping place rather than sleep on the bed on which he ate.
There in the Mishnah in Arakhin.
The Gemara there, 24a, explains that we do not say: since he presently lacks these items, others will not lend them to him either. Nimukei Yosef at the end of HaMekabel writes that the same applies if he did not own utensils of that type, for the court does not purchase them for him.
There in the Mishnah, in accordance with the first Tanna, and so write the other poskim.
Tur in the name of Rabbi Yehudah Barceloni; Nimukei Yosef likewise cites him and agrees, and Baal HaTerumot writes the same in Shaar 1, because the Mishnah states only that his tefillin are left to him, since that mitzvah is uniquely incumbent upon his person and tefillin are worn as garments.
He also brought proof for this from the Mishnah at the end of Bikkurim, which says that a creditor takes them, like a Sefer Torah. We have received the explanation that this means a creditor collects them for his debt, and a woman for her ketubah.
Rabbeinu Yerucham, Netiv 7, part 2, writes this, and states that Rabbi David HaKohen wrote likewise.
