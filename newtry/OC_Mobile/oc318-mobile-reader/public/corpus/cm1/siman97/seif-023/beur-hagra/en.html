When the time for payment arrives... As Rabbeinu Tam and the Rosh explain, arranging what the debtor retains applies at collection. Then the remaining property is not returned. But when property is merely held as collateral, there is no such arrangement, and articles needed by the debtor are returned. Even according to Rambam and the Shulchan Aruch above, the arrangement includes only the items listed in the baraisa, as shown by the Mishnah's rule about returning a pillow and plow, articles the debtor would otherwise need to retain. See the Rosh and Tosafos 114a.
They say to the borrower, "Bring..." and not... The wording "they give him" shows that beit din determines and gives him what he may retain.
Even tools... Erchin 24a and Bava Metzia 116a show that even tools used for preparing food are not categorically exempt at the collection stage. Nimukei Yosef and the Tur cite the Ramah that the rule protecting a person's trade tools is not limited to food-preparation implements. Any occupational tools by which he earns a living are treated similarly. This differs from Rabbeinu Tam. The Maggid Mishneh writes the same.
(Collected notes) Even food-preparation implements. The Rabbis dispute the exemption of a yoke, which is a food-production implement, as Bava Metzia 116a and the cited Rishonim explain. (End of collected notes.)
