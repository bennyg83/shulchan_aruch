(Seif 4: even a wealthy person.) It is difficult why the law here says "for a temporary need" rather than stating the rule without qualification as at the beginning of the siman. Furthermore, what relevance does the statement, "and to benefit him even with words," have here? It appears that there are two textual inferences from the verse, "If you lend money to My people, to the poor person with you." The word "poor" is juxtaposed to two particular matters, implying that even toward a wealthy person there is a mitzvah regarding matters not included in those two. First, the phrase "if you lend money" indicates that regarding money the poor person has a special priority, whereas to benefit someone with words and advise him properly is a mitzvah even toward the wealthy. Second, an ordinary loan is for thirty days. The poor person has priority in such a normal loan, but regarding a short-term loan there is a mitzvah even toward a wealthy person. Therefore the law says, "It is a mitzvah to lend to him for a temporary need," meaning not an ordinary thirty-day loan.
