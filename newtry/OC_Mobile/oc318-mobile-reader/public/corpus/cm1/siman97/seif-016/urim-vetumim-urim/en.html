The pillow, etc. The creditor may not use it at all while it is in his possession. This is the view of Ramban and Ran.
And the plow. Although it appears from seif 8 above that a plow is not considered a food preparation utensil, since the debtor needs it the creditor must return it when necessary.
And he shall not make movable property. Under Talmudic law, the movable property of orphans is ordinarily not subject to a creditor's lien. These articles, however, are already encumbered to this creditor. See Tumim, where I remained uncertain whether, if the debtor sold collateral that had been returned to him for temporary use, the creditor may seize it from purchasers.
Rather, he shall collect, etc. This means even after the debtor's death. Ordinarily movable property would not remain subject to the debt after death, but these articles do. Certainly during the debtor's lifetime the creditor may in any event collect from all movable property.
He need not return it. Sema writes that if the creditor holds an article necessary to the debtor, such as a pillow or bedding, he may not sell it. See Tumim, seif katan 15, where I wrote that this does not appear correct. He need wait only thirty days, as with ordinary collateral.
Whose reward is written alongside it. Scripture says, 'So that he will bless you.' See Tumim. It appears that if the creditor took collateral by force, or took a food preparation utensil or a widow's utensil, then as long as he has not fulfilled the positive commandment of return he remains in violation of the prohibition. Beit din should therefore compel him to return it, unlike Sema. See there.
But if they wish. See what I wrote above in siman 107 concerning this matter.
