The pillow. The Sema writes that a pillow at night is mentioned as a stronger example. Although it is not absolutely indispensable, since a person can sleep on bedding or a mat, in a place where people ordinarily use a pillow the creditor must return it. The Shach writes that the creditor may not use it while it is in his possession, as is implied by the Ramban in Milchamot at the end of Shevuat HaDayanim and by the Ran there.
His children. By strict law, movable property of orphans is not subject to their father's creditor. This explains the continuation, "rather, he shall collect," for otherwise the statement would be difficult. Even during the debtor's lifetime the creditor may collect from him whenever he wishes, provided that he returns the item not as collateral but as full payment, as stated in seif 23. Even after the enactment of the Geonim there remains a practical difference, as explained in siman 107. Sema.
Is not. Darkei Moshe, siman 73, seif 19, writes that the Mordechai implies that according to all opinions an item continually needed by the debtor, such as a pillow or plow, must remain until the debtor redeems it. Accordingly, when the text says that the creditor need not return it, this must mean that he nevertheless may not sell it. Sema.
