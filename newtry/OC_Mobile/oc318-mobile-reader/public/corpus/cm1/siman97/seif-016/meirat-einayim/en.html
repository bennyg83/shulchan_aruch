The pillow at night, etc. This is the Mishnah's language. Two verses are written: "You shall not sleep with his pledge," meaning your soul shall not sleep while his pledge remains in your possession, followed by, "You shall surely return the pledge to him when the sun comes," referring to nighttime clothing. Another verse says, "until the sun comes you shall return it to him," referring to daytime clothing, which he uses all day until sunset and then returns to the lender. A pillow is mentioned to teach that even though it is not absolutely indispensable, because one could sleep on a mat, where people ordinarily use a pillow it must be returned.
And the plow by day. Although a plow is not itself a food-preparation utensil expressly prohibited by the Torah, as explained in seif 8, since the borrower needs it for the work by which he earns his livelihood and it is the implement through which food is ultimately produced, it must be returned during the day.
It does not become ordinary movable property in the hands of his heirs. Under strict law, the movable property of orphans is not subject to a creditor's lien. Therefore the ruling concludes that after the borrower dies, the lender collects from the pledge. Otherwise, even during the borrower's lifetime he could collect from it whenever he wished by returning it not as a pledge but as payment. The rule remains relevant even after the enactment of the Geonim.
Rather, he collects from the pledge, etc. The Tur concludes that he need not return it to the borrower's children; the Mechaber states this below in seif 19.
He is not obligated to return it. Darkei Moshe implies from Mordechai that everyone agrees that an item the borrower needs constantly, such as a pillow or plow, may not be sold immediately and the lender must wait until the borrower redeems it. Thus "he need not return it" means he need not surrender possession permanently, but he still may not sell it.
Its reward is stated alongside it. The verse says, "He will bless you, and it shall be righteousness for you before Hashem your God."
