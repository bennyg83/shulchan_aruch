A widow, whether poor. The Sema writes that the same applies to a divorced woman. This requires study. A widow is compelled to pay; the rule is merely that collateral is not seized from her. The reason must be that we do not cause distress to a widow, whose heart is broken and anxious. That reason does not apply to a divorced woman. Rabbi Shimon disagrees and holds that collateral may be seized even from a wealthy widow. It is therefore possible that regarding a divorced woman even the first Tanna agrees with him. If she denies the claim, she must swear. The Sema refers to the glosses on the Darchei Moshe, where he explains that this applies specifically when the creditor seized collateral in the presence of witnesses, so that he has no migo. Rivash, siman 488, explains Rambam in the same way, and Kesef Mishneh cites him at the beginning of chapter 3 of Hilchot Malveh. This is contrary to Baal HaTerumot's understanding of Rambam, cited by Beit Yosef above in siman 72, seif 18. The Sema objects that if so, the rule that she must swear upon denial is obvious, for even while the collateral is in the creditor's possession she would have to swear. I do not understand the objection. The ruling is needed lest one think that because collateral may not be seized from a widow even by order of beit din, she is not compelled to pay even when she admits the debt, and certainly need not swear when she denies it. Rambam therefore teaches that although collateral may not be seized from her, beit din nevertheless compels her to pay. If she admits, she must pay; if she denies, she must swear. This is also the implication of the Maggid Mishneh and is stated expressly by Rivash, siman 488. Rivash writes that the questioner asked how she can be made to pay upon admission if beit din may not seize collateral from her. He answers that Rambam's statement, “If she admits, she pays, and if she denies, she swears,” does not mean that the creditor may assert a claim against collateral up to its value through a migo that he purchased it. Rather, Rambam means that although collateral may not be seized from her, if she admits that she owes the debt she must pay, meaning that beit din compels her to satisfy the debt and collects from her property for the creditor. The Torah prohibited only taking an article from her as collateral, not collecting property in satisfaction of the debt. The later authorities write likewise. If she denies, she must swear despite being a widow, like any other litigant. This also resolves the objection left unanswered in Gidulei Terumah, folio 286, column 3, against Rivash, namely, why Rambam inserted this law here when it is supposedly an independent rule unrelated to taking collateral from a widow. There is no difficulty. Since Rambam stated that collateral may not be seized from her, one might think that she is not obligated to pay because beit din cannot take collateral. Rambam therefore teaches the general rule that although she may not be subjected to seizure for collateral, she remains liable to pay. Cited cross-references in the source: 70, 331, 480.
