See Tosafot Rabbi Akiva Eiger on the Mishnah, chapter 9 of Bava Metzia, letter 77, who writes that the unqualified language of all the poskim implies that no property at all may be taken as a pledge from a widow, and that the Torah's use of "garment" is not specific. However, Shiltei Giborim cites Riaz as holding that the Torah prohibited only her clothing, bedding, and similar items, while other utensils, such as silver cups and the like, may be taken from her as a pledge because they are not included in "garment." See Responsa Chatam Sofer, Even HaEzer, part 2, end of siman 136, who writes that where the widow's clothing was sealed only as security for another claim, the matter comes close to violating the prohibition against taking her property as a pledge. Even if the clothing was not removed from her house but merely sealed by way of attachment, practical halachah requires study, and in any event it is not pious conduct.
