A widow, whether poor or wealthy, may not have collateral taken from her after the time of the loan, even by an agent of beit din. If someone violated this rule and took a widow's garment as collateral, it is forcibly returned to her. If she admits the debt, she pays; if she denies it, she swears. If the collateral was lost or burned before it was returned, the person who took it is liable to lashes. When do we say that the creditor himself may not take collateral and that even an agent of beit din may not enter the borrower's house to take it? This applies to the borrower. From a guarantor, however, one may forcibly take collateral even without authorization from beit din and may enter his house to take it. Some say that regarding utensils used in food preparation there is nevertheless no distinction between a guarantor and a borrower. This exception does not apply to an arev kablan, whose law is like that of the borrower. Likewise, one who is owed wages by another, whether wages for his own labor, for the labor of his animal or utensils, or rent for his house, may take collateral without authorization from beit din and may enter the debtor's house to take it. If, however, he converted the unpaid wages into a formal loan, it is forbidden.
