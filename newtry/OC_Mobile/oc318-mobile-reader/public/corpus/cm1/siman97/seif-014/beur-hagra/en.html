Even an agent of beit din. Otherwise the Mishnah would not need a separate prohibition regarding a widow, since Rabbi Shimon disputes only the case of a wealthy widow. See Tosafos.
And if he transgressed... Following Rava in Temurah 4b. This view understands the Gemara's discussion of returning the pledge as referring specifically to this prohibition, not to food-preparation tools and a widow's property, unlike Ramban.
(Collected notes) They return it from him... The language implies immediate return. Ramban and Rashba disagree, as above. (End of collected notes.)
And if she admits. See Maggid Mishneh and Rivash siman 488. Their wording is difficult, because why should her admission be necessary? If she denied it, the creditor could swear and collect. Mishneh LaMelech and Sema explain that the case is one where the pledge was taken in the presence of witnesses, as indicated by the rule that he receives lashes.
If the pledge was lost... This requires study. See Raavad and Maggid Mishneh. Rambam appears to have had a different text in the sugya in Makkos, as is clear from his Sefer HaMitzvos.
(Collected notes) If the pledge was lost... See Tosafos, Makkos 16. Rambam follows the Rif's reading distinguishing whether the positive command was fulfilled. According to that reading, if the pledge was burned or lost by accident, the violator may be unable to fulfill the command to return it, yet he is not liable for accidental loss because a creditor holding collateral is not liable for unavoidable accidents. This explains Rambam's wording "burned or lost," meaning through accident. Other passages concerning theft differ because a robber remains liable even for accidents. (End of collected notes.)
(Collected notes) If it was lost... But the Raavad, Rashi, Tosafos, and other poskim disagree with this. (End of collected notes.)
