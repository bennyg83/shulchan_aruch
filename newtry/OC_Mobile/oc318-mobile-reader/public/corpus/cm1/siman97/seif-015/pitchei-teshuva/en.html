For repayment to a creditor is a mitzvah. See Eretz DeRabbanan, addenda, letter 177, who writes that according to the responsum of the Rosh cited by Beit Yosef in Yoreh Deah, siman 238, concerning one who swore not to sell his own property in order to repay a debt, it appears that the Rosh holds repayment to a creditor to be a Torah mitzvah, and therefore such an oath does not take effect. Mabit, part 1, siman 51, likewise writes this based on Rashi in the chapter HaKotev. Although one can question whether that derivation is only an asmachta, perhaps the cases suggesting so concern heirs, who pay only out of honor for their father, whereas the borrower himself has a Torah obligation. Mabit cites Rashbatz as deriving the rule from the verse concerning the person from whom one demands payment, and the Ramban likewise writes so at the end of Bava Batra.
We do not compel him to hire himself out. See Baer Heitev and Shaar Mishpat. Shaar Mishpat concludes that although all agree beit din does not compel a debtor to hire himself out to repay his debt, whether he personally is obligated to do so depends on a dispute. According to Tosafot he has no such obligation. According to the Mordechai in the name of Maharam, which is also the Rama's view in siman 333, he is personally obligated to hire himself out even though beit din cannot compel him.
And he cannot imprison him, etc. See Pilpula Charifta, chapter Eizehu Neshech, siman 38. Concerning the Gemara's statement that nowadays they take a document of acquisition from him, wait some time, and then lease the property back to him, in order not to close the door before borrowers, he writes that this proves it is proper to enact measures for repayment of debts even beyond strict law, such as imprisoning the debtor and similar measures. Even where the debt arises from an iska or another arrangement from which the lender profits, the rationale of not closing the door before lenders still applies, as is evident there. See there. Tumim, seif katan 13, writes: "In our times the custom has spread to imprison a borrower when he cannot pay, and no one protests. Perhaps this is because all are presumed to conceal assets. This requires study, for there is no clear source on which to rely." See Ateret Tzvi regarding enactments concerning fugitives.
