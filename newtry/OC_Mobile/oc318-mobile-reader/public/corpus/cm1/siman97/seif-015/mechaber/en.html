Some say an agent of beit din is forbidden to enter the debtor's house to take collateral only when the seizure is intended to secure payment before the debt has fallen due. But when the due date has arrived, the creditor seeks to collect, the debtor refuses to pay, and it is presumed that he owns movable property which he is concealing, an agent of beit din may enter his house, seize collateral, and pay this person's debt. Repaying a creditor is a mitzvah, and the debtor is beaten until his soul departs in order to compel its fulfillment. He is not compelled, however, to hire himself out or perform labor in order to pay. Even if he stipulated that the lender could seize his body and wrote this in the document, the stipulation is ineffective; the lender may neither imprison him nor enslave him. {Rama: This applies when he lacks the means to pay. If he has the means but refuses, beit din imprisons and beats him until his soul departs and compels him to pay. Some say that even if he lacks the means, if he swore to remain imprisoned until he pays, he must fulfill his oath. See Rivash 484, note 90, and below at the end of this siman.}
