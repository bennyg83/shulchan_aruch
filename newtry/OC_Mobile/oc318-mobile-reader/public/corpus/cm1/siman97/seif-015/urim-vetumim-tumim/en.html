Beit din imprisons him. Responsa Rashba disagrees. See Chiddushei Gershuni, which brings proof from Rashi in Pesachim 91a, s.v. "a Jewish prison," where Rashi gives as examples imprisonment to compel a man to divorce a woman whom he is forbidden to keep, or to compel payment of money. This shows that even in the Temple era people who refused lawful payment could be imprisoned until they complied. The Rosh and Rif in Moed Katan likewise explain "pursuit" as confinement in a narrow place from which the person cannot escape. Thus everything depends upon the judgment of beit din. In our times the established practice is plainly to imprison a debtor who claims inability to pay, and no one protests. Perhaps the practice assumes that such debtors are concealing assets. Even so, the basis for an unqualified rule is not entirely clear and requires study.
