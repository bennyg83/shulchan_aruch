Seif 8: Implements used in preparing food, etc. Note: See Sema, who raises a difficulty. In my opinion the Mechaber’s intent is as follows. If the lender himself seized an implement used in preparing food, he transgressed two prohibitions: first, a lender himself may not seize collateral even if the object is not used for food preparation; second, even an agent of beit din may not seize an implement used for preparing food. Therefore, although in each prohibition standing alone the act may have some legal effect subject to returning the object when the debtor needs it, where the lender violated both prohibitions together his act is wholly ineffective. There are two reasons. First, just as elsewhere one positive command does not override two negative prohibitions, here too the duty to return the article when the debtor needs it cannot neutralize two separate prohibitions. Second, absent an explicit verse we would have said that an act performed in violation of the law is ineffective. The verse reveals that in the ordinary case of one prohibition the seizure can have effect subject to the duty of return. That textual exception applies only where the Torah revealed it, namely the single-prohibition case. Where two prohibitions were violated, no such revelation exists, and we revert to the principle that the prohibited act has no legal effect. This explains the Mechaber’s precise wording, “If he seized it,” meaning that the lender himself seized the article, in which case he must return it entirely. Examine carefully.
