Until she claims her ketubah. Meaning that he cannot require an oath from her on a doubtful claim beforehand. But when she comes to collect her ketubah, he may require her to swear on a doubtful claim concerning her guardianship during his lifetime, even if he divorced her and does not claim that he previously set aside payment for her. Thus she has no oath obligation because of the ketubah itself, but she must swear concerning the shopkeeping before collecting the ketubah. Concerning her spindle and dough, however, he has no oath against her. This explains the preceding seif. I will write further that this is the view of the Rosh and Tur, unlike the apparent implication of Tosafot.
