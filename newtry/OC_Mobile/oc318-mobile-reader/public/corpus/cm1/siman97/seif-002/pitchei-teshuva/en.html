See Tosafot Rabbi Akiva Eiger on the Mishnah, chapter 9 of Bava Metzia, letter 77, who writes that the unqualified language of all the poskim implies that no property at all may be taken as a pledge from a widow, and that the Torah's use of "garment" is not specific. However, Shiltei Giborim cites Riaz as holding that the Torah prohibited only her clothing, bedding, and similar items, while other utensils, such as silver cups and the like, may be taken from her as a pledge because they are not included in "garment." See Responsa Chatam Sofer, Even HaEzer, part 2, end of siman 136, who wrote to the questioner: "Regarding the widow's clothing sealed under your seal, if there is no claim to the garments themselves and they are merely being held as security for another claim, the matter comes close to violating the prohibition 'you shall not take as a pledge.' Although perhaps you did not remove them from her house but only sealed them by way of attachment, the matter requires analysis and practical halachah requires study. In any event, this is not conduct of the pious."
