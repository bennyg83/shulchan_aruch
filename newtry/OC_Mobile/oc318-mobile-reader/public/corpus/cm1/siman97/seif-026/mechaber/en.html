When does the rule that a creditor may not collect the wife's clothing apply? To her ordinary weekday garments. A creditor may collect her Shabbos and Yom Tov garments, although some disagree. It goes without saying that if she has rings or vessels of gold or silver, they are available to the creditor, provided that the husband purchased them for her. But property that she brought into the marriage, whether nichsei melog or nichsei tzon barzel, may not be collected by the husband's creditor if it is known to be property that she brought in, or property that the husband stipulated for her at the time of the marriage. If its origin is unknown, she is believed regarding garments and jewelry of a kind that women ordinarily bring into a marriage. If she brought money into the marriage as a dowry, the creditor may collect from that money. Rama: Some say that nevertheless, if the dowry remains in her possession or in her father's possession, the creditor may not collect from it. Even if her husband was taken captive and they redeemed him, those who redeemed him may not subsequently collect reimbursement from these assets.
