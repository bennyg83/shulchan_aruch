But Shabbos garments, etc. Such expensive garments are not ordinarily given to the wife or children absolutely. The husband gives them on the understanding that if he later needs the value he may take them back and sell them. Therefore a creditor may collect from them. I explained elsewhere how to reconcile the Tur, who disagrees.
Some disagree. This refers to those who disagree specifically with collection from Shabbos and festival garments. They do not disagree regarding the following ruling that coins and gold utensils may be collected, for the reason stated above. Rama therefore inserted the dissenting view between two parts of Rambam's ruling. Although Darkei Moshe seems to follow Mordechai that even jewelry is protected from creditors, here in Shulchan Aruch Rama appears to follow Rivash, siman 128.
If there were rings among them, etc. Mordechai rules that if the husband expressly transferred the jewelry to her as her own, the creditor cannot collect it.
From what she brought into the marriage, etc. Responsa Rosh, klal 39, siman 12, discusses Shimon, who owed Reuven and married a woman whose brother-in-law gave Shimon a document over his house. When Shimon was dying he instructed that the document be returned to his wife. The Rosh ruled that Reuven cannot collect from that security. If the house is treated as dowry, the creditor does not collect it; if it is treated as collateral, Shimon instructed that the collateral document be returned.
Money in the dowry, etc. The reason is that money is given to be spent and enters the husband's ownership immediately.
