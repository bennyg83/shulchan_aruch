He is obligated to return it to him. Meaning at the time the borrower needs it for work, in the case of food-preparation utensils. Rama's gloss, that the law of a private creditor is like that of a beit din agent, refers to this as well. "Food-preparation utensil" is not meant exclusively; any item the borrower needs is returned to him when needed, as Rambam writes explicitly. Rama comes only to exclude things the borrower does not need at all, as he says, "but if the lender took other items as collateral, etc."
A treasurer of hekdesh. A treasurer need not return the pledge even at the time of work. This applies whether one consecrated a maneh for Temple maintenance or for the poor, because the poor too acquire from Heaven's table, and the verse concerning return of a pledge, "and it shall be righteousness for you," does not apply toward Heaven, since all righteousness is His. So writes Baal HaTerumot.
