(Collected notes) And although... One might explain the passage as referring to food-preparation tools, but the arrangement among these laws and the Rama's gloss show otherwise. The Shulchan Aruch appears to follow Rambam, who requires immediate return, unlike Ramban and Rashba, who require return only when the debtor needs the article for its function. The precise wording here is difficult, especially the distinction involving the person who takes the pledge, because that distinction appears expressly only in the law of returning collateral when needed. The formulation therefore requires study. (End of collected notes.)

And this is specifically... The Gemara says, "Come, return it to him." The Maggid Mishneh, citing Ramban and Rashba, explains that even food-preparation implements need be returned only when needed for their function. Thus the Mishnah says the plow is returned by day. The Yerushalmi says similarly of an axe and plow: one may seize them at night and return them by day. This is the view of the Milchamos and Nimukei Yosef, and explains the statement above in seif 6 that he returns it. The item in the Gemara was returned because the debtor needed it then, but initially one may seize it when not in use.
