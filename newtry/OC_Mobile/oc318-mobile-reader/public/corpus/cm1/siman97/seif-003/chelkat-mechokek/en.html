This is not called claiming her ketubah, etc. Responsa Maharam cited by Mordechai gives two reasons. First, she need not swear concerning the shopkeeping unless she must in any event swear because of her ketubah. Second, this is not called claiming the ketubah because she is already in possession of the land. According to the first reason, if he divorced her and does not claim prior payment, he cannot require an oath about the guardianship, because she has no ketubah oath. The Rosh and Tur, however, do not write that way. According to them, even a divorced woman with no ketubah oath may still have to swear about her business dealings when she claims her ketubah. Accordingly, the same should apply to an widow whom the husband exempted from oath or for whom specific land or movables were designated. Even though she has no ketubah oath, when she seeks to extract her ketubah she may be required to swear concerning the shopkeeping. Tosafot in Ketubot 88b appears to follow Maharam's first reason: because the mishnah's first clause speaks of heirs swearing her when she claims her ketubah, the latter clause also mentions heirs. That implies a divorced woman claiming her ketubah cannot be sworn about the shopkeeping if she has no ketubah oath. The Rosh and Tur, however, appear to follow Maharam's second reason, that the decisive factor is whether she seeks to extract her ketubah. A woman in possession of land is not considered a claimant. I saw Bach distinguish between a divorced woman and a widow who has no oath obligation, but I do not know the source for such a distinction. Once the matter depends upon claiming the ketubah, what difference is there between widow and divorcee? The mishnah itself states that whenever she claims her ketubah the heirs can swear her and does not mention divorce at all.
