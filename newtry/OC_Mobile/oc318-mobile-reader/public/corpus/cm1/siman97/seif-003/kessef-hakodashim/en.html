And his tefillin. Although a creditor does not collect a debtor's tefillin and when arranging payment for a debtor we leave him his tefillin, perhaps a creditor who already holds the tefillin may retain them until the debtor gives him another pledge, if he says with certainty that the debtor has another item to provide. Even without certainty, perhaps he may hold them until the debtor swears that he has no other pledge, or accepts a general ban. This resembles a case of 'I do not know whether I became obligated to return.' If the tefillin were already in his possession before the debt, perhaps it is closer to 'I do not know whether I repaid.' Even if he seized them afterward, perhaps the principle that a tree stands destined to be cut gives the matter a similar character. This requires study.
