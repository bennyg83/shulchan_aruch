It need not be said that she may not take future support, etc., until she is supported from them. The wording implies that the point is not merely future support but even support already consumed. In my opinion the meaning is that when she originally seized the property she did so in order to support herself in the future, and now, when the creditor comes to collect, she has already consumed it for support.
Until the lender is paid, etc. Even according to the view that "her food" in the Torah refers to maintenance, so that a husband's obligation of support has a Torah basis, the later creditor still precedes her. The Torah obligated the husband to support her when he has means, so that she eats with him as part of his household. Here he has no available means because the creditor's property stands for repayment. Even the thirty days of food left to the debtor come from what would otherwise belong to the creditor, and the Torah commanded that this allowance be for him, not for her. Rabbinically the husband must endeavor to support her even when he lacks means, as reflected in the ketubah formula, "I will work and support you," but her rabbinic right, even if written in her ketubah, does not outrank the lender's written debt, which arose from a Torah monetary obligation. If she herself borrowed money for support under a note earlier than her husband's later note, her lender's claim runs against her, not directly against the husband. When she later seeks support from him, the husband's creditor already holds an enforceable lien. This preference applies particularly to the husband's written debt, upon which the lender relied as a lien. An oral lender lacks the same reliance and does not precede her support claim; they then come as concurrent creditors. The reason is therefore not merely that support is rabbinic, but that the creditor's enforceable written lien has priority.
However, if the dowry money is still in her possession, she may seize it for her support. Mahari Weil explains that she may seize even for future support. This resolves the wording here, which says "for her support," implying that any surplus would go to the creditor, whereas in seif 26 Rama writes that if the dowry remains in her possession the creditor does not collect from it. Future support has no fixed limit, and therefore even a large amount may be held for it, as explained in Even HaEzer siman 93.
