So too writes Ba'al HaTerumot, Gate 1, in the name of a responsum of the Rif, based on the Mishnah cited above in Arakhin: but not for his wife and children. The same applies to a creditor. He explains that the creditor's lien is d'oraisa, as taught by Ulla, Bava Batra 175b, while support of one's wife and children is d'rabbanan. The Bach writes as practical halachah that because there is a dispute among the authorities, and we rule that a wife's support is d'oraisa, as stated in Even HaEzer siman 69, if her claim preceded the creditor's and she seized movable property, it is not removed from her possession. A creditor holding a document cannot nullify the wife's lien, which is also d'oraisa. However, the Semag also follows Rif, Rambam, and Ba'al HaTerumot, that seizure does not help her. It appears to me that although Rambam and Semag hold that support is d'oraisa, their reason here follows the first reason of Ba'al HaTerumot and Rif, by analogy to Arakhin, where she receives no support, since the law is derived by the verbal analogy of 'mikhah' from Arakhin, Bava Metzia 114a.

It is explained there that with regard to her support, the wife is herself a creditor, because the husband has already become obligated to her, either by enactment of beit din or by the clause written in her ketubah, 'and your support,' etc. Beit Yosef. The rule that a creditor with a document precedes an oral creditor applies to land, but with movable property there is no priority, as Rabbenu writes in siman 104. See there.
