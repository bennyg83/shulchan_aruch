Rather, even... because a wife has no... even... Follow the rationale: why is the wife not given priority? Because her lien is rabbinic, as stated in Kesubos 47b, whereas a creditor's lien is Torah law. According to the view that liens are not Torah law, a creditor does not collect from purchasers, and the same applies to property already encumbered to another creditor. Rashba writes this in responsum 1122, proving that every debt is comparable to encumbered property from Bava Kamma. See also below, siman 101 seif 4 in the gloss.
And even if he went... although... Although the husband must ultimately pay, as stated in Kesubos 107b, he is not directly indebted to his wife's creditor. His obligation is to his wife, and she in turn owes the creditor; collection from him operates through Rabbi Nasan's rule. The Rosh writes so. This is also shown by the Gemara's discussion of a woman who performs mi'un. Were the husband already directly obligated to the creditor, her later mi'un would not alter that obligation. The issue therefore returns to the fact that her own lien is rabbinic.
