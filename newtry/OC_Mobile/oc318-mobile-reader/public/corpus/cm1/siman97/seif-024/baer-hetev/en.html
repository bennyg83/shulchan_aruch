According to the Mechaber's ruling in siman 104, seif 13, that an earlier oral loan precedes a later documented loan, the matter requires study. See the Sema there. It also requires study according to the authorities who hold that liens are rabbinic, as noted at the beginning of siman 39. Further, the Tur in Even HaEzer 69 rules that a wife's maintenance is a Torah obligation, and the Rambam rules likewise at the beginning of chapter 12 of Hilchot Ishut; the Maggid Mishneh discusses this at length. See Responsa Mahara Sasson 48. It may be answered that when the Mechaber writes here that if the lender's debt is oral, he is not addressing priority at all, but relies on what he wrote in siman 104. Rather, he is explaining the converse of the first case. When the lender has a documented debt, even if the wife's maintenance obligation arose earlier, she does not collect maintenance first. If the lender's debt is oral, the matter returns to the general rule governing two creditors who come to collect.
