It goes without saying that a wife does not receive her future support before the creditor is paid. Even if she seized her husband's property, such as when he traveled overseas and she seized some property for her support, it is removed from her and given to the creditor. A wife's support is collected from neither land nor movable property until the creditor is paid, even if her right preceded his debt. Even if her husband traveled overseas, she borrowed for her support under a promissory note, and her documented support loan preceded the other creditor's documented loan, the support loan is not collected until the holder of the husband's note is paid first. If the husband's debt was oral, while she borrowed orally or under a document for her support, whichever creditor first comes to collect takes precedence. If both come together, they divide the available property. {Rama: If her dowry money is still in her possession or in her father's possession, she may retain it for her support. If her husband owed non-Jews, they sued him in their courts, the wife appeared with her ketubah, and the non-Jewish creditors were displaced because their law gives a wife priority to collect her dowry, then if a Jewish creditor subsequently seeks his debt and demands the property she collected in their court, the law follows the creditor. A ketubah and dowry were not granted for collection during the husband's lifetime, as explained in Even HaEzer, siman 53. See notes 6 and 107.}
