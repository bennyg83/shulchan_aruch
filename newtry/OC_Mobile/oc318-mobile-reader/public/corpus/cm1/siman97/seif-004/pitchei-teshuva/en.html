For repayment to a creditor is a mitzvah. See Eretz DeRabbanan, addenda, letter 177, who writes that according to the responsum of the Rosh cited by Beit Yosef in Yoreh Deah, siman 238, concerning one who swore not to sell his own property in order to repay a debt, it appears that the Rosh holds repayment to a creditor to be a Torah mitzvah, and therefore such an oath does not take effect. Mabit, part 1, siman 51, likewise writes this based on Rashi in the chapter HaKotev, who derives the obligation from the verse requiring one's "yes" to be righteous. One can question this, because at first glance that derivation appears only to be an asmachta, since the plain meaning of the verse concerns honest measures. This also appears from Rashi in the chapter Mi SheHaya Nasui, in the case of a small parcel of land belonging to orphans. Perhaps, however, that case is different because it concerns orphans, who are obligated only because of the honor of their father, whereas the borrower himself has a Torah mitzvah to repay. Mabit there cites Rashbatz as deriving the rule from the verse concerning the man from whom you demand payment, and the Ramban likewise writes so at the end of Bava Batra.
