For the purpose. The Sema explains that even if the borrower does not intend to destroy the money deliberately, whatever he spends without a substantial need is considered destroying it, because he thereby causes a loss to the lender. The Taz explains that if he spends it for the very purpose for which he borrowed it, then even if it is lost there is no prohibition, for the lender lent it on that understanding. If he spends it for another purpose but the money is not thereby lost, there is likewise no prohibition, because the lender did not lend it exclusively for that particular undertaking. So long as the money remains intact, the lender does not care that he changed from what he had stated before him. The prohibition exists only when he changes from the purpose for which the money was lent and loses it in that undertaking.
