Not at the time of the loan. Since the passage discusses an agent of beit din, it cannot refer to the moment of the loan.
And even if... The verse says, "You shall stand outside and the man..." showing that even an agent of beit din must remain outside, although he is not himself entering to seize the pledge, because the borrower brings it out to him.
And the agent may... The Gemara permits him to seize forcibly, which must mean by force; otherwise there would be no distinction between the creditor and the court's agent. Rambam.
And even outside he may not... See Tosafos, Bava Metzia 115a, whose second explanation is primary.
Not at the time... The Gemara 113a asks from "he shall not take millstones as a pledge." If the verse applied at the time of the loan, it could have answered accordingly, showing that the prohibition is not about collateral voluntarily given when the loan is made.
But houses... rather collection. See Maggid Mishneh, Hilchos Malveh chapter 3, and below seif 15 and seif 23. The Gemara in Bava Metzia and Bava Kamma uses the language of beit din assigning a house in collection. The restrictions on taking a pledge are more severe, even for beit din.
And he returns it. Baal HaTerumos writes this in the name of Ramban. Ramban holds that even tools used for preparing food need be returned only at the time they are needed for work. See Milchamos and Nimukei Yosef. See seif 12 and seif 8.
