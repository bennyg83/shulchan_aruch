(In the Tur, seif 18) “But if he has two.” This implies that even though the debtor works with both tools, the creditor may nevertheless take one. One cannot say that a person performs the work with only one, because the first clause specifically speaks of one who has only one tool, necessarily one he actually uses, for otherwise it would be obvious that the creditor may take it. The continuation, “but if he has,” therefore likewise means that he uses both. This is also evident from the later distinction concerning implements of his trade, which likewise refers to tools he uses. In the law of arranging the debtor’s necessities, it is obvious that an idle tool is not left with him. Likewise, the phrase “he does no work from which he supports himself through wages” means that he does work with both tools but does not support himself from the wages, and even so one may be taken. This is difficult from the Tosefta cited by Maggid Mishneh and Beit Yosef: if he has five plowshares, the creditor may not take even one. If the Tosefta refers to all five being necessary for his work, surely leaving him two should be sufficient.
