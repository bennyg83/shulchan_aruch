The wording of the Tur, seif 10, from the Mishnah, Bava Metzia 113a.
Sefer Bedek HaBayit writes that if the creditor transgressed and took collateral himself, he acquires it.
Statement of Shmuel there.
He explains there that they impose a cherem upon the borrower until he produces all his property, or brings a written list of all he owns before beit din, and the court arranges what he may retain. Sema. See siman 99, seif 1.
The wording of the Rambam, chapter 3 of Hilkhot Malveh, law 4, as explained by the Mishnah and the statement cited above.
Rabbeinu Yerucham writes, Netiv 31, part 2, that a beit din agent may strike him if he resists in the marketplace. Sema.
The wording of the Tur, seif 13, explicit in the Torah: "He shall not take a millstone or upper millstone as collateral, for he takes a life as collateral."
So too is written in Sefer HaTerumot, gate 1, in the name of the Ramban.
Meaning, beit din may not send its agent to close the mill in which he grinds until he redeems it, for the reason stated by the Mechaber. If the creditor himself closes the mill in order to hold it as security, he does not violate "You shall not enter his house to take his pledge," because this is not included in the category of a pledge taken from the house, and he is not obligated to return it daily. Sema.
