It is a positive mitzvah to lend, etc. The Rashba was asked why we do not recite a blessing over gifts to the poor and loans to others as we do over other mitzvot. He answers that fulfillment does not depend upon the giver alone, because perhaps the poor person will refuse to accept the gift or loan. This answer appears inconsistent with the Rosh's explanation at the beginning of Ketubot for why one does not recite "Who sanctified us with His commandments and commanded us to marry a woman." The Rosh answers that the mitzvah of procreation can be fulfilled through a pilegesh. See there. That answer itself depends upon the dispute whether a pilegesh is permitted. Why did the Rosh not answer more simply that marriage does not depend upon the man alone, because perhaps the woman will refuse to marry him? Evidently, the Rosh does not accept the Rashba's rationale. According to the Rosh, another answer must therefore be given. Lending to the poor is associated with a condition of misfortune and curse, for the blessing says, "you shall lend and you shall not borrow," and "there shall be no needy among you." Our sins have caused the opposite condition. It is therefore inappropriate to recite a blessing over a mitzvah whose occasion reflects human distress and misfortune.
