One who sets up his wife as a shopkeeper, etc. The rule in this seif follows only the opinion cited in the next seif, that a husband may require an oath from her concerning the shopkeeping whenever he wishes, while concerning her spindle and dough he may require an oath only by way of an incorporated oath or on a certain claim. But according to Rabbi Shimon, whose view the Tur adopts, even if he set her up as a shopkeeper or appointed her as a guardian he cannot require such an oath. Under that view there is no distinction between shopkeeping and her spindle and dough. It is therefore difficult that the Mechaber states this rule without qualification here, when in the next seif he records a dispute. See what I write nearby.
