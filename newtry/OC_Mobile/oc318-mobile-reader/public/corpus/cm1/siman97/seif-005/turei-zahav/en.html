(See there, however, if the funds of her dowry) According to Sema, seif katan 58, Rama’s language refers to future support, and that resolves the matter. I am surprised, because it resolves nothing. Whatever exceeds the amount needed for support is collected by the creditor, as stated in Even HaEzer 53, and the excess belongs to the heirs; the same should apply here in favor of the creditor. This contradicts Rama’s later statement that the creditor collects nothing at all from the dowry. It appears to me that Rama’s intent is as follows. Even according to the view cited by the Mechaber in seif 26 that a creditor may collect from dowry funds, all agree that for her maintenance the wife may retain dowry money still in her possession, with only the excess going to the creditor. In seif 26, Rama then cites the dissenting view that the creditor may not collect dowry funds at all. Rama thus resolved the relationship between the opinions. This is why he did not write “some say” here as he does later, for the present rule is accepted by all. If she brought a house into the marriage as dowry, her husband’s creditor cannot collect that house according to any opinion. In Even HaEzer 90, seif 14, there is a dispute concerning the husband’s sale of guaranteed dowry property that is movable, implying that all agree a sale of land is void. The Rosh likewise states this in a responsum cited in Even HaEzer 118. If land mortgaged to Shimon, etc., is treated as though she brought it to her husband as dowry, no lien of the husband’s creditor ever attached to it, just as the husband could not sell it.
