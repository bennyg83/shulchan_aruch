Or he said, "I will take a little, etc." This teaches that even though the worker already took the food into his own possession in order to eat it, we do not say that he has already acquired it absolutely and may therefore give it to whomever he wishes, as the dissenting opinion cited by the Tur holds. The Tur himself rejects that view. The reason is that a worker's right to eat is not an added component of his wages granted to him by the Torah, for if it were, he could give his property to whomever he wished. Rather, the Torah granted the worker a gift out of kindness, like the gifts to the poor, leket, shikchah, and peah. We therefore say that the Torah grants it to him only when it is actually placed into his mouth to chew. See what the Ir Shushan wrote, and my gloss there showing that his formulation is imprecise.
