Seif 20. For they were particular even about the fourth blessing of Birkat HaMazon, that workers should not recite it. Note: See the Sema, who writes that workers are exempt from zimun. See Magen Avraham's objection. In truth, the Sema's reading of the Tur is difficult, because why would it matter to the Tur whether workers are exempt from the fourth blessing when they are joining a zimun or when they are reciting Birkat HaMazon alone? Yet the Tur's own words also trouble me, because Berakhot 46 explicitly discusses the blessing of workers, and Rashi there appears to state clearly that workers are obligated in zimun, contrary to the Sema. One could perhaps explain the Sema more narrowly. If workers are eating together with others and finish before the others, an ordinary diner might have to wait for the others to finish so they can make zimun, whereas workers need not delay their employer's work for that extended period. But if all diners finish together, the employer does not object to the brief delay required for zimun, so the workers should join. This would also fit the proof cited by Magen Avraham and seems consistent with some of the Sema's wording. Yet the Gemara remains difficult, because Rashi there appears to explain the baraita as distinguishing directly between two workers and three workers. According to the proposed reading, Rashi could instead have explained the whole baraita as speaking of three workers, with the difference being whether they finished together or not, which would have produced a greater novelty. His failure to do so suggests that workers are always obligated in zimun when three are present, contrary to the Sema. This requires study.
