(And they do not deduct it) Although I know that I am unworthy to dispute the authorities, I must state that money should not be extracted on the basis of this ruling. The Rosh reasons that every removal without payment is itself an extraction, comparing it to a mortgage involving rabbinic interest where the lender has already consumed produce equal to the debt and is not removed without receiving his principal, because rabbinic interest is not recovered by beit din. But these cases are not comparable. If Reuven collected rabbinic interest from Shimon while Shimon separately holds money belonging to Reuven, Shimon may not retain that money as reimbursement for the interest. Indeed, even a creditor holding a mortgage is not removed without payment precisely because the produce already taken is legally retained and has become his money. In an unresolved legal doubt, by contrast, possession governs. According to the Rambam, seizure may help even after the doubt; according to the Rosh, it does not help after a doubt of law arises, but possession acquired before the doubt certainly does, as Tosafot explain concerning the priest who seized a doubtful firstborn. Rabbinic interest is different because the Sages themselves allowed the recipient to retain what was already paid. An unresolved monetary question rests on doubt, and one who already holds the opposing party's money may retain it where the rules of possession allow. Therefore, if wages are owed to him, there is no reason he may not deduct the disputed amount. The contrary ruling is astonishing.
