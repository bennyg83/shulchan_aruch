He may not eat from another vine, etc. The Gemara leaves unresolved whether a worker may eat only from the very object upon which he is working or merely from the same species. It attempts to resolve the question from a baraita stating that one working with figs may not eat grapes, implying that figs from another fig tree might be permitted, and answers with a case where one tree is trained over another. Rashi explains that if one requires both the same species and the very produce upon which one is working, the baraita speaks of intertwined trees. Thus, where one fig tree is trained over another, he may eat because he is working on both, but he may not eat grapes while working on figs even though the branches are intertwined. Since the underlying question remains unresolved, we follow the leniency favoring the party in possession. Therefore, if the worker already ate, it does not appear that we extract payment from him merely because he ate from another tree of the same species. The Ir Shushan's stricter ruling in the intertwined fig and grape case is not clear to me.
