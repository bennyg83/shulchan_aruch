A worker who performs labor, etc. The main rules of this siman are derived from the verses in Ki Tetzei: 'When you come into your fellow's vineyard, you may eat grapes as your desire, to your satisfaction, but you shall not put any into your vessel.' The Sages interpret this as referring to a worker. They derive that the permission applies to ordinary private produce, not consecrated produce, to actual eating rather than sucking out the juice and discarding the seeds, and not to improving the fruit with another food so as to eat more. 'To your satisfaction' excludes gluttonous eating, and 'you shall not put into your vessel' teaches that the worker may eat when he is doing the final work that places produce into the owner's vessel. From 'You shall not muzzle an ox while it threshes' we likewise learn permission to eat from detached produce before completion of the work for tithes. The Gemara compares worker and animal, teaching the parallel rules for attached and detached produce.
