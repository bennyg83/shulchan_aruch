[Shach, letter 1] So it is in the Gemara. Note: This follows the Ritva cited in Shitah Mekubetzet, whose words are: This is understandable according to the one who holds that theft from a non-Jew is prohibited, for one may say that here too the worker may not eat, since the Torah treats it as theft because the non-Jew is not included in the mitzvah permitting a worker to eat. This implies that he explains your fellow, but not a non-Jew, to mean that eating is prohibited because the non-Jew is not subject to that mitzvah and the worker's eating from him is theft. Even according to the conclusion that, for the view permitting theft from a non-Jew, the verse excludes consecrated property, it remains true that according to the view prohibiting such theft the verse excludes both a non-Jew and consecrated property, as Shitah Mekubetzet later explicitly cites from the Ritva. According to our text of the Gemara, however, the phrase this is understandable according to the one who holds theft from a non-Jew is prohibited means that a verse is needed to permit the worker to eat. It therefore appears to exclude and permit in the case of a non-Jew, necessarily even allowing him to place food into his vessels. One may answer that this was only the initial assumption. Initially, according to the view that theft from a non-Jew is prohibited, your fellow could be understood as excluding a non-Jew and permitting even placing food into his vessels. But at the conclusion, according to the view that theft from a non-Jew is permitted and the verse is used to exclude consecrated property, it may be that the one who prohibits theft from a non-Jew also uses the verse for consecrated property. Why invent a novel permission for a worker to place food into his vessels when we can interpret your fellow as excluding consecrated property? In any event, eating from a non-Jew's produce is certainly permitted, since even at the initial stage the Gemara treats it as obvious that a non-Jew should not be more stringent than a Jew, as it does not suggest that a verse is needed to prohibit eating. Examine carefully.
