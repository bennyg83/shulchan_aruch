(Seif 3: Wheat intended for bread) The Sema writes that this applies specifically to wheat, whose ordinary use is bread. This is difficult from the baraita in Bava Metzia concerning produce subject to a worker's right to eat. Rashi makes the rule depend upon whether the produce is ordinarily processed into bread, whereas Tosafot derive the categories from the verse and do not distinguish what is commonly baked. The Gemara's discussion of terumah and tithes likewise indicates that the statutory classification, not ordinary frequency, controls. Since the completion of labor for challah is also derived from the threshing-floor model, all grain species fit for challah remain unfinished for this purpose whether or not the particular owner usually bakes them. Nevertheless, because Rashi makes the matter depend on ordinary practice, one should be stringent and not permit a worker to eat produce when he knows that this employer does not normally make bread from it. The distinction therefore follows the employer's practice, not simply whether the species is wheat, barley, or another grain.
