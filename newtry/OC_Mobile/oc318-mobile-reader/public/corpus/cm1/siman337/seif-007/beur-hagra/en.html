Nevertheless, etc. This is the Rosh's language. The halachah follows the Sages and not the anonymous first clause, for we find many anonymous Mishnah statements that represent an individual opinion, as when the Gemara says, "the anonymous Mishnah is Rabbi Meir." The term "Sages," however, denotes a plurality, and their reasoning is also more compelling, etc.
Others say specifically, etc. As above, s.v. "like the Sages."
(Collected note) Others say, etc. The Rosh and Tur. This is also the view of the Rif and Rambam, who omitted Rabbi Elazar's statements.
And even, etc. See Beer HaGolah. Although Rashi and Rosh's language implies that Rabbi Elazar's other statement is unrelated to the dispute in the Mishnah, the Rif and Rambam, who omit it, hold that it too is part of the dispute. There is also proof from the Gemara's statement that both teachings were necessary. The Tur likewise rules so. See Beit Yosef.
