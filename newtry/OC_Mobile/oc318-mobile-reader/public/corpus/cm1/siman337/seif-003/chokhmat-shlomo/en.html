Seif 12. If he fixed with the employer that he may eat up to a specified amount, etc. Note: The Gemara asks whether a worker eats from his own entitlement, meaning as an addition to his wages granted by the Torah, or whether he eats from Heaven's grant rather than as wages. Rashi repeatedly explains that according to the first view it is part of his compensation. This is difficult. We rule that wages are not payable until the end of the employment period, and according to the view that wage liability itself does not begin until completion, how can the worker eat at any time during the work? One must say that the Torah granted him this right independently of ordinary wage law. But if so, in what sense can it be called wages? Why should this supplementary compensation be stronger than the principal wage itself? Especially in light of Pesachim, where Rabbi Akiva derives that destruction of chametz must occur before Yom Tov because kindling on Yom Tov is prohibited, and Pnei Yehoshua explains that we do not presume a special decree of Scripture overturning an established general rule where the verse can be explained otherwise. Here too, since the general rule is that wages are payable only at the end, why assume that the Torah created a special wage component payable from the outset, when the passage can instead be understood according to the other view, that the worker eats from Heaven's grant and not as wages? This requires study. There is also a practical question within the view that he eats from Heaven's grant: is the restriction only before the food actually reaches him, or does it continue even after he has taken it? Rashi's language suggests the former, while the silence of other poskim may imply that even after it reaches him he may not transfer it to his wife or children. A practical difference could arise whether he can use it to effect kiddushin. I cannot elaborate further now.
