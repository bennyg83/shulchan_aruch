From things that grow from the ground. Similar to a vineyard and threshing, as mentioned above.
While attached, etc. Initially the law teaches when the worker begins to have a right to eat, namely from the time the produce completes the relevant stage of work and not before. It then teaches how long he may continue eating. It is as though it says: a worker may eat even from attached produce, but only once it has reached maturity and he has begun placing it into the owner's vessels. He may also eat from detached produce while working with it until the stage of work that creates liability for tithes or challah is completed, as explained.
One who harvests grapes, picks olives, and gathers figs, etc. All these cases involve a worker who eats while harvesting the produce from where it is attached. Each type of harvesting has its own term: grapes are harvested by betzirah, olives by mesikah, and the other produce likewise has its particular term.
At the final obligation. This means the final stage that creates the relevant obligation, as explained in the following seif. See there.
And one who makes cheese. Meaning one who forms cheeses. One who churns or sets milk is causing it to coagulate. These are not products that grow from the ground in the manner of a vineyard and threshing.
Even one who weeds among onions, etc. It is not only where he removes other inferior vegetables from among them that he may not eat, because we derive from "you shall not put into your vessel" that a worker may eat only at the completion of the crop's development, when it is being placed into the owner's vessel. Even if he is weeding out small onions that will never grow large and removes them from between the larger onions to give the larger ones room to spread, there is now a placement into the owner's vessel of those small onions. Nevertheless, since the principal purpose of the work is for the large onions and their work is not complete, he may not eat even from the small ones.
