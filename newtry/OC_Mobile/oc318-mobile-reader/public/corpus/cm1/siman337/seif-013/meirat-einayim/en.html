To suck the grapes, etc. I also explained this at the beginning of the siman. It is derived from the verse's word "and you shall eat," excluding sucking out only the juice.
To singe the ears in fire or crush them on a rock. Grapes too can be improved by singeing or crushing on a rock, even fully ripe grapes. Tosafos write in Rabbeinu Yitzchak's name that he crushes and breaks them into small pieces so that he can put a little into his mouth at a time. In the Perishah I addressed why the Tur and Mechaber grouped the prohibition of singeing with the rules that he may not dip his bread in salt and may not suck the grapes. Those latter prohibitions are clearly established in the Gemara, whereas singeing is an unresolved question. We have already explained that with an unresolved monetary question we do not extract payment and do not deduct the value from his wage, unlike conduct that the Gemara clearly forbids.
