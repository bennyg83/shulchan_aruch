And in matchmaking. The same law applies to brokerage. Shach. See Beurim: the same applies to a hired worker. In all these cases, specifically where the agreed compensation exceeds the ordinary rate by more than one sixth may the promisor say, "I was merely putting you off," and this is specifically where no kinyan was made. If a kinyan was made, it is effective even where the compensation greatly exceeds the ordinary rate, because he knowingly accepted the sudar kinyan. This applies only to these types of services. With a mitzvah obligation, such as one who promised a dinar to have another cross a river for him, or promised compensation for returning a lost object, even a kinyan does not help unless the payment was already made. Regarding matchmakers and brokers, the rule that only ordinary compensation is owed applies specifically where there is an established rate. In a place where there is no fixed rate, he must pay the full amount he stipulated.
