For medical treatment he must pay him. If one promises another compensation for revealing a secret that will enable him to make a substantial commercial profit, there is room to compare this to payment for medical treatment. He may therefore be obligated to pay the full sum promised even when it greatly exceeds the amount of labor involved. It is stronger than an ordinary brokerage fee. If the agreed compensation was that the recipient would be obligated to sell or obtain specified merchandise for him at a fixed price, and before performance the merchandise became more expensive, perhaps the promisor can say that he never contemplated the increase. If he said that he would either provide the goods from his own stock or purchase them, the wording may leave the choice with the one who undertook the obligation, and after the price rose he need not supply goods from his own property. If he later stated that he had in fact purchased the goods and afterward claims that he did not, his own earlier statement can create sufficient grounds for a hesset oath.
