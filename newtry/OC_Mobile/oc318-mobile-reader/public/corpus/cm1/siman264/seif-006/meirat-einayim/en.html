For he caused him no loss at all. Nevertheless, the ordinary wage due for his labor and use of his tools must be paid. The case concerns a person who normally hires himself out to perform work for others with those tools, so his lost employment is ascertainable. Had he not been occupied with this rescue, he would have been doing other paid work. He is not obligated to abandon his livelihood without compensation merely to save this person's property. The same reasoning applies to the ferry operator mentioned immediately afterward. It further appears that even if it is known that at this particular moment he had no other job, and likewise the ferry operator had no passenger to transport, he must nevertheless be paid the ordinary wage. Throughout the year, when someone asks him to transport a passenger or perform work with his tools, whether heavier or lighter, people customarily pay him. It is not proper that this person should receive the benefit for free merely because his own property or body needed rescue. At minimum, therefore, the rescuer receives the ordinary market wage.
