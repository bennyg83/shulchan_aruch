(Or he stipulated this before a beit din.) The Tur’s text reads: “When does this apply? Where the owner of the donkey was present and the rescuer did not stipulate. But if the owner was absent, or was present and the rescuer stipulated with him, or the rescuer stipulated before three persons,” and so forth. The statement about stipulating before three requires analysis. If the donkey’s owner is present, why should the rescuer not have to stipulate specifically with him? Even if a beit din is present, how may they authorize him to take another person’s property while that owner is present without asking whether he agrees? If they asked and he did not agree, what authority does the beit din have? If the owner is absent, however, no stipulation is required, as stated first. We must therefore say that the teaching is that even when the owner is absent, if a beit din is present the rescuer must stipulate before them. The phrase “or he stipulated before a beit din” is intended by way of negative inference: if he did not stipulate, he does not receive the full value. The Sema, seif katan 8, writes that this applies even where the other person was present and the rescuer did not stipulate with him. This is astonishing for the reason stated. The structure of the Tur’s language also proves otherwise. He divided the matter into two clauses, rather than writing, “When does this apply? Where the owner of the donkey or a beit din was present and he did not stipulate, but if either was present,” which would imply equivalence and that a beit din can substitute for the owner. It is clear that the owner is primary. Nimukei Yosef likewise proves this in HaGozel Batra in the name of Ran. Ran objects to Rif’s version, “If three were present, he stipulates with him before the beit din,” because if the owner is present, why must he stipulate before a beit din? A private stipulation with the owner suffices. Evidently, the case of stipulation before a beit din is where the owner is absent. Were the Sema correct that a stipulation before beit din suffices even without stipulating with the present owner, Ran’s question against Rif would disappear. The explanation I have written of the Tur is therefore correct.
