Seif 4, gloss. Note: I was asked by Rabbi Moshe, moreh tzedek of New Konstantin, about a communal enactment limiting the number of shops. The enactment had long been firmly established with the approval of leading rabbis. One person sought to breach it by obtaining the nobleman's permission to build twenty new shops. Some members of the community therefore compromised with the nobleman and agreed that every existing shop would pay eight gold coins in tax, where previously only four had been paid. Those who were not present before the nobleman objected that they had never agreed. I answered that all are obligated to contribute. This is explicit here in siman 264, seif 4, gloss, and likewise in siman 163 regarding communal expenses. If beit din determines that the arrangement benefits everyone, including by lowering the value or competitive pressure upon their own shops, all who benefit must pay. The same principle appears where danger or a decree threatening one community could spread to others, and in siman 158 concerning one who benefits from another's protective wall. A beneficiary cannot say that he did not need the protection. As for the amount, although the agreement with the nobleman fixed an equal sum per shop, the community should apportion the burden internally according to the value of each shop, the extent of the depreciation or protection received, the location of the proposed new shops, and how much each existing shop would have been harmed. More valuable shops and shops in busier locations should contribute proportionally more. The precise assessment belongs to beit din together with experienced householders. This was my ruling.
