"However, there shall be no pauper among you." We expound this as though it says, "Guard yourself against becoming impoverished." For this reason, it is not specifically his own lost object that takes precedence. Rather, any clear monetary loss or loss of work caused by performing the return takes precedence, as the Tur and Mechaber likewise write later in this siman and in siman 265. In the Perishah I explained that the Gemara and authorities nevertheless use the formulation "his lost object" because even if the other person offers to pay the value of what he would lose, the owner can say regarding his own utensil or garment, "I prefer to save my own article itself." This does not apply to work that he performs only to earn wages.
And he should not be overly exacting and say, "Mine comes first." Meaning, in every case involving rescue of another person's property a person can find some basis to excuse himself, saying, "I should first attend to my own affairs lest I lose a debt, or lest this return cause me to lose some profitable opportunity." Therefore the Sages say he should not do this unless the loss to himself is clear and demonstrable.
And in the end he will need the support of others. In the Perishah I wrote that of such a person it is said, "The fear of the wicked will come upon him." He casts off the yoke of the mitzvah because he fears that fulfilling it may cause financial loss, and in the end he suffers loss. This also explains the popular expression that excessive exactness leads to poverty: one who is exacting about every possible personal financial concern will ultimately become poor.
