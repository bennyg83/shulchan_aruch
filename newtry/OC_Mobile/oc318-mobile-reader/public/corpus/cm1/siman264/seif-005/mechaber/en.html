If two travelers, one carrying wine and the other honey, and the honey jar cracks, and before the honey spills the wine owner empties his wine and saves the honey in his barrel, he receives only the appropriate wage. If he stipulated, 'I will save yours and you will pay me the value of mine,' or made that condition before beit din, the honey owner must pay. The wine owner must empty his wine to save the honey when the owner agrees to reimburse him, though some disagree. If the honey has already spilled onto the ground, it is ownerless and whoever rescues it does so for himself. Rama: Some say that even before it spills, if the jar is so broken that it would certainly spill without rescue, it is already ownerless. The same applies in comparable cases, such as a city fire from which Jews fled in fear; what another rescues is his, as one acquiring ownerless property.
