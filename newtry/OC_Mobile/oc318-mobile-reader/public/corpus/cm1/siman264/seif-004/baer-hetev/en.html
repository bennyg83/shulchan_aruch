His donkey. Meaning, the same law applies to the other person's donkey. Even if he stipulated that the other person would pay him the value of his donkey, descended to rescue the other's donkey, his own donkey was lost, and the other's donkey came up by itself, he receives only his ordinary wage, like any other person. If he did not stipulate beforehand with the other person or with beit din, he receives not even a wage, despite suffering a loss through his efforts, just as in the case where he descended to rescue and failed to rescue. So writes Maharam Padua in responsum 63. Nevertheless, it appears that in these laws as well, where one descended to rescue and failed or the property came up by itself, if the owners were not present, his failure to stipulate is treated as though he had stipulated with them, and they must at least pay him the ordinary wage of another person. In my responsum I discussed at length the proof Maharam brought from the Gemara for ruling that even where he stipulated and the property came up by itself, he receives only his wage. In my view, that proof is not conclusive, and it appears that where the property came up by itself after the stipulation and he suffered a loss through his rescue effort, the other party must pay the full value of his donkey. Thus writes the Sema. The Shach writes that Maharshal likewise ruled in siman 40 that where he stipulated, even if the property came up by itself, the other party must pay him the full amount stipulated. See there.
Who were captured. If one person was captured and another redeemed him with money, the captive must reimburse him, but only up to his own value. If he was captured in a matter involving danger to life, he must reimburse him even beyond his value. Likewise, if a person fled and the official seized his sacred books, and another person redeemed them, he must reimburse the entire amount paid because of the disgrace to the sacred books. All this is demonstrated with proofs in Yam Shel Shlomo, Bava Kamma, chapter 6, siman 15. See there. Shach.
That the truth. The Darchei Moshe challenges Maharam Padua from the Mordechai at the end of HaGozel concerning two partners in one pledge. An official belonging to one partner seized the pledge that was in his possession, and he incurred expenses until he recovered it. The second partner refused to contribute to the expenses, claiming that his own official was more powerful than the first partner's official and could have recovered it from him without charge. The Mordechai rules that the second partner need pay only the ordinary wage, implying that at least that wage is owed. The Darchei Moshe answers that there it remained possible that his official could have recovered it, but he had not yet done so, unlike this case where it had already emerged by itself. This is the meaning of the Rama's words, 'If it emerged only through his efforts... even though the truth is with him...' based on this difficulty. Sema.
