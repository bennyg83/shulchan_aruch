This applies specifically, etc. It is comparable to one who surrounds his fellow's field on three sides. Although he caused the other party an additional enclosure expense, he is not automatically charged for the full benefit. See Bava Kamma 20b and Rashi there.
But if he saved, etc. This is comparable to one who fenced all four sides, where the payment follows the quality of the fence, even if the surrounding owner built it. It is also like an unpaid custodian who hired shepherds and staffs in order to save the deposit.
“And if not.” Same as:
Although, etc., nevertheless, etc. As with the surrounding fence, even though the beneficiary could claim that minimal protection would have sufficed, since he in fact benefited from a stone wall he pays according to the wall that was built.
And if it appears, etc., therefore, etc. This follows the challenge raised in the first chapter there from the Mishna concerning one who surrounds his fellow's field.
It appears to me, etc. This is comparable to one who owns a house in another courtyard or a courtyard in another city, as taught in the Tosefta cited by Rif and Rosh in the first chapter of Bava Batra.
And likewise anyone, etc. The Yerushalmi relates that one who lent money and lodged in the borrower's house could be charged fair rent. Only one who merely pays another's debt is exempt, because he is viewed as merely driving away a lion from the other's property. The same applies to supporting another man's wife. By contrast, one who enters another's property and improves it, surrounds his field, or hires laborers and directs them to work on another's property is entitled to compensation for the benefit conferred. See 226.
(Likkut) Likewise, anyone who acts, etc. This is like one who descends into another's property without permission, one who surrounds his fellow's field, etc., and one who hires a worker and shows him another person's property, etc. Tosafos, Bava Kamma, daf 58a, end of s.v. "alternatively." And where he improves, etc. End.
