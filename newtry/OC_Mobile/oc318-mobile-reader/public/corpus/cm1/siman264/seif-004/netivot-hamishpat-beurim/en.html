Since he did not stipulate. Although the earlier clause says that if the property came up by itself the owner is exempt even where the rescuer stipulated, the lack of stipulation is still relevant here. Had he stipulated, the owner would at least have to pay the ordinary amount he would have been willing to pay someone else for the effort. Without stipulation, he can be entirely exempt, like the earlier case. But if the property could not have been recovered except through this person's intervention, the owner must reimburse all necessary expenses even without prior stipulation. Rama's statement that he need pay only according to the benefit received applies only where the claimant's underlying account is true, as shown by Maharam Padua, responsum 63, which required full reimbursement even without stipulation. This proves that Sema's first explanation, that absence of stipulation itself shows waiver, is not the primary reason. Ran at the beginning of Shenei Dayanei likewise says that one who gives another benefit without specification generally does so with expectation of payment. The main rationale is Sema's second reason, matching Tosafot: the owner can say, "Had you not acted, I would have hired someone else more cheaply." Where no other rescue was realistically available, that answer disappears and full necessary expenses are owed.
