If one undertook a rescue but failed, he receives only the appropriate wage. Rama: This is only when he stipulated with the owner. If he made no stipulation and failed, he receives nothing because he conferred no benefit; the same applies if the owner's donkey emerged by itself. If he actually rescued it, the owner cannot claim that it would have emerged without him, and must pay his wage. Similarly, if two people were imprisoned and one incurred expenses, the other owes nothing if released without his fellow's efforts and no stipulation was made. If he was released only through the other's efforts, he must pay according to the benefit assessed by beit din, even if he truthfully claims that he had sufficient influence and did not need those efforts. If beit din finds that no additional expense was incurred for him, because the rescuer would have borne all those expenses for himself alone, he owes nothing under the rule that one benefited while the other suffered no loss. Thus, one who rescued his own books together with another's need not be reimbursed if rescuing the other's books caused no additional expense. This applies when he initially acted to save his own and incidentally saved the other's. If he acted on behalf of both, the other must pay for the benefit received. In general, one who performs work or a benefit for another is entitled to compensation; the recipient cannot say, 'You acted gratuitously because I did not instruct you.'
