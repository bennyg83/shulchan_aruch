Some say that one should not administer an oath to a pregnant woman, since she is removed, etc. Rama’s use of “since” implies that he regards it as obvious that a pregnant woman is removed from the synagogue when a general ban is pronounced, even if the ban is not directed at her. I have not found this stated anywhere, and the wording of Binyamin Ze’ev is different. He writes that one should pronounce a general ban in the synagogue, but if the woman is pregnant one should wait until she gives birth, because the common practice is to avoid administering an oath to a pregnant woman. Even where the procedure is merely to pronounce a general ban in the synagogue, one should not do so until she gives birth, because the ban is intended against her and there is reason for concern. Thus, Binyamin Ze’ev derives the law of a ban from the practice concerning an oath, whereas Rama here derives the law of an oath from the law of a ban. Binyamin Ze’ev also implies that one refrains from pronouncing the ban only when it is directed at her, and then it should not be pronounced for her even if she is not physically present in the synagogue. Both points differ from the implication of Rama’s wording here.
