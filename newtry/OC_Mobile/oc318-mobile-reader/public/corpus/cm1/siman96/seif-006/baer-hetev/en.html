Or. The Sema writes that she is not compelled to sell her ketubah for its present market value, because with respect to her husband she will certainly waive it. One who sells a promissory note to another and then waives the debt has effected a valid waiver, as stated in siman 66. See also siman 424. Nor is there presently a debt of the husband that can be reached under Rabbi Nathan's rule, since the time for payment of the ketubah has not yet arrived. In siman 86, seif 5, the Shach disputes this. See there.
Conducts business. See the Shach, who extensively disputes this ruling and concludes as follows: The primary ruling appears to be as Maharshal ruled, that the husband must pay on her behalf according to the extent to which she conducts business within the household. Nowadays, for modest sums, husbands are obligated to pay on behalf of their wives when the wife is legally liable, because of the enactment made for the stability of commerce. Everything depends on the judge's assessment. At times there are capable women who support their husbands and all the husband's money is in her hands. It is then obvious that even for a large sum the husband must pay everything, according to the circumstances, local custom, and the judge's sound discretion. Responsa Maharam Alshich, siman 19, likewise rules that if women customarily transact in large sums, their acts are binding even for large amounts. This applies, however, to a loan or deposit from which they derived benefit. If she was negligent with a deposit, threw it into a fire, caused bodily injury, or the like, the husband is exempt. See Responsa Maharam Mintz, siman 17.
They remove her. This wording implies that Rama holds it obvious that a woman is removed from the synagogue when a ban is proclaimed, even where the one imposing the ban does not specifically intend it against her. I have not found this stated explicitly anywhere. Moreover, Binyamin Ze'ev writes: If the woman is pregnant, one waits until she gives birth, for people are careful not to administer an oath to a pregnant woman, and even a general ban should not be proclaimed until she gives birth, since the ban is intended against her. Thus, it is specifically when the ban is being imposed because of her that even if she is not in the synagogue no ban, even a general one, is proclaimed on her account. This is contrary to the implication of Rama's words here. Sema.
