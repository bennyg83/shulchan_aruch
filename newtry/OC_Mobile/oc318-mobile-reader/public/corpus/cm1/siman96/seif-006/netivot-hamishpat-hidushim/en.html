And not like those who disagree. The Shach concludes that the primary law follows those dissenters, and this is common practice regarding a wife who conducts business, where the husband is liable. This applies even where there are no witnesses and he merely admits the matter or cannot swear. Everything depends upon the scope of the business she ordinarily conducts. If she does not conduct major transactions, her major transactions have no effect. Specifically where the husband derives benefit, such as where she borrowed money or bought merchandise on credit, even if the merchandise was later lost, he is liable. But if she was negligent with a deposit, the husband is not liable. Likewise, if it is known that she borrowed secretly from her husband, he is not liable. If the husband dies and others seek to collect from the orphans on the basis of writings the wife gave, they must prove that those writings reached their hands during the husband's lifetime. Otherwise there is concern of collusion.
