The deaf mute, etc. See Tumim regarding a mute person who is fully competent but cannot speak. If he conducted business and became obligated in an oath, it does not suffice for him to write, 'I swear under oath that my statement is true and that I do not owe so and so.' Rather, the opposing party says to him, 'I place you under oath that if you possess anything belonging to so and so, you must give it to him, or write in your own hand that you owe it,' and the mute person then writes that he possesses nothing. This constitutes an oath according to all opinions. See there also concerning a mute person who undertook an obligation upon a condition and accepted that condition under oath. There is room to rule stringently that the oath takes effect. But absent such a condition, if he merely accepts an oath upon himself in writing, it is ineffective because Scripture requires expression with the lips. With a healthy person who can speak but writes the oath instead, there is greater room for stringency. See there.
