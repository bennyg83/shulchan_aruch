(In the Tur at the end of this siman, and they ask her how she gave the identifying sign, etc.) Bedek HaBayit adds to this responsum of a Gaon: “Although one should not ordinarily hear litigation involving a woman in private, this case is different because of the great public need.” The Arukh, entry hesset, writes that when a woman must swear concerning money, the oath is administered in a manner consistent with her dignity and she is not treated like a sotah. It appears that his intent is only that she not be humiliated as a sotah. Her legal status is nevertheless no less than that of a man. In the chapter HaKotev, a woman became obligated to swear in Rava’s court, and the opposing party said, “Let her come and swear in the city so that she will be embarrassed,” etc.
