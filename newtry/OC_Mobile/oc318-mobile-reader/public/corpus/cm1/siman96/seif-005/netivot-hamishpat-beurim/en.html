A deaf person. A mute person is treated like a competent person for all legal purposes. See Tumim, who discusses at length whether writing, "I swear," constitutes an oath. It does not, because the Torah states that an oath is expressed with the lips. The exceptions are the oaths concerning testimony and a deposit, where a person can become liable when another imposes the oath and he responds with denial. Tumim therefore writes that in those categories a mute person can incur liability through another's imposition of the oath and his written denial. Ketzot HaChoshen discusses this at length, but his words are difficult in my eyes.
