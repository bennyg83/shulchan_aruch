Seif 6. (We compel her. See siman 15, seif 5 in the gloss.) If a woman refuses to accept the Torah law of inheritance of her paternal brothers, under which a son inherits and a daughter does not, and she seeks to obtain an inheritance through the secular courts, while her husband asks what measures may be taken because she refuses to leave the house, one may deny her access to mikvah even though this causes her distress. Her husband may also be prevented from providing her food and other needs. If she independently conducts business and therefore does not depend financially on him, others may be instructed not to conduct business with her and she may be excluded from communal prayer. As for preventing her children from attending a teacher, it appears that this should not be done, because the father's obligation to teach his son is independent and one should not cause the child a loss on account of the mother's misconduct. If the father himself is capable of teaching the child, however, perhaps attendance with the teacher may be withheld, especially if the resulting loss of business time to the father may induce the woman to submit to Torah law. It is also proper to prevent her husband from residing with her there, provided doing so will not create a risk of prohibited seclusion or other impropriety, until she complies fully with the halachah.
