Some say that one does not administer an oath. The Ran writes in the chapter Shevuat HaDayanim in the name of Rashba that the custodian is not liable even when negligence is certain. Once the Torah exempted him from the law of custodians, it exempted him even from liability for negligence. Rashba proves this from the end of the chapter HaChovel, concerning a purse with which a man was negligent. The Gemara invokes the exposition, “to guard, and not to distribute,” showing that whenever Scripture excludes an item from the law of custodians, the custodian is exempt even for negligence. According to what I wrote above in siman 66, seif katan 122, that passage is not a proof. It therefore appears that if negligence toward a minor's property is known or admitted, the custodian is liable. This is also implied by the authorities. Cited cross-references in the source: 60.
Even though the claim was brought after he became an adult, provided that the delivery occurred while he was a minor. It appears that the Rama deliberately stated the case in this direction and not the reverse. The Mechaber quoted Rambam's wording, “but if he guarded for a minor,” which implies delivery during minority, and might suggest that this suffices. The Rama therefore teaches that it does not suffice. Conversely, if the delivery occurred after the claimant became an adult, an oath is administered even if the claim was brought while he was still a minor. This follows the Ramah cited by the Tur in seif 10. The Tur presents his dissenting view last, implying acceptance, and the reasoning is persuasive. Raavad likewise holds this view. The Sema writes otherwise, but my conclusion appears correct.
