Which oath is taken in a claim by a deaf-mute, a mentally incompetent person, or a minor, and the law of a married woman. It contains 6 se'ifim.
No oath is taken in response to the claim of a deaf-mute, a mentally incompetent person, or a minor, whether the minor asserts his own claim or his father's claim. An admission of part to a minor is regarded only as returning a lost item. Similarly, if the defendant denies the entire claim and one witness testifies for the minor, he does not swear, because there is only one witness and no proper claimant, since a minor's claim is not a complete claim. {Rama: Some say he must swear on the basis of the witness.} Thus, if a minor tells an adult, 'You have one hundred of mine,' or, 'My father had one hundred in your possession,' and the adult says, 'I have only fifty of yours,' or says, 'I have nothing of yours,' while one witness testifies that he does, the adult is exempt from a Torah oath. But if he guarded property for a minor and claims that it was lost, he takes a custodian's oath, because this oath does not arise from a claim. {Rama: Some say a Torah-level custodian's oath is not taken for a minor. This applies even if he brings the claim after reaching adulthood, provided the property was given to the custodian while he was a minor. This is the primary view.} Similarly, if a person admits that he was the minor's partner or guardian, beit din appoints a guardian for the minor, and the partner and others in comparable positions take the oath imposed upon an uncertain claim.
