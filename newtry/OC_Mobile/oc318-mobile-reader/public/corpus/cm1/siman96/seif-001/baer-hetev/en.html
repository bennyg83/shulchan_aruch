Deaf mute. The Sema explains that this refers to a Torah oath, as stated in seif 2 regarding a minor. A deaf mute and an incompetent person are comparable because they lack legal capacity. See Responsa Mahari HaLevi 97.
Lost object. Although siman 75 and many other places explain that a defendant is generally not believed through a migo that he could have denied the entire claim, because a person is reluctant to deny another directly, that applies to an adult claimant. Against a minor, and especially a minor advancing his father's claim, the defendant could more readily deny everything. This is explicit in the Gemara. Sema.
Claims. Maharshal rules likewise in Bava Kamma, chapter 9, siman 48. See also Responsa Ri MiBeit Levi 45. Shach. The Taz writes that although in siman 75 the Mechaber rules that a person may be sworn on an uncertain claim supported by one witness, the Beit Yosef explains that a minor's claim is weaker. The Taz questions this, because the partners' oath may be imposed on an uncertain claim, and guardians are appointed for minors. A minor's uncertain claim should not be weaker than an adult's uncertain claim. See there.
Guarded. The reason is that even with an adult, a bailee's defense is not a definite claim. The bailee swears on an uncertain matter that the item was lost without negligence. Therefore there is no difference between a minor and an adult. The verse "if a man shall give" in the section of bailees excludes only a definite claim and a case with one witness. Sema.
Bailees. The Ran writes in the name of the Rashba that the bailee is exempt even if it is certainly known that he was negligent, because once the Torah exempted him it exempted him even from liability for negligence. He cites proof from the end of the chapter HaChovel regarding a purse that a certain man negligently guarded. The Shach writes that according to what he explained in siman 66, seif 39, there is no proof from there. He therefore concludes that if it is known or admitted that the bailee was negligent with a minor's property, he is liable. This is also implied by the authorities. See Responsa Radbaz I, 230.
Who claimed from him. The Sema writes that the same applies in reverse, if the claim was made while the claimant was a minor but the property had been given while he was an adult, such as where the minor's father had deposited it, as stated above regarding other claims. The Maggid Mishneh writes likewise. The Shach, however, holds that if the property was given while the claimant was an adult, an oath may be imposed even though the claim is now made during minority, following the Ramah cited by the Tur. Since the Tur cites that dissenting opinion last, it appears to be his ruling, and the reasoning is persuasive. The Raavad agrees.
And he shall swear. The Mechaber follows his ruling in siman 93, seif 2, that a migo does not exempt one from an oath. The Tur, however, writes here that the defendant is exempt if there are no witnesses that he had been a partner, because the Tur follows the Rosh, who disagrees with this principle, as explained at the beginning of siman 96. Sema.
