One does not take an oath on the claim of a deaf mute, mentally incompetent person, or minor, etc. This means a Torah oath, as explained below in seif 2. A deaf mute and a mentally incompetent person are treated like a minor because they lack legal competence. “Deaf mute” here means one who neither hears nor speaks, whose claim is communicated by gestures. A minor is excluded by the verse, “If a man gives,” which the Sages expound as “a man, and not a minor.” The verse also says, “the matter of both of them shall come before the judges,” equating the litigation with the original delivery, so that adulthood is required at both stages. Thus, where the property was delivered while the claimant was a minor and he later grew up and sued, the original delivery was deficient for purposes of a Torah oath. Conversely, where the original delivery was made by an adult, his adult heir may litigate upon it, but a minor who merely comes with his father’s claim is excluded. The Tur understands Rabbeinu Yitzchak in this manner, unlike Rashi, who holds that one does take an oath in response to a minor who comes with his father’s claim. See the Beit Yosef and the Perishah.
