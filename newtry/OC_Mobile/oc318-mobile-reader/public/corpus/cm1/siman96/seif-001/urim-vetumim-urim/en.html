On the claim of a deaf mute, mentally incompetent person, or minor, etc. A deaf mute and mentally incompetent person are obviously excluded because they lack legal capacity. The deaf mute meant throughout halachah is one who neither hears nor speaks. Concerning a minor, both the original delivery and the claim must occur after majority, because Scripture says, 'If a man gives.' Therefore, if the property was delivered while the claimant was a minor, no Torah oath arises. Likewise, if his father delivered it but the child makes the claim while still a minor, there is no legally effective claim. Most authorities conclude this way, unlike Rashi, who holds that if the delivery occurred during majority, a Torah oath applies even though the claim is made during minority. See Sema.
Like one who returns a lost object. Ordinarily, one who admits part of a claim is not treated like a voluntary returner, because a person is reluctant to deny everything outright. With a minor, especially where the minor's claim is based on what his father told him, the defendant is not similarly intimidated and would readily deny everything. His partial admission is therefore like voluntarily returning a lost object. See Sema.
A lost object. If the father delivered the property and died, and the son, after reaching majority, claims that he saw his father lend it, even if he witnessed this while still a minor, it appears that the claim generates a Torah oath. See Tumim. If, however, the son first sued while still a minor and the defendant admitted part of the claim, and the son then renewed the claim after reaching majority, one can argue that the later claim is ineffective because the admission preceded a legally effective claim. See Tumim.
And some say that he must swear. This appears to be the primary practical ruling. Ri HaLevi, who holds that no oath is required, follows his own view that a claim based on a witness is not considered a definite claim. According to our ruling that it is a definite claim, this is stronger than a custodial oath. Beit Yosef nevertheless ruled otherwise because Rambam also writes like Ri HaLevi, while Beit Yosef understood Rambam in Hilchos Gezeilah chapter 7 to hold that one may make a claim on the basis of a witness, requiring a distinction between the cases. In truth, however, the words of Rambam are not decisive, for he may hold that one cannot make such a claim on the basis of a witness. The two issues therefore depend upon one another. Taz and Knesset HaGedolah write similarly.
But if he guarded property for a minor. A custodial oath does not arise from the claimant's allegation. The act of accepting the deposit itself creates the oath obligation. Therefore there is no difference whether the owner was an adult or a minor. The exposition of 'If a man gives,' excluding a minor, is interpreted in the chapter HaShutafin in a different context and does not apply to custodians at all.
And some say that one does not swear, etc. According to this view, the custodian is likewise exempt for negligence, because we rule above at the end of siman 66 that where the laws of custodians do not apply, one is exempt even for negligence. According to Rambam, who disagrees there and imposes liability for negligence, one who accepts property from a minor for safekeeping is liable for negligence.
Only where the delivery occurred while he was a minor. If the father delivered the property for safekeeping, so that the delivery occurred while the owner was an adult, but the claim is now made by a minor orphan, Sema holds that the orphan cannot impose the oath. Shach disagrees, and the matter is discussed in Tumim.
If he admitted that he was a partner. This follows the Mechaber, who holds that a migo does not exempt from an oath. According to Tur and Rosh, however, no oath is required, because he has a migo that he was never a partner. Even according to what was written above in siman 92 that such a migo can involve brazenness, there is no comparable brazenness toward a minor. See Tumim. I wrote that even according to the Mechaber it requires study whether, if he admits that partnership money belonging to the minor remains in his possession, he must swear. See there.
Beit din should appoint a guardian. See Tumim. I wrote that the guardian is needed so that we can determine whether there is a suspicion amounting to at least two silver ma'ah. See there.
