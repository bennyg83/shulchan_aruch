In the Shulchan Aruch, seif 1: and one witness came. Although in siman 75 the Mechaber rules that one takes an oath on an uncertain claim when there is one witness, the Beit Yosef answers that a minor's claim is weaker here. This is surprising. In the oath of partners, one swears on an uncertain claim, and guardians are appointed for minors, as stated nearby. A minor's uncertain claim should be no weaker than an adult's. According to our master's view, the matter is correct. Rambam holds that with one witness and an uncertain claim there is no Torah oath. The Maggid Mishneh explicitly writes that Rambam follows his teacher, Rabbi Yosef Halevi, whom our master cites in siman 75 as holding this view. The Beit Yosef disagrees with our master and says that chapter 4 of Hilchot Gezelah indicates otherwise, but his words are very difficult. On the contrary, in law 13 there, the Raavad asks regarding Rambam's case where one witness testifies that the defendant stole and the owner says that the utensil in his possession is stolen: should he not be one who is obligated to swear but cannot swear, given that there is no definite claim? The answer is that since the utensil is seen in his possession, this is treated as a definite claim. Rambam holds likewise, as is implied by the Maggid Mishneh there.
