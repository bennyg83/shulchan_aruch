(There, when she is pregnant.) Regarding what is written at the end of siman 96, that a pregnant woman is not made to swear, Urim VeTumim writes there that this does not apply to an oath imposed by Torah law. Perhaps the same is true of a shevuat heset that arises from a concrete monetary basis, in accordance with one formulation in the Gemara concerning shevuat heset. Perhaps, too, where the claimant comes from a distant place and delaying the oath would cause a loss or would prevent the oath entirely, this leniency does not apply. The rule that a pregnant woman is not made to swear applies only when her pregnancy is visibly recognizable, not merely because she has not menstruated, since errors in such assessments are common. If the pregnant woman herself wishes to take money by means of an oath, it appears that the choice is hers to swear and collect.
