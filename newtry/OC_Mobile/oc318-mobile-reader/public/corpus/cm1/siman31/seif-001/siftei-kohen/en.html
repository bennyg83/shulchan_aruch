Two sets of witnesses contradict one another, and one witness from this set and one from the other set come together. This is the ruling of all the authorities. It requires study, however, what the law is where two individual witnesses contradict one another, for one of them is certainly lying. Are they likewise disqualified from joining together in a different testimony, or not? In siman 30, the Tur and all the authorities discuss two witnesses who contradict one another only with respect to that testimony. Regarding another testimony, they mention disqualification only in the case of two sets of witnesses. This implies that two individual witnesses who contradict one another are not disqualified from another testimony, because neither one has been contradicted by two witnesses. This requires study. So wrote one scholar, who related that this question gave rise to a major dispute in the holy community of Brisk after such an incident occurred there, and the scholars left it unresolved. It appears to me, however, from the Ran in the chapter HaIshah Shenitarmelah, that two individual witnesses are also disqualified from another testimony. The Gemara there states: one witness says that she was divorced and one says that she was not divorced. Both testify that she was a married woman, while the one who says she was divorced is only one witness, whose word does not stand against two. The Ran explains that this formulation shows that her status as a married woman rests only on these witnesses. He asks how that status can be established through them, since they contradict one another and one must be disqualified, just as two contradictory sets may each testify separately but one witness from each set may not join. He answers that both agree she was divorced and dispute only whether the document was nearer to him or to her, a matter in which witnesses may err. It follows that just as contradictory sets are disqualified from joining in another testimony, so too are two individual witnesses. This is also evident from the Ritva and Ramban cited below, siman 46, seif 37, seif katan 107. See also Tosafot, Rosh Hashanah 24a.
