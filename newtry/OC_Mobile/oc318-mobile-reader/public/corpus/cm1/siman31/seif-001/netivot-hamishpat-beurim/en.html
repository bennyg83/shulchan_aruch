For certainly one of them lied. Rabbeinu Yerucham says that if two groups contradict each other only regarding part of a composite testimony, the uncontested portion can remain valid. For example, both groups may agree that the land belonged to the claimant's ancestors but disagree regarding later years of possession. Even though one group must be false as to the later possession, that does not retroactively invalidate the earlier uncontested ancestry testimony. At the time they testified about ancestry they were still presumptively qualified, and the later contradiction concerns another portion of the case. This differs from a single unified testimony in which a disqualified member was already part of the testimonial unit from the outset. Here, the later-invalidated component does not necessarily destroy the earlier valid component.
And he shall swear regarding the remainder. Urim VeTumim is uncertain whether witnesses from one of two contradicting groups can later disqualify the opposing group from taking an oath. In my view the same principle that prevents their later testimony from extracting money prevents them from removing another person from his presumption of fitness. But where one group has not yet been contradicted on the new matter, that group is still presumptively qualified and can testify regarding a third person's disqualification. A presumption of ownership is stronger than a presumption of personal fitness for this purpose; if such witnesses can sometimes extract money in a later independent case, all the more they can establish disqualification.
