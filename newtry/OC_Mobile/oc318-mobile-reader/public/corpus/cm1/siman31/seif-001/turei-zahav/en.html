Shulchan Aruch, seif 2: “He shall swear concerning the remainder while holding a sacred object,” etc. Since he must in any event pay one of the documents, the admission of his mouth should not be stronger than the testimony of witnesses. Raavad objects that no Torah oath is imposed concerning a lien on land, and a document creates such a lien. Kesef Mishneh answers that because this document cannot collect from purchasers it is not treated as a land lien. Raavad further objects that even an express admission is not partial admission where denial was impossible, for a partial admission creates an oath only when the defendant could have denied. Kesef Mishneh leaves this unresolved. Yet Beit Yosef at the end of siman 88 cites Maggid Mishneh that Rambam applies that limitation only to a document encumbering all property. Here this document creates no such lien, so the difficulty disappears.
