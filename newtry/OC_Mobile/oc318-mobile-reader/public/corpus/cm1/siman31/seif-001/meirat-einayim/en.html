They contradict one another. The Tur explains: for example, one set says that so-and-so borrowed one maneh from another on a certain day, and another set says he did not borrow. Both testimonies are void. This applies where the second pair says the lender or borrower was with them the entire day and they know no loan occurred. If instead the second pair says to the first, "You were with us," that is hazamah, and we follow the later pair while the first is disqualified, as explained in siman 38. If the contradiction concerns only details, such as one pair saying the loan was one maneh and the other saying two hundred, or one says a black maneh and the other a white maneh, even if they refer to the same day, the contradiction is only in secondary examinations. Since they are two independent pairs and each pair stands without the other, the claimant need not claim both formulations. He collects the smaller amount, because the witnesses can err in such details. The stricter requirement that the claimant claim both applies where one pair itself contains the conflicting witnesses and neither witness can establish anything without the other.
