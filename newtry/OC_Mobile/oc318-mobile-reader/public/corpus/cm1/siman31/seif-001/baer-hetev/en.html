False. The rule that the testimony of both groups is void applies where the second group says, "He did not borrow, because the lender or borrower was with us that entire day." But if they say to the first witnesses, "You yourselves were with us," that is hazamah, and the later group is believed rather than the first, as explained in siman 38. If the contradiction concerns the amount, one group saying one hundred and the other two hundred, or black coins versus white coins, even if both speak of the same day, then since the contradiction concerns checking details in which people commonly err and there are two independent groups, neither of which needs the other's testimony, the claimant collects the lesser amount, even if he claimed only one hundred. It is specifically where a single pair of witnesses consists of one saying one hundred and the other two hundred, so that neither witness's testimony suffices without the other, that the claimant must sue in accordance with both. Where the testimony is wholly invalid, the defendant need not take a Torah oath against it, but he must still take a hesset oath as on an unsupported claim. Thus concludes the Sema. The Shach writes that the same is true of two individual witnesses who contradict one another: they are disqualified from other testimony as well. He brings proof from Ran, Ritva, and Ramban.
