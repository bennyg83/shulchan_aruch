(Netivot HaMishpat, seif katan 1) At the end of the passage, and also the Shach's question, etc.; he does not testify that he saw, etc. Note: The Rambam, Hilchot Gerushin 12:5, states: "and one witness came and said, she had been a married woman and was divorced." The Ran likewise speaks of the migo that, had the witness wished, he could have remained silent initially and not testified that she had been married. It therefore appears that he interprets the case like the Rambam, namely, that the witness says both that she had been married and that she was divorced. Independently, it is difficult to say that because we suspect the witness of speaking on the basis of an assumption regarding the divorce, we should disregard the information contained in his words that she had been married. It also requires study why the Ran did not ask more simply how the witness who says she was divorced can combine with the other witness to establish that she had been married. On either possibility, his testimony should not prohibit her: if his testimony of divorce is true, she is divorced; if it is false, he is a false witness and should not be believed concerning the same testimony that she had been married, since all of his statement was within toch kedei dibbur. If it had been later than that, the Ran could not have argued that he has a migo because he could initially have remained silent. The same question applies to the Ran's case where the witnesses' signatures are not independently authenticated and two witnesses identify their signatures, but one adds that the debt was paid. Several authorities do not accept his statement that it was paid. Why should we not say on either possibility that, if he lies about payment, he should likewise not be believed in authenticating his signature?
