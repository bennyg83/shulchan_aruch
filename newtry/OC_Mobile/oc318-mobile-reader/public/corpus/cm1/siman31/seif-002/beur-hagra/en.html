And he shall swear concerning. He follows the approach of his teacher, Ri Migash, that collection is only from free assets, and it has the status of an oral loan regarding the oath, as stated in Bava Batra 128b concerning one who produces a promissory note, etc., and as written above in siman 88, seif 29. See Baal HaTerumot, sha'ar 29, chelek 3, "and we must clarify, etc.," where Ri Migash writes, etc.; this refers to both cases.
(Collected note) And he shall swear, etc. Because he does not collect from encumbered property, as appears from Baal HaTerumot. This requires study, however, because the Rambam and Shulchan Aruch mention it only in seif 3. It therefore appears that Ri Migash wrote this only regarding seif 3; see Shach. Nor is this considered heilach according to the opinion in siman 75, seif 4, and certainly according to what was written above, since he cannot collect from encumbered property, it is not heilach, as Rashi writes at the beginning of Bava Metzia and the Ritva there. It is also unlike the rule in siman 87, seif 2; see there. (End of collected note.)
And if he produced. Ketubot 44a; see Tosafot there, s.v. "Mishum."
(Collected note) And if afterward he produced, etc. This implies even before the same beit din. On this the Rav writes, "and some say, etc." The Rambam's reason is that we rule like the opinion in chapter 4 of Yevamot that once judgment has been established, it stands. (End of collected note.)
And even if it is written. Baal HaTerumot, like the law of a shopkeeper and workers, where each one alone collects without an oath. See below, siman 91, seif 2.
