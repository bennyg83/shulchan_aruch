If a lender holds against one borrower two promissory notes, one for one hundred and one for two hundred, and the borrower denies both notes, with one set of witnesses signed on one note and a different set signed on the other, then if the lender produces both at once he collects only one hundred. He must take an oath while holding a sacred object regarding the remainder, and the note for two hundred is taken from him and torn. If he produces only one note, he collects it. If he later produces the second note, he collects that as well. Some say it is advisable for him to burn the smaller note so that the borrower will not be able to make him swear that he has two notes bearing the signatures of these sets of witnesses. If two lenders each produce a note against the same borrower, one signed by one of these sets and the other by the other set, and the borrower denies both, each lender swears and collects. Even if the notes contain a clause of trustworthiness, they must swear.
