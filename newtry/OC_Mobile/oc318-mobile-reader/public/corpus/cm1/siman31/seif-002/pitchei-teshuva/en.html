There is no testimony here. The Beit Yosef cites Rabbeinu Yerucham in Meisharim that if two witnesses contradict one another only regarding part of their testimony, while on another part they do not contradict one another, the uncontested portion remains established. Responsa Beit Yaakov, siman 98, asserts that the Ran in the second chapter of Ketubot holds otherwise, but says Rabbeinu Yerucham's view is more reasonable. Shaar Mishpat rightly questions this, since the words of the Ran there are unrelated to Rabbeinu Yerucham's rule. Divrei Emet, kuntres 8, end of siman 3, makes the same objection. Knesset HaGedolah, Hagahot Beit Yosef, letter 1, notes that Rabbeinu Yerucham's rule is an explicit Gemara in Chezkas HaBatim according to Rav Nachman, whose ruling we follow: where witnesses were contradicted, they are contradicted only regarding that point. Maharshal in his glosses to the Taz writes similarly. See Tumim, seif katan 2.
