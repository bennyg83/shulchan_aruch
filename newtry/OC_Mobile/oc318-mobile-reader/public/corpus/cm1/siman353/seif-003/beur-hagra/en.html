(Supplement) And some say that he need not, etc. Tur cites Ri that after despair and a change of possession the purchaser acquires completely and owes neither the object nor its value. Rambam, by contrast, still obligates the purchaser in the object's value, allowing deduction of the purchase price only because of the market enactment. Therefore, when the thief is notorious, the purchaser receives no deduction. Several readings in Tur and Raavad are corrupt. The correct reading is that the stolen object need not be returned except through payment of its value. Rashba explains that after despair the object is fully acquired under the latter version of the Gemara. This also resolves the cases in Sukkah and Bava Batra. Ri and Rashba's position is primary. The formulations attributed to Rambam and the gloss in siman 356 are extremely difficult, especially regarding heirs, to whom the market enactment does not apply. See simanim 50, 60, 356, 357, and 368. (End of supplement.)
