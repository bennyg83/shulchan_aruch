(Netivot HaMishpat, seif katan 2) When the victim later learns. Note: This requires study. One can say that even in ordinary theft the owner's presumed ye'ush applies only where he learned that it was stolen before the thief transferred it. But if he learns only after it has already been given to another person, why should he be presumed to despair if he has witnesses that the object belongs to him and can recover it from the person who now holds it? The Gemara's question is therefore proper.
