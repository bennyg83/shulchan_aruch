Note: Yam Shel Shlomo, Bava Kamma chapter 7, writes that if the thief deposited the stolen item with another person, the owners then despaired of recovering it, and afterward the thief sold it to that depositary, the depositary does not acquire it because the earlier deposit already constituted a change of possession before despair. Maginei Shlomo, in his novellae at the end of Kiddushin, disputes this and holds that a deposit is not called a change of possession, since the item remains in the legal domain of its owner. When the thief later transfers ownership to the depositary, that is a change of possession after despair. See there.
