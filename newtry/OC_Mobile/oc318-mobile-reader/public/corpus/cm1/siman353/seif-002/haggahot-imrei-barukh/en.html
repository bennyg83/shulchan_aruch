(Shach, seif katan 4) Unless it is known that the owner despaired beforehand. Note: See Even HaEzer 28, Chelkat Mechokek seif katan 1, and Shitah Mekubetzet to Bava Metzia 22b, passage beginning "the Raavad wrote." Know that the Shach's proof from the Gemara's phrase, "but did the thief see him so as to despair," can be rejected. One can say that transfer of domain is effective even if it precedes ye'ush, and nevertheless the Gemara's question is sound. The baraita states generally, "he took from this one and gave to that one, what he gave is given." But if the owner learned only after the object reached the recipient that his stolen property had come into that person's possession, he would not despair at all. Even though ordinary theft is presumed to generate ye'ush, that is only where the owner does not know who stole it and it is no longer under his control. Here, however, he can sue the recipient. Why should the baraita assume a case where the owner first learned of the theft and only afterward learned that the stolen object was in another's hand?
