A change of possession without prior despair does not acquire according to any view. Rosh and Tur nevertheless rule stringently like Rabbah, and Rama rules likewise in Even HaEzer 28. See 28 and 968.
And if there is, etc. Tosafot in Sukkah and Bava Kamma teach that a weak change of name may join with despair, but without despair it does not acquire. Rosh, however, speaks of despair together with an inferior change of name. Tosafot offer two approaches: either only a substantial and irreversible change of name acquires, or even an inferior change of name acquires without despair. Under both approaches a reversible change of name does not acquire. Tur's formulation follows Tosafot in Sukkah, not the passage cited by Beit Yosef. (End of supplement.)
(Likkut) And if there is, etc. What is stated there on daf 67a, that he does not acquire, refers to a case without despair, as Tosafos write there at the end of s.v. "this." See also their further answer, etc. Without despair he certainly does not acquire, as stated there. R' Yitzchak agrees that even rabbinically he does not acquire, for the acquisition discussed there is itself only rabbinic, as Tosafos in Sukkah write. This accords with R' Yitzchak's view that a reversible physical change effects acquisition rabbinically, whereas regarding a reversible change of name he writes that it does not acquire at all. See there. End.
(Likkut) And if there is, etc. So writes the Tur. The Rosh, however, writes that despair combined with a weak change of name, etc.; see chapter 7, siman 2. His words are based on Tosafos on daf 67a, s.v. "this," in their first answer. At first, Tosafos had written on daf 66b, s.v. "change of name," that every change of name works only together with despair, and they similarly write at the beginning of the above passage. According to that, when Rav Zeira answers, "change," etc., he means even together with despair. Tosafos then ask, "If so," etc., and because of this return to distinguish between a minor and major change. Nevertheless, they do not retract the requirement that the change be irreversible, as Rashi writes there, s.v. "beam," etc., whereas regarding a bar, etc. Their second answer is that every change of name, even a weak one, acquires without despair. According to both answers, a reversible change does not acquire even together with despair. The Tur's words, however, are taken from Tosafos in Sukkah, as stated there. What the Beit Yosef writes, that the Tur learned this from Tosafos on daf 66b, s.v. "is there," is astonishing and a major error, because that passage does not discuss despair or change of name at all, but a reversible physical change, which Tosafos hold acquires rabbinically even without despair. This follows their general approach, as they write there on daf 65b, s.v. "they," and the Rosh likewise writes in their name and at the beginning of the third chapter of Sukkah, as explained above. End.
