And some disagree. See Shach, seif katan 4, who rules that a change of possession before ye'ush does not acquire, connecting this to Abaye's ruling that ye'ush without prior knowledge is not ye'ush. His underlying argument is that both rules rest on the fact that the second possessor received the object while it was still in a prohibited state. If later ye'ush could cure a prior change of possession, one could likewise argue that later knowledge should cure ye'ush that began without knowledge. In my view Rambam can nevertheless be defended. Bava Kamma 115 presents the dispute between Rav and R. Yochanan concerning change of possession followed by ye'ush. The simple structure of the sugya indicates that the halachah follows R. Yochanan that such a sequence does not fully exempt the acquirer. Yet Rambam can still rule that the body of the object is acquired while its value remains payable. The Gemara's discussion there can be understood as addressing whether the second possessor is exempt even from paying value. Rav, who elsewhere holds ye'ush alone can acquire, says the later possessor may be fully exempt. R. Yochanan says the second person must at least restore value. Thus Rambam's rule that either order of ye'ush and change of possession can acquire the body while leaving a value obligation is consistent with R. Yochanan. This also answers Shach's proof from the rule of ye'ush without knowledge. There the Gemara compares a thief or robber with something swept away by a river. If change of possession followed by later ye'ush merely acquired the body while still requiring payment, that would not equal the full exemption of a river-loss. The comparison therefore remains meaningful. Moreover, if ye'ush preceded the transfer, according to Shach and others the acquirer may be exempt from payment where the thief was not notorious. According to the analysis I give below, the obligation to pay value after ye'ush and change of possession itself depends on the underlying dispute among Amoraim. Thus there is no contradiction between Abaye and Rambam.
