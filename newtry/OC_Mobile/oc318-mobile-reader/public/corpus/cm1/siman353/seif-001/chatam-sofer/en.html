Be'er HaGolah seif katan 9, "and so writes the Beit Yosef." Note: It is true that this is what appears from the Beit Yosef, but it is an error, for the two positions are as distant from each other as east from west. According to the Rivam, the purchaser returns the money to the owners and keeps the utensil for himself. According to the Raavad, however, he returns the utensils to the owners in exchange for money, meaning that they reimburse him for what he paid. This is why the Rashba challenges him from the case of the customs collectors, as is also explicit in the Gemara, Bava Kamma 114a. See there. The words of the Beit Yosef as cited in Be'er HaGolah therefore require study. Likewise, in the Tur, simanim 357 and 368, the words attributed to the Raavad must be corrected to state that the object is given back to its owners in exchange for its value. See the Maggid Mishneh at the beginning of chapter 5 of Hilchot Geneivah, where it is clear that the Raavad did not write in accordance with the Rambam. This requires very great study.
