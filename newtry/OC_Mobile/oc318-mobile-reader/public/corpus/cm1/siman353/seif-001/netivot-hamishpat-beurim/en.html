He stole a lamb and it became a ram. See Shach, seif katan 1, concerning Bach's question from the Gemara's statement that a yearling calf can still be called an ox. Shach answers that we follow ordinary human language, and although Scripture sometimes uses the broader term "ox," people may call the animal by a different name after it matures. In my view a pure change of name without any physical change acquires d'oraisa only where the name has changed even in Torah language. Rashba writes similarly regarding whether two things are considered the same species: where Torah itself distinguishes their names, the distinction has legal significance. Tur and Mechaber refer here to a different form of change, namely a change of name that results from an actual physical change. Siman 360, seif 6, shows that in a physical change it is enough that ordinary human language now calls the object by a different name. Thus there are two categories. A purely nominal change requires a Torah-level change of designation. A physical change that produces a new common name can acquire even if Scripture still sometimes uses the older general term.
