For example, he stole a lamb and it became a ram, etc. Maharshal objects that the Gemara says a lamb becoming a ram and a calf becoming an ox are not changes of name, because Scripture calls even a newborn by those terms. In my opinion the Rosh, Tur, Rabbeinu Yerucham, and Mechaber are correct. In all legal matters we follow ordinary human speech, even leniently, and today people plainly do not call a newborn animal a ram or ox. The Gemara's scriptural verses show only that those names existed in biblical usage and may have been common in Rabbi Ilai's time. They do not establish contemporary usage for every place and age. Nedarim similarly concludes that although Scripture may call roasted food 'cooked,' vows follow ordinary language according to place and time. The same principle applies to contracts, marriage documents, and all other legal matters. Thus, in contemporary usage the maturation is a change of name.
