The judgment is reversed. This applies even where all three judges are experts, even where the parties accepted them, and even where they held governmental authorization, because an error in an explicit and elementary law is no judgment at all. The Tur states this, and the Rosh says explicitly: an error in an explicit law is always reversed, even where all three are experts, were accepted by the parties, or received authorization from the Exilarch, because such a judgment is wholly unfit to stand.
The Nimukei Yosef writes that where a matter remains unresolved in the Talmud, such as an inquiry ending in teiku, and the court rules stringently against the defendant rather than leniently, this is an error in an explicit law because the procedural rule is established. This is unconvincing. An explicit error exists only where the Mishnah or Talmud definitively shows that the judge was wrong. A teiku does not prove error. Such a ruling is not even necessarily an error in discretionary judgment; since no definite error can be shown, the ruling stands and the judge is exempt. The Nimukei Yosef follows his own view that seizure never helps in a teiku. But I demonstrated in Tekufo Kohen that the primary law follows the Rambam and many authorities that seizure does help, and that this is also the Rif’s view. A teiku is therefore comparable to a dispute among authorities where a judge who follows either side has acted validly. The statement that “a judge who ruled like Rabbi Elazar has acted validly” is itself used by many authorities to prove that seizure helps. Accordingly, where the judge awarded the property in a teiku, the result stands and he does not pay from his own funds. This is the plain meaning of the Talmud and the Rosh and Tur, who say that where the matter cannot be resolved there is no judicial error and the ruling stands. Even were ordinary self-help seizure ineffective in a teiku, here the property was delivered under authority of an accepted or expert court, which is seizure by permission and is effective according to all views. The Nimukei Yosef himself recognizes such a distinction elsewhere.
