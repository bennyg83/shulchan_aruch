Or in the words of the poskim. Chavot Yair writes in the addenda that every ruling stated without qualification by Beit Yosef in Shulchan Aruch and not disputed by later authorities is treated like an explicit mishna. Where Rama cites dissenting opinions and adopts them, his ruling likewise has that status in lands that follow Rama. Where Rama writes 'some say' without deciding, a clear decision of Sema or Shach is treated as an explicit rule. If neither decides, money cannot be extracted. Nevertheless, if a judge followed Rama's gloss to extract money, the judgment is not reversed, for otherwise Rama's citation would have no practical significance. Where Sema and Shach disagree, in my opinion Shach's authority is not weaker than Sema's, and one may follow Shach even to extract money; his ruling is likewise comparable to an explicit rule because the law follows later authorities. I have heard that it is considered extremely difficult to extract money against Sema, so at minimum one might say that a judge who followed Sema is not deemed to have erred in an explicit rule. Even so, my view is as stated, unless the judge recognizes that Shach's rejection of Sema is clearly mistaken for a compelling reason. Chavot Yair further writes that he does not know what, in our time, would qualify as an error of judicial discretion that can definitely be shown to be wrong, since anything recorded in the books of the poskim is treated as an explicit rule.
