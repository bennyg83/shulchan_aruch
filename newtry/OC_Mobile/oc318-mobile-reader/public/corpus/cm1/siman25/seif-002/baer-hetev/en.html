The world. The Shach writes that this is not literal. The same rule applies throughout the entire region in which the court sits, such as the custom concerning the forbidden fat covering the stomach among the Jews of the Rhineland and similar matters. The governing principle is that everything follows the local custom. Nimukei Yosef writes that where no general practice can be found favoring one view, the judge should rule according to where his own understanding inclines. Darkei Moshe cites this. Sema.
It is reversed. If there is a greater authority who can reverse the judgment, the judgment is reversed where reversal is still possible. Such a greater authority can reverse only the ruling of an expert judge who was accepted by the litigants, or of three ordinary judges one of whom is expert enough to understand the superior authority's reasoning. In other circumstances, such as a single expert judge who was not accepted, or three ordinary judges who were accepted, and the other configurations discussed by the poskim, the superior authority does not reverse the judgment at all, and what was done remains effective subject to the specific law governing that case. Whenever the superior authority can reverse the judgment, if before the error became known the successful litigant fed the disputed item to dogs, traveled overseas, or otherwise made restitution impossible, the judge is exempt from payment, whether he was an expert accepted by the parties or one of three ordinary judges in the relevant case, because the losing litigant caused his own loss; there was a greater authority who would have reversed the judgment. If the judge himself physically took and transferred the property before the superior authority learned of the matter, and restitution later becomes impossible, then an expert accepted by the parties is exempt, while three ordinary judges who were not accepted must pay. From this it follows that if one erred in discretionary judgment and ruled that a kosher item was treif or the like, while the prohibited item remains before us, the ruling stands and another sage, even one greater than the first, cannot permit it merely because general practice follows the other opinion, except in the circumstances explained above. Whenever a judge is exempt because he is an expert, he is not exempt until he establishes that he is truly expert, learned, analytically capable, and experienced in such matters for many years. Even in the present era there are experts for these purposes. Thus the Shach. The Sema writes that the Mechaber's ruling that where the judge is exempt the judgment itself is reversed because otherwise the litigant would suffer a loss follows the Rif and Rambam. The Rosh and Tur disagree and hold that the judgment is not reversed. Since Rama makes no gloss here, one must say he relied on what he writes in seif 3, "and some say," etc., that the judgment is not reversed, which applies here as well.
Their judgment. Meaning that acceptance by the parties is effective with respect to these judges, so that if they err they are exempt from paying. Sema.
He shall not take it out. Whether seizure is effective in a matter subject to dispute, allowing the possessor to say "I rely on this authority," is discussed in Maharik, shoresh 94. Darkei Moshe cites it and writes that perhaps such seizure is effective only regarding a bridegroom's dowry. See Even HaEzer, Tur siman 55 and Shulchan Aruch siman 52. Elsewhere we do not find that it is effective. The Mordechai, however, holds that seizure is effective in every such case. See the end of siman 139, where in a dispute that cannot be resolved by division we apply the rule that whoever is stronger prevails. Thus the Sema.
And in number. Darkei Moshe writes that if one authority is greater in wisdom and the other greater in number, we follow the one greater in number. This means he has more students, or alternatively that most of the students agree with him. Sema.
Many. In a Torah prohibition we do not necessarily follow a majority of authorities who all rule leniently if they arrive at that conclusion for different reasons. This limitation applies where we are trying to resolve a doubtful question from written works. If the majority of sages themselves are present before us, we follow them even when they do not all rely on the same reason. If two authorities reach the same ruling from two distinct biblical verses, they count as only one view, because certainly one interpretation is mistaken, since two verses were not written to teach the same point. This limitation applies to Scriptural derivations. With respect to the Mishnah, Gemara, and poskim, they count as two. Shach.
The later authorities. The Mordechai writes that in every place the halacha follows Rav Alfasi unless Tosafot disagree with him. Sema.
