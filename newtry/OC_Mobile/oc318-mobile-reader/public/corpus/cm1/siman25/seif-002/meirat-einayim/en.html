And he acted like one of them without knowing. Hagahot Maimoniyot writes that where two Tannaim or Amoraim disagree and the judge knew only one opinion, unaware of the other, and ruled according to the opinion he knew, this is not called a discretionary error. What he did remains done and he does not pay from his own property.
Where the accepted practice already spread, etc. Rambam explains the Gemara's phrase that the general course of halacha followed the other opinion in this way. The Tur cites additional interpretations. Nimukei Yosef writes in the name of Rabbeinu Yonah that where no general practice favors either side, the judge may rule according to where his own reasoning inclines.
And he received authority from the Reish Galuta. The same applies to authorization from a court in Eretz Yisrael, except that authority from an Eretz Yisrael court extends only within Eretz Yisrael, whereas authority from the Reish Galuta had a broader reach. Rambam writes this explicitly.
Since he is an expert, etc. Meaning, because he is an expert, the parties' acceptance or the official authorization benefits him by exempting him from payment. Without expertise, those factors do not have the same effect, as explained in the next seif.
What he says that the judgment is reversed. This follows Rif's approach that whenever the judge is exempt from paying, Chazal reverse the judgment so that the litigant does not bear the loss caused by the court. The Rosh and Tur disagree and hold that the judgment is not reversed. Rama relies on the later 'some say' to record that dispute.
Three ordinary judges have the law of one expert judge. Meaning the litigants' acceptance protects them from personal payment if they err. The Rosh and Tur write this according to their approach, and Rama applies the same practical rule to Rambam's framework.
He should not extract money in a case of legal doubt. Whether seizure is effective in a disputed legal issue where one party says 'kim li' like the authority supporting him is discussed by Maharik. He suggests that seizure may be effective only in the specific ketubah dispute between Rashi and Rabbeinu Tam, where Rashi's reasoning is strong though Rabbeinu Tam is later. Mordechai at the end of Ketubot holds more broadly that 'kim li' can be asserted wherever authorities dispute. See also the end of siman 139 concerning a dispute where neither party is in possession and division is not appropriate, where the rule of 'whoever is stronger prevails' can apply.
Wherever there is a doubt of law. At the end of the work I wrote at length the general rules of safek din.
In wisdom and in number. Darkei Moshe cites Hagahot Ashri that if one authority is greater in wisdom and the other greater in number, the one greater in number is followed, meaning he has many students or most students support his view.
Everywhere, etc. Mordechai writes that the halacha generally follows Rif unless Tosafot disagrees with him.
