Seif 1: “If it is impossible to restore,” etc. This concerns a judge who did not personally transfer the object. “He fed it” means it was fed by his ruling. Even Rambam’s wording can mean the questioner acted on the judge’s instruction. The Gemara’s case of Rabbi Tarfon, who “gave it to the dogs,” is explained as not having done so personally. If the judge acted with his own hands, he is a direct tortfeasor, and there should be no distinction between an error in an explicit Mishna and one in discretionary judgment. The exemption rests on absence of intent to damage. The Sema’s contrary ruling, exempting even direct feeding because the judge intended a practical ruling, is difficult, for then why is he liable in discretionary error?
