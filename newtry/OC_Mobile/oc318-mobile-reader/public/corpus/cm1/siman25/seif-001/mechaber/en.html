When a judge who erred must reverse the ruling and when he must pay. This siman contains 5 seifim.
Any judge who adjudicated a monetary case and erred, if he erred regarding matters that are explicit and well known, such as laws stated expressly in the Mishnah, Gemara, or the rulings of the poskim, the judgment is reversed and the case is decided correctly. Rama: Some say that if it appears to the judge and the scholars of his generation, on the basis of compelling proofs, that the law is not as stated by the poskim, he may disagree with them where the matter is not stated in the Gemara. Nevertheless, one should not be lenient regarding a matter in which works accepted throughout most of Israel have ruled stringently unless he has received from his teachers that the practice does not follow that stringency. If reversal is impossible, for example because the person who received the money wrongfully went overseas, is violent and cannot be compelled, or because the judge declared something impure that was pure, or ruled a kosher animal to be treifah and it was fed to dogs, or the like, the judge is exempt from payment. Although he caused a loss, he did not intend to cause damage. Rama: Some disagree. Other poskim also disagree. Regarding rulings in matters of prohibition and permission, if he has attained the level fit to issue rulings, even if he does not possess formal semikhah, he has the status of an expert. See Yoreh Deah, siman 242, regarding semikhah in the present era.
