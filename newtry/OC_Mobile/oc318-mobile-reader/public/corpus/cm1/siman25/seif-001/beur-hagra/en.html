Every. Everything written in the Shulchan Aruch in this siman is the Rambam's language and follows the Rif's approach, except what is written in seif 3, "and if it is impossible," which is what the Rif cites in the name of a Gaon: "and we also see that he says, etc."
Every judge. Meaning, there is no distinction here between an expert judge who was accepted and one who personally transferred the property, or not; the essential point is that he be a judge. But this is not so regarding what is written in seif 5.
If he erred, etc. Like Rav Sheshet, whose view we follow in chapter 12 of Ketubot, chapter 6 of Shevuot, and chapter 1 of Sanhedrin.
However, some say. As stated, "Rabbi said: if an explicit ruling comes, etc.," because a judge has only, etc.
(Collected note) However, some say, etc. As stated in many places in the Talmud, where the Amoraim said, "the halacha does not follow this Mishnah." (End of collected note.)
(Collected note) By force of proofs, etc. As stated in the chapter HaCholetz: "What does 'it is not a Mishnah' mean, etc.?" See there. (End of collected note.)
(Collected note) Since it was not mentioned, etc. With regard to the Gemara one has no authority to add or subtract, and certainly not to disagree, as stated in HaPoalim: Ravina and Rav Ashi are the end of authoritative instruction. (End of collected note.)
(Collected note) Nevertheless, one should not be lenient, etc. Avodah Zarah 36a: "in every case he may annul, etc., since it spread, etc." The Ran there writes that there are three categories: if a decree spread among most of Israel, even a greater beit din cannot annul it; if it did not spread, a greater beit din may annul it; if it did not spread and most of the community cannot endure it, even a lesser beit din may annul it. (End of collected note.)
And if it is impossible. There in the sugya: "and if so, why does he need the case of one who erred, etc.?" Rav Nachman bar Yitzchak said to Rava, etc.: "you did nothing."
Although he caused. Meaning, even according to Rabbi Meir, whose view we follow on this point, all agree that he is exempt.
And there are those who disagree. Meaning, he is liable. They understand that the sugya there follows the Sages. See the Rosh and Tosafot there, s.v. "Hashta, etc.," and chapter 9 of Bava Kamma 100a, s.v. "Tihar, etc."
(Collected note) And there are those who disagree. Their dispute depends on the sugya in HaNizakin. The Rif rules like Rabbi Yehuda concerning one who cooks, as written in Orach Chayyim, beginning of siman 318, and so rules the Rambam. He also establishes the Mishnah there concerning one who renders food impure, etc., where he acted unwittingly, and likewise where the produce fell and split, etc., in accordance with Rabbi Yehuda and Rabbi Shimon. Likewise, the rule that a prohibited substance that was inadvertently nullified, etc., follows Rabbi Yose, as stated in the Yerushalmi cited by Tosafot there. Tosafot in Chullin 15a, however, follow Rabbi Meir from the Shabbos passage: "on the next day he went out and taught publicly, etc." There, in a rabbinic matter, we impose a penalty, like Rav Nachman bar Yitzchak, all according to Rabbi Meir. Tosafot in Gittin write that the liability in Bechorot, even where the judge did not personally transfer the property, follows Rabbi Meir's view that we penalize an inadvertent act because of an intentional one. So too Tosafot, Bava Batra 22b. But the Rif and Rambam rule like Rabbi Yehuda that we do not impose such a penalty. This is the meaning of "although he caused damage, he did not intend, etc." See Yoreh Deah, siman 110, seif 7, what is written there, and Orach Chayyim, beginning of siman 318. (End of collected note.)
And regarding issuing halachic rulings. Anyone who is qualified and able to judge or issue a ruling under Torah law is exempt, as stated at the beginning of Sanhedrin 3a: "if so, etc." We also say, "and mighty are all her slain, etc." When Rabbi Akiva said to Rabbi Tarfon, "you are an expert," that is not literal; rather, it means he had reached the level fit to issue rulings and was therefore acting under compulsion rather than negligence.
