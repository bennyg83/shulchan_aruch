If the judge's error cannot presently be clarified and the litigant has already paid or waived money because of an oath imposed by that judge, the burden of proof lies on the party seeking to undo the result. Perhaps the judge did not err. Shach, citing Beit Yosef. This follows Rambam's view that even an error in judicial discretion can be reversed so long as the judge did not physically transfer the money. Under Rosh, however, there is a complication. According to Tur, an error in discretion stands and must be carried out even before execution. According to Rabbi Meir HaLevi Abulafia, the error need not be carried out once discovered, but if the litigant already compromised and paid money in order to avoid the oath, even he can agree that the prior judgment has effectively been executed and stands. The point is especially difficult for authorities who say that where the judge is exempt from payment the judgment itself is reversed to prevent the litigant's loss. Here the judge certainly will not reimburse a compromise, because he can say, "Who told you to compromise? You could have sworn." The resulting allocation therefore requires further study.
