Who is not an expert. The Sema writes that this means one who is neither sufficiently learned nor analytically proficient. The Shach explains that the practical difference in whether the judgment is reversed concerns matters such as the standard thirty-day period granted by beit din, or the fruits produced by property in the interim.
And if. The Kesef Mishnah explains the rule as follows: if the judge personally took the property from one litigant and transferred it to the other, then even if the recipient still has the property, the first litigant may immediately recover from the judge. If the judge did not personally transfer it, the litigant must first seek recovery from the other party; only if that party lacks assets may he recover from the judge. The Shach writes that the Kesef Mishnah explained this correctly, unlike the Sema. He adds that one who studies Rabbenu Ovadiah of Bartenura at the beginning of Zeh Borer will see that his words likewise incline toward the Kesef Mishnah's interpretation. The Ir Shushan also rules so, and this is the primary view.
