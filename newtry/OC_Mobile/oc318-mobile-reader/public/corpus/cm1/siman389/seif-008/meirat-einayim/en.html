For example, a lion's ordinary manner is to trample, etc. The Tur cites Ri: a lion's normal manner is to trample and eat, or to seize and leave. Therefore in either of those forms it is forewarned from the outset, and if the damage occurs in the injured party's domain the owner pays full damages, while in the public domain he is exempt under the laws of foot. But if the lion seizes the animal and then eats it, that is not its ordinary manner. The damage is treated like horn of an innocuous animal and creates half damages even in the injured party's domain. A wolf and other wild animals ordinarily seize prey, so they are forewarned for that behavior. Trampling is not their ordinary manner and is therefore like innocuous horn. A snake too is forewarned from the outset and is accustomed to all forms of harmful action. The lion differs because it is the king of the animals and does not retreat before anything. It does not wait for the animal it tramples to die before eating. It may trample it with its foot and eat it, or tear it into pieces in anger and leave it. A wolf and other animals are more timid. They do not ordinarily eat while the prey is still moving, but first seize it. This is the sense of the phrase "a wolf tears and eats." The Rosh explains this. A snake that kills is treated as forewarned even though it receives no benefit, because that is its ordinary conduct rather than an act of unusual rage. See the Derishah.
