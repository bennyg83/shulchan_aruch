[Seif 14] If it belongs to one person. This means that the owner of the produce may place his produce there, in which case the owner of the oxen has no right to bring his ox inside, since it may eat the produce. When no produce is there, the owner of the oxen may bring in his ox, and the owner of the produce then has no right to bring in his own ox. Therefore, if the ox damages the produce through tooth or foot, the owner is liable, because relative to the produce the area is the injured party's domain. If it damages by horn, meaning that the owner of the produce also brought his ox there, the owner of the damaging ox is exempt, because the owner of the produce brought his ox in without permission. This is the Nimukei Yosef's explanation. It appears to me that if the ox of the owner of the oxen injures the produce owner's ox by tooth, such as rubbing against it for pleasure and knocking it down, for any damage occurring through enjoyment is a derivative of tooth, or by the ordinary course of walking, which is a derivative of foot, the owner of the oxen is likewise exempt. Relative to an ox, the place is the damaging party's domain. The text mentions horn only because oxen ordinarily injure one another by goring, which is a derivative of horn. If the produce owner's animal damages the animal of the owner of the oxen, he is liable, because with respect to oxen this is the injured party's domain. But if the produce owner's animal damages produce that the owner of the oxen placed there, even though the damaging animal was brought in without permission, the damaged object, namely the produce, was also placed there without permission. The area is therefore considered the damaging party's domain, since one who seeks recovery must show that the damaged object was lawfully present there.
