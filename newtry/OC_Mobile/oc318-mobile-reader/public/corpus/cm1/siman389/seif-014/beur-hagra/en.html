If it is, etc. There the Gemara says: rather, plainly it is a domain belonging to neither of them, but to one person. It must refer to a case of this kind, since the words cannot be read literally, as the Rashba asks, for then what would "neither this one nor that one" mean?
(Collected note) If it is, etc. This is the meaning of "rather, it belongs to one," for the phrase cannot be understood literally. Moreover, it would merely repeat the first case, a domain belonging to the injured party, and what then would be the four categories? (End of note.)
(Collected note) For horn he is exempt, since it is, etc. The Gemara asks there, "we have arrived at the Rabbis." Had this distinction applied, it could have answered with such a case, and this is certainly true of tooth and foot. Hence the wording, "for tooth and foot," etc. (End of note.)
For horn, etc., the injured party's courtyard. This is not literal. It means only that it is not the damager's courtyard. The Gemara's wording there is followed: "rather, plainly," etc., and the latter clause teaches, etc.
