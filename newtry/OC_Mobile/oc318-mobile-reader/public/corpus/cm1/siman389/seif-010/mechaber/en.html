The foregoing applies when the animal entered the injured party's domain and caused damage. If the injured party entered the damager's domain and the homeowner's animal injured him, the owner is exempt from all liability, for he may say, 'Had you not entered my property, you would not have been harmed.' The Torah states, 'He shall send forth his livestock and it shall consume in another's field.' Rama: This wording is from Rambam and requires study, because that verse concerns tooth and foot and teaches exemption in a jointly used domain, whereas exemption in the damager's own domain applies even to horn and is based upon the argument, 'Had you not entered.' The exemption in the damager's domain applies only where the injured party entered without permission. If he entered with permission, such as workers entering to collect their wages, and the homeowner's ox gored them, the owner is liable.
