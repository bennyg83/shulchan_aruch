A non-Jew cannot become an agent. Shach in siman 243 writes that a non-Jew may serve as an agent for another non-Jew, citing Mas'at Binyamin, and calls it obvious. Beit Shmuel, Even HaEzer siman 5, seif katan 9, writes that when a non-Jew gives something to another non-Jew it is a case of indirect facilitation and is permitted, because even if he gives it to his agent there is no agency for a non-Jew. He cites Terumat HaDeshen and infers that even one non-Jew cannot act as agent for another, unlike Mas'at Binyamin, siman 96, who rules simply that agency exists between non-Jews. There is no proof from Terumat HaDeshen.
In siman 299 he discusses a non-Jew who buys chickens to castrate them and permits a Jew to sell them to him where the purchaser does not castrate them himself but hands them to his servant. He treats this as indirect facilitation. Although they were handed to an agent to castrate, there is no agency for a transgression, and Terumat HaDeshen adds that perhaps the same applies from one non-Jew to another, and certainly so because generally a non-Jew has no agency toward a Jew. It is clear that Terumat HaDeshen's reasoning concerns only agency for a transgression. He did not want one to argue that the rule denying agency for a transgression applies only to a Jew, whereas a non-Jew's agent might carry the principal's transgression. He therefore writes that all the more so there is no such agency for a non-Jew, since ordinary agency of a non-Jew toward a Jew does not exist. But where there is no transgression, his wording actually implies that agency between two non-Jews may exist, in accordance with Mas'at Binyamin.
