A man or a woman. Gittin 62b; Kiddushin 52a.
And even a married woman. Eruvin 79; Bava Metzia 12; Nedarim chapter 8; Gittin 64 and 65.
(Collected note) And even a married woman. Gittin 23b in the Mishnah: “even the women, etc., and her yevamah, etc.” (End of note.)
And even a slave. There and there; Bava Metzia 98b, “not through his slave,” implying that the slave of another is valid. In Gittin 23: “A slave cannot, etc.,” implying that for a matter in which he himself is legally capable, he is valid. There as well: “The words appear, etc.; and if someone challenges you, etc.” This is why it also says “and a maidservant,” and likewise there and there.

Therefore, etc. Meaning that the homeowner did not send either the pundion or the flask with the child, and therefore the shopkeeper is liable for both. It follows the Rashbam’s explanation in the name of “some explain.” Although the Mishnah deals with a flask that the homeowner handed to him, the ruling here speaks of one handed over by the shopkeeper because of the Gemara’s question there, “Granted, etc.” The answer, “for example, where he took it,” is not written here but in its place in the laws of theft. See siman 359 seif 5.
And placed it in his hand, etc. According to the explanation of the Rashbam and Tosafos.
And if he specified, etc. There: “Rabbi Yehudah holds, etc.” This shows that in this case the Sages agree.
