Even a married woman. Rambam writes this. In the Perishah I explained that in my opinion this means even one man's wife may act as an agent for another man. The novelty is that although she is subject to another person's authority and therefore is not entirely similar to the principal, she may nevertheless serve as his agent.
Even a male or female slave. Meaning, even Canaanite slaves, who are obligated in some mitzvot. Although with respect to acquiring an eruv on behalf of others one may not confer it through them, nor through a wife whom he supports, as the Tur and the Mechaber write in Orach Chaim siman 386, there is a distinction. There the others must acquire the eruv that remains in the possession of those through whom he seeks to confer it, so their hand is like the hand of the person conferring it and it is as though the eruv remains in his own hand. With agency, by contrast, this concern does not apply, because they deliver the item to the person to whom they were sent. See the Perishah.
And they are subject to some mitzvot. Meaning, although the slave is not entirely like the Jewish principal, who is obligated in all mitzvot, he is nevertheless subject to some mitzvot.
He sent him only to inform him. Meaning, to inform him that he needs oil.
And he should not have sent it except through a competent person. This applies specifically to the pundion that remains intact in the shopkeeper's possession. Since the pundion belongs to the minor's father, the shopkeeper may not derive benefit from it until the father receives its equivalent in exchange. The flask, by contrast, does not remain in the shopkeeper's possession, and he is not required to guard it more carefully than the father himself, who entrusted it to the minor. Although the shopkeeper took the flask in hand to measure into it for the minor, what of it? From the outset it was property knowingly exposed to loss by the father. The same applies if he had returned the pundion itself to the minor and had not wished to send anything through him, and it was then lost from the minor's hand; the shopkeeper would be exempt. See the Perishah.
And he measured for others, etc. In that case he has used another person's property without permission and is liable even for unavoidable accidents until he effects a complete return.
And if he expressly stated and said, etc. The reason is simple. One can no longer say that he intended the shopkeeper to send through a competent person, because he explicitly instructed him to send it through his minor son.
