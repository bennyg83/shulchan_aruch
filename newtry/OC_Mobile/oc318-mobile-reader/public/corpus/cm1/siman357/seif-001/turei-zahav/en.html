[Seif 1] And he recognized his articles. The Tur's meaning is straightforward. Where a person is not accustomed to selling his household goods, he must identify the articles by signs. There remains a concern that he saw those signs while the articles were in the seller's possession, and a further concern that he recently needed money and sold them. If witnesses saw people tunnel through his wall and carry out a bundle, even without knowing what the bundle contained, that circumstance establishes that stolen property left his house, and his identifying signs are then sufficient to show that the articles were his and were not sold. The same applies without evidence of a tunnel where witnesses testify that the articles belonged to him and a public report arose that they were stolen, because one not accustomed to selling his goods is presumed not to have sold them. This is the Rosh's view, accepted by the Tur. Where the owner is accustomed to selling his goods, however, the greater concern that he sold them is overcome only if witnesses saw these very articles carried through the tunnel. This resolves the Beit Yosef's questions. The Beit Yosef also objects to the Raavad: since there was despair and a change of possession, why must the purchaser return the stolen article itself? There is no question. The owner and purchaser dispute whether there was theft at all. The owner says: you acquired it from my thief, certainly at a low price; receive your purchase price under the market enactment and return my goods. The purchaser says: I did not buy from a thief, and even if I bought cheaply you have no claim against me. Where the owner is not accustomed to selling and the required evidence exists, the court accepts the owner's claim, subject to his oath. If the purchaser then says that since he is treated as a purchaser from a thief he will not return the article itself but will pay the difference up to its full value, he is correct because despair and change of possession have transferred title. The present dispute, however, concerns only who receives the benefit of the bargain price. The Tur's phrase that the owner removes the article from the possessor means that the purchaser insists on retaining it for less than its value, not that he offers to complete its value. This is the Raavad's view. According to Rabbeinu Yitzchak, by contrast, the case cannot involve despair, because after despair and change of possession the purchaser would retain the article even if the owner's account were true. His explanation must therefore concern a case before despair.
