One who recognizes his utensils or books after a theft becomes known in the city. It contains three seifim.
If a householder who does not usually sell his utensils reports a theft and recognizes his utensils or books in another's possession, or if he does sell but these are items ordinarily lent or rented, then upon witnesses establishing ownership, the possessor swears while holding a sacred object how much he paid, receives that amount from the owner, and returns the property. Some say that by claiming theft the owner weakens the presumption arising from items ordinarily lent or rented, since he does not claim lending or rental. If the owner usually sells such items and they are not ordinarily lent or rented, they are not removed from purchasers merely because a theft was reported. But if guests stayed in his house, he cried out at night that his property was stolen, a forced entry was found, and the guests were seen leaving with bundles while everyone identified the goods as his, he is believed. The possessor then swears how much he spent, receives reimbursement, and returns the goods.
