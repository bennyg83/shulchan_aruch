a) The language of the Rambam, chapter 1 of Hilkhot Geneivah, law 10, from the Mishnah and Rava's statement in Bava Kamma at the end of 114b, as Rav Ashi concludes on 115a. The Kesef Mishneh writes that the Rambam's words are clear: Rava's statement that one need not investigate so extensively means that it is enough merely that a report of theft had circulated, as Rav Ashi concludes, even if no people came and lodged in the owner's house, unlike Rashi's explanation.
b) The Maggid Mishneh writes that this law is not explicit in the Gemara, but the Rambam understood from the distinctions made in the law of one who enters another's house, explained above in siman 90, seif 11, that a similar distinction applies here. The Raavad disagrees and holds that even if no report of theft circulated, the claimant may swear that he did not sell the items and recover them. His reasoning appears to be that when he says they were stolen he is believed by migo, because he could have said they were lent, and therefore is believed with an oath. The Rambam rejects such a migo where no report of theft circulated, because we do not presume ordinary people to be thieves. The Maggid Mishneh writes that this is correct.
gimel) I do not understand the Rama's view in this gloss. The Tur's words were based on his assumption that the Rambam was discussing even where no report of theft had circulated. According to our version, however, the Rambam speaks only where a report of theft had circulated, as explained above in the name of the Maggid Mishneh. According to both the Raavad and Rambam it is possible that the Rosh and Tur would also agree in such a case, as the Beit Yosef writes. I saw that the Sema attempts to reconcile the gloss and prove that this is indeed the Rambam's view; the reader may choose, and this is not the place to elaborate.
