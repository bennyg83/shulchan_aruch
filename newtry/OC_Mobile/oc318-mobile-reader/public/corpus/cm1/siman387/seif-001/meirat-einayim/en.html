We appraise the damaged item for the tortfeasor, etc. Meaning, even though Reuven directly killed Shimon's animal or broke his utensils, we do not say that Reuven takes the carcass or broken pieces for himself and pays Shimon for a new animal or utensil, as is the law of theft and robbery. There that rule is a specific scriptural decree: regarding robbery the verse says, "as that which he robbed," and regarding theft it says, "if the theft is found alive, he shall pay double." A tortfeasor is different. Even where he damages directly with his hands, the law is like one ox damaging another, concerning which the verse says, "and the dead animal shall be his," meaning the injured party's. The victim keeps the remains, and where the damaging ox is mu'ad the tortfeasor pays only the difference between the damaged item's prior value and its current remains.
