A non-Jewish laundress. See Taz, who rules that if the owners are not pursuing her, we say that they certainly already despaired of recovery, or alternatively that she settled with them by giving them something else. This is somewhat strained. No posek states a general rule that merely because owners are not pursuing someone they are presumed to have despaired. Ketzot HaChoshen also objects that perhaps they despaired only after the object came into the present holder's possession, which would be a change of domain followed by despair, and perhaps the laundress excused herself with a claim of ones. Possibly Taz reached his conclusion from circumstance. Since she launders only for people of the town, if she had not settled with the others their pursuit would certainly have become known. Likewise, if the garment had not remained with the laundress for a substantial period before reaching the present holder, the pursuit would still have been heard. We may therefore assume that it remained with her long enough for the owners' despair to precede its arrival in the holder's possession.
