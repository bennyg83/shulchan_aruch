I do not accept the view that passage of many days after the laundress gave the sheet to the recipient creates acquisition through despair. We rule that a change of possession followed only afterward by the owner's despair does not create acquisition, because the item came to the recipient unlawfully before despair. Therefore even after many days the recipient does not acquire if there had been no despair before the laundress delivered it. See Rama, siman 356, seif 3.
Taz adds that the laundress may have compensated the original owner for the sheet and thereby removed him from the property. I do not find this persuasive either. Even if she paid money, so long as the actual sheet remains intact it returns to its original owner, like mistaken judicial appraisal where the actual jewels are later found and return to their owner. Rambam likewise rules that even where a custodian paid for an animal, the animal itself can return in the applicable circumstances. Moreover, how do we know she paid? Perhaps she exempted herself by claiming unavoidable accident. Even under the authorities in siman 295 that a custodian who pays can acquire the lost object, that is specifically where he paid voluntarily without forcing the owner into litigation. Perhaps the laundress paid only after being compelled in court. Mordechai's phrase that the owners are "pursuing" their property does not mean merely that they presently have not despaired, because later despair would not help after the item already came unlawfully to the recipient. It means there was no prior despair before delivery. The correct rule is therefore that the sheet returns to its owner unless it is known that the owner had already despaired before the laundress gave it to the present holder.
