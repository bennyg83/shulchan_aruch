He may use it. The Tur explains that we presume the owner gave the craftsman permission to sell his garment. For this reason the permission applies only where the craftsman said, “Take your garment,” without specifying a particular garment, and it does not apply when the craftsman's wife or children handed it over even if they said merely “a garment,” because they presumably do not know of any arrangement between the owner and the craftsman authorizing sale. This also explains the difference between a craftsman's premises and a banquet or house of mourning, because in the latter settings people do not ordinarily authorize others to sell their garments.
