(Seif 2, gloss) The same applies to a gentile laundress. This ruling is copied from Terumat HaDeshen and is surprising, because Mordechai in HaGozel Batra states the law explicitly, yet Terumat HaDeshen does not cite it. Mordechai writes that a gentile laundress washed garments for several Jews, lost one person's sheet, took another person's sheet, and gave it as a replacement. He ordered the recipient to return it because the other owners had not despaired and were pursuing the laundress, and because market protection was not enacted where a thief used stolen property to pay a debt. If the laundress pledged the sheet to him, however, market protection would apply to the pledge. Mordechai's emphasis that the owners were pursuing her establishes the matter as certain. It follows that if the recipient does not know whose sheet it is and it remains with him for so long that an owner would necessarily have investigated, the owner has presumably despaired and the laundress likely satisfied him with something else. Terumat HaDeshen, siman 319, concerns only a case where two people are before us and each claims the sheet. If no one appears and protests, we may presume after a substantial delay that the laundress settled with the owner.
