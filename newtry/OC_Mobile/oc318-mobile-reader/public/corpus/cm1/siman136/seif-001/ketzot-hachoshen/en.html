Terumat HaDeshen writes concerning laundry mistakenly exchanged by a gentile laundress that if Reuven knows with certainty the sheet is not his, he must return it. Even if he is merely uncertain, if Shimon gives clear distinguishing marks proving the sheet is his, the mistake or theft is established and Reuven may not retain it. His wording implies that clear distinguishing marks work only where Reuven himself is uncertain. If Reuven claims with certainty that the sheet is his, even strong identifying marks from Shimon do not remove it from his possession. As I explained at length in siman 297, seif katan 1, even a strong identifying sign generally cannot extract property from a possessor, even from heirs who themselves claim uncertainty, unless additional circumstances, such as the possessor's lack of means, support the claim.
