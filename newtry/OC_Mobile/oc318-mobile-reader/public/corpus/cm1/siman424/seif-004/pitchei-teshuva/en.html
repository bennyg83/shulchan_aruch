One who injures his children, etc. See Responsa Shevut Yaakov, part 3, siman 140, concerning a teacher who became angry with his student and struck him because of his studies, injuring him, and whether he is liable for the four categories of payment or is exempt. He answers that it appears clear that he is exempt from all of them. This follows from tractate Makos, where the Gemara derives from the case of chopping wood, which is a voluntary act, that the father who strikes his son, the teacher who disciplines his student, and an agent of beit din are excluded. One cannot say that this merely exempts them from exile by a Scriptural decree while they remain liable for the four categories of payment, for the Gemara there explicitly explains that they are exempt because they are performing a mitzvah. For that same reason they should be exempt from all payments, just as we say regarding one who runs on Friday at twilight. Moreover, the Gemara compares these three cases to one another, and an agent of beit din is exempt even if he causes injury, as explained in Beit Yosef at the end of siman 8 and in the gloss to Shulchan Aruch there, without dispute, etc. This is likewise explicit in Terumat HaDeshen, siman 218, cited above in siman 421, seif 13, in the gloss, that the rule is not limited to one's son and student; rather, even with respect to any person who is under one's authority, it is appropriate to strike him in order to separate him from a transgression, etc. All the more so is this true for neglect of Torah study. Although we rule that he should strike him only with a shoelace and not cruelly, as stated in Shulchan Aruch, Yoreh Deah, siman 245, seif 10, nevertheless, after the fact he should not be fined on that account. Although Responsa Raanach, part 1, siman 111, writes that an agent of beit din who struck someone out of anger and rage is liable, as cited by Knesset HaGedolah above in siman 8, nevertheless, in the heat of Torah study a person's temper may flare, especially because it is very distressing when one studies with a student who does not apply his mind to his learning. He should therefore be exempt from all payment. Even so, in order that he not become accustomed to acting this way, and as a protective measure, I ruled that he must pay the physician's fee. These are his words; see there. According to his view, one must say that the laws in this seif concerning one who injures his children refer to a case where he did not strike him in order to discipline him. However, Responsa Kiryas Channah, siman 22, does not write so, as I cited briefly in Pitchei Teshuva to Yoreh Deah, siman 245, there. Rather, he maintains that here the law applies even where he struck him to discipline him, and the statement that "the father who strikes his son, etc., is excluded" means only that he is exempt from exile. With respect to the four categories of payment, however, an inadvertent act is included like an intentional one, etc. He discusses this at length there. This requires further study.
