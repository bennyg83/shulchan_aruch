Since the act carries beit din's death penalty. Meaning, if he inflicted the injury intentionally. Therefore even when he acted inadvertently he is exempt from monetary payment, as explained in the preceding seif. Rambam writes that although destructive conduct is ordinarily treated differently, since he gratified his evil inclination when he injured his fellow, he would incur death for this injury if he committed it intentionally. For the same reason he is liable even though he did not act for practical benefit. See Perishah.
On Yom Kippur, even intentionally, etc. This is the correct text and is also the Tur's wording. On Yom Kippur, even one who intentionally wounds another or performs labor does not incur a court-imposed death penalty, but rather karet. Although the labor also violates a negative commandment for which lashes would be imposed after proper warning, and we generally rule that where one act entails both lashes and monetary liability only the lashes are imposed, bodily injury is different. Scripture expressly reveals that monetary payment must be given and lashes are not imposed, because the verse uses the language of giving payment, as explained at the beginning of siman 420.
