[Sema, letter 3] In the case of bodily injury it is different, for Scripture revealed that he must pay money and is not flogged, since the language of giving is written regarding it. Note: In my humble opinion, this is the unresolved point of law according to what Tosafot Yeshanim write in Ketubot 32a, that humiliation and depreciation are included within the law of bodily injury. At first glance, however, it is difficult. According to Rabbi Yochanan’s answer, “Even if you say that the case concerns a na’arah, here it is where he was warned,” Rabbi Yochanan nevertheless agrees that in a case of bodily injury he pays money and is not flogged. If so, the Mishnah in Makkot remains difficult, since there are also payments for humiliation and depreciation. It is strained to say that he establishes the case as involving a mentally incompetent woman. Rather, Tosafot must hold that when Scripture revealed regarding bodily injury that he pays money, this applies specifically to the prohibition against striking, because that prohibition was permitted in the exceptional case of an agent of beit din, as the Gemara states there. By contrast, with the prohibition of forbidden relations, even though the monetary liability is itself for bodily injury, we say that he receives lashes. If so, the same applies to one who injures another on Yom Kippur. Examine this carefully.
