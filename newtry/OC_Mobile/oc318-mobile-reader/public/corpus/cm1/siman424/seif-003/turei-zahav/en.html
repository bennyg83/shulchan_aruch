Seif 7, some say a Torah scroll. Rav Chisda and Rabbah bar Rav Huna dispute in the Gemara: one says the money should be invested in a Torah scroll, and the other says in a date palm that produces fruit. The Rambam rules like Rabbah, while the Rosh rules like Rav Chisda. In the chapter Chezkas HaBatim they also differ regarding money deposited by a minor. There the Rosh does not decide, while the Tur in his abridgment and in Even HaEzer siman 86 cites the date-palm view. This requires explanation. It appears that in the deposit case there is no practical difference. Each sage gives one acceptable example and they do not truly dispute, so the Rosh did not decide. Here, however, concerning compensation belonging to one's son, there is a practical difference. A father is independently obligated by a positive commandment to acquire a Torah scroll with which to teach his son. Rav Chisda holds that he may use the son's compensation to discharge that obligation, whereas Rabbah bar Rav Huna holds that he cannot satisfy an existing personal obligation with the child's money. He must therefore purchase a fruit-producing date palm with the compensation and acquire the Torah scroll from his own funds. The father's benefit from being relieved of support is not comparable, because he is not strictly obligated to support even a young son, as stated in Ketubot. The Rosh follows the lenient view allowing the father to use the money for a Torah scroll. The Rambam adopts the stricter view, because the father's Torah obligation cannot be discharged with the child's compensation. In the laws of deposit, however, the Rambam writes “a date palm or a Torah scroll,” since there is no legal difference there. The Tur's mention of a date palm in Even HaEzer and in the abridgment does not establish that only a palm is valid. A palm is simply more readily available. A Torah scroll is not commonly found for sale because one may not ordinarily sell it, and commissioning a new scroll is less practical. The Sema's distinction, that the ruling here concerns money not yet received, is remote and fails also to reconcile the Tur and Rosh. One should not invent such distinctions between Amoraim without necessity.
