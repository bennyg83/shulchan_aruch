If she had an additional ketubah amount or melog property, etc. The phrase "additional ketubah amount" does not grammatically refer to the melog and tzon barzel property mentioned with it. The Tur and Rambam did not group all three together. The Mechaber combined them and ruled that beit din compels her to sell her financial interests at their present discounted value, each according to its law. Tzon barzel property and melog property that presently exist can be sold to her husband or to another person, and the money she receives is then used to pay her husband for his injury claim. If the husband himself purchases her interest, he deducts that value from what she owes him. The additional amount of her ketubah is different. She cannot effectively sell it to another person because of the concern that she might later forgive it to her husband. Therefore the only practical buyer is the husband himself. The phrase "additional ketubah" means the amount this husband added beyond the minimum Torah or rabbinic ketubah obligation. The Mechaber mentioned all three together only for convenience and relied upon the obvious distinction that existing melog and tzon barzel assets may be sold to the husband or others, while the additional ketubah interest is practically saleable only to him. When he concludes, "if she had none," he means she has none of these three and possesses only the basic ketubah obligation against her husband. She cannot sell any portion of that basic ketubah, whether at a discounted value or otherwise, because the Sages fixed the minimum ketubah at the prescribed amount and feared that if even a perutah were missing she would become too easy in his eyes to divorce. Therefore the injured husband has no present means of collecting from her unless he divorces her and then deducts the injury debt from her ketubah, or she gives him a promissory note for the amount.
