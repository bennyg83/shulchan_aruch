And he did not draw blood from them. Meaning, in that case he does not incur the death penalty for striking them. At the end of Parashat Emor it is written, "One who strikes an animal shall pay for it, and one who strikes a person shall be put to death." The "person" there means one's father or mother, for striking anyone else would not incur death. The Torah juxtaposes striking a parent with respect to capital punishment to striking an animal with respect to monetary payment. Just as one who strikes an animal is liable in payment only when he causes an injury that draws blood, for shortly before it says, "one who strikes the life of an animal," and blood is the life, so too one who strikes a parent is liable to death only where blood is drawn. And just as one who strikes an animal is liable whether inadvertently or intentionally, because a person is always forewarned with respect to damage, so too the phrase "one who strikes a person shall be put to death" applies even to an inadvertent blow in this respect, namely that the act bears a potential death liability and therefore no monetary payment is due for the injury because of kam lei bid'rabba minei had it been intentional. The same rule therefore applies even where the blow was inadvertent.
