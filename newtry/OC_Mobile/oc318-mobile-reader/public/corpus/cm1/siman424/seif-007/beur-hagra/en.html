And some say, etc. Rabbah bar Rav Huna was senior, as Shabbat at the end of chapter 8 shows, where Rav Huna addressed Rabbah his son. This follows his approach. See siman 423, seif 2.
And some say that he is not, etc. Rosh and Tosafot explain the Mishna on 88a this way. Rambam follows Tosafot's first interpretation.
(Supplement) Seifim 6 and 7, one who injures, etc. According to Tosafot and Rosh, both baraitot concern loss of employment. When children are supported at their father's table and the father injures them, he is exempt whether they are adults or minors; if others injure them, the payment belongs to them. When they are not supported, the payment always belongs to them, except that the surplus earnings of a minor daughter belong to her father whether he or another injured her. This concerns loss of employment. The other four heads of damages always belong to the children, except that the damage payment of a minor daughter belongs to the father, whether she is supported or not and whether the father or another injured her. Rambam, however, holds that when children are supported by their father and he injures them, he is exempt from all five heads, whether they are adults or minors.
(Supplement) And some say, etc. The wording implies that according to Rambam the father is liable for all five heads, and Maggid Mishneh says likewise. This requires great study, because medical expenses clearly belong to the daughter, as do pain and humiliation; even a husband acquires only a share of humiliation according to Rabbi Yehuda ben Beteira.
