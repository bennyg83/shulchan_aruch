We do not say that he pays the excess as full damages. Meaning, we do not simply offset the two injury payments against one another and require the party whose injury to the other was greater to pay the difference as full damages, as the Tur writes at the end of siman 421, seif 19, and the Mechaber in seif 13 there. The novelty is that where a man injures another man's wife, the law is different. The reason is that the woman has no available funds from which to pay for the injury she inflicted upon the man, while part of what the man owes for injuring her belongs to her husband, as the Tur and Mechaber explain. Therefore the man who injured the woman must give her husband the entire portion of her damages belonging to him. The portion belonging to the woman need not be paid to her. One might have thought that he should pay even that portion, the husband would then buy land with the money and consume its fruits during her lifetime, and the injured man would not collect the damages she owes him until the husband dies during her lifetime, at which point he could seize that land, with any remainder collected from her ketubah. The ruling teaches otherwise, because the Sages treated her portion as property unknown to the husband, from which he does not consume fruits. Rashba explains all this in his responsum. Therefore the amount belonging to her from the damages for her own injury is offset against what she owes the man for injuring him. For any remaining amount she owes, he takes a written obligation against her and waits until she is divorced or her husband dies.
