For he has some share, etc. We rule like Rabbi Yehuda ben Beteira at the beginning of Ketubot chapter 6, as stated in Even HaEzer 83. Therefore it resembles injury to his own body, as the Gemara explains. See siman 83.
And the portion that belongs, etc. Although the husband consumes the produce and she cannot waive his rights as she could not waive melog property, at the time of injury these were assets unknown to the husband, and he had not acquired them. Ketubot 78b says this according to Rabbi Shimon, and even concerning one who transfers property to evade a husband the Gemara says on 79a that they treated him, etc. All the more so here. See siman 40.
