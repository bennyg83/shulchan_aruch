For the proceeds of her labor, etc. This is the reason regarding compensation for loss of work, as the Gemara says, "Rav concedes," etc.
And the money, etc. This is the reason regarding depreciation. See Rashi, s.v. "for he diminished her value," and Tosafos 87a, s.v. "since," etc.
And some say, etc., and some say, etc. These positions are all brought by the Tur. The Tur writes that regarding loss of work the Rambam says that if she is supported at her father's table the payment belongs to him, since her labor belongs to him, while the Rosh says that even if she is supported at his table, where others injured her the payment belongs to her. If the father injured her, he is exempt; if she is not supported at his table, even the father who injured her must pay her. The Tur understood the Rambam's words here as referring to a daughter supported at the father's table because the Rambam says, "since her labor belongs to him." Yet that reading is difficult. The baraisa explicitly distinguishes children supported at another's table, and the Maggid Mishneh derives that if the father's own daughter is supported at his table and he injures her, he is exempt, while the earlier Rambam passage must refer to one not supported at his table and only to excess earnings. The Tur's reading of the Rosh is likewise very difficult, for the baraisa expressly says that even when others injure them there is a distinction based on support. The Rosh's language is best read as saying "if she is supported," not "even if she is supported," the latter word being a scribal error. His continuation, "and if she is not supported," then follows naturally. Thus there is no substantive dispute between the Rambam and Rosh. If she is not supported by the father, excess compensation for loss of work belongs to the father whether another person or the father caused the injury, subject to the details of the Gemara. If she is supported by him and another injures her, the payment for loss of work belongs to her; if the father injures her, he is exempt regarding that component, as with his sons in seif 7. I will now explain their language in detail: the opening rule regarding depreciation follows Rabbi Yochanan; the rule regarding loss of work is conceded even by Rav and is stated in the baraisa; pain, medical expenses, and humiliation are different because Rabbi Yochanan disputed Rav only regarding depreciation; and the rule concerning a father's injury of his daughter likewise concerns loss of work. The gloss stating "specifically," etc., refers to a daughter not supported at his table, as the Maggid Mishneh explains.
(Collected note) And some say, etc., and some say, etc. The Tur presents these as differing readings of the Rambam and Rosh, but the correct interpretation is as explained above, and there is no actual dispute in the essential law. (End of note.)
