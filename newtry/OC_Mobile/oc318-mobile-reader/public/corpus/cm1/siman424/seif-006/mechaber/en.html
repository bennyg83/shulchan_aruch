If one injures another person's minor daughter, compensation for damage that reduces her sale value belongs to her father, as does compensation for lost time, because her earnings and sale price belong to him. Some say this applies only while she is supported at his table; if she is not supported by him, the compensation belongs to her. Others say that even while she is supported by him, compensation for injuries inflicted by others belongs to her. Compensation for pain, medical expenses, and humiliation belongs to her, as does bodily damage that does not reduce her sale value. A father who injures his own daughter must pay her pain, medical expenses, and humiliation. Rama: It appears that this applies only where she is not supported at his table. If she is supported by him, some say he is exempt, as explained below.
