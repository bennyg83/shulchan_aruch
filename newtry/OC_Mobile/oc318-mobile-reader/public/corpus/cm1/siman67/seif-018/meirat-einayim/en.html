A prozbul is not cancelled. This is taught in the Mishnah in tractate Shevi'it: When Hillel saw that people refrained from lending to the poor and thereby violated the Torah's warning, 'Beware lest there be a base thought in your heart,' he instituted the prozbul. Rashi explains that 'proz' denotes an enactment and 'bul' denotes the wealthy, meaning that Hillel enacted a protection for lenders so that they would not violate the Torah by refusing to lend. The Gemara explains that although by law the Sabbatical year cancels the debt, the Sages had power to enact that it not be cancelled because property declared ownerless by beit din is ownerless. Since the effectiveness of a prozbul rests on the power of beit din to declare property ownerless, they also said that a prozbul may be written only before an important beit din that has authority to exercise such power, as the Mechaber concludes.
