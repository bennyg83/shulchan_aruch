Pruzbul. Rambam holds that pruzbul works only when Shemitah itself is rabbinic, not when Shemitah operates by Torah law. Raavad disagrees, and Rashi also appears to hold that according to Rava's answer, pruzbul can work even against Torah Shemitah through the power of hefker beit din hefker. According to what I wrote above in answering Ritva's question why lenders could not simply stipulate that Shemitah should not cancel the debt, Rambam's position is understandable. If Shemitah is rabbinic, Chazal can strengthen their enactment beyond ordinary Torah monetary conditions in common situations. A blanket stipulation by every lender could therefore be ineffective, making a special institution like pruzbul necessary. But if Shemitah is of Torah force, then the general rule applies that a condition concerning monetary rights is valid. The creditor can stipulate that the debtor will not invoke the cancellation. In that setting, why would Hillel need to institute pruzbul at all? There is also a proof from the beginning of Makkot. Tosafot reject Rashi's interpretation that the Mishnah can be established through pruzbul and instead explain that the lender delivered his documents to beit din directly. If pruzbul were effective even under Torah Shemitah, why did Tosafot not simply say the lender had made a pruzbul, as the Yerushalmi does? The answer is that the Mishnah of false witnesses states a general Torah law and must apply even when Shemitah is fully Torah-level. Direct delivery of the documents to beit din prevents cancellation by Torah law; pruzbul, according to Tosafot and Rambam, does not. This strongly supports Rambam. Raavad's position is difficult for another reason. Even if Hillel theoretically had authority through hefker beit din to neutralize Torah Shemitah, historically Hillel lived during the Second Temple period, when according to Rambam Shemitah was rabbinic. Why should we assume Hillel enacted a mechanism for some future Messianic era when the full Jubilee and Torah Shemitah would return? Rambam, as is his practice, codifies future Torah law and therefore says that in such a restored Torah Shemitah, Hillel's pruzbul would not operate. Perhaps Raavad follows Ramban, who holds Shemitah remains Torah law even today. Then Hillel necessarily enacted pruzbul against Torah Shemitah and the dispute is coherent. Tosafot Gittin 36 also fit Rambam. Their discussion indicates that if direct delivery of notes to beit din is a Torah mechanism, Rava's answer of hefker beit din does not mean Chazal generally created pruzbul against Torah Shemitah. One might ask from Tosafot Yevamot 89, who say Chazal can sometimes uproot Torah law actively where they establish something resembling an existing Torah mechanism. Since pruzbul resembles direct judicial delivery, perhaps they could do so here. But that would explain only their legal power, not why Hillel actually chose to legislate for a period in which Torah Shemitah was not operative. Therefore Rambam's restriction is the more natural practical understanding.
