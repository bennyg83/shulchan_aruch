This is the text of the prozbul, etc. These words are the lender's declaration, which he states orally before the judges. The judges then write the matter in a formula such as: 'We, three sitting together as one court, were present when so-and-so the lender came and said before us: I transfer to you, so-and-so, etc.' All three then sign below as judges, each signing his name and the title 'judge.' Alternatively, they may sign as witnesses, meaning that they attest that he made this declaration before them when they were sitting together as a court of three. The Tur gives this as his first explanation and afterward records another reading and formula. See there.
And what is written, 'I transfer.' The word does not mean that he physically transfers the promissory notes, because a prozbul does not require delivery of the documents, as explained in seif 11. Rather, it means, 'I transfer these matters to you, the beit din.' According to this, Rama did not need to add that the same rule applies to oral debts, for there was never any basis to distinguish between oral and written debts once the Mechaber did not state that he was speaking specifically of written promissory notes.
