I transfer to you. This does not mean physically transferring the debt documents. If the documents themselves are delivered to beit din, Torah law already prevents shemittah from cancelling them. A prozbul is used where only the legal authority to collect is transferred through words.
And the judges, etc. Its formula is: so and so came before us and said, "You, so and so and so and so, I transfer to you all the debts I have, so that I may collect them whenever I wish," and the judges sign.
Or the witnesses. This wording implies that three are required because the prozbul functions through beit din. Although regarding cancellation of a get, where the Mishnah likewise says that it is cancelled before a beit din, we rule that two suffice, there the beit din merely establishes a fact. Here the power of beit din must operate to remove the borrower's monetary right, so three are required. This is how one must explain the Tur. Most poskim, however, disagree and hold that just as two suffice for cancellation of a get, two also suffice for prozbul. This is the correct practical law.
The same applies that he may, etc. Sema writes that Rama stated this unnecessarily because it is obvious once physical transfer of the documents to beit din is not required.
