The Sabbatical year cancels a loan. This is derived from the verse, 'That which is yours with your brother, your hand shall release,' meaning that your loan which is in your brother's possession is to be released. This does not apply when you hold a pledge from your brother. Even if the property is in your brother's hands, if it is not there as a loan but as a deposit, the Sabbatical year does not cancel it, because wherever a deposit is located it remains in the possession of its owner and is not called 'with your brother' in the sense of a debt. Nor does the owner need to press him for repayment.

That he designated the field for his loan. This means that he defined the boundaries of the field and told him, 'From this field you shall collect your debt.' The field is then more specifically designated for the debt and is regarded as though already collected. See the Ran in the chapter HaShole'ach, where he cites Ramban as holding that if the borrower merely designated a field, the Sabbatical year cancels the debt even more readily than in the case of a mortgage where the lender actually possesses the field, because designation alone is merely an apotiki, effective only if the borrower does not pay. The Ran concludes, however, that according to Rambam one must say the reverse: a mortgage is more readily subject to cancellation than a specifically designated field, because in the latter case the borrower has singled out that field for the loan. This is also the Mechaber's view, following Rambam. Therefore, under the first opinion, a designated field is not cancelled at all, for that is Rambam's position in Hilchot Shemittah, chapter 9. Even the dissenting opinion cited afterward is more lenient with a designated field than with the mortgage discussed above and holds that if the borrower is unable to remove the lender except for even one day, the debt is not cancelled, unlike the aforementioned mortgage.
