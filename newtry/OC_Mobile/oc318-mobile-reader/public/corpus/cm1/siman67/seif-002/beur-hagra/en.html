(Collected note) And a mortgage in a place... And if... This is Rambam's language in chapter 7 of Hilchos Malveh. It implies specifically until the end of its term. The phrase "at any time" excludes the end of the term. This is also the Rosh's view there, unlike Rashba and the Shulchan Aruch, which state "and whenever..." See the Rosh there, seif 35, Yoreh Deah siman 172, and Beit Yosef there. (End of note.)
(Collected note) In a place... and a place... This is Rambam's language in chapter 7 of Hilchos Malveh. The Rif in Bava Metzia writes likewise. It appears that his view follows the Rosh there. In the first clause he says "at any time" because in such a locale Shemittah cancels the debt whenever the Sabbatical year arrives, unlike the opinion below that any mortgage that cannot be redeemed does not cancel. This is also the ruling below in siman 278, seif 7. (End of note.)
(Collected note) And a place... Until the end of its term. Specifically in such a case. So writes his teacher, the Riem, in Bava Batra 125b, as explained in siman 278, seif 7. See Ri Migash there at length. Rabbeinu Ephraim and Ramban also write this plainly. (End of note.)
(Collected note) Except that he concluded... See Beer HaGolah. This also appears from the Yerushalmi, which concludes in agreement with our Gemara that even a document containing a warranty clause is canceled. Rabbi Yirmiyah says this is where the borrower has no land, implying that if he has land it is not canceled. Rabbi Yose responds that even if he has land it is canceled, and his view accords with Rav, who states that when specific land was designated for repayment, Shemittah does not cancel the debt. They said this only where he designated land; otherwise it is canceled. Thus, although Rabbi Yose holds that an ordinary warranty does not prevent cancellation, specific designation does. (End of note.)
(Collected note) Some say... This follows the objection raised against Rambam: why should this differ from a mortgage? That baraisa follows Beis Shammai, as explained there. Sefer HaTerumos therefore explains Rambam as speaking in this same type of case. (End of note.)
(Collected note) Whenever he cannot... Rashba writes in a responsum: in a place where the borrower may redeem the mortgage, Shemittah cancels it. When is that? If it is still within the term, it is difficult, because the lender cannot press him for payment and it resembles a ten-year loan. If after the term, then even in a place where the borrower ordinarily cannot redeem before the term, he can redeem after the term. We must therefore say that whenever the lender cannot be forced to release the mortgage even for a single moment, Shemittah never cancels it. Beit Yose cites this in Yoreh Deah 172 and writes that Maggid Mishneh and Rabbeinu Yerucham say likewise in Rashba's name. The Rosh and Tur there disagree, as does Rabbeinu Yerucham in the name of the Rif and Rivash in the name of Ramban. See Beit Yose there. The Rosh answers Rashba's question by deriving that in a place where redemption is permitted, the lender may compel, etc. See there, seif 35. The first opinion here is the Rosh's view. Thus the phrase "until the end of its term" is deliberate; the first clause says "at any time" because it seeks to include whenever Shemittah arrives, while the second says "until..." (End of note.)
