Store credit, etc. The Beit Yosef writes that if one sells an item to another in an ordinary sale, as soon as he sells it the unpaid purchase price is like a loan and the Sabbatical year cancels it. Store credit is different because merchants commonly allow a debt to remain outstanding for a year or two and collect only later, without pressing the customer in the interim. It is therefore like a loan whose due date was fixed for after the Sabbatical year, which is not cancelled. The same applies to wages, because a worker commonly leaves his wages with the employer for a year or two without pressing him for payment, so the money remains there as a deposit or loan until after the Sabbatical year. The Ir Shushan gives different reasons and omits these explanations, but they do not appear correct to me.
