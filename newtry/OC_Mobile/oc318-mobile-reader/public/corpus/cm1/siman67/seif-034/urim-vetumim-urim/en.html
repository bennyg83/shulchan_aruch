He is believed by virtue of a migo, etc. The reason a migo is required, and we do not say that he is also believed because of the presumption that he would not abandon such a safeguard, is explained by the Sema: that presumption alone is insufficient, for perhaps the borrower never accepted the lender's condition. This does not fully answer the difficulty concerning shop credit accounts. See Tumim for what I wrote there. I therefore wrote either that, regarding the claim, "I had a prozbul and it was lost," there is an additional consideration arising from the period beginning with the time of danger, or that since the borrower expressly contradicts him, we do not rely on the presumption alone. See there, where I wrote that the lender may in any event be required to swear.
