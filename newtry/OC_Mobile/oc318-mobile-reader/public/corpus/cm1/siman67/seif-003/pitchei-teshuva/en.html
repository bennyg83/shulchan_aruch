And some say that any person likewise, etc. See Bach, who concludes that in practice one should follow the Rambam, that this leniency applies specifically to a Torah scholar, as the Beit Yosef testified was the custom in Egypt. See Responsa Chatam Sofer, Choshen Mishpat, siman 113, who wrote to a questioner that he had received his letter containing a formula by which the questioner purported to transfer his debts before the local beit din. The poskim derive from the scholars of Rav Ashi’s academy, who would transfer their debts orally to one another, that Torah scholars who know that shemittah today is rabbinic may suffice with an oral declaration before beit din and the judges need not write the prozbul, as the Rambam rules. This implies that fundamentally a mere notification to beit din that one transfers one’s debts is insufficient; rather, the written prozbul itself effects the exemption. Nowadays, however, Torah scholars are treated leniently and an oral declaration before beit din suffices without writing the document. If so, the declaration itself removes the prohibition of shemittah, and it may therefore need to be spoken specifically before beit din. The spoken declaration is the operative act, comparable to chalitzah, where writing ‘I do not wish to take her’ does not substitute for the required statement, as Tosafot explain in Gittin 71a. Even where writing can sometimes stand in place of speech, that would be only if the person stood before beit din and wrote his words there so that the writing functions like his statement. The questioner, however, wrote his declaration at home. At that time it was not before beit din and had no effect, and when the writing later arrived before beit din it obviously could not itself effect the transfer; it merely informed the judges of his wish to make a prozbul. The judges therefore must write an actual prozbul, and that prozbul will remove the shemittah prohibition. They may date it only from the date of the original letter written in the questioner’s city, because a later-dated prozbul is invalid. Chatam Sofer then provides a formula substantially as follows: ‘We, the three judges sitting together, received a writing known to us, the undersigned beit din, and we clearly recognize the handwriting and signature of the person signed below. The writing stated: I transfer to you, the judges of such and such a place, every debt owed to me so that I may collect it whenever I wish, signed by so and so. Since it has been established before us that this is so, we have authenticated and confirmed it properly, and this prozbul shall have full force through Monday, the twenty-fourth of Menachem, the date on which the above writing was written. We therefore sign here in the holy community of such and such in the year 5565, which is a shemittah year.’ See there.
