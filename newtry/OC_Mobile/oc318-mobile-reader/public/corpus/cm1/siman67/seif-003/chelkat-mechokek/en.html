Even if she has witnesses. Tosafot explain that the husband does not rely upon the witnesses and may think they say she is a virgin merely to enhance her status. Rambam writes that even if she is in fact a virgin, because a married woman is ordinarily presumed to have had relations, the Sages established for her only one hundred and she is treated as one who had relations for all purposes. The Gemara in Ketubot 12a, which implies that if the husband did not have relations immediately after kiddushin and later finds no signs of virginity he has a claim concerning virginity, means that he may come before beit din to investigate whether she committed adultery after becoming his wife. He cannot, however, reduce her ketubah below one hundred, because we are witnesses that he married her under the presumption that she had previously had relations. It is as though she had stipulated with him: even if you do not find me a virgin, you will still give me one hundred. See Maggid Mishneh and Bach.
