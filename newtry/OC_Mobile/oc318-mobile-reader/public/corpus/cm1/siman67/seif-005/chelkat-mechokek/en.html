And she was found to have had relations, she does not receive even one maneh. Darkei Moshe writes that all the more so she does not receive the additional ketubah amount, and Hagahot Alfasi states the same. Their words are difficult. Ketubot 100b teaches that a girl who performs mi'un, a woman prohibited as a secondary relation, and an aylonit have no principal ketubah. Shmuel explains on 101a that this means only the one hundred or two hundred, but they still receive the additional amount. A baraita says likewise: women whom the Sages described as having no ketubah, such as one who performs mi'un, have no one hundred or two hundred but do have the addition; women whom the Sages said must leave without a ketubah, such as one who violates religious practice, lose even the addition, and certainly the principal amount. Thus the hierarchy is the reverse: loss of the principal does not automatically imply loss of the addition. Indeed, below in siman 116:1, one whom he married without knowing of a prohibition may have no principal ketubah but still have the addition. Why then should a woman married under the presumption that she was a virgin and found not to be one automatically lose the addition? Rav Sheshet in Ketubot 11b initially suggests that such a case is not a complete mistaken transaction and that she receives one maneh. Although Rava disagrees and treats it as a complete mistaken transaction, why should she be worse than a widow to a Kohen Gadol whom he married without recognizing the prohibition, which is likewise a mistaken transaction yet she still has the addition? Nevertheless, below in siman 68:1, based on Rambam, it appears that where there is proof, or she admits that she deceived him, she loses even the addition. We must therefore distinguish deliberate deception here from a case of a prohibited marriage in siman 117 where he simply failed to know the legal status. Below in siman 68, seif katan 13, I give a sound reason and proof for this distinction.
