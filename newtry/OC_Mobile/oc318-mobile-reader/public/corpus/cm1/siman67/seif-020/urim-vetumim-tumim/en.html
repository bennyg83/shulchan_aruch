For he may say it even when they are not present, and so forth. This rule requires study. The poskim debate whether pruzbul requires a particularly expert or distinguished beit din. If the creditor could simply stand anywhere and declare, "I transfer my debts to such-and-such beit din," without the judges being present, then it is difficult to understand why this requirement would ever create a real obstacle. Surely Jewish communities throughout the Diaspora would always contain some suitable beit din to name. R. Yechiel, cited by Mordechai, therefore holds that the declaration must actually be made before the beit din. Mordechai argues from the Yerushalmi, which says the relevant items may even be "placed in Rome." But Ran in Perek HaSholeach appears to understand that statement as referring to the debt documents themselves: even if the creditor's notes are physically in Rome, he can stand before beit din and declare, "I transfer them to you." Physical production of the papers is unnecessary. The Yerushalmi's wording "placed" fits that reading very well. Accordingly, it does not prove that the beit din itself may be absent. The present lenient ruling therefore requires further study and should not be treated as clearly established against R. Yechiel and the plain structure of the institution.
