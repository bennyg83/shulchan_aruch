As long as he can collect the debt, a prozbul may be written. The Rosh in the chapter HaShole'ach and the Tur write that although the Sabbatical year cancels debts only at its conclusion, a prozbul may nevertheless be written only until the end of the sixth year. Yet even the Rosh agrees that the lender may not demand his debt throughout the seventh year. His statement that the debt is cancelled only at the end means only that if the borrower pays voluntarily during the seventh year, the lender may accept the payment. Rambam, however, holds that throughout the seventh year the lender may still sue for the debt in beit din until the year's end. Thus everyone agrees with the general rule stated here, namely, that as long as the lender can collect the debt through the judges, a prozbul may be written. Their dispute is only how long the debt remains judicially collectible. So writes Darkei Moshe, seif 32.
