It is released. This condition is directed at Shemittah itself, stipulating that Shemittah should not release the debt, and is therefore a condition against what the Torah commands. This differs from stipulating with the borrower that 'he will not release himself from paying,' meaning that the borrower will not exempt himself from payment of this debt even in Shemittah. That condition is directed not at Shemittah but at the borrower, as though he says: although Shemittah releases the debt and you may no longer demand it from me, I undertake personally to pay you. A person can obligate himself in money that the Torah did not otherwise obligate him to pay.

One can explain the distinction as follows: since by force of the mitzvah of Shemittah the debt is already released and removed from the lender, he cannot extract it merely because of a customary document formulation. The borrower can say that the lender intentionally omitted the customary clause in order to fulfill the mitzvah of Shemittah.
