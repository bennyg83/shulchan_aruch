If a lender stipulates with the borrower that the seventh year shall not cancel the debt, it is nevertheless canceled. If he stipulates that the borrower himself shall not cancel this debt, even in the seventh year, the condition is valid, because the borrower has imposed upon himself a monetary obligation the Torah did not impose. {Rama: Likewise, if a document describes the money as a deposit, Shemittah does not cancel it, because that wording was used for this purpose. If local documents ordinarily contain such wording but one document omits it, it is not treated as though written, although customary omitted clauses are supplied in other matters, as stated above in siman 42.}
