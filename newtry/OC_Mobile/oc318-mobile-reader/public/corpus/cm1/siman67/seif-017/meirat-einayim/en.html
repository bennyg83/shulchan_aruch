If she impaired it, etc. The remainder then leaves the category of an ordinary ketubah obligation and has the status of a loan.
One who comes by virtue of a non-Jew has the status of the non-Jew, etc., and Shemittah does not release it. Although this principle benefits him, and Rama rules similarly below in siman 154, seif 18, one can say that even the Mechaber, who there limits the principle 'one who comes by virtue of a non-Jew is like the non-Jew' to a disadvantage, would concede here. The reason is that documents of non-Jews are enforceable under dina d'malchusa, as explained in siman 45, seif 7, where the Mechaber himself likewise applies the principle beneficially. See what I write there further.
Therefore, one who purchased, etc., Shemittah does not release it. Rashba concludes there that if he later established the amount as an ordinary loan debt, Shemittah does release it.
But otherwise, etc. Meaning, he does not sue on the original non-Jewish document, but claims reimbursement because he had to pay on the other's behalf. That reimbursement became an ordinary loan, which Shemittah releases. This is explicit there.
