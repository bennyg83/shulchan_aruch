Rosh in Perek HaSholeach cites the Tosefta: "This is the matter of the release: something that Shemitah cancels, it also cancels the oath attached to it; something that Shemitah does not cancel, it does not cancel its oath." Knesset HaGedolah cites Maharit asking why Rosh needed the Tosefta when the Mishnah already says Shemitah cancels an oath. I do not understand the question. Rosh cites the Tosefta to teach that only an oath attached to a debt cancelled by Shemitah is cancelled. A loan is cancelled, and the oath attached to that loan falls with it. A deposit held by another person is not cancelled by Shemitah. Therefore the custodian's oath is likewise not cancelled. The Mishnah merely states generally that Shemitah cancels an oath. It does not spell out this distinction between loan-oaths and custodian-oaths. The Tosefta therefore teaches an important limitation, exactly as Rosh intended.
