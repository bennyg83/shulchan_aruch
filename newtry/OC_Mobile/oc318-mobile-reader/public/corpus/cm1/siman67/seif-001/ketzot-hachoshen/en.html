A reason for the custom. Rosh explains that the custom not to cancel debts in Shemittah can stand because once the custom became universal and everyone knew it, each loan is treated as though the lender had stipulated that this particular debt would not be canceled by Shemittah. I still find difficulty during the seventh year itself. Although cancellation occurs only at the end of Shemittah, once the seventh year begins beit din does not ordinarily enforce collection and the lender himself may not press the borrower, as Rosh writes in Perek HaSholeach. That restriction is not merely monetary waiver. It is a prohibition of collection. A private monetary stipulation cannot waive an independent prohibition. See siman 26, where a kinyan that creates no actual monetary right is treated as a kinyan on words. The custom therefore still requires explanation for the seventh year before the actual cancellation takes effect.
