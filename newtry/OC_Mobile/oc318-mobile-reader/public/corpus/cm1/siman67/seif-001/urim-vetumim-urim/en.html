When the Jubilee is observed. Scripture says, "This is the matter of the remission." The verse speaks of two remissions. One is remission of debts, which occurs in every shemittah year. The other is remission of land, namely the Jubilee, when all sold ancestral fields return to their original owners. Whenever the Jubilee, the remission of land, is observed, remission of debts in shemittah applies throughout the world, because it is a personal obligation. But when the Jubilee is not observed because all the inhabitants are not dwelling in Eretz Yisrael, remission of debts likewise does not apply by Torah law.
And by rabbinic law, etc. Why did the Sages not also institute a rabbinic Jubilee as a remembrance of the Jubilee? Tosafos write that if they had done so, people would be unable to work the land in both the forty ninth and fiftieth years, while the Torah's miraculous blessing in the forty eighth year sufficient for four years would not apply to a rabbinic observance. This answer is insufficient according to those who rule like R' Yehudah, who holds that the fiftieth year counts both as the Jubilee and as the first year of the next cycle. According to him, shemittah and Jubilee would be consecutive only in the first fifty year cycle, when shemittah falls in year forty nine and Jubilee in year fifty. Thereafter such consecutive years almost never occur. Calculate and see. Sema explains that because Jubilee applies only in Eretz Yisrael the Sages did not institute it, whereas remission of debts applies everywhere. This is strained. It appears better to say that since the agricultural aspect of shemittah remained in force in some form, the Sages also instituted remission of debts so that the mitzvah should not be observed only in part. Jubilee, however, had entirely ceased, so the Sages did not institute it. One may also say that remission of debts is passive, requiring only refraining from collection. The Sages were willing to cause monetary loss through inaction, as Abaye explains in Gittin 36b. Jubilee, by contrast, requires affirmative action. A person already in possession of his field would have to return it to its ancestral owner, and the Sages did not institute such an affirmative transfer of property.

The principal calculation is that the year 320, etc. See Tumim, where I discussed at length the basis and origin of this calculation. I will give you a short rule for always determining the year of the shemittah cycle. Take the number of years from Creation. For every complete hundred, retain only two years, and combine those retained years with the remaining years. Then divide the result by seven. A remainder of zero marks a shemittah year. For example, in the present year, 5539, take the five thousand five hundred years and disregard the full hundreds except for two years from each hundred. This yields one hundred ten. Disregard one hundred again, leaving twelve, then add the remaining nine years. The total is twenty one, divisible evenly by seven. Thus this year is indeed a shemittah year.
