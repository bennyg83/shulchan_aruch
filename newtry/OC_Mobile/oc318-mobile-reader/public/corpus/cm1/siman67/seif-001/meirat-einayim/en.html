Release of debts does not apply, etc. In parashat Re'eh it is written: 'At the end of seven years you shall make a release. And this is the matter of the release: every creditor shall release what he has lent his fellow; he shall not press his fellow or his brother, because a release for Hashem has been proclaimed. The foreigner you may press, but what is yours with your brother your hand shall release.' From the phrase, 'this is the matter of the release, every creditor shall release,' Chazal derive that the verse speaks of two releases: release of land, namely the Jubilee when fields return to their owners, and release of debts, when the lender's loan is released. The two are linked to teach that when release of land, meaning Jubilee, does not apply, namely when not all the inhabitants are in Eretz Yisrael, as stated concerning Jubilee, 'proclaim liberty to all its inhabitants,' release of debts likewise does not apply by Torah law.
And by rabbinic law it should, etc. The reason for the rabbinic enactment is so that the Torah of Shemittah should not be forgotten from Israel. But plowing and planting were not rabbinically prohibited in Shemittah and Jubilee merely so the institution not be forgotten, because most of the public could not endure a prohibition against working the land for two successive years, Shemittah and Jubilee. So write Tosafot in chapter HaShole'ach, Gittin 36. Only when Shemittah and Jubilee applied by Torah law was the blessing fulfilled that the sixth year would produce for three years. In my humble opinion another reason may be suggested. Since sanctity of the land depends specifically on Eretz Yisrael and has no application outside the Land, once there is nothing corresponding that can be practiced outside Eretz Yisrael, Chazal did not institute it in Eretz Yisrael either. Release of debts, however, is a personal obligation and can apply everywhere. See Rashi there and Perishah here.

A reason for the custom, etc. Responsa Rosh also gives a reason why he left them with their custom: since the practice not to release debts had spread and everyone knew it, it was as though the lender had stipulated that the debt not be released in the Sabbatical year.
The primary calculation is that the year 5320, etc. This follows Rambam's calculation. According to his reckoning, the year 5089 was a Shemittah year in the days of the Tur, 5264 in the days of Rabbi Levi ibn Chaviv, 5306 and 5313 in the days of Rabbi Yosef Karo, and 5327 in the days of Rama, all consistent with Rambam's calculation. See Derishah for Rambam's reasoning. From this calculation one can determine that in our time the Shemittah year was 5369 and will again, speedily in our days, be 5376.
