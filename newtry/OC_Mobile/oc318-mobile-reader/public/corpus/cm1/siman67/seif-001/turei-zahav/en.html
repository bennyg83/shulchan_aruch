(Seif 2: Some say that in a place where redemption is not permitted, etc.) The Sema understood this opinion as applying only to specifically designated property, so that inability to redeem for one day suffices there, whereas an ordinary mortgage must remain nonredeemable until the end of its term. The Levush appears to agree. This is incorrect. The source is the Rashba, who discusses even an expressly designated apotiki and then proves his distinction from a mortgage. He states that Shemittah cancels even an express apotiki unless local practice prevents redemption, and that inability to redeem even for one day is enough. Thus designated property and mortgage follow one rule. The Rashba also agrees with the Raavad that temporary inability to redeem constitutes “not redeemable,” unlike the Rosh. This dissenting opinion therefore differs from the first in two respects: it applies the redeemability distinction even to designated property, and it treats even one day of nonredeemability as sufficient. The Mechaber first states the undisputed case where redemption is possible at all times, then records the dispute over what qualifies as nonredeemable. The Sema’s attempted reconciliation is unnecessary.
