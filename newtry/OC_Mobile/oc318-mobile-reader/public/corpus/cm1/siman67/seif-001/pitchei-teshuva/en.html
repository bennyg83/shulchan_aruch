The reason for the custom. See Baer Heitev and Responsa Mishkenot Yaakov, siman 31, who gives another reason and seeks to justify the Jewish custom. See Levush, seif 1, who writes that although most poskim agree that cancellation of debts in the Sabbatical year does not apply today by Torah law, the Sages nevertheless ordained that shemittat kesafim continue as a remembrance of the Sabbatical year. In seif 9 he writes that our custom of paying no attention at all to shemittah is an exceedingly great leniency, involving transgression of a rabbinic law for which death at the hands of Heaven is mentioned, and he strongly criticizes reliance on it. He notes that Maharik, responsum 92, and Terumat HaDeshen sought ways to uphold the custom, but in his view their arguments are very strained and do not accord with the halachah, so that upon examining their words one finds no sound basis for reliance. See also Be'er HaGolah, note 8, in the name of the Shelah and the Bach. Tumim likewise discusses this at length in words of ethical admonition and urges stringency, particularly because the Ramban holds that the law remains of Torah force even today. One who fears and trembles at the word of Hashem should therefore certainly take his view into account. See there.
