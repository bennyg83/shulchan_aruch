Not at the time of the loan. The Tur states: Likewise, if he took a pledge from him, not at the time of the loan but through beit din, and took utensils from him, even though he must return them to the borrower, the Sabbatical year does not cancel the debt. This differs from a pledge taken directly at the time of the loan, which need not be returned. The meaning is that a utensil used by day must be returned by day and a utensil used by night must be returned by night, pursuant to the Torah law requiring return of a pledge, as explained by the Tur later in siman 97. The reason the debt is not cancelled is that once he takes the pledge through beit din he acquires a proprietary interest in it against his debt until payment. When he returns it at the time the borrower needs it, he is effectively lending his own property back to him. The proof is that the borrower must return a daytime utensil to the lender at night.
