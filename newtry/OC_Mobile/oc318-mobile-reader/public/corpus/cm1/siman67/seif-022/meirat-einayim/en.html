A prozbul is written only where there is land. Rashi explains that Hillel instituted a prozbul only for an ordinary loan, and an ordinary loan is generally extended to someone who has land upon which the lender relies for collection. Others explain that where the borrower owns land, the debt is regarded more as though already collected and held by beit din. According to both reasons, any amount of land suffices. According to those authorities who hold that land is never subject to ona'ah, its precise value is irrelevant. According to the other authorities, the reason is that the same parcel can be collected, returned, and collected again until the debt is fully satisfied. See Perishah.
Even a perforated flowerpot. Through its holes it draws nourishment from and grows by means of the ground, and is therefore considered land.
And someone owes him, etc. Meaning, someone owes the borrower. That debt is encumbered to the present lender under Rabbi Natan's rule, as the Tur explains below in siman 86.
Even without his presence. Although one ordinarily cannot impose a liability on another person in his absence, where the legal form is one of acquisition or benefit to that person, even if a disadvantage will ultimately result from that benefit we classify it according to its initial legal form as an acquisition. This is because release of debts is treated leniently, particularly nowadays.
