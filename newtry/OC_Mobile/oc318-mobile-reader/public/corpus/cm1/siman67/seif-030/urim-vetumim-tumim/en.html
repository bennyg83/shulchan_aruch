His version of the Tosefta said that throughout the seventh year the creditor may not press the borrower, although if the borrower pays voluntarily the creditor need not say, "I release you." Beit din likewise does not collect the debt during the year. Only at the end of Shemitah does the debt itself become fully cancelled, at which point voluntary payment requires the creditor to say, "I release you." Accordingly, Rosh holds that one should not write a pruzbul during the seventh year itself. The prohibition of pressing has already begun. Radbaz, as cited by Knesset HaGedolah and Bach, tries to compromise. He says a debt made before Shemitah cannot receive a pruzbul during the seventh year, in accordance with Rosh. But if the loan itself was made during the seventh year, Rambam's rule applies and a pruzbul can still be written before the end of the year. This avoids a difficulty in Sanhedrin concerning a note dated Nisan of the Shemitah year. If a loan made during Shemitah could never receive a pruzbul, later dating might improperly improve the creditor's rights. But I do not see the basis for this compromise. If the Torah's command, as Rosh explains it, is that throughout the seventh year one may not press one's fellow, why should it matter whether the loan was created before the year or during it? Once the loan exists during Shemitah, the prohibition of pressing attaches. Bach brings proof from the Mishnah of one who slaughtered a cow and divided it on Rosh Hashanah, where if the month was intercalated the debt is cancelled. That proof is not decisive. Rosh agrees that the actual debt cancellation occurs only at the end of the year. His point is merely that during the year the creditor cannot press and cannot create a pruzbul. The cow case therefore does not itself resolve when pruzbul may be written. One might derive support for Rosh from the Yerushalmi's question: "Is the debt even collectible on Yom Tov?" If the only restriction were Yom Tov, the question is straightforward. But under Rosh, a Shemitah-year debt is also not ordinarily collectible during the year. Still, that is not a contradiction. The Shemitah prohibition itself is the beginning of the Torah's divestment of the creditor's power. A debt temporarily noncollectible because of Yom Tov is different: it resembles a loan whose maturity lies after Shemitah, because the inability to collect arises from an external legal condition rather than from Shemitah itself. Rosh and Baal HaIttur do have a strong proof from Sanhedrin. The Gemara says that a note dated Nisan of the Shemitah year is unusual because people do not commonly lend during Shemitah. Rashi explains that lenders fear the debt will be cancelled. Why is a Nisan loan materially different from a loan made the preceding Elul? In either case the lender has months before the end of Shemitah to sue, obtain judgment, or prepare a pruzbul. Especially pruzbul is an easy device. According to Rosh the difference is clear: once the seventh year begins, the creditor cannot press and cannot write pruzbul. Lenders therefore avoid lending during the year. According to Rashi and the authorities who permit pruzbul during Shemitah, this Gemara is much harder. Perhaps Rashi would say that pruzbul requires land. A borrower might sell his land immediately, refuse a land grant made on his behalf, and leave too little time for formal litigation before the end of the year. An ordinary loan also has an initial thirty-day period, and court processes can extend further. Thus lenders may still be reluctant to lend late in Shemitah. This is possible but strained. Ibn Ezra, in his Torah commentary, explains "at the end of seven years" in a way that can be read as the beginning of the seventh year. He should not be understood as denying the accepted Mishnah that actual cancellation occurs at the end. Rather, he can be read like Rosh: the royal divestment begins with the prohibition of pressing during the seventh year and is completed at its end. Because the issue is significant and Rosh has substantial support, a God-fearing person should act stringently and prepare a pruzbul at the end of the sixth year, thereby satisfying all views.
