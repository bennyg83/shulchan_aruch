One who delivers his documents to beit din, etc. This is not the same as the prozbul discussed below in seif 18. There he does not physically hand the promissory notes to the judges, but orally declares before them that he transfers the debts into their hands, which is why a written prozbul is needed. Here he physically deposits the debt instruments with the judges, and therefore no writing is needed. Tosafot explain this in chapter HaShole'ach. According to what the Tur and Rama write below in seif 20, that any person can make the declaration orally, one must say that by Torah law Shemittah always releases the debt unless the physical documents are delivered to beit din. Hillel then instituted the prozbul so that oral delivery alone, without physically handing over the documents, should be effective. In Derishah there I wrote that the Gemara and Rosh, together with a reconciliation of the Tur, imply that Hillel instituted a written prozbul for his generation, while Rav Nachman later instituted that an oral declaration without a written prozbul suffices. This is what Rama means in his gloss to seif 20, where he says that some hold that any person may state the matter orally.
