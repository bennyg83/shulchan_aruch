He is not believed. A person is not believed by his own admission when that admission causes loss to others, as the Tur and Shulchan Aruch write in siman 77. Sema. The Bach asks that he should be believed through a migo, since he could have waived the debt. He answers that this is indeed so, and the case is only one in which, by force of the particular claim, he is not believed, such as where he sold the debt through ma'amad sheloshtan and therefore cannot waive it, as stated in siman 66, seif 29. According to what I wrote there, however, that even where he sold it through ma'amad sheloshtan he may waive it, the same is true here: the seller is believed in every case, except that he must reimburse the purchaser under the law of indirect damage for whatever loss he caused him, namely the full value the document would have had if there had been a prozbul. In siman 86, seif 5, I wrote that where the lender himself says the document was one of accommodation, he cannot waive it. Thus there is no need for the distinction made here between a loan and a sold debt. Shach.
He is believed. The Shach challenges all of this and rules that even if the seller has assets he is not believed. Since he sold the document and it left his possession, he has no power by his own admission to cause a loss to the purchaser. See there.
