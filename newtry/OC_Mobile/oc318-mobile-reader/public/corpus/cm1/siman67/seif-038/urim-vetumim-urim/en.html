He is not believed. Even though he has a migo that he could have waived the debt, see Bach and Shach. There is no difficulty, because the presumption that a person would not abandon such a safeguard is a strong presumption, and we do not say a migo against a presumption.
And the seller must pay the purchaser. See Tumim. It appears from the language of the Tur and Baal HaTerumot that he pays only the money the purchaser gave for it. Although the purchaser thereby suffers a loss, this was a defective purchase, since he should not have bought a debt instrument that had passed through Shemittah without a prozbul. See there.
