We advance a claim for the purchaser. Knesset HaGedolah asks why the Tur does not say that we advance for the purchaser the claim that the borrower and lender had stipulated that Shemittah would not cancel the debt. The answer is that a direct condition, "Shemittah shall not cancel this debt," is ineffective as a condition against what the Torah states. Only a formulation such as, "You shall not cancel this debt against me," is effective, because it imposes a new personal monetary obligation on the borrower. Rambam and the Shulchan Aruch explain this in seif 9. If that condition was only oral and was not written into the debt instrument, it created only a separate oral obligation. The purchaser of the document acquires only the written debt through the assignment of the instrument and its liens. He does not acquire a separate oral debt except through the special three party transfer. Therefore advancing that oral condition would not help the purchaser. By contrast, if we advance the claim that a prozbul existed, the original written debt itself remains fully valid and the purchaser benefits directly. This is why the Tur relies on the prozbul claim.
