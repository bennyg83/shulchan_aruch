If he claimed, etc. This is not an admission concerning a different category from the claim. The claimant asserts that the defendant owes money because of damage by this ox; they dispute only the amount. The rule that one who claims wheat and receives an admission of barley is exempt rests on waiver, jest, or a similar rationale, and applies here only to exempt him even from the lesser animal. This is specifically where the claimant says, 'you know,' as explained above. Otherwise no oath is required. If the damager says he is uncertain, he is not one who cannot swear and must pay. Rambam derives this from the Mishna's statement that the burden of proof rests on the claimant. Raavad explains that the oath concerns whether an innocuous or forewarned ox caused the damage. The Mishna's exemption concerns a case where the defendant says he is uncertain. Maggid Mishneh's explanation is mistaken, as Shach also notes.
