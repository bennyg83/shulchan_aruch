A Torah oath on the remainder. There is another difficulty. A deposit that remains intact is ordinarily treated as "here it is," and partial admission of a deposit can be *heilach*, exempting from the Torah oath, because the object remains in the owner's legal domain. An innocuous ox is similarly the very property from which the injured party collects, and under Rabbi Akiva the ox is ultimately awarded to him. Why is an admission concerning part of that ox not likewise *heilach*? The Shach answers that the ox still requires appraisal. I would explain more precisely that at the moment of admission the claimant's share is not yet identified. Until appraisal we do not know what precise portion of the ox corresponds to the damage. Nimukei Yosef makes a similar distinction where a garment is claimed but no specific portion is identified: because the defendant may satisfy the obligation from whichever portion will ultimately be measured, the admission is not the equivalent of handing over a specified object. My brother, Rabbi Yehudah Kohen, added another reason. The rationale of *heilach* is that the admitted money is treated as though it had already been paid before the claim. A fine, however, does not become legally due before a claim is brought before beit din and witnesses establish liability. It therefore cannot be treated as though the admitted part had already been delivered before the claim. This is a sound additional explanation.
