And if he seized, etc. The Gemara there. Although one who admits a fine is exempt, seizure nevertheless helps even in the first case of one large and one small animal, as the Maggid Mishneh correctly writes. This is unlike Nimukei Yosef, who establishes the sugya according to the view that half damages are monetary rather than a fine. The Rambam does not hold so, as I explained above, for he establishes the sugya according to the view that half damages are a fine. The Shach follows Nimukei Yosef and challenges the Maggid Mishneh from the case of Rabban Gamliel. His challenge is sound, but the Rambam's reason is that the exemption for admitting a fine applies only when he admits the essential event. Where witnesses establish the essential event, his admission regarding the remaining detail is not treated as an admission to a fine. This is why seif 3 says, "if he claimed," etc., "and swore," etc. The wording indicates that the rule applies even when both animals were tam. The Raavad's reading that this refers only to the latter clause does not fit the Rambam's language. This also explains why the Gemara challenges the latter clause from Rabbah bar Nasan, because otherwise it would already be a case of admitting a fine when there is no clear proof that both animals caused the damage. Tosafos' question at the beginning of chapter 5 can likewise be answered because he did not say, "you certainly know."
