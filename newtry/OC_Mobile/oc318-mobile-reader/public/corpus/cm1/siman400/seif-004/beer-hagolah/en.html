nun) Ibid., in the Gemara.
samekh) The Maggid Mishneh writes that the Rambam cannot mean that there is no clear evidence which of the oxen caused the damage but there are witnesses that one of them did, for then why would he say the defendant is exempt? Above he ruled in a comparable case that the damaging party pays, because whenever there are witnesses the case is unlike one who claims wheat and receives an admission concerning barley. Rather, the Rambam must mean that there are no witnesses at all. The owner of a tam is exempt because one who admits a penalty is exempt, and where one ox is tam and one is forewarned the case resembles a claim of wheat with an admission of barley.
ayin) I do not understand the Rama's intent, for these are the Rambam's words copied exactly. The phrase 'some distinguish' is merely the Maggid Mishneh's explanation of the Rambam, as stated above. The Sema also notes this.
peh) Ibid., in the Gemara. The Maggid Mishneh writes that this certainly refers to both oxen being tam, as is explicit in the Gemara. Although one who admits a penalty is exempt, the Raavad explained that half damages differ from other penalties, because if the injured party seized payment it is not removed from him. This is the Rambam's view. The laws of this clause and the preceding one are parallel. Above, the Rambam discusses witnesses who establish liability even though the claim concerned only one of the oxen, all the more so where the claim concerns both. Here he discusses a case with no witnesses at all, where the defendant is exempt under the wheat-and-barley principle even though the claim concerns both, all the more so above where the claim concerned only one. Other commentators disagree.
tzadi) Tur, seif 5, in the name of his father the Rosh, as explained in the Rosh's rulings.
