If there were two damaged animals, one large and one small, and two damaging animals, one large and one small, and the injured party claims that the large animal damaged the large and the small damaged the small, while the owner of the damaging animals claims that the small damaged the large and the large damaged the small, the burden of proof rests upon the claimant. The same applies where one damaging animal was innocuous and one forewarned, and the injured party claims that the forewarned animal damaged the large and the innocuous animal the small, while the other party claims the reverse. If the claimant brings no clear proof, the alleged damager is exempt, because this resembles a claim for wheat met by an admission concerning barley, in which the defendant takes a rabbinic oath and is exempt even from the value of the barley. Rama: Some distinguish where witnesses testify that one of the animals certainly caused the damage but do not know which one. In that case the owner pays according to his own version, and this is not treated as a claim for wheat met by an admission concerning barley. The Mechaber follows the first opinion, treating it as an admission to a different claim. If the injured party seized property, he collects for the small damaged animal from the large damaging animal and for the large damaged animal from the small damaging animal, in accordance with the damager's admission. This is so even if the seizure occurred before witnesses. He does not collect according to his own claim, only according to the other party's admission. Seizure helps only if made before coming to beit din; after litigation begins, seizure is ineffective even to collect according to the damager's account.
