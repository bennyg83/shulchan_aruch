[Seif 3] And he takes a Torah oath. The Sema cites the Maggid Mishneh's understanding, but it appears to me that this is not the proper account of the dispute between the Rambam and the Tur. They disagree about why one who was claimed against for wheat and admitted barley is exempt. The Rosh and Tur hold that the claimant is treated as having admitted that the defendant does not owe him barley, and a litigant's admission is like one hundred witnesses, as stated in siman 88. Consequently, even witnesses cannot revive the barley claim. For the same reason possession does not help there, as I wrote in siman 88. The Rambam, however, holds that the defendant's statement concerning barley is not a clear admission. It is treated as a dismissive response, like saying, “I was merely putting you off,” as I wrote there in the name of the Raavad, and the Ran likewise cites this in the name of the Ramah. Therefore, whenever witnesses establish the barley debt, the defendant is liable on the strength of their testimony, and we need not rely on his own admission.
