An ox pursuing another ox, two oxen pursuing, or two being pursued. It contains four seifim.
If one ox pursued another and the pursued ox was found injured, and the injured party says, 'Your ox caused the damage,' while the other owner says, 'No, it struck a rock,' the burden of proof rests upon the claimant. This is so even where the injured party states his claim with certainty and the alleged damager is uncertain. If the injured party says, 'You know that your ox caused the damage,' the owner must take a rabbinic oath that he does not know. This applies where the ox was forewarned. If it was innocuous, he is exempt even from that oath, because had he admitted liability he would still have been exempt from payment on his own admission, since half-damages for an innocuous ox are a penalty.
