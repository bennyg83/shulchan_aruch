Even if the injured party makes a definite claim and the tortfeasor is uncertain. The Rambam and the Tur and Mechaber imply that this applies even to a forewarned animal. Maharshal rules that with a forewarned animal that pursued another, where the injured party claims definitely and the tortfeasor is uncertain, the tortfeasor is liable, relying on Rav Huna and Rav Yehudah. His ruling is incorrect. Since we rule against Rav Huna and Rav Yehudah and leave money in its present possession, the same rule applies in every monetary case, including a forewarned animal.
And if the injured party claims: You know, etc. If the claimant asserts that the defendant certainly knows, the ordinary rules of oath apply. But where the claimant himself concedes that the defendant was not present and therefore does not know, there is no basis for imposing an oath merely because the claim is definite.
He takes a heset oath that he does not know, etc. This is the proper oath where the claim creates only rabbinic oath liability and the defendant denies knowledge. The burden of proof remains on the injured party.
