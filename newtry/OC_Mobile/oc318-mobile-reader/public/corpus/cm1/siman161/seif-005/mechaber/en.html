If one partner in a courtyard wishes to keep an animal or a mill there, or to raise chickens there, the other may prevent him. The same applies to any activity that local residents do not ordinarily conduct in their courtyards. With respect to all such matters, the partners may prevent one another, except for laundering, because Jewish women do not ordinarily expose themselves to humiliation by laundering at the riverbank. Accordingly, in a place where men do the laundering, so that this reason does not apply, the others may object. Even with respect to women, the permission applies only when they launder within their own four cubits and there is no slope carrying the water into another partner's portion. If there is such a slope, and certainly if one wishes to launder within another's four cubits, the others may object.
