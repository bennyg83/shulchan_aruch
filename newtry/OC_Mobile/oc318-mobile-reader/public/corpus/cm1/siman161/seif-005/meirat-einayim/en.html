Because it is not the way of daughters, etc. Therefore, even if the local custom is for daughters to wash clothing at the river, a person who wishes to depart from that practice and says, "I do not want my daughter to demean herself at the river," is legally entitled to do so. We do not follow such an inferior custom. Otherwise the ruling would be difficult, because in other matters, if the local practice is to perform an activity in the courtyard, one cannot object. The preceding language, "and similarly other matters that people do not ordinarily do," implies that where it is ordinarily done, custom governs. This is straightforward.
