Sema, seif katan 4, "in Ir Shushan." Note: in my opinion, the Sema's reasoning is difficult. If so, even in a city where four or five houses stand near the wall, with one slightly closer than another, we should say that all are considered one house, yet we have never heard such a rule. The reasoning of the Ir Shushan can, however, be explained well. In a courtyard, where the concern is only thieves from the city who seek money, either the thieves prevail, in which case all residents face monetary danger, or the residents prevail or reach a settlement, in which case even the property of the person closest to the entry will be saved or restored. In a city, by contrast, the concern includes danger to life. If the person living closest to the wall is killed, then even if the townspeople later prevail, they cannot restore a life.
