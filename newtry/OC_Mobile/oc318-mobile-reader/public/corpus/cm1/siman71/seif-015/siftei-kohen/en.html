When the borrower instructs, etc. The Sema explains that beit din need not have independent proof that the borrower expressly instructed the witnesses. Where local scribes do not insert a credibility clause without the borrower's instruction, the appearance of the clause in a note creates a presumption that he instructed them to write and sign it.
It appears to me that this is only when he said so at the time of the loan, etc. When a written note contains a credibility clause, we presume either that the borrower agreed to it at the time of the loan or that a kinyan was made regarding it. If it is clearly known that he agreed only after the loan and no kinyan was made, the clause is ineffective.
As explained in this siman, seif 2. The Bach sharply objects that seif 2 speaks only of credibility as two witnesses, whereas the present case concerns ordinary credibility. But Rama is referring to the Mechaber's formulation in seif 2, and the Mechaber himself does not distinguish between the two forms. Nevertheless, as explained above, the correct halachic conclusion is to distinguish: ordinary credibility granted after the loan does not require a kinyan, while empowerment as two witnesses does.
