Tumim sought to prove that a written undertaking is equivalent to a kinyan from Tosafot in Ketubot 87, where Tosafot ask concerning the Gemara's question, "How could she have known that she would be appointed an administrator? Perhaps he stipulated with her only after she became an administrator," and Tumim asks that after the marriage is comparable, with respect to the ketubah, to a stipulation made after the loan, which is ineffective without a kinyan. I do not understand his argument. Certainly, if the stipulation was made when the money was delivered to her for purposes of administration, and she refused to accept the money except on condition that he would not be able to impose an oath upon her, there is no clearer case of a stipulation made at the time money is delivered. It is immaterial that the ketubah obligation already preceded it, for she accepted the money that made her an administrator only on this condition. The principal rule therefore appears to follow the view of the Rama. Since a clause of ne'emanut, credibility, is comparable to qualifying a relative as a witness, as when one says, "My father is trusted by me," as explained by the Tur in siman 22, it appears there that a kinyan is specifically required, while a written clause alone is ineffective without a kinyan.
