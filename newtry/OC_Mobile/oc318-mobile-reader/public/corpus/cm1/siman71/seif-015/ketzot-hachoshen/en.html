It appears to me. The Mechaber's language implies that a credibility clause written in the document can be effective even when added after the loan. Although the Mechaber elsewhere requires a kinyan when credibility is granted after the loan, writing the clause into a formal document can itself function like a kinyan, because a person can create a new obligation through a document even where he previously owed nothing. This is unlike a bare oral credibility clause, which is only words. Ramban holds that a kinyan not to retract is a kinyan on words unless the party obligates himself to perform the outcome of the ruling or testimony. If so, a document saying merely, "The creditor shall be believed," might likewise be only words. Yet Nimukei Yosef rules concerning acceptance of a disqualified judge that kinyan can strengthen even a verbal acceptance, and he compares this to neighboring rights and mortgages. The Mechaber evidently follows that approach. Therefore a credibility clause written in a formal debt instrument is effective even if added after the loan, while Rama may require an actual language of obligation, such as, "I obligate myself to pay if the creditor says I have not paid."
