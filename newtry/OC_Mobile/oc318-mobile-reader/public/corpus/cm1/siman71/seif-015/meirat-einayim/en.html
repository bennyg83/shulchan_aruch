When the borrower instructs, etc. It does not appear that this means that beit din must know independently that the borrower so instructed the witnesses. Rather, the same applies when a document containing a credibility clause is presented before them in a place where such a clause is not customarily written without the borrower's instruction. We presume that he instructed them to write and sign it, for otherwise the scribe would not have written it and the witnesses would not have signed it. Here the purpose is only to teach that the credibility clause is effective without a kinyan, and that people do not ordinarily write an express kinyan concerning such credibility. The concern would otherwise be that the witnesses might say they did not write it at the borrower's instruction but merely because of local custom. Therefore, in a place where it is not customary to write it, and there is no such concern, a credibility clause written at the borrower's instruction is effective without a kinyan. See the Perishah. Rama's gloss, 'It appears that he said so at the time of the loan,' comes only to exclude a case in which it is known that he did not say so at the time of the loan, in which case it is ineffective.
