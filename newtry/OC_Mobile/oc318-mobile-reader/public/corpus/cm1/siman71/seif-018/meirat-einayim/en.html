Containing ordinary credibility. Necessarily 'ordinary credibility' here is not literal. If so, the borrower trusted the lender only against himself and the lender could not collect from the heirs except with an oath, as explained in the preceding seif and as is clear in the Gemara, in the Tur here, below in siman 108, seif 13, and in the Mechaber there. A person cannot bequeath to his children money that he himself could have collected only through an oath. Rather, the 'ordinary credibility' mentioned here means that the borrower trusted the lender against himself and his heirs. It is called 'ordinary' because he did not also trust the lender's heirs against himself and his heirs or those deriving rights from them, which would be considered express, comprehensive credibility, as the Mechaber writes immediately afterward. This terminology is somewhat strained.
The correct reading is: 'If the document contains express credibility exempting him and those who derive rights from him, against the borrower and those who derive rights from him, they collect without any oath.'
