Even if he went out, etc. Baal HaTerumos and the Tur write that whenever beit din imposes a cherem, the litigant need not answer amen. Even if he is in the depths of the sea, the cherem applies to him. The Rama likewise rules in Yoreh Deah, siman 228, seif 33, that the cherem applies even if the person did not hear it at all. As for the ruling of the Tur and Mechaber below, siman 87, seif 22, that he answers amen, the Sema explains that there the person is present and beit din is directly administering the oath to him.
See below, siman 16, seif 3. Some say that a cherem is imposed to uncover testimony. The Sema attempts to reconcile the rulings by distinguishing a definite claim that witnesses exist from an uncertain claim. This is strained. In siman 16 the subject is a cherem imposed to locate testimony, because beit din has a duty to investigate and bring the true judgment to light. Here the cherem is imposed upon whoever owes money to the claimant, comparable to the procedure with orphans where they are told to impose a curse in their home upon whoever stole from them. This is why the Rama refers specifically to the opinion that a cherem may be imposed to uncover testimony. The Bach likewise extends this to theft and similar cases, and the Sema below in siman 267 supports that conclusion.
