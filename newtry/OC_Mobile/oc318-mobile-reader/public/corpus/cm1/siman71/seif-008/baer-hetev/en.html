Takes effect. The Tur concludes that any cherem imposed by beit din takes effect without the litigant answering amen. Even if he were in the depths of the sea, the cherem would apply to him. Rama rules similarly in Yoreh Deah 228:33. When the Tur and Shulchan Aruch state in siman 87, seif 22 that the person administering the oath should have him answer amen, that refers to a case where he is present and the oath is administered to him before beit din. Sema.
Person. The Tur and Shulchan Aruch make a similar statement there. To avoid contradiction, the Sema explains that there the claimant says with certainty that he has witnesses but does not know who they are, whereas here he does not make a definite claim. Therefore, in the present case a written notice is issued only for orphans. On that reading, the Rama should not have written "some say," since no opposing authority is found. See siman 28, seif 2. The Shach answers that there the cherem is imposed to discover testimony, which is a duty of beit din in order to clarify the truth, whereas here the cherem is imposed generally upon whoever owes the claimant money. This is why the Rama specifically refers to "testimony." The Bach indicates that in cases of theft and similar matters they also imposed such a cherem, and the Sema in siman 267, seif katan 4 likewise indicates this.
