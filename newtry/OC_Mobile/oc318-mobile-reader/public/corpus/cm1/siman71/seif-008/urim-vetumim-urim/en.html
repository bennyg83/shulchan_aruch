That they grant it to every person. Sema distinguishes another case where a litigant makes a definite claim that he has witnesses. Here the party himself asserts only uncertainty, perhaps the deceased left property or rights somewhere, and therefore the court takes special action chiefly for orphans. The Shach distinguishes testimony, which is directly subject to beit din's authority, from property held privately by another person, where there is no automatic judicial duty to search. His distinction is correct, with an additional point. In testimony there is no humiliation in calling for witnesses. If people know relevant testimony, it is normal that beit din needs them. Here, however, issuing a general ban suggests that Jews are suspected of wrongfully retaining another person's money until beit din threatens them. That is degrading. Therefore the Sages used this extraordinary measure chiefly to protect orphans. Other distinctions are also possible. See what I wrote above in simanim 15 and 28.
