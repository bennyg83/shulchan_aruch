We do not write a writ of excommunication, etc. The Tur, in the name of Rav Hai Gaon, writes: 'We are not accustomed to give permission to anyone who comes and says, Write me a writ of curses and authorize me to impose an excommunication publicly. Rather, we say to him: If you are bringing a claim against a particular person, then there is a judicial proceeding between you. If not, entrust your judgment concerning the one who robbed you to God in your own home and curse him as you wish.' End quote. This language indicates to me that merely because of doubt one may not bring before beit din a number of persons whom he suspects and impose a general excommunication upon them, unless he has a definite claim against his fellow. From the Beit Yosef, however, it appears that he had a different textual version, on the basis of which he wrote that if one suspects several persons that one of them is the robber, he may bring all of them before beit din and impose a general excommunication. The Ir Shushan likewise writes this. See the Perishah.
And even if he has gone out, etc. This is also the language of the Tur. The word 'and even' with a vav is to be understood as 'that even' with a dalet. The Tur concludes there that with respect to every excommunication imposed by beit din, the litigant need not answer Amen. Even if he is in the depths of the sea, the excommunication takes effect upon him. The Tur and Mechaber write in siman 87, seif 22, that the person being adjured answers Amen, because there he is present and the other party administers the oath to him in beit din. See what I wrote there further on this matter.
For an excommunication is granted to every person. The Tur and Mechaber also write this there. To avoid a contradiction between their rulings, since here they write that a writ of curses is written specifically for orphans, we must say that what they wrote in siman 16 applies specifically where one claims, 'I certainly have witnesses, but I do not know in whose possession they are,' as is stated there explicitly. Here, however, the matter concerns one who does not make a definite assertion. Accordingly, Rama should not have written this as a 'some say' opinion, since I have not found anyone who disputes it. See below, siman 28, seif 2.
