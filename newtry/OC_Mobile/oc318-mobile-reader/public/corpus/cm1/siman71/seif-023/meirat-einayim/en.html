And if they were partners in the debt, etc. Beit din descends to his property, etc. In the Perishah I explained that this applies specifically where they are partners in the debt. Since the debt belongs to both of them, what the borrower gave to one of them was given at least somewhat within his authority. Therefore the borrower is discharged, and beit din proceeds against the property of the recipient and pays his fellow his share. If they were not partners, however, the other creditor collects his share from the borrower, and the borrower then recovers from the recipient the excess that he gave him. A practical difference arises where the borrower's property is of average quality while the recipient's is inferior, or where one of them is easy and the other difficult to collect from.
