Within his power. Responsa Maharanach 21 understands that if it is clear to us that the borrower paid the other creditor, that payment exempts him. The clause saying that whoever produces the document may collect does not mean that payment to the other creditor is ineffective, but only that the borrower cannot insist on paying each creditor only his own share. The Shach says this is not compelled. The clause may mean that because the producer of the document was expressly trusted, the borrower subjected himself to that producer and cannot say that payment to the other creditor counts as payment while the document remains in the producer's hands.
Partners. The Sema says this applies specifically when the creditors are partners, for then money paid to one of them is at least partly paid within the partnership's authority. If they are not partners, the producing creditor collects his own share from the borrower, and the borrower then recovers from the other creditor whatever excess he paid him. A practical difference arises where the borrower's property is of medium quality and the recipient's property is inferior, or where one person is easier to collect from than the other. The Shach finds the distinction strained. Since even when they are partners in the debt, if the creditor who admitted payment has no property his admission cannot prejudice his colleague, yet if he has property beit din first collects from his assets, the same logic should apply when they are not partners. The Shach therefore explains that the Tur and Shulchan Aruch mean only that when the creditors are partners and the admitting creditor lacks assets, the other creditor collects only his own portion; if they were not partners in the debt, the other creditor could collect the entire debt. The Taz writes similarly. See siman 93, seif 14.
