But not outside his presence. For in his presence he cannot brazenly lie, and for that reason the grant of credibility is effective. Outside his presence this consideration does not apply, and therefore an oath is required. See Tumim, where I note that many authorities disagree, and I left the matter requiring study.
And if he specified. If the document states that the lender and his authorized representatives are believed, and the borrower claims, "I did not repay the lender, but I repaid you, his representatives," the Sema, seif katan 33, holds that the credibility clause does not help and they must swear. The Shach and Taz disagree and hold that the credibility clause applies even in this case. Their view is correct.
