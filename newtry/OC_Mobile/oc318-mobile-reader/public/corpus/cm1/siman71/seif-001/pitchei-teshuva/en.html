The Shach rejects this. See Responsa Rabbi Akiva Eiger, siman 137, where the questioner wished to resolve from this the doubt of the author of Shoshanat HaAmakim, rule 11, concerning two against two in a case where there is a majority. The question is whether, although chazakah does not resolve two against two, a majority, which is stronger than chazakah, might resolve it. Here, however, it appears that two against two concerning repayment of a document are not resolved by the principle, 'Why is your document in my possession?' which is stronger than an ordinary majority because money may be extracted on its basis. All the more so, then, an ordinary majority should not help. Rabbi Akiva Eiger answers that the cases are not comparable. Although the presumption arising from possession of the document is stronger than an ordinary majority, testimony itself is also stronger than a majority. The presumption here functions as an evidentiary proof, as though 'we ourselves are witnesses,' and thus effectively constitutes an additional set of testimony. We then apply the rule that two witnesses are equivalent to one hundred. By contrast, where there is a majority, one can say that two against two create a doubt, and in a case of doubt the law directs us to follow the majority. This is especially so according to what was proven in the preceding siman, cited above in siman 34, seif 28, that in monetary law we restore the matter to the Torah law. If so, one would otherwise have to ask why we do not set two witnesses against two and then preserve the document through the presumption, 'Why is your document in my possession?' Necessarily, the answer is that this presumption itself has the force of witness proof and is therefore like an additional set of witnesses. Accordingly, no proof can be brought from here regarding a majority. This is the substance of his response. See there, and see what I write below in siman 78, seif 1, seif katan 1.
