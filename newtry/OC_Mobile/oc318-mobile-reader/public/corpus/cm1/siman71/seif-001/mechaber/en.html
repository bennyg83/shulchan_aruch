A borrower who grants the lender oral trustworthiness, and what effect that trustworthiness has. This siman contains 23 seifim.
If the lender stipulates with the borrower that he shall be believed whenever he says that the borrower has not repaid him, the lender collects without an oath even if the borrower claims that he paid, and this applies even to an oral loan. Rama: This applies specifically where witnesses know that the borrower granted him this trustworthiness. If there are no witnesses and the borrower admits that he trusted him but says he later paid, the borrower is believed through a migo. See above, siman 69, seif 2. If the borrower produces witnesses that he paid, however, the lender collects nothing. If he stipulated that the lender be believed like two witnesses, then even if the borrower produces witnesses that he paid, the lender collects without an oath, even if he produces one hundred witnesses, because two witnesses have the same legal force as one hundred. Rama: Even if the borrower has a migo and could have exempted himself with another claim, he is not believed through that migo against the express trustworthiness he granted the lender. But if the borrower said, "You are believed against me like three witnesses," since he specified a number, if the borrower pays before four witnesses the debt is considered paid.
