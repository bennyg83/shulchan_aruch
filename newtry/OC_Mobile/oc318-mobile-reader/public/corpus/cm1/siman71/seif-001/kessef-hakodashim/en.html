After the loan. Credibility equivalent to two witnesses ordinarily requires a kinyan sudar. If he wrote such a clause into a document after the loan, perhaps it is effective even without a kinyan, because a person can obligate himself through a written instrument without a kinyan. On the other hand, because the obligation is not definite, it may resemble a guaranty assumed after the loan, which requires a kinyan and is not established merely through writing because of the concern of asmachta. The Shach, seif katan 7, appears to imply that writing alone is not enough unless it was at the time of the loan or accompanied by a kinyan. If, however, he writes that a particular witness will be believed, or that a written statement from him will establish that the debt remains unpaid, this is not full credibility but rather resembles saying, 'Do not repay me except before two witnesses,' which is effective after the loan without a kinyan. Although the borrower himself wrote it, it is not merely empty words, since presumably he did so with the lender's consent, all the more so where it is incorporated into a formal document.
