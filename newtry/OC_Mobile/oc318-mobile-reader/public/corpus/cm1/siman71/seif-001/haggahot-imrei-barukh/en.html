(Netivot HaMishpat, seif katan 9) And this is very astonishing. Note: There is no difficulty. One may explain, as the Tumim does, that he agreed that the money should become a gift if the other party denied the debt and swore falsely, similar to Rav's rule in Bava Kamma 106a regarding one who denies a deposit and later witnesses come. But regarding the imposition of a ban mentioned in seif 4, this explanation cannot work, because he imposes the ban precisely so that the other remains under the prohibition until he returns the money. So long as he has not returned it, the ban remains, and there is therefore no gift at all.
