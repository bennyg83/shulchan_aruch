Even though the instrument contains ne'emanut. See Shach, seif katan 29, who cites a responsum of the Rosh. The facts there were that after Shimon sued Yehudah, claiming that he had given him fifty gold coins to deliver to Reuven in payment of Shimon's debt, Shimon still lacked authorization from Reuven. Yehudah then acted cleverly and obtained a power of attorney from Reuven so as to exempt himself from the oath by relying on the ne'emanut clause that required payment to Reuven's authorized representative. The Rosh ruled that Yehudah remained obligated to take the oath that had already become incumbent upon him before he obtained the authorization. Reuven himself, however, certainly need not swear that Yehudah did not deliver the money to him, even if Yehudah also says that he delivered it to Reuven. See what I wrote above in siman 58, seif katan 8, because Shimon had granted Reuven ne'emanut. The Shach then writes that even if Shimon claimed that he paid Yehudah after Yehudah had become Reuven's authorized representative, Reuven would collect without an oath. The meaning is that Reuven need not swear that Yehudah did not deliver the money to him, because of the ne'emanut granted to him. Yehudah, however, must swear, as the Sema writes in seif katan 32. There the Sema states that if the borrower claims, “I paid you,” the recipients must swear, and the same applies here. Although Shach later, in seif katan 39, disagrees with the Sema there, that disagreement concerns an oath required before collection. Here Reuven collects his debt, and Yehudah afterward takes a hesset oath. On this all agree, for even a lender who was granted ne'emanut may be required to take a hesset oath after collection. All the more so Yehudah, for vis-a-vis Yehudah the case is legally comparable to one after payment.
