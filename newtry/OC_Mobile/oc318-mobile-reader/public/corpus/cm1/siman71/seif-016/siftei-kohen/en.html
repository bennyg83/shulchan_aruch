Even though the due date passed, etc. The Sema explains that this remains true even long after maturity, because otherwise the continued possession of the note would be inexplicable. He reasons that ordinary credibility was written primarily to prevent a claim of payment after maturity, since a person is presumed not to pay early. This would imply, however, that credibility as two witnesses is effective only during the term, which is difficult. The better explanation is that the clause presumptively remains effective for as long as the creditor holds the note. This is indicated by the Tur and Baal HaTerumot, who say that whenever the note emerges from the creditor's hand, he is believed without an oath.
