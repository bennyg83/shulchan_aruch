That has passed. The Sema writes that this applies even long after the due date, because otherwise the lender's possession of the document would itself show that the debt was unpaid. Indeed, if the clause applied only until the due date, it would be unnecessary, because an ordinary credibility clause is written so that the borrower cannot claim payment after the due date, whereas within the term there is already a presumption that a person does not pay early. It is strained to say the clause was intended only for the single day on which the debt falls due. The Shach comments that according to this reasoning, credibility like two witnesses would help only within the term. It therefore appears more correct that ordinary language implies for as long as the lender possesses the document. His teacher likewise writes.
