And he comes to collect from his heirs, it does not help. The reason is that although their father himself could not have claimed, 'I paid,' we advance for the heirs the claim that their father paid. Had the borrower been alive, the lender would not have dared deny payment in his presence after the borrower actually paid. Therefore the borrower trusted him against himself. He did not trust him against his heirs, because he feared that the lender might brazenly deny before them the payment their father made.
Unless he expressly exempted him as against himself and his heirs. It appears that even if the borrower wrote such language, it helps only so that the lender need not swear when the heirs merely say, 'Perhaps our father paid you.' But if the heirs say, 'We ourselves paid you,' that credibility does not cover their own payment. Even if the clause explicitly states that the lender is believed even if the borrower's heirs claim, 'We paid you,' it is doubtful whether the father's acceptance can bind them in that respect. At first glance it does not, because once the heirs say they themselves paid the debt, their father had no power to weaken their rights and take their money. Likewise, if the borrower trusted the lender and the lender's heirs, that means only that the lender's heirs need not swear that their father never told them the debt was paid or that they found no document or writing showing payment. But if the borrower or his heirs claim, 'We paid you,' the lender's heirs must swear. This is also implied in Even HaEzer, siman 98, which sets out the laws of credibility in a ketubah, from which the creditor rules are learned. It states only that the woman may be trusted against the husband's heirs so she need not swear that she does not retain property placed in her hands during his lifetime or that their father did not set aside funds for her, and that her heirs may be exempt from swearing that their mother never told them she had been paid. It does not say that the clause helps if the husband's heirs claim, 'We paid you or your heirs.' Understand this carefully.
