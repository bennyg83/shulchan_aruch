Unless he specified, etc. In Even HaEzer 98, Rama rules regarding a ketubah that even an express exemption from swearing against heirs is ineffective, following Razah, Rabbeinu Chananel, Rabbeinu Tam, and Rosh against Rif and Rambam. This appears inconsistent with his acceptance here of the Mechaber's rule. One may answer that in the case of a wife there is a greater concern that the husband set aside funds for her, as the Bach and other authorities explain. Nimukei Yosef, however, cites Rabbeinu Chananel and the sages of Narbonne that no credibility clause, even an express one, helps against the borrower's heirs, whether in a ketubah or an ordinary loan note. Although their separate ruling that the creditor's heirs cannot collect because one cannot bequeath an oath is rejected by most authorities where the note contains credibility, their view that a living creditor must swear against heirs may still be shared by the other authorities who invalidate credibility in a ketubah. Rivash expressly equates the two. The practical ruling therefore requires study. All this is where no kinyan was made. If a kinyan was made, the credibility is effective according to all opinions, even for a ketubah.
Who exempts him and his heirs. The Sema suggests that such wording exempts the creditor only when the borrower's heirs say, “Perhaps our father paid you,” but not when they say, “We ourselves paid you.” Likewise, empowering the creditor and his heirs would exempt the creditor's heirs only from the oath that their father did not tell them of payment, but not from an oath where the borrower says he paid them directly. This is not correct. Baal HaTerumot discusses precisely such clauses and shows that broad exemption from every oath includes both a claim that payment was made to the deceased creditor and a claim that payment was made to the heirs themselves. Indeed, once the heirs are exempt from the special heirs' oath, they are certainly exempt from an oath that they themselves were paid. Baal HaTerumot rejects the narrower reading, and Baal HaItur and the Bach likewise discuss only whether an ordinary clause includes the special heirs' oath. There is no basis to require an oath merely because the alleged payment was made directly to the heirs. Had the Sema seen these sources, he would have retracted.
His heirs are included, etc. If the clause says “those who come by my authority,” Ramah and the Tur disagree whether heirs are included. The Bach rules like Ramah that heirs are not included, and the Rivash appears to incline that way.
