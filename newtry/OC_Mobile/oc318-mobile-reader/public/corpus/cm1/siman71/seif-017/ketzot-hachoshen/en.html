And from his heirs. Authorities asked that the Tur in Even HaEzer rules that a waiver of oath does not help against heirs. In my view there is no contradiction. The Tur already rules that credibility granted after a loan requires kinyan. Therefore valid credibility in an ordinary loan exists only through kinyan or at the time of the loan, which is itself like kinyan, and that stronger undertaking is effective even against heirs. The ketubah rule is different. Ritva explains that Abba Shaul's rule, that a waiver of oath does not bind the heirs, applies where there was no kinyan concerning the heirs' oath. Even if the waiver was written at the time of marriage, when many marital obligations are acquired through mere agreement, we do not assume that the husband fully intended to uproot the rabbinic enactment requiring an oath from one collecting from orphans. But where an actual kinyan was made, the waiver is effective even against heirs. Thus in marriage one can distinguish between credibility effective against the husband through the special force of marital agreement and credibility against heirs, which requires kinyan. In an ordinary loan, by contrast, there is no valid after the fact credibility without kinyan in the first place. Once validly created, it therefore extends to heirs as well.
