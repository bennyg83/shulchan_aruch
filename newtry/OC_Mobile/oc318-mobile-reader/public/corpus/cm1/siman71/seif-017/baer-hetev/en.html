From his heirs. Although their father himself could not have claimed payment, beit din makes that claim for his heirs. If he had been alive, the lender might not have dared deny payment to his face, and for that reason the borrower granted the lender ne'emanut against himself, not against his heirs, before whom the lender may be more willing to deny payment. Sema. This also somewhat resolves the question raised in siman 69, seif 5 why, according to the Rama who does not allow a claim of payment against a signed writing, collection from orphans nevertheless requires that the debt still be within its term. Even though the father could not have claimed payment, the heirs may receive such a claim on his behalf because of the concern that the lender might act more boldly against them.
Specified. The Sema suggests that if the heirs themselves claim, "We paid you," the ne'emanut clause does not help against that claim. He even questions whether an express clause saying that the lender shall be believed despite a claim by the borrower's heirs that they themselves paid can be effective, since a father may lack power to deprive his heirs of their property. The Shach strongly disagrees and notes that Baal HaTerumot and Baal HaIttur expressly state the opposite. The Taz likewise disagrees with the Sema. The Shach further notes that in Even HaEzer 98 the Rama rules regarding a ketubah that even an express exemption from oath against the heirs is ineffective, following authorities who disagree with the Rif and Rambam. He suggests that a ketubah may involve a greater concern that payment was set aside in advance. Nimukei Yosef cites Rabbeinu Chananel and all the sages of Narbonne that no form of ne'emanut, even express, helps against heirs in either a ketubah or a documented loan when collecting from the borrower's children. The matter requires study. If a kinyan was made, however, the ne'emanut is effective according to all opinions, even for a ketubah, as the Tur states in Even HaEzer.
Included. If the document says "those who come by my authority," there is a dispute between the Ramah and the Tur whether heirs are included. The Bach rules like the Ramah that heirs are not included, and the Rivash likewise inclines that way in responsum 494.
