(Supplement) Unless he specified, etc. See the end of Even HaEzer 98 in the gloss. The authorities generally imply that specification is effective. The difficult passage in Mordekhai must be understood as distinguishing writing from a ketubah created by verbal undertaking. The Yerushalmi distinguishes a creditor, whose collection is rooted in Torah law, from a ketubah, which is rabbinic. Accordingly, an oral exemption may suffice in the latter setting, and the authorities rule without qualification.
(Supplement) And if he wrote, “It shall be,” etc., his heirs, etc. See Bava Batra 115b and 159a, where an heir may claim through the ancestor. Those who come by authorization are not automatically included among heirs, because that phrase denotes an express grant of authority. Rivash 494 and Tur in the name of Ramah, although Tur disagrees; Bach rules accordingly.
