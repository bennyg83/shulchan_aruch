Before witnesses. This is where the lender claims that the payment related to another debt. If he denies ever receiving the money, he must pay, as stated in seif 1. Shach.
Forever. The Sema writes that even if the word "forever" was not written, the law should be the same, because once the borrower was granted credibility equivalent to two witnesses, it follows that he is exempt without an oath, cherem, or an oath by way of gilgul. The repetition of the word means that even after payment he is fully exempt. The Shach objects that Baal HaTerumot states explicitly that the word "forever" must be written. The Taz likewise regards that word as essential.
Effective. This applies only to ordinary ne'emanut, not where the party was granted credibility equivalent to two witnesses. The preceding statement, however, that a receipt is effective even if written elsewhere, applies even where he was granted credibility like two witnesses, provided he possesses a handwritten receipt from the other party. Sema.
Above. The Shach writes that the Rama's words require study. They imply that the law is identical to siman 70, which would permit a claim that the money was applied to another debt. Yet the Tashbetz, the source of this ruling, does not say this; he discusses only the first case, where the parties stipulated that a receipt would be valid only if written upon the original document. The Shach suggests that the Rama may mean only that the receipt is effective and exempts the borrower, as though the lender had imposed such a condition, and perhaps even more strongly. He leaves the matter requiring study and notes that in siman 70 he likewise questioned whether the Rama's ruling permits the lender to claim that payment was for another debt under the Rambam's version and the rulings of the Mechaber and Rama.
