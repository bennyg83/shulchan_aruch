(Seif 5) “A light or severe oath, and an oath by extension, forever.” The Sema says that “forever” is unnecessary. Once the creditor is believed like two witnesses, it is already clear that he is exempt from every light or severe oath and from an oath imposed by extension; repetition of the word would therefore teach that the exemption continues even after payment. For this reason, he says, the Tur omits “forever.” This is difficult, because the words “light or severe” are equally unnecessary once credibility has been granted, and even an oral creditor with such credibility is exempt from an oath by extension. In my opinion, “forever” is the decisive expression. Redundant clauses such as “without a light or severe oath” may be included merely to beautify and clarify the document or to guard against an erring court, just as documents sometimes state an acquisition that would be effective even without the clause. The Rambam and a responsum of the Rashbam explain that such surplus language proves nothing when it may have been written for documentary completeness. Only the explicit word “forever” establishes continuation after payment, as stated in Sefer HaTerumot and by the Mechaber. I have also seen a Tur in which a distinguished scholar emended the text to include “forever” at the end.
