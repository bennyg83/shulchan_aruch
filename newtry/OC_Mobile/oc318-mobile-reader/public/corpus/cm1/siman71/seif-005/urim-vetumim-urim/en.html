He is subject only to a hesset oath. The reason the lender cannot collect despite witnesses is not that by granting credibility the borrower is considered to have waived the debt, for then no oath at all should be required. Rather, it is as though the borrower disqualified those witnesses from this transaction permanently, and therefore only an oath remains. See Tumim. I concluded that the oath should not be phrased in a manner that directly contradicts the witnesses, such as, "I was not paid." Rather, the lender swears that he does not presently hold the borrower's money. We can then say that perhaps the lender has some old outstanding claim against the borrower. Nevertheless I left the matter requiring study, because immediately after such an oath the lender would become disqualified from testimony and oaths by virtue of the testimony of these two witnesses. See there, where I discussed this at length.
And by means of gilgul. This wording follows the common formula, but in truth even without it the claimant cannot impose a rolled oath, although the borrower was released only generally from oaths, in accordance with the Rosh in the name of the sages of Narbonne, as I wrote above in siman 93, seif 7, and Even HaEzer 98:4. One cannot distinguish that every rolled oath is based on an uncertain claim, like the oath of partners below and the oaths concerning guardianship, whereas here the claimant asserts certainty but cannot impose an oath only because of the credibility clause. There is no Talmudic basis for such a distinction.
Forever, etc. The Sema holds that "forever" is not literal. Rather, the additional wording exempting him from an oath, seemingly unnecessary because the lender already made him equivalent to two witnesses, teaches that the exemption continues even after payment. The Shach concludes that "forever" is specifically required, because no inference can be drawn from apparently redundant language that may merely be conventional wording. The Taz writes likewise, and this is correct.
Unless he expressly stated it. Meaning that the document states that if the repayment is not recorded upon the document itself, the borrower will not be believed. Since this is not a standard scribal formula included in all documents, its plain meaning is that repayment must actually be written upon the document. The same applies to any other wording that indicates an intention that payment be recorded on the document itself. See Tumim.
And if he brought witnesses, etc. This refers back to the case where he expressly stipulated that repayment must be written upon the document. Even so, it is like the condition, "Do not repay me except before witnesses." The practical difference is that without such an express stipulation, the lender cannot say that a witnessed payment was for another debt, whereas with the express stipulation he can make that claim. Likewise, where there is no formal beit din document, the borrower may ordinarily claim, "I had a receipt and lost it." But if he granted credibility and stipulated that a receipt must be written on the document, he must prove repayment with witnesses. See Tumim.
And he repaid before other witnesses. This means where the lender did not stipulate that he himself would be believed like two witnesses. If he did, other witnesses do not help. Sema. See Tumim.
