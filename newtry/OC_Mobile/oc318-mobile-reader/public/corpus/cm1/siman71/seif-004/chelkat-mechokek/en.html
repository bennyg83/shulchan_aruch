For he certainly is pleased with this. Tosafot in chapter Na'arah, Ketubot 48, s.v. 'and shall I not support you,' write in the name of Rabbeinu Yitzchak that the case is one in which, before he went overseas, his sons and daughters were supported from his property. Even so, we do not support them from his estate while he is absent. Otherwise, even if he became mentally incapacitated his children should not be supported if he had not previously supported them. This also appears in Sefer Agudah. Tosafot on 107a likewise write in the name of Rabbeinu Shimshon, s.v. 'what distinction,' that the case concerns children who had been dependent upon his table, and nevertheless they are not supported because he departed voluntarily. Mordechai, chapter Na'arah, initially writes that when one goes overseas, beit din supports his wife but not his children, and cites Rabbeinu Yitzchak as explaining that the final chapter establishes this as a case where he had not previously arranged, through an administrator, to support his sons and daughters; had he previously supported them, money would be given from his property because he is pleased with this. It then cites another explanation saying the opposite, that even where they had been dependent upon his table, they are not supported. One who studies the Tosafot cited above and Sefer Agudah will see that the Mordechai contains a scribal error. Initially it should read 'some explain,' and later it should read 'Rabbeinu Yitzchak explains the opposite,' in accordance with Tosafot and Rabbeinu Shimshon. Rama copied Rabbeinu Yitzchak's view as he found it in the Mordechai without noticing that Rabbeinu Yitzchak's actual conclusion is the reverse.
