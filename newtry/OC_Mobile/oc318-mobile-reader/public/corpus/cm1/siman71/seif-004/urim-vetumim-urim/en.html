From his handwriting. Against the debtor's own written acknowledgment, the lender's credibility clause does not help. What difference is there between the debtor saying orally that he paid and putting that statement in writing? See above, siman 70. The writing must be authenticated in beit din. Otherwise, according to Rabbi Sherira Gaon, it may not be authenticated from two comparison documents in some circumstances. There should also immediately be witnesses who saw the receipt in the debtor's hands. Otherwise, meanwhile he may incur obligations to other creditors, in which case he may no longer have power to issue a receipt or waive the debt where Rabbi Natan's lien attaches. Even if the receipt bears an earlier date, perhaps debtor and creditor are colluding by antedating it. Therefore the debtor should secure possession of the receipt before witnesses. Once that is done, there is no comparable concern of collusion concerning the date.
