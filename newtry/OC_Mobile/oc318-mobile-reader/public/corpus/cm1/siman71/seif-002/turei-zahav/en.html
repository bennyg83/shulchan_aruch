(See there.) "But if he brought witnesses that he repaid." This implies that ordinary creditor credibility remains effective against one witness. The Sema writes that he was asked from Responsa Rosh, rule 68, section 17, concerning a document containing ordinary credibility language, where the lender must swear against one witness. I say that the honorable rabbi's difficulty is misplaced. An even stronger difficulty could have been raised, because that responsum indicates that even explicit strong credibility language does not eliminate the oath, since it speaks of "complete credibility." In truth there is no difficulty. The one witness there testified that he heard in the butchers' market that the lender admitted to the borrower that the document had been paid. Even explicit credibility language does not overcome an admission, because admission is a separate matter and is not included in ordinary credibility language, as the Mechaber writes in seif 13. The Rosh therefore ruled that the lender must swear. This appears to me simple.
