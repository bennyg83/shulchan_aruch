He does not become bound. The Bach holds that ordinary credibility is effective in every circumstance, even without a kinyan after the loan, and this appears from several sources. Yet the Mechaber here and the Beit Yosef imply that even ordinary credibility granted after the loan requires a kinyan. The central distinction is this: empowering the lender as two witnesses after the loan creates a new monetary obligation, since even if witnesses testify to payment the debtor must pay again. Such an undertaking requires a kinyan. The Rif, Nimukei Yosef, Ramah, Rabbeinu Yerucham, Rashbat, Mordechai, Maggid Mishneh, and most authorities support this. A stipulation made at the time of the loan is effective without a separate kinyan because receipt of the money completes the undertaking. Raavyah's comparison to a waiver is unpersuasive, since the creditor is not merely waiving something already in his possession. By contrast, ordinary credibility does not create a new obligation. It is comparable to an instruction not to repay without taking the note or a receipt, and is therefore effective even after the loan without a kinyan. The Rosh, Baal HaTerumot, and the other authorities cited support this distinction. Accordingly, ordinary credibility needs no kinyan, but credibility “as two witnesses” granted after the loan does.
