And they did not make a kinyan from him. The Tur cites Ramah that a credibility clause accepted after the loan requires a kinyan. Bach says ordinary credibility is effective in every case, but the Shach correctly infers from the Mechaber and the authorities cited by Beit Yosef that even ordinary credibility after the loan requires a kinyan. Rashi's language in Shevuot does not prove otherwise. Rashi explains the case of one who tells the lender, "You are believed whenever you say I have not paid," as taking place at the time of the loan. He had to establish it then because Abaye and Rava initially understood the clause as granting credibility even against witnesses, which is a major new undertaking and certainly requires either kinyan or the consideration of the loan. According to our practical law, ordinary credibility is weaker, and one might have thought no kinyan is needed. Yet the Shach concludes that ordinary credibility too is comparable to accepting a disqualified judge and can be effective through kinyan. Ran, however, indicates that even ordinary credibility contains two disadvantages when it is accepted after the debt already exists, because the borrower both relinquishes his ordinary defense and grants procedural superiority to the creditor. Therefore the requirement of kinyan is substantial. The essential rule remains that credibility accepted at the time of the loan stands by virtue of the loan consideration; credibility added afterward requires a valid kinyan.
He is not bound. Where, after the loan, the creditor grants the borrower credibility to say that the document has been paid, the Sema leaves the matter requiring study. The Shach notes that waiver does not ordinarily require kinyan. According to what I wrote above, however, even a formulation of waiver may remain retractable until the borrower actually relies upon it, just as one who says, "My father is accepted by me as judge," may retract before final judgment in a matter of waiver. Thus, before the borrower has actually said, "I paid," the creditor may still retract from the procedural concession unless there was a binding kinyan.
