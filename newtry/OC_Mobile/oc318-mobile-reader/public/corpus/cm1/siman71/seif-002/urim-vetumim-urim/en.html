And they did not make a kinyan from him, etc. See Tumim. I concluded that according to the Mechaber's view above in siman 22, where one accepts a relative or otherwise disqualified person as a witness, even after that person testified in beit din the case is still considered before final judgment. Accordingly, here a kinyan is plainly required for an ordinary credibility clause and for credibility equivalent to two witnesses, whether the condition was oral or written in a document. According to the opinion in siman 22 that once the disqualified witness testifies in beit din the matter is considered after final judgment, then before the lender states in beit din that he has not been paid, all agree that without a kinyan the borrower may retract from any credibility clause, whether ordinary or equivalent to two witnesses, and even in a documented loan. After the lender has testified in beit din, an ordinary credibility clause distinguishes between an oral loan, which still requires a kinyan, and a documented loan, which does not. But credibility equivalent to two witnesses always requires a kinyan, even in a documented loan. Consider this carefully.

He is bound. One may ask why a kinyan is needed. Why does it not suffice that he obligates himself before witnesses, saying, "If the lender says that I did not repay him, I obligate myself to pay," when a self imposed monetary obligation made before witnesses is binding even without a kinyan, as explained above in siman 40? I already wrote there that when the obligation is not absolute but conditional upon a future negative circumstance, a kinyan is required. See there.
