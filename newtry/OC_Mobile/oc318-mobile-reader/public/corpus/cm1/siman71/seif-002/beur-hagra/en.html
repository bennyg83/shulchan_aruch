When this is effective. According to Rif in Sanhedrin, although acceptance of two disqualified persons is ineffective without formal acquisition, it becomes binding if an acquisition was made. At the time of the loan, however, no acquisition is needed, like a guarantor in the final chapter of Bava Batra. This discussion concerns a trust clause equivalent to two witnesses; ordinary trust against one witness requires no acquisition.
(Supplement) When this is effective, etc. Shach infers from Rif that formal acquisition is needed only for trust equivalent to two witnesses. That proof is not decisive because Rif discusses a post-judgment case. Before final judgment one may retract even from accepting a single disqualified person. Still, once the creditor has declared that payment was not made, the matter reaches final judgment and Shach’s rule applies.
(Supplement) Specifically, etc. Rashi likewise writes this there, and the case concerns even an ordinary clause of trust.
