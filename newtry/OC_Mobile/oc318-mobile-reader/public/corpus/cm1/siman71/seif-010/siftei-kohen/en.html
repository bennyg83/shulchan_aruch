For the credibility clause is ineffective. If the borrower says, “Swear that I did not repay you, so that my note not remain deposited without purpose,” the lender must swear even though the note contains a credibility clause; if he refuses, the note is returned to the borrower without an oath. Conversely, if the lender asks that the borrower swear hesset that he paid, the borrower must swear; if he refuses, the note is returned to the lender without an oath. If each demands that the other swear, they must compromise or the note remains deposited until Elijah comes. This follows from the Rosh's responsum. According to the authorities who hold that even with both parties' consent the finder may return the note to neither party because of possible collusion, this entire practical rule does not arise. It is therefore somewhat difficult that the Mechaber cites that view in siman 65 but here records only the Rosh's ruling.
