The credibility clause does not help. The creditor must take an oath. See Tumim. Even according to the opinion that where a debt instrument is found in a third party's hands and the third party does not know its status, it is not returned even if the debtor admits the debt, as with a lost document in siman 65, the present case can still be understood. It may involve a deed containing no property responsibility, or a borrower who is prepared to pay immediately in cash once the lender swears, after which the third party will tear the deed. There is then no concern of collusion affecting purchasers. The lender nevertheless refuses to swear because he relies on his contractual credibility. The law teaches that this credibility has been weakened because the deed is in another person's possession and that person does not know why. See Tumim, where I use this to answer the Shach's question.
