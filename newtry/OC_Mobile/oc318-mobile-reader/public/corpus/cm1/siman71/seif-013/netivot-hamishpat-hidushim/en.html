One who falsely denied. Even if he became established as one who falsely denied in relation to another person, because his credibility has been weakened. If the lender knew that he was already established as one who falsely denied and nevertheless granted him credibility, that credibility remains effective. If he had already been formally established as a false denier in beit din, judicial matters are considered public knowledge and he cannot say he did not know. See Beurim. The same applies if he became suspected of false oaths: the credibility clause is void. See there. With a handwritten obligation containing credibility, once the debtor becomes established as a false denier the lender cannot collect upon it at all. With contemporary commercial bills that do not name the lender, however, collection is still possible.
