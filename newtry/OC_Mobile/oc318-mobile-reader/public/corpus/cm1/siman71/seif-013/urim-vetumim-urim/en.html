A false denier. The borrower granted credibility to the lender while he was presumed upright. Once the lender became judicially established as a false denier in another matter, even one unrelated to this borrower, his credibility is impaired and the contractual clause falls away. If the borrower already knew at the time of the agreement that the lender had such a status and nevertheless granted him credibility, the clause certainly remains effective. Gidulei Terumah writes that if the lender became established as a false denier in beit din, he cannot later say, "I did not know of his reputation," because such a status is public. See below, siman 92, regarding a person suspected of false oaths. The sources there imply we do not always assume such matters are publicly known. See what I write there.
