Credibility is ineffective, etc. This ruling requires study. The Rosh did not decide it absolutely. He wrote only that because swindlers are common, it is proper to be stringent, meaning stringent toward the creditor who seeks to collect from purchasers, and not allow collection without an oath. Raavad, Baal HaMaor, and Ramah hold that where the credibility clause preceded the sale, it is effective against purchasers, and their reading of the Tosefta appears primary. Ramban's effort to interpret “those who come by his authority” as excluding purchasers is strained, for the parallel phrase concerning the other party plainly includes successors. Rashi and Bartenura expressly explain it as purchasers. Therefore, if a creditor already seized property from purchasers and refuses to swear, he may plausibly say that he relies on Rashi, Bartenura, Raavad, Baal HaMaor, Ramah, and perhaps even the Rosh, since in that posture the purchasers are the claimants. This applies only where the note expressly extends credibility against purchasers. Ordinary credibility certainly does not bind them.
