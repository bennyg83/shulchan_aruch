Purchasers. The Shach writes that this ruling requires study. The Rosh did not rule this way unequivocally; he wrote only that, since swindlers are numerous, it is proper to be stringent in accordance with Rabbeinu Chananel. His intent is a stringency upon the claimant, who seeks to collect from purchasers, namely that he may not collect until he swears. Accordingly, since the Raavad and other authorities disagree and maintain that when an express stipulation of trustworthiness in the document preceded the sale to the purchasers it is effective against them, and their view appears primary from the Tosefta, who can say that if the lender has already seized property from the purchasers and refuses to swear he cannot say, "I rely upon those authorities"? It is also possible that even the Rosh would agree where the lender has already seized, for then imposing the stringency would operate against the purchasers, who are now the claimants. This applies, however, only where the stipulation expressly granted the lender trustworthiness even against purchasers. An ordinary, unspecified stipulation of trustworthiness certainly does not avail against purchasers according to any opinion.
