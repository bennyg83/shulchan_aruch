If a document grants credibility, local scribes often insert such a clause without consulting the parties merely to strengthen documents, and the borrower says it was written without his permission, then if the witnesses themselves are present and say they inserted it only because other documents customarily contain it, the credibility clause is not relied upon, even if the document says, 'We made an acquisition from him concerning everything written above,' because it is not the universal custom to include such a clause. {Rama: The same applies to other documentary enhancements. See above, siman 61, seif 5.} If the document expressly says, 'We made an acquisition concerning everything expressly written above,' and the credibility clause itself is explicit, the witnesses are not believed to say he never instructed them to grant it or that they wrote it by mistake; once they testified, they may not retract. It is therefore a good practice to state expressly in documents that an acquisition was made concerning the credibility clause, eliminating doubt.
