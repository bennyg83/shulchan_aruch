But children from age six and upward. The Mechaber does not clarify whether this is only where the father is not known to be wealthy. Even when he is present, if he is not wealthy we do not seize his assets for their support but embarrass him and publicly announce his refusal. One might have thought that in his absence the law should be stronger, because we ordinarily presume that if he were present he would not be worse than an impure bird that feeds its young, and so long as we have not heard an explicit refusal we should presume him compassionate. The ruling teaches that we nevertheless do not descend to his assets. But where he is wealthy, perhaps we do descend to his assets under the law of charity even in his absence. Rambam, however, Hilchot Ishut chapter 12, writes explicitly that we do not do so even if he is wealthy. His basis is Ketubot 107a: where one went overseas, beit din descends to his property to support his wife, but not his sons and daughters and not for another matter. Rav Chisda interprets the other matter as adornments, Rav Yosef as charity. According to everyone charity is not given from his assets, and they dispute adornments. Rambam already rules that adornments are not given, all the more so charity.
