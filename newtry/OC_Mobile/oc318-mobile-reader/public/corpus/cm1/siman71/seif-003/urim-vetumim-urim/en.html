He admitted before us, etc. The Shach cites many authorities who disagree and hold that even regarding an admission the disqualified witnesses are not believed. See Tumim. In truth this is a doubt in law, and the burden of proof rests upon the one seeking to extract money.
They are believed. Even if the admission was made outside the borrower's presence, the borrower cannot say that he admitted merely so as not to appear wealthy. See above, siman 81, and Tumim.
Because he disqualified them only with respect to repayment. If he expressly stipulated that they are not to be believed concerning any testimony, whether an admission or a waiver, the condition is effective even for those matters, because every monetary condition is binding. If the wording is uncertain whether he intended that breadth, the holder of the document has the lower hand and the burden of proof remains on the claimant. Sema and Shach.
Likewise, if they testified that he waived the debt. Here there is no dispute. All agree that they are believed, because waiver is not included in repayment but is a separate legal act. See above, siman 82.
For the credibility clause does not, etc. If the document states that the lender is believed regarding the principal amount, then because it specifically says "the principal amount," we construe the clause as preventing a claim that the document was written as an accommodation or that the debt is invalid because of interest and similar matters, but not as preventing a claim of repayment. Likewise, if it says that he is believed in all matters of the document, we interpret it as referring to matters affecting the document itself, such as an accommodation document and similar defects, not repayment. Rashba, Sema, and Shach. See siman 82.
