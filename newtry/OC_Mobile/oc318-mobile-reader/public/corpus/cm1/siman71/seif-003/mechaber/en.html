Credibility granted against witnesses applies when they testify, 'He paid before us.' If they testify, 'He admitted before us that payment was made,' they are believed, because the creditor disqualified them only regarding payment, whereas an admission is a different matter. Likewise, they are believed if they testify that the creditor waived the debt. {Rama: The same principle applies in every similar case: credibility extends only to the matter expressly entrusted, not beyond it. Thus, if one accepted money for a business venture and granted the investor credibility against him and his heirs to say that no loss occurred to that money and concerning everything relating to the principal, and the recipient later died leaving property with another, the investor is not believed to say that this property came from the partnership, even had the recipient remained alive, because that claim was not included in the grant. But whatever the agreement expressly covers is effective. If it grants credibility concerning the amount of expenses and bribes, that is effective just as for the principal, because every monetary condition is valid. See also simanim 4 and 8.}
