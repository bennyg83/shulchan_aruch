ch) The wording of the Tur in the name of Responsa Rif. So too writes Baal HaTerumot, gate 50; the responsum is recorded there. The Rambam likewise writes this in the cited chapter.
t) There in the name of Baal HaTerumot, according to all opinions, because forgiveness is a separate legal act.
y) This is explained in the question cited by the Tur in seif 14. The recipient trusted the giver only where some circumstance would prevent him from taking the partners' oath. As the responsum explains, he trusted the giver only when the recipient would raise an uncertain claim and therefore be unable to swear, not when he raised a definite claim. While the giver was still alive, the recipient sought to return to him money in the form of documents from that business. The giver did not accept all of them and said that he recognized some documents as not having been created from his money. The Rosh ruled that the assets remain presumptively in the possession of the orphans and cannot be removed without proof. In my humble opinion, the Rama abbreviated too much here and should have explained this. The Sema writes that the Rama understands that this was merely the factual setting of the incident, and that the same law would apply if the giver trusted him generally that no loss had occurred.
k) If the document says that he trusted him regarding the principal amount, or states, "You shall be believed by me concerning every matter of this document," the Rashba writes in Responsa, simanim 991 and 1, that this wording appears ineffective against a claim of payment. It merely prevents him from claiming that the document was a document of trust or similar defect. The Beit Yosef cites this, from simanim 24 and 25.
