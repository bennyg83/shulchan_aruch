He admitted before us. The Shach cites many authorities who hold that there is no distinction between acknowledgment and repayment. Therefore the possessor may say, "I rely upon those authorities." Regarding waiver, everyone agrees. See Beurim. Even according to the Mechaber, this applies specifically where the lender admitted after he had already received the payment. But if, while receiving the money, he first acknowledged that he accepts these funds as repayment, the admission does not override the credibility clause, even according to the Mechaber. By contrast, if after payment he admits that the debt was paid, even outside the borrower's presence, he cannot say, "I admitted merely so as not to appear wealthy." If the credibility clause expressly includes both acknowledgment and repayment, it is effective. The general rule is that a monetary condition is valid. If the language is doubtful, the holder of the document is at the disadvantage: the burden of proof rests upon the one seeking to extract money. Sema and Shach.
