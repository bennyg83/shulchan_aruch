For the trustworthiness applies only to that regarding which he granted him credibility. The Rashba likewise wrote in responsum 1056 regarding a deposit document stating that he granted the creditor full credibility, like two witnesses, concerning the principal sum mentioned therein. Since the wording specifies the principal sum, we say that he granted him credibility only against a claim that the transaction was merely a sham or involved interest, but the debtor may still claim, 'I repaid you.' See there. The Rashba similarly wrote in responsum 991 that if the document states that he shall be believed regarding all matters of the document, the law is likewise as stated above. And he granted the giver credibility against himself and against his heirs. In the question addressed to the Rosh, and also in his responsum there, it is written that he did not grant him credibility in an unrestricted manner, but only that if the recipient would not be present he could swear the partners' oath. It must be that Rama understood that this was the factual circumstance of that case, and that the same rule applies where he granted him credibility without qualification and no loss occurred.
