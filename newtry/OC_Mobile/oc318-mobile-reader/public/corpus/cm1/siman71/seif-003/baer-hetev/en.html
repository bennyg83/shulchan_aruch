Regarding payment. But if he clearly indicated that he made the lender credible even concerning this matter and similar matters, the lender is certainly believed regarding everything. The general rule is that every monetary condition is valid, provided its language definitely includes the condition. In a doubtful case, the burden of proof rests upon the claimant. Sema. See above siman 61, seif 5 in the gloss. The Shach cites many authorities who disagree with the Mechaber and hold that there is no distinction between an admission and payment. The Rashba writes in a responsum that even if witnesses come and testify that the lender admitted before them that he had been paid, the lender is believed because the borrower made him credible against two witnesses. He writes that their teachers agreed to this, that this was the tradition in his hands, and that this is their practice. See the Shach at length.
And he left. The responsum of the Rosh and the Tur state that the case is where the deceased left other property, but it was not cash in hand and consisted only of debt documents. The ruling is that the creditor is not believed to take the available cash up to the amount of his debt. Rather, he must also take a proportional share of the debt documents. See there. Sema.
It is effective. The Rashba writes in a responsum that if a document states that the lender shall be believed regarding all matters of the document, the credibility extends only to matters intrinsic to the document itself, such as where one says, 'I gave you this hundred,' and the other says, 'I did not receive it,' or where the borrower claims it was an accommodation document, and similar claims. It does not make the lender credible to say, 'I have not been paid,' because that is not a matter of the document itself unless the text says so explicitly. The Rivash writes likewise at the end of siman 491, as does Responsa Maharanach, siman 61, at length. Responsa Maharibal, part 1, klal 19, siman 25, is uncertain where the document states, 'with all the usual strengthening clauses of a scribe and with every appropriate force,' whether this includes all types of claims such as accommodation, interest, payment, waiver, and the like. Otherwise, what additional force would the clause have? See there. Shach.
