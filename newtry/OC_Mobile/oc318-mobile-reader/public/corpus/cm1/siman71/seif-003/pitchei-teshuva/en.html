A credibility clause is effective. See Baer Heitev, which cites the Rashba through the statement that such credibility does not necessarily make the creditor believed to say that he was not paid. In the glosses of Rabbi Akiva Eiger, of blessed memory, he notes from the Rashba that had the debtor trusted the creditor also regarding payment, he should have written an unqualified credibility clause. Responsa Lechem Rav, siman 14, derives from this that an unqualified credibility clause includes claims of trust, interest, and payment. If the document says that the creditor is believed only so long as the instrument remains intact, is not torn with a beit din tear, and has no payment notation written upon it, that wording reveals that the credibility concerns payment alone.
