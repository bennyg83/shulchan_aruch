If a creditor grants the borrower credibility to say a document was paid, that credibility applies against the creditor's heirs as well. If the creditor dies and his heirs seek collection, the borrower is believed to say it was paid. He must nevertheless swear that it was paid, even during the creditor's lifetime, unless the clause makes him credible without an oath. The creditor cannot use this document to collect from the borrower's heirs or purchasers. Even if the borrower admits nonpayment, the creditor may not seize from purchasers, because collusion is suspected. If the borrower claims he paid part or all, while the creditor denies any payment, the borrower pays the portion he admits and takes a heses oath. If the clause makes him credible without a heses oath, he takes no oath.
