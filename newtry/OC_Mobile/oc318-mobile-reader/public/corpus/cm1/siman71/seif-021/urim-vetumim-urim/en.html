And the heirs come, etc. Why do we not say that the lender's credibility was granted only in the borrower's own presence, because the borrower trusted that the lender would not dare lie to his face, whereas against heirs who cannot personally recognize the falsehood the lender may be brazen? One must answer that this concern alone is insufficient to extract money where the matter is doubtful, for the burden of proof rests upon the claimant. Above, by contrast, the lender himself was the party seeking to extract money, so even a slight concern weakened his special credibility. Here the borrower or his heirs seek to defeat the lender's document, and the rule is reversed. The Taz forced a different answer, but it is unnecessary.
But he must swear, etc. As to the difficulty raised from siman 42, where uncertain language in a receipt is construed in favor of the holder of the debt instrument, see what I wrote above in siman 42 at length. Here we say that the borrower granted credibility only to reduce the claim to the status of an oral loan, but no further, and therefore the lender must swear.
Even during the lender's lifetime. This implies all the more after his death. The Shach is uncertain whether, when the lender's heirs claim, "Perhaps you did not repay our father," they may impose an oath, since theirs is only an uncertain claim. According to the Rama below in siman 75, however, where there are grounds for suspicion one may impose an oath even on an uncertain claim. Here there are such grounds, for if he paid, why did he leave the document outstanding?
And he swears a hesset oath. This is not considered a partial admission requiring a Torah oath. Since the lender was granted credibility, the borrower can be brazen, and the law is therefore like that of one who denies the entire claim. Shach and Gidulei Terumah. The Sema's statement that one does not swear on documents means that a document generally represents a lien on land, and no Torah oath is imposed on a claim against land. Here there is no collection from purchasers, so that reasoning does not apply in its ordinary form. This, however, is the intent of the Sema. The Shach writes that the words are a scribal error, but the matter is clear as I have explained.
