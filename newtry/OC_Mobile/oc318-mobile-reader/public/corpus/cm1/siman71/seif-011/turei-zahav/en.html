(Seif 21.) "A lender who granted credibility to the borrower." This is difficult. Perhaps he granted credibility only in his own presence, because the borrower would not dare lie to his face, as stated in seif 20. One may answer that if so, the clause would harm the borrower more than benefit him. The borrower would repay and fail to retrieve the document because he relied upon the credibility clause, and after the lender's death he would have to pay a second time to the lender's heir. He could not protect himself from the heir, since the lender will inevitably die eventually. By contrast, in seif 20, concerning credibility granted to the lender, there is no such concern. Even if the clause is ineffective when the lender is absent, it still benefits him when he personally sues the borrower.
