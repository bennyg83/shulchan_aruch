Who are obligated to pay. Mahardam questions the Rosh: if the credibility clause reduces the note to an oral loan, why do we not advance for the heirs the defense that their father paid, as we ordinarily plead for heirs and purchasers? There is no difficulty. We plead for heirs only a defense their father could have asserted by his own legal right. Here the father would not have been believed except because the creditor personally granted him credibility. The creditor may therefore say, “I trusted your father when he himself would say he paid, but I did not trust you, and I say that he never claimed payment.” This reasoning applies even after maturity, as the Sema and Mahari ibn Chaviv state. The Ir Shushan incorrectly limits the view to a death within the term on the ground that a person does not ordinarily pay early. The cited authorities clearly apply it after maturity as well.
