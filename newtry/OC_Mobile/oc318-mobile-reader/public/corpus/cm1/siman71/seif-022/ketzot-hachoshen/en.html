Who are obligated to pay. If the lender dies, the borrower can ordinarily claim payment even though the original credibility clause did not bind the lender's heirs, because the very existence of the credibility arrangement weakens the usual presumption that a paid document would have been retrieved. One might ask why the same should not apply when the borrower dies. The answer is that this clause concerns a debt still within its term. Even if the document presumption is weakened, the presumption against payment within the due date remains. Therefore the borrower's heirs are still obligated. The same reasoning implies that if the lender dies while the debt remains within its term, his heirs can collect on the strength of the presumption against early payment even though they did not inherit the special credibility itself.
