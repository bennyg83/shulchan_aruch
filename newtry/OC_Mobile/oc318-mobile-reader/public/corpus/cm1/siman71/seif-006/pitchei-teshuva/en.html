Responsa Chemdat Shlomo, at the end of siman 9, writes that if the document grants the creditor "all laws of credibility" without limiting the clause specifically to an oath, it is as though he was expressly exempted from accepting a ban as well, and the debtor may impose only a general ban. This may be affected by the custom recorded by Tumim in siman 82, seif 9, that whenever a general ban is mentioned, the practice is that the person expressly accepts it. Even that is uncertain here, because where one was expressly exempted from acceptance of a ban, the other party may certainly impose only a general ban without requiring his acceptance. Chemdat Shlomo ends with some hesitation. Netivot HaMishpat, however, writes plainly that where the creditor was expressly exempted from accepting a ban, the debtor may not compel him to accept one even according to the custom cited by Tumim; he may only declare a general ban on his own.
