(Seif 9) “The law favors the debtor.” A claim that payment was applied to another debt cannot prevail against witnesses, and likewise cannot prevail against the creditor’s own written acknowledgment. The Tur, citing the Rosh, concludes, “unless the debtor trusted him like two witnesses.” This does not mean that even such express credibility defeats the creditor’s handwriting; the rule in seif 4 shows that it does. The Rosh means to reject the thought that an acknowledgment is too weak to contradict a credibility clause. Against ordinary witnesses, the creditor prevails only if expressly trusted like two witnesses. A written acknowledgment, however, is itself treated as testimony even without special wording, and no credibility clause can contradict one’s own admission. This is also the view of my revered father-in-law, contrary to the Sema’s strained distinction between this handwriting and that discussed in seif 4.
