[Shulchan Aruch] Unless he stated it explicitly. Note: Responsa Maharach Shashon, siman 3, writes in the name of Responsa Maharshadam and Maharashach that if a document states a trustworthiness clause “etc.,” it includes trustworthiness also against him and his heirs, for him and his heirs, just as is customarily set out explicitly in deeds, because the scribe merely abbreviated all of that with the word “etc.” This appears in Maharshadam, Even HaEzer, simanim 116, 174, and 216; Maharashach, part 2, siman 137; and Mahar Aharon Sasson, siman 53. See also Responsa Lechem Rav, siman 50.
