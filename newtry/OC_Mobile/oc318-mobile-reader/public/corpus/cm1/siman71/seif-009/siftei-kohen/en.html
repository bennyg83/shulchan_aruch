The law is with the borrower. The Sema explains that when the creditor received money before witnesses, he cannot claim that it was applied to another debt, as stated in siman 58, and a handwritten acknowledgment is equivalent to witnesses. He limits the ruling where the creditor was empowered as two witnesses. In my view, the Rosh and Tur were speaking generally, not specifically about a handwritten acknowledgment. Where the creditor himself wrote an acknowledgment, he thereby neutralized the credibility clause and cannot later claim that the payment was for another debt. The Bach and Gedulei Terumah rule likewise.
With the borrower. According to the opinion in siman 58 that the note is not torn and that money seized by the creditor is not removed from him, it requires study whether the same applies here or whether the creditor's own handwriting is stronger evidence.
