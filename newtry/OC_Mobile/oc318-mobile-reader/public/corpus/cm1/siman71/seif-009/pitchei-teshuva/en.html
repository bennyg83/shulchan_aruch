Not to impose a severe ban. The Sema writes that this is because he does not place the ban and curse directly upon him. The Shach writes that this is specifically where the document explicitly exempts him from accepting a severe ban. If he merely exempted him from an oath, the opposing party may compel him to accept a severe ban before beit din. See there. Responsa Chemdat Shlomo, end of siman 9, writes that if the document grants him 'all laws of credibility' and does not specify only an oath, this is equivalent to explicitly exempting him from acceptance of a severe ban as well. The opposing party can then impose only a general ban, except perhaps under the custom cited by Tumim, in siman 82, seif 9, which I cited there in seif katan 7, that whenever a general ban is mentioned it is customary for the person to accept the ban explicitly. Even this is not clear, because here it may be considered as though he explicitly exempted him, and where there is an explicit exemption from accepting a severe ban, one certainly cannot impose more than a general ban. He concludes that this still requires some study. From the end of his wording it appears that he retains some uncertainty even where the document explicitly exempts him from accepting a severe ban. Netivot HaMishpat, however, writes plainly that where there is an explicit exemption from accepting a severe ban, the opposing party has no right to compel him to accept one even according to Tumim's custom. He may only impose a general ban on his own. See there.
