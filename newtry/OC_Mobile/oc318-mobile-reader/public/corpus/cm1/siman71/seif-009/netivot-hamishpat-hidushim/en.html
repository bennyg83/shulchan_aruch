The law favors the borrower, and the lender cannot claim that payment was for another debt, even if the borrower gave him credibility equivalent to two witnesses. This is the Shach's ruling, unlike the Sema, who holds that with credibility equivalent to two witnesses the lender may claim another debt even against the borrower's handwritten receipt. The Shach remains uncertain whether seizure helps where there is a handwritten receipt against an ordinary credibility clause and the lender claims that payment applied elsewhere, as at the beginning of siman 58. But with a witnessed receipt, if the document is not mentioned in the receipt, seizure can help. If the receipt states that payment was made on the document, then even if the identifying details of the document are not written there, seizure does not help. See Beurim.
