(Seif 17) “Unless he expressly exempted him and his heirs.” The Sema says this helps only when the debtor’s heirs claim, “Perhaps our father paid you,” but not when they claim, “We ourselves paid you.” Even an express stipulation granting credibility on that point may be ineffective, because a father may lack power to diminish his children’s rights in that manner. Likewise, if the debtor trusted the creditor and his heirs, the clause merely exempts the creditor’s heirs from swearing that their father did not tell them the debt was paid. If the debtor says, “I paid you,” the clause does not exempt them from an oath. These statements are very surprising. In siman 108, seif 17, the Beit Yosef cites Sefer HaTerumot, gate 14, concerning a debtor who trusted the creditor and his heirs. Some say the clause prevents the debtor only from telling the heirs, “I paid you,” but not from saying, “I paid your father.” Sefer HaTerumot disagrees. The first view’s reason is that the debtor granted credibility only where he cannot boldly deny payment in the face of the recipient; an heir would not deny having personally received the money. But where the debtor says, “I paid your father,” the heir can readily deny it, and perhaps no credibility was granted. Sefer HaTerumot holds that the heirs were trusted in every respect. Thus, at minimum, the clause more plainly defeats a claim of payment to the heirs than a claim of payment to their father, contrary to the Sema; according to Sefer HaTerumot’s conclusion it defeats both. The same principle applies here. A debtor’s grant of credibility to the creditor and his heirs against the debtor and his heirs operates in every form. This explains why the Tur in Even HaEzer, siman 98, does not mention a claim by the husband’s heirs, standing in the debtor’s place, that they personally paid the wife: that claim is plainly ineffective, all the more so when even “our father paid” is ineffective. A difficulty remains, however. In Even HaEzer 98 the Tur concludes that an exemption regarding heirs is effective only with a formal acquisition, following Abba Shaul in HaKotev, whereas here he rules like the anonymous first opinion there, which disagrees with Abba Shaul. The Rama there rules that an express exemption is ineffective against heirs, and it is likewise surprising that he comments here neither on the Mechaber nor on this conflict.
