Some say that if there are two Torah scholars. This means two courts in the same city. Although a plaintiff can sometimes insist on traveling to a greater beit din or place of scholars, one cannot necessarily demand the lesser court in the same city. Bach explains that where both courts are local, if the smaller court encounters a difficult issue it can readily consult the greater one. Tur disagrees more strongly. If two courts in the same city are equal, the lender may choose either. But the defendant cannot insist on the smaller court where a greater beit din exists locally. Sema and many later authorities follow Tur. In Tumim I discuss Semag and Tur at length and conclude: if one beit din is clearly greater, the defendant cannot insist on the smaller. If both are equal and in the same city, the defendant may choose between them. If one equal court is local and the other is three parasangs away, the defendant cannot force travel because an equivalent court is available locally. But if the court three parasangs away is materially greater than the local one, even if it is not the Great Beit Din itself, the defendant may insist on going there because the distance is not excessive.
