(In the Tur, seif 1.) "But if he does not know, such as one who denies everything." In the Rosh cited by the Beit Yosef there is an opinion that if both parties make claims against one another, the law is naturally that each can compel the other. The Gemara in the final chapter of HaGozel deals with such a case. The Rosh, however, rules in accordance with the latter answer, distinguishing between a place of assembly of scholars and the Great Beit Din. In any event, he cites the first reasoning to explain the distinction within the place of assembly itself, namely whether one may compel the other to litigate in his own city or to travel to the assembly. This is the meaning of our master's words.
