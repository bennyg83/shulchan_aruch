The Sema writes: Beit Yosef, siman 11, cites the Rosh regarding a defendant who denies having been summoned. The claimant brings witnesses that he was summoned; the defendant then admits it and produces witnesses that he came immediately after being summoned. This is comparable to one who says, 'I never borrowed,' who is not believed afterward to say, 'I repaid,' even if he brings witnesses to support the latter claim. Thus states Darkei Moshe, siman 11, seif 3. However, Beit Yosef and Darkei Moshe at the end of siman 11 quote the Rosh as saying that the claimant stated, 'I will bring witnesses,' whereupon the defendant retracted and admitted. This implies that the defendant retracted before the claimant actually brought witnesses. The Sema's version, that the claimant brought witnesses, appears intended to answer the Bach's question at the end of siman 11. The Bach asks from siman 80, where the Tur indicates that according to both the Ramah and the Rambam, a person may retract before witnesses arrive, even if he retracted because he saw witnesses approaching, provided that he later brings witnesses supporting his second claim. The Bach leaves the question unresolved. The Sema apparently understands the Rosh as referring to a case where the claimant said he would bring witnesses, actually brought them, and only then did the defendant retract. Yet the plain language of the Rosh in Beit Yosef and Darkei Moshe does not imply this. Rabbi Yerucham likewise writes: 'Reuven says, I will bring witnesses, and from fear of the witnesses Shimon retracted and admitted that he had been summoned.' The phrase 'from fear of the witnesses' indicates that they had not yet arrived; he merely feared that witnesses might be brought. It therefore appears that the Rosh holds that whenever one admits from fear of witnesses, it is as though witnesses had already arrived. This follows Rabbi Elazar son of Rabbi Shimon in chapter Merubah, Bava Kamma 75, according to Rav, for the law there follows Rav. Although the Sages disagree with Rabbi Elazar son of Rabbi Shimon, the Gemara asks whether Rav's view depends on that dispute and answers that Rav can say: I rule even according to Rabbi Elazar son of Rabbi Shimon, for he spoke only where the admission was caused by fear of witnesses; but here he admitted on his own and witnesses arrived only afterward. This implies that all agree with Rabbi Elazar son of Rabbi Shimon where the admission was prompted by fear of witnesses. There is no contradiction from siman 80, as the Bach asks, because there it is not known that the retraction resulted from fear of witnesses. The ruling there is merely that if he retracts and witnesses later contradict his first claim, he is believed when he brings witnesses supporting his second claim. Examine it there and you will see that this is correct. Here, however, where the claimant said, 'I will bring witnesses,' and the defendant retracted out of fear of them, it is as though the witnesses arrived before his retraction, in accordance with Rabbi Elazar son of Rabbi Shimon. This applies, however, only if witnesses do in fact arrive afterward. Nevertheless, Beit Yosef below, siman 79, cites an anonymous manuscript ruling like the Sages and not like Rabbi Elazar son of Rabbi Shimon, and the Rama rules likewise there in seif 9. The practical law therefore requires study.

Rather, he must clarify, etc. This ruling is puzzling. It is also difficult for me, because the responsum of the Rosh states that he must clarify his expenditures and beit din must assess them for him. This implies that neither clarification alone nor an assessment by beit din alone suffices; both are required. The reason appears to be that clarification alone is insufficient, since he cannot be permitted to spend whatever he wishes, while an assessment by beit din alone is insufficient, since perhaps he did not actually spend that amount. It would therefore appear necessary to emend the Rama's words to read, “then beit din shall assess it for him.” The same emendation may be made in the Levush, thereby removing the Sema's objection. However, in Darkei Moshe he cites the responsum of the Rosh as follows: “He must be reimbursed for whatever he can establish that he spent, while for whatever he cannot establish, beit din shall assess how much it was appropriate to spend, and that amount shall be paid to him.” This requires study.
