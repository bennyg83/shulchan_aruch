Expenses incurred to compel him. Mechaber's words imply that the refusing party must reimburse only expenses incurred in secular court in order to compel him to submit to Jewish beit din. But if the claimant paid bribes or expenses in secular court in order to obtain a substantive judgment requiring payment, even if that secular judgment happens to accord with halachah, the defendant need not reimburse those expenses. An exception is where the claimant had a written debt, the defendant refused to appear, and beit din issued a valid ruling in the defendant's absence that he owes the debt and then authorized resort to secular court solely to enforce that Jewish ruling. In that case the secular proceedings merely compel performance of a Jewish judgment. But where beit din itself cannot adjudicate payment in the defendant's absence, it cannot authorize secular-court expenses to establish liability, because it does not yet know whether the defendant truly owes. Such expenses were incurred without beit din authorization. Sema later discusses a defendant who denies ever having been summoned. That case is not about reimbursement of secular-court expenses, for those require proof of authorization by beit din, and such authorization itself establishes that he refused. Rather, it concerns a defendant who refused the first beit din and went elsewhere, forcing the claimant to travel there and incur expenses. The claimant seeks reimbursement because of the original refusal.
We do not say that he should swear, etc. Urim VeTumim asks: an injured party ordinarily swears and collects, and here too the claimant was damaged because the other party caused him expenses. In my view there is no difficulty. In the ordinary injury case the damage occurred unexpectedly and could not have been documented in advance. Here the claimant can ordinarily incur the necessary expenses in a manner that allows him to prove them. If exact proof is impossible, beit din can assess the amount. If neither proof nor assessment is possible, then Urim VeTumim is correct: he should swear and collect like other injured parties. See Shach, who requires proof and appraisal. The claimant need prove only the kinds of expenses he incurred, such as how many messengers were sent and how many trips were made. Even if he cannot prove the exact sums paid, he may recover at least the lowest reasonable amount according to beit din's appraisal, as in siman 375.
