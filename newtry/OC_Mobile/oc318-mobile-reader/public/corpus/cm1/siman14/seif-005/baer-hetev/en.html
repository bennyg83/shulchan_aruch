His expenses. Yam Shel Shlomo writes that this refers to expenses caused by the other party's recalcitrance. Other expenses, such as sending a messenger to collect the money or sending after the defendant to a particular place, need not be reimbursed. Shach.
Recalcitrant. The Beit Yosef cites the Rosh concerning a defendant who denies that he was summoned. The claimant then brings witnesses that he was summoned; the defendant admits this but produces witnesses that he came immediately after being summoned. His first denial is treated like the statement, "I never borrowed," so that he can no longer claim, "I repaid," even if he produces witnesses supporting repayment. The Shach writes that the Rosh's language implies an even broader rule: even if the claimant has not yet produced witnesses, but the defendant retracts out of fear that witnesses will be brought and says that he was indeed summoned but came immediately, the same law applies. However, below in siman 79, seif 9, in the gloss, the Rama does not rule this way. The practical law therefore requires study. See there.
Or. The Shach writes that this ruling is surprising. Responsa Rosh likewise implies that neither mere clarification nor a beit din appraisal alone suffices, and that both are required. It would therefore appear that the text should be emended to read, "then the beit din shall appraise it for him." However, Darkei Moshe does not imply this. The matter requires study.
