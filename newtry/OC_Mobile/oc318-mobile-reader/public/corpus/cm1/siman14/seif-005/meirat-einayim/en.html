He is obligated to reimburse, etc. This applies where the defendant was found liable in the case. Rivash, siman 475; Darkei Moshe.
From the time he became recalcitrant. Beit Yosef in siman 11 cites the Rosh: if the defendant denies having been summoned and the claimant produces witnesses that he was summoned, and the defendant then acknowledges this and produces witnesses that he came immediately after the summons, it is as though he first said, "I never borrowed." He is not believed afterward to say, "I paid," even if he brings witnesses supporting the latter claim. Darkei Moshe, siman 11.
We do not say that he should swear how much he spent, etc. The reason is that he was not given credibility through an oath to extract money from another person. He must prove the amount. The Mechaber below in siman 158:9 rules that where one surrounded another's field and cannot prove his expenses, he swears an oath akin to a Torah oath and collects. The answer appears there itself: he spent with permission and for the surrounded owner's benefit. Similarly, Rama below in siman 91:3 allows an oath and collection. The Tur and Mechaber at the end of siman 375 likewise rule that whether the improver's position is superior or inferior, he swears how much he spent and collects. There too, even though he entered the field without permission, the landowner suffers no net loss because the property has been improved by at least the amount he pays. Here, by contrast, whatever he pays is a pure loss to him. This is unlike the Ir Shushan's explanation that the claimant cannot simply spend whatever he wishes expecting the defendant to pay all of it.
