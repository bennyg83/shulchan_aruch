A person found liable in court need not reimburse the opposing party's expenses, even if he required that party to litigate in another city. This applies when he did not refuse to submit to judgment but merely wished to litigate elsewhere. If he refused to appear before a beit din and the claimant incurred expenses to compel him to submit to judgment, he must reimburse all expenses incurred from the time he became recalcitrant. Some say that if the claimant sued him in a non-Jewish court and incurred fees for judges and advocates, the defendant need not reimburse those costs even though his refusal forced the claimant to bring him there. {Rama: Others disagree and hold that if expenses had to be incurred to compel him through a non-Jewish court, he must reimburse them, provided that the claimant acted with authorization from a beit din, as stated below in siman 26; this appears to me to be the primary view. If one person tells another, 'Go to litigate in another place and I will follow you,' and the first travels there but the second does not, the second must reimburse all his travel expenses. When a recalcitrant party must pay expenses, the claimant does not merely swear how much he spent and collect it; he must prove the amount, or the beit din appraises the expenses, and the recalcitrant party pays that amount (Responsa of the Rosh, klal 107). See also klal 73, siman 2.}
