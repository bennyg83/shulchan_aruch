To pay him all his expenses. This applies where the recalcitrant party ultimately loses the case. If one sued him to reimburse the expenses of a summons because he had refused to appear before the first beit din in another city, and the defendant answered that he had never been summoned, but witnesses were then produced, and afterward he claimed that he had never refused to come, he has been established as a false denier and is not believed. If before the witnesses appeared he admitted that he had been summoned but claimed that he had not refused, then if his admission was not prompted by fear of the witnesses he is believed. If he admitted because of fear of the witnesses, the matter requires study, Shach:
Through non Jewish courts. See Beurim, seif katan 5, that he is required to reimburse such expenses only where they were incurred to compel the defendant to litigate before a Jewish beit din. But if the expenses were bribes paid in order to collect directly from the defendant, then where the plaintiff holds a written loan document and beit din is permitted to rule in the defendant's absence, beit din may issue the ruling without him and authorize resort to the non Jewish courts, including the expenses necessary there to recover the money:
That he swear. Since reimbursement of the expenses causes the other party a loss, clarification is required. See Beurim, seif katan 6, that if it is impossible to establish the expenses through proof or through an appraisal, the claimant swears and collects the amount of his loss.
