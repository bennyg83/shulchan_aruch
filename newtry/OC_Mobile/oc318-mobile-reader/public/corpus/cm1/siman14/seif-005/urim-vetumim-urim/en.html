From the time he became a refuser. Yam Shel Shlomo and Shach say that expenses for sending a messenger after the money or after the refuser personally are not necessarily recoverable. Beit Yosef cites Rosh: if the plaintiff claims reimbursement for summons expenses and the defendant denies ever being summoned, but witnesses then prove the summons and the defendant afterward says, "I was summoned but did not refuse," he is not believed. His first total denial is like saying, "I never borrowed," which includes denial of repayment. If before witnesses appeared he admitted the summons and denied refusal, he can be believed if the admission was not made from fear of the witnesses. If it was prompted by fear that witnesses were about to arrive, Rama's rule in siman 79 applies and his later revision may not be accepted. Shach.
We do not say that he should swear. Sema understands that because the defendant received no direct benefit from the plaintiff's litigation expenses, we do not allow the plaintiff simply to swear and collect even though the expenses were authorized. If they can be proved by witnesses or objectively assessed by beit din, they are collected; otherwise they are not. In Tumim I argue that this is not correct. Where objective clarification is possible, we do not rely on an oath. But where clarification is impossible, the claimant should swear and collect, analogous to an injured party who swears and collects under the special evidentiary rule.
