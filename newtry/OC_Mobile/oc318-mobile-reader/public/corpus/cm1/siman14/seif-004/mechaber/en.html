Some say that if a judge sees that a litigant suspects him of inclining the judgment against that litigant, the judge must explain the reason for the ruling even if the litigant did not ask. {Rama: All the more so if the litigant says, 'Write for me the reason you judged me as you did' (Tur). Some say this applies only if he was judged under compulsion; otherwise the reasons are not written, and this is the primary view. When the judges must provide a written explanation, there is no time limit; whenever he requests it, they write and give it to him. They need not write the legal reasoning and proofs, only the parties' claims and the ruling. He must pay immediately; if he later overturns the ruling, the money is returned. A written explanation is required only when an inferior beit din's ruling is being taken to a superior one. A Great Beit Din need not write an explanation, because we do not suspect it of error; otherwise there would be no end to the matter.}
