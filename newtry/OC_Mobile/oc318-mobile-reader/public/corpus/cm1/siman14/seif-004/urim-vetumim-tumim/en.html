All the more so if he says, "Write for me," etc. Sema asks why Rama says "all the more so." In a case where there is no suspicion of improper judgment, the court need only write the litigants' claims, not the reasoning for the judgment, as Rama himself states in this gloss. His answer is strained. There is no difficulty with Tur, because the distinction that where the litigant merely requests a written decision the judges need write only the claims and not the reasoning is not universally accepted. Tosafot and Rosh apparently do not accept it. Otherwise, what is their question from Bava Metzia, where the Gemara says in a particular case, "Such a ruling requires explanation," implying that reasons need be supplied because there is room for suspicion, whereas in Sanhedrin a litigant who asks for a written explanation receives one? There would be no question. One could answer that in Bava Metzia the court must disclose the reason itself because of suspicion, while in Sanhedrin it need only record the claims. Indeed Shitah Mekubetzet and other authorities cite precisely that distinction as an answer to Tosafot's question. The fact that Tosafot did not use it indicates that they do not accept it. They understand Sanhedrin's phrase "write for me for what reason you judged me" as requiring disclosure of the court's reasoning. Tur can therefore properly say "all the more so." Rama, however, also cites Nimukei Yosef's distinction, so the wording requires explanation. It appears that Rama distinguishes between writing and oral explanation. The judges need not necessarily write the legal reasoning into the formal decision. If the matter goes to a superior court, those judges can infer the legal rationale from the claims and the result. But when the litigant asks the original judges directly why they ruled against him, they must explain their reasoning orally. His question itself indicates concern that they may have mistaken the law or treated him improperly. That concern should be removed. Thus the Gemara's phrase "for what reason" means that they must tell him the reason, not necessarily write it into the document. Accordingly, Rama's phrase "all the more so" is accurate. Where the litigant expressly asks, the judges certainly must explain the entire rationale orally. Bach likewise writes that the reason must be stated orally. This is the correct interpretation.
