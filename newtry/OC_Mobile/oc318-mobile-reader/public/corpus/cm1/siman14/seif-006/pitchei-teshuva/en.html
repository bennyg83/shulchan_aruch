They remove him to be judged elsewhere. See Responsa Mayim Chayim, siman 1, who writes that from the unqualified wording of the Rama and the authorities it appears that this applies even if there is a permanent beit din in the city. This is also proven from siman 33, seif 6 in the gloss. He further writes that, at first glance, it appears that if they judged the case in that city, even after the fact their judgment is invalid, meaning where they judged him against his will. This follows because the Rama writes in siman 33, seif 6, regarding the father of the groom and the father of the bride, that after the fact the judgment is valid, whereas here, concerning a wealthy and violent litigant, the Rama states the rule without qualification, implying that here even after the fact the judgment is invalid.
