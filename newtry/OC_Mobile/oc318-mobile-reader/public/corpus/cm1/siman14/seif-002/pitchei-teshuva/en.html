Or two travelers. Rabbi Yehoshua Heschel Ashkenazi notes in his glosses that at first glance this appears contrary to Ramban in Parashat Shoftim, who writes that litigants cannot compel one another to adjudicate anywhere other than the beit din of their own city, and that even if both happen to be in another city one may say, “Let us go to the beit din of our city.” It appears, however, that the present case concerns two travelers who are not from the same city, but each from a different city. If they do not adjudicate where they presently meet, the plaintiff would have to follow the defendant to the defendant’s locale. Therefore, since both are presently together, they adjudicate there. Ramban, by contrast, speaks of litigants who are both residents of the same city. This distinction is logical.
