There, in the gloss: “Some say that regarding a great beit din” The Sema asks why Rama inserted the Rosh’s view into Rambam’s words and added “in that country.” The point is that Rambam, who does not distinguish a great beit din from a central tribunal and speaks of different “places,” refers only to locations within one country, since today one cannot compel travel from country to country as one could to the supreme court. His wording says places, not countries. Rama then adds that some maintain that even today an important tribunal has the status of the great beit din, even in another country. Thus even within Rambam’s approach there is a dispute concerning present conditions.
