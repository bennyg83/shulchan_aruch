Sema explains the gloss of Mordechai to Kiddushin in the name of Maharam of Rothenburg: there is concern that the distant authority receiving the written case will favor the Torah scholar. Sema says that even if the beit din omits the scholar's name, the authority might recognize his handwriting. Therefore the scholar should not write his own submission; beit din should copy it. That is not the main point. The better explanation is the form of presentation. If the scholar writes the arguments in his own first-person voice, the receiving authority can tell from the nature and quality of the arguments that a Torah scholar is the litigant and may be inclined to favor him. If beit din instead copies the claims in the court's neutral voice, presenting the proofs as the court's own formulation, the receiving authority has no reason to infer that one litigant is a Torah scholar. This is the proper meaning.
