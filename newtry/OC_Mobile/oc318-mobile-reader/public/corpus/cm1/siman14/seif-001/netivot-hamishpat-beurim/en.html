When is this said, in other disputes, etc. See Sema's interpretation. In my view his words require study. Why should a partner's claim be weaker than other claims? If one partner says the other holds his property and refuses to return it, that resembles a theft claim. Likewise, why should a rental claim differ from an ordinary damages claim? It appears that the phrase 'this one claims and that one claims' refers to a dispute where neither is currently defendant in possession, such as where each says, 'It belonged to my ancestors,' or, 'It is mine,' or where both physically hold the item, or the property is a third maneh with neither side possessing it. In such cases both are claimants. But where one person sues and the other is the defendant in possession, the claimant has a stronger procedural standing because the defendant is withholding property from him. Thus, if a lender says, 'I lent you one sela,' and the borrower says, 'I gave you a pledge worth two sela,' the borrower should be able to say that as to his affirmative claim for the excess value he wishes to proceed before the higher court. It is strained to say that because the pledge also serves as security for the loan, everything must be litigated together locally. Even if so, the borrower could surely say, 'I will first pay your debt and then sue you separately before the higher court.'
And the same law applies today. This refers back to the prior distinction concerning the Great Beit Din between claimant and defendant. Rama now says the same applies in our time where there is no such supreme court, but certain places function as a central court. The lender can compel the borrower to proceed there, but the borrower cannot similarly compel the lender. There are textual errors in Sema: one reference should be to seif 4, and another phrase should read that they did not trouble him to leave the country.
