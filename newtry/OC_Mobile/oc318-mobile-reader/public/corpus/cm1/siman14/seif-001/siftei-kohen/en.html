Let us ascend to the Great Beit Din. The Mechaber copied the language of Rambam, who does not distinguish between the Great Beit Din and a beit havaad, meaning a place where scholars gather. Tosafot, the Rosh, and the Tur do distinguish between them. So writes the Sema. Maharshal likewise rules this way in Yam Shel Shlomo, Bava Kamma, chapter 10, siman 10. See there.

If two scholars are equally ordained, etc. See Responsa Mahari ben Lev, part 3, end of siman 97.
We compel the borrower, etc. The Sema writes that this is only when the lender is not otherwise traveling there, citing Maharik, shoresh 90. There is no reason for this distinction, and Maharik erred in understanding the responsum of the Rashba. The responsum attributed to Rashba is in fact a responsum of Ramban, cited by the Sema in seif katan 7 and 8. Gloss: If the lender is otherwise traveling there, the borrower can compel him. The Bach likewise explains the source this way and disagrees with Maharik. He rules that the lender may compel the borrower to litigate there even when the lender must travel there in any event and the borrower does not. See there.
