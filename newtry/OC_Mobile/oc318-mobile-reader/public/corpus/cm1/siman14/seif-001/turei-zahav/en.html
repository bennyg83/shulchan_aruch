(Seif 1.) "When does this apply, when this one claims," etc. It appears to me that the explanation is as follows. At the beginning of the siman it says that two litigants became embroiled, etc. This refers to a dispute over ownership of some matter or the like, where there is no proper distinction between claimant and defendant because the item is not in either one's possession, and each is in effect a claimant. The same applies where the matter concerns a lender, an injured party, or a victim of theft, and it has already been established to some degree that he is a lender or injured party, while the opposing party seeks to defer him to the Great Beit Din. In such cases we do not listen to the one seeking removal, because there is no true defendant in possession. Thus the claim, "Let us go to the Great Beit Din," is effective only where it is established that one party is the defendant. In that case we listen to the claimant. The Sema's discussion here is not clear to me.
