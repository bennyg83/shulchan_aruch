(Collected note) When does this apply? In other, etc. The rule of "a borrower is servant to the lender" does not apply. However, the Rosh there states the reverse: whenever that principle does not apply, both sides may say, "I will go to the Great Beit Din." He explains the Bava Kamma passage, where the defendant may say so, as a case in which each has a claim against the other, or where the defendant says, "It never happened," so that the principle does not apply. The Rashba there similarly explains that only when a borrower is the claimant may he say so, but not when he is the defendant, because a borrower is servant, etc.; see the Tur. The Rambam, however, maintains that Shmuel in Bava Kamma there follows the initial understanding of Rabbi Yochanan in Sanhedrin. He therefore omitted Shmuel's ruling, because Rabbi Yochanan necessarily disagrees with Shmuel, as the Rambam rules that this applies specifically when they sent both a summons and, etc.; a summons alone does not suffice. According to Shmuel a summons alone would suffice, since the defendant may say so without taking his documents, as the Rosh writes there. Rabbi Yochanan holds that he cannot say, "I will go to the Great Beit Din." The Rosh follows his own view, as above, and likewise according to Rabbeinu Tam's resolution, as stated in the gloss. (End of collected note.)
