Seif 1. Note: See the Shach’s lengthy discussion whether exact hours are required in calculating years, and see Urim VeTumim. It appears possible to prove from the Gemara that they are. Our Sages expounded Moshe’s words, “I am unable to go out and come in today,” as meaning: “Today my days and my years were completed.” The commentators ask that elsewhere our Sages taught, regarding “because you did not believe in Me,” that had Moshe and Aharon sanctified Hashem properly, their time to depart the world had not yet arrived. In truth there is no contradiction. “My years were completed” does not mean that a fixed heavenly allotment of years had necessarily run out, but that Hashem fills a righteous person’s years from day to day, so that his life ended on the anniversary of his birth rather than in the middle of a year. Yet why speak of both “my days” and “my years”? One expression should suffice. Furthermore, since Moshe was born on the seventh of Adar and died on the seventh of Adar, how could he say on that day, “I am one hundred and twenty years old today”? Once the night of the seventh of Adar began, the sixth had ended and he had already entered the next year. This is especially difficult according to the view that one day in a year can count as a year. It must be that exact hours are relevant in years. Only at the corresponding hour on the seventh of Adar did he complete the full one hundred and twenty years. Thus the Sages said that his “days and years” were completed today, teaching that just as a day can be reckoned by a complete twenty-four-hour period and by hours for certain laws, so too a year is completed only when its corresponding hours have elapsed. This appears to me, with Hashem’s help, a clear proof.
