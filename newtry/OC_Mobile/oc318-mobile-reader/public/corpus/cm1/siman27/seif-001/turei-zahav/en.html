(Seif 1: he receives another set of lashes.) The Tur does not write that he violates two prohibitions. It is difficult to understand why, in truth, he receives only one set of lashes. One may answer based on Tosafos in Shevuot 36, who write that the prohibition against cursing an ordinary person is derived by a common denominator from the deaf person and one's father. In chapter 4 of Sanhedrin, page 66, the Gemara says that the warning concerning one's father is derived from "You shall not curse elohim," meaning a judge. Thus, regarding a judge there are not two independent warnings. The warning applying to an ordinary person itself ultimately derives from the verse concerning a judge, except that Scripture reveals that an ordinary person is treated like a judge for this purpose. For lashes, once the offender has already been lashed for cursing the judge under "You shall not curse a judge," how can he be lashed a second time by virtue of the ordinary person's prohibition, when that prohibition itself derives from the same verse? The words of Rambam and the Shulchan Aruch, who state that there are two prohibitions, therefore require explanation. This seems to me a pleasing and acceptable resolution.
