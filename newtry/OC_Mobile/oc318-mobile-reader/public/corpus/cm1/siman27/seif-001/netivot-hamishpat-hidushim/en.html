Or by one of the names by which non Jews refer to God. This implies that such a term has the legal status of a Divine appellation, Sema. Due to our many sins, much of the public is not careful in this matter and says in the vernacular phrases meaning, "God should punish him," or, "God should strike him," thereby violating a Torah prohibition. More generally, people unfortunately fail to treat Divine appellations in foreign languages with proper care, imagining that a written or spoken foreign term is merely descriptive. This is an error. They write in debt instruments a French expression meaning "with God," which is a Divine appellation, and the document is later discarded. The Sages once established a festival because they succeeded in preventing the Divine Name from being written in ordinary legal documents, since after a debtor repaid, the Name would be cast away. Now, unfortunately, people have imitated foreign practice and the corruption has returned even more strongly. This rebellion contributes to the humiliation of Israel, particularly where Divine appellations are common and are treated disrespectfully and needlessly. The scholars of the generation must act wisely and persistently to rebuke the public and abolish this practice. If it was before witnesses. The reason the Shulchan Aruch mentions both lashes and the separate prohibition of cursing a judge, even though due to the absence of classical semichah we have no court empowered to administer such lashes, is that there remains a practical consequence for disqualification from testimony. We rule below in siman 34 that one who violates a prohibition that does not carry lashes is not disqualified as a witness by Torah law. Thus the fact that two prohibitions are incurred also has practical relevance. If a person did not know that cursing an ordinary fellow Jew is forbidden, he is not necessarily disqualified as a witness, as explained below in siman 34 regarding prohibitions that the public commonly treats as permitted.
