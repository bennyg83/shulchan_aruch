If there was no prior warning, if he cursed without using the Divine Name or a substitute for it, or if the curse was implied by his words, such as saying, 'May so-and-so not be blessed by Hashem,' he does not receive lashes. The same applies to one who curses the dead. Nevertheless, the act is forbidden. If he reviled a Torah scholar, he is placed under a ban; if the judges wish, they may administer lashes for rebellious conduct and punish him as they deem appropriate. If he reviled an unlearned person, they punish him as the needs of the time require. Even if the person reviled forgives him, he is punished, because he has already sinned and incurred liability. If a person became liable to excommunication for behaving contemptuously toward the beit din, the beit din may waive its honor and refrain from excommunicating him, provided that this will not diminish the honor of the Creator. If the people have become defiant toward the honor of the Torah and its judges, the beit din must strengthen the matter and punish as it deems appropriate.
