The dead calve is exempted from the “excellence in the blood of even the one whose curse has not known is dead and thinks that he is alive:
Even if the person insulted forgave, etc. Sema and Bach explain that Rama's word "insulted" is not exact. The clause refers to one who curses a sage or another person, whether with a curse that carries Torah lashes or with a lesser prohibited curse. Personal forgiveness cannot erase the Torah prohibition itself. Who can forgive the violation against Heaven? But where the wrong consisted only of verbal humiliation or insult for which beit din would discipline the offender in order to vindicate the victim's honor, the victim may waive that honor. This follows the rule that beit din can waive its own honor in an appropriate case. The distinction is correct and straightforward.
