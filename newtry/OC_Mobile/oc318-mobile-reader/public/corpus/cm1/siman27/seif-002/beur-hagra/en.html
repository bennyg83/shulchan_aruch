(Collected note) For example, if he said, etc. The Mishnah there does not state it this way, because there it concerns an oath. Regarding a curse, however, it cannot be understood that way, and one must explain as written here. This is learned from the statement there, "May He bless you and do good for you"; nevertheless it is not an actual curse, even though he said "God, etc.," as stated in Kiddushin 61b regarding "if you walk in My statutes, etc." (End of collected note.)
(Collected note) For example, if he said, etc. So the Rambam explains the Mishnah there, that "God" refers to "may He bless you, etc."; see his commentary. This depends on a major dispute. See Ran, Nedarim 11b, s.v. "LeChullin, etc." The Rosh there, however, writes that in such a case even Rabbi Meir agrees, and so rules the Tur in siman 204. The Rambam similarly writes in chapter 1 of Hilchot Nedarim. The Kesef Mishnah and Beit Yosef strain to explain that what the Gemara says there, "they thought," was ultimately rejected, so that even Rabbi Meir agrees, as in the cited baraita. The Lechem Mishneh writes that the Rambam follows the Rosh, and Tosafot write similarly. If so, the Rambam's words and those of the Tur and Shulchan Aruch here require study, according to both the Kesef Mishnah's resolution and the Lechem Mishneh's. See below, siman 241, whether we rule like Rabbi Meir, and the Rosh and Ran there. Further study is needed because according to Rashi in Shevuot there, at the end of the chapter, with a bare expression of prohibition even Rabbi Meir agrees that one is liable. Likewise according to Tosafot there, s.v. "Hachi Garsinan," who write, "it appears to answer, etc.," here, where the unique Divine Name is unnecessary, all agree that one is liable. One must answer as stated there in the name of the Ritva, that this applies specifically to a severe prohibition. The Rosh and Ran in Nedarim there, however, answer that vows are a prohibition involving monetary consequences, and so rule the other decisors. See the Ran. (End of collected note.)

And even if he waived it. See Beer HaGolah. Makkot 13b, concerning those liable to kareit, etc., "for a lower beit din, etc." Thus a matter dependent on beit din cannot be waived. Further, throughout the Talmud we derive those liable to lashes from those liable to capital punishment by beit din; see Makkot 5a, 5b, Sanhedrin 10, 33, and Bava Kamma 86b.
(Collected note) Even if he waived it, etc. This requires study, for the Rambam wrote this concerning witnesses and warning, where one cannot waive liability for lashes. The words here may be understood similarly. (End of collected note.)
