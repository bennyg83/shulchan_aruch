If he said to her, 'Behold, you are my wife,' 'Behold, you are bound to me,' etc.; if he said to her, 'Behold, you are designated for me,' 'Behold, you are set apart for me,' 'my helpmate,' etc. The wording of the Gemara, Kiddushin 6a, is: Our Rabbis taught, if he says, 'Behold, you are my wife,' 'Behold, you are my betrothed,' etc., she is betrothed. They asked: what is the law if he says, 'set apart for me,' 'designated for me,' 'my helpmate,' 'my counterpart,' 'my restrained one,' 'my rib,' 'my enclosed one,' 'under me,' 'my seized one,' or 'my acquired one'? They resolve from a baraita that one who says, 'my acquired one,' effects kiddushin, as it is stated, 'When a man takes a woman.' They further ask about the term 'my charufah.' The Gemara then asks: in what case are we dealing? If he was not speaking with her about matters of kiddushin, how does she know what he means so as to consent to kiddushin? Rather, if he was speaking with her about kiddushin, then even if he gave her the item silently she would be betrothed. The Gemara answers that he was speaking with her about kiddushin, but when he gave it he used one of these expressions. The question is whether these expressions signify kiddushin or perhaps signify engaging her for work. Rashi explains that if they are expressions of kiddushin she is betrothed, but if they mean that she should come perform his work, his words reveal that he does not presently intend kiddushin.
