Even if the person insulted forgave, etc. This requires study, for the words of the Rama imply that even insults directed at a Torah scholar cannot be forgiven. Yet Maimonides and the Tur clearly imply that the Sages said this only concerning one who curses any person, because at the moment of the curse he already violated a Torah prohibition, and the victim lacks authority to forgive that violation. By contrast, ordinary verbal insult and degradation may be forgiven. These are the words of the Sema. The Bach explains the Rama differently: whether the offender would have incurred lashes by Torah law, such as where he was warned and cursed using the Divine Name or an appellation, and he is called merely an insulter because lashes are not administered today; or whether he was not warned, cursed without the Divine Name or an appellation, or said, “May he not be blessed,” where only a Torah prohibition applies, nevertheless, since he sinned and already incurred punishment under Torah law, the victim cannot forgive it, and the court punishes him as it sees fit. These are the words of the Bach. This explanation is difficult.
