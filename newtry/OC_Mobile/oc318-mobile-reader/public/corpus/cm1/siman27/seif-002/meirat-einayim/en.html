Even if the insulted person waived, etc. This requires study. Rama's words imply that waiver is ineffective even for insulting a Torah scholar. Rambam and Tur, however, indicate clearly that the Sages said this only regarding actual cursing of any person. The curse already violated a Torah prohibition at the moment it was spoken, and the victim has no power to waive that prohibition. But ordinary verbal insult and degradation can be waived, just as one who deserves excommunication for insulting beit din can be forgiven by the court, as the Mechaber concludes here. Although the Tur uses the phrase "they strike him," it means lashes for the Torah prohibition, not disciplinary lashes merely for insult, as Rambam and Kesef Mishneh make explicit. Rama's wording therefore requires study.
