Meaning, one should not say that since a tam ox pays only from its own body, if Reuven's ox gored Shimon's ox and caused damage of one hundred, and afterward Shimon's ox gored Reuven's ox and caused damage of fifty, and Shimon's ox was then lost, Reuven should lose his claim because he has nothing from which to collect, while Shimon should collect fifty from Reuven's ox. The ruling teaches otherwise. As soon as they damage one another, their liabilities are offset against one another to the extent of the respective damages, and only the excess is subject to payment of half-damages. Sema.
From its body. The Nimukei Yosef writes in the chapter HaManiach: One should know that when we say that they pay half-damages on the excess, this applies only to the amount each can actually collect from the other. Thus, if an ox worth two hundred gored an ox worth one hundred and diminished its value by eighty, and the latter then gored the former and diminished its value by one hundred, we do not set forty, the half-damages owed by the first, against forty of the fifty owed by the latter, and then allow the first owner to collect ten from the latter's ox. That would distort justice, because the latter ox, which caused one hundred in damage, is now worth only twenty, and the first owner can collect only from its body, while the first ox, which owes fifty to the latter, is still worth one hundred and can satisfy the debt. Rather, we offset twenty against twenty, the amount each can collect from the other, and the latter owner collects twenty from the first ox, representing the excess of his half-damage claim. It follows that at times the party who suffered the smaller damage collects more than the one who suffered the greater damage. Ibid.
