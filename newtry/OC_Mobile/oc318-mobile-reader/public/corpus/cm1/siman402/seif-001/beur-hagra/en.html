And even, etc. Tosafot, s.v. they taught, writes that even according to Rabbi Yehuda there is a practical difference, and Nimukei Yosef writes likewise, even according to Rabbi Akiva.
And specifically, etc. See Sema. This accords with Ketubot 110a, where Rami bar Hama says, 'here, etc.' Even according to Rava there, who says, 'let him collect them, etc.,' that does not apply here, because payment is collected only from the body of the damaging animal.
For example, if it caused damage, etc. The Tur writes this to exclude the explanation that Rosh cites in the name of Rashi.
Some say, etc., that every, etc. This follows what is stated on 24b, like the case of a walking animal that kicked one lying down. The meaning is that even Rava disagrees only because the owner can say, 'when you have, etc.' Even a person has permission in such a case, for regarding monetary matters the Gemara states at the beginning of the third chapter, 'one hundred panes, etc.' Although the rule that one who deviates loses protection does not generally apply to a person, see Tosafot 27b, s.v. this is the correct text, and Hagahot Ashri there. All the more so it applies to an ox. This is the meaning of, 'and the same applies below, etc.' See what is written there.
And the Mechaber, etc. The Rama's words are difficult, because no authority disputes this point, and the Mechaber did not mention it at all, relying instead on what he wrote in siman 451. The Rama was led to this by the Tur, who writes that Rosh limited the ruling to where both animals began at the same time, and then adds that when they began together the innocuous animal likewise pays full damages like the forewarned animal, which he inferred from Rashi. The Tur then says that Rabbenu Yitzchak disagrees and holds that whether they began together or successively, collection follows the rule previously explained. This appears to imply that Rabbenu Yitzchak disputes even the first limitation, and the Rama understood that the two points depend upon one another, since Rabbenu Yitzchak is nowhere found explicitly disputing the first. Thus, by explaining the Mishna like Rabbenu Yitzchak, as indicated by the words, 'for example, if it caused damage,' the Mechaber would also accept his view on the second point. This is incorrect. Rabbenu Yitzchak does not dispute the first rule, nor is it Rashi's view. Rather, the Tur's text from 'whether they began' through 'successively' is a scribal error, as Yam Shel Shlomo explains. See 451.
