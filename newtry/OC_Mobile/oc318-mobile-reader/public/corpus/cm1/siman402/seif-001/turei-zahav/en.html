Seif 1, some say there is no distinction. The Sema writes that the Rama was imprecise because there is no dispute in this matter, and that is correct. The Tur writes: “So the Rosh inferred from Rashi, but Rabbeinu Yitzchak does not agree; rather, whether they began simultaneously or one after another, collection is made as I first explained.” The phrase “one after another” means after the animals separated, as the Tur had explained earlier. The Tur did not cite Rabbeinu Yitzchak as disputing the first rule. The dispute concerns the second rule, whether according to Rabbeinu Yitzchak an innocuous ox can pay more than half damages when both began together. Rabbeinu Yitzchak compares that case to one after another following separation, where it is obvious that an innocuous ox pays no more than half damages. Maharshal writes that Tosafot never disputed the first rule and that the text contains an error. He understood the Tur's “one after another” as referring to the first case, where one animal began and, before they separated, the other retaliated. That is not correct; it means what I have explained. Thus, the Rama's first ruling here is undisputed. It is nevertheless difficult why the Shulchan Aruch omitted this rule here, though it records the parallel rule concerning people in siman 421. Perhaps that omission prompted the Rama's comment. The Rama should also have stated that with oxen, even if the second animal injured the first more than the first injured it, the second owner is exempt for the excess, unlike the rule concerning people explained later. Maharshal writes that even if the second ox killed the first, its owner is exempt for the excess, while the owner of the first remains liable for the damage his ox caused to the second.
