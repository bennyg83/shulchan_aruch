Two oxen that injured one another, whether both were innocuous or both forewarned. It contains one seif.
If two innocuous oxen injured one another, half-damages are paid on the net difference. Thus, if one caused damage worth one hundred and the other damage worth fifty, the first fifty offsets the second fifty, and half of the remaining fifty is paid, even if one of the oxen was subsequently lost. This applies only if each ox is worth enough to cover the amount collectible from its body. If not, only the amount actually collectible from that ox is offset. If both were forewarned, full damages are paid on the net difference. If one was innocuous and the other forewarned, and the forewarned ox caused greater damage, its owner pays full damages on the excess after crediting half the damage caused by the innocuous ox. For example, if the forewarned ox caused one hundred and the innocuous ox caused fifty, the forewarned ox's owner pays seventy-five. If the innocuous ox caused greater damage, its owner pays half-damages subject to credit for the full damage caused by the forewarned ox. Thus, if the innocuous ox caused one hundred and the forewarned ox caused twenty-five, the innocuous ox's owner pays twenty-five. Rama: Some say all this applies only where both began simultaneously, or where after they separated the second returned and injured the first. If one began the attack, the aggressor pays and the other is exempt, because when one deviates and another responds with a deviation, the responder is exempt. This is also ruled in siman 421 regarding two people who injured one another. The Mechaber's rulings here and there are not fully consistent.
