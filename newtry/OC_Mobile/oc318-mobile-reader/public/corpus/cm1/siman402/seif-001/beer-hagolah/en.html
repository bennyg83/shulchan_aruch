a) The language of the Tur, and so writes the Rambam at the end of chapter 9 of Hilkhot Nizkei Mamon, from the Mishnah in the third chapter of Bava Kamma 33a.
bet) Ibid., in the Mishnah.
gimel) The Tur in the name of Rabbeinu Yitzchak. The Beit Yosef writes that this also appears from the Rambam in chapter 9, and this is the ruling we follow.
dalet) In my humble opinion, a distinction should be made. Here the Rosh imposes liability based on the principle that one who deviates from the ordinary course and another then responds is responsible for the consequences, and on that point the later authority disagrees with him. But in siman 421, regarding two people, the Rosh explains that the second person has permission to injure the first in order to save himself. Even there, however, an assessment is required: if he could have saved himself with a lesser injury, he must pay the excess. This concept of self-protection does not apply to oxen. The Sema explains differently; the reader may choose.
