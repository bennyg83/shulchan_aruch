Because of the seller. See Sema, seif katan 1. If the buyer expressly stipulated responsibility “because of me,” Sema says this can exclude ordinary third-party responsibility. Beit Yosef explains that responsibility “because of me” means repossession by the seller’s creditor, while general responsibility includes a prior theft claim. At first glance this is difficult. If the buyer expressly insists on protection against the seller’s creditor, although a person sometimes buys land for only temporary use, all the more so one would expect him to insist that the field not turn out to have been stolen altogether, which would undo the purchase and require return of fruits. He would not throw away his money. Shitah Mekubetzet on Bava Metzia 15 asks this and answers that the ordinary reason unstated responsibility is presumed is that a seller wishes to maintain his trustworthiness. A person who himself stole the field has already shown that he is not concerned with honest dealing, so we cannot infer ordinary responsibility from his conduct. Accordingly, if the seller did not himself steal the field but had purchased it in good faith without knowing it was stolen and then resold it, responsibility for the theft should be included even in responsibility “because of me.” Even where he himself stole it, Shitah concludes that the matter requires further analysis.
