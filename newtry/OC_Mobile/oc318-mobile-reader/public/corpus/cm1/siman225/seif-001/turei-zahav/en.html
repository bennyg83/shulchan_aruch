(Seif 6: except for unavoidable libation.) It appears to me that nevertheless, before the libation occurred, where he knows that the wine is about to be rendered forbidden, as in the responsum of the Rosh cited by the Tur, in which the oppressor came to plunder the villages where the wine was located, if the seller warns the buyer either to take the wine or cancel the sale, while the seller will go there and retrieve it, the seller is certainly heeded. The buyer has no right to cause the seller a direct loss. The responsum of the Rosh did not discuss such a case. There, the seller also wished to leave the wine in place, but sought to strengthen the buyer's position by treating the wine as remaining in the seller's domain and under the seller's fortune, so that the sale would be void. Nevertheless, even in the case I described, if the buyer says, “Go and save your wine and place it before me in a safe location,” the law is with him. He cannot, however, compel the seller to leave the wine in a dangerous place while the seller bears responsibility for it.
