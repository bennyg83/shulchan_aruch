A columnist named for his father's response, C. B
He explains that the language can be read narrowly in either direction. One can infer that the seller accepted only liability for breaking and spilling, but not for any other loss. Conversely, one can infer that the buyer accepted only the risk of deterioration in taste, not other losses. We therefore leave the money in the possession of its holder and do not remove it from the buyer. Moreover, the accident of libation is comparable to an outside accident of spilling or breaking. The parties mentioned spilling and breaking because those accidents are common, whereas libation is uncommon and did not occur to them to specify. The buyer, however, accepted only liability for the wine itself if its taste deteriorates, and an outside accident is not included in that undertaking.
