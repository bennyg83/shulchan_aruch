The ones of libation. Sema, seif katan 12, says the parties did not contemplate such an event. At first glance this is difficult. If libation by a non Jew is an unusual ones, the seller should plainly be exempt under seifim 3 and 4. Perishah asks this and answers that because the buyer stipulated only regarding souring, the seller remains responsible for every other ones. In my view that is difficult. Even if the buyer expressly said “all ones that may arise,” the seller would still be exempt from an extremely unusual ones. The fact that souring was specified proves nothing, because souring is common. Rather, the explanation is that theft by a non Jew is an ordinary, foreseeable ones for which the seller is liable under seif 3. If a non Jew comes in the course of such a foreseeable attempted theft and incidentally renders the wine prohibited by libation, that consequence is included in the ordinary risk of the non Jew’s entry. Sema’s statement that the parties “did not contemplate it” means only that they did not separately list remote events such as fire and the like; the libation here flows from a common class of risk already covered by the undertaking.
