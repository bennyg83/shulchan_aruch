But if he brought Jewish witnesses, the seller is liable, because a Jewish beit din would likewise have awarded the property to the claimant, and in such a case the law of the kingdom is law. This is stronger than a case where the seller himself gave it to the claimant on the basis of witnesses. See Beurim: this applies specifically to witnesses of theft. But witnesses together with evidence that the object was seen in his possession, although in a Jewish beit din such evidence would permit recovery, are ineffective when the holder is a gentile, because the presumption that whatever is in a person's possession belongs to him does not apply to a gentile, since an ordinary gentile is presumed capable of robbery.
