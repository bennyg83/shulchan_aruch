The seller is not responsible for unavoidable accident. If the gentile challenger had already asserted his claim before the purchaser bought, and the purchaser did not know of it, the sale is a mistaken sale. We do not say merely, "Show your document of seizure," because an existing challenge is itself a defect in the property, like a reduction in value. Tosafot says that where a field was sold with warranty and a claim had already been lodged against it, the seller cannot simply say, "Take your field back for your money," because the outstanding challenge has already reduced the field's value. There is no greater defect than that. This differs from a challenge that arises only after the purchaser completed a valid acquisition and began using the property. In that case, as the Shulchan Aruch states elsewhere, he cannot cancel the sale merely because the later challenge arose. But if the challenge existed before the acquisition and was unknown to him, it is a preexisting defect and the sale is voidable.
