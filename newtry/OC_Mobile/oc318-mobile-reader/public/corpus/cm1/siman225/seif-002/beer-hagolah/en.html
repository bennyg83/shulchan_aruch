The Maggid Mishneh writes that this is always the law: he is liable only if the property is taken from him under our law, as stated in Bava Kamma 9a, 'show me the judgment under which it was seized from you.' Rashi explains that it must have been lawfully seized. This is primary. Even if the party who seized it was a Jew, if he took it unlawfully the seller is not liable. The Kesef Mishneh writes that this reasoning is sound because everything follows what our law would require. He nevertheless remains uncertain why the seller is not liable when the property was removed under the king's law, since the law of the kingdom is law. Perhaps an unqualified acceptance of warranty means liability only where Torah law imposes liability, not merely the law of the kingdom, despite its legal validity. Even this answer is not fully satisfactory.
