And he brought gentile witnesses. If he brings Jewish witnesses, the seller is liable, because had the matter come before a Jewish beit din the claimant could likewise have seized the property. In such a case the law of the king and his courts is recognized, even though if the purchaser had voluntarily handed over the property without legal compulsion he could not return against the seller. The Tur adds: even if the item was a donkey and the gentile took the donkey but left the saddle, so one might think the gentile was telling the truth because he took only what he claimed as his own, the seller is nevertheless not liable. The Gemara states this. In the Perishah I explained that the saddle is mentioned because people ordinarily object to placing a saddle on a horse or donkey for which it was not fitted, since an ill fitted saddle can cause injury. One might therefore have inferred from the gentile's leaving the saddle that his claim was genuine. The ruling teaches otherwise.
