Even if the land was sold by document. See Shach, seif katan 1, citing Rosh in the name of Ri: even according to the view that responsibility is not a scribal presumption, that is true where the parties deliberately used a deed and omitted responsibility, which may indicate a condition to exclude it. But with an oral sale before witnesses, ordinary responsibility is presumed. I find this difficult. Shmuel, who holds that responsibility is not a scribal presumption, says in Bava Metzia 19 that a scribe should consult the seller before writing clauses of improved collection, improvements, and fruits. According to Ri, why must the scribe consult? If the witnesses heard no express condition excluding responsibility, why should they not write the ordinary responsibility clauses without further consultation? Shach’s abbreviated citation from Ramban concerns the rule that a deed may be written for the seller when the seller acknowledges before witnesses that he sold the field for money. Why is there no concern that perhaps at the original sale he did not accept responsibility and became obligated only later when the deed was delivered, creating a date problem like a loan deed? The answer is that an ordinary sale carries responsibility. This supports the general rule.
