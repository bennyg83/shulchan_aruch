The buyer before a Jewish beit din. See Sema, seif katan 5, who says that even if the dispossessing party has witnesses of theft or a loan, if the buyer voluntarily delivers the property based only on those witnesses without a beit din judgment, he cannot recover from the seller, who may say, “Show me your authorization to collect and I will pay.” This is understandable for a creditor. If the buyer pays the creditor without litigation, he resembles one who pays another’s debt without authorization. The seller can say, “I could have appeased him,” and the debtor’s assets function like guarantors; before beit din directs payment, a volunteer may lose his right of recourse. But with witnesses proving theft, the matter is different. The buyer can still compel the person who took the field to produce and establish those theft witnesses before beit din. Once the witnesses are accepted, beit din can write the buyer an authorization to recover even after he already delivered the field. Perishah says Maggid Mishneh writes otherwise, but the passage there appears to concern only a taking not established according to law. Therefore Sema’s intent should also be limited: so long as beit din has not accepted the theft testimony, the buyer cannot rely upon it for recourse. If he later brings the witnesses and beit din formally establishes the theft, an authorization to recover from the seller may then be written.
