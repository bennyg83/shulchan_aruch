The foregoing applies when the seller made no special condition. If he stipulated that he would be liable for every accident or loss that might occur to this land, then even if a non-Jew comes and seizes it because of the seller, the seller is liable. But if the river that irrigated the land dries up, or the river changes course and flows through the land so that it becomes a pool, or an earthquake comes and destroys it, the seller is exempt. These and similar events are extraordinary accidents that the seller could not reasonably have contemplated when he made the condition. The same principle applies to every monetary condition: we assess the intent of the person who stipulated, and the condition includes only the kinds of events known to have motivated the stipulation and that were in his mind when he made it.
