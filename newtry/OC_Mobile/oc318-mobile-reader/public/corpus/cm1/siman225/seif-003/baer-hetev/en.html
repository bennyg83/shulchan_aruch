Unavoidable circumstance. If a Jew sued him and he prevailed in judgment, but the claimant refuses to obey the ruling and is a violent person, this is an unavoidable circumstance and the seller is not liable under his general responsibility. Divrei Rivot, siman 110. This applies to a very common unavoidable circumstance. An event that is neither common nor exceedingly rare is not included unless expressly accepted. Divrei Rivot, siman 81; this also appears from R. Shlomo Yonah, siman 40. Maharashdam, siman 33, however, writes that even such an intermediate circumstance is included. See also there, siman 104; Mekor Baruch, siman 51; Responsa Rivash, siman 150; Raanach, part 2, siman 31; Rashach, part 2, siman 32; and Responsa Maharil, siman 37. If there is doubt whether an event is very common or only of intermediate frequency, the burden of proof rests on the claimant. Divrei Rivot, siman 81; Maharashdam, simanim 33 and 100; Rashach, part 1, siman 21. Captivity is an uncommon unavoidable circumstance. Mordechai, chapter Mi SheAchazo; Maharik, shoresh 7. See Responsa R. Betzalel Ashkenazi, siman 31. A fall or rise in currency values is an uncommon circumstance, Maharashdam, siman 75. Knesset HaGedolah writes that this was correct in his time, when currency devaluation had been unheard of except on that occasion, but in our time, when it has happened repeatedly, it is considered very common. Lechem Rav, siman 69, writes similarly although he lived in Maharashdam's period. See Responsa R. Moshe Alshich, siman 115. The rule that an uncommon circumstance is not included applies when one accepts responsibility without specification. But if he says that he will not advance any claim of unavoidable circumstance whatsoever, this broad language includes even an uncommon circumstance. Maharam Alashkar, siman 32. Knesset HaGedolah writes that the Rashba cited by the Beit Yosef here, who says that even if one writes that he accepts every unavoidable circumstance in the world an uncommon event is not included, does not disagree, because saying 'I accept every unavoidable circumstance in the world' is not identical to saying 'I will not advance any claim of unavoidable circumstance whatsoever.' See Ralbach, siman 127; Mishpetei Shmuel, siman 29; and Rashach, part 1, siman 88. All this is cited in Bnei Chayei. See there for further discussion.
