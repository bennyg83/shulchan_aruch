The wording of Rambam there, law 15. The Maggid Mishneh explains his words as Rama writes in the first opinion: the case is where one person overloaded beyond the proper amount, so that the load itself became a pursuer. This is the incident involving the donkey there. In my humble opinion Rambam follows Rashi: the people were already in the boat, and this person later brought in the donkey; the donkey's weight threatened to sink the boat and was therefore a pursuer. Nimukei Yosef likewise writes this. According to the text quoted by the Maggid Mishneh this works well. According to our texts and Rif, which read that the donkey entered before the other, one must explain that it was an unusually dangerous donkey, kicking and leaping, and one passenger pushed it out so the boat would not sink. Rava exempted him because the donkey was a pursuer. Mordechai, cited by the Ramah, writes that if it is customary to bring donkeys aboard, the one who throws it overboard is liable. In my humble opinion this is difficult, for even a person who behaves abnormally and endangers the entire boat may be stopped even at the cost of his life. Beit Yosef also questions it. Rama presents the Maggid Mishneh's explanation as one opinion because Raavad and Nimukei Yosef interpret Rambam differently. The Maggid Mishneh's explanation is correct as law. Sema has another explanation of Rambam. This is not the place to elaborate.
