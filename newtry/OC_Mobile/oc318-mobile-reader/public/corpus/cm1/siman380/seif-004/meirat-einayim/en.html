See the Derishah, where I cited the Maggid Mishneh and Beit Yosef's explanation of Rambam so that his ruling here concerning a ship in danger of breaking does not contradict what he writes at the end of chapter 12 of Hilchot Gezeilah, based on the baraita in the final chapter of Bava Kamma, that in such a case the loss is apportioned according to the weight of each person's cargo. They also thereby answer Ra'avad's objection to Rambam. According to their explanation, the present case is where one or two passengers loaded cargo last and thereby overloaded the ship to the point that it was in danger of breaking and sinking. If one of the earlier passengers then throws overboard the cargo added by those last persons, he is exempt. The other passage concerns a ship that is not overloaded but is endangered by waves, and one passenger lightens the ship so that it can move more easily and escape the waves. Even if he throws overboard the merchandise of the person who loaded last, we do not place the entire loss on that person's property, because he was not negligent by overloading. The bad fortune of all caused the danger. Therefore the loss is divided among all passengers according to the weight of their cargo, as the Tur and Mechaber write above at the end of siman 272. The one who threw the goods overboard also fulfilled a mitzvah by saving the passengers and everything aboard and is no more personally liable than anyone else. It appears that Ra'avad, who objects to Rambam, would agree with this legal distinction; he simply did not understand Rambam's present words that way. See the Perishah. I wrote another resolution in the Perishah. Rambam here, at the end of the laws of damages, is not addressing who ultimately bears the loss of the cargo thrown into the sea, but only whether the individual who physically caused the damage is personally liable as a tortfeasor. His point is that one who lightens the ship is exempt from personally paying the entire loss because the cargo itself was endangering the ship and he acted to save life and property. The distribution of the ultimate loss is a separate question. If it is known that the owner of the thrown cargo was the one who overloaded the ship at the end, that loss falls upon him. If not, the loss of the discarded cargo is apportioned among everyone according to the weight of each person's load, which is the baraita in the final chapter of Bava Kamma and which Rambam records at the end of Hilchot Chovel. This also explains why Rambam there uses plural language, "they lightened the ship," for he is no longer discussing the liability of one individual who acted on his own, but the collective apportionment of the loss. Examine carefully.
