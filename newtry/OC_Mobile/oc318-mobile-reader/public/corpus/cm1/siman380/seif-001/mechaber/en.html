One who tells another to tear his garment or another's, and the law of a pursuer or pursued person who breaks utensils. It contains four seifim.
If one says, 'Tear my garment' or 'Break my jug' on condition that you are exempt, the actor is exempt. Some say explicit words of exemption are unnecessary where the exchange clearly implies them. If no exemption was stated, he is liable despite permission, when the objects first came into his hands for safekeeping, such as borrowed or deposited objects. But if they did not come into his hands as a bailee, then when the owner says, 'Take this utensil and break it,' or, 'Take this garment and tear it,' he is exempt even without explicit exemption. Regarding one who says, 'Throw a maneh into the sea and I will owe you,' some impose liability and others exempt.
