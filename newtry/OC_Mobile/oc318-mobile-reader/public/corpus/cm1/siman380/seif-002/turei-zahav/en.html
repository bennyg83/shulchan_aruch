[Seif 4] Some say specifically. This wording is imprecise. These are the words of the Maggid Mishneh, who explains the Rambam's ruling, cited by the Mechaber, that the load is the pursuer. The Rama abbreviated excessively. He should have written that it is known that this person placed his load aboard last, and therefore his load is the pursuer. This is the Maggid Mishneh's intent, and Maharshal cites it likewise, for otherwise there is no way to identify which load should be called the pursuer. That is the Maggid Mishneh's view. I wonder, however, why the Rambam's words should not be understood simply. The Rambam certainly agrees that all the owners must share the loss, as in the case of a wave threatening to sink a ship. His point here is only that the person who cast the load overboard is not personally liable, because he performed a mitzvah. Every load on the ship has the status of a pursuer, and casting one person's load is treated as casting a proportional part of each person's load. They therefore divide the loss among all of them according to the weight of their cargo.
