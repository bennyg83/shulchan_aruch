"The utensils of so and so, on condition that you be exempt," etc. Everyone agrees he is liable. Even if the person expressly said, "on condition that you be exempt," the one who breaks the property is liable because he knows it belongs to another. Although the two clauses appear together in the Gemara, the cited authorities understand each as applying in all forms. In the first clause, where he tells him to break his own property, he is exempt even if the speaker says "No." In the second clause, where he tells him to break another person's property, he is liable in every case.
