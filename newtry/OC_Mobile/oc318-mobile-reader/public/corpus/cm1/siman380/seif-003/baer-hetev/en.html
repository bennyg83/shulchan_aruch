Forbidden relations. The Tur's wording includes both those liable to karet and those liable to execution by beit din, as well as one who pursues a male in order to violate him. The Sema explains that although one liable to karet is not subject to execution by beit din, the pursuer is nevertheless exempt for breaking utensils because the rule that the greater liability precludes monetary payment applies, since he has incurred a capital liability at the hands of Heaven. This is very difficult. The accepted halachah rejects the view of Rabbi Nechunya ben HaKanah, and the rule that the greater liability precludes payment is applied only to offenses punishable by execution by beit din, as stated in Ketubot 30 and Shevuot 33 and in many places, and so rule all the authorities. Moreover, even Rabbi Nechunya agrees here that at the time of pursuit there is monetary liability, as stated in Ketubot there. See above siman 350 and below siman 385. Rather, the matter is simple: here the pursuer becomes liable to be killed because the pursued parties are permitted to kill him to save themselves. This is explicit in Bava Kamma 117 and Sanhedrin 74. Rashi there likewise explains that once he pursued, he rendered himself liable to death. According to the Sema's explanation, the law of a pursuer would be unnecessary. This is clear, and my revered teacher likewise wrote so in his glosses to the Sema. Shach.
