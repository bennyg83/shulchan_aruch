That he must hire others. The duty to save another person with one's own body is learned from "you shall restore it to him," which includes restoring another person's body from danger. The prohibition "You shall not stand idly by your fellow's blood" adds that one must even spend money and hire others when necessary to save him. The Rosh and Tur write that if the endangered person has money with which he could have saved himself, he must reimburse the rescuer for the expense. The Rosh brings proof. It is surprising that the Mechaber and Rama omitted this.
He violates "You shall not stand idly," etc. Hagahot Maimoniyot writes that the Yerushalmi concludes that one must even enter a situation of possible personal danger in order to save another from certain danger. The Beit Yosef cites this and writes that the rationale is that the other person's danger is certain while the rescuer's is only doubtful. The Mechaber and Rama omitted this too. Here the omission can be explained because the Rif, Rambam, Rosh, and Tur did not include this rule in their halachic rulings.
