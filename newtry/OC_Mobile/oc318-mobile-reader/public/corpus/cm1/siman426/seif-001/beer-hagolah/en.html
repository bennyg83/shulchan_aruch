a) The language of the Rambam, chapter 1 of Hilkhot Rotzeach, law 14, from the baraita in Sanhedrin, chapter Ben Sorer uMoreh, 73a.
bet) The Gemara's interpretation of the baraita there. The Rosh writes that the person who was saved must reimburse his rescuer for the money the rescuer spent, because a person is not obligated to save another's life with his own money where the endangered person has funds from which to reimburse him. The Tur likewise writes this. The Sema expresses surprise that the Mechaber and Rama omitted it. Hagahot Maimoniyot writes that the Yerushalmi concludes that one is obligated even to place himself in possible danger in order to save another. The Beit Yosef and Kesef Mishneh write that the reason appears to be that the other's danger is certain while his own is only possible. The Sema writes that the Mechaber omitted this law because the Rif, Rambam, Rosh, and Tur did not record it. Whoever preserves one Jewish life is regarded as though he preserved an entire world.
