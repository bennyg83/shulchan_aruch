With one stroke of the hoe he acquires all of it. Ketzot HaChoshen questions whether one who takes possession of the property of a convert who died without heirs, and the estate includes movable property, acquires the movables by agav together with the land. Perhaps acquisition by agav works only where another person's intent conveys the property, and one cannot acquire ownerless property by agav. The practical difference is where the land cannot acquire as a courtyard, for example because it is unguarded and he did not say, "Let my field acquire for me." Ketzot discusses the issue at length and concludes that ownerless property can indeed be acquired by agav. Netivot HaMishpat here and above, siman 202:2, disagrees. He proves from Ran in Kiddushin that acquisition by agav is an unusual mode of transfer and therefore must be limited to the form stated in the source, namely where another person conveys the property. Noda BiYehudah, second edition, Orach Chaim 63, likewise rules simply that agav is found only in a gift or similar transfer where another person's intent conveys, not in ownerless property. Responsa Be'er Yitzchak, Choshen Mishpat 4, discusses the question at length and rules like Ketzot HaChoshen.
