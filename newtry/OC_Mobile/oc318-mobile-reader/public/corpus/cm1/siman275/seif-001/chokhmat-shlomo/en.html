Seif 11. One who takes possession of the documents of a convert who died without heirs. Note: See Shach's explanation of Baal HaTerumot. In my opinion Baal HaTerumot can be understood differently. According to Rashi, who interprets the case as a debt instrument, why does the Gemara ask whether the finder intended to acquire the documents? It implies that if he expressly intended the document, he would certainly acquire it. Yet we rule that the physical document belongs to the borrower who pays the scribe, and the lender holds it only for purposes of the lien. Once the convert lender dies, the lien terminates. Even if another person seizes the document, he should have to return it, just as one who seizes a Jewish borrower's pledge from the possession of a convert must surrender it. Rashi must therefore be speaking where the convert himself paid the scribe, making the physical document his property; then intent to acquire the document could indeed transfer it. Baal HaTerumot found that scenario strained because ordinarily the borrower pays the scribe. He therefore explains that if the passage concerned a debt instrument, the lien would unquestionably have terminated, and even express intent to acquire the paper would not help. Consequently he interprets the case as a deed of sale, where the paper itself can be acquired if one intends it. This, in my opinion, is the correct explanation. Regarding one who hoes in the property of a deceased convert thinking it is his own and therefore does not acquire, see Ketzot HaChoshen's discussion whether a transferor's intent can supply the missing acquisition. In my Even HaEzer, siman 166, I proved from Rashi that even another person's transferring intent does not help.
