Shach seif katan 3, "what is the question?" Note: This is Tosafot's question in Bava Batra 24a. See there. It must be that the Shach holds that the Rambam ruled like Rashi in the chapter Mashilin, 39b, unlike Tosafot there, and in accordance with the plain sense of the sugya at the end of Bava Metzia, that looking at ownerless property can acquire it even against one's will. See Shitah Mekubetzet there, who struggles with this point. Indeed, this is also implied by the Rambam, Hilchot Yom Tov 5:14. See there. See also Nimukei Yosef in the first chapter of Bava Metzia, who writes concerning the statement, "since he fell upon it he revealed that he prefers acquisition by falling," that if a person says, "I do not want the enactment of the Sages," we heed him. This implies that if the acquisition were d'oraisa, we would not heed him. Shitah Mekubetzet there writes similarly. Nevertheless, there is no proof that he agrees with Rashi in every respect, for perhaps this case is different because he does want to acquire. If so, he cannot say that he will not acquire through a d'oraisa mode of acquisition. But if he does not want to acquire at all, he may not acquire against his will. See the Tur in this siman, in the dispute between Rabbi Yeshayah and the Ramah. It appears that the person certainly wants to acquire the stones, but intends to acquire them through building. Rabbi Yeshayah nevertheless holds that he acquires them through his hand, which is a d'oraisa acquisition, while the Ramah disagrees on that point.
