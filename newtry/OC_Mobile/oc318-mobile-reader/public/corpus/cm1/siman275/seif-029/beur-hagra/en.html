A convert, etc. Rambam explains the Gemara's statement, 'The practical difference is where they heard that he died,' as referring to the convert: initially, before the property was dispersed, they heard that the convert had died. 'And he had not died' refers back to what they heard, meaning that afterward they heard he had not died and their hold on the inheritance thereby became uncertain, unlike Rashbam's explanation. 'And afterward he died' likewise refers to a later report that the first report had been correct and the convert had already died. According to Rava, one who first took possession did not acquire because his ownership had become uncertain; according to Abaye he did acquire. This does not involve a son or fetus at all. Similarly, when Rava says there, 'That case is different,' etc., he means the time when they heard that he had a son, etc. These are the three clauses: 'they heard that he had not yet died' is the practical difference, while 'or,' etc., is what was mentioned in the Baraisa. The same distinction could have been stated regarding the death of the son and fetus, but the Gemara states the greater novelty that even where there was no fetus at all, according to Abaye the first possessor's failure to acquire is only because of the fetus. See Maggid Mishneh; his words are very confused. See Lechem Mishneh.
(Likkut) A convert, etc., and afterward, etc. This is Rambam's wording. See Maggid Mishneh, who discusses it at length unnecessarily, and whose words I already challenged above. Rambam's words are difficult for another reason: he does not record the practical difference between Abaye and Rava stated in the Gemara. Rambam's text, however, evidently read: 'The practical difference is where they heard that he died, he had not died, and he died.' This means that first they heard that the convert died, afterward they heard that he had not died, and then they again heard that the first report had been true and that the convert had in fact died earlier. Thus Rambam writes, 'and they heard that he had not yet died,' etc., and afterward, 'they heard that the first report was true.' None of this appears in our text of the Gemara, which does not discuss the convert's death in that sequence. According to our version it should have said, 'they heard that she miscarried,' like the Baraisa. According to Rambam's explanation, however, the clauses refer to the convert. The same would apply if she miscarried or the son died, but he chose the simpler case. See Tosafos, daf 142b, s.v. 'and the halacha,' where Ri Bam explains this way; the Rosh likewise rules so there and in chapter Almanah L'Kohen Gadol, and writes there that Rambam also rules this way. The Rosh there struggles greatly to explain Rava as disagreeing with Abaye and holding that an inheritance that comes automatically is not acquired, based on the Gemara's question, 'What is the practical difference?' Hagahos Ashri in Bava Basra there writes that according to Ri Bam and Rambam the halacha follows Abaye. According to what I have written, everything is straightforward: Rava does not disagree with Abaye on this point. That is precisely why the Gemara asks, 'What is the practical difference?' since Rava agrees with Abaye. This also appears at first glance from the sugya in Niddah 43a, which says, 'specifically a son,' etc., because he dies first. If a fetus could not acquire in any event, this reason would be unnecessary. One can reject this, however, because that entire statement is Rav Sheshes's, and he follows his own view in Bava Basra that both the first and second possessors acquire. This also resolves Tosafos's difficulty there and in Bava Basra, s.v. 'inherits,' where they ask how, etc. According to this, Rav Sheshes certainly knew the law that 'he inherits the property if,' etc.; what he did not know was the point that the fetus dies first. Rav Sheshes answers the Niddah passage concerning 'he inherits,' etc., and the Bava Basra passage concerning, etc., and the Gemara placed the statements together. In any event Rambam's textual version is correct, as explained. End.
