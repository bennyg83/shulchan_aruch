And he does not intend to acquire. If a person appoints an agent to fence or build upon the property of a deceased convert in order to acquire it for the principal, the principal acquires even if the agent does not know that the property is ownerless and thinks it already belongs to the principal. An agent is like the principal, and the principal himself has the requisite intention to acquire. Shitah Mekubetzet in Bava Metzia supports this. Rabbi Yochanan says that one who lifts a found object for another acquires it for him. The Mishnah speaks of a rider saying, "Give it to me," rather than, "Acquire it for me." Rosh explains that the rider does not want the walker to know it is a found object, lest the walker acquire it for himself. He therefore merely says, "Give me this object," and the walker does not intend to acquire it for the rider. That is why, after lifting it, the walker can sometimes say, "Although I lifted it for you, I now wish to acquire it for myself." The implication is that had the rider appointed him to lift the object for him, the principal would acquire even though the agent himself did not know it was a found object. Tosafot gives a parallel rule regarding agency for transgression. Where a custodian unknowingly pulls stolen property, the principal can become liable because the custodian did not know it was stolen. A thief or robber becomes liable for unavoidable accident only through an acquisition act, just as in sale or gift. Since the agent did not intend a theft acquisition, the principal's liability can be explained only because the principal's intention combines with the agent's act through agency. Accordingly, in ordinary monetary acquisition the principal's intention can suffice where the agent performs the required act without knowing its acquisitive character. Delivery of a get is different. A delivery agent must know that he is delivering a get and must communicate its divorcing effect to the woman. If he does not know it is a get, the woman is not divorced merely because the husband intended divorce.
