A field that a gentile sold to a Jew, etc. At the beginning of siman 194 and the end of siman 274 the Tur and Mechaber write that so long as the deed of sale has not reached the Jewish buyer's hand, his mind is not fully settled. The field is therefore treated like ownerless property, and if another person first takes possession through one of the methods explained there, that second person acquires it. The Rambam writes that if the first buyer, who paid the money, first takes possession by plowing or another form of acquisition, he acquires. The Tur holds that even if the first buyer takes possession before the second claimant, the second may still acquire because the first buyer's mind is not settled without the deed, unless the first took possession before paying. Rama states the dissent in siman 194:2. Here the Mechaber again follows the Rambam and says, "and he had not yet taken possession," implying that if the first buyer had taken possession he would acquire. Rama says nothing here and relies on what he wrote in siman 194. According to the dissenters, therefore, the phrase means that the first buyer had not taken possession before giving the money.

And he took possession by eating the produce. In siman 192 the Tur and Mechaber explain that according to the Rambam, eating produce can acquire land in a sale. The Raavad and Tur disagree and hold that in a sale one does not acquire merely by eating produce or standing animals there, but only through an act that benefits or improves the land. Rama rules like them there. It therefore requires study why Rama does not disclose his view here. The answer is that he relied upon siman 192, which is the principal place for laws of acquisition in a sale. Here the subject is acquisition from ownerless property, and everyone agrees that ownerless land is not acquired merely by eating its produce. The same question arises in the Tur itself, where the Tur appears to use language suggesting acquisition through eating produce. In the Perishah I was forced to explain that the Tur speaks of one who claims he bought the field with deed and possession, and mentions the eating of produce merely as evidence supporting his purchase claim. Even that is strained. Regarding Rama, the explanation that he relied on siman 192 is sufficient.
