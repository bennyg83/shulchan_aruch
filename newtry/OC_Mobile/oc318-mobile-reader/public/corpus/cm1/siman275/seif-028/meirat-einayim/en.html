But if it was in his courtyard. Meaning, the courtyard is guarded, making it like his hand, as explained in siman 268. The Tur likewise says so here.
However, if it is something he could not have been expected to know about, etc. Mordechai, chapter 2 of Bava Metzia, writes of a convert who died in another person's house and someone else took possession of gold hidden in the convert's belt. Riva ruled that the owner of the house did not acquire it through his courtyard, because he had no greater reason than anyone else to know it was there. Rama cites this in Darkei Moshe. See the beginning of siman 268 in the Perishah and Derishah concerning when a person's courtyard acquires for him.
Or through witnesses, etc. This means that he died within the term, as explained above in siman 108, and is stated explicitly there.
Like one who comes to collect from heirs. Meaning, he may collect only with an oath. Darkei Moshe cites Baal HaItur in this wording. The Mechaber likewise writes it at the end of this siman, and the Tur states it at the end as well. Perhaps Rama writes it here to teach that the person has the status of an heir for other matters too.
