He sold an item worth seventy for sixty and one perutah, etc. The Mechaber carefully chose this example because in that form the discrepancy is not exactly one sixth from either side. Rambam and Tur, however, write, for example, one sold something worth sixty for fifty and a perutah, or for seventy less a perutah. The Maggid Mishneh questions why the first case should be treated as waiver. If an item worth seventy is sold for sixty, that is legally one sixth of the money received. Likewise, if an item worth sixty is sold for fifty and a perutah, although the wrong is not exactly one sixth of the item's value, it is more than one sixth when measured against the money received. He calculates the fractions in detail and concludes that so long as the seller received no more than fifty one dinars and six fourteenths for an item worth sixty, the discrepancy remains within the zone of legally significant overreaching. How, then, can the Rambam say that selling for fifty and a perutah is waived? The Maggid Mishneh suggests that the Rambam may hold that where the total value is sixty, the clean integral sixth is ten. We measure only by a division that yields a whole number, rather than dividing sixty into seven fractional portions. Thus ten is treated as the operative sixth, and because the difference between sixty and fifty and a perutah is slightly less than ten, it is waived. The Maggid Mishneh admits that this is strained and concludes that the Rambam's text should be corrected to read, 'an item worth seventy for sixty and a perutah.' For this reason the Mechaber here did not copy the Rambam and Tur literally but followed the Maggid Mishneh's correction. The Ir Shushan copied the Tur's wording and did not attend to this distinction.
