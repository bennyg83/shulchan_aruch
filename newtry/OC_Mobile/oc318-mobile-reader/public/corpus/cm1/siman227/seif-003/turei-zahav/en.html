(Seif 7.) "And if he can establish." Our master comments on the Rosh's language, "there is room to infer," etc. The Beit Yosef asks what inference is involved. The Sema explains in seif katan 17 that the period may have passed, etc., but I do not understand his explanation. How is this different from a case where there were no merchants in the city, where the period for inspection begins only when merchants later arrive? Moreover, why did our master not state the rule clearly? It appears to me that the novelty is this: where the buyer was prevented by circumstances for the entire ordinary inspection period and only afterward that obstacle passed, and he claims, "It is true that afterward I investigated and learned that I had been deceived, but I did not immediately return the item because I assumed you would not believe that I had previously been prevented, since I could not then prove the obstacle. I therefore waited until now when I can establish it," our master teaches that once he establishes that earlier obstacle, he can still rescind. If he could have established the matter earlier and did not, he certainly waived the claim. Our master derives this from the Rosh's phrase, "if he was negligent and did not investigate," which is otherwise unnecessary. The Rosh could simply have written that if he delayed without an unavoidable obstacle and did not return it, he waived. Rather, he teaches incidentally that waiver is inferred only while he fails to investigate. If he says that he did investigate and has some separate reason for delay, such as inability to prove the prior obstacle, then once he can establish it he may still rescind. This appears to me correct.
