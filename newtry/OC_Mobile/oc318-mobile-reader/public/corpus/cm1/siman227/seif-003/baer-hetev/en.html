Rambam and Tur, however, give as examples where one sold an item worth sixty for fifty and a perutah, or for seventy less a perutah. Maggid Mishneh questions this: Why should the discrepancy be waived? If one sells an item worth seventy for sixty, the onaah is one-sixth of the money paid, even though it is not one-sixth of the item's value. Likewise, where an item worth sixty is sold for fifty and a perutah, although the discrepancy is not one-sixth of the item's value, it is more than one-sixth relative to the money received. Maggid Mishneh concludes, 'Perhaps the author's view is...' See there. Taz writes that whenever the discrepancy can be described as one-sixth of the money, we follow that measure. But where the description of one-sixth does not apply at all, we measure against the value of the merchandise. The general rule is that whenever the discrepancy qualifies as one-sixth from any perspective, that classification governs. We do not reason a fortiori from a smaller amount to a larger one, because the Sages fixed their measures uniformly and made the rule depend upon one-sixth. Therefore, where an item worth sixty is sold for fifty and a perutah, since the onaah itself cannot be called one-sixth at all, we measure only against the merchandise, and the discrepancy is waived. This is also the implication of Mordechai cited by Rama in seif 4. Taz. See Responsa Maharashdam, siman 260.
