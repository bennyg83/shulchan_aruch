See Shach, seif katan 4. The Shach writes in siman 227 that where the purchaser has not yet paid, the ordinary time limit of 'enough time to show it to a merchant' may not bar his claim because he has a migo. It appears that this does not apply merely because the purchaser later seized property of the seller after the time had elapsed. The Shach's reasoning works where the purchaser's own money remained in his possession from the moment of sale, so that he could rely on his ability to deny waiver while still holding the funds. If he was not in possession during the relevant period, a later migo may be a migo against a strong presumption that he waived the claim. Even weaker presumptions, such as the rule that a person ordinarily does not repay before maturity, can defeat a migo; all the more so the established presumption of waiver after the statutory time. The Shach's doubt therefore concerns the special case where the purchaser had the money continuously and could reasonably rely on that leverage. If the seller later obtains the money after the statutory time, perhaps the purchaser can no longer rely on the earlier migo because he does not have it now. The Shach's point may be only that his earlier possession strengthens the claim because he reasonably relied on the fact that the money was still his. This requires study.
