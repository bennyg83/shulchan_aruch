Ibid., Taz, seif 7, "and he asked." Note: in my opinion the difficulty may be resolved as follows. One might initially have thought that the period "until he shows it" is a fixed measure, so that even if an unavoidable circumstance occurred, the Sages did not distinguish, and once that period elapsed his time to complain expired. Although the Rosh makes clear that this is not so, one might still say that this applies only to requiring the seller to return the amount of the overcharge, because the Sages did not wish the injured buyer to lose his money merely because of a uniform rule. But where the seller returns the amount of the overcharge and the buyer suffers no monetary loss, one might have thought that he cannot rescind the sale even when the overcharge exceeded one sixth, for regarding cancellation of the sale the Sages did apply a uniform rule. The Tur therefore teaches that he may still retract. He inferred this precisely from the Rosh's wording, which says that he "forgoes his overcharge," rather than that he "forgoes the money of his overcharge." This indicates that the Rosh's inference applies to the entire transaction.
