Meaning, although this resembles an exchange because he did not carefully determine how much money was in his handful, and the verse says, "When you sell a sale," rather than exchange, nevertheless, since he used the language of sale it has the law of a sale and the rules of ona'ah apply. This is the Sema's explanation. See Responsa Lechem Rav, siman 159; Responsa Rashach, part 3, siman 62; Responsa Rashdam, siman 379; and Mekor Baruch, siman 55.
Produce. Meaning, he took a handful of produce and said, "Sell me your produce for one or two sela'im." Therefore the Mechaber was precise in his wording. In the first clause he speaks of one who buys with money given by estimate, meaning he acquires a particular item in exchange for a handful of money. In the latter clause the reverse is true: he buys a handful of produce for a stated sum. In both cases the acquisition is valid and the ona'ah must be returned. The latter case is comparable to what the Tur and Mechaber write at the beginning of siman 209: "I sell you this heap of wheat for such and such," where the sale is valid and the laws of ona'ah apply according to the market price. What difference is there whether he took a handful of produce or sold him a heap? It is therefore surprising that the Rama writes here that some say the matter is uncertain, whereas in siman 209 he states without dispute that ona'ah applies. The Tur records a dispute here only where one buys with a handful of money, for the Rosh is uncertain whether the ona'ah must be returned in such a case. It appears that the Rosh said this specifically where the money itself is unmeasured, because this is not an ordinary mode of purchase but resembles exchange. By contrast, people commonly buy a heap of grain or produce without measurement or counting. I therefore conclude that the Rama's gloss applies only to the case of a handful of money. This is the Sema's ruling. The Taz disagrees and writes: What relevance does it have to the law of ona'ah whether the form of purchase is common? Such distinctions apply specifically to the rabbinic enactment of meshichah. It is therefore clear to me that the dissenting opinion disputes both cases. In siman 209 the purpose is only to teach that an acquisition can be effected in this manner, and the statement that ona'ah applies was copied incidentally, relying for the actual law of ona'ah on what is written here. As a matter of practical halachah, one should rule like the Rif and Rambam that ona'ah applies, as the Beit Yosef writes that we follow them. We do not abandon their certainty because of the Rosh's doubt.
