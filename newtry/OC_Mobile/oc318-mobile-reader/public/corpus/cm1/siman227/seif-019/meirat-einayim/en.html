Sell me your cow for these. Rambam in chapter 13 concludes: although he acquired by way of exchange, he acquires and must return any overreaching. Even though the transaction resembles barter, because the buyer did not carefully count how much money he held in his hand, and Scripture says 'when you sell a sale' rather than barter, nevertheless since he used the language of sale the transaction has the law of sale and is subject to overreaching.
Produce in an unmeasured heap for a sela, etc. Meaning, he takes a handful or heap of produce and says, Sell me this produce for one or two sela. The Mechaber carefully distinguishes the opening case, where he acquires an item by money that is itself uncounted in his hand, from the later case, where the produce is the unmeasured object being purchased for a stated amount of money. In both cases the sale is valid and overreaching is recoverable. The second case resembles the Tur and Mechaber's ruling at the beginning of siman 209: 'I sell you this heap of wheat for such and such,' where even though the amount of the heap is unknown, the sale stands and overreaching is measured by the market price. What difference is there between selling a handful of produce and selling a heap without measurement? It is therefore surprising that Rama immediately cites a dissenting view saying the matter is doubtful, apparently even regarding unmeasured produce. In siman 209 the Tur and Mechaber rule without dispute that overreaching applies. The Tur records the dispute only where the money itself is uncounted and is exchanged for a cow or produce. The Rosh doubts there whether overreaching can be recovered because that is not the ordinary form of a sale but resembles barter. Produce sold in an unmeasured heap, however, is a common form of sale. Therefore Rama's gloss must be limited to the opening case of uncounted money. See the Perishah.
