Exactly one sixth. Meaning, this excludes a disparity either less or greater, as explained in seifim 2 and 3. The Tur writes in the name of the Rosh: Chazal assessed that up to one sixth there is waiver, because in ordinary buying and selling neither purchaser nor seller can determine the exact price with absolute precision. People ordinarily waive less than one sixth, whether measured against the item or the money, even if the absolute sum is large, so long as the legal proportion of one sixth is not reached.
Six for five or seven for six. In both cases the seller was wronged by one sixth under one of the two methods of calculation. If property worth six is sold for five, the error is one sixth of the item's value and more than one sixth of the money received. True, if the five dinarim received are divided into six parts, each part is one dinar minus one sixth of a dinar, while the seller lost a full dinar by giving property worth six for five. Nevertheless, the sale is not void because from one perspective, namely the value of the merchandise, the discrepancy is exactly one sixth. Likewise, if he sold property worth seven for six, the seller was wronged by one sixth of the money received and less than one sixth of the merchandise's value. If seven is divided into six portions, each portion is one dinar and one sixth, and he sold it for six dinarim, so measured against the item the loss is one sixth minus one sixth of a dinar. Nevertheless, the law is ona'ah rather than waiver because measured against the money received the discrepancy is exactly one sixth. The same applies in reverse where the purchaser was wronged. If he bought property worth five for six, although his loss is more than one sixth of the merchandise value, or bought property worth six for seven, where his loss is less than one sixth of the money he paid, the law remains ona'ah rather than waiver or voiding the sale whenever one of the two measures yields exactly one sixth. Shmuel states this explicitly in the Gemara. I quoted his wording in the Perishah.
And he must return the entire amount to the injured party. Meaning, we do not say that because less than one sixth is ordinarily waived, he need return only the tiny amount that raises the discrepancy from below one sixth to the one-sixth threshold. The entire ona'ah must be returned. This is comparable to one who says, "I sell you a beit kor of land, more or less," where up to one quarter kav per se'ah is waived, but if the discrepancy exceeds one quarter kav per se'ah, the entire discrepancy is deducted, as the Tur and Mechaber write in siman 218, seif 8. A similar principle appears in siman 229, seif 1.
