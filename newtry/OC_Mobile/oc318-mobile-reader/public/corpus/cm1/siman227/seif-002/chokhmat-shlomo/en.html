Seif 9. In the gloss: The party accused of onaah says that the market price changed, etc. Note: See my novellae to Even HaEzer, siman 31, where I challenged this from the position of Ramah that we evaluate according to the present state, and I discussed a resolution at length. I now see a proof for Ramah from Bava Kamma 105a. Rav Pappa says there that whether the stolen object still exists or not, the thief need not pursue the owner to another place, because we do not concern ourselves that the object may rise in value. This is codified in Choshen Mishpat. Thus we do not suspect a possible change in market price. This is a fortiori. Ordinarily future uncertainty is treated more seriously than past uncertainty, for we do not suspect that a person already died where a presumption of life exists, yet we may be concerned that he will die in the future because presumptions do not govern the future. If even regarding the future we do not worry that the price may rise, certainly regarding the past we should not suspect that the market price had changed. This supports Ramah, in accordance with what I wrote in Even HaEzer. Still, the present case can be distinguished. There is an indication that the price probably changed, because people ordinarily do not knowingly subject themselves to onaah. A person normally checks whether the item is worth the price before completing the transaction. Therefore there is reason to assume that he did not initially accept a loss and that the current claim arose because the market subsequently changed. Without such an indication, however, we do not suspect a change in price. On this basis the Rama's ruling here can accord with all opinions. The practical ruling still requires study. I have now seen that this is in fact an explicit dispute between Rashi and Tosafot in Arachin 18, s.v. "that he gives according to the time of payment." Therefore the Mordechai's ruling here is not certain, since Ramah and Tosafot disagree with it while Rashi agrees with the Mordechai. The result is a doubt in law. See my Even HaEzer novellae, where I brought a proof for Rashi.
