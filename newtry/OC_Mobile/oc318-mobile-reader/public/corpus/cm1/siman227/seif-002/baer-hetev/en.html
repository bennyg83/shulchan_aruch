Onaah. Although selling an item worth six for five involves one sixth of the purchase price and more than one sixth relative to the money paid, it nevertheless has the law of ordinary onaah rather than cancellation, because relative to the value of the item the discrepancy is exactly one sixth. Likewise, where one sells an item worth seven for six, the seller is disadvantaged by one sixth relative to the money received but by less than one sixth relative to the item's value, and nevertheless the law is onaah rather than waiver because relative to the money he received there is exactly one sixth. The same applies in reverse when the buyer is disadvantaged. If he buys an item worth five for six, although the excess is more than one sixth of the item's value, or an item worth six for seven, although the excess is less than one sixth of the money paid, the law is still onaah, not waiver or cancellation, whenever the discrepancy equals exactly one sixth under either calculation. This is explicit in the Gemara in Shmuel's statement. So writes the Sema. See Responsa Ri le-Beit Levi, siman 6, folios 27 and 28.
The entire amount. Meaning, we do not say that since less than one sixth is waived, he need return only the perutah that brings the discrepancy up to one sixth and above. Rather, once the threshold is exceeded, the entire discrepancy is adjusted. This is like the rule concerning a beit-kor of earth sold 'whether less or more,' where up to one quarter-kav per seah is waived, but if the excess is greater, the whole excess is deducted, as stated in siman 218, seif 8. A similar rule appears at the beginning of siman 229. Sema.
