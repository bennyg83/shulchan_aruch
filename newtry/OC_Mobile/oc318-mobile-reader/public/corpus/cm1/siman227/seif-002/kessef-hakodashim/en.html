Within the time needed to show it to a merchant. Where a buyer may return a purchase because of ona'ah only within the time needed to show it to a merchant, if the seller immediately leaves the area this does not by itself allow the buyer to return the item after that time, even if he did not use it. The buyer should notify beit din, or at least witnesses, within the prescribed time. This appears reasonable, and is also implied by the rule that a seller may retract indefinitely, whereas the Mishnah did not state a special extension for a buyer who is distant from the seller, which would have been an even greater novelty. However, if the buyer is in a village where there is no valid witness before whom he can establish the ona'ah, even though the local measure is still the time needed to show it to relatives or others who can appraise it, what could he have done? Telling invalid relatives would accomplish nothing. Perhaps he may claim ona'ah until he has had a reasonable opportunity to reach valid witnesses without extraordinary expense. This requires careful study.
