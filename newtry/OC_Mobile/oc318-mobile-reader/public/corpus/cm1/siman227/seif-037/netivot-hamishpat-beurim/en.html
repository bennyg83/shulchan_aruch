They divided the movable property. The case appears to involve produce or other items that were appraised, because otherwise exchange itself has no ona'ah, as explained in seif 20. Ran at the beginning of HaIsh Mekadesh likewise explains that the movable property was divided with an equalization payment. Maharit, responsum siman 19, discusses one who has no ownership share in the body of merchandise but is entitled only to a portion of the profit, with no share in loss, when the merchandise is sold. If he sells his profit interest, the transaction is not subject to ona'ah. Ketzot HaChoshen cites Tashbetz concerning one who showed gold to a goldsmith before buying it and promised him half the profit as compensation for his appraisal. The goldsmith said it was gold, but it was later found to be copper. Tashbetz holds the broker liable by comparison to butchers who receive the intestines of kosher animals as their compensation and are therefore paid custodians. Ketzot rejects the analogy. In the butcher case, had the butcher performed properly and not ruined the animal, he would certainly have earned his compensation. Here, had the appraiser correctly identified the metal as copper, there would have been no purchase and therefore no profit and no compensation at all. His promised wage depended entirely on there actually being a profitable transaction. Ketzot's distinction is persuasive.
