The Maggid Mishneh writes that Rambam appears to interpret the Yerushalmi's statement of Rabbi Yochanan, 'They taught this only regarding an ox for a cow,' etc., as applying where there is ona'ah. But where there is no ona'ah, even produce exchanged for produce effects acquisition, because exchanges of this kind apply even to produce, as Rambam writes in chapter 5 and above in siman 203. Although the Yerushalmi's plain meaning may not concern ona'ah, according to Rambam's view that exchanges of this kind apply even to produce, one can say that regarding ona'ah there is a distinction between utensils and produce paralleling the Yerushalmi's distinction regarding acquisition. The reason is that utensils last longer than produce. His words imply that even where the parties are particular about exact value, there is no ona'ah with utensils.
