But to my opinion. As you see that Jehovah is more than a move, he drinks a serving as we sell you:
By an amount in which people ordinarily make mistakes. This is the correct reading, and it is Rambam's language in chapter 15 and the Tur's in seif 26. The meaning is that if the item is worth six gold coins and the seller sells it for seven, he should not merely say, 'I know it is worth only one gold coin, and on condition that you waive any overreaching.' Rather, he must say, 'I know it is worth only six dinars.' Then the buyer knows the precise amount of the overcharge and knowingly waives it. Even if the seller says, 'I know it is worth only five,' while in truth it is worth six and he sells for seven, there is still no claim of overreaching. He expressly conditioned that there be no claim, and the purchaser cannot say, 'When I saw it was worth more than five I assumed it must therefore be worth seven.' He should have considered that the seller may have been mistaken by one unit, which is a common error, and thought it worth only five. Thus it is treated as though the amount of the overcharge had effectively been disclosed.
