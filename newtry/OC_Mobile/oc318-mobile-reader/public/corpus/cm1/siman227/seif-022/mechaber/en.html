If one sells an object worth one hundred for one hundred and says, "This object is worth only one zuz, on condition that you have no claim of overcharge against me," the purchaser may still claim overcharge. He may say, "When I heard you say it was worth only one zuz, I understood that you meant merely to increase my appreciation of the bargain." The waiver is effective only if the seller specifies the actual amount of overcharge with certainty, or an amount sufficiently close that ordinary judgment could recognize it, so that the purchaser certainly knew what he was waiving.
