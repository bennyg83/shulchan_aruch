The documents. See Ketzot HaChoshen above, siman 126, seif katan 9, who advances a novel distinction. The exemption from ona'ah for documents applies specifically where one sells a written promissory note through writing and delivery. Because the document itself is not intrinsically monetary property, it was excluded from ona'ah by the scriptural general and specific formulation, which includes only an item whose substance itself is money. But an oral debt transferred through maamad sheloshtan is subject to ona'ah, because the debt itself is monetary property. The fact that it cannot be consecrated is for another reason, namely that it is property not presently in one's possession, but its substance is nevertheless money. See there. Divrei Chayim, Hilchot Halvaah, siman 36, questions this. In his view, the Gemara in Bava Metzia 56 needed the reason that documents are not intrinsically monetary only because a document is a movable object that can pass from hand to hand. An oral debt, by contrast, should be excluded from ona'ah even without that rationale by the phrase "from the hand of your fellow," which indicates something transferable from hand to hand. An oral debt is no better than land, which is excluded because it is not movable. See there. See also Chavat Daat to Yoreh Deah, siman 173, seif katan 8, concerning the ruling that where an object has no known market price and no fixed appraisal, it may be sold at a higher price for payment due at a later date. Chavat Daat writes that ona'ah should not apply there even if, after separating the item's value from the charge for waiting, the difference reaches the ordinary ona'ah measure. The reason is that one must appraise the debt itself according to what it is worth, and the sale of a promissory note is not subject to ona'ah. His unqualified wording implies that he likewise assumes an oral debt is not subject to ona'ah, because he makes no distinction.
