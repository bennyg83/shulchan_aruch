It is subject to the law of ona'ah. The Tur writes in the name of the Rosh that Shimon's agreement to purchase according to Levi's appraisal is no stronger than one who expressly says, "on condition that you have no claim of ona'ah against me," which we rule does not remove the law of ona'ah. All the more so here, because Reuven did not intend to sell it for more than its value, nor did Shimon intend to purchase it for more than its value.
If Levi is a merchant, etc. If he is not a professional merchant, he would be liable even if he received no payment, because he caused damage. But if he is a merchant and expert, he is exempt when acting without pay and liable when paid, like an expert shochet who spoiled the shechitah, as will be explained below in siman 306. See the Tur, who discusses the responsum at greater length from seif 30 onward, and who also cites another lengthy responsum of the Rosh afterward in seif 35. Since there is not much additional practical law in those discussions, the Mechaber omitted them.
