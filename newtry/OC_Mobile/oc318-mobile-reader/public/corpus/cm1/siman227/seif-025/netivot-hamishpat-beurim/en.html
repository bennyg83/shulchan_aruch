According to Levi's appraisal. From Mechaber's statement that the ordinary law of ona'ah applies, it appears that specifically one sixth is required. This is difficult from siman 206, where one says, "I sell you this land according to the appraisal of three," and three assess it, but one party asks that three greater experts assess it. We do not listen to him. That law derives from Avodah Zarah 72. Shach there cites Shitah Mekubetzet that the reason is perhaps the later assessors are more expert, and says that if it is known they are more expert it requires study whether we listen to them. This implies that if it is established that the original assessors erred, everyone agrees the transaction can be void even in land, which ordinarily has no ona'ah. As I explained above in siman 103, where a person says simply, "according to the appraisal of experts," his intent is an expert appraisal. If later experts establish that the first group erred, it turns out that the agreed mode of pricing was never properly fulfilled, and therefore the transaction can be void for even a small error. Here, however, he said specifically, "according to Levi's appraisal," naming a particular individual. Even if Levi's valuation later proves inaccurate, the parties chose that particular person rather than an abstract expert standard. There is therefore ordinary ona'ah only at the usual threshold. A second distinction is possible. Here Levi appraised the item before the kinyan, so once the parties entered the transaction they knowingly accepted his figure and it resembles an ordinary sale at an agreed price. But if they first made a kinyan committing themselves to sell according to Levi's later appraisal, and Levi then appraised incorrectly, even a minimal error can void the transaction because the kinyan was made under the mistaken assumption that Levi was a competent appraiser. The responsum of Rosh says that he pledged the item according to Levi's appraisal, implying the appraisal preceded the acquisition. The first explanation nevertheless appears primary.
