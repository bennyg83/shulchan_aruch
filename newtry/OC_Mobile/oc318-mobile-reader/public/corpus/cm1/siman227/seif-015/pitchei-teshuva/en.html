Precious stones, and so forth. See Baer Heitev and Responsa Knesset Yechezkel, siman 85, concerning an enactment established long ago in his community that ona'ah claims would not apply to transactions in precious stones and pearls. The enactment stated that because it was common for people to raise ona'ah claims in that trade, and most members of the community earned their livelihood from it, making commerce impossible, the community sought rabbinic guidance how to regulate matters without violating Torah law. The sages ruled that in commerce involving precious stones and pearls, an ona'ah claim would remain available only so long as the merchandise was still in the seller's possession. Once the purchaser took the merchandise and left the seller, even if he had not yet paid and had not signed a bill of exchange, he would be required to keep the goods at the agreed price and would have no ona'ah claim. A case then arose where the seller was overreached by more than half and sued the purchaser. Knesset Yechezkel writes that the enactment appears to have been instituted only regarding purchasers, for the commercial reason stated, but not regarding sellers, who remain governed by ordinary Torah law. Moreover, even regarding a purchaser, the enactment applies only where the ona'ah is less than half. If the purchaser paid twice the true value, the enactment does not apply. The enactment itself expressly stated that the community did not wish to act contrary to Torah law. How, then, could the sages have authorized a rule that contradicts Torah law? Rather, they must have ruled according to Rabbeinu Chananel, who follows Rabbi Yehudah. The sages have authority to select one recognized halachic view for public regulation, as the Rosh writes. Yet even according to Rabbi Yehudah, an overcharge of double the value remains actionable, as stated explicitly in the chapter HaZahav. He therefore concludes without hesitation that the seller remains governed by Torah law, and if a purchaser was overreached by double the value, the sale is void even at the great fairs. See there:
