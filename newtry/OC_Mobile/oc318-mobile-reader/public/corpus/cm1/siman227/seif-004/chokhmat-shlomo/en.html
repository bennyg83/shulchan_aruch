Seif 21. On condition that you shall have no claim of onaah against me, etc. Note: Rashi to Makkot 3b, s.v. "he has no claim of onaah against him," writes that a stipulation contrary to the Torah is valid, whereas if he says, "on condition that there is no onaah in it," this is not a stipulation that the other party forgive his rights. Rashi's wording requires study, because its two parts seem inconsistent. At first he implies that a stipulation contrary to Torah law is itself valid, not because of waiver. If so, why should it matter that the second wording does not express forgiveness? Although Rashi concludes that the transaction would be a mistaken sale, what role does waiver play in the explanation? If Rashi means that even in monetary matters a stipulation contrary to Torah law is normally void and it works only because the injured party forgives his right, then why did he initially write that a stipulation contrary to Torah law is valid? He should have written that in monetary matters such a stipulation is valid because of waiver. His unqualified wording suggests that any stipulation contrary to Torah law is valid, even outside monetary matters. Rashi's words therefore appear internally inconsistent and require study.
