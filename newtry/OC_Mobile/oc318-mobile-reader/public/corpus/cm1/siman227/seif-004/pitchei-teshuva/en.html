At the end of his discussion, Netivot objects to Ketzot and writes that the proposition that when both parties know of the ona'ah it is as though they expressly stipulated waiver is not persuasive. First, and so forth. Second, Bava Metzia proves that in the case where the seller says, "I know it is worth only one hundred," the language "on condition," and so forth, is specifically required. See Responsa Galia Masechta at the end of siman 4, who writes that Ketzot is correct. Whenever the knowledge is clear and obvious in such a way that waiver is certain, no express "on condition" is needed. Only in the case where the seller says, "I know it is worth only one hundred," is there room to say that the purchaser's waiver is not absolute, because the purchaser may think the seller is merely praising his merchandise and that it is actually worth more.
