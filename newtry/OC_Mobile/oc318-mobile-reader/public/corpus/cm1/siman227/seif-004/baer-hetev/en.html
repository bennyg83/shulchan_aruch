To retract. Since the aggrieved party may retract, the sale was never finally completed, and therefore the other party may also retract. Once the aggrieved party has accepted the transaction, however, the sale immediately becomes final and the party who caused the onaah can no longer retract. Although the Tur writes at the end of siman 226 and in siman 190 that where the seller repeatedly demands his money, once the sale is void as to the seller it is void as to the purchaser as well, and even if the seller later agrees the purchaser may retract, although his rights are then inferior, that case is different. There, the seller's repeated pursuit of the money immediately reveals that he would not have sold but for his need for the funds. The sale therefore became void as soon as he was not paid. Consequently, even if he later consents to uphold it, the purchaser may retract because the sale had already been nullified. Here, by contrast, the aggrieved party did not initially reveal an intention to retract. Therefore, when he later accepts the transaction, it is as though he accepted it from the outset, and the party who caused the onaah may no longer retract. Ibid.
