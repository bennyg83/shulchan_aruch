Likewise, if it became known that such an item came into his possession, etc. This implies that it must be clearly established that the seller later had another article of the same sort in his possession. If the parties dispute this, even if the purchaser definitely claims that the seller later encountered such an item, the seller prevails, although he is the one seeking to retain money. We presume that a seller ordinarily does not sell merely in order to retract, and that he did not know of the overreaching until now. I proved this in the Perishah from the Rif as cited by the Rosh in responsum, who writes explicitly that the purchaser must establish that a similar transaction later came to the seller's attention and that he did not retract then. Rama's ruling in the following seif that where they dispute a market price change the wronged party bears the burden is different, because Rama gives the reason there: market fluctuations are common. The Rosh's responsum cited by the Tur requires careful study because the Tur abbreviated it, as I noted in the Perishah.
