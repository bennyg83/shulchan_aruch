Became known. This implies that the rule applies only when it became clearly established that an item comparable to the one he sold came into his possession. If the parties dispute this, then even if the buyer claims with certainty that the seller had an opportunity to encounter such an item, the seller prevails, even though he seeks to extract money. We presume that a person ordinarily does not sell an item with the intention of later retracting, and that he certainly did not know of the overcharge until now. This is also evident from the Rif as cited by the Rosh in a responsum, who writes explicitly that the rule applies only once it is established that a comparable item came into the seller's hands and he did not retract at that time. As for the Rama's ruling in the next seif, that when they dispute whether the market price changed the injured party must bring proof, the reason is stated there: a change in market price is common. The Rosh's responsum cited by the Tur requires study, for the Tur abbreviated it. See there. Sema.
