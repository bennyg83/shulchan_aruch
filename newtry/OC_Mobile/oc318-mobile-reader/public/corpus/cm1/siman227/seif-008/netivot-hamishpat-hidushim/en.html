For the merchandise is in his possession. According to this, if the transaction was made by kinyan sudar and the merchandise is still in the seller's possession, the rule is reversed: if the seller was the one subjected to ona'ah, he cannot retract, while if the buyer was subjected to ona'ah, the buyer may retract until the end of the period required to show the merchandise, calculated from when it came into his possession. With land, and with chazakah of land, he cannot retract after the period required to show it has elapsed (Shach).
If it becomes known that it came into his possession. Specifically where the buyer proves that it came into his possession; otherwise the seller is believed. For the reason, see Beurim. See Beurim, where the question was asked: when he does not know whether there was ona'ah at all, he is believed by migo that he could have said there was no ona'ah; but where he has a migo that he could have said “I paid,” he is not believed.
