The time limit for claiming ona'ah applies to the purchaser, because the purchased item is in his possession and he can show it to knowledgeable people. The seller, however, may claim ona'ah indefinitely, and certainly may claim that the sale was void for excessive ona'ah, because he may not know the value of what he sold until he later sees a comparable item sold in the market. Therefore, if the item is of a type that does not vary in quality and is uniformly priced, such as pepper and similar commodities, the seller may retract only for the amount of time needed to inquire about the market price. Likewise, if it becomes known that while the seller still had an opportunity to claim he learned the market value and knew that he had erred but did not demand relief, he may no longer claim, because he has waived the discrepancy.
