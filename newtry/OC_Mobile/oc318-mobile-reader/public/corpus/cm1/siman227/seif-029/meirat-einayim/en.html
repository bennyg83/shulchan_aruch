Slaves and documents, etc. The Gemara derives this from the verse concerning overreaching, 'when you sell a sale to your fellow, or buy from the hand of your fellow.' The word 'hand' indicates movable property that passes hand to hand, excluding land. Slaves are excluded because they are compared to land. The word 'sale' teaches that the object itself must be monetary property and itself be sold, excluding promissory notes, whose physical paper is not the debt but merely evidence. The word 'your fellow' excludes consecrated property. Tosafot explain that land's exclusion is not because people are willing to pay more for land, for slaves and documents are excluded as well even though that rationale does not apply. It is a decree of Scripture. The Tur does not list consecrated property among items exempt from overreaching, although Rif and Rosh copy the Mishnah that does list it. When they later explain the exclusions from the verse, however, they omit consecrated property. I believe their reason is that today we have no Temple treasury consecration in the classical sense. Funds dedicated to the poor or to a synagogue have the law of ordinary private property, as the Rosh writes explicitly and the Tur cites at the end of siman 95 and siman 212, and likewise in Orach Chaim siman 153. Such property is included in 'your fellow' and is subject to overreaching. Consecration of the body itself needs no such reason because it is not an ordinary commercial transaction and misuse is subject to meilah.
But more than half. This is surprising. The Rosh and Tur write in Rabbeinu Tam's name that specifically up to half there is no overreaching in these excluded categories, while at exactly half the rule of overreaching already applies, and they prove this from the Yerushalmi. Tosafot in Bava Kamma and Bava Metzia do quote Rabbeinu Tam in language like Rama's, but it must not be literal. Their purpose was only to reject the view of Rif and Rambam that these items are wholly exempt from overreaching. Rama therefore should not have copied Rabbeinu Tam's wording literally instead of stating it as the Rosh and Tur explain it.
Like he sold one more m. According to one of the most familiar with two dinners, ie no more than a sailor and knowledge of the Almighty, D. D. D. D. D.D., said, “I am not jealous of things and theft of opinion, and no one shall be given to his brothers as well as if he does not know his own:
It is overreaching. The Tur says that the sale is void, even at half. Rama's word 'overreaching' likewise means cancellation of the transaction. He means that the prohibition of overreaching written in the Torah still applies to these categories at this larger threshold. The Sages fixed the ordinary cancellation threshold as more than one sixth. For these items that are otherwise exempt from ordinary overreaching, the corresponding threshold is one half. The Tosafot and poskim similarly use the word overreaching in this context to mean cancellation of the sale.
