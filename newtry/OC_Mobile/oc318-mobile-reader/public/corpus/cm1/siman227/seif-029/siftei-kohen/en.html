But more than one half. See the Sema, seif katan 50, where he concludes that the authorities must have spoken imprecisely. In my opinion they spoke precisely, as is explicit from Tosafot in Bava Kamma there and from the Agudah in the chapter HaZahav. The Yerushalmi provides no proof to the contrary. On the contrary, the Yerushalmi itself may be speaking imprecisely. See there. Tosafot in Kiddushin, chapter HaIsh Mekadesh, 42b, states explicitly: the Yerushalmi likewise says that up to one half of their share is overreaching. If 'one half' is precise, then the case of one who bought an item worth one hundred for two hundred in the chapter HaMekabel cannot mean exactly two hundred, but slightly less. If the figure two hundred is precise, then the phrase 'up to one half' is imprecise and means slightly more than one half, while at exactly one half the sale remains effective. Although Tosafot leaves the matter unresolved, their words show that the Yerushalmi may readily be imprecise. It follows that Tosafot in Bava Kamma and in HaZahav should be understood precisely, as their language clearly indicates. One who studies the Gemara in HaMekabel will also see that it is strained to say that the example of one hundred for two hundred is imprecise, as I wrote above in siman 66, seif 38. It is possible that even the Rosh, who writes that the case in HaMekabel is imprecise, did not intend this exclusively. He could equally have said that the Yerushalmi was imprecise. This appears to be the Rama's view in the gloss. Nevertheless, concerning the practical law, I concluded above at the end of siman 66 that the principal ruling follows the Rif and those who agree with him. See Responsa Maharashdam, siman 58, page 53, column 4, who writes that it is a monetary doubt. See also Responsa Mahari ibn Lev, kelal 20, siman 108; Responsa Maharit, end of siman 93; and Responsa Rabbi Avraham ibn Chaim, siman 69.
