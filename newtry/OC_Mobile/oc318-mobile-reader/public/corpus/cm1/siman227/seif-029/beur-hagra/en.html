Even if he sold, etc. Meaning that although Bava Metzia 57a quotes Rava otherwise, the halachah does not follow that statement, because Rav Nachman, a later authority, rules in HaMekabel regarding an item sold for one hundred, etc. The same follows from Kiddushin chapter 2 concerning a disparity greater than one-sixth, and from Bava Kamma chapter 1 concerning an item worth money, etc.
Some say, etc. According to the Bava Metzia sugya there and Rabbeinu Tam’s explanation that where the disparity exceeds one-half it is called voidance of the sale. This does not conflict with the passages in HaMekabel and Kiddushin. Tosafos explains the Bava Kamma source nonliterally, only to mean that land has a broader threshold than movables. Bava Metzia 58b states that Rabbi Yehudah says even one who sells, etc., and asks “up to how much, etc.” Since it says “even one who sells,” the same principle applies to land. Kesubos 100b similarly records Rabban Shimon ben Gamliel’s view and asks the threshold. The Yerushalmi in Kesubos likewise discusses this. See Tosafos in Kiddushin and Bava Kamma and Tosafos in Bava Metzia there.
But more than, etc. Although Tosafos in the chapter Almanah and the Rosh in Bava Metzia do not state this, and Tosafos in Kiddushin remains uncertain, Tosafos in Bava Kamma states it explicitly. The same is implied by the passages in HaMekabel, HaZahav, and Almanah cited above.
