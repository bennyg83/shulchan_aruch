He has no ona'ah claim. See Responsa Radbaz, part 4, siman 269, who writes that even voiding the sale for ona'ah greater than one sixth does not apply. Rambam states that even a needle exchanged for armor is valid, and armor is obviously worth many thousands of needles. See there. This is straightforward. Radbaz further writes that even a defect does not annul a barter transaction, because a needle exchanged for armor presents no greater "defect" in value. This is extremely difficult. Machaneh Ephraim, Hilchot Mechirah, siman 9, already objects. The primary ruling is as Radbaz himself concludes at the end of the responsum: if there is an actual defect in the needle or armor, the transaction may be rescinded, because the recipient can say, "I required a sound utensil." Netivot HaMishpat likewise writes simply that claims based on actual defects certainly apply, because a person does not accept defective goods. Responsa Galia Masechta, siman 10, rules likewise.
