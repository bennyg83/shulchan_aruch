Seif 29, gloss. But more than half. A person wrote a will giving one son all his movables, and as to a house worth and originally purchased for several thousand, he wrote to that son in the language of sale for one thousand plus an obligation to pay from it a half-male inheritance note for his daughter and the like. According to the Shach at the end of siman 66, onaah may not apply at all. Urim VeTumim inclines to apply onaah where the disparity exceeds half, but regards the matter as doubtful, and the Shach in siman 227 likewise cites it as a doubt. In this particular case, however, the better view is that there is no realistic claim of onaah. The father plainly knew the value and used the language and reduced figure merely to provide a formal explanation for transferring the inheritance almost entirely to one favored son rather than to a son of his divorced wife. With land, deviation from the ordinary inheritance scheme is especially sensitive, as is known from the law of the half-male inheritance document. His continuing to reside in the house until his death also shows that the arrangement was really an inheritance transfer. There may additionally have been fiscal reasons for stating a lower value because a document written at full value would have required more expensive stamped paper under imperial law, and perhaps the conventional concern of avoiding the appearance of wealth also applies. In any event, it is difficult to characterize the father as a mistaken seller when his intention to transfer for less than market value was evident to everyone familiar with the circumstances. I later learned that the arrangement was made so that the son would support him and care for him throughout his illness and life. If so, there is no onaah at all; at the least, the disparity is not properly measured as one-half because the son's services were part of the consideration. It is also exactly the case of a person who sells under pressing personal need and knowingly accepts a figure so far from market value that no ordinary mistake is plausible. His intention is evident from his conduct to everyone.
