From a Jew. Maharshal rules in Bava Kamma, chapter 5, siman 41, that if a Jew and a non-Jew jointly overcharged another Jew, and under non-Jewish law the injured party cannot collect the non-Jew's half of the ona'ah because they tell him, figuratively, "Open your eyes and see," then since nothing can be collected from the non-Jew, the Jewish partner must return the entire amount. We rule like Rabbi Natan that whenever collection cannot be had from one party, collection may be had from the other. Moreover, presumably the injured Jew principally relied upon the Jewish party, not upon the non-Jew. However, where the defect is a mistaken purchase, such as one who bought reddish wheat and it was found to be white, or silver and it was found to be tin, and similar cases as discussed in siman 233, even under their laws one can collect from the non-Jew. In that case the Jew certainly pays only his own share, even if the non-Jew is violent and practically uncollectible. This resembles the ruling in siman 410, seif 37, cited in the name of dissenting authorities, which the Shach considers primary. Shach.
