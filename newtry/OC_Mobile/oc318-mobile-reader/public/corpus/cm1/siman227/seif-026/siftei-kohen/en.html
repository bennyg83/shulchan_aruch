A donkey from a Jew, etc. Maharshal rules at the end of Bava Kamma, siman 41, concerning a Jew and a non Jew who deceived another Jew in a transaction. If under non Jewish law the injured Jew cannot recover the non Jew's half of the overreaching, because they say figuratively, 'Open your eyes and see,' so that nothing can be collected from the non Jew, the Jewish partner must return the entire amount. We follow Rav Nachman that wherever recovery cannot be had from one party, it is collected from the other. Moreover, the injured Jew presumably relied primarily upon the Jewish seller, not upon the non Jew. If, however, the defect constitutes a mistaken transaction, such as where the animal was represented as dark colored and found to be white, or silver was found to be tin, and similar cases described in siman 233, so that even under their law recovery can be had from the non Jew, then the Jewish partner pays only his own share. This applies even if the non Jew is violent and cannot practically be compelled, comparable to the ruling in siman 410, seif 37, in the name of some authorities, which is the principal law.
