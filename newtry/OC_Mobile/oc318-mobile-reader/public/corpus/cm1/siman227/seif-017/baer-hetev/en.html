To my tongue. A person is not familiar with the coins that need an expert on it. Name:
Shabbat. Meaning, for the honor of Shabbat. Even a wealthy person who has other money available to spend is not given a longer period, because an ordinary person who is not expert in coins spends the coins whose soundness is doubtful in his eyes, while he keeps in his box those that he knows are good. Ibid.
Under pressure. This is also the wording of the Tur. Meaning, if the coin can circulate with difficulty, even as a matter of exceptional piety there is no need to exchange it. Do not wonder at this, for the Sages said something even stronger: after the period in which one may show the item to a merchant has elapsed, even if one sold an item worth 100 for 200, the sale stands and there is no obligation to return the ona'ah. This is unlike the Levush, who writes that if the coin cannot circulate even with difficulty, it is obvious that he must exchange it even after a long time. That is not correct. Although the Rambam's wording also indicates the Levush's view, the Maggid Mishneh already questioned him and wrote that the Gemara does not indicate so. For this reason the Mechaber did not copy the Rambam's wording, as he generally does, but instead copied the Tur's wording, which accords with the Gemara. This is the Sema's ruling. The Taz disagrees with him and concurs with the Levush. See what he writes in explanation of the Rambam and the Tur on this matter.
