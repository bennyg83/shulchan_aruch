Or where he purchased through a broker. Meaning, even if the purchaser knew the goods belonged to the owner, since the owner handed them to a broker to sell to whoever came to buy them, the owner certainly relinquished the expectation that the purchaser would know they were his and therefore delivered them to the broker on the understanding that the ordinary laws of ona'ah would apply. See below in seif 30, where the Mechaber cites a dispute concerning an agent who wronged the purchaser, whether the sale is void for any amount of error. Here, however, he rules without qualification concerning a broker that the law of ona'ah applies. The Tur likewise writes in both places in accordance with the Mechaber. One can distinguish a broker, who has his own connection to the transaction and a financial share in it, so the principal cannot say regarding him, "I sent you to improve matters, not to cause harm," as explained in simanim 182 and 185. In this case everyone agrees. See the Perishah for further discussion.
