Where he bought through a broker. Meaning, even if the purchaser knew the goods belonged to the householder, nevertheless, since the owner handed them to a broker to sell to whoever came to buy, the owner surely accepted the possibility that the purchaser might not know they belonged to him, and therefore entrusted them on the understanding that any overreaching would have to be returned. Sema. The Taz disagrees and holds that if the purchaser knew they belonged to the owner, overreaching need not be returned. Here the case is where the purchaser appointed the broker as his agent. Then even if the purchaser knew, because the agent did not know, the overreaching must be returned. The Sema further asks that seif 30 rules that if an agent is overreached on behalf of the purchaser, the sale is void for any amount. He distinguishes that a broker has a financial interest in the transaction, and therefore we do not say the sale is void for any amount. See Beurim: his distinction is extremely difficult.
