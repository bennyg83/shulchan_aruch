But if he did not know. See Sema, seif katan 42. The primary ruling follows Taz: if the buyer knew that the item belonged to the principal, he has no ona'ah claim against the broker, as Taz proves from Rosh cited by Tur. According to this, Sema's question from seif 30 is also resolved, because our case can concern an agent who did not disclose that he was acting as an agent. In that situation the transaction is not void for even a minimal discrepancy, as explained in siman 182. Sema's attempt to distinguish between a broker and another agent is very difficult, for siman 185 indicates the opposite.
