A broker. Meaning, even if the buyer knew that the goods belonged to a private owner, since the owner delivered them to a broker to sell to whoever would come to purchase them, he certainly despaired of the buyer knowing that they were privately owned and entrusted them to the broker on the understanding that any ona'ah would be refundable. In seif 30 the Mechaber records a dispute where a buyer's agent overcharged whether the sale is void for any amount, yet here concerning an ordinary broker he rules simply that the law of ona'ah applies. One can distinguish a broker, who has an interest and share in the transaction, and against whom the principal cannot say, "I sent you to improve matters, not to harm me," as explained in simanim 181 and 182. On this all agree. This is the Sema's ruling. The Taz rejects this explanation. He explains the Tur as follows: whether the buyer himself purchases from the private owner without knowing that he is the owner, or the buyer sent a broker to purchase, in which latter case even if the buyer himself knows that the seller is the private owner, nevertheless, since the broker is the one effecting the purchase and does not know that the seller is the owner, the law of ona'ah applies. This also explains the wording "purchased through a broker"; according to the Sema it should have said "sold through a broker." One cannot object that in the Taz's case the transaction should be void for even the slightest deviation because the principal may say, "I sent you to improve matters, not to harm me," since the case can be one where he expressly told him that his agency was valid whether he improved or worsened the terms.
