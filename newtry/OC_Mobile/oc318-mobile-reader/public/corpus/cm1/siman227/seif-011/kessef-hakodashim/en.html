Seif 30. But an agent. A wife who regularly conducts her husband's business may perhaps have greater authority than an ordinary agent, so that a minor onaah below one-sixth does not automatically invalidate her transaction. I have nevertheless heard an inclination to treat her like an agent. At first glance she may resemble a partner, as it appears that even regarding the other partner's half, cancellation requires one-sixth although partners are not authorized to distort one another's interests. Perhaps the reason is that because the acting partner also bought or sold his own share on the same terms, his conduct itself shows that he did not regard the price as a distortion. That rationale does not apply to a wife conducting business for her husband. Another possibility is a market enactment: if every slight deviation invalidated her transactions, others would refuse to deal with her. Her ordinary commercial acts may therefore remain valid for the kinds of small disparity merchants commonly tolerate, such as less than one-sixth. The same policy may explain the law of partners, as reflected in the Shach's two answers in siman 77 concerning a partner's deviation. His first answer there may apply equally to a wife conducting business, because the parties accept such latitude so that people will be willing to transact with her.
