(Seif 19, in the gloss: others say that the matter is doubtful, and so forth.) The Rosh's view is that he was uncertain whether the law follows Rabbah or Rabbi Abba, and because of the doubt we do not extract money. The Sema writes that this gloss applies only to money sold by rough estimate. I am astonished at these words from his holy mouth. What relevance does the frequency of the transaction have to the law of overreaching? Only regarding the rabbinic enactment that movable property is acquired by drawing is there room to distinguish between common and uncommon cases. It appears entirely clear to me that the alternative opinion disputes both cases. Fruit sold by rough estimate is also uncommon, and for that reason the Tur did not separately mention fruit sold by rough estimate, since there is no distinction between the two. What the Tur wrote earlier in siman 209, “this heap of wheat,” was not intended to teach the law of overreaching, but only that acquisition is effective in such a case. Therefore he cited the Rambam there and, incidentally, copied the rule that overreaching applies, relying for the law of overreaching upon what he wrote in the laws of overreaching. This is clear. As to practical law, although I am unworthy to decide, I will state my share: one should rule like the Rif and Rambam that overreaching applies here, as the Beit Yosef writes that we follow them. We should not abandon their certainty because of the Rosh's doubt.
