And needless to say in a case of voiding the sale. See Responsa Rishmei She'eilah, siman 57, which raises a doubt where the first purchaser has already resold the item. Is the second sale also void because the first purchaser sold something that was not truly his, so that the original seller can compel both the first and second purchasers to annul their transactions? He brings proof from Mishneh LaMelech, Hilchot Ishut, chapter 5, halachah 7, who proves at the end from Rambam, Hilchot Mamrim, chapter 4, halachah 3, that voiding a sale for ona'ah is not treated as theft. His father replied that the second sale remains valid. Even if voiding the first sale were treated as establishing that the first purchaser wrongfully held the item, the second purchaser certainly need not accept the bare assertion of the original seller and first purchaser that their sale was mistaken. Perhaps there is collusion between them, or perhaps the original seller sold under financial pressure and both parties knew of the ona'ah. How is the second purchaser to know that there was no scheme? Therefore, regarding the first purchaser's liability to the original seller for the full value of the item, if the first purchaser accepts a cherem that he did not know the transaction was void for ona'ah, then he sold with the seller's implied authorization. Had he destroyed the item by hand or given it to a non Jew, he would not have been required to pay from his own funds beyond the applicable liability. Likewise, now that he sold it to another Jew he need pay only what he actually received. If he cannot accept such a cherem, however, he must pay the original seller the full value of the item.
