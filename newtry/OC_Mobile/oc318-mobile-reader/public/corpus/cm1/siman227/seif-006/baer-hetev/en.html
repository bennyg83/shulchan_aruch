To be uncertain. The Tur writes in the name of the Rosh: It would appear that since the Torah said, "You shall not wrong one another," even a perutah is included. The Sages merely said that people cannot measure values with absolute precision up to one sixth. But if the seller or buyer is expert and knows that he is overcharging the other party, while the other does not know, it is forbidden to wrong him unless he explicitly disclosed the matter and stipulated it with him. Alternatively, perhaps since this is the normal manner of buying and selling, an discrepancy of less than one sixth is included in the sale and is not legally deemed ona'ah at all. This requires study, and one who fears Heaven should satisfy both possibilities. Meaning, if a God-fearing person was overcharged by less than one sixth, he should not pursue the one who overcharged him and say, "You have not fulfilled your obligation toward Heaven, for I did not know of this and did not waive it; return it to me." But if he himself happened to overcharge another by less than one sixth, he should return the amount to the injured party. Nevertheless, it appears that even if the buyer still holds the purchase money, he cannot say, "I will not waive the amount by which you overcharged me, even though it is less than one sixth," because his personal view is nullified against the general practice of people, who ordinarily waive such an amount. The money is therefore taken from him and given to the seller. This is the Sema's ruling. The Taz writes that this requires study, for there is an explicit Gemara in Bava Batra 95. There, regarding ona'ah, the parties originally intended an exact equivalent value. Nevertheless, with less than one sixth, the discrepancy is not readily discernible in a maneh and people waive it. This implies that their original intent was exact equality. The Rosh concludes, "and one who fears Heaven," etc. It appears to me that when it is not certain that the discrepancy is less than one sixth and he wishes to return it, the recipient should not accept it from him, for perhaps the amount was legally waived. If the one returning it is also God-fearing, he should give it to him as a gift.
