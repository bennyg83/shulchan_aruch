(Seif 22: to enhance my estimation.) This means that just as it is clear that the item is worth more than one zuz, so too it is clear that the sale was made without any mistake at all. This implies that whenever the buyer knows there is at least some overreaching, though he does not know its amount, the seller's statement that it is worth only one zuz is effective, for it cannot then be said that he spoke merely to enhance the buyer's estimation. According to the Rambam, however, even in such a case the law of overreaching applies until the buyer clearly knows the exact amount of the overcharge, because it is a matter without a fixed measure, as the Tur writes in siman 232 concerning defects. We do not follow the Rambam there, as explained in that siman. One should not ask that the Tur here cites the Rambam's words that the seller must specify the amount of the overcharge. The intent is that by specifying it, there is certainly no room to say that the seller spoke merely to enhance the buyer's estimation where the buyer did not know at all that there was an overcharge. But where the buyer knew that there was some overcharge, even though the seller says the item is worth only one zuz, that statement is effective, as explained. In my humble opinion, this is also proven from the Tur's view in siman 232, seif 10.
