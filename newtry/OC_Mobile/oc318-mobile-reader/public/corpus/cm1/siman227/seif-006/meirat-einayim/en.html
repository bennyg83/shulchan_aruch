There is room to doubt whether it is permitted, etc. The Tur writes in the Rosh's name: Since the Torah says, 'Do not wrong,' even a perutah is included in the prohibition. The Sages merely fixed one sixth as the practical threshold because exact values cannot always be calculated. Accordingly, if the seller or purchaser is expert and knows he is wronging the other, while the other does not know, perhaps it is forbidden even below one sixth unless he expressly discloses and stipulates. Or perhaps ordinary commerce is conducted on the understanding that discrepancies below one sixth are part of the transaction and are not legally called overreaching at all. The matter requires study, and a God fearing person should satisfy both possibilities. This means that if he was wronged by less than one sixth, he should not pursue the other party and insist that nothing was waived. But if he himself wronged the other by less than one sixth, he should return the amount. Nevertheless, it appears that a purchaser cannot say while still holding the purchase money, 'I personally do not waive this sub one sixth discrepancy.' His individual preference is nullified to the ordinary commercial practice that such amounts are waived, and the money is extracted from him for the seller.
