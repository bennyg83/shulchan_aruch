On condition that you have no claim of ona'ah against me, etc. Although if one says regarding shemittah, "on condition that the Sabbatical year shall not release my debt," we say that monetary conditions are valid and the debt is not released, as the Tur and Mechaber write above in siman 67, there is a distinction. In the shemittah case the borrower knows the right he is waiving. Here he may not know that there is any ona'ah for him to waive, as the Mechaber concludes. The Tur likewise writes that even if the seller says, "on condition that you have no claim of ona'ah against me," the purchaser may think there is only a very small discrepancy. This is unlike the Levush, who says the condition is ineffective because it contradicts Torah law. That is not correct. He is not stipulating that Torah law be nullified; he is stipulating that the other party waive the monetary ona'ah.
And it need not be stated if he said, "on condition that there is no ona'ah in it." This language means, "on condition that the law of ona'ah shall not apply," and one cannot stipulate in this way to cancel Torah law. Likewise regarding shemittah, if he says, "on condition that the Sabbatical year shall not release the debt," his words are ineffective. The matter therefore returns to ordinary Torah law: less than one sixth is waived; exactly one sixth, the transaction stands and the ona'ah is returned. The Tur writes this in Rashbam's name. He further writes that Rashi holds that in this formulation the sale is a mistaken transaction and the injured party can entirely retract if he wishes, even if the discrepancy is only exactly one sixth. The Tur's language implies that he agrees with Rashi, as I wrote in the Perishah. The Mechaber, however, writes "and it need not be stated," implying that the legal result is the same as where he said, "on condition that you have no ona'ah claim against me." In that earlier case it is obvious that with exactly one sixth he does not rescind the entire sale; rather, the condition is ignored and the ordinary law applies.

The following seif likewise says, "for he certainly knew the specific matter he waived." See the Perishah here and in siman 232, where I wrote that even according to the halacha that a person can transfer or waive an unquantified monetary amount, in a waiver of this kind he must specify the amount. Otherwise he can say, "I did not know there was so much ona'ah that I intended to waive it."
