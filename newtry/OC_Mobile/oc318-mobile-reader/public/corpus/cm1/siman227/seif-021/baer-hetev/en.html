Onaah. Although if one says in the Sabbatical year, 'on condition that the debt shall not be released,' we say that a monetary condition is effective and the debt is not released, as stated in siman 67, that case is different because the creditor knows the right and waives it. Here, however, perhaps the buyer did not know that there was onaah and therefore did not knowingly waive it. The Tur likewise writes that even if the seller says, 'on condition that you have no onaah claim against me,' the buyer may have thought the discrepancy was only slight. So writes the Sema. See Responsa Mabit, part 1, siman 126, and Responsa Menachem Azariah, siman 42.
In it. This sounds like a condition that the transaction itself shall not be subject to the Torah's law of onaah. One cannot stipulate to annul a Torah law. The case therefore reverts to ordinary Torah law: less than one sixth is waived, exactly one sixth stands and the onaah is returned. So writes the Tur in the name of Rashbam. He further writes that Rashi holds that in such a case the transaction is a mistaken sale and the disadvantaged party may rescind entirely if he wishes, even if the discrepancy is exactly one sixth. The Tur's wording implies that he agrees with Rashi, but the Mechaber's phrase 'and needless to say' implies that he treats this like the case where one says, 'on condition that you have no onaah claim against me,' where it is clear that at exactly one sixth the sale is not entirely rescinded. Rather, the condition is ignored. Sema.
