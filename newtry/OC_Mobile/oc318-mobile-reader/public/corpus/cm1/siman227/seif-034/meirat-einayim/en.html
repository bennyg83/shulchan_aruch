He hired him to sow land for him, etc. Meaning, Reuven hired Shimon as a contractor to sow Reuven's field with Reuven's seed. Shimon is responsible only for the labor and effort of sowing. The Gemara leaves unresolved whether we follow the land, so that he is a land contractor and there is no law of overreaching, or the seed, so that overreaching applies. The Maggid Mishneh explains the Rambam, whose language is like the Mechaber's, this way, and says the Raavad likewise understood the Gemara. The Raavad asks: if it is treated like land, why should the contractor not simply return and sow until the proper amount has been reached, since his work is not yet complete? He answers that adding more seed does not necessarily improve the field, because the second sowing may fall in the same place as the first and one seed may spoil the other. Ramban challenges the whole interpretation that the case concerns a contractor hired to sow with the owner's seed, arguing that this falls under the law of one who undertook, 'If I leave the field fallow and do not work it,' and therefore explains the Gemara differently.
