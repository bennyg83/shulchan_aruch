When does this apply, etc.; likewise, etc. As above, because the reason is that the injured party did not know, etc. See Perishah.
The one who overcharged said, etc. Meaning that originally there was not enough disparity for ona'ah, but a change in the market later made the disparity reach the ona'ah threshold.
Upon, etc., because the manner, etc. Because the burden of proof rests on the claimant. This follows the rule at the end of HaMadir: whenever a doubt arises while property is in someone’s domain, that person bears the burden of proof. This differs from “found here, presumed to have arisen here,” and also from Rabbi Nasan’s rule in Bava Batra 153b concerning whether a person was healthy or ill, because market prices ordinarily change, as the Gemara says on 154. See Tosafos, Kesubos 20a, s.v. “establish,” and Yevamos 31, s.v. “and the land, etc.”
