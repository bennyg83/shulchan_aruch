For market prices commonly change. Mordechai discusses a coat purchased for a community, where the community later claimed it had been overpriced. He rejects the idea that the present market price alone proves the earlier overreaching. Market prices can change from morning to evening according to the buyers present and current demand. He distinguishes the law of Rabbi Natan concerning present majority status, where we follow the current bodily status because the person was originally presumed healthy and most people are healthy. There the present status joins a bodily presumption. This creates difficulty from Tur in Even HaEzer, who rules that if a man betrothed a woman with an item and we do not know whether it was worth a perutah at the time, we follow its present value. If it is now worth a perutah, the first kiddushin can be treated as definite. How can current market value establish past value when market prices naturally fluctuate? Beit Shmuel cites Rabbi Meir HaLevi Abulafia's rule without noting the tension, while Rama here rules like Mordechai that current price does not establish prior price.
