One who hires another. Meaning, he hires him for his labor on a daily basis, so he is a true employee. The Tur likewise writes, "one who hires a worker." He has the legal status of a servant. Although he may retract even in the middle of the day, as explained below in siman 333, so long as he has not retracted he bears the legal status of a servant. A contractor, by contrast, who undertakes to complete a defined task whenever he wishes, does not have the status of a servant, as the Tur and Mechaber write below in seif 37.
And servants are not subject to ona'ah. Although the verse, "you shall bequeath them," concerns Canaanite slaves, one who hires himself as a worker is also called a servant. See the Perishah.
And the amount by which he was wronged, etc. This implies even more than one sixth. Terumat HaDeshen writes this explicitly, and the Mechaber likewise states it expressly in seif 35. There I wrote that this applies specifically after the work has already been performed. Terumat HaDeshen says the same. See there.
