(Seif 23: through a broker.) The Sema writes in seif katan 42 that this applies even if the buyer knew that the seller was a private householder. This is exceedingly surprising. Perhaps the householder instructed the broker not to sell the item for less than a fixed amount above its true value, and the broker sold it accordingly. Since the buyer knew that the money would reach the householder who appointed the broker, the buyer certainly caused his own loss. Moreover, this appears proven from the responsum cited by the Tur in seif 35 concerning an article that Shimon gave to a broker. It concludes that if Shimon is a private householder, the law of overreaching does not apply. This is explicit as I have written. It therefore appears to me that the Tur means as follows. Either the buyer himself bought from the householder, but did not know that the seller was a householder, or the buyer sent a broker to purchase for him. In the latter case, even if the buyer himself knows that the seller is a householder, since the broker is the one who effects the purchase and the broker does not know that the seller is a householder, the law of overreaching applies. The wording “he purchased through a broker” is now precise. According to the Sema's explanation, it should have said “he sold through a broker.” One should not object that according to my explanation, since the buyer appointed the broker, the buyer may retract even for the smallest discrepancy, claiming, “I sent you to improve matters, not to impair them.” The case may be one in which he expressly told the broker to act whether for benefit or for detriment.
