Fenced. The owners are obligated to guard their animals so they do not cause damage. This is comparable to an animal eating from inside a shop, as stated in siman 391, seif 9, and the owner cannot say, "You should have closed your shop." The Tur, however, writes that Rabbenu Tam and the Rosh disagree and hold that this is not comparable to eating from inside a shop. So writes the Sema. The Shach likewise rules that if the landowner did not fence his property and an animal entered and ate, the animal's owner is exempt. The Torah's liability for tooth "in another's field" applies only where the animal burrowed under the fence or where the fence fell at night. He discusses this at length and concludes that since Halachot Gedolot, Rabbenu Chananel, Raavan, the Rosh, and the Tur all hold the owner exempt, that is the law. The traditions of Halachot Gedolot and Rabbenu Chananel are received from the Gemara, and I have also demonstrated that their reasoning is primary. Shach.
