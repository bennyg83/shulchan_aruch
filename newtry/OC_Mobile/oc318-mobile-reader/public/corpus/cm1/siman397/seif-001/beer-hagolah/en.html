The wording of the Tur in the name of Ri, from the incident involving the goats of Bei Tarbu, as Rabbi Yosef said to Abaye, Bava Metzia chapter 2, 53b.
In my humble opinion this requires study. According to Rosh, the owner can say, 'Fence your field,' following Rabbenu Chananel, as the Tur writes immediately below. According to Tosafot, who bring proof from an animal eating inside shops, it appears the animal owner must pay even without prior warning. Raavad likewise writes this, and the Maggid Mishneh in chapter 5 of Hilkhot Nizkei Mamon writes in the name of most commentators that payment is due even without warning. In my humble opinion, warning serves only to require removal of the damaging animal. Even if the owner pays the damage, the victim should not be forced repeatedly to litigate, and sometimes a person may be unable to do so, as Tosafot write concerning the goats of the marketplace. Ritva and the Ramah, cited by Nimukei Yosef, likewise hold that the owners are placed under ban until they remove the danger.
