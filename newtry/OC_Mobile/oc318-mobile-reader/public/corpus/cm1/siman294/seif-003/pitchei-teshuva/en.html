Responsa Beit Ephraim, siman 35, questions Shach's ruling at length and ultimately cites Maharshal, volume 2, Choshen Mishpat, siman 189, who in a case like that of Terumat HaDeshen relies on Terumat HaDeshen and holds liable the person through whom the debt arose if he failed to make proper efforts. Maharshal writes that his teacher Rabbi Yosef Taitatzak esteemed the responsa of Terumat HaDeshen like those of the Rosh, and he defends them against Beit Yosef's criticism. One cannot say, "Even had I been there I could not have saved it"; rather, we presume that had he been there he might have saved it. Maharshal says similarly elsewhere. Responsa Rashach, volume 1, siman 189, rules the same way in that case. In volume 2, siman 46, Rashach discusses Reuven, who acted as factor for merchants, and Shimon who loaded merchandise on a ship and instructed Reuven to arrange insurance with a banker. Reuven did so. The ship was wrecked, and after the six month period fixed by Venetian commercial law, the insurer repeatedly delayed payment. Much time passed and Reuven was then taken captive. Rashach writes that if Reuven was negligent in collecting from the insurer, relying improperly on his delays, he is certainly liable. Whether reliance on those delays was itself negligent should be determined by expert merchants according to accepted commercial practice. If they deem it negligence, he must pay.
