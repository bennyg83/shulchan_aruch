If he said that he admits himself and he has a moustache, he would say that Nana was not in the place of Ría, as he was instead of a witness in his cell:
Even if there are witnesses that it was stolen. At first glance Mechaber seems to imply that when witnesses prove the object is no longer in the custodian's possession, the custodian need not swear further. Ketzot HaChoshen and Maharchash challenge this from the barrel-carrier case and from Rambam, who writes expressly that the custodian must still establish that he did not act negligently. The correct distinction is this. The oath that he did not commit shelichut yad is only a secondary oath derived through gilgul. The verse "if there is no witness" implies that where there are witnesses to the loss, that secondary oath disappears. But the oath that he did not act negligently at the time of the loss can still be a principal custodial oath. The verse does not prove otherwise. Therefore a paid custodian who proves only that the animal died or was stolen may still need to swear that the event was one that exempts him, because some forms of loss remain his responsibility. An unpaid custodian may sometimes need no further oath where the very proved event would be exempt absent negligence, because we do not presume people negligent without reason. This also explains why Abaye can require an unpaid custodian to swear even when the thief is known: that oath may concern the circumstances of the guarding, not merely whether the object remains in his possession. Accordingly, one should not treat all these oaths alike. Proof that the object is gone can eliminate the oath concerning present possession and shelichut yad, while an oath concerning negligence may remain where the legal category of the custodian requires it.
