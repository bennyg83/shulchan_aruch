He must bring proof through witnesses and is exempt. Scripture says, "There is no witness, an oath of Hashem shall be between them." This teaches that where there is no witness he swears, but where witnesses are available he must prove the claim through evidence. The phrase "and is exempt" means even from an oath. Although one could theoretically suspect that he made unauthorized use of the item, we do not presume people to be wicked merely to impose an oath on that possibility alone, unless it arises through a rolling oath appended to the oath that the item was lost by accident or stolen. The oath concerning loss or theft is imposed because there are grounds for suspicion. The depositor certainly gave the item to him, the custodian admits receiving it, and it is now missing. Theft and unavoidable accidents are uncommon. Regarding the verse, Rabbeinu Tam explains that "an oath of Hashem shall be between them if he did not put his hand to his fellow's property" concerns a case where the item is not before us and we may suspect the custodian consumed it. The Maggid Mishneh explains that the verse means the oath of theft or loss exempts him only if he did not previously make unauthorized use. If it is known that he did, he is not exempt by swearing that it was stolen. See the Perishah.

He must swear that he was not negligent, etc. These authorities reason that since he has not thereby been established as a serious wrongdoer, we remain concerned for negligence. This differs from making unauthorized use for one's own benefit, where he actively violated the depositor's wishes. Certainly it differs if he intended to diminish the object, which is outright taking by hand.
