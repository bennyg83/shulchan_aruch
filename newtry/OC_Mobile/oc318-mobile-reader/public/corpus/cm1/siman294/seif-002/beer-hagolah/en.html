Gimel) There, seif 2, from the baraita of Isi ben Yehudah in the seventh chapter of Bava Metzia 83a.
Dalet) This is the language of the Mechaber and includes Rambam's ruling in chapter 6 of Hilkhot She'eilah U'Pikadon, halachah 3: the custodian is exempt from an oath, and we do not say that perhaps he first misappropriated the item before it was lost. The Maggid Mishneh explains that once it has been established that the principal oath concerns whether the item was stolen, and the custodian brings proof that it was stolen without negligence, he should likewise be exempt from the oath concerning possible misappropriation. This also appears from the statement in HaOmanin that where witnesses are available, he brings proof and is exempt, meaning proof that he was not negligent. Tosafot write similarly regarding Rav Sheshet's statement nearby, and the Tur cites Rabbenu Yitzchak in seif 4. See the Rama's gloss below.
Heh) There, seif 4. Rambam likewise writes at the beginning of chapter 4, based on Rav Sheshet's statement on Bava Metzia 6a. The Maggid Mishneh writes that the primary oath expressed in the Torah can be understood as the oath that the property was stolen, and the verse means that the householder approaches beit din to swear that it was indeed stolen, etc.
