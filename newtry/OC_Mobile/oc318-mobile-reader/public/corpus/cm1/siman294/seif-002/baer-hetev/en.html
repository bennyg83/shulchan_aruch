Was negligent. Rabbeinu Yerucham writes that it is insufficient for him to swear merely that the event occurred unintentionally, because that formulation implies that he admits negligence and that he failed to guard it properly, only that he did not act intentionally. This is explicit in the Gemara at the end of the chapter HaOmanin and in the Rosh there, and the Bach writes likewise.
To derive benefit. The Shach writes that this supports Rashi's explanation of the oath "I did not put forth my hand against it," meaning that he did not use it for work or derive any other benefit from it, and not Rabbeinu Tam's interpretation that he did not consume it. This also appears to be the view of the poskim, and in my opinion it is primary. For at the end of HaMafkid the Gemara says that the dispute is whether unauthorized use requires a deficiency or does not require a deficiency, and we rule that it does not. This implies that according to all opinions "putting forth one's hand" means using the item for work or some other benefit. This also resolves the Bach's question why the Tur omitted Rabbeinu Tam's explanation. See there for the Shach's further objections to the Bach.
