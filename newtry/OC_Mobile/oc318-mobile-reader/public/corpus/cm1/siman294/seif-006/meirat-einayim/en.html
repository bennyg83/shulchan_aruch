He litigates with the thief, etc. This is the Rambam's language. The Maggid Mishneh explains that custodians are required to trouble themselves to recover the object from the thief. If they cannot recover it, they are exempt from paying. Rashi interprets differently. Once the thief is identified, the custodian must pay the depositor in any event, and the custodian then seeks recovery from the thief. The Tur states this explicitly. The Beit Yosef writes that because the Rambam's language resembles the Gemara's abbreviated formulation, it could perhaps be interpreted like Rashi and the Tur. In the Derishah I explained that this is not correct. The Gemara says, "Whether an unpaid or paid custodian, he litigates with him and does not swear." Rashi explains "litigates" as litigation between depositor and custodian: the custodian pays the owner and then recovers from the thief. The Rambam explicitly writes, "he litigates with the thief," which plainly supports the Maggid Mishneh's reading that the custodian's duty is to bring the thief to judgment. See further in siman 305.
