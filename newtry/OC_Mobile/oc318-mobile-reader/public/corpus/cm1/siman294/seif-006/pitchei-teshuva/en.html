Comparable to a case where the thief became known. See Baer Heitev. Responsa Beit Ephraim, siman 35, extensively questions Shach. He cites Responsa Maharashdam, Choshen Mishpat II, siman 189, in a case like that of Terumat HaDeshen, who rules that one may rely on Terumat HaDeshen and hold liable the person through whom the debt was endangered if he failed to exert proper effort. Maharashdam records the great esteem in which his teacher Rabbi Yosef Taitatzak held Terumat HaDeshen and rejects Beit Yosef's criticism. The negligent party cannot simply claim that even had he been present he could not have saved the property, because it may be presumed that had he been there he would have saved it. Similar rulings appear in Maharashdam I, siman 444, and Responsa Maharashach I, siman 189. Maharashach II, siman 46, discusses a merchant who was instructed to arrange insurance through a banker for goods shipped by sea. The ship was lost, the insurer repeatedly delayed payment, and the merchant was later captured. Maharashach rules that if the merchant was negligent by relying on the insurer's delays instead of collecting when payment became due, he must pay. Whether his reliance amounted to negligence should be determined by expert merchants according to commercial practice. See also above, siman 207, seif 13, end of seif katan 17.
