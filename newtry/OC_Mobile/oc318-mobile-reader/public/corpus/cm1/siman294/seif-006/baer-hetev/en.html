When the thief was identified. I found that at the end of siman 335 the Beit Yosef writes that the words of Terumat HaDeshen are difficult in his eyes. I examined the matter, and in my humble opinion he is correct. This is not comparable to the case where the thief is identified after an item entrusted to a paid custodian was stolen from his house or domain. There the custodian certainly must do everything possible to restore it to its owner, because he was a paid custodian. Here that consideration does not apply. Moreover, there he is exempt only by oath, and perhaps if he brought witnesses that it was stolen through an unavoidable accident and the thief was then identified, he would be exempt. The same would apply here regarding Shimon's debt. Terumat HaDeshen itself writes initially that, according to straightforward reasoning, Reuven should not be obligated to exert himself at all, but concludes by claiming an exact parallel in the Gemara from the case of an identified thief. Once it has been shown that there is no proof from the Gemara, it is reasonable that Reuven is not obligated to exert himself. Shach.
