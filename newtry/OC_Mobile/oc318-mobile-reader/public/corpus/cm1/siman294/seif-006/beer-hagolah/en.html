Yod) This is the language of the Rambam at the end of Hilkhot She'eilah U'Pikadon, from the dispute between Abaye and Rava in the tenth chapter of Bava Kamma 108b. Rambam rules like Rava, as do the poskim.
Kaf) Meaning, an armed robber. Rashi and Tosafot explain likewise, as does the Tur.
Lamed) The Tur writes in seif 10 that the custodian must pay the owner and then pursue the thief in court to recover from him. Rashi likewise explains this. The Maggid Mishneh writes that the other commentators object: strictly speaking, the custodians should not be required to pay for an unavoidable accident merely because the thief was identified. Rather, the meaning is that they are obligated to exert themselves and litigate with the thief, because sometimes the thief is powerful and difficult and the owners do not want the burden of litigating with him. This is Rambam's view. The Kesef Mishneh and Beit Yosef write that they do not know how the Maggid Mishneh determined Rambam's intent, since Rambam's wording simply follows the Gemara and can bear whichever explanation the Gemara itself bears. In my humble opinion the Maggid Mishneh inferred it because had Rambam meant that the custodian must actually pay the depositor, he would not have omitted such a significant liability without stating it clearly. See siman 305, seif 2, where the Tur's wording that a paid custodian must pay is quoted.
