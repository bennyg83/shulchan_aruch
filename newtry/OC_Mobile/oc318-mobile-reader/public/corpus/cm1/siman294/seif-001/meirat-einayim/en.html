He demanded the deposit from him and he denied it, etc. It appears that even if the original deposit was not made before witnesses, nevertheless, if the depositor demands his deposit and the bailee denies it, saying, "It never happened," and witnesses then testify that they saw the deposit in his possession at the very time he denied it, he immediately becomes a robber with respect to it and is liable even for accidents. He cannot later say, "When I said it never happened, I meant only that it never came to me as a deposit, but rather through a sale," because if that had been his meaning he should have explained himself and not issued an unqualified denial. See also the beginning of siman 296, in the Mechaber, where the bailee is liable for the oath of bailees even when the deposit was not made before witnesses. The reason there is different, as I explained there.
He immediately becomes a robber with respect to it, etc. The word "immediately" means even before he swears to his denial, as the Tur and Mechaber write at the end of this siman. At the beginning of siman 75 it is explained that one who denies a loan does not thereby become a robber, though there is no practical difference regarding liability for accidents because a borrower is in any event liable for accidents. There I cited Rashi's wording that this applies even if witnesses testify that the loan money was in his possession when he denied the debt. Since money lent is given to be spent, we say that he denied it because he needed the money at that time but intended to repay later. A deposit, by contrast, was not given for expenditure or use in its present form.
