[Shulchan Aruch] And he is liable for unavoidable accidents. Note: I was uncertain about a case where, at the time he denied holding the deposit, it was not in his possession, and the circumstances were such that the denial cannot be explained as a temporary attempt to evade payment. For example, both the custodian and the deposit were in a meadow, and he denied holding it. Is he thereby liable for unavoidable accidents? One may say that a denial cannot be stronger than actual robbery. Without an act of acquisition, the item does not enter the robber's domain so as to make him liable for unavoidable accidents. Only with respect to disqualification as a witness must the denial be in circumstances where it cannot be attributed to temporary evasion. Yet the wording of the sugya implies that even with respect to liability for unavoidable accidents, where the deposit remains in a meadow, the denial must be of a type that cannot be attributed to temporary evasion. The underlying reasoning, however, requires study.
