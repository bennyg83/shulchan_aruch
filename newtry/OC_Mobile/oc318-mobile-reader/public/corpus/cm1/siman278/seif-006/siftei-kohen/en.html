The ordinary son cannot say, 'Give me a portion of the land in exchange for the improvement,' even when the land is large enough to be divided. This is evident from the Gemara in Perek HaGozel Kama and Perek HaMekabel and from the decisors. Rashi states this explicitly in Perek HaGozel Kama, Bava Kamma 95b, in the passage beginning 'the firstborn and the ordinary son.' This is plainly the intent of the Rambam and the Mechaber, and it is stated even more clearly in the Levush. This is unlike Sema, seif katan 10. Perhaps the Sema means a case where the firstborn agrees to this arrangement, but the plain meaning of his words does not indicate that, especially since the Rambam and Mechaber specifically intended to teach that the ordinary sons need not be given land. See Responsa Mabit, part II, siman 284.
