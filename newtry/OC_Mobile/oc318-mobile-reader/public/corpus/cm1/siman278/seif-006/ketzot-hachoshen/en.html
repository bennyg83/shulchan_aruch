If the heirs changed the property. In Bava Batra the Gemara distinguishes between grapes that were harvested or olives that were pressed, and grapes that were actually made into wine. According to Rav Yosef, even wine can remain subject to the firstborn's protest, but the Gemara asks that it was originally grapes and is now wine. This question is sound according to Shmuel and Rav Yosef because both accept that a substantial change can acquire for the one who effected it. Nimukei Yosef gives a different explanation. Since the firstborn does not ordinarily take a double share in appreciation produced by the heirs and receives it here only because he protested, the protest can preserve only what was already present in the estate. Wine that did not exist at the time of the protest is not taken doubly even if the grapes were. On this explanation, Tosafot's difficulty concerning whether change itself acquires disappears.
