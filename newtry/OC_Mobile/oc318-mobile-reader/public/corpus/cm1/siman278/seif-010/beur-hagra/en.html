For a creditor, etc. This follows the opinion of Ri Migash cited by Tur, that an ordinary heir cannot divest himself of his regular share because he stands in his father's place. Raavad disagrees and holds that he may disclaim even the ordinary share. The rule compelling heirs to satisfy the debt applies when they possess their father's assets, as explained at the beginning of siman 107. The special rule concerning a firstborn relates not to creditor collection but to retraction: an ordinary heir may later retract his disclaimer, whereas a firstborn share is treated as a gift. If a gift was rejected from the outset, the recipient never acquired it. See Tur and the discussion in seif 8. This is the meaning of 'and some say, etc.' See 40.
If they were, etc. Maggid Mishneh, chapter 1, and Tosafot there, s.v. 'if so, etc.'
If he had land, etc. As stated on 125b, 'this matter, etc.' See Rashbam there, s.v. 'as though he said.' Even the Western scholars do not disagree in such a case. See siman 248, seif 7, Gittin 48b, and the corresponding opinion in Bava Batra 136b. See 248.
Or, etc. A loan is excluded because the money was given for expenditure. Stolen property, however, when it must be returned intact, remains in the owner's legal possession. This requires study, since stolen property that has not been despaired of cannot be consecrated by either party. Moreover, what novelty would remain in the ruling on 123b, 'if he left them, etc.'? The Gemara challenges only the latter clause, indicating that the first clause is independently necessary. See 81.
