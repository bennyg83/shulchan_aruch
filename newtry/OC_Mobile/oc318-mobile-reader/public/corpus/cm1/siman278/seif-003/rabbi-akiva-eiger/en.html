[Shulchan Aruch] Even though it is documented in a note. Note: Regarding an iska note, see Responsa Chut HaShani, siman 1; Responsa Shevut Yaakov, part 1, siman 172; and Ateret Tzvi to this siman, who cites Responsa HaRibot, siman 78, as explicitly holding that the loan half is considered prospective property and is not treated as presently possessed property. Regarding obligations or bonds, see Responsa Noda BiYehudah, siman 234. Banknotes, in my opinion, have the status of actual currency and are not considered prospective property. My relative, the gaon Rabbi Meir Weil, likewise wrote to me that this was the ruling of the gaon who served as av beit din of the holy community of Berlin, of blessed memory. Regarding an iska note in which the stipulation mentioned in Shulchan Aruch, Yoreh Deah, siman 177, seif 5, was made, whether under that arrangement even the deposit half is considered prospective property, see Shevitot Yom Tov by Rabbi Yom Tov Alankona, Even HaEzer, siman 6.
