The firstborn has a right in the firstborn portion before division. Although the Torah calls it a gift, as it says, "to give him a double portion," and a gift does not become a gift until it reaches the recipient, this is why he may renounce it and why it does not automatically satisfy his creditor, as the Tur and Mechaber write below in seif 10. That is only because if he reveals that he does not want the portion, he does not acquire it against his will. But when he sells it and thereby reveals that he does want the firstborn portion, he acquires it and can sell it. See the Perishah.
He waived throughout all the property. We say that since he waived his right to take double from this item, he likewise waived it in the others. This follows from the rule that he has a right in the firstborn portion before division. Otherwise his taking only an ordinary share at first would not reveal waiver, because at that stage he would have had no firstborn right to waive. This is why the Mechaber uses the word "therefore.
