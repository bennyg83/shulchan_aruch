The rule that a firstborn is considered to have waived his additional share applies when he did not protest. If he protested to his brothers before two witnesses and said, "I am dividing these grapes equally with my brothers not because I have waived my firstborn share," this is a valid protest and he has not waived his right in the other property. Even if he protested while the grapes were attached, and they later harvested and divided them equally, he has not waived his right in the other property. But if they pressed the grapes and divided the wine equally and he did not protest once it had become wine, he has waived meaning that he remained silent and relinquished a claim that he could otherwise have asserted his right in the other property. This is comparable to one who protested regarding grapes and then divided olives equally with them, for by doing so he has waived his claim generally. The same applies in similar cases.
