Property that is only expected to come into the estate. A firstborn does not receive a double portion in property that is ra'ui rather than presently held. If an ordinary brother is in possession and wishes to say that the firstborn's entire double share should be satisfied from the ra'ui property, such possession should not help, nor should a migo. The Torah assigns each heir his precise share in every category of the estate according to its laws, and each item must therefore be divided accordingly. Conversely, even if the firstborn is in possession of property that is presently held, he cannot insist on taking his entire inheritance only from that property and leave the others the ra'ui property. A loan owed to the deceased may be different because Chazal treated it in some respects as though it were presently held, as discussed in seif 7. Regarding the Shach, seif katan 8, who invokes dina demalchuta that when a pledge is lost the loan is lost, thereby giving the pledge the status of something that causes monetary liability, perhaps this suffices to allow transfer or sale of the secured right. However one formulates the interest in the pledge, its monetary value may be only the discounted value of the uncertain benefit it provides. Even if a gentile purchaser cannot be absolutely certain of collecting even that amount should the pledge burn, perhaps the issue remains only a rabbinic doubt according to the later authorities who treat commercial custom or dina demalchuta, in contexts resembling situmta, as weaker than a Torah acquisition. It should be checked whether those authorities said so only as a stringency.
