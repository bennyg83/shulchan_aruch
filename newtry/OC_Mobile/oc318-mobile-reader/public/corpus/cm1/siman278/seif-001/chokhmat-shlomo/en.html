Seif 1. Regarding the sugya of "he shall recognize" and the argument that a father could simply give all his property as a gift, see my work Tov Taam, additions to Yoreh Deah, sixth edition, siman 266. There: A firstborn receives a double portion only, etc. I record several novel rulings. First, I was asked about a man with two wives, one of whom conceived first but the other gave birth first. Which child is the firstborn? I was told that Rabbi Chaim of Volozhin resolved from the verse that the beloved and hated wives "bear him sons," mentioning the beloved first, yet afterward the firstborn is said to be the son of the hated wife. I independently reached the same issue and discussed it above and in my Torah writings. At first one might infer that conception determines firstborn status. With twins, however, the Sages say the fetuses can reverse order in the womb, and Jacob's case was miraculous. One could also argue that where one drop divided into two embryos we cannot know which completed formation first; birth gives one child certain firstborn status, while the other's priority in formation remains doubtful, and a doubt does not displace certainty. With two different wives, if one pregnancy was visibly established earlier, one might think conception controls. I later saw, however, that Bekhorot proves the opposite. A child born by Caesarean section is not a firstborn because the verse requires actual birth. This shows that inheritance firstborn status depends on birth, not conception. Rashi on Toldot likewise explains that Jacob grasped Esau's heel in order to be first in birth as he was first in formation, so that he would open the womb and take the firstborn right according to law. Rashi's double formulation shows that even inheritance follows birth. If formation controlled, the Mishna in Bekhorot should have listed twins as a case where one is firstborn for priestly law and the other for inheritance, but it does not. Therefore the child born first is the firstborn for inheritance as well. I also use this to explain the Midrash that Esau's companions mocked the sale of the birthright. Their claim depended on Esau's hidden intent not to transfer sincerely. But if they invoke what is known before Heaven despite contrary external words, then before Heaven Jacob was first in formation and needed no sale from Esau at all.
2. Regarding a Jew who owns a village or town and dies leaving a firstborn and an ordinary son: does the firstborn receive a double portion in lease income from tavern rights or in tax concessions connected to liquor and similar privileges? In my opinion, rent deriving from the land itself is like rental income from a house, and the firstborn receives a double share. If the property were physically divided into three shares, the firstborn could take two and the other heir one, and the income generated by the larger land share would naturally be greater. The same remains true before division. As to a royal tax concession, if the king grants the same concession even to distillers who own no land, the concession derives from the business rather than the real estate and the firstborn has no extra share. But if such concessions are granted only to estate owners by virtue of their villages and towns, the right grows out of the land itself, and because the firstborn has two shares in the land he likewise receives two shares in that benefit. The lease income is even clearer, since it directly arises from the land.
