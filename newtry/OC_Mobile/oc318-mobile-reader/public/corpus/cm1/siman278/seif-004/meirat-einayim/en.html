He takes a double share in the cow and its offspring. Not only in the cow itself, meaning the increase in its fatness, where no new object has arisen because the body of the cow was already possessed by the father at death, but even in the offspring, which is a new entity not then in existence. Since the offspring came automatically from the cow that was possessed by the father, the firstborn takes a double portion. This excludes improvements produced through the labor and effort of the brothers or through their expenditures, and likewise houses they built or vineyards they planted, where everyone agrees the firstborn does not take double. The Mechaber says so in seif 6. This is based on a baraita in Yesh Nochalin. The Gemara asks whose view it follows and answers that it is Rabbi's, who says a firstborn takes a double share in post death appreciation. Rav Pappa then says that regarding a palm that grew thicker or land that accumulated alluvial soil everyone agrees the firstborn takes double, while they dispute grain that became ears or blossoms that became dates, because one view treats that as automatic appreciation and the other says the object has changed. The Rambam understands Rav Pappa as answering the original question and showing that the baraita can even follow the Sages, because a rented or grazing cow is like a palm that thickened, where everyone agrees. The Rosh and Tur understand Rav Pappa as an independent statement. According to them, only the thickening palm and accumulated soil are universally agreed, while a rented or grazing cow remains the subject of Rabbi's dispute with the Sages. Since the law follows Rabbi against an individual colleague but not against multiple colleagues, they disagree with the Rambam and Mechaber specifically in seif 4, not in seif 6. This explains Rama's dissent.
