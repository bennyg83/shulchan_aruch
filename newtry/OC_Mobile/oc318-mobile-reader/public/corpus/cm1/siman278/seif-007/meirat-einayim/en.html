It is treated as possessed property even if the pledge was taken at the time of the loan. The Tur also writes this. In the Perishah I explained that although a lender does not acquire a pledge taken at the time of the loan, Rabbi Yitzchak derives from "and it shall be righteousness for you" that a lender acquires a pledge taken after the time of the loan. Therefore, even a pledge taken at the time of the loan, since it is physically held by the lender, is considered collected and presently possessed in the father's hand for purposes of the firstborn's double share. The Ir Shushan explains that it is possessed because the lender acquired the pledge. That is incorrect, since a pledge taken at the time of the loan is not acquired in that sense.

Provided the time has not yet arrived, etc. If the repayment date has arrived, from that point the pledge is regarded as redeemable and as though the lender's special possession has ended.
It is also called possessed property with respect to the borrower, etc. Meaning, not only if the repayment date arrived during the lender's lifetime, but even if it did not arrive before the lender died, so that the lender's firstborn takes a double portion in the pledge, nevertheless when the pledge returns to the borrower's heirs it is treated as possessed property for them. Rama writes a similar rule at the end of this siman: land encumbered to others is still called possessed property. The Tur also cites the Raavad's different view, distinguishing the body of the pledged property from its value. I do not know why the Mechaber and Rama omitted that view.
