Expert. The Shach writes that Responsa Mahari ibn Lev, vol. 3, siman 30, rules that since the authorities dispute the liability of an expert appraiser, even where he was paid we do not extract money because of the doubt. Maharshal, chapter HaGozel Etzim, siman 24, rules like the Rashba that a fully qualified expert, such as Dankhu and Issur, is exempt even when paid. His principal proof is that otherwise Rav Papa should have answered the Gemara in that manner. In my humble opinion this is insufficient to decide the matter, for Rav Papa may indeed have answered in that manner; see there.

Primary. The Shach writes that Maharshal rules like the Rosh and those who follow him, while in his view the primary ruling is like the Rif and those who follow him, as the Rama writes; see there for his proof. He further cites Shiltei Giborim that the same applies where one asks another whether a certain person is financially sound and trustworthy enough to lend to, and the other says that he is, but it later turns out otherwise. According to Rabbeinu Ephraim, the adviser is exempt unless the lender told him, "See, I am relying upon you." See also Shiltei Giborim, chapter Get Pashut, 244b. Maharshal rules likewise. Therefore, it appears to me that where a merchant sold goods on credit in the ordinary course of trade, and Reuven also seeks credit, and the merchant asks another merchant whether Reuven is worthy of credit and wealthy, and he answers that Reuven is wealthy but Reuven later proves poor, the adviser is exempt for this reason. Nevertheless, where he was told, "See, I am relying upon you," he is liable in all these cases. It appears to me that the same applies where the circumstances clearly show that the other party relied upon him. See above, siman 129, seif 2 in the gloss, and what is written there. The Shach discusses this law further at length.
