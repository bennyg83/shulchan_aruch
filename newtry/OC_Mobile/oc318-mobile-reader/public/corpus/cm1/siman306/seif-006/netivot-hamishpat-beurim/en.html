The first view appears primary. If appraisers acting for a creditor assessed property and the creditor then had to accept it according to their appraisal, this is unlike one who shows a coin to a moneychanger. The appraisers act with the authority of beit din. If they erred in a matter comparable to an explicit Mishnah, they are exempt from payment, as Shach explains in siman 25. Shach asks why an expert artisan should be more exempt than an ordinary person when the owner did not explicitly say, "See, I rely on you." The answer is straightforward. With an ordinary person, failure to say that phrase shows the owner did not necessarily rely on him, because why should he? But with a recognized expert, reliance is presumed. When one consults an expert, on whom else would he be relying?
