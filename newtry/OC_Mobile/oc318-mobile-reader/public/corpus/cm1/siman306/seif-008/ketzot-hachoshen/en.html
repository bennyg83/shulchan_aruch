One who plants trees. Rosh was asked about Reuven, who gave spun material to Shimon to weave. Reuven claimed that Shimon had exchanged good material for inferior material. Shimon admitted that an exchange occurred but said it happened without his knowledge. Reuven did not know how much had been exchanged, and Shimon likewise said he did not know. Rosh ruled that Shimon is obligated to swear but cannot swear, and therefore must pay according to an appraisal of how much the fabric was diminished. He compared it to the case, "Fifty I know and fifty I do not know." I do not understand the comparison. The rule that one who is obligated to swear and cannot swear must pay in that partial-admission case applies where the claimant makes a definite claim. Here Reuven himself says he does not know how much was exchanged. A doubtful claimant ordinarily does not create the Torah oath of partial admission. The ruling therefore requires study.
