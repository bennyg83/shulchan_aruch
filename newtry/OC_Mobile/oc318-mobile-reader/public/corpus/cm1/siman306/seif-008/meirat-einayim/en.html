Some say that the same applies to an individual. This dispute between the authorities applies likewise to the other professionals listed by the Mechaber, such as a butcher, a bloodletter, and a scribe, as is stated in the Tur. See there.
Likewise, a town butcher who rendered an animal a neveilah, etc., is removed from his position. In the Derishah I wrote that the Maggid Mishneh says that Ra'avad asked: either he works for payment, in which case what irretrievable loss is there, since he must pay for the damage, or he works without payment, in which case what loss is there in removing him? He answers that even when he works without payment we penalize him so that henceforth he may not work either without payment or for payment. Alternatively, the case is one where he works for payment and there remains a substantial irretrievable loss, because although he pays the value of the animal, he does not pay for the owner's embarrassment or the embarrassment caused before his guests. End of his words. That answer cannot be applied to a planter. Perhaps in the case of a planter the reason is that he caused a loss to the body of the tree that he planted. In addition, had he planted it properly it would already have grown fruit, and there is no payment for that prospective enhancement because we do not know how much it would have been. The Gemara therefore calls it an irretrievable loss both with respect to a planter and with respect to a butcher and the other professionals listed.

Perhaps his intent follows the wording of the verse in Parashat Mishpatim, "If he survives for a day or two," which the Sages expound as one day that is like two, meaning a full twenty-four-hour period.
