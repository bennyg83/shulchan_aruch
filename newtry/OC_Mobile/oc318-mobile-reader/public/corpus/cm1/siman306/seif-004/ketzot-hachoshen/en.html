Shiltei Gibborim asks whether the blessing recited over slaughter counts as compensation, since a mitzvah or blessing has recognized value, sometimes assessed at ten gold pieces. It does not make the slaughterer a paid custodian or paid professional for these purposes. The clearest proof is the finder of a lost object. Rabbah and Rav Yosef dispute whether the finder is an unpaid or paid custodian because of the incidental benefit of being exempt from giving charity while occupied with the mitzvah. If the mitzvah itself, whose value can be assessed at ten gold pieces, counted as payment, everyone would have to treat the finder as paid. The explanation is that mitzvot were not given for material benefit. Their spiritual value does not constitute the type of compensation that creates a paid custodial relationship. Therefore the blessing over slaughter does not make a gratuitous slaughterer a paid professional.
