I rely upon you. If a money changer appraises an item at a certain value and the owner then brings it to him and purchases or sells it on that basis, although the original statement may not yet have amounted to, 'I rely upon you,' because the owner could still have asked a different price, once the transaction is brought to him and no other expert is present the owner is relying on him. If the expert had admitted the error then, the owner could have recourse against the first seller or other party, and therefore the money changer may be liable even for a substantial error. If the item was merely shown to him and then sold to another based on his silence, liability is more doubtful. Similarly, if one says, 'I know which craftsmen here are reliable; give me money and I will hire one for you,' and then the craftsman absconds and cannot be collected from, that may be equivalent to saying, 'See, I rely upon you,' because the intermediary actually accepted the money. If he merely said, 'So-and-so is reliable,' there is no guaranty, since the other person may not truly have relied upon his statement. If money was given to the intermediary merely so that he would negotiate the fee with a townsman, liability is less clear. If the craftsman was reliable at the time and only later a new circumstance caused him to flee, the intermediary is certainly exempt. If there had been an opportunity to collect from the craftsman and the principal delayed without informing the intermediary, the intermediary may thereby be released from any guaranty, particularly if the craftsman had willingly intended to pay. If the principal seeks to reduce or alter the agreed wages, the guaranty may nevertheless remain in force. This requires careful study.
