[Shach, letter 3] For it is a doubt in the law. Note: If a craftsman said he would do the work without charge and afterward said that he wants payment, it appears at first glance that if a craftsman does not acquire the enhancement of the utensil, he cannot collect, because he performed the work with the intention not to be paid and no wage obligation ever arose. But if a craftsman acquires the enhancement of the utensil, one may say that when he repaired it he automatically acquired that enhancement and may say, I do not wish to transfer it to you for free. This resembles one who says, I wish to give you as a gift that which you will acquire, who may retract. Nevertheless, it is possible that when one works without charge a craftsman does not acquire the enhancement of the utensil. The law requires study. See Gittin 20, s.v. Amar lei Rava.
