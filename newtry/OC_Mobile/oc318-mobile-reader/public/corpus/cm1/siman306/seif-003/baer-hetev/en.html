His wool. This applies specifically if the vat boiled over before the dye fell into it. After the dye fell in and increased its value, he gives the owner the value of his wool plus its improvement. So the Gemara, Bava Kamma 99a, explains the Mishnah according to the opinion that a craftsman does not acquire ownership through improvement of a vessel. Tosafot Yom Tov and Lechem Mishneh, chapter 10 of Hilkhot Sechirut, question why the authorities did not explain it this way, since they rule that a craftsman does not acquire the improvement. This is no difficulty, because the authorities merely preserved the language of the Mishnah. Shach.

He said. See the Shach, who cites Maharshal's statement that this necessarily concerns a case in which there is no improvement at all and the garment is not worth what it was previously. If there were improvement, it would make no sense to say, "we do not listen to him," since he could sell the garment and keep the excess for himself. The Shach disagrees with him at length.
