Although some disagree. This refers to Terumat HaDeshen, siman 186. In my view his ruling is correct and no authority truly disagrees. Whenever accepted practice prohibits the slaughter, even only as a stringency, if that rule is explicitly recorded in the slaughter manuals studied by local slaughterers, the slaughterer must pay. He knew that this defect would invalidate the slaughter and should have guarded against it. The Tur's ruling that money is not extracted where we are stringent merely from doubt is different, because there the slaughterer could not reasonably have anticipated the concern. Hagahot Ashri's exemption in a case of doubtful neveilah likewise concerns doubts not stated in the manuals used by slaughterers. The ruling that one who paused while only a minority of the simanim remained is exempt may also concern a period in which that invalidation was not universally known. The Mechaber in Yoreh Deah 23 indeed does not treat every minimal pause in every portion of the simanim as invalid. Under our practice, however, any pause at any point is treated as invalid and this is elementary knowledge for every trained slaughterer. He is therefore liable because he should have anticipated the result. It is strained to say that the Rama rejected Terumat HaDeshen without reason. The Sema's claim that the Mechaber and Tur disagree is not supported. Maharshal likewise rules that where accepted slaughter practice prohibits the defect, the slaughterer must have guarded against it.
