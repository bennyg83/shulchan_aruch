Regarding monetary law we do not extract money because of doubt. One might ask from Rama in siman 232:2, where he writes that even an animal we prohibit as tereifah only because of doubt nevertheless voids the sale, and the seller cannot say, "Bring proof that it was actually tereifah." The cases differ. There, returning the buyer's money and returning the animal to the seller is not considered a major extraction of money. Each side essentially returns what it received. Here, by contrast, the butcher would have to pay the entire value of the animal from his own pocket. We therefore do not extract money because of doubt.
Although our practice is to prohibit it, etc. This appears correct. The Mechaber too evidently holds this, because he writes that regarding monetary law we do not extract because of doubt, implying that in matters of prohibition we are stringent because of that same doubt. The Tur writes explicitly that we are stringent in prohibition and are concerned that the knife may have been damaged by the hide. Rama's phrase "so it appears to me" means that this is his halachic conclusion against the dissenting opinion he cites afterward. Rama uses this expression in similar fashion in many places.
