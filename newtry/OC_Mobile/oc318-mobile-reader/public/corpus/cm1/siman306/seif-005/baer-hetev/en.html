Because of doubt. In siman 232, seif 12, the Rama rules that even an animal that we forbid as possibly tereifah voids the sale, and the seller cannot say, "Bring proof that it was actually tereifah." The Sema answers that there the return of the purchase money is not viewed as such a strong extraction of money, because the seller returns the money he received for the animal and takes back his animal, whereas here the slaughterer must pay the value of the animal from his own pocket. This answer is difficult, for ultimately money is still being extracted from him. In truth, there was no difficulty to begin with. There, because people avoid the animal even on account of the doubt, there is no greater defect than that. The buyer can say, "Had I known it was possibly tereifah, I would not have bought it." The sale is therefore void and the money returns. This is explicit in the Ramban, Rashba, Ran in the chapter Elu Tereifot regarding a needle found in the thickness of the reticulum, and Rivash, siman 499, cited briefly by Beit Yosef in Yoreh Deah, siman 119. That case is unlike ours, and the Sema overlooked these poskim. Shach.
Who disagrees. The Shach writes that the ruling of the dissenting authority, Terumat HaDeshen, is correct and no one truly disagrees with it. Wherever our practice is to prohibit something, even if only as a stringency, once the prohibition is stated explicitly in the laws of shechitah used by slaughterers for their instruction, slaughterers in those communities must pay if they failed to guard against such a circumstance. See there, where the Shach brings proof. He concludes that Maharshal likewise ruled concerning a pause during the slaughter of a minority of the simanim: since our practice is to prohibit it, the slaughterer is liable. The same applies to anything stated in our accepted shechitah manuals; he was obligated to be careful regarding it.
