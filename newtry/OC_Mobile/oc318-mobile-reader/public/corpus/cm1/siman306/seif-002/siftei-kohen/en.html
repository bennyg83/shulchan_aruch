Because a craftsman does not acquire the enhancement of the article. In my view this is a matter of unresolved law. The Behag rules like Rav Asi that a craftsman acquires the enhancement he creates and, if the article is ruined, pays only the value of the raw materials. This is also the view of Ri and Rabbeinu Tam, as cited by the Rosh in Kiddushin and by the Semag. Terumat HaDeshen accordingly rules that a possessor may say, “I rely upon Rabbeinu Tam and those who agree with him.” It appears that the Rosh himself remained uncertain. In every place he records both the Rif's view and Ri's view without deciding between them. Maharshal's claim that the Rosh ruled without qualification in Bava Metzia that a craftsman does not acquire the enhancement is not compelling, because there the Rosh merely copied the Rif and relied upon his fuller discussion in Kiddushin. Rabbeinu Yerucham likewise records both views without decision in one work, though elsewhere he writes simply that the craftsman pays the value of the finished chest rather than only the wood. The Tur and his abridgment may imply that the Rosh rejected acquisition of the enhancement, but this is not conclusive. The Maadanei Melech and Shiltei HaGibborim also indicate that the matter remains doubtful. Maharshal's proof from the majority opinion of the Sages is not decisive. According to Rava's explanation that the dispute concerns betrothal with a loan and a perutah, the law may still follow Rabbi Meir. The Rosh in Kiddushin expressly indicates that the question cannot be decided from that dispute. His words in Bava Kamma may contain an omission, or they may be understood as follows. Rava did not explain the dispute as whether wages accrue continuously, because under that explanation the law would not follow Rabbi Meir, whereas Rava sought an explanation under which the law does follow him. This preserves Rav Asi's explicit ruling. The plain course of the discussion in Bava Kamma, Rashi's explanation, and the rulings regarding immersion of an article given to a non-Jewish craftsman also support the view that the craftsman acquires the enhancement. Accordingly, in monetary law the matter is at least doubtful, and the craftsman need pay only the value of the raw materials, because the burden of proof rests upon the claimant. The Tur's ruling that one who hires a contractor violates the prohibition against delaying wages is nevertheless correct. That ruling concerns a contract priced by units of work and does not depend upon whether the craftsman acquires the enhancement. The Sema's explanation to the contrary is not necessary. Shiltei HaGibborim further writes that if the completed article is stolen or lost after the craftsman enhanced it, according to the Rambam and those who agree with him he pays the value of the article together with its enhancement, whereas according to Ri he pays only the value of the original materials. This dispute applies to all crafts. It concerns a case in which the craftsman has not yet received his wages. If he already received them, all agree that when he ruins the article he must return the value of the finished article. The printed words “the wages” at the end of that passage are a scribal error and should read “the chest.”
