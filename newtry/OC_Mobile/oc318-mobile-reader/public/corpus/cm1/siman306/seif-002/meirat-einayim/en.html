How so? He gave a craftsman a cabinet, etc. Meaning, it is not only where the cabinet or chest had already been fashioned and he gave it to the craftsman for repair, and the craftsman broke it, that the craftsman must pay the owner. Even if he gave him only raw wood, the craftsman fashioned it into a utensil and afterward it broke in the craftsman's possession, he must nevertheless pay the owner. As soon as the craftsman made the utensil, the owner acquired it, and the craftsman has merely an ordinary monetary claim for his wage. We do not say that the craftsman acquires the enhancement of the utensil, meaning the increase in value whereby the raw wood became a cabinet or chest.
For an artisan does not acquire the enhancement of the utensil. The Tur likewise writes this here and below in siman 339. See, however, Even HaEzer siman 28, where the Tur and Shulchan Aruch write in the name of Rabbeinu Yitzchak and the Rosh that we rule that an artisan does acquire the enhancement of the utensil. Earlier there they write that Ramah holds that we rule that an artisan does not acquire the enhancement. In Yoreh Deah at the end of siman 120 the Tur also records a dispute concerning this. The Shiltei Gibborim and Maharshal already raised this contradiction. I addressed it in the Derishah to Even HaEzer. According to the Constantinople printing there, the matter is properly resolved. Examine it carefully.
