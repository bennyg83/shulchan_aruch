And it became a neveilah while he was paid, they are liable. A paid shochet who causes an animal to become nonkosher through improper slaughter is ordinarily liable. If the failure occurred because the bird suddenly moved, however, it appears he is exempt because this is a complete accident. When the bird moves, the shochet must immediately remove the knife, and sometimes that occurs before the required amount of shechitah was completed, especially under our stringent standards regarding even a minimal pause. A paid shochet who failed visibly to cut the majority of both simanim is liable even if he is an expert. If the animal would otherwise be found kosher on examination, he should pay according to the value of a kosher slaughtered animal, because the defect arose only after the animal had already entered the slaughter process and could no longer live. By contrast, if the defect came from failure to check the knife properly before slaughter, and the knife was lost afterward, perhaps he pays only the value of the animal alive as subject to uncertainty whether it would have been kosher. Yet there is reason to say that once later examination shows the animal itself was kosher, the loss he caused should be valued as that of a kosher animal. One who takes another's animal and slaughters it, and it turns out to have been a tereifah, may nevertheless owe the loss between the animal's live value as a doubtful kosher animal and its value as a definite tereifah. If the owner sold the animal on condition that it be slaughtered immediately, with the buyer to pay the kosher price if kosher and the tereifah price if not, and a third party improperly slaughtered it, then if examination shows it was otherwise kosher there is strong reason to require payment according to the kosher price. The wrongdoer destroyed an existing contingent receivable comparable to one who burns a promissory note. Still, if the act occurred at the very beginning of slaughter and the owner could theoretically have chosen not to slaughter at all, the matter is less clear. By reason, it may remain valued as a doubtful live animal rather than as a completed sale.
