Even an unpaid bailee who transferred it to a paid bailee. Meaning, although a paid bailee must guard it more carefully than an unpaid bailee with respect to theft and loss, as explained in siman 291.
The first bailee is liable. Nevertheless, if the injured party wishes, he may litigate against the second bailee. The point is that he may also proceed against the first, because it was negligent for the first bailee to transfer it to the second, since the owner can say, "You are trustworthy to me under oath, but the other person is not." This is unlike an owner who gives his ox to a bailee, where the bailee is liable, as stated in the preceding seif, because there the owner placed it with the bailee with authorization. The same applies if the first bailee gives it to his son, as the Mechaber writes later, for that too is authorized, since anyone who deposits with another does so with the understanding that the latter's wife and children may handle it. This is also explained in siman 291.
