And some say even for the animal itself. See Responsa Chavot Yair, siman 204, concerning one who untied another's cow that had been fastened to iron window bars in the public domain, and the cow was lost. He inclines to hold the person liable in human court. Even authorities who exempt one who merely breaches a fence and the animal then escapes do so because that act is only indirect causation. Where the person performs a direct act upon the animal itself, liability is stronger. Chavot Yair concludes that even if there remains enough doubt that beit din should not extract money initially, if the owner has already seized payment we certainly do not remove it from him. This distinguishes the case from one where liability exists only toward Heaven. See there.
