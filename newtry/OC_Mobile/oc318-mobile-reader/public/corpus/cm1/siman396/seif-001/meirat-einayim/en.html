They are forewarned from the outset. This was explained above in siman 389 in the Tur and Mechaber.
Minimal guarding is sufficient for them, etc., through "if he guarded with minimal guarding, he is exempt." In the Perishah I explained that according to the Rambam the owner is completely exempt, even from half damages. Tosafot and the Rosh hold that he is exempt only from full damages as for a forewarned ox, but he still owes half damages. An animal that has become forewarned cannot have a stronger exemption from half damages than it had while innocuous. The Gemara explains why minimal guarding suffices for a forewarned animal but not for an innocuous one. Ordinary oxen are presumed to receive at least minimal guarding, because virtually everyone guards his ox to that extent. Yet the Torah still imposed liability upon an innocuous ox, showing that it requires superior guarding. Then regarding a forewarned ox Scripture says, "and he did not guard it," indicating that some guarding suffices. This is a restriction following a restriction and therefore reduces the guarding requirement. Concerning tooth and foot, which are forewarned from the outset, Scripture says "and it shall consume" and "and he shall send," implying liability when the owner acts as though he actively consumed or sent the animal by failing to guard at all. If he provided any meaningful guarding, he is exempt.
