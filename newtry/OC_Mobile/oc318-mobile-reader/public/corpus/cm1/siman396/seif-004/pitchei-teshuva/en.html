And some say even for the animal itself. See Responsa Chavot Yair, siman 204, concerning one who untied another person's cow that had been fastened to the iron bars of a window in the public domain, after which the cow was lost. He discusses the matter at some length and inclines to the view that the person is liable in earthly law. Even those who exempt one who breaches a fence before an animal that is subsequently lost do so because his act is merely indirect causation. Here, however, he performed a direct act upon the animal's restraint. Chavot Yair concludes that even if one could still question whether money may be extracted on this basis, certainly if the owner has already seized property from the damager it is not removed from him, unlike a case where the damager is liable only in the heavenly court.
