One who breaches, etc. Rambam interprets the liability as referring to the resulting damage, because the Gemara cites it regarding the Mishna. Although the Mishna exempts unless he actually led the animal out, here he intended that it cause damage. Rambam treats this as garmi, imposing liability only where there was intent to cause damage. See Be'er HaGolah and simanim 25, 386, and 388.

And some say, etc. This is Rashi and Tosafot's view. Ramah, however, holds him liable for the animal itself. Tosafot in Sanhedrin explains that this reading makes the phrase 'before the animal' understandable even according to the Gemara's initial assumption and resolves their objection.
