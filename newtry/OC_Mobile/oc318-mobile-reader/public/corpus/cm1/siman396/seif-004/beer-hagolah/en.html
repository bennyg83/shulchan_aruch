The wording of Rambam, chapter 4 of Hilkhot Nizkei Mamon, law 2, from the baraita and sugya there at the end of 25b.
The Maggid Mishneh explains that Rambam means the person is exempt from the animal's loss but liable for the damage the animal subsequently caused. He was challenged from the preceding Mishnah, which says specifically that if robbers took it out they are liable. Rambam answered by distinguishing between robbers who intend to steal the animal rather than merely breach the fence, and this person who intends to breach the fence but not steal. He gives support for this distinction.
In the name of other commentators, and the Maggid Mishneh writes that later authorities agree that he is liable for the animal if it is lost, and that Raavad holds so. Tur likewise writes this in the name of the Ramah. Beit Yosef explains that this follows the view that the Gemara's challenge, 'let him also become liable for the animal,' means that once they breached the fence in front of it, it is as though they destroyed the animal directly.
