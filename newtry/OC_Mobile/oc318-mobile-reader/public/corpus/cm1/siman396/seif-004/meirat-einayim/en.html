Even for the animal itself he is not liable. The reason is that it is only indirect causation of damage, since he did not perform an act upon the body of the animal. See above, siman 157, Tur seif 6 and the Mechaber there. It appears that he is exempt for the animal only under human law, but is certainly liable under heavenly law, all the more so than in the case of an unstable wall.
And he is liable under heavenly law. See Perishah. Tosafos write that he is liable only for the animal that caused damage, but not for the wall, because it was already unstable. Rosh writes that it is possible he is liable even for the wall, since it could have remained standing another day or two until workers were found to repair it. This also appears to be the view of the Tur and Mechaber, who state without qualification that he is liable. According to Rambam, he is liable even under human law for an unstable wall. See Derishah.
