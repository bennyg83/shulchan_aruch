If they guarded it properly, etc., through "and the owners are liable." This wording appears in our printed Rambam in chapter 7 of Hilchot Nizkei Mamon, and the Maggid Mishneh explains it as concerning horn damage. The Tur objects: If they guarded it properly, why should the owners be liable? The Raavad raises the same objection. The Beit Yosef writes that the Maggid Mishneh's explanation, that it concerns horn, resolves the difficulty, but that is not correct because even for horn the owner is not liable after superior guarding, as the Tur and Rambam themselves state at the beginning of this siman. The Kesef Mishneh records that the sages of Lunel asked the Rambam this question, and the Rambam himself responded that the text contains a scribal error and should read: If they guarded it with superior guarding and it escaped and caused damage, the custodians are exempt. If they guarded it with minimal guarding, an unpaid custodian is exempt and the owners are liable, while a paid custodian, renter, or borrower is liable. The error is actually visible in our printed Rambam, which reads: "If they guarded it with superior guarding, the custodians are exempt and the owners are liable, even if it killed a person. If they guarded it with minimal guarding, an unpaid custodian is exempt, and if a paid custodian, etc." The phrase "and the owners are liable" plainly interrupts the sentence and belongs in the latter clause. I further explained in the Derishah that this was likely also the Maggid Mishneh's actual text, and that a printer altered his words to fit the corrupt Rambam edition. The Mechaber's ruling here is therefore surprising, because he adopted the Maggid Mishneh's printed wording even though it contradicts the opening of this very siman.
