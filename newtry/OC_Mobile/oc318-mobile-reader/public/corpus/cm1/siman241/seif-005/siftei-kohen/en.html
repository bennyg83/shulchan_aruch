One who waives to his fellow, etc. It appears to me that the language of waiver is appropriate here because if the recipient takes whatever he wishes without permission, the owner would otherwise have a monetary claim against him; the owner can therefore waive that claim. This differs from a case where a person's object is in his fellow's possession and the possessor refuses to take it without the owner's knowledge. In that case, how can the owner say, "it is waived to you," when there is no existing claim to waive? It is also possible that our case concerns one who permits the recipient to take property without the giver himself knowing which property or how much movable property he will take. All this is contrary to the Sema. I found this among my early writings.
