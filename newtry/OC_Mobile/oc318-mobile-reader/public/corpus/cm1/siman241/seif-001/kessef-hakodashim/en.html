Except for money that he owes. One person paid a fixed sum toward the purchase of liquor, and they fixed a date when the buyer would take delivery and pay the balance. The buyer failed to come on that date. The seller then wished to deduct for evaporation and shrinkage during the summer from the time of the agreed delivery until the buyer appeared. Later the seller said he would waive the shrinkage if the buyer collected the liquor by a newly fixed date, but afterward sought to retract that waiver. There is room to question whether this is a waiver that requires no kinyan. Waiver does not ordinarily apply to a tangible object still in existence. If the shrinkage legally falls on the buyer, requiring the seller to deliver a larger quantity than otherwise due resembles a new obligation upon the seller, not mere waiver. On the other hand, since the buyer is not compelled to take the liquor except through the seller's ability to deduct from the money already held the difference between the current value and the original agreed price, the seller's statement may be treated as waiver of that monetary claim. There is also room to say that if the entire vessel had already become the buyer's property, then the buyer would otherwise have to bear the shrinkage, and the seller's declaration not to charge it functions as waiver because the seller has not yet received the shrinkage amount. The law may therefore depend on the original agreement. If they contracted for this specific vessel, the statement can be understood as waiver. If they contracted only for a fixed quantity, it is more like a new obligation to supply additional liquor. If one obligated himself to deliver merchandise not presently in his possession, but readily available in the market, or sold goods that were in his possession and afterward took them for his own use without the buyer's knowledge, there are related questions about valuation and waiver. If the seller later orally settles the debt amount with the buyer, waiver ordinarily needs no kinyan, and perhaps retraction is possible only while they are still engaged in the same matter. Yet because payment extinguishes a debt immediately, this may be stronger than a monetary acquisition in movable property before physical delivery. A sale of merchandise kept in the seller's house through situmta is treated as though the goods were in the buyer's domain, and if the seller then waives money, that waiver may take effect without a kinyan even though the seller physically possesses the goods and there were no witnesses.
