He waived it for his fellow. Beit Meir, Even HaEzer siman 38 seif 35, questions the rule that a waiver requires no kinyan. Does this apply only where the creditor says the waiver directly to the debtor, as the wording “waived for his fellow” suggests, or can a person waive a claim before witnesses, or even privately to himself, and thereafter be unable to collect because the debtor has already acquired the benefit of the waiver? Perhaps a waiver differs from an ordinary gift. In a gift, the recipient's acquisition at least occurs in the donor's presence; with a waiver made outside the debtor's presence, perhaps the debtor acquires nothing until he hears of the benefit. Beit Meir initially finds some indication that presence is required, but later, in siman 106 seif 4, concludes that a waiver is effective even when made outside the debtor's presence.
