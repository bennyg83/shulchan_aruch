Sema, seif katan 18, cites Beit Yosef in siman 207 in the name of Rashba: one who gives a field on condition that the recipient give him five gold coins every year may have a personal condition for future years, but amounts that already accrued during the donor's lifetime are payable to his heirs. Netivot HaMishpat suggests this must refer not to a true suspensive condition on the validity of the gift, but to an independent personal obligation assumed by the recipient; otherwise it conflicts with Rama's rule that “to me” means not to the donor's heirs. The wording, however, sounds like a genuine condition. In fact, Darkei Moshe in siman 207 already raised this question. A gloss of Rabbi Yosef Engel answers that after several years passed without payment and the donor did not repossess the field, we may infer that he intended the unpaid annual amounts to become ordinary debts. The accrued obligations therefore pass to his heirs even if future performance of the personal condition would not.
