He waived. See Responsa Ra'anach, end of siman 77; Responsa Mabit, part I, siman 113, and part II, simanim 25, 96, and 314; Responsa Rashach, part III, siman 122; and above, siman 202.
Document. The Baal HaItur likewise writes this on folio 93b. See there carefully, so that one should not raise a difficulty from the law of one who waives a debt, above in siman 67. He cites the Yerushalmi, where this matter is disputed, and rules leniently. That Yerushalmi is at the end of the first chapter of Gittin: regarding one who waived a debt evidenced by a document, Rabbi Chananya and Rabbi Mana dispute it, one maintaining that he has not waived it until he delivers the document to the debtor. Accordingly, this is a case of uncertainty in the law. See Responsa Rashdam, simanim 235 and 278, and Responsa Ra'anach, siman 77. Shach.
Disqualified. The same law applies even where a kinyan is required, provided that the witnesses do not contradict one another. This is also stated in the responsum of the Rosh cited by the Darkei Moshe and by the Rama in his gloss at the beginning of siman 195. The text states that a kinyan is not required only to teach a greater novelty. One might have thought that, since no kinyan was required at all yet he nevertheless attempted to effect one, he thereby revealed that he intended to transfer ownership only through a particularly strong kinyan, and that he therefore intended the kinyan to be valid only if witnessed by qualified witnesses. It therefore teaches us that this is not so. The same is stated in Maharik and is cited in Darkei Moshe. Sema.
Object. The Rama's distinction between money and an object requires study. He should instead have distinguished within money itself, between money held by the recipient as a debt, for which the language of waiver is effective, and money held by him as a deposit, for which the language of waiver is ineffective because the money remains extant in the recipient's possession. This is why the Mechaber was precise and wrote regarding a deposit, "or he gave it to him." It is possible that the Rama merely used the usual case, since money is not ordinarily deposited but is hidden in the ground, unlike an object. More plausibly, anything that remains extant is called an "object," even money, in the sense of the phrase, "a matter that his heart desires." Sema.
To retract. The same law applies to a compromise entered into by mistake. See above, siman 12, the end of siman 25, and siman 207. See also Yoreh Deah, siman 164, regarding one who mortgaged his field to another and said, "If I do not repay you within three years, it shall be yours," where all produce consumed after the three years must be repaid to him. Sema.
