See Ketzot HaChoshen, seif katan 3. The Ketzot questions Maharik, who proves that mechilah is ineffective for a specifically identified object from the rule that an eved Ivri cannot be freed merely by saying "go," because his body is acquired. The Ketzot asks: a gift certainly works for an identified object, so why would it not work for an eved Ivri? It appears that Maharik's intent is different. If the language of mechilah were effective, no kinyan would be necessary, since mechilah does not require a kinyan. It would operate as relinquishment of the master's right, and even though the servant has no independent hand with which to acquire himself, once the master's right is relinquished he would automatically leave the master's domain. A gift, however, requires an act of acquisition. What act of acquisition can the eved Ivri perform upon himself? Neither meshichah nor hagbahah applies. Only a written instrument works by the special Torah rule that his document and his hand come simultaneously. Oral language alone cannot effect any kinyan. Thus, once it is established that mechilah does not apply to a specifically identified object, the bare language of mechilah is legally ineffective, and Maharik's proof is well understood.
