Be'er Yitzchak, Even HaEzer siman 77, writes that he commonly rules that a creditor's possession of the debtor's handwritten note does not prevent an effective waiver. Kenesset HaGedolah, as cited by Ketzot HaChoshen above siman 12, explains that a waiver is ineffective where the creditor holds an ordinary debt instrument because the instrument is treated as though the debt were already collected. But Gittin 38 indicates that even Beit Shammai does not treat a deed without property liability as collected. A handwritten note does not collect from encumbered property, so it is not considered already collected; a waiver should therefore be effective according to all opinions.
