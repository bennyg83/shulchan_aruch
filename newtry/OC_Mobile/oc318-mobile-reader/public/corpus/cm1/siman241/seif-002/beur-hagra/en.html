If he waived, etc. Kiddushin 16a, “let him say to him,” etc.; Bava Metzia 112a asks whether he may retract, etc. Necessarily the case is without a kinyan. Kesubos 104 and the case of one who claimed wheat, etc., are also explained by some authorities on the basis of waiver.
Or he gave, etc. Bava Batra 85a, concerning property in the purchaser's domain, etc. This applies specifically to a transfer, whereas mere waiver is not relevant to an object itself, as stated in the Rama; Maharik.
Some say, etc. Since the body of the pledge is not acquired by the creditor, even though he acquires rights sufficient to become liable for theft and loss, and Kiddushin there infers from “let him say,” etc., that waiver is effective. All the more so this applies to a document.
Wherever waiver, etc. This is unlike Bava Batra 152a concerning a dying person who wrote a kinyan, etc. There, acquisition is legally required and the Sages relaxed it only so that his mind would not become distressed. Here, by contrast, an expression revealing that he waived the right is sufficient.
And the language of waiver, etc. As stated in Kiddushin there regarding a Hebrew servant. Although the servant is in possession of himself, as Tosafos in Gittin explain, waiver is effective, and all the more so with a Hebrew servant; the same applies here.
A person, etc. As stated at the beginning of chapter 4 of Nazir concerning one who said, etc., in the first chapter of Kiddushin regarding one who was speaking with the woman about marriage, and in many other places.
Every waiver, etc. Bava Batra 41a; see Tosafos in Bava Metzia 66b.
Even, etc. At the end of the first chapter of Gittin.
