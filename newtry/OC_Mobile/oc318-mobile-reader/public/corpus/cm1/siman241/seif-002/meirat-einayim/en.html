Or he gives him the deposit that had been deposited with him. Meaning that the deposit was already in the recipient's hands. The Mechaber carefully uses the language of "giving" for a deposit, because the language of forgiveness is not appropriate to an identifiable object presently in the recipient's possession. Forgiveness properly applies to a debt, where the money was given for expenditure and no specific object remains. Rama therefore writes immediately afterward that if one has an object in his fellow's hands and says, "It is forgiven to you," nothing occurs. It requires study why Rama distinguishes there between money and an object. The real distinction should be whether the money itself is in the recipient's hands as a loan debt, where forgiveness works, or is held merely as a deposit, where it does not. Perhaps Rama states the ordinary case, because money is not commonly deposited in kind but is usually concealed, unlike ordinary objects. More likely, any identifiable thing is called an "object" in this legal sense, even coins. See the beginning of siman 195.
He even had a bill or a quaint. P. and should be given to return to the recipient of the stranger or the other banker who has paid him his debt:
Wherever forgiveness requires no kinyan, or he transferred it before disqualified witnesses, etc. The same rule applies even where a kinyan is required, so long as the parties do not contradict one another. So states the Rosh in responsum, as cited by Darkei Moshe and Rama at the beginning of siman 195. The Mechaber states the greater novelty by saying that no kinyan was required at all. One might have thought that because the party nevertheless attempted to make a kinyan, he revealed that he did not wish the transfer to take effect through simple forgiveness but only through a particularly strong kinyan, perhaps specifically before valid witnesses. The ruling teaches otherwise. Maharik likewise writes this, as cited in Darkei Moshe.
Any forgiveness made in error may be retracted, etc. The same applies to a compromise made in error. See siman 12, the end of siman 25, and siman 207. See also Yoreh Deah siman 165 concerning one who mortgages his field and says that if he does not repay within three years the field will belong to the lender. Produce eaten after the three years must be repaid, because the continued waiver was based upon error.
