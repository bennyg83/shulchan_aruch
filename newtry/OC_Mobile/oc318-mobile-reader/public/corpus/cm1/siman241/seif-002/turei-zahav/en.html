(See there: but not land.) The Beit Yosef shortly afterward cites another responsum of the Rashba concerning one who executed a deed of waiver over land that he himself possessed. The waiver is effective if an act of acquisition was made from him, contrary to the questioner's thought that this was merely a removal of claims and arguments. Since an act of acquisition was made from him, it was made from the substance of the land itself. Accordingly, it appears that the same applies where one waives an object to another. Although we stated above that simple waiver is ineffective, an act of acquisition from him makes it effective. Why should an object differ from land? One cannot answer that in the land case the document contained wording effective by law, for then it would be obvious that nothing remains after the act of acquisition. Rather, as I have written, the act of acquisition repairs the language of waiver, just as it repairs the language “I have no claim or dealings concerning this field” at the beginning of chapter HaKotev.
