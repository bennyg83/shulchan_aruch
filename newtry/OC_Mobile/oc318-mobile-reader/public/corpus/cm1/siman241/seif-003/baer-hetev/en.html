That he may take. It appears to me that the language of waiver is appropriate here, because if the recipient takes whatever he wishes without permission, the owner would otherwise have a claim against him, and the owner can properly waive that claim. This differs from a case where one's object is in another's possession and the possessor does not wish to take it without the owner's knowledge. In such a case, what would it mean to say, "It is waived to you," and what claim does the owner have against him that he could waive? It is also possible that the present case concerns permission to take property where the giver himself does not know what the recipient will take, whether money or how many movable items. All this is contrary to the Sema. Shach. The Taz writes: "Whatever he took, he took." According to what the Sema wrote at the end of siman 73, that wherever a person has permission to expend an item the language of waiver is appropriate, this case too is readily understood, and there is no need for the Sema's explanation here.
