(Seif 4: others disagree.) They bring proof from siman 214, concerning one who says, “I sell you a house from among my houses,” which is at least a valid sale even though he did not identify the particular house. In my humble opinion, according to the Rambam, who is the first opinion here, the case in siman 214 is where he shows the buyer his property and says, “one of these houses,” which is equivalent to identifying the field. It appears that the law follows the dissenting opinion, for according to the Rambam, one who transfers four cubits of land together with movable property must expressly identify those four cubits, as the Maggid Mishneh writes. Yet the established custom is to transfer movable property by virtue of four cubits of land without identifying any particular land. Necessarily, then, we follow the dissenting opinion that no identification is required.
