One who waives to his fellow whatever he will take. Shach, seif katan 5, is difficult. According to his first answer, waiver language should work even where one robbed an object, because there is certainly a monetary claim. A deposit likewise involves a claim. His second answer, that the waiver concerns something not yet known, whether money or an object, is also difficult. If an object is later taken, why should defective waiver language now acquire it? Money actually present is also like an object, as Sema writes. The simple explanation appears to be analogous to a davar shelo ba la'olam. A prior kinyan concerning a future object is ineffective, but if the recipient later takes the object with the transferor's consent, acquisition can be effective. The present consent to take supplies a current gift, even if the earlier ineffective arrangement influenced that consent. Rosh in Bava Metzia 66 explains that such mistaken gift or waiver can nevertheless stand. The same applies where the earlier "waiver" was ineffective only because the wording was defective rather than because the object did not yet exist. When the transferor later knowingly allows the recipient to take it, that conduct itself supplies a presently effective gift. The distinction between a future-object defect and a wording defect is immaterial, because the real acquisition now rests on his present consent.
