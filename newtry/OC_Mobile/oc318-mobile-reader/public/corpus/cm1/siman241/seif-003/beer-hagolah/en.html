This applies even where there was an attempted transfer, as shown by the law of one who sells the fruits of a palm tree: if the buyer seized and ate them, what he ate remains his, Bava Metzia 56a. Even though the seller allowed him to eat only because he mistakenly thought his attempted transfer was effective and would have stopped him had he known it was ineffective, the buyer nevertheless keeps what he ate, because a mistaken waiver is still a waiver, as stated there on 67a.
