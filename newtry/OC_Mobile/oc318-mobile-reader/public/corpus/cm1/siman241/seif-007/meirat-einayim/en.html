Even though one who says, "on condition that you give me," etc. Meaning, ordinarily we interpret this language strictly, as explained in Even HaEzer at the end of siman 143, above in siman 209, seif 7, and below in siman 247, seif 3, as the Tur, Mechaber, and Rama write based on the Gemara. One might therefore have thought that here too, if the recipient dies, his heirs cannot fulfill the condition and receive the gift because the giver said specifically, "that you give me." The ruling teaches that this case is different. Parenthetical gloss: Beit Yosef in siman 207, in the material related to seif 18, cites Rashba: if one says, "This field is given to you on condition that you give me five gold coins every year," the recipient gives him five gold coins each year throughout the giver's life. Once the giver dies, the recipient need not pay his heirs for future years. But any amount that became due during the father's lifetime and remained unpaid at his death must be paid to his sons. Nimukei Yosef in chapter HaMokher Et HaBayit writes likewise. Darkei Moshe cites this there.
Unlike a condition in gittin. The reason is that there we ordinarily assume he intends to cause her distress and therefore wants the specific object or performance from her personally.
