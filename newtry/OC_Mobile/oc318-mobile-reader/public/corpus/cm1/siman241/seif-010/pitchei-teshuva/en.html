He is exempt from paying. Mishneh LaMelech, Hilchot Zechiyah chapter 3 law 10, raises a question regarding a gift on condition of return where the object is lost through unavoidable accident within the period and the recipient is exempt from paying. Must he nevertheless return the fruits or benefits he consumed while he held the property? One possibility is yes. Since the object was not returned, the condition was not fulfilled and the gift is retroactively void. The recipient then held the property only as an ordinary custodian. His exemption from paying for the accidental loss follows because he was neither a borrower nor a paid guardian, but he should return the fruits that belonged to the donor. Another possibility is that the condition “on condition that you return it to me” was implicitly limited to a case in which the object still existed. If it was lost by unavoidable accident, the gift was never cancelled, and the recipient keeps the fruits. Mishneh LaMelech considers the first approach more persuasive: unavoidable loss cancels the gift, the recipient is merely a custodian, and he must return the fruits. The fact that he is liable for negligence supports this understanding. Shaar Mishpat writes that Shulchan Aruch's ruling in Orach Chaim siman 658 also indicates this view. See Mishkenot Yaakov, siman 45, and Chatam Sofer, Choshen Mishpat siman 1.
