The recipient must bring proof. Shevut Yaakov, III, siman 171, discusses Reuven, who promised Shimon a gift on condition that Shimon approach the governmental authority and exert himself on Reuven's behalf. Shimon claims that he went and did everything in his power, though his efforts failed, and he demands the gift. Reuven says that he does not believe him and demands proof. Shevut Yaakov writes that at first glance the rule here is straightforward: where a gift depends on a condition, the recipient bears the burden of proving that he fulfilled it. Yet Rama in Even HaEzer siman 38 cites two opinions concerning an affirmative condition, one of which accepts the party's own statement where no one affirmatively contradicts him. Beit Hillel already notes the apparent conflict. One possible explanation is chronological: Choshen Mishpat was Rama's later work, and perhaps he retracted the latter opinion he recorded in Even HaEzer. Levush may reflect this by omitting Rama's gloss in Even HaEzer and relying on the rule in Choshen Mishpat. After further consideration, however, Shevut Yaakov distinguishes between conditions in kiddushin and divorce and ordinary monetary conditions, based on Ran in chapter HaOmer. In practical terms, the theoretical conflict may not affect the case before him, because even if there are two opinions, Shimon seeks to extract the gift from Reuven and therefore bears the burden. The only possible difference might be where the recipient had already seized property of the donor without witnesses. The matter requires study. See also Pitchei Teshuva to Even HaEzer there.
