Zayin) Rambam, chapter 6 of Hilkhot Nachalot, halachah 12, from the Gemara's conclusion that perhaps an Israelite apostate is different, etc.
Chet) The Maggid Mishneh writes that these words are correct. It is well known that property declared ownerless by beit din is ownerless, and Rambam testifies there that this was the custom in the western lands.
Tet) The same applies if the deceased has a father: the inheritance is given to the father, as the Rama writes in the gloss that an Israelite inherits his apostate relative. The Mordekhai likewise writes so, as cited by the Sema.
Meaning, although a gentile inherits his father by Torah law, these relatives do not inherit through the gentile line. The reason is explained in siman 277, seif 9.
