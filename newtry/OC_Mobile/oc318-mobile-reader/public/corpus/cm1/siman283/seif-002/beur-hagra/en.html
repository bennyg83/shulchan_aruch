A Jew, etc. See Beer HaGolah. Although one might say the intent is merely that perhaps an apostate transmits inheritance to his children, as stated below, "and if there is, etc.," nevertheless, just as he transmits inheritance, so too he inherits. As stated in Sanhedrin, "although he sinned, etc." Mordechai and Rosh.
And if they saw, etc. See Tosafos to Avodah Zarah 26b in the name of the Yerushalmi, and Rosh, chapter 5 of Bava Metzia, siman 52, that it is permitted to lend with interest to the Kutim of Caesarea. Although one could distinguish interest because there is no mitzvah to sustain him, the principal reason is the power of beit din to render property ownerless.
And if there is, etc. As above. Since they themselves did not commit the transgression, they are not penalized, as stated in many places: they penalized him, etc. At the end of Bava Kamma, "the wicked prepares, etc." In the chapter HaIshah Rabbah, "she, etc.; her offspring, etc.," only because the conditions of a ketubah have the same law as the ketubah.
Even if he gave, etc. Meaning, if he gave to others the inheritance he had inherited from his father, they do not acquire it, because he himself does not inherit his father, as stated in the responsum that beit din penalized apostates. Shulchan Aruch writes that his heirs inherit as though they were the heirs of his father, comparable to Bava Batra 159a, where a son who sold his father's estate during his father's lifetime comes by virtue of his grandfather. Likewise with a firstborn son there. The same applies here. His gift is ineffective because he acquires no rights in the property under the enactment of the later Sages. See rule 17, siman 10.
Others say, etc. So it is written in Kiddushin there, as in the first chapter of Kesubos regarding a fine: although she acquires it d'oraisa, we give it to her because she will go, etc.
And a Jew, etc. As above.
Even if he deposited, etc. Only regarding a convert who has no heirs does the depositary acquire the property, as stated in siman 275.
Even, etc. For they are not his children, as stated in chapter 2 of Yevamos.
But if, etc., because whoever is in possession, etc. At the end of Bava Kamma they dispute concerning the property of an informer, etc., and certainly an apostate. Since no halachic ruling was issued, we lack authority to remove it from one who acquired it during the apostate's lifetime. But so long as he did not take possession wrongfully, it remains a deposit with him and he did not acquire it during the apostate's life. Upon death, the heirs acquire immediately. See Kesubos 85a: "do you have witnesses that they demanded it of him, etc.?"
A Jew, etc., if, etc. For we treat him as dead, and a son does not inherit his mother from the grave, etc., unlike above, where he says he comes by virtue of his grandfather, as explained.
And a husband inherits, etc. As with a woman whose husband went overseas and she remarried. See Tosafos to Yevamos 89b, s.v. "keivan, etc.," and as stated there 91a, "one might have thought concerning her, etc." The reason is that the conditions of the ketubah have the same law as the ketubah, as stated at the beginning of chapter Af Al Pi. Since they come by virtue of her, they are no better than she is. This is why above it says, "if the apostate is an adult, etc.," unlike here, where his hand is as hers, as stated below, and we rule like Rava that his hand is stronger than hers. See Mordechai to Kiddushin there. This is the meaning of "the penalty does not apply," as above: they penalized her, etc. Since his hand is as hers, he acquires.
