They acquired whatever was in their possession, etc. My learned son-in-law, Rabbi Mansh, raised the following difficulty. Rashi ruled this way because he held that regarding the property of an informer the law was not decided in accordance with either opinion. His words clearly imply that if we ruled that an informer's property may not be actively destroyed, those holding the property would not acquire it here. We, however, do rule that an informer's property may not be actively destroyed, as the Rif, Rosh, Rambam, all the decisors, and the Tur write below at the end of siman 388, seif 13. Even according to the alternative view in the gloss there, that one may take the property for himself, those in possession would acquire it here. Nevertheless, the Rama should not have stated the law here without qualification, since some authorities dispute that alternative view. I have also written there that Maharshal disagrees with it and that his view is primary. Indeed, Rashi's own words here clearly indicate that he disagrees with that view.
