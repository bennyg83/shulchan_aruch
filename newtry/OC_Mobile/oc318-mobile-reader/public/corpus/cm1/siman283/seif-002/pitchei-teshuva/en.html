Chatam Sofer was further asked by Rabbi Akiva Eiger about a custodian holding a bill of exchange deposited by a Jew who later converted and died as a gentile, leaving Jewish heirs. The debtor was poor and the heirs did not know of the note. The question was whether the custodian was morally obligated to inform the heirs, given Rama's rule that with a tangible deposit the custodian acquires it against a convert only if he made unauthorized use of it while the convert was alive. Rabbi Akiva Eiger suggested that a loan is different because money lent is meant to be spent, so no separate act of misappropriation is needed, and the lien may have lapsed during the convert's lifetime. Chatam Sofer agrees at length that in a loan the lien can lapse automatically. He also notes that, in any event, it is difficult to extract money from the poor debtor against the opinion of Rabbeinu Baruch cited by Mordechai and Rama. He therefore concludes that the custodian would act improperly by producing the note and handing it to the heirs. To satisfy all moral concerns he could reimburse the heirs only the negligible paper value of the note. He notes, however, that Mahari Weil, cited by Darkei Moshe, may imply a distinction in a loan between whether the due date had arrived before the convert's death; that point requires study.
