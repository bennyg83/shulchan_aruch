They acquire what is in their hands. See Shach, seif katan 20, who asks: siman 388 cites an opinion that one may take for oneself. Even if other authorities disagree, Rama should not have written the present law unqualified. His question is surprising. Rashi rules plainly that property cannot be extracted from the one who already took it because the halachah was not decided like either side. All the more so Rama may rule here that we do not remove property from the possessor where one cited opinion even permits taking it initially. Rama evidently considered that view primary enough not to cite the opposing opinion in siman 388.
