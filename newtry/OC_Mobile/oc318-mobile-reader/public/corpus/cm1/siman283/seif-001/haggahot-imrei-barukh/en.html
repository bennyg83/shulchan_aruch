(Shach, seif katan 2) And it is explicit from his words. Note: This is indeed stated in the Mordechai. But Responsa Emunat Shmuel, siman 17, writes that even though we rule regarding a moser that one may not destroy his property, because of the principle "a wicked person prepares and a righteous person wears," that rationale does not apply to an apostate. See there. This requires study from Avodah Zarah 26b, Tosafot, passage beginning "I teach," and from Raavad's glosses to Hilchot Avodat Kochavim 2:5. His view there is to distinguish: if the apostate married a gentile woman and has no valid Jewish offspring, one may destroy his property; otherwise he is no worse in this respect than a moser. It appears from his words that even taking the property for oneself is prohibited. See also Mordechai, chapter Eizehu Neshech. This responsum of Rashi is cited in Responsa Maharam of Rothenburg, siman 480, with a different wording from the Mordechai: regarding the property of a moser one Amora permits its destruction and another forbids, but regarding a sectarian everyone agrees that his body is ownerless and all the more so his property.
