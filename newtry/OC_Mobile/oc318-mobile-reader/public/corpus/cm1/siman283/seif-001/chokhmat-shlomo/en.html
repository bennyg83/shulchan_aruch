If the non-Jews do not know of it, he is not obligated to pay their heirs, etc. Note: See the Sema, who writes that if the non-Jews know about it, this constitutes theft from a non-Jew and is prohibited. This is the opposite of the Gemara and the poskim. Avoiding repayment of a loan owed to a non-Jew is permitted in itself even during the lender's lifetime, and is prohibited only where it would cause a desecration of God's Name. The prohibited category of theft from a non-Jew applies to something taken into one's possession without the owner's consent, like the classic case of taking a spear. But where the property came into one's possession lawfully and with the owner's consent, retaining it through nonpayment is not categorized as theft; the prohibition is only because of desecration of God's Name. The Shulchan Aruch's formulation here is therefore correct, and the Sema's words are exceedingly difficult. See also Even HaEzer 28 concerning betrothal with a non-Jew's collateral, where it is likewise evident that a debt is treated as avoidance of repayment rather than theft. Understand this well.
