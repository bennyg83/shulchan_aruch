See Baer Heitev, who cites the question raised by his learned son in law, etc. See Chatam Sofer, Choshen Mishpat, siman 154, who resolves this difficulty properly. Netivot HaMishpat also expressed surprise at the question. See there further concerning a bailee who held, as a deposit, an exchange note. The depositor later abandoned Judaism and died in that state, leaving Jewish heirs. The borrower was poor, and the heirs did not know of the note. The question was whether the bailee fulfills his obligation toward Heaven if he does not inform the heirs, so that they will not press the poor borrower. The doubt turned on Rama's ruling that with a deposit one requires an act of misappropriation during the apostate's lifetime, whereas otherwise it must be returned to the heirs. Perhaps, however, a loan, which is given to be spent, requires no act of misappropriation, because the lien lapses automatically during the apostate's lifetime and the borrower owes nothing to the heirs. Chatam Sofer agrees at length with his father in law, Rabbi Akiva Eiger, that with a loan given for expenditure the lien lapses automatically. He adds that the wording in Beit Yosef citing an Ashkenazic responsum contains a scribal error, and should read, "From where do we know that this is not considered misappropriation?" for its author actually considered it misappropriation. In addition, who can extract money from the borrower against the position of Rabbeinu Baruch in Mordechai, Bava Kamma, siman 194, the dissenting view cited by Rama in siman 388, seif 13? He concludes that the bailee would actually act improperly by producing the note and handing it to the heirs. If he wishes to satisfy every possible obligation, he should include for the heirs merely the value of the paper itself, suitable for ordinary use. See there. This requires comparison with Mahari Weil, siman 1, cited in Darchei Moshe, letter 2, which appears to distinguish regarding a loan between one whose due date has arrived and one whose due date has not yet arrived, and holds that specifically before maturity the holder acquires.
