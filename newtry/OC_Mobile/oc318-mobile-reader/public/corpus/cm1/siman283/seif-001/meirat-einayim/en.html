A non-Jew inherits his father by Torah law. This is derived from the verse, "For I have given Ar to the children of Lot as an inheritance." See the Perishah.
A convert does not inherit, etc. Because he is considered like a newly born child.
Lest he return to his rebellion. The Gemara's expression is "lest he return to his former way." Rambam and the Mechaber explain this as returning to rebellion. In the Perishah I noted that Rashi in the first chapter of Horayot explains it differently.
A condition is effective in this inheritance. Meaning, with this rabbinic inheritance he may direct the property to one who would not ordinarily inherit and displace the normal heir. Rambam uses this phrase at the beginning of chapter 6 of Hilchot Nachalot, referring back to what he had written there that in ordinary Torah inheritance a condition is ineffective. The Tur writes: where a non-Jew and a convert inherit their non-Jewish father, the convert may say to the non-Jew, "You take the idolatrous objects and wine used for idolatry, and I will take the money." Once the prohibited items have already entered the convert's possession, this is forbidden. This is a Mishnah in Demai, chapter 6. Since the convert does not inherit his father by Torah law, the property is not treated as already belonging to him before division, so he may arrange the division this way without being considered to derive benefit from the prohibited items. It is surprising that the Mechaber omitted this.
Even if he had a son whose birth was in holiness. The Mechaber already states this at the beginning of siman 275.
If the non-Jews do not know, etc. But if the non-Jewish heirs or other non-Jews know about the property, the person remains in violation. Mordechai writes this there. The reason appears to be that since a non-Jew inherits his father, taking it is included in theft, and theft from a non-Jew is prohibited. If they do not know, it has the character of a lost object. The Ir Shushan writes that where they know, it must be returned because of kiddush Hashem.
