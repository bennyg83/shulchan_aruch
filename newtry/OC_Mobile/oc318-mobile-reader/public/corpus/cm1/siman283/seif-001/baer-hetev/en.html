Effective. Meaning, he can cause one who is not legally fit to inherit him to inherit and can uproot the prescribed inheritance. The Tur writes: 'A gentile and a convert who inherited their father may have the convert say to the gentile, Take the idolatrous objects and wine used for idolatrous libations, and I will take the money. But if those items have already come into his possession, it is prohibited.' This is a Mishnah in Demai, chapter 7. The reason is that since the convert does not inherit his father by Torah law, the prohibited property is not regarded as already being in his domain. Therefore he may make this arrangement. It is surprising that the Mechaber omitted it. Thus writes the Sema. This law was already stated in Yoreh Deah, siman 146, seif 4. Shach.
They know. But if they know, he must repay them. The apparent reason is that since a gentile inherits his father, withholding it falls within the prohibition of robbery, and robbing a gentile is forbidden. The Ir Shushan writes that where they know, he must return it because of sanctification of Hashem's name. Sema.
