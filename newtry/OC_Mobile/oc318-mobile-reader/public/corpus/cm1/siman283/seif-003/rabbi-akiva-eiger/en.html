[Rama] In place of his wife who converted out of Judaism. Note: Beit Yosef writes that although the ordinary marital laws no longer apply to her, since he may not live with her, nevertheless, because if she returns to Judaism and it becomes clear that she was not willingly unfaithful he may take her back, the marital bond is not entirely severed. Alternatively, although the marital rights that benefited her ceased, his own rights did not cease because he did not sin. See there. Regarding the principal ruling where her father died after she converted, Mordechai rules in the name of Ravyah, and Terumas HaDeshen builds his ruling upon that source. This is difficult to me, because the manuscript responsum in the name of Ravyah cited by Beit Yosef, new matter 1, states specifically that she was Jewish when her father died, so that she had already acquired her inheritance. Beit Yosef writes that this appears to be the responsum to which Mordechai referred when he said, "Ravyah likewise ruled this in practice." This is puzzling, because the statements appear contradictory. According to Mordechai, even if she converted during her father’s lifetime her husband inherits her, whereas according to the manuscript responsum this applies only if she converted after her father’s death. This requires further study.
