If it is impossible to return it, etc. The Rosh writes in a responsum, cited by the Tur: since the captive woman became assimilated among the non-Jews, married, and had children, she certainly never acquired the money donated for her redemption, and her children do not acquire it after her, because the donors did not contribute on the understanding that she would consume it while living among non-Jews. They donated it only for her redemption. Therefore, if it were possible to return each person's contribution, that would be best, because the funds never left the donors' ownership until she was redeemed. Since this is impractical because the expense of returning the contributions would exceed the principal, they should be used for communal needs. It is preferable to use them for redemption of captives, keeping the principal available in case that captive woman can eventually be redeemed with it. End of the Rosh's words.
