"He shall send forth his livestock and it shall consume." This is the correct text. From "he shall send forth" we derive damage by regel, and from "it shall consume" we derive damage by shen.
The Tur and Mechaber also write the word "also" regarding an animal that eats, etc. This teaches us not to understand "it shall consume" as referring merely to taking from the injured party's field rather than to the eating itself. Rather, "it shall consume" means that it eats, etc. Nevertheless, the term does not apply exclusively to eating but to anything by which the animal destroys property while deriving benefit from it, as explained below.
And did not destroy them completely. This is learned from the fact that the Torah places shen and regel in one verse, while trampling by regel does not necessarily destroy an object entirely.
It rubbed itself against a wall, etc. But if it rubbed against another animal and injured it, one can say that it intended to injure and this is a derivative of keren, for which half damages are paid, unless even after the other animal moved away it continued and rubbed against a wall. In that case it is revealed retroactively that even when rubbing against the animal its intent was for its own benefit. See what I wrote in siman 1, seif 3.
