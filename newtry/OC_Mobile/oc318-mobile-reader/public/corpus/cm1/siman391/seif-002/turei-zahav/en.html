It rubbed itself against a wall, and so forth. The Sema writes that it rubbed itself against another animal, and so forth. I am greatly astonished at the source for this distinction, for there is no reason for it. Damage can certainly occur even to a wall, for otherwise what would there be to pay? One might then equally say that it intended to damage the wall. The simple explanation is that we prefer to attribute the act to an ordinary behavior rather than an unusual one. A wall is mentioned because rubbing against a wall is the usual and more common case.
