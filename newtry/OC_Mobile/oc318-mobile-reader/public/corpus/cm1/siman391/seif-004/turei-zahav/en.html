Because the cow's mouth. In the Tur's citation of the Ramah, the word “because” does not appear. Rather, it begins a new ruling: “The cow's mouth,” and so forth, with a practical difference even in such-and-such a place, according to the Beit Yosef's accurate manuscript. At first glance this is difficult. Why is this practical difference needed, since the rule already matters where the animal took food in the public domain and ate it in the private domain? According to another reading in the Tur, “and there is a further practical difference,” one may likewise ask why Tosafot and the Rosh needed to find the case of one who hands over food through a legally incompetent person, as Maharshal asks. According to what I wrote immediately above, the accurate reading is resolved. The Mechaber's wording here, “because the cow's mouth,” is not needed as the rationale for the first rule concerning taking from the public domain. That first rule rests on the reason I explained. Rather, the Mechaber additionally teaches that there is an independent principle that the cow's mouth is considered the injured party's domain, and he records it here for completeness. Hence the text gives a separate practical difference, because the first rule would be liable even without this principle.
