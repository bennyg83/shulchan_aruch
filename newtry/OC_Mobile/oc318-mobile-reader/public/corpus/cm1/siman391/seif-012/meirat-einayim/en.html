The matter remains doubtful. Although the Mechaber ruled earlier in seif 7 that if an animal took fruit in the public domain and then went and ate it in the injured party's domain, some obligate full damages, here the animal is standing in the injured party's domain but stretches its head to eat in another domain. This may be considered an unusual manner of eating, and therefore the law remains doubtful, unlike when it walks from one domain to the other.
The same applies if the animal is standing in the public domain, etc. This is surprising. According to everyone, if it takes food in the injured party's domain and eats it in the public domain, it pays only for the benefit it derived. All the more so here, where the act is unusual, there is stronger reason to exempt from full damages. Why then did Rama write that the matter is doubtful? Rashi also writes this "the same applies" regarding the Gemara's question about something rolling from one domain to another, whether it is regarded as being in the injured party's courtyard. Tosafos reject Rashi's explanation precisely because of this difficulty, and explain that the question concerns food rolling from one domain to another, as the Tur cites in the name of Rabbeinu Yitzchak. Perhaps Rashi distinguishes between food that rolls on the ground from one domain to another and food that the animal lifts in its mouth and carries from one domain to another. This requires study.
But if the food is a long object, etc. Until, "whether in a private domain," etc. This resolution is stated in the Gemara according to all opinions.
Some disagree and hold, etc. See Perishah. Since the Tur does not cite here his father Rosh as disagreeing, it appears that he understood that Rosh does not disagree in this case. Since in any event the injured party is in possession with respect to the amount of benefit, that possession is effective for the remainder as well.
