lamed) The language of the Rambam there, law 4. The Maggid Mishneh writes that whenever the place of eating would impose a stringency upon the damaging party, the Rambam treats the matter as an unresolved question. His reading is: Rabbi Zeira asked, what is the law where food rolls, for example where it stands in private property and rolls from the public domain into private property? The Raavad's gloss accords more closely with our textual versions.
mem) I also do not understand why the Rama inserted our version and Rashi's explanation into the Rambam's words. According to the Rambam, the damaging party is exempt in that case, as he writes in law 5 concerning a dog that entered a courtyard. The Maggid Mishneh explains that where an object is taken in one domain and eaten in another, we follow the domain in which it is eaten whenever that produces a leniency for the damaging party. He derives this from the sugya concerning the dog that took a cake on 23a. Tosafot likewise challenge Rashi from that sugya and explain differently, as the Tur cites. Perhaps the Ramah omitted the case because he holds it is uncommon for food to roll by itself from the injured party's domain into the public domain. I saw that the Sema also raised this point.
