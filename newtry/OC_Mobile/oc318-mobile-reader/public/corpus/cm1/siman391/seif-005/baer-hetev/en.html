To pay. The reason cannot be that Reuven is Levi's agent, because Levi did not instruct Reuven to act, and even if Reuven cannot pay, the rule remains that there is no agency for a transgression, as explained at the beginning of siman 182 and in siman 292, seif 4. Rather, here the person who handed the food to the cow did not himself damage the object. He merely placed it before the cow while it was still intact, and the cow committed the entire destructive act. The person who handed it over is therefore comparable to a robber: from the moment he lifted property not his own, he became one who wrongfully uses it and thus a robber. This is analogous to one who steals and feeds the stolen item to another, where the victim may collect from either party, as stated in siman 361, seif 5. If the person who handed it over has funds, however, the victim collects from him because he is directly liable as a damager, while the animal's owner did not act with his own hands, since without the handing over the animal would not have eaten it. Shach.
