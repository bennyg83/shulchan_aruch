In the injured party's domain. For it is written, "and it shall consume in another's field," meaning the field of another person, namely the injured party. See what I wrote at the end of siman 389.
The mouth of the cow is considered like the injured party's courtyard. Meaning, although the verse says "and it shall consume in another's field," and here the taking, which is the beginning of the consumption, did not occur in the injured party's field, while the destruction that occurs in the cow's mouth after the taking appears to take place in the damager's courtyard because the ox belongs to the damager, nevertheless the law treats the cow's mouth, meaning the tooth, the eating, and the destruction in its mouth, as though it occurred in the injured party's courtyard, since at the time of eating the animal is standing in the injured party's courtyard. The Tur writes this in the name of Ramah. In Derishah I wrote that some authorities disagree and require that at the time of taking the food as well the animal be standing in the injured party's courtyard. Then the owner is liable even if a deaf-mute, incompetent, or minor placed the food in its mouth, because the animal itself remained in the injured party's courtyard and the eating and destruction occurred there. Although this takes place in the animal's own body, which belongs to the damager, we rule that the cow's mouth is considered the injured party's courtyard when the animal is there from beginning to end. Note that the Tur writes the principle that the cow's mouth is like the injured party's courtyard only in the latter case, where the animal was in the injured party's domain but the food was in a high place and it could not take it until a deaf-mute, incompetent, or minor placed it in its mouth. The Mechaber, however, also uses this as the reason for a case where the animal took food from the public domain, walked into the injured party's courtyard, and ate it there. According to that, he should not have blended the cases by writing "a further practical difference," but should have said, "Another practical difference." I discussed this at length in Derishah.
