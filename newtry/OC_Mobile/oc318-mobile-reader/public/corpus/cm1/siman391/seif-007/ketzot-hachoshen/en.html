Because he has nothing with which to pay. Maadanei Yom Tov explains that although the competent person who placed the produce before the animal is himself liable, where nothing can be collected from him the other liable party must pay under Rabbi Natan's rule governing joint tortfeasors. The Shach objects that Tur elsewhere rejects precisely this extension of Rabbi Natan. In my view, if the animal could have taken the food by itself, the person who merely brought it closer is not a direct tortfeasor. If he actually lifted the produce, he is like a robber, and the injured party can collect from either him or the animal owner. But where the animal could not have reached the produce without his act, he is a direct tortfeasor, like one who places another's animal upon another's standing grain. Mordechai states this explicitly. Accordingly, in the present case, where Tosafot and Tur establish that the animal could not take the produce by itself, the person who put it into the animal's mouth is a true tortfeasor. If he has no assets, Maadanei Yom Tov's application of Rabbi Natan is understandable. Rosh's own wording does not expressly require that the animal have been unable to reach the produce. That suggests he may distinguish simply between one who lifts the produce, who can have the law of a robber, and one who merely facilitates the animal's eating. The exact reconciliation between Tosafot, Rosh, Tur, and the Shulchan Aruch remains difficult.
