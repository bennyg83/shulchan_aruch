More, etc. As stated in seif 8.
And if it took, etc. The Tur writes this in the name of the Ramah. Tosafos does not agree, for Tosafos there, s.v. "ordinarily," writes that both taking and eating are required. The Ramah understands the verse "and it consumed" as focusing upon the eating. This avoids forcing Tosafos' wording in s.v. "and let him be liable." Rather, the cake lay upon another's coal in another's domain. The Rashba likewise explains this in the name of the Raavad. Although according to that reading Tosafos' rule cannot be proven from the attempted resolution alone, it is still established by the Gemara's later formulation concerning tooth. See Tosafos cited above and what I wrote in seif 12.
Because of its mouth. The printed wording is erroneous and should read "its mouth"; see Beer HaGolah. This is the Gemara's conclusion, which resolves the question from that sugya after first seeking a proof elsewhere.
If the one who handed it, etc. Otherwise, even where the animal could have taken it itself, the person who handed it is independently liable, as explained below. See Hagahos Ashri there and Tosafos 53b, s.v. "an ox," and 6a, s.v. "to include."
Or where there is no, etc. This follows the first opinion in siman 410 seif 37. The Shach's words are very remote from the sugya.
