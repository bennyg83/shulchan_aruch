Because he has no means to pay, etc. The reason cannot be that Reuven became Levi’s agent, for Levi never instructed Reuven to act, and even if Reuven cannot pay, there is no agency for a transgression. Maadanei Yom Tov explains that where one of two joint tortfeasors cannot pay, the other is liable under Rabbi Natan’s rule. This is difficult because the Tur elsewhere rejects that principle where payment cannot be collected from the other wrongdoer. I therefore distinguish this case. The person who placed the food before the cow did not itself consume or destroy it; at that moment the food remained intact. The cow caused the entire physical damage, while the feeder became liable as a robber from the moment he lifted property not his own. The case is therefore like one who steals and feeds the stolen property to another, from either of whom the owner may collect. If the feeder has means, however, the owner collects from him because he is also liable as the active facilitator, whereas the cow’s owner did not act directly, since without the feeding the cow would not have eaten it.
