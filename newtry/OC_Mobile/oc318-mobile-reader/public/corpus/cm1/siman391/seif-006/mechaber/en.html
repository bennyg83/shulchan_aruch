If a wild animal tears apart another animal and eats it, or eats raw meat, this is its ordinary manner and the owner pays full damages. But if a dog kills a lamb, or a cat kills and eats large chickens, this is unusual and the owner pays only half-damages for the reduction in value caused by the killing. For the value of the carcass that it ate, however, the owner pays full damages in the injured party's domain, and in the public domain pays the amount of benefit received, because eating carrion is not unusual, even the carcasses of large animals.
