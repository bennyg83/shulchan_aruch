A lamb, etc. The Tur. The Gemara's qualification concerning large ones applies only to chickens, as in the Rif's reading, "large chickens," and likewise at the end of chapter 3 of Kesubos. The Rambam explicitly writes "small lambs." There is a scribal error in the Rosh, chapter 2, seif 3, which should be reversed; this is also how the Rosh's abridgment reads at the end of chapter 1.
Specifically, etc. The proof is from the end of chapter 1, "an animal is not muad," etc., without listing six tam categories or such a case, and similarly in chapter 2 concerning eating clothing. When 23a says, "this teaches," the point is that otherwise the event is an accident and there is complete exemption, unlike Rashi, s.v. "ordinarily." Yam Shel Shlomo disagrees, arguing that the poskim did not explain it this way. His proof, however, still requires explanation, since the Mishna could have listed an animal eating what it does not ordinarily eat, such as meat, and similar examples. See there for his answers.
