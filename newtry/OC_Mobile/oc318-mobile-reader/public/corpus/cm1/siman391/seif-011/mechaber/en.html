If an animal walks in the public domain, stretches out its neck, and eats from upon another animal, even if it stopped to do so, its owner pays only the amount of benefit, because animals ordinarily eat from one another's backs. If it jumped and ate from the other animal, the owner pays the full damage, because the top of the other animal is regarded as the injured party's domain. Rama: Some say this applies only where the animal could not reach the food without jumping. If it could have eaten without jumping, the place is treated as the public domain. The same applies where a person's basket hangs behind him and an animal eats fruit from it.
