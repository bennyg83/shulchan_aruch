The same applies if his basket was, etc. Here too there is a distinction whether the animal jumped and ate or ate without jumping. According to the Rambam's view, it appears that if produce is placed in the public domain in a manner that could be eaten without jumping, but the animal nevertheless jumps and eats it, the location is treated like the injured party's domain. The Tur cites the Ramah as a third view. He holds that if an animal eats from the back of another animal or from a person's basket, full damages are payable even without jumping, while in the public domain, if it jumped, the owner pays all the damage, and if it did not jump he pays only the benefit received. I discussed these views at length in the Derishah.
