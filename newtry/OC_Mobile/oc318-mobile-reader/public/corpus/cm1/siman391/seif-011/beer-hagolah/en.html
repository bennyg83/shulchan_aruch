yud) The language of the Rambam, chapter 3, law 10. The Kesef Mishneh cites Ilfa and Rabbi Oshaya's statements on 20a, as Rava explains them regarding jumping. The Nimukei Yosef explains that 'jumping' means that the animal raised its legs and stood upon the other animal, eating while upright. But if it merely stood slightly so as to stretch out its neck, it is exempt, because the other person's animal is not fully equivalent to the injured party's domain. The Tur explains that otherwise it is treated as the public domain.
kaf) The Tur and Rashba write that Rava's reference to jumping means that the animal jumped upon a person carrying a basket behind him, or onto a platform in the public domain. Since it is normal for the animal to eat barley or vetch from the basket, it is also normal for it to jump there. The reason for liability in these cases is that they are treated like the injured party's courtyard, even though located in the public domain, because those persons have permission to pass there with their loads.
