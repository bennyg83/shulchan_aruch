[Shulchan Aruch] And if it became overheated, he is liable. Note: See Nimukei Yosef, who holds that even an unpaid custodian is liable. See Tosafos at the end of their discussion and Maharsha there. If the owner was in his service at the time, it would appear at first glance that the custodian is nevertheless liable, because he deviated from the owner’s instructions. It is as though he stipulated that if he changes the agreed manner and an event occurs that can be attributed to that change, an event that might not have occurred otherwise, it shall be treated as though he took the item without permission and it was then lost through an accident. See Nimukei Yosef. Furthermore, even if liability for overheating or slipping rests only on negligence, there is reason to impose liability even where the owner was in his service, because direct negligent conduct may be treated like direct damage, similar to what Kesef Mishneh writes in chapter 1 of Hilchos Sechirus.
