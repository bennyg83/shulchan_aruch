If it slipped, he is exempt. In the following seif the Mechaber writes that if it became overheated he is liable and explains the reason. The Mechaber copied the Rambam's language from chapter 4 of Hilchot Sechirut. The Maggid Mishneh notes that the Rambam omits the first clause of the Mishnah in HaOmanin, which says that one who rented a donkey to travel in the mountains and took it through a valley, or the reverse, is liable. The Gemara explains that clause in several ways, such as where the animal died from a change in the air, exhaustion, or a snakebite, and then gives a fourth explanation according to Rabbi Meir. Since the Rambam omitted the first clause, the Maggid Mishneh infers that he did not accept those earlier explanations and held that the Mishnah's first clause follows Rabbi Meir, whose view is not the halachah. According to this, when the Rambam and Mechaber say that if he changed the route and the animal slipped in the valley or overheated in the mountain he is exempt, certainly they would also exempt if it died naturally. More than that, if it died from the air or a snakebite, they also hold he is exempt because the burden of proof rests upon the claimant. We say it might have died from that cause even without the change of route. The reason they state the narrower case of slipping in the valley is simply because they follow the Mishnah's wording, which says it in contrast to the case where the changed route directly caused slipping in the mountain. Tosafot writes similarly. Rama's gloss immediately below, mentioning exhaustion or death from changed air, follows the Tur and the consensus of other poskim besides the Rambam. He does not frame it explicitly as a dispute perhaps because, without the omitted Mishnah clause before us, one can explain that the Rambam also accepts the same practical law, or because Rama understood the Rambam's omission for another reason.
