One who rents a donkey. Mordechai records a case where Reuven rented a cow to Shimon for six peshitim per week with a stipulation that if it died Shimon would pay a zekuk. Shimon used it for six weeks and it died. He argued that if he pays the full cow value, he should not also pay rent because that would be interest. The teacher cited there distinguished the case: perhaps the parties fixed rental only on the understanding that the animal would be returned, not if its value had to be paid because of death. In my view even this does not necessarily eliminate all rent. Ritva explains that where the renter deviates from the agreed use and the animal is lost, the original rental rate can fall away, but he still owes payment for the benefit actually received before the accident. Rent is compensation for the period of use, while payment for the animal is compensation for the later loss. These are distinct. Therefore, even if the original fixed rental clause no longer controls after the animal's destruction, the renter should ordinarily pay the reasonable value of the use he actually enjoyed. This appears the strongest approach.
