One who rents a donkey. See Shach through his discussion of the phrase, "When I repay you I will not give you..." The rule of Mordechai applies specifically where the animal was appraised at a fixed amount at the beginning of the rental and the renter agreed to pay that fixed amount if the animal were lost, even if it later depreciated. In such a structure, if the animal dies and he pays the original fixed principal, the transaction is treated retroactively like a sale from the time of meshichah. Therefore he need not also pay rental. But in an ordinary rental, if the animal depreciates or weakens through normal work, that depreciation belongs to the owner, and if the animal later dies the renter pays according to its value at the time of death. There is then no reason he should not also pay the rental price for the use he received. The Mishnah concerning one who agreed to take an animal on a mountain and instead used it in a valley proves this. If the deviation creates liability, the original rental price may be canceled, but because he descended to use the animal as a renter he still pays at least the value of the benefit received, namely the lowest ordinary rental rate. Thus there is no dispute between Mordechai and Ritva. Mordechai speaks of a special appraisal that functions retroactively as a sale if the animal is lost. An ordinary renter pays both the value owed for the loss and the rental for the use, with normal work-related depreciation remaining the owner's burden.
