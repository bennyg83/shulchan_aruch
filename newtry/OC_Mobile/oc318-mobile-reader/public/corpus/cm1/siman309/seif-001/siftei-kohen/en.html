One who rents a donkey, etc. The Mordechai records a case in which Reuven rented a cow to Shimon for six peshitim per week and stipulated that if it died Shimon would owe a zekuk. After six weeks of work it died. Shimon argued that he would pay the cow's value but not the rent, because collecting both would be interest. The first ruling compared this to one who appraises a cow to another and held that both principal and rent are payable. His teacher answered that the cited law merely shows there is no prohibition of interest where the parties expressly stipulated both payments. Without such an express condition the renter may say, “I fixed the rent only on the assumption that the animal would be returned; if I pay its value, I do not owe rent.” The first ruling is taken from Raavan. It appears that Raavan too meant only to reject the interest concern and did not disagree with the teacher's contract interpretation. The practical ruling therefore follows the teacher: both principal and rent are collected only where the renter expressly undertook to pay rent even if the animal died and he paid its value. See Responsa Mabit, part 2, siman 304.
