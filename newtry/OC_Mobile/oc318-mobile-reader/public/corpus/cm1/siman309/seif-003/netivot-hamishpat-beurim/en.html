At the end of eight days. See Sema, seif katan 5, who writes that eight days is not exact; that was merely how the incident occurred. The same rule applies after a longer period if there is evidence that the animal died because of the unauthorized change. Rosh and Tur expressly require proof. Where there is no such proof, the renter is exempt. We do not say that because it is impossible to know whether the animal died from the negligence, one unable to swear must pay. That rule does not apply where the defendant had no reasonable way to know. For the same reason Rosh writes that after a longer period, the owner must show witnesses that the animal was progressively deteriorating. Once the animal is back in the owner's possession, the renter is not expected to know what happened to it, and therefore his lack of knowledge does not create payment liability.
If he was pressed to continue his journey and could not hire another donkey. Rosh's responsum and Darkei Moshe add that there was no trustworthy person with whom he could leave the disabled donkey. Sema says that if he could have left it safely and did not, he is liable. At first glance this is difficult. If he had urgent need to continue and no other donkey could be hired, why could he not place the load on this donkey? He may even sell the animal in order to hire another where necessary, as siman 310 teaches. Why should the existence of a trustworthy person with whom to leave the donkey matter to transporting his goods? Rosh's language is best read as creating two separate duties. Ordinarily, he must hire another donkey for the load, and he must leave the injured donkey in the village rather than drive it onward, even empty. If he urgently must proceed and there is no trustworthy person with whom he can leave the injured animal, he need not remain behind. He may take the injured donkey along empty. Separately, if there is another donkey available to hire, he must hire it for the load. This duty applies even where there is no trustworthy person with whom to leave the first donkey. Rosh's concluding phrase, that if there was neither a trustworthy person nor another donkey available he is exempt, should therefore be read disjunctively. If no other donkey can be hired, he may continue using the disabled donkey for the load even if someone trustworthy is available. If another donkey is available but no trustworthy person can take custody of the first, he hires the other for the load and brings the first along empty. If both another donkey and a trustworthy custodian are available, he must hire the replacement and leave the injured animal behind.
