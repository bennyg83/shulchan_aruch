And it died after eight days. The number eight is not legally significant. That was simply what occurred in the actual case. See the Tur.
The renter is liable. The responsum cited by the Tur says: Although many animals can travel out and back in one day, this owner stipulated that the donkey be returned only the next day because he knew his animal lacked the strength to make the round trip in one day. The renter who changed the arrangement was negligent. The owner was not obligated to accept the animal when returned in damaged condition, and from that point he had only the status of an unpaid custodian. Since the animal died eight days later, this is strong evidence that it died because of the excessive exertion. Nevertheless, because it died while in the owner's custody, the owner must swear like an unpaid custodian that he was not negligent in feeding or watering it and did not ride it for his own needs during those eight days, except perhaps to take it to water, which is not negligence. See there. If one rented an animal for eight days to travel far and returned after only two days without making the trip, see siman 335:1 in Rama.
And he placed a burden upon it. The responsum, also cited in Darkei Moshe, states that even if he placed no burden upon the animal, if he could have left it in a protected place and failed to do so, and the animal became more damaged by continuing along the road, he is liable. If he placed a burden upon it, he is liable even where there was no protected place available, because in any event he should not have loaded it.
