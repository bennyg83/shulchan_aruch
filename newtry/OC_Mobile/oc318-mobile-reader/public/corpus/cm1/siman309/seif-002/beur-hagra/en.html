He deviated, etc. See Rosh, who rules in accordance with all the explanations there, because he holds they do not disagree. Rabbi Yochanan speaks of an ordinary case where the climate is not different. See Tosafos there, s.v. "kegon, etc." The Rosh writes that the same applies in the reverse direction, from mountain to valley or valley to mountain, if the climate is different and harsh that day. This is the meaning of "and likewise if, etc.; and likewise every, etc." It is Rabbah's explanation there, for example, where it is known that snakes are more common in the place to which he took the animal than in the place to which he was supposed to take it. Mountain and valley are mentioned because they differ regarding snakes, as I explained regarding climate.
