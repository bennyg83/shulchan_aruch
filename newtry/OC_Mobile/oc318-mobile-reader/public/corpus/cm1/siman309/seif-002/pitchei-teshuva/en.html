Rabbi Akiva Eiger, in his novellae to Bava Metzia 80, asks that since both workers accepted the plowing task, they should be like guarantors for one another, as explained in Choshen Mishpat, siman 76. If so, the owner should be able to collect half from each on a certainty: either that worker was negligent himself or he is liable as guarantor for the other. He answers that this resembles the law of two persons with identical names in Bava Batra 48, codified above in siman 49, seif 10, where one bought land from the other and a promissory note was produced against one of them, and the creditor cannot collect from the purchaser by a double sided argument. So too here, although each worker is guarantor for the other, the principal debtor himself cannot presently be compelled because he may say that perhaps he was not negligent. Consequently the creditor cannot collect from the guarantor either. Even according to Tumim at the end of siman 49, who rules that if one of two identically named people also guaranteed the other, collection can be made from him either as debtor or guarantor, because if he says he did not borrow he thereby admits that the other did, our case is different. There, the guarantor admits that the other person is actually liable, and only the debtor's denial creates the need to collect from the guarantor. Here, the underlying facts are genuinely uncertain as to whether the negligence was by the one who led the animal or by the one who held the implement. Each is exempt by the law of monetary doubt. The guarantor likewise does not admit that the other is liable, but claims that the other too is legally exempt because of the doubt. Therefore no one can be charged by a double sided argument. This appears correct in practice. See also Ketzot HaChoshen below, siman 386, seif katan 1, and siman 388, seif katan 10.
