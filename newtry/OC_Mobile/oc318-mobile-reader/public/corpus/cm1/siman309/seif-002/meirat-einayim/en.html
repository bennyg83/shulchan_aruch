The air of the road is different. This applies specifically where it is known that the air was materially different. The Rosh writes so. Otherwise the renter would be exempt even according to Abaye, who elsewhere says that the air of the marsh killed the animal. That rule applies where the case began with negligence, such as one who should have kept the animal in a stable but negligently let it out to a marsh, creating initial exposure to theft or loss, and it later died from an accident that may have resulted from that negligence. Then one can say that the marsh air caused the death. Here the renter did not act negligently merely by going from mountain to valley or the reverse. The driver ordinarily adjusts carefully so that the animal does not slip on the mountain and does not overheat in the valley. Tosafot writes this. Nimukei Yosef in the Rambam's name gives another rationale for exemption. See the Derishah.
