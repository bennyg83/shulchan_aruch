To plow on a mountain and he plowed, etc. Rashi explains in chapter HaOmanin, Bava Metzia 80a, that all the plowing implements belong to the owner of the oxen and his youths accompany the animal. One holds the goad and directs the cow along the furrow, and one walks behind the plow and presses the stake into the ground. The iron attached to it, called the plowshare, is the implement referred to in the Mishnah. End of Rashi's words.
The renter is exempt. Because he deviated in a manner that improved the situation: mountain soil is more difficult to plow than valley soil, where furrows are readily formed.
With the artisans. These are the youths mentioned above who are skilled in the work of plowing. The following law explains which one of them must pay.
The renter's claim is against the artisans. They should have been careful in their work, like other artisans who plow on a mountain, so that the plow would not break, as explained afterward.
They are exempt from the renter. They can tell him: you caused the damage by deviating, for we were hired by the owner only to plow in a valley.
The owner must pay them their wages. Meaning, even if the owner cannot recover anything from the renter because the renter is poor or violent, he may not retain the youths' wages and say, "Why did you listen to him and plow on a mountain?" They may answer: even if we heard from the renter that he rented your cow to plow in a valley, since you did not stipulate with us not to follow his instruction to plow on a mountain, we assumed you intended us to follow the renter's directions and that you had no objection to his doing so.
The one holding the implement. Because he caused it to penetrate too deeply into the ground.
Ascending. Meaning, where the plow is going upward over stones and rocky ground.
Both are liable. Because in such terrain the plow can easily break, the person holding the goad must also be extremely careful in guiding the animal. When the animal deviates even slightly from the furrow, the plow can break. We therefore say that the break resulted from both of them. This is unlike the Ir Shushan, who writes that because it is uncertain which one caused the break, they both pay. Tosafos reject that explanation, for if that were the reason they would not both be liable, since we do not rule like Sumchus that disputed money is divided.
