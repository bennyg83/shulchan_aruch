If he testified. Tosefta: Rabbi says that even in monetary cases, if he did not know that there was a relative or disqualified witness among them, the testimony may be upheld through the remaining witnesses. The Rosh explains that the lender did not know and did not summon them, as above at the end of seif 1, "and if he summoned, etc." See the Rosh, who cites the Rif and Ramban.
(Collected note) If he testified, etc. As stated in the Tosefta cited by the Rosh and according to the Rif's explanation of the Gemara, as above. (End of collected note.)
(Collected note) Some say that testimony, etc. See the Rosh, who writes that this is learned from "if they came merely to see, etc." Just as we depend on the intention of the disqualified witnesses, so too on the intention of the valid witnesses. This is unlike what is written in the gloss above, "even if they did not, etc.," and corresponds to the dissenting view here. (End of collected note.)
(Collected note) And he is believed, etc. Just as he is believed to say that he came merely to see, etc. Ibid.; see Shach, seif katan 11. (End of collected note.)
And there are. The Rosh there, who explains the Tosefta differently; see there.
Since. Meaning, according to the "some say" in seif 1.
