(Netivot HaMishpat, same passage) The words of the Rashba and Nimukei Yosef are astonishing, etc. Note: See Ritva to Yevamot, who first answers like the Rashba and Nimukei Yosef, then writes that there is a stronger explanation: how could they know the matter, since a matter of forbidden marital status cannot be established by their own admission? Even if they admit it and we believe them, their admission accomplishes nothing, just as a man who says, "I divorced my wife without witnesses," accomplishes nothing. The Ritva calls this explanation correct. According to the Rashba one may say that chalitzah is not classified as a matter of forbidden marital status. See Rambam, Hilchot Sanhedrin 16:6, who writes that the forbidden status itself may be established by one witness, giving as examples testimony that a woman is divorced or a zonah. Tosafot Rid to Kiddushin 66, however, says that testimony that a woman was captured or divorced so as to disqualify her is a matter of forbidden marital status. See Shev Shemateta, Shemateta 6, chapter 15; Mordechai, chapter Mitzvat Chalitzah; and Tosafot, Gittin 2b, end of the passage beginning "just as."
