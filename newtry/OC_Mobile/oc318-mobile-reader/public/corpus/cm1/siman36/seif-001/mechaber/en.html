Where many witnesses testified and one was found to be a relative or otherwise disqualified. This siman contains 2 seifim.
If many witnesses testify and one is found to be a relative or disqualified, all the testimony is void when all of them intended to testify. If they did not all intend to testify, the testimony of the others remains valid. How is this determined? Beit din asks them, "When you saw this matter, did you come in order to testify or merely in order to observe?" Anyone who says, "I came to testify," is separated into the group of intended witnesses. If a relative or disqualified person is found among those who intended to testify, all their testimony is void, even if they did not know of one another. If all the witnesses are valid, then both one who intended to testify and one who did not initially intend to testify but saw the event and later gave precise testimony may be relied upon, and judgment may be rendered on his testimony. Rama: They may not, however, write a document on the basis of what they saw, even if they witnessed a kinyan and an ordinary kinyan is made with writing in mind, since they were not summoned for that purpose. See siman 39, seif 5. Some say that merely intending to observe in order later to testify does not disqualify the group unless the person also comes to beit din and actually testifies. Both conditions are required, intent to observe for testimony and actual testimony in beit din. Rama: If a disqualified witness signed a document and thereby invalidated the entire testimony, nevertheless, if the valid witnesses remember the testimony after seeing the document, they may return and testify separately before beit din. Beit din may then record their testimony, and it is considered a valid document. A communal enactment signed by a disqualified witness, or a document signed by a litigant himself, remains valid through the other signatories, because the disqualified signer did not intend to act as a witness. See siman 45, seifim 12 and 13. If the claimant specifically summoned qualified witnesses and relatives or disqualified people were also present, even if the latter intended to testify and did so, the testimony of the qualified witnesses is not void. Likewise, when a person needs testimony and proclaims under cherem that everyone who knows testimony for him should come and testify, and both qualified and disqualified people testify, the testimony of the qualified witnesses is not void, because his intention was only to summon those fit to testify.
