(Shulchan Aruch, seif 1: even if they did not know, etc.) The Sema’s objection to the Rama can be answered. The Rosh writes against the Rif that this qualification is not found in the Talmud, for had the law depended on whether the valid witnesses knew of the disqualified witness, the Talmud should have stated that the court asks whether they knew of one another. Since the Rambam mentions nothing about such knowledge, it is most reasonable that he agrees with the Rosh. Otherwise he too should have stated that the matter depends upon knowledge, as the Rosh inferred from the Talmud’s silence. The Rama is therefore correct: even according to the Rambam, who does not require the disqualified witness actually to testify before the beit din and holds that his intention to testify suffices to invalidate the group, the valid witness need not know of the disqualified one.
