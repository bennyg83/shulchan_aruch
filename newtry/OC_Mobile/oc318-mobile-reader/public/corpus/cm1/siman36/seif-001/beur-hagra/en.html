A communal enactment, etc., is valid. Meaning, where they accepted him upon themselves, like "my father is trusted upon me, etc.," Sanhedrin 24a.
Or a document signed. Meaning, not only a communal enactment where they accepted him, for even a litigant himself can be accepted, as with "swear to me by the life of your head, etc.," and in Bava Batra, chapter Yesh Nochalin, "do you wish to swear and collect, etc.," and in the Tosefta even if he swore to him by the life of the principal, etc. Rather, even in ordinary documents the entire testimony is not invalidated, as stated in Makkot there: "would the murdered person save, etc.?" The rule applies to those who establish the matter. See Rosh there, siman 13, in the name of the Ra'avad. The litigant himself is not included in the category of witnesses. For this reason the early authorities said regarding "let two of them remove themselves, etc." that removal is effective and it is not considered testimony that began in disqualification, despite Tosafot's question there, because at the beginning he was a litigant and there was no testimony at all.
