A relative or otherwise disqualified witness. Noda BiYehuda, second edition, Even HaEzer, siman 76, cites a responsum of Beit Yosef in the laws of kiddushin, siman 10, where a great authority ruled, with Beit Yosef's agreement, that the rule that if one witness is found to be a relative or otherwise disqualified all the testimony is void applies only to a Torah-level disqualification. A rabbinic disqualification is not strong enough to invalidate the qualified witnesses who joined him. Knesset HaGedolah likewise cites Rabbeinu Beit Yosef, responsum siman 24, that when a witness is disqualified only by rabbinic law, the testimony stands through the remaining witnesses. Tumim, however, at the end of seif katan 11, writes that he searched and found no such statement in Beit Yosef's responsum. The discussion there whether a rabbinically disqualified witness invalidates the others concerned a case where no proclamation had been made, so even that witness himself was not yet legally disqualified. Where proclamation has been made, a fully rabbinically disqualified witness certainly invalidates the entire testimony. Tumim proves this from Ritva, Kiddushin chapter 2, who discusses one paid to testify, whose testimony the Sages declared void, and asks whether he invalidates all the testimony joined with his. Ritva concludes that he does not, because such a person is not a fully disqualified witness. This implies that a witness who is fully disqualified rabbinically does invalidate the entire testimony. Tumim declares this the clear practical rule. It requires study in Beit Yosef's responsa, because it is very difficult to suppose that two great authorities, Knesset HaGedolah and Noda BiYehuda, would attribute to him something not found there. Noda BiYehuda discusses several further laws relevant to this siman, some of which I cited in Pitchei Teshuva, Even HaEzer, siman 42, seif katan 9. See also Noda BiYehuda, first edition, siman 45, and Rabbi Akiva Eiger responsa, siman 85, regarding whether the rule that all testimony is void when one witness is disqualified also applies to testimony concerning a woman's marital status when the disqualified witness is wicked in a manner that disqualifies him even for such testimony, as explained in Even HaEzer 17:3. I cited their discussion there, seif katan 20.
