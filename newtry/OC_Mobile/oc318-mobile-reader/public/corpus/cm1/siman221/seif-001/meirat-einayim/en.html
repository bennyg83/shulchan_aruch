One who seeks to purchase from his fellow, etc. If the seller was the one who initiated the demand, etc. A similar rule is stated by the Tur and Mechaber in Even HaEzer at the end of siman 29 concerning kiddushin.
And afterward one returned, and they again completed meshichah without specification, we say, etc. The novelty is that even if the seller originally retracted because he thought he had sold too cheaply and said, “I will not give you the item unless you add to the first price,” and the purchaser continued persuading him until the seller handed over the merchandise without restating a price, we do not automatically say that the purchaser accepted the seller's new demand. One might have thought this follows from the preceding rule, where one says one hundred and the other two hundred. Nimukei Yosef in HaOmanin makes a related distinction concerning a worker hired for four who began the work, after which the employer retracted and the worker persuaded him to continue. The employer cannot later say, “I was appeased only because you agreed to reduce the wage.” The worker may say, “I persuaded you on the basis that I would perform excellent work for you.” Ramban explains that this is so even if the employer had expressly said, “I do not want you to work unless for one sela less than the original wage.” This differs from the Tosefta's case of one selling an item, one saying one hundred and the other two hundred, because there no prior price had ever been fixed. Where no price was fixed there is no grievance against the party who withdraws, so when the other returns and accepts the item without specification we presume he agreed to the other's demanded price. With workers, however, a price had already been fixed and there is a grievance against the one who retracts. Therefore, when he is later appeased without a new express agreement, we say he returned to the original price in order to remove the grievance. See the Derishah and siman 332.
