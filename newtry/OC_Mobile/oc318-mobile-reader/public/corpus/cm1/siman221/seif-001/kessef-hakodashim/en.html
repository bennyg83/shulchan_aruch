Seif 1. If the purchaser came and performed meshichah. Regarding siman 221, if the merchandise was already in the purchaser's possession as a deposit, perhaps his later use of it proves acceptance of the sale. He should not be able to say that he used it merely on condition to pay for the benefit, unless the item is of a kind normally used for hire. Where it is clear that his use was only because of the contemplated sale, that use may be decisive. In village tavern leases, however, if the landlord demanded two hundred and the tenant offered one hundred, and the tenant had occupied the tavern in the prior year, his remaining in the premises may not itself prove agreement because tenants are not necessarily removed immediately upon expiration. If the tavern cannot practically remain closed, and the landlord does not send liquor or another person to sell it, perhaps the tenant's selling the landlord's liquor is analogous to the landlord demanding performance, which in Even HaEzer at the end of siman 29 can reveal assent. On the other hand, the tenant could say he had no duty to sell at all and merely saved the landlord from loss, so the act may not show agreement to the demanded rent. This requires careful practical evaluation. If during the year the local ruler demanded an increased payment from the landlord for the two villages occupied by landlord and tenant, threatening to expel both otherwise, one may consider Maharik, shoresh 4, cited in siman 163, where communities potentially exposed to the same accusation must help even if the danger to them is remote. There the reasoning emphasizes possible danger to life, suggesting that mere monetary risk might differ, though Maharik also brings proof from expenses of a lock against theft, which concerns money. Perhaps ordinary social practice decides whether such an expense is one the tenant would certainly have paid to preserve the tenancy. A distinction may also exist between being asked in advance to contribute to the expense and seeking reimbursement only after the landlord paid it himself. If a seller initially said, 'I will sell for two hundred if you waive our dispute,' and the buyer offered one hundred with the waiver, but the next day they finalized a sale for a little more than one hundred without expressly mentioning the waiver, the seller may argue that the earlier waiver condition remained because he never retracted it. Siman 221 indicates that where one side later initiates completion of the transaction, that may be treated as acceptance of the other side's previous terms. Sometimes use of a broker obscures this inference. The relative value of the waiver compared with the increased purchase price may also show whether the parties intended the waiver to remain. Similar reasoning applies where a tenant remains in a house after the landlord stated a higher rent and the tenant offered less. His continued possession may be like a purchaser's meshichah after the seller stated his terms, because we do not presume him to be occupying as a trespasser. If the landlord expressly warned him that unless he agrees to the stated rent he must leave, and the tenant remained silent and stayed, this is even stronger than ordinary meshichah and ordinarily indicates acceptance of the landlord's price. The Rama in siman 333 regarding a chazzan who continues into a second year without being rehired may suggest that mere passive continuation does not always prove continuation on prior terms. Yet where there has been a specific prior dispute over price and the tenant later continues after the landlord's demand, the later unexplained conduct may be interpreted in light of the other party's expressed terms, just as the final silent act in siman 221 is interpreted from the prior negotiations. If the landlord or his agent later gives explicit warning and the tenant nevertheless stays, then according to all views it appears comparable to a purchaser who performs meshichah after hearing the seller's terms. According to the Shach in siman 333, perhaps even without renewed warning the continued occupation is enough. This entire matter requires careful study.
