For the settlement of the land. The Sages assessed that the first owner will certainly plant other trees just as he planted originally, whereas if he removes these trees the second owner may not plant replacements. Sema:
All three years. During the first three years the tree owner can say, "Had I planted new olive trees, their fruit would have been orlah. Because they arrived with my clod of earth, they escaped that prohibition." After three years, however, the land owner can say, "Had your trees not been here I would have planted new ones." The tree owner cannot respond, "Just as you shared my benefit during the first three years, I should share yours afterward." The land owner can answer that had he planted new trees, although he could not have eaten their fruit during the first three years, he could at least have sown beneath them; now that the trees are mature and broad, he cannot sow beneath them. Sema:
Everything belongs to the land owner. Although the fruit during the first three years may be orlah, the rule matters regarding branches or appreciation from thickening of the tree. Sema:
The value of his olive trees. During the first three years, according to the Sema, the land owner need not immediately pay the full value of the trees; he instead gives the tree owner half the fruit. The Taz disagrees and holds that he must pay immediately. How can the land owner use another person's olive trees against the owner's will when the owner protests that he does not want a share of the fruit?
