Three tefachim. This applies specifically when the garment will fall back onto the board, so that the board is the surface from which he must acquire it and it therefore has to be raised three tefachim above it. If, however, through his striking it the garment rose from the board and while descending fell to the ground, then even if it rose only slightly above the board, since it rose through his force and was three tefachim above the ground where it would ultimately fall, he acquired it. When the Tur and Shulchan Aruch later say, 'but if it did not rise upward through his striking,' they mean that it did not rise at all. See Perishah, where I brought proof for this. So writes the Sema. The Shach writes: see the Bach and Yam Shel Shlomo at the end of Chullin, who challenges the Rosh regarding this law. His words are puzzling, for the Rosh certainly proved it from the Gemara's question, 'if for the purpose of acquiring,' as explained by Rashi. The Rosh's question is how he can acquire through lifting by beating the nest when he is not holding the birds. This is clear.
