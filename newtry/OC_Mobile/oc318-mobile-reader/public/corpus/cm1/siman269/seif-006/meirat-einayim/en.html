And the one who lifted it is not believed, etc. This is the difference between a case where his fellow told him to lift it for him and a case where he independently lifted it for his fellow. If he lifted it on his own initiative for his fellow, then even if after lifting it he said that he had lifted it on his fellow's behalf, he is later believed to retract and say, "I was only joking with you," or, "I said so merely so as not to make myself appear wealthy." And some say that if Reuven did not have money, etc. The Mordechai writes in Raavyah's name that this is unlike a found object, which Reuven himself could have picked up from the ground. In a purchase, which depends upon completing an acquisition, if Reuven could have procured money and purchased it before the seller sold to someone else, then Reuven acquires his share. But if he could not, he does not, because this is a case where one person benefits and the other loses nothing. Darkei Moshe cites this. See what I wrote above in siman 183:4 reconciling Rama's statement here with what he wrote there. The source of Rama's ruling here is the Mordechai in the first chapter of Bava Metzia. It states that if inexpensive merchandise comes to Reuven and he tells Shimon, "Buy it for the two of us and we will divide the profit," and Shimon agrees, they divide the profit. The Mordechai compares this to one who lifts a found object for another. Raavyah disagrees, and his view is the alternative opinion Rama cites here. What Rama wrote above in siman 183 derives from Hagahot Mordechai in the fourth chapter of Bava Metzia, where a responsum of the head of the academy states: Reuven told Shimon, "I have a purchase opportunity in such and such place, buy it for the two of us." Shimon went and bought it. Reuven then said, "Give me my half." If Reuven had given Shimon money to purchase jointly and had made a kinyan with him, beit din compels Shimon to give him his share. In the first Mordechai passage, the case explicitly concerns inexpensive merchandise and compares it to lifting a found object. The Hagahot Mordechai passage does not mention discounted merchandise at all, but merely says, "I have a purchase opportunity in such and such place." One could therefore reconcile the sources simply by saying that the first case, where Shimon's agreement or Reuven's available money creates a joint acquisition, concerns unusually cheap merchandise and therefore resembles a found object, while the second passage concerns ordinary merchandise and no such comparison exists. This is unlike Rama's own formulation, for in siman 183 he also states the law concerning discounted merchandise. Darkei Moshe there first cites the Mordechai's view and then the Hagahot Mordechai as though both concern the same type of discounted merchandise. Therefore I wrote there a reconciliation according to Rama's own understanding as well. What appears correct to me I have written. Examine carefully.
