[Because he brought him. “And I did not know who was going to do this, and I would like to do it, and I would not know what it was like to do with it, and I would like to do it, and I would like to do it.”
The one who lifted it, etc. Although the Gemara initially thought that saying 'give it' is equivalent to saying 'acquire it,' and therefore held that one who says 'give it' is believed, with respect to credibility the Gemara nevertheless understood from the outset that 'acquire it for me' is stronger. He is not believed to say, 'I intended it for myself,' because ordinarily he acted according to his companion's instruction. Only when he actually intended to act for his companion are the two expressions equivalent. Examine this carefully.
"And if he said to me, I will be able to do it with it, and if I say, I will give it a chance, and I will be able to do it with it, and I will be able to do so with it," he said, "and I will not be able to do it."
