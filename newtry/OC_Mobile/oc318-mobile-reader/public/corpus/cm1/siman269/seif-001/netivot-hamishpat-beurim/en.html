One who lifts a found object. See Sema, who writes that he must specifically say that he is lifting it for his fellow. Shach disagrees and writes that intent alone suffices. Ketzot HaChoshen disagrees with Shach because unspoken intentions are not legally effective. He overlooked Rashba's novellae to Kiddushin 50, who writes that only where unspoken intent contradicts one's spoken words do we say that unspoken matters are ineffective. Ordinary unspoken intent, where there is no contradictory statement, is effective, as with terumah, which may be designated by thought. The proof Ketzot brings from garments dyed for a particular person is no proof, because there the circumstances themselves indicate that they were dyed for that person, since they are that person's garments. If, however, he expressly says that he is lifting the object for himself, even if he admits that inwardly he intended for his fellow, the fellow does not acquire, because unspoken intent that contradicts spoken words is ineffective.
