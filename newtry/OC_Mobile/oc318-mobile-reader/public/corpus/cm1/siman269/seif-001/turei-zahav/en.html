(Seif 5, and it rose three handbreadths through his force.) This is difficult. How does it differ from one who causes birds to flutter from their nest, cited by Beit Yosef, where we do not require that they be moved three handbreadths from the nest? Tosafot’s words cited by Beit Yosef imply this, for they write, “some of them fly somewhat upward.” It appears to me that here the board lies upon the ground, and therefore it must be raised three handbreadths from the ground, because there is no acquisition whatsoever within three handbreadths of the ground. The Tur’s later statement, “but if it did not rise,” refers to a case where the board itself is elevated above the ground. This is stated explicitly in the Rosh’s abridgment, for the principal distinction is between upward and downward movement, as Beit Yosef cites from Tosafot and Rosh. It is therefore proper to distinguish whether the board is above or below. The Sema, seif katan 6, explains this differently, but I do not know his source. The language later, “since it was raised through his force three handbreadths from the ground,” implies that the essential requirement concerns distance specifically from the ground.
