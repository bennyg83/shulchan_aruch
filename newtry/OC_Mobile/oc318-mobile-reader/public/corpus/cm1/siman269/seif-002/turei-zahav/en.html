(Seif 6, “Give it to me.”) The Tur writes that whether the finder first says, “I lifted it for you, but now I wish to acquire it for myself,” or whether he lifted it on his own initiative, the law follows the stated distinction. His phrase “lifted it on his own initiative” may mean that even if, while lifting, he said he was lifting it for his fellow, he cannot retract, because at the moment of lifting he became the other’s agent and acquired it for him. Nevertheless, while the object remains in his hand he is believed to say, “In truth I lifted it for myself. Although I said I was lifting it for my fellow, that was not my intent.” This is difficult. Why should one who says “Give it to me” be worse than one for whom another lifts a found article without any request by the intended recipient? Why may the finder acquire it for himself after lifting it for his fellow? The Sema, seif katan 9, writes that where the first person said “Give it to me,” the finder may retract even if he initially said he was lifting it for his fellow as his agent. This is astonishing. How can he retract after his fellow already acquired it through his lifting? Moreover, Maggid Mishneh, chapter 17 of Hilchot Gezelah, writes: “When he says, ‘Give it to me,’ the finder does not become his agent to acquire it by lifting, but only to deliver it to him.” This implies that had he become his agent, the agency would certainly be effective and he could not retract. It appears to me that the matter is as Nimukei Yosef writes in the first chapter of Bava Metzia: “Here the finder himself may say, ‘You said “Give it to me.” I did not take it with intent to acquire it for myself on your behalf, but only with intent to give it to you as you requested. Now I do not wish to give it to you.’” Thus he may say that at the time of lifting he was not the other’s acquisition agent at all. Even though he said he was lifting it “for his fellow,” he meant only after delivery. This is also the Tur’s intent in writing, regarding “Give it to me,” that the finder may say, “I lifted it for you,” meaning for you upon delivery. By contrast, where he lifts it on his own initiative for his fellow, he certainly becomes the fellow’s agent at the moment of lifting and acquires immediately for him. His only possible claim is that inwardly he did not intend what he said, as explained above. But if he admits that he became an agent to acquire for the other at the time of lifting, it appears clear to me that even where the other said “Give it to me,” he cannot retract.
