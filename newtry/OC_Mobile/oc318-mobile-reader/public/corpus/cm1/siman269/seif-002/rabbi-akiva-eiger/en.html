[Shulchan Aruch] A deaf-mute and a competent person together. Note: If the competent person lifted it high enough that if the deaf-mute were to release his hand the object would be detached from the ground, see the Tur, who records a dispute among the authorities. The Raavad holds that the acquirer obtains it, whereas the Rosh holds that the deaf-mute's grasp prevents the competent person's acquisition. See the Bach and Derishah, who write that according to the Raavad, if the competent person lifted it to that height, he acquires it, because there is no concern that they will quarrel: it can be explained to the deaf-mute that since the competent person raised it to that height, he acquired it. Consequently, if the deaf-mute also lifted it, he too acquires, since once the competent person acquired, the deaf-mute also acquires so that they not come to quarrel. But if only the deaf-mute lifted it to that height and not the competent person, since in any event the competent person did not acquire, the concern for quarrelling over this garment does not apply, and the deaf-mute likewise does not acquire.
