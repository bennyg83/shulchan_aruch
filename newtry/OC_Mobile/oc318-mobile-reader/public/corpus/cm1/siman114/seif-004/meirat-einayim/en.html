From the principal and half the improvement. "Principal" means the field's value when the purchaser bought it and the price he actually paid. Whatever increase exists now above that is improvement, and from that additional improvement the creditor collects one half. In siman 115 the Mechaber and Rama distinguish between improvement produced by expense and passive improvement, whether the field was made an apotiki, whether the document expressly included land and improvement, and whether the holder is a purchaser, gift recipient, or heir.
Under the enactment of the Geonim that he has nothing, etc. This refers to the oath imposed on one who claims he has nothing with which to pay, as explained at the beginning of siman 99.
If the borrower is with us in the country. Meaning in the same city or within a day's journey. If he is farther away, the creditor collects from the purchaser without waiting for the borrower to swear that he has no assets, because that oath is only a later Geonic enactment and the purchaser can pursue the borrower afterward.
They make the foreclosing creditor swear while holding a sacred object. Rambam and the Tur use this wording. Although the borrower's oath that he has nothing is also taken while holding a sacred object, that oath is a later Geonic enactment whose severity was added by those later authorities. The creditor's oath, by contrast, is an oath of the Mishnah, and therefore the wording emphasizes that it is taken while holding a sacred object.
And that he did not sell it to another. Otherwise this purchaser can tell him, "You are not my opposing party; deal with the person to whom you sold it."
He includes in his oath that this is not a trust document, etc. Only when he must take the main oath that the debt was not paid did Chazal require him to include these additional matters. If it is independently established that the debt was not paid, he need not swear merely that it was not a trust document. Therefore the ruling concludes that within the due date he can collect without an oath.
He collects without an oath. This is Rambam's view. Rav Hai and the Rosh require an oath, as the Tur records in siman 78, where the Mechaber himself presents the dispute. Although that passage speaks of a borrower who died, the same reasoning applies where he is alive but absent, and perhaps even where he is present, for the reason explained there.
