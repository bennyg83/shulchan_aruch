And beit din tears up the writ of authorization, etc. The Mechaber follows the order of the Rambam's presentation in chapter 22 of Hilchot Malveh VeLoveh, law 6. Immediately before this, the Rambam writes that if the borrower has no unencumbered property, the court writes the lender a writ authorizing him to search for purchasers who bought after the debt was created. After purchasers are found, the initial writ is torn up and a writ of seizure is written. The Mechaber already presented the Rambam's wording in this order at the end of siman 98, from seif 8 onward. He began with a writ concerning unencumbered property and concluded with the writ of seizure and transfer regarding encumbered property. There he gave the general form of these documents. He then set out in sequence the laws of collection from unencumbered property, and afterward the laws of actual collection from encumbered property. The present words, "a lender who knows of purchasers," therefore mean that after the initial authorization has already been written and encumbered property is found through it, the authorization is torn up and a writ of seizure is written. The Tur follows a different order based on the Rosh, as I explained in the Perishah at the end of siman 102.
