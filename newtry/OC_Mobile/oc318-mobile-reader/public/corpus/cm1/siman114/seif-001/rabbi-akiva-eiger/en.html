[Shulchan Aruch] And if it is within the fixed term, he collects without an oath. Note: Even claims that the debt was forgiven or sold are no longer relevant as independent claims. Nevertheless, those matters arise only through gilgul of the oath that the debt was not paid, as the Tur states explicitly. Even according to the Rama above, siman 82, seif 10, that even where the creditor was trusted regarding payment the debtor may require him to swear that he did not forgive the debt, one must say that we nevertheless do not advance such a claim on behalf of purchasers so as to require an oath without gilgul. This is like one who collects from the property of orphans within the fixed term, who collects without an oath, and we do not advance on their behalf the possibility that he forgave the debt. See what I wrote in the gloss there. However, I find it difficult why we require him, by gilgul, to swear that he did not sell the debt. A doubtful claim cannot generate gilgul unless there are grounds for suspicion, as stated above in Shulchan Aruch, siman 94. Here there are no such grounds, because it is uncommon for purchasers to buy a debt without receiving the document, as the Rosh writes in the first chapter of Bava Metzia, siman 48. The oath that he did not forgive the debt presents no difficulty, because when beit din advances for purchasers a claim that the original debtor himself could have asserted with certainty, it is treated as though that claim were definite, as is evident at the beginning of the second chapter of Ketubot. But regarding a sale, the debtor himself could not know with certainty that the creditor sold the debt. The claim is therefore merely doubtful, and gilgul should not apply absent grounds for suspicion. This requires study.
