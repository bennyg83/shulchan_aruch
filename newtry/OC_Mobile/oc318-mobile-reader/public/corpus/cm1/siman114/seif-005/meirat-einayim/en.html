If they erred by any amount, their sale is void. This means even though they conducted appraisal and announcement. In the case of the borrower himself, and likewise with orphans where beit din sold for their needs, even an error where property worth one hundred was sold for fifty leaves the sale valid according to all opinions when the required announcement was made, as the Tur and Mechaber state in siman 109:3. Nevertheless, regarding a purchaser's property, beit din has the status of an agent, whose sale is void for even the smallest deviation, as the Tur and Mechaber write below in siman 182. The reason is that the borrower or his heirs are themselves obligated to pay the lender and to sell their property voluntarily. When they did not do so and beit din performed everything required through appraisal and announcement, the sale stands. A purchaser, however, has no personal obligation to pay the lender's debt. Beit din therefore acts as his agent. Just as where an agent errs to the purchaser's detriment the purchaser may void the sale because the agent deviated from his mandate, so too where beit din errs in the purchaser's favor the lender may void the sale.
Some say that his law is like that concerning orphans. Above in siman 103:4, Rama did not append to the Mechaber's identical ruling a note that some disagree. The Tur there also appears to accept the Rambam and Rif, whom he cites as holding that if they erred by any amount the sale is void, whereas here the Tur writes regarding the Rambam that it does not appear correct to distinguish between purchasers and orphans. In the Perishah, however, I explained both there and here that careful examination of the Tur shows that he did not accept the Rambam there either. Rama's omission there therefore requires explanation. It may be that the Rambam stated this rule specifically regarding collection from a purchaser, that an error of any amount voids the transaction, while the Tur understood the rule regarding collection from the borrower without his consent to be the same as collection from a purchaser. The Rif also appears to distinguish only orphans, where beit din is regarded as the father of the orphans and the sale is treated as though the heirs themselves, after maturity, had made it. Therefore, regarding collection from the borrower's own property, neither the Tur nor Rama wrote anything additional in siman 103, relying upon what is written here concerning the Rambam's actual case of a purchaser. From this law one learns the analogous rule for collection from the borrower.
