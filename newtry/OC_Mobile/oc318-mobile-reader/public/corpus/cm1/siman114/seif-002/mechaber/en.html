When the creditor finds a purchaser holding encumbered property, the purchaser is compelled to litigate with him and cannot say, "Go first to the borrower. Once the borrower is held liable in judgment and you receive a writ authorizing collection from purchasers, then I will pay you." Rather, the purchaser himself must go to the borrower and compel him to appear in judgment. Nevertheless, where the borrower is accessible, we notify him, for example when he is no more than one day's journey away. If the purchaser asks for time to seek him out, he is given up to thirty days, provided his purpose is to bring the matter to a truthful adjudication and not to use evasive tactics.
