If the purchaser wishes, etc. Sema holds that even if the purchaser wishes to pay the creditor the actual current value of the field in cash, and that value is less than the creditor's total debt so that the debtor will still owe a balance, the purchaser may nevertheless remove the creditor by paying that value. According to Sema, the rule below in seif 6, that the purchaser cannot do so when the entire debt will not be paid, concerns a case where appraisal and announcement have already occurred. Here, before appraisal and announcement, the purchaser may always remove the creditor. See Tumim, where I discussed this at length and showed that this is incorrect. In no circumstance may the purchaser remove the creditor unless the creditor's entire claim against the debtor is extinguished. So long as any part of the creditor's claim remains against the debtor, the purchaser cannot remove him. The term 'removal' itself implies complete removal, and this is why the text uses that term rather than ordinary payment. All this assumes that the creditor does not say, 'The field is worth more to me.' If he does, see what I wrote below in siman 115.
Only one hundred. The field was actually worth no more than one hundred, even though the creditor was willing to value it at two hundred. The purchaser owes only its actual value. If the creditor accepted two hundred, it is obvious that the purchaser may return against the debtor for the full two hundred. Ritva states this explicitly in Kesubos.
Appraisal and announcement. Rama holds that after appraisal and announcement the purchaser can no longer remove the creditor. The Gemara's explicit case where the purchaser says, 'Take two hundred and leave,' concerns a stage before any final announcement. Sema, however, holds that so long as there has not yet been formal transfer and final vesting, the purchaser may remove the creditor even after appraisal and announcement, according to all opinions, provided that he pays the entire debt. If he does not pay the entire debt, he cannot, as Shulchan Aruch rules below in seif 6. See Tumim, where I wrote that Sema's position is the correct practical law.
