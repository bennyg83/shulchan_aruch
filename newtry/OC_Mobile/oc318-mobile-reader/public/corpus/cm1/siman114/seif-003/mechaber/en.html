If the purchaser wishes to remove the lender by paying him money, he may do so and retain the land, after which he may demand from the seller what he paid on the seller's behalf. If the land was made an expressly designated lien, with the borrower saying, 'You may obtain payment only from this property,' the purchaser cannot remove the lender with money. If the purchaser bought two fields successively for one hundred each, the lender's debt was two hundred, and the lender seized for one hundred the field purchased first, then when he seeks to seize the second field for the remaining one hundred, the purchaser may bring him two hundred and say, 'If you wish to retain the first field you already collected in exchange for the full two hundred, that is well; otherwise, take two hundred and depart.' The law follows the purchaser. If the lender chooses to accept the first field for two hundred, the purchaser may recover only one hundred from the seller. {Rama: Some say that once beit din has conducted an appraisal and announcement, the purchaser can no longer remove the lender, because an appraisal is not reversed for a purchaser. See above, siman 103, seif 9. All this concerns one purchaser who bought both fields. With two purchasers, the second cannot tell the lender to accept the first field above its value or remove him with money, because the second purchaser is not the lender's litigant.}
