If the creditor, etc. The Gemara there states that Rami bar Hama thought the creditor should give one thousand and receive one thousand. This indicates that only when the creditor is paid the entire debt may he raise the valuation. Rosh therefore writes there, in discussing Rav Hai Gaon’s view, that perhaps that case concerned full payment, like the case in our sugya. Although another bidder may raise the valuation, as stated in seif 5, that is for the borrower’s benefit. Here, however, whatever the purchaser gives is immediately taken by the creditor, as Rosh explains. Although a writ of dispossession is issued for only five hundred because the purchaser gave the creditor land rather than money, had the purchaser paid the full thousand in cash, the creditor would certainly have had to return all his money. See Tosafot there, s.v. ‘what.’ It follows that a purchaser cannot remove the creditor unless the whole debt is paid. Although a creditor may ordinarily be removed even from part of the field, as indicated in Bava Kamma 96a and Bava Metzia 110b, there the entire debt is in any event satisfied. This accords with Perisha and not with Sema, seif katan 4. See Shach there.
