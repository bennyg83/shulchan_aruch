The Mechaber copies this ruling from the Rosh and Tur, who hold that an appraisal is not returned to a purchaser at all. Their case therefore concerns a purchaser who, before the final transfer, seeks to cancel the appraisal by paying the creditor. The Rosh distinguishes between a payment satisfying the entire debt and one leaving a remaining balance. But according to the Rambam and those who hold that an appraisal is returned even to a purchaser, once the creditor has already received the land in final collection the purchaser can nevertheless remove him. Certainly, then, before the creditor has even been placed in possession, the purchaser should be able to remove him. If the appraisal will later be returned in any event, what difference should it make whether the purchaser presently pays the whole debt or only the value of this field? This seems clear. The Mechaber himself ruled above in siman 103 that a purchaser can recover property through the return-of-appraisal rule. He therefore should not have cited this limitation without qualification. The matter requires study. Perhaps the Mechaber states the rule for a case following the opinion, cited above in siman 103, that once the payment date has passed the creditor may sell the land. Suppose the creditor sold this land to another even before the appraisal and announcement process was completed. A sale or inheritance by the creditor terminates the return-of-appraisal rule. The purchaser can then seek only to remove the creditor's claim with money, without relying on later return of the appraisal. In such a case the distinction between paying the entire debt and paying only part of it applies. The Rosh's treatment of the two-mansion case in the name of Maharam appears to assume such a scenario. Alternatively, the same law can arise where a later creditor first collected the land through beit din, and an earlier creditor subsequently comes to seize it from him. The later creditor may say, "I will give you money and retain the land I have already collected." Whether he can do so then depends on whether he pays the earlier creditor's entire debt or only part. The doctrine of return of appraisal does not apply between two creditors in this way, because there is no special equitable reason to prefer the second creditor over the first. This also explains the Mechaber's ruling and indicates that the same principle applies between creditors. See below in siman 116, seif katan 1, where I discuss this further.
