They give the field to the one who adds, etc. The Tur writes that if, during the public announcement, someone offers more than beit din's valuation and the creditor wants the field, the creditor must take it at that higher amount. Appraisal and announcement are for the borrower's benefit: if someone offers more, that is preferable; otherwise the creditor receives it at the original appraisal. But if the purchaser himself raises the bid, we do not listen, because he is certainly acting for his own advantage, reasoning that he loses nothing by raising the price since the field will remain in his possession and he will later recover the increase from his seller.
And the entire debt will not thereby be paid, etc. But if the entire debt would be paid, the purchaser can remove the creditor because the creditor has not yet actually received the land.
