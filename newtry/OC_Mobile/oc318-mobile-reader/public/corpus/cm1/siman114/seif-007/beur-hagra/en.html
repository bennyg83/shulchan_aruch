If the creditor, etc. As explained in the preceding seif, the purchaser cannot remove him unless the entire debt is paid. Heirs differ, because they may remove the creditor even when the entire debt is not paid. See siman 109:1, where the creditor’s claim is discussed. Rosh writes that if money comes into the heirs’ hands they may remove him by paying the property’s value, because even a judicial appraisal is reversed in favor of an heir, unlike a purchaser. See Tosafot, Bava Metzia 35a, s.v. ‘a purchaser,’ and Rosh there. Sefer HaTerumot, gate 3, part 6, considers a case in which a purchaser or heirs wish to remove the creditor with money, while the land is worth only half the debt and the creditor says, ‘To me it is worth the amount of my debt.’ It concludes that the creditor cannot increase the value beyond the land’s market value. The Jerusalem Talmud, Ketubot 4:14, tells of a woman whose ketubah was twenty dinars when the only house was worth ten. Rabbi Hanina required either the house or twenty dinars, but Rabbi Mana said that since the house was worth only ten, she was treated as having a ketubah of ten; thereafter, if she demanded land and the heirs offered money, the law favored the heirs. Raavad and Ramban disagree and rule for the creditor, relying on the Bavli in Ketubot. The Jerusalem Talmud was interpreted differently regarding an widow’s dwelling. Yet according to Rosh’s distinction between heirs and purchasers, the Yerushalmi and Bavli do not conflict. Although Rosh himself explains the Yerushalmi concerning dwelling, its plain continuation concerns a ketubah. It first states that where no house exists the heirs rent one for her, and then records the case of the twenty-dinar ketubah. Rabbi Mana’s reasoning shows that heirs may remove her by paying the property’s value. The same principle applies to rent for a widow’s dwelling. See citations 81 and 109.
