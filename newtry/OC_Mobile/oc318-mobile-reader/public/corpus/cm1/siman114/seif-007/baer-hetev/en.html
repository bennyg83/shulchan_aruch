To the purchaser. This is because accepting the purchaser's proposal would cause a loss to the borrower. If the purchaser were heard, the borrower would not be discharged from the lender for the excess amount up to the full debt. This differs from a lender who comes to collect from heirs, as the Tur and Shulchan Aruch write in siman 109, seif 1. There, even if the lender wishes to accept the field left by the borrower in satisfaction of the entire debt, the heirs are nevertheless heard when they say, 'We will give it to you only at the beit din appraisal.' There is no loss to the borrower there, since he has already died, while the heirs benefit from the appraisal because if they later obtain money they may redeem the field from the lender at the appraised value, since an appraisal is reversible in favor of heirs, as explained there. Sema. The Shach writes: according to what I ruled at the end of siman 115, seif 1, even if the borrower has died and left no other assets at all, the purchaser is not heard here. This is the primary ruling.
