We do not listen. The reason is that this would cause a loss to the borrower: the creditor would receive the entire field against the full amount of his debt, yet the purchaser could not recover from the borrower for the balance of the debt. By contrast, if the purchaser receives the field at its appraised value, he may still recover the remaining debt from the borrower. See Tumim for a lengthy discussion. If the borrower has died, even the purchaser may make this argument, as with an heir above in siman 109, because there is no one from whom he can recover and there is no corresponding loss to the borrower. According to what I wrote in Tumim, however, the case there involves the creditor actually being paid on his debt, and then this distinction does not apply to heirs. See there also for the distinction between a purchaser's right to remove the creditor with money as strict law and the rule of doing what is right and good.
