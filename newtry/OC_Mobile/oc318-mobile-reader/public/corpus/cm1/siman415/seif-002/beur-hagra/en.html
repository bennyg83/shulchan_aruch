And if they caused damage at the time, etc. Tur and Nimukei Yosef write that this is no worse than the case on 26b of a stone that a person did not know was resting in his garment, where he is liable for damage. At the moment of falling he is a direct damager and is liable even for inadvertence and compulsion. After it has fallen, liability is only under the category of pit, like one's stone, knife, or burden, and applies only where ordinary wind moved it and he abandoned it. Thus one who hid an object in a sound wall, or the wall owner where the wall was unstable, may be exempt. See Nimukei Yosef and Rosh.
(Supplement) And if they caused damage, etc. Tur infers this from Rosh's comparison specifically to one's stone, knife, or burden after falling. See 411.
