Even if he has other movables, etc. We do not say that perhaps the debt documents being offered for collection will ultimately be uncollectible. That uncertainty is already reflected in the appraisal. If there is a collection risk, beit din discounts the documents accordingly. Every appraisal is based on what the item would sell for. In Tumim I therefore concluded against the Shach, who argues that where the debtor has other assets the creditor cannot be compelled to accept debt documents. The principal law follows Mechaber and Rama. I nevertheless wrote that regarding a woman collecting a ketubah it requires study whether debt documents can be assigned to her in payment. I also wrote that if the creditor wants the debt documents and the debtor refuses, the creditor cannot compel the debtor to give them, even if the debtor has nothing else except those documents and inferior land. This is unlike the Shach, who holds the creditor can compel him. I further wrote that if beit din already transferred debt documents to the creditor, the original creditor cannot later waive those debts, not only where the debtor has no other assets but even where he does, because collection through beit din has Torah force and is treated as though the money had already been collected. Regarding a married woman's ketubah, if she herself owes another creditor and the question is whether her ketubah can be appraised and transferred to that creditor, the principal ketubah amount certainly cannot be transferred. As to the dowry portion, see Tumim, where I left the matter doubtful because she may never actually come to collect it, and Rabbi Natan's lien may therefore not apply. If so, judicial transfer may likewise be inapplicable. I further concluded that even if the debt documents have already matured, we do not say, "Why appraise them? Let beit din collect the money and pay the creditor." Rather, the documents themselves are appraised and transferred according to their present value. The same rule applies not only to debt documents but even to a bare debt. Beit din appraises the debt and transfers it to the creditor, even where the underlying debtor is a gentile. This is implied by Responsa Maharam.
