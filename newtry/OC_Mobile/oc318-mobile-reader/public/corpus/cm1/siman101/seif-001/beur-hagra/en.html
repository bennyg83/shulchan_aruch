And if he does not have, etc. An oath is not imposed on a claim based merely on uncertainty except in the specific cases listed in the chapter All Who Take Oaths. Rashi and Mordechai state this explicitly. Ketubot 92a also supports it, though the suggested strategy there is difficult because the Sages would appear to teach falsehood. Tosafot resolve that objection.
Even if we see, etc. Ketubot 86a discusses one who conceals his money, yet no oath was imposed.
(Supplement) Ketubot 92a provides the same proof. Tosafot explain that where a party faces loss, advising a legally effective claim does not constitute teaching falsehood. See citation 5.
