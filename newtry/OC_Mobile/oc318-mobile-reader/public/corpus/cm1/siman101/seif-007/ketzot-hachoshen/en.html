If the borrower has money. The Tur, following Tosafot and Rosh in Perek Mi SheHaya Nasui, writes that where payment in cash would cause the borrower a loss, he may satisfy the creditor with land. This raises a question where the debtor expressly stipulated that he would pay only money. Does the stipulation remain binding even in a case of loss, since monetary conditions are generally effective? The sugya concerning Reuven who sold land to Shimon with responsibility, converted the price into a loan, and then died shows that Shimon can protect himself by giving the heirs land and then collecting that land back. Tosafot uses this to prove that where payment in cash would cause loss, the debtor may pay with other property. Since sale of merchandise is treated by Ritva as though the parties stipulated for cash, the sugya might suggest that even an express cash stipulation yields in a case of loss. A better explanation is that an actual express condition remains binding even in a case of loss. The implied condition in a merchandise sale is narrower. It means the seller does not have to accept unrelated property in place of cash, but it does not exclude payment through the very merchandise that was sold. Ritva compares this to a worker, and even a worker can be told to take the very product he created. Therefore in the land sale case Shimon can satisfy the heirs with the same land he bought. This is why Rambam and Tur use language indicating the known land itself. This also resolves Bach's question on Rambam. The sugya elsewhere appears to say that in a case of loss a creditor can be paid with any property. That is true for an ordinary creditor. A merchandise seller has the additional implied cash condition, so he can be forced away from cash only by returning the actual merchandise sold, not by some unrelated property. The broader principle is that the creditor's right to cash when the debtor has money is itself rabbinic. Torah law ordinarily lets a creditor collect inferior land. That is why the Sages could suspend the cash preference where enforcing it would cause special loss. The same is true of the rule that the creditor is repaid in the same type of coin originally lent. These are rabbinic collection rules, not absolute Torah requirements.
