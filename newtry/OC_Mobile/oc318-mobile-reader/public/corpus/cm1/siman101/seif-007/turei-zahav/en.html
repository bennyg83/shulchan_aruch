(In the Tur, seif 1). Regarding the case introduced with “How so? Reuven,” it is difficult why this elaborate scenario is needed. The Tur could have stated briefly that Reuven and Shimon owe one another, one of them dies, and the heirs seek to collect their debt from their debtor in movables while they themselves are not required to pay in movables. The debtor may therefore satisfy them with land so that he can then recover that land from them. This is stated explicitly in the Gemara, Ketubot 110. Although the particular scenario chosen by the Tur appears in the chapter Mi SheHayah Nasui, that merely reflects the facts of the incident. Why did the Tur, who was formulating the law, need the entire scenario? It appears that he intended to teach an additional novelty. Although the debtor's obligation to the heirs arose from his purchase of land from them, so that at the time of sale he clearly entered the transaction intending to pay money, for had he intended to give land he would have retained his own land, as Beit Yosef writes in the name of Ritva in the third new ruling, nevertheless, because of the loss he would otherwise sustain, he may satisfy them with land. This also explains why the Tur states that he first appeased them with money. Even if land had been seized from him, the same law would apply. Rather, this teaches an additional point: although he paid the heirs' debt in money and thereby revealed that he wished to retain the land and give them cash, he may nevertheless later give them land. Tosafot explain somewhat differently why it was necessary to state that he appeased them.
