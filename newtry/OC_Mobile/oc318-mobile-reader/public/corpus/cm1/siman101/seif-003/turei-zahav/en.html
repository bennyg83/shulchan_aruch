(On a loan). Sema, seif katan 8, explains this on the basis of the rule that one does not collect from encumbered property where free property is available. This implies that where no free property is available, the creditor could collect from the debtor of his debtor. This is puzzling, for Rama concludes that the debt owed by the second debtor is not yet due, and in that respect there is no distinction: even if nothing at all can be found in the original debtor's possession, the creditor cannot collect from the second debtor before the due date. This is explicit in siman 86, seif 9. In Responsa Rashba, responsum 1,122, however, the phrase “before the due date” mentioned by Rama does not appear. There the reason that one does not collect from encumbered property where free property exists applies properly: the original debtor has land with which to pay, while his debtor has money, and the law of Rabbi Natan does not transfer the debt directly from the latter to the creditor so long as the creditor can in any event obtain payment from the original debtor's land. This explains the phrase “after the due date” regarding the debtor of the debtor. But this cannot explain Rama's formulation, which expressly speaks of a debt not yet due. Rama's words also require study, for why did he state the obvious rule concerning a debt before maturity, when that rule is not mentioned by Rashba? He should have written Rashba's actual distinction, which turns not on maturity but on whether free property is available. Nevertheless, the practical law appears plainly as I have written.
