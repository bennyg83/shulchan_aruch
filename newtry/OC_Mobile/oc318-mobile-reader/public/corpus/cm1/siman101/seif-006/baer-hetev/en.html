Who stipulated. This does not mean that he made a formal condition at the time of sale, for if so the same rule would apply to a loan. Rather, it refers to the ordinary practice of merchants, who sell merchandise and fix a date for payment. In such a case the buyer must make an effort to pay in cash, because every merchant ordinarily intends that. This differs from an ordinary loan, whose purpose is generally to perform a kindness for the borrower. For this reason the Ir Shushan rules that where a loan document states that a sum was given for a half profit investment, since the investor's intention was to benefit and earn a profit, the recipient must pay him in cash when the due date arrives. This applies specifically where it is known that the investor intended the money to be returned in cash at the fixed date. If one simply gives another a sum to trade with on a half profit basis, since commercial practice is to exchange merchandise for merchandise and profit in that manner, the investor must wait until the recipient sells his merchandise for cash, or else accept the merchandise itself together with the profit that has accrued on it. This is obvious. Thus writes the Sema. See also siman 336, seif 2.
