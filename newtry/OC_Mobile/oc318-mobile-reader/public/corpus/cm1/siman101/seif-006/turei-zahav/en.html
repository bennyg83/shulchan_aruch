(Seif 9) To sell it immediately. Sema writes in seif katan 12 that even though no buyer is presently available, the property is appraised according to what a buyer would pay were one to come. I do not understand this. If so, lenders would be discouraged from extending credit, for what is the creditor to do with property for which no purchaser can be found and which he does not need? Moreover, the Rosh wrote in principle 8, responsum 4, that no postponement may be allowed because of price, for that would discourage lending. Even regarding consecrated property the rule is that consecrated property has only its place and its time. This is precisely the appraisal of beit din: the property is valued at the amount for which it can be sold immediately, in a manner that ensures that the creditor can find a purchaser for cash. I also find difficulty in Rama's ruling in siman 109, seif 2, concerning a time of plague, followed by the view of those who disagree because such a time is not a proper time of sale. According to Sema, why not appraise it as though a purchaser had come? Nor do I understand his proof from siman 103. There the appraisal is certainly made at the reduced value reflected by the buyers who are then present. Yet it may still be that even at that reduced price no one will buy, in which case the creditor must accept the property. See what I have written in siman 109 for the practical conclusion.
