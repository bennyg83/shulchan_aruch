See Shach, seif katan 3, who proves that where the debtor has no other assets, promissory notes are subject to collection, and when beit din assigns them to the creditor, writing and delivery are unnecessary. According to this, the same applies to an oral debt, because the Shach's principal proof derives from the lien of Rabbi Natan, and that lien applies even to an oral debt. The same law applies to partners, who may divide through beit din even oral debts, because the Shach compares the two laws. It further appears that even debts owed by non-Jews may be divided in this fashion. The deed of division that beit din gives each partner effects acquisition for that partner of the debts allotted to his share. See Shach, seif katan 3, where several textual errors should be corrected in accordance with the sense of his discussion, including that writing and delivery are unnecessary and the practical difference where the debtor is not present, as well as his discussion of a later creditor who collected first and left promissory notes, and his statement that even without Rabbi Natan's lien it remains a debt.
