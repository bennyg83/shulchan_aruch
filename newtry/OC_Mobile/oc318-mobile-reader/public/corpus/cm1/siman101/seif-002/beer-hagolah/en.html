From Rav Chama's statement to Rav Pappa, etc., Ketubot 86a.
Tur, seif 3, in the name of his father the Rosh, who writes likewise in his rulings at the beginning of Bava Kamma.
He explains there that since the creditor gave money, he may collect that which most closely resembles money. This means movable property, because if it cannot be sold in this town it can be sold in another town. Baal HaTerumot writes likewise in Shaar 4 in the name of a responsum of Rif, that movables are literally called money, whereas land is called money's equivalent.
There, and the Rosh likewise writes, as does Baal HaTerumot there, from Ulla's statement that by Torah law a creditor collects inferior land, as it says, "Stand outside," meaning that the debtor brings out what he ordinarily chooses to give, Bava Kamma 8a.
