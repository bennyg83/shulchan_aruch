Unlike those who say, etc. The rule that one collects money rather than land where the debtor has money is itself a rabbinic enactment, because the lender originally gave money. The oath that the debtor has no money is likewise a rabbinic enactment. We do not make an enactment upon an enactment. The Shach disagrees and requires an oath based on a responsum of Rosh. See Tumim. I established that Rama is correct. Even if it was previously known that the debtor had money and he now says that it is all gone, we do not make him swear, but only place him under a ban. However, if the creditor makes a definite claim, "I know with certainty that you have money," then there is a monetary difference worth at least one perutah between collecting cash and collecting land. There is therefore an ordinary monetary claim of a perutah, and everyone agrees the debtor must take a hesset oath. In any event many authorities hold that he always swears.
