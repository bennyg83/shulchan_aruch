Unlike the opinion that says otherwise. The ordinary implication of the decisors is that whenever the creditor may be paid with land, the debtor no longer needs to swear that he has no cash. A responsum of Rosh appears to disagree. The Shach reconciles it by saying the early sources speak according to Talmudic law, whereas after the enactment of the Geonim the debtor must swear he has no money even if he gives land. In my view no oath is necessary. As explained above, even a debtor who actually has cash can sometimes pay with land where cash payment would cause him a loss. A sincere person may prefer to surrender property rather than take an oath, even a true oath, and may be forced to sell assets at a heavy discount if cash alone is demanded. That is itself a meaningful loss. The Sages established only a modest preference for cash collection. Therefore, unless it is actually known that he has cash and no special loss exists, we do not force him to swear merely to avoid payment with land.
