And this is the primary ruling. See Baer Heitev, who writes that the Shach disagrees. See Tumim, who concludes that the Rama's view is primary. Even if it was known that the debtor originally had money and now claims that he no longer has it because he lost it, we do not impose an oath upon him, but only a cherem. Nevertheless, if the creditor asserts with certainty, "I know that you have money," then there is clearly a difference of at least a perutah between collection in money and collection in land. There is therefore a definite monetary claim worth a perutah, and according to all opinions the debtor must take a heset oath. See there, and see what I wrote above, siman 82, seif 3, seif katan 4.
