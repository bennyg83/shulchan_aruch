It therefore appears correct, etc. The authorities discuss whether a debtor who possesses land but claims to have no movable property must take the Geonic oath even when he offers the land in payment. Some early authorities appear to exempt him, reasoning that once he is prepared to satisfy the debt with land, beit din does not impose two separate burdens upon him. This may refer only to the law of the Gemara before the Geonic enactment, or to a debtor who is not at all presumed to possess money. The Rosh and Rashba, however, indicate that after the Geonic enactment he must swear even when offering land, because otherwise every deceptive debtor could conceal his cash and force the creditor to accept land at an inflated valuation. In an age of widespread deception, this oath should certainly be imposed. The debtor's willingness to surrender land does not prove that he lacks movable assets, and the creditor may demand verification that no more readily collectable property is being hidden.
