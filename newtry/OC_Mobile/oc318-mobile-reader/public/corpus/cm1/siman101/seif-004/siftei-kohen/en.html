If the money was in their hands as a loan, it is not removed from them before the time. The Rama's wording requires explanation, because the Rashba's responsum does not expressly distinguish the period before maturity. Rather, it discusses a case in which the first debtor has land or movable property. The meaning is that with an ordinary loan, even where no fixed term was stated, the borrower may say that he still needs the money. So long as the creditor is not harmed, the funds are not taken from the second borrower when that would cause him loss, for one does not collect from encumbered property where unencumbered property is available. It is enough that the original debtor pay other movable property. A deposit is different, because the bailee cannot claim a continuing right to use it. The references to a debt not yet due should be understood broadly: whenever immediate extraction would cause loss because the borrower has been granted time or still reasonably needs the funds, the money is not taken prematurely.
