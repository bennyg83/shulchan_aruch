In a loan. The Sema writes that the reason given there is that this falls under the rule that one does not collect from encumbered property when unencumbered property is available, as the Tur and Mechaber state in siman 86. The Shach writes that Rama's wording requires explanation. Responsa Rashba does not distinguish there between before and after the due date, but speaks where the first borrower has either land or movable property. It appears that the meaning is as follows. In an ordinary loan, even where no fixed date was set, the borrower may say, "I still need the money." Therefore, when the first lender does not object to leaving it with him, we do not remove it from the borrower, because the borrower's loss makes it analogous to encumbered property where unencumbered property is available, and it suffices to collect from the original debtor's unencumbered movable property. A deposit is different, because the custodian cannot claim that he still needs the deposited money for some further period. The words of Rama here and of Beit Yosef and Darkei Moshe at the beginning of siman 86, which speak of the due date not yet having arrived, should be understood similarly: where immediate collection would cause a loss, we do not collect before the due date, and the same applies if the lender has now granted the borrower an extension, and in similar situations. I discussed this at length in siman 86. The Taz writes similarly and disputes the Sema.
