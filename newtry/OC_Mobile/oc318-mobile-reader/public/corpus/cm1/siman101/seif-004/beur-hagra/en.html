If he has no, etc. Bava Batra and Bava Kamma describe this as prudent advice, implying that ordinarily the borrower cannot be compelled except in such circumstances.
If there was money. A deposit remains legally in its owner's domain, as stated in Kiddushin 47a.
But if, etc. Since land remains available, one does not collect from encumbered property where unencumbered property exists, as taught at the beginning of Gittin. This property is considered encumbered. See citation 40.
Before the due date. This is Rama's language. He holds that after the due date the property is in the creditor's domain, based on Pesachim 31a and Rabbi Natan's rule. That passage applies even where the debtor has money in addition to land.
