But if the debtor did not own the land then and acquired it only after he had already borrowed from both creditors, and both documents contain after-acquired-property clauses, the Gemara says they divide. Some authorities explain that the lien does not take effect until the land is acquired. At that moment both liens arise simultaneously, so they divide. Others explain that the after-acquired-property lien arises immediately when the clause is written, but when he later gives the same clause to the second creditor he retracts from the first to some extent. The retraction is only as to half, so they divide. Alternatively, we are uncertain whether the second clause constituted a retraction at all, and since neither creditor is in possession they divide.
