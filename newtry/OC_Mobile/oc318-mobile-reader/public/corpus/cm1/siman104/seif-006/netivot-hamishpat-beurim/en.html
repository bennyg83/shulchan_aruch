Ketzot HaChoshen seeks to answer the Shach's objection from the rule that the creditor collects half the appreciation even though the purchaser is in possession. He answers that because the creditor takes the body of the land together with the appreciation and credits its value against the debt, the creditor is considered the possessor, since land stands ready for collection, as stated in Bava Metzia, Perek HaMekabel, 110a. Although there the case concerns an apotiki, here too, since the debt corresponds to the land and its appreciation, the creditor can say, It is worth the entire debt to me, and because he takes all the land, and so forth. I do not understand this. As to the appreciation that was not subject to his lien, once the second creditor seized it, it is as though it was never subject to the first creditor. The first creditor need not be given either the appreciation or its value. Just as when the purchaser receives half the appreciation he receives land, so too if he receives all the appreciation by virtue of being the possessor, he receives land and not money. Moreover, what relevance is there to saying, It is worth that much to me, even as to the principal land? If he says it is worth that much to him, he will have no share in the appreciation at all, and as to the appreciation itself he cannot say, It is worth that much to me. His objection to Nimukei Yosef, that this resembles one party saying the whole is mine while the other says half is mine, will be explained below in siman 112.
