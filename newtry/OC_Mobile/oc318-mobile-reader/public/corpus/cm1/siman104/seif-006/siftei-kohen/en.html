Whoever first collected has acquired. The Beit Yosef understands the authorities to mean this even where the creditor took possession without beit din. In my view, the Nimukei Yosef is correct that if one creditor seized more than his proportion without the debtor's consent, the excess is removed from him. The phrase “what he collected, he collected” refers to property awarded by beit din or voluntarily transferred by the debtor, not to unilateral seizure. Seizure does not establish rights in land. This also explains why a creditor takes only half of the improvement from a purchaser in the case of successive loans followed by acquisition. The purchaser's possession of the improvement was not created by an award of beit din or by the seller's later transfer, and therefore it is treated as ineffective seizure against the competing lien.
