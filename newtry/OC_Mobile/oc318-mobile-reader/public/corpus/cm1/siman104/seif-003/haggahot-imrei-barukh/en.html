(Shach, seif katan 21) As stated in Tosafot in chapter Mi SheMet, etc. Note: See Ketubot 38b, Tosafot, passage beginning "does a daughter have rights in the grave," where they explain a practical difference if she owes money to others. But according to the Ra'ah and Ritva in their novellae there, who say that the matter is revealed retroactively to have belonged to him, one can say that it is not considered prospective property. Still, study is required whether it should be considered prospective because a fine, before standing in judgment, remains contingent: perhaps the defendant will admit and become exempt. I later saw that Sefer Hafla'ah there raises part of this point in discussing the Shach's words.
