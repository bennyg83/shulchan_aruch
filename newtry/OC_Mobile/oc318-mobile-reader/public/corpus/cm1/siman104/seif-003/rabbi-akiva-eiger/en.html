[Sema, letter 15] It is as though he sold it to him. Note: Even according to what we rule below, seif 13, in accordance with Rav Huna, that an earlier oral loan has priority over a later documented loan, one may distinguish. There, the oral creditor also has a Torah lien. Here, by contrast, the issue is after-acquired property. For this reason the Beit Yosef likewise holds that where one borrowed, borrowed again, then acquired property, and did not write an after-acquired-property clause to the first creditor, the later creditor collects all of it. The Shach’s position in seif katan 15, that where one borrowed, borrowed again, then acquired property and did not write such a clause to the first creditor, and likewise where one borrowed, acquired, and then borrowed, the creditors divide, rests on the view that in every case a document is not treated like a sale. Rather, the oral creditor is earlier only because of the Torah lien. Here, where there is no Torah lien, there is no rule of priority and they divide. All this, however, follows Rav Huna. According to the Rif, who holds that a later documented loan has priority over an earlier oral loan because a lien is treated like a sale, the law here would seemingly certainly follow the Sema: where one borrowed, acquired, and then borrowed, the later creditor acquires the lien; and where one borrowed, borrowed again, then acquired property and did not write an after-acquired-property clause to the first creditor, the later creditor is first. Mishneh LaMelech likewise writes this. Since the Shach rules in siman 102 like the Rif, yet rules here that they divide, his reasoning requires explanation. See there.
