With respect to the rule that if the later creditor collected first, etc. I already explained at the beginning of this siman that this means as follows: if both creditors come together to collect, they divide movable property. The Tur likewise states without qualification here that there is no priority regarding movable property. When the Tur continues, "Therefore, if the later creditor went first, etc.," it means one thing and moreover another: not only is the later creditor equal when both come together, but even if he went first and collected the entire amount, he retains it all. The Mechaber selected only one of these consequences because he wished to continue with the law, "Moreover, if the first creditor stood by, etc.," which relates specifically to a case where the later creditor went first and collected. This is also proven by Rama's gloss, "However, if he seized documents, etc., they divide." Why should they divide? If seizure had no effect, still the earlier creditor would not be worse than where both come together, according to the view that priority applies there. Rather, it is clear that even when both come together the earlier creditor has no priority in movable property. Rabbeinu Yerucham explicitly states this elsewhere. See my responsa.
