They made an announcement for one day. The Tur says that they announced for thirty days. There is no practical difference, because the rule depends on local custom and the law of the kingdom. The Tur quotes the actual facts of the Rosh's responsum, where the announcement continued for thirty days.
If the creditors disputed, etc. The responsum concerns Reuven, Shimon, and Levi, who were each owed money by a gentile and each sent an officer to seize collateral from the gentile. It was unknown who had priority except according to the gentile pledgor, and each creditor claimed that the seizure had first been made for him. The collateral was not physically held by any of them. The ruling was to follow whichever creditor the gentile identified. It appears that the same law applies if the borrower is a Jew. The facts simply involved a gentile borrower. I therefore do not know why the Ir Shushan writes specifically, "if the borrower is a gentile," implying that the rule is limited to such a case. If his reason is that the officer is the one testifying, the preceding case likewise speaks of collection under a governmental document and applies even to a Jewish borrower. Where the authorities have the upper hand, their established procedures must be followed.
