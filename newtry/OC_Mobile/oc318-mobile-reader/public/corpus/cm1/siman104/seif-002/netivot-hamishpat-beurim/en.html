He is believed. See Sema, seif katan 8, who extends the rule even where the borrower is a Jew. Tumim rejects this. A creditor who seizes property where doing so harms other creditors acquires nothing, and there is no agency for a non Jew. All the more so a mere attachment does not help, as the gloss in seif 4 states. Tumim therefore concludes that the rule applies only where the debt itself is owed to a non Jew. By borrowing from a non Jew, the Jewish borrower accepted that the creditor's security rights would operate according to the applicable non Jewish law. The second ruling, involving a claim that collection occurred improperly, likewise applies only in the non Jewish creditor context. Tumim is correct.
