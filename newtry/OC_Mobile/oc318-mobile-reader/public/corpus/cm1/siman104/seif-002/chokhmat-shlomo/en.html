Seif 13. 'A loan documented by a written instrument and an earlier oral loan.' Note: If there are two documents, and the earlier creditor did not receive an express lien on movable property together with land, while the later creditor did receive such a lien, it would initially appear that this follows the same law as an oral loan versus a documented loan. According to the Mechaber, who holds that the oral loan takes precedence, here too the first creditor should take precedence even though movable property together with land was not expressly pledged to him. According to the authorities cited by the Shach who hold that a documented loan takes precedence, the later creditor, who has an express lien on movable property together with land, should take precedence. However, according to the Shach's statement that where the debtor expressly created the lien all agree that the documented loan takes precedence, the same is true here. Since movable property becomes subject to the lien only when expressly pledged, all agree that the later creditor takes precedence, and the property is given to the one whose document contains the express lien on movable property together with land. This appears clear to me.
