An earlier oral loan takes precedence, etc. The reason is that since liens are of Torah origin, there is no distinction with respect to unencumbered assets between a documented loan and an oral loan. The Tur then writes that the Rif disagrees and holds that a documented loan takes precedence even if later in time, because the lien created by a document is like a sale. Just as an oral loan cannot collect from later purchasers because it lacks publicity, so too it cannot collect from unencumbered assets that became encumbered later, because it lacks publicity. Darkei Moshe rules according to the majority of authorities, following Rav Hai as the Mechaber does. The Tur then adds that among two oral loans, the earlier has priority. The Mechaber need not state this because it follows from his ruling that an earlier oral loan takes precedence even over a later documented one. See Rama's first gloss at the beginning of this siman, where he writes that if a later lender collected before an earlier oral lender, what he collected remains his, based on the Ran, who follows the Rif. To reconcile Rama's citation there with his ruling here according to Rav Hai, see what I wrote above. In the Perishah I further explained that the Tur's case here appears to be one where the debtor expressly encumbered his property to each lender before witnesses. This also resolves Rama and prevents contradiction with the Tur's later citation of the Raavad. Baal HaTerumot explicitly distinguishes in this manner. All these priority rules apply specifically to collection from unencumbered land, not movable property, for it has already been explained in this siman that even two documented loans stand equally as to movable property and the later lender has the same right as the earlier one.
