A documented loan, etc. The Mechaber follows Rav Hai, based on the Maggid Mishneh's understanding of the Rambam. In my view, this ruling is not clear. The Rambam's unqualified language may still concern a documented loan, since he speaks of collection both from the borrower and from purchasers, and the Rambam often writes generally while addressing a written obligation. Baal HaTerumot records both views and ultimately follows the Rif. The Rif, Baal HaTerumot, Ramban, Ran, and Ritva therefore form a substantial body of authority against Rav Hai. Even Rav Hai appears to speak only of an ordinary document. Where assets were expressly encumbered in the document, he should agree that the written creditor has priority, for an oral creditor never collects from encumbered property. Rashba's contrary responsum is a lone opinion. The Sema's distinction between an oral loan with and without acceptance of responsibility also requires analysis. As to competing oral loans, the Tur gives priority to the earlier one, but the Ran rules that they divide equally, and that appears primary.
