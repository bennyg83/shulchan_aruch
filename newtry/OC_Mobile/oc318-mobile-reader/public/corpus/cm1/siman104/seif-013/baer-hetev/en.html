Earlier. The Tur concludes that the Rif disagrees and holds that a written loan has priority. The Tur further writes that an earlier oral loan has priority over a later oral loan. The Sema explains that this refers to a case in which the borrower subjected his assets to each creditor in the presence of witnesses. All these rules of priority apply to collection from unencumbered land, not to movables, for movables have no priority even between two written notes. The Shach discusses the matter at length and concludes that, as a matter of law, the creditors should divide. See the Bach and the many responsa cited there, including Maharashach, Mabit, Maharashdam, and Tumat Yesharim. The Radbaz, part 1, siman 170, writes that an oral lender is believed only where the holders of the written notes admit his claim; an admission by the borrower alone raises concern for collusion. Even if the holders of the notes do not themselves raise that argument, this is a case of opening one's mouth for one who cannot plead. Knesset HaGedolah.
