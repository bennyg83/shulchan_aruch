For a creditor does not collect property that is merely prospective. The Shach says one could hold that a creditor does collect prospective property, and that the result here is nevertheless correct because the heirs can say, "We come in place of our father's father." He therefore treats the issue as an unresolved legal doubt. See Tumim. I clarified the practical differences between the two approaches and proved from Tosafot that where the creditor's document lacks an after-acquired-property clause, everyone agrees he cannot collect property that was merely prospective. I also concluded that the principal practical law follows Mechaber and Rama: a creditor does not collect prospective property under any circumstances, even today after the Geonim enacted collection from movables. As to wages for labor, Sema holds that a creditor cannot collect them. The Shach distinguishes between an ordinary creditor and a ketubah claimant, saying an ordinary creditor can collect but a ketubah claimant cannot. See Tumim. I concluded that the Shach's result is correct, though not for the reason he gives. The law of the debt document itself belongs primarily in Even HaEzer.
