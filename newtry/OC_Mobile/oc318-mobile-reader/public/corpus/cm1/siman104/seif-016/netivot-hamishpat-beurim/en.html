A creditor does not collect property that was merely prospective. See Shach, through his practical distinction where property fell to the debtor after his death from his maternal grandfather. Tumim cites Raanach, who asks: If the maternal grandfather died after the debtor, a son does not inherit through his deceased mother in the grave; but if the maternal grandfather died first, the property is not merely prospective, because the debtor had already acquired it in his lifetime. He answers that the case can be where the maternal grandfather died first, but his estate was subject to the ketubah of the maternal grandfather's wife during the debtor's lifetime, so it was considered prospective. Tumim writes that the example was not stated regarding the paternal grandfather because then the heir could argue that the property was also prospective as to his father and that he inherits only by virtue of his grandfather. See there. His words are very difficult. The Mordechai and Mahari Weil wrote that a husband and a firstborn do not inherit property that was merely prospective, but a son certainly inherits his father even in such property. As to the principal question whether a creditor collects from prospective property, Tumim elaborates with strained explanations. The principal view appears to me to be that of Ketzot HaChoshen. The argument, I come directly from my grandfather, is certainly insufficient by itself, because the creditor can answer that the debtor's father first acquires the property and it is subject to me through him, as Tumim himself writes. Therefore, one also needs the reason that property which was merely prospective did not become subject to the lien. Nevertheless, there is still a mitzvah upon the son to pay his father's debt from his father's assets. For this, the argument that he comes directly from his grandfather is necessary: that mitzvah applies only where the property could have reached the son only through his father. Where the assets can come to him directly from his grandfather without passing through his father, there is no such mitzvah. See there.
