For there is no, etc. Rosh at the end of Bechoros and Ramban derive from this Mishnah that damages are not collected from property merely anticipated to come to the debtor, even though the obligation is treated like one written in a document. Ramban further brings proof from the Tosefta and Yerushalmi, cited by the Rosh there. See there. See also Sefer HaTerumos, shaar 43, part 4.
(Likkut) For there is no, etc. Sefer HaTerumos writes this in the name of Ramban, and Rosh at the end of Bechoros derives it from this Mishnah and additionally from the law that damages are not collected from prospective property, as stated in the Tosefta and Yerushalmi cited by Sefer HaTerumos and the Rosh. Sefer HaTerumos in the name of Rosh of Lunel, and the Rosh in the name of R' Ephraim, write that a creditor does collect, while Rabbeinu Chananel is uncertain. The Rosh wonders at them from the Mishnah mentioned above, but he overlooked Tosafos, Bava Batra 159a, s.v. "and let them say," where Tosafos asks, "But still," etc., "however, one does not," etc. End.
(Likkut) For there is, etc. See what I wrote elsewhere that Tosafos disagree with this and write that collection is possible because the heir stands in the place of his father's father. Ramban, however, writes in Bava Batra 125b that the reason is precisely that a creditor does not collect from prospective property. The Yerushalmi cited by the Rosh in Bechoros proves that the reason is the status of prospective property, for it derives from "his vineyard" an exclusion of property merely anticipated rather than presently possessed, and R' Elazar says in the name of R' Nasa, "where he dug," etc. Moreover, if Tosafos were correct, why would a verse be needed in such a case? Ramban writes this as well in his novellae. He adds another proof from the beginning of chapter Af Al Pi, concerning "conditions of the kesubah," etc., where the Gemara mentions improvement but does not likewise mention prospective property, because a creditor too cannot collect from such property. The same is implied by Rav Nachman's rule that "orphans who collected land," etc. See Be'er HaGolah, which cites Tur, and the same is in Shulchan Aruch, siman 107, seif 1. See what I write there. End.
(Likkut) For there is, etc. Ri Migash does not appear to agree. In Bava Batra 125b he discusses whether a woman's kesubah may be collected from a debt owed to the deceased, but writes nothing about an ordinary creditor. Ramban likewise understands this to be his view. Tosafos in chapter HaKosev, Kesubos 84a, s.v. "for the kesubah," in Bava Batra 125b, s.v. "and he does not," and in Bechoros 51, s.v. "and not," follow the same approach, consistently with what they write in Bava Batra 129a. Rif also appears to hold this way. Ri Migash writes there in Rif's name that an outstanding loan is considered prospective property with respect to a firstborn son, a husband's inheritance, and a woman's kesubah. This is why regarding the sugya in HaKosev, "and now that the Sages instituted," etc., he mentions collection by an ordinary creditor but not by a wife for her kesubah, following his approach. Rosh there adds both a woman's kesubah and an ordinary creditor. See what I write in Even HaEzer, siman 100, seif 2. R' Ephraim likewise holds that a creditor collects, and Rabbeinu Chananel is uncertain. See Maharshal's responsum at length and Rama's responsum. Nevertheless, the primary ruling is as written here, based on the proofs above. End.
