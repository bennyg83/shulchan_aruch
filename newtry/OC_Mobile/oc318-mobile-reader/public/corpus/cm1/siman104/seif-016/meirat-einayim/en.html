Because a creditor does not collect from property merely expected to come into the debtor's estate. The Mordechai writes that for this reason a creditor does not collect from wages earned by the debtor that had never come into his possession before he died. Darkei Moshe, seif 17, writes that the Ramban rules likewise, unlike the Beit Yosef, who writes that if the document contains an after acquired property clause a creditor may collect from assets that the debtor's sons inherited on account of their father. See there. See also Maharshal's responsum concerning a half male inheritance document that states that the beneficiary receives from both presently held and expected property. He ruled that it is effective even with respect to property that would later fall to the father in law by inheritance after the document was written, and he cites a responsum of Rama that disagreed. Below, siman 107, the Tur rules that a creditor may collect from a loan receivable that the debtor left at death, even though such a receivable is considered expected property with respect to a firstborn son's double portion, as explained in siman 278.
