Before he borrowed, etc. In seif 10 the correct reading is "before he borrowed from both," and the Beit Yosef also writes "before he borrowed from the second." The Ir Shushan explicitly writes "before he borrowed from the other." In the Derishah, however, I wrote that the proper reading is "from both" rather than "from the second." Otherwise, if he acquired the movable property between the two loans, it is analogous to one who borrowed, then acquired property, and then sold it, where the purchaser retains it unless the first lender received a clause covering after acquired property. So too with two loans: if the property was not in the borrower's possession at the time of the first loan but came into his possession before the second, it belongs solely to the second creditor, whose lien is comparable to a sale. I explained this in the Perishah and Derishah based on the Rif and Ramban and proved it elsewhere. I later saw that Baal HaTerumot, followed by the Tur and Mechaber in siman 111:23, uses language like "or before he borrowed from the latter, and he transferred it to the first together with land, the first acquires it." Baal HaTerumot repeatedly assumes that when movable property is assigned together with land, the lender also receives a clause covering after acquired property. The Tur similarly follows him in siman 113. Their reason is that it must be demonstrable that the movable property was in the borrower's possession before both loans, which is difficult to establish. Therefore lenders who require assignment of movable property together with land generally also require a clause covering after acquired property, even if the property is currently in the borrower's possession, so that a later purchaser or creditor cannot claim it was acquired only after the first loan. Accordingly, one can preserve the reading "before he borrowed from the second" if the first lender received an after acquired property clause, for then he has priority even if the borrower acquired the goods between the two loans, even if the second lender also received assignment together with land and such a clause. This likewise explains why the Mechaber in siman 113:3, discussing two loans and property acquired between them where the later creditor seized it, says that it is removed from him if the first creditor had an after acquired property clause. Since the point there is to teach that marketplace protection does not apply between two lenders, one might have expected a simpler case in which the movable property existed before the first loan and was assigned to both together with land. Rather, the practice is to include an after acquired property clause so that the later party cannot claim the goods were acquired between the loans. Even then, of course, the later party can still claim that the goods were acquired only after both loans, in which case they divide, but one takes whatever precautions are available. Nevertheless, if the text is emended to "before he borrowed from both," the matter is clearer. In siman 111, however, one must explain that the first lender received an after acquired property clause, as Baal HaTerumot had stated immediately beforehand.

It is like one who borrowed from one and then another, assigning movable property together with land and including after acquired property, and afterward acquired the property. This is the correct reading. Our editions of the Tur and Baal HaTerumot read "together with land and acquired," which does not read well, since it should simply have said that he borrowed twice and then acquired the property. In the Perishah I therefore suggested the reading: he borrowed from one and then another, assigned movable property together with land and with an after acquired property clause, and afterward acquired the property. This is a greater novelty, because even though he transferred the movable property together with land and included after acquired property, if he acquired it only after both loans there is still no priority between the creditors. The word "and afterward" corresponds to the Aramaic "and then." See the Perishah.
