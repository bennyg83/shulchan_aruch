All on one day. Responsa Amudei Or, siman 104, discusses a case in which Reuven borrowed one maneh from Shimon and on that same day borrowed from Levi an object worth one maneh. Several days later the borrowed object was lost through unavoidable accident while in Reuven's possession. When Shimon's loan became due, both Shimon and Levi sought payment, but Reuven had assets sufficient to satisfy only one of them. At first glance, the result depends upon the dispute between the Rif and Rambam on one side and the Rosh and those who follow him on the other concerning Bava Kamma 112a. According to the Rif and Rambam, who rule that the borrower's property becomes encumbered from the moment of borrowing, the two liens arose on the same day and are equal. According to the Rosh and his school, who rule that the lien arises only when the accident occurs, as the Shulchan Aruch rules in siman 341, seif 4, Shimon's lien arose earlier and he takes precedence. Amudei Or concludes, however, that even according to the Rambam and those who agree with him, the borrower's assets are encumbered from the time of borrowing only by rabbinic law. Thus in this case the lender, whose lien from the date of the loan is Scriptural, takes precedence over the owner of the borrowed object, whose lien from that same day is only rabbinic.
