Many. See Even HaEzer at the end of siman 90 regarding a woman who joined her husband in obligating herself to a second creditor, and see the Beit Yosef here. The Darchei Moshe also discusses at length a case in which a husband first became obligated to Shimon, then he and his wife obligated themselves to Levi and mortgaged land to him, while the wife's ketubah was already secured by that land, and the husband then died, which claimant collects first. Sema. The Shach refers to Responsa Mabit, siman 129, and Responsa Mahari ben Lev, siman 95, and notes that the Ran's ruling depends on the view that an earlier oral loan has no priority. Since the Mechaber rules in seif 13 that an earlier oral loan does have priority, the ruling here that there is no priority among them requires study. See there carefully.
One. Meaning, the dates of the loans were on the same day. The due dates do not matter. Even if witnesses testify that one loan was made first and its note was delivered first, so long as the note itself does not disclose which lien preceded the other, neither lien takes effect before the end of that day, by which time both loans had already been made.
