[Shach, letter 17] To collect from unencumbered property, contrary to Ran. Note: It may simply be said that we rule that they divide because of the other rationale written by Tosafot in the name of Rabbeinu Tam: since both claims are true, the Sages at least enacted that they divide, in order not to close the door before borrowers. See Pnei Yehoshua there, who explains that as a matter of strict law this is a case of monetary uncertainty, and in such circumstances discretionary adjudication is preferable, as in the case of an exchange. A practical difference arises with a document containing a kinyan. According to Ran’s rationale, we rule that beit din uses discretionary adjudication, as Ran states explicitly. According to Rabbeinu Tam’s rationale, however, even there the law is that they divide. Moreover, we must explain the matter this way because it is clear that we do not accept the Rif and Ran’s rationale that the lien begins only at the end of the day, when the effective date can be inferred from the document itself. This is evident from our ruling above, siman 43, seif 23, in the case of Reuven and Shimon. According to that rationale, Shimon ought to collect from Iyar onward, for even if his document dates from the first of Nisan, since it merely says “Nisan,” the lien should not take effect until the end of Nisan, because the exact date is not evident from the document. Although Shimon would in any event be an oral creditor from Nisan and therefore earlier than Reuven, in accordance with the ruling here that an earlier oral loan precedes a later documented loan, this does not solve the problem. Once Reuven has collected, Shimon has no further right in Reuven’s land, because where an oral creditor collected before a documentary creditor, what he collected remains collected. Nor can Shimon say, “I left property available for you,” because even if he now proves that the loan was on the first of Nisan, he cannot dispossess Reuven, who already collected first. It follows necessarily that Shimon is treated as a documentary creditor from the time the document came into his hand, and the reason they divide here is that the case involves monetary uncertainty. Examine this carefully.
