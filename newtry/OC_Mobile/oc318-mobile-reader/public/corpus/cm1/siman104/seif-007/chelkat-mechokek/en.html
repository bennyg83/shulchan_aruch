And likewise if they sold land, etc. See Choshen Mishpat 109:5. Ramban and Rambam hold that only when beit din places property with a creditor by judicial appraisal and errs in the valuation is the sale void. But when beit din sells orphans' property to an ordinary purchaser, the purchaser should not be in a weaker position than the orphans, and there is no ona'ah remedy regarding land. Therefore, if the purchaser was overcharged, even greatly, he cannot retract. If the orphans were underpaid by one-sixth, however, the sale is void, although they may choose to uphold it if the shortfall is returned. According to this, the same should apply here. Only where beit din transferred the property directly to the widow by appraisal would the special judicial error rules apply. If they sold to a purchaser, his law should be that of an ordinary private buyer. The language here does not suggest such a distinction, and it is surprising that it was omitted. Rabbeinu Yonah, Raavad, and Rosh disagree with Ramban and Rambam. See there.
