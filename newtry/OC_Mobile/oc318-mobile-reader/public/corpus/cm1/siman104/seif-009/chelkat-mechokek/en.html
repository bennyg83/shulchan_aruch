Even if they erred by double. But if they erred by more than double, the sale is void. Ramban likewise writes that when the Gemara says that even if they sold property worth one hundred for two hundred the sale stands, this means specifically two hundred, but if the price exceeds double the sale is void. I saw that the Kesef Mishneh writes that perhaps this follows the view that ona'ah applies to land when the disparity exceeds double, as Rama rules in Choshen Mishpat siman 66, seif 38. His words are not clear, for we do not find that Ramban follows the view of Rabbeinu Tam and the Rosh, and the Maggid Mishneh states plainly in chapter 13 of Hilchot Mechirah that there is no ona'ah for land at all, even if property worth one thousand is sold for one dinar. How then could he cite Ramban here as following Rabbeinu Tam's approach? Rather, Ramban certainly wrote this according to all opinions and inferred it from the language of the Mishnah. Although ona'ah does not apply to land when an owner sells his own property, an appraisal error by judges in a place where no public announcement is made is one sixth, while in a place where an announcement is made it is up to double.
