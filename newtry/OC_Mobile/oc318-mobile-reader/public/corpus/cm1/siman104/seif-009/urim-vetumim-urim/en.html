A later creditor cannot, etc. Since beit din sold the property, the purchaser comes through the rights of the earlier creditor and therefore has the same priority as that creditor. The same applies to a widow who is authorized to sell without beit din. Her sale is treated as though made by beit din.
But if the debtor sold it. In that case the purchaser's rights are no stronger than those of the seller, namely the debtor. If the property remained in the debtor's hands, the creditor could collect it, and the same is true in the purchaser's hands. See Tumim. The same applies where a creditor holds a note for two hundred against the debtor, the debtor sells land worth one hundred to a purchaser, and then pays the creditor one hundred. The creditor may still collect the sold land for the remaining hundred even though the hundred he already received came from the sale proceeds. See there.
