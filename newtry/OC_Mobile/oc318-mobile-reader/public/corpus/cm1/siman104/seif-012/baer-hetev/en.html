There the two instruments cannot ordinarily both be given effect because they relate to one specific field. If the second contains an additional benefit, however, both are given effect there as well. Whenever both documents can be sustained, we sustain both. Here too, because a person may borrow twice on one day, we assume that is what occurred. Would we say that a borrower acted foolishly by ordering two notes for one loan? The Sema rejects the Levush's reason that witnesses are presumed not to sign twice on one debt, since that reasoning would apply only where the same witnesses signed both notes. The Shach responds that the Sema's criticism is unwarranted: where different witnesses signed each note, it is obvious that both are collected because witnesses would not sign falsely; even where the same witnesses signed both, both are collected, because we do not suspect that they forgot or erred, and the presumption is that witnesses do not sign twice on one debt. The Levush states this expressly.
