Notes whose dates are all the same. If one creditor holds two promissory notes, see Tumim, who writes that where there are two deeds creating liens, even if there was one loan divided into two notes, the creditor collects according to the number of notes. But with two handwritten obligations, since the writing itself creates no proprietary lien, if there was one loan and it was divided into two writings he receives only one share. See there, where he asks why, with movable property, which is not subject to a property lien, collection should nevertheless follow the number of notes. He answers with difficulty that the mitzvah resting upon the borrower is nevertheless apportioned equally. According to this, it appears that where one loan was made at one time and divided into two promissory notes, even though those notes create a lien, since movable property is not susceptible to such a lien he receives only one share. One loan creates only one debt, just as with an inheritance involving two handwritten obligations.
