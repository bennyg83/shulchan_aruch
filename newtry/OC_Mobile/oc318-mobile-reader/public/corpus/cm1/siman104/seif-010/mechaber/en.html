If several promissory notes bear the same date, or the same hour in a place where hours are recorded, and all their holders come together to collect; or if creditors whose debts arose at different times come together to collect movable property, regarding which there is no priority; or they come to collect land the borrower acquired only after borrowing from the last of them, and there is not enough property to satisfy every debt, the available property is divided among them. How is it divided? If dividing the property equally by the number of creditors gives the smallest creditor no more than the amount of his debt, they divide it equally. If an equal share would exceed the smallest debt, they first divide enough of the total equally to satisfy the smallest creditor. That creditor withdraws, and the remaining creditors divide the balance in the same manner. For example, there are three debts: one hundred, two hundred, and three hundred. If the available property is three hundred, each takes one hundred. The same applies if less than three hundred is available: they divide it equally. If more than three hundred is available, they divide three hundred equally, the creditor owed one hundred withdraws, and the remaining two divide the balance in the same manner. If five hundred or less is available, they divide three hundred equally, the first creditor withdraws, and the remaining two divide the remaining two hundred or less equally, after which the second creditor withdraws. If six hundred is available, they divide three hundred equally, the creditor owed one hundred withdraws, the remaining two divide two hundred equally, the creditor owed two hundred withdraws, and the final one hundred is given to the creditor owed three hundred, who thereby receives three hundred in all. They divide in this manner even if there are one hundred creditors who come to collect together. See note 34.
