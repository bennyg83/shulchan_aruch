They take one hundred each. This rejects Rabbeinu Chananel's view that they divide proportionately to the size of the debts, like the rule concerning a dying person who says, 'One hundred to this person and two hundred to that person,' where an insufficient estate is divided proportionately. There, because the rule depends upon estimating the grantor's intent, we properly assume that he intended the larger beneficiary to receive more and the smaller beneficiary less. Here, however, the reason the creditors divide is that both liens took effect simultaneously. The lien of the creditor owed one hundred attaches to the land just as fully as the lien of the creditor owed two hundred. Until the first creditor receives his entire hundred, his lien is equal in strength, and he therefore shares equally.
Likewise, if there are found, etc. If one creditor has two notes against the same debtor, even if both are dated the same day, see Tumim. I concluded that the primary ruling follows Tzemach Tzedek: division is according to the number of notes. One creditor holding a single note for two hundred receives one share, while another holding two separate notes for one hundred each receives two shares, even if both loans were made at one time and were merely split into two notes. But if the instruments are only handwritten acknowledgments or similar writings that create no property lien, then if the loans were made at different times, division may follow the separate writings. If the loan was made once and merely divided into two portions or two writings, he receives only one share as though the entire debt appeared in one writing. There is no distinction whether the handwritten note names a particular creditor or is payable to any bearer, like the bearer notes commonly used in Poland. The writings do not themselves create the lien, rather the loan does, and that loan occurred at one time. If the instrument is a customary commercial bill governed expressly by commercial law, such as a Wechsel instrument, it requires study whether one should follow the commercial rule and divide according to the amount of each debt, like Rabbeinu Chananel. If six hundred is found there, they divide three hundred equally, etc. The reason they first divide three hundred and then the remainder, rather than immediately dividing the whole amount according to what each can ultimately receive, is that the first three hundred is intermediate property and the remainder inferior property. They first divide the intermediate property, and then divide the inferior property, so that each receives equal treatment within each quality class. See Sema. In Tumim I left unresolved why movable property should not be divided proportionately according to Rabbeinu Chananel's view.
