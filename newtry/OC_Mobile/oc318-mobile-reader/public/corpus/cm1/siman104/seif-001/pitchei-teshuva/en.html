Whoever precedes, etc. Responsa Tzemach Tzedek, siman 109, discusses a man who, before marrying, executed a debt instrument to his wife in the form customarily used in that country for one thousand gold coins. He later died with many creditors. If the wife were to collect the instrument according to its terms, nothing would remain for the creditors. A local enactment provided that where the estate is insufficient to pay both creditors and a wife's ketubah, the wife collects only one hundred gold coins on her ketubah. The question was whether this debt instrument had greater force than an ordinary ketubah and entitled her to priority for its full amount, or whether it too fell within the enactment. The responsum answers that if, as the question implies, she has no ketubah document other than this note, she receives only one hundred gold coins. The same applies if she has a separate ketubah but the debt instrument itself states that it represents the ketubah and its addition. Although the enactment mentions only a ketubah, a debt instrument given in place of a ketubah is necessarily included, for otherwise the enactment would accomplish nothing. But if she has this independent debt instrument against her husband in addition to a ketubah and its addition and she claims both, the independent note is not included in the enactment, for she is no different from any other creditor. She therefore collects the full one thousand gold coins under the independent note, and only one hundred gold coins under the ketubah and addition because of the enactment. See there at length.
