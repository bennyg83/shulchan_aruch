One who has... Kesubos, Perek Mi SheHayah Nasui, 90a and 93b.
Whether movable property. Responsa attributed to Ramban say so, and Tosafos and the Rosh imply it from their resolution of the passage in Erchin. Their explanation that once movable property was actually collected, the collection stands, implies that priority applies initially even to movables. See Rashi and Tosafos in Erchin and the Rosh in Kesubos. The Rif also appears to support this. In general, all poskim compare creditors to purchasers. So long as movable property remains in the debtor's possession, a creditor may collect it, as the Gemara says, "even the cloak from his shoulders."
Even if the time arrived... Erchin 23a concerning one who consecrated his property, even though a ketubah was not intended for immediate collection, and consecration precedes it.
Whether a loan... Kesubos there.
Whether from purchasers... Kesubos 95a.
Even if beit din assigned it to him. Bava Basra 132 regarding one who divided property in her presence, because of the leniencies of ketubah law.
However, when he comes... Yerushalmi, Perek Mi SheHayah Nasui, cited by the Rosh.
He may claim... Bava Basra 112b.
Some say that one may not... Similar to a debtor who sells movable property before the due date, where the creditor cannot protest.
But an oral loan. This is how the Erchin case is resolved. It must follow the view that a loan obligation stated in the Torah is not treated as though written in a document.
Unless he actually collected. Like a purchaser, because one does not collect from an earlier buyer where later property remains. Kesubos 50b.
And if he sold them... rather... Bava Kamma 8b: he may choose from whom to collect. See Rashi, who explains that collection from Shimon is allowed only because land remained in Shimon's possession.
And if he sold them to a non-Jew. Gittin 41a.
Likewise if... Learned from the case where inferior land remained. All the more so movables, which are the debtor's primary means of payment, as stated in Kesubos 86a.
