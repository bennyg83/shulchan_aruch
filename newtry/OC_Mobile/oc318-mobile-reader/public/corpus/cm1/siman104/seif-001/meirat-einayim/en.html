Whoever's document acquired its lien first has priority in collection, whether from land or movable property, etc. The Mechaber's words require explanation, because there is no priority among creditors with respect to movable property, as the Tur and Mechaber explicitly state below in seif 10. Although the Ramban writes in a responsum cited by the Beit Yosef that only where the later creditor already collected movable property do we say it is not taken from him, but if both creditors come simultaneously the later creditor receives nothing in the presence of the earlier creditor, the other authorities do not accept this. I have discussed this at length in a responsum and demonstrated from the Ramban's language that he did not write this as a final ruling but only as a possible position. Darkei Moshe likewise writes in seif 10 that we do not rule like that Ramban, and so too Baal HaTerumot and many other authorities. It is strained to say that the Mechaber refers to movable property assigned together with land, since he does not discuss that until seif 3. Rama's following gloss also proves otherwise, because he writes that if the later creditor collects, the earlier creditor can no longer remove the property from him. If the movable property had been transferred together with land, the earlier creditor could recover it, because between an earlier and later creditor there is no marketplace protection, as the Beit Yosef and Mechaber explain in simanim 60 and 111. The Mechaber's conclusion, "whether from the borrower himself or from purchasers," is likewise difficult, because movable property cannot ordinarily be seized from purchasers, and even where it was transferred together with land, marketplace protection prevents seizure from purchasers, as stated in siman 60 and below in simanim 111 and 113. Furthermore, he begins with both land and movable property but later says, "If the later creditor went first and collected land," implying that the latter rule is limited to land. It therefore appears that the Mechaber's principal purpose here is merely to teach that priority depends upon the time the lien was acquired, not upon the earlier due date. In this respect land and movable property are alike: even with movable property we do not say that the creditor whose repayment date arrived first takes it alone. His formulation should be understood thus: whoever's document acquired its lien first is, under Torah law, first to collect from land or movable property, even from purchasers, since we hold that liens are of Torah origin. The practical consequence after rabbinic enactments is at least that a later creditor whose payment date arrived first does not thereby gain priority, and there is also a practical application today where movable property was assigned together with land. But this latter application is not the Mechaber's main purpose. After stating the Torah principle, he proceeds to the law under the rabbinic enactment and therefore says, "If the later creditor went first and collected land," thereby excluding movable property. He may also have mentioned land because he wishes to state the further novelty that even if the later creditor collected it in the earlier creditor's presence and the earlier creditor remained silent, the earlier creditor may still recover it. A creditor is unlikely to remain silent when another creditor takes movable property before him, for fear that it will be hidden or lost, unlike land. This is unlike the Ir Shushan, who understood the opening words to mean that the rule of no priority in movable property applies only after the later creditor has already collected, but if both appear together the earlier creditor takes first. That position seems to have arisen from the Mechaber's wording and from the Beit Yosef's citation of the Ramban, but I have shown that the Ramban himself did not rule that way.
