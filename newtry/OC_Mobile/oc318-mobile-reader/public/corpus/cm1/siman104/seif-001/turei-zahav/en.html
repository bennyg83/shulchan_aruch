(Seif 1). When the senior creditor comes to collect, the borrower cannot object that perhaps the later creditor will damage the field, as we are concerned in siman 98. Responsa Rashba, responsum 1,118, states that we fear damage to land only where there is reason to suspect that the note is forged or has already been paid, as in siman 98, where the borrower claims that the note is forged. The wording “but if the borrower,” etc., implies that even before the borrower has transferred anything, the senior creditor cannot prevent him from giving property to the later creditor; only beit din should not facilitate such conduct, lest others do likewise and cause harm. This is indeed Rashba's ruling in responsum 1,111. There, however, Rashba follows the view of Ran and does not accept that a creditor may restrain a person who is dissipating his assets even before the debt is due. Since our law allows such restraint, as stated in siman 73, the senior creditor should certainly be able to prevent the borrower from giving the property to the later creditor. Rama's ruling therefore requires study. Responsa Maimoniyot, Mishpatim, section 41, discusses a case in which the creditor himself seized the property; see there.
