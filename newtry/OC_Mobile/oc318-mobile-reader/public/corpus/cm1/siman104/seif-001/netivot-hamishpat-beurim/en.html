Whether land or movable property. See Sema, seif katan 1. Tumim writes that movable property is subject to a creditor only by virtue of the debtor's general obligation, and because a creditor ordinarily does not rely specifically on movable property, there is no ordinary rule of priority among creditors regarding it. Therefore, if a note expressly pledges land and movables, even without agav, the creditor has shown that he relied on the movables. Tumim argues that this creates priority at least against other creditors, as seen from the fact that such property can be collected from heirs in certain circumstances. However, if a later creditor already collected, he resembles a purchaser, and the earlier creditor cannot recover the movables from him without an agav lien. Tumim proves this from Tosafot at the beginning of Perek Mi SheHaya Nasui in Ketubot, who establish the case with movable property and nevertheless recognize a form of priority. According to what appears from Sema in siman 107, however, even where the note says "land and movables," if it does not add language extending the lien during life and after death, the creditor does not collect movables from heirs. It would therefore appear that priority likewise does not arise without agav. Ketzot HaChoshen writes that so long as the creditor does not demand payment there is not even a mitzvah to repay. This is certainly incorrect. Even if the creditor forgot the debt and makes no demand, the debtor remains obligated to pay. This is unlike a vow or oath whose obligation may depend on the promisor's intent when he formulated it. The mitzvah to repay a creditor is imposed by the Torah and does not disappear because the creditor has not yet demanded.
There are those who say that with property not presently in existence, and so forth. This qualification applies only to the concluding case involving an oath. As to the absence of priority in property acquired in the future, all opinions agree.
