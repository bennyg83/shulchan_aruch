Whether land or movable property. See Tumim, where I clarified that there is essentially no dispute among the authorities. With ordinary movable property there is no priority at all. Even initially beit din does not award it to the earlier creditor, but the creditors divide it, as stated below. If the note expressly encumbers movable property together with land, everyone agrees that even if the debtor sold it, the earlier creditor may collect it from the purchaser, and its law is like land. If the note mentions both movable property and land but does not bind the movable property through agav, the creditor may collect it from heirs by virtue of the note. Initially, when multiple creditors seek collection, the earlier documented creditor therefore has priority. But because there is no agav, the lien lacks publicity. If the debtor sells the property, the creditor cannot recover it from the purchaser, and if a later creditor already collected it, the earlier creditor cannot remove it from him, because that collection is equivalent to a sale. This is the case addressed by the Mechaber here. It was common to write notes in this form, mentioning movable property and land, as Rambam records. Therefore the Mechaber first gives the general rule that earlier creditors collect first from both land and movable property. But when discussing a later creditor who already collected, he specifies land, implying that if the later creditor collected movable property it is not removed from him. This distinction is clear.
Whether from the borrower or from purchasers. This refers to land. Movable property is not collected from purchasers, as the Mechaber concludes, and if a later creditor who collected it cannot be displaced, certainly a purchaser cannot be. The Mechaber merely used a general expression.
And if the later creditor collected first, etc. With movable property it is not removed from him, because his collection is treated as a sale, as explained.
Lest he conceal them, etc. This refers to a note expressly encumbering movable property together with land, where the earlier creditor could recover it from a later creditor because the market protection enactment does not protect a later secured creditor. The creditor seeks attachment because he fears the debtor will conceal the property.
But an oral loan, etc. See what I write below in seif 13 at length.
And if he sold them to a non-Jew. See what I wrote above in siman 107 at length.
Some say that with something not yet, etc. The phrase 'some say' does not refer to the basic rule concerning property not yet in existence. There is no dispute that if the debtor did not expressly encumber after acquired property, there is no priority in it, for we rule that if one borrows twice and afterward acquires property, the creditors divide because the property did not yet exist. If he expressly encumbered after acquired property, everyone agrees that priority applies, since we rule that an after acquired property clause creates a lien. Rather, the phrase 'some say' refers to the concluding issue, whether an oath taken by the debtor creates an additional obligation. On that point the authorities disagree. See what I wrote above in siman 99.
