Into the lender's wife's hand. Although one who deposits property generally does so with the understanding that the custodian's wife and children may handle it, a borrower is different because all the benefit is his. See what I wrote above in siman 291 and in siman 72.
He is like a drunk guard. Dhuil and she enjoyed her in the days of her query, which would be described to her at least when she came near her husband.
For it had already fallen before the orphans, etc. See above in siman 122 regarding agency, where the Tur and Mechaber rule that one should not pay even to the claimant's agent with witnesses without a power of attorney, because perhaps the claimant died and the right fell to the orphans.
And what he writes, 'if he sent it, there is doubt whether he is liable.' This is the language of Mordechai. See siman 122:7, where the Mechaber writes that even with a power of attorney, if the principal dies the authorization can fail unless the claim itself was transferred through a valid kinyan.
