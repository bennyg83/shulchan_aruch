When a borrower returns the borrowed item to its owner, if he sends it through another person and it dies before reaching the lender's possession, the borrower is liable because the item remains under his responsibility. Some say the same applies if he returned it to the lender's wife and it was lost by unavoidable accident. Beit Yosef, siman 340, seif 2, in the name of Rashba. See above, siman 72, seif 31, and the end of siman 120, and siman 291, seif 21. If he sent it through another person with the lender's consent and it died, he is exempt. If he sent it through the lender's Canaanite slave, then even if the lender told him, "Send it," and it died on the way, the borrower remains liable because the slave's hand is legally the hand of his master and the item has not yet left the borrower's responsibility. This applies when he returns it during the term of the loan. If he returns it after the loan term has ended, the strict liability of a borrower has ceased and he is treated as a paid custodian. The same is true if the item remains in the borrower's house after the loan term has expired: he is no longer a borrower and is treated as a paid custodian. Rama: If the lender told him to send it but died before the borrower managed to send it, he should not send it, because the property now belongs before the heirs. If he nevertheless sends it, it is uncertain whether he remains liable. Mordechai, beginning of chapter HaShoel.
