(Seif 1: Even if it is lighter, etc.) The Maggid Mishneh derives this from the Gemara's case of a borrowed axe that broke, where Rava required witnesses that the borrower made no change. The unqualified word “change” includes every deviation, even from heavier to lighter use. The Sema limits liability to a change that may have caused the loss, but that contradicts the Maggid Mishneh's inference. If causation were required, there would be no source for liability when changing to lighter use. The correct rule is that any deviation creates liability after the object is lost. During the loan, the borrower may ordinarily use the item for a lighter task and the lender cannot object, just as a renter may do. Once the object is destroyed, however, the lender may say that his stipulation was not followed and that he never accepted the risk of any altered use. This resembles the rule concerning a particular ship: before loss, some substitutions may be harmless, but once the ship sinks, the party may rely upon the exact designation and demand the legal consequence. Thus the lender's insistence gains force after the loss. The borrower is liable even where the change plainly did not cause the destruction. The Sema himself appears to accept this principle elsewhere.
