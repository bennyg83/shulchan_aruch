Specifically where the accident occurred because of the borrower, etc. See the Ir Shushan, who writes that if the accident occurred independently of the borrower, such as one who borrowed a house and it burned accidentally, the borrower is exempt. This is imprecise. In such a case he is exempt in any event because the laws of bailees do not apply to slaves and land, as the Ir Shushan himself writes in siman 301, seif 1.
It goes without saying that he is liable for theft and loss. In the Perishah I cited the Gemara in chapter HaShoel, which specifically needed to derive a borrower's liability for theft and loss by an a fortiori argument from a paid bailee. We could not simply derive it from the Torah's explicit liability of a borrower for an animal that breaks a limb or dies, because one might have said that liability applies only to breaking or death, which the owner cannot undo, whereas with theft or loss the owner could exert himself to pursue the thief and recover the property. The ruling therefore teaches otherwise. This is why the Tur and Mechaber explicitly write "it goes without saying," rather than relying on the intuitive a fortiori inference from liability for accidents.
Rather, even if it died, etc. Since the borrower did not deviate from the authorized use and performed the work with the owner's permission, the owner caused his own loss by lending an animal for labor it could not withstand.
Even if it is lighter. See above, simanim 308 and 311, where the Rosh and Tur conclude that a renter may change an animal to a different type of work that is not heavier, and all the more so to lighter work. One cannot say that the law is stricter for a borrower because he receives all the benefit, for there is no reason for such a distinction where the animal died because of the work. The Torah exempts a borrower when the animal dies because of its work. How would this differ from the Tur and Mechaber's ruling in siman 342, concerning a borrower who lends the item to another person and it is damaged through the work, that if the first borrower can prove that the second did not deviate from the use for which he had authorization, the first borrower is exempt? It therefore appears that when the Mechaber writes here not to change even to lighter work, he means that if he did change even in a slight manner, and there is some concrete circumstance before us through which the death can plausibly be attributed to that change, we attribute the death to it. This resembles what the Tur and Mechaber write at the end of siman 309, seif 2: if one rents an animal to take it to a specified place and takes it elsewhere, and the climate differs, we attribute the death to that change. Similarly, in siman 291 concerning negligence followed by accident, an unpaid bailee is liable where there is even a remote basis to connect the accident to the negligence. Examine this carefully.
Even in the very same type of work it is possible to deviate. Meaning, such a deviation is considered sufficient to create liability where the damage can be attributed to it.
