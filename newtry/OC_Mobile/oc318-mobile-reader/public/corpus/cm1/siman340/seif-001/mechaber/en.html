A borrower is liable for unavoidable accidents, the law of an animal that dies from its work, and delivery through a child or servant. It contains eight seifim.
One who borrows an animal or movable property is liable if it is unavoidably lost, dies, breaks, or is captured. Rama: This applies only when the accident arose through the borrower. If it arose through the lender, he is exempt. Thus, one who borrowed collateral held by a non-Jew in order to obtain an additional loan is exempt if the collateral burned, because it would have burned through the lender's situation regardless. Certainly he is liable for theft or loss. But damage caused by the work for which it was borrowed is exempt, even death from that work, provided he did not use it for another purpose. If he changed the use, even to a lighter task, we attribute the death to the deviation. Even within the same type of work, excessive use, such as plowing day and night contrary to ordinary practice, is a deviation.
