One who borrows an animal. The defining feature of a borrower is that all the benefit belongs to him. If the owner receives even a small benefit from the arrangement, the borrower no longer has the full law of a borrower for unavoidable accidents, because the owner's benefit makes the arrangement resemble rental. Although less than a perutah is not enough to create the ordinary status of a paid custodian, it can still suffice to prevent the arrangement from being one in which all benefit belongs exclusively to the borrower.
He swears the custodial oath that the animal weakened because of the work. This is difficult according to the Shulchan Aruch's view that the principal custodial oath is that the object is no longer in the custodian's possession, while the oath concerning accident arises only through gilgul. Here the animal itself is returned intact, though weakened. There is no principal oath that it is not in his possession. Why then should he be required to swear specifically that the weakening was caused by the work?
