The Sema also writes this, and it is the primary ruling, for this is the view of Rabbeinu Chananel, Tosafot, the Maggid Mishneh, the Ran, and Nimukei Yosef. It appears that even the Rosh ruled otherwise only because he follows his own view that once the owners withdraw, the bailee becomes responsible. This is explicitly implied by his words in chapter HaShoel, cited by the Beit Yosef at the beginning of siman 307. According to the opinion there that the bailee is not liable until he performs meshichah, the same applies here. Accordingly, the Rama is difficult: here he rules without qualification like the Mechaber, while above he cites the Rosh's opinion. This is also explicit in the Maggid Mishneh and Nimukei Yosef cited by me in siman 291, seif 1: according to the opinion that striking the animal with a stick to make it move suffices to create liability, ordinary cases all the more require meshichah. See there. This requires study. Nimukei Yosef further writes in the name of the Ran that even if the borrower explicitly says, "Strike it with a stick and I will become liable to you," he remains exempt. The Beit Yosef and Darkei Moshe cite this. Shach.
