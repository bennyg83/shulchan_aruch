Into the hand of the borrower's agent. Tur explains this as including his hired worker, household dependent, and an agent formally appointed before witnesses. The detailed rules of agency are explained in siman 121.
If the borrower told him, "Send it to me." Shitah Mekubetzet cites Ran's question: how can the messenger become the borrower's agent when the borrower never spoke to him? He appears to be the lender's agent, not the borrower's. One answer is that the borrower's words are treated as though he said to the lender, "Tell him in my name to bring it to me." Once the lender conveys that instruction and the messenger accepts it, he becomes the borrower's agent through the lender's appointment. Ran gives another answer: the borrower's liability rests on guarantor law. It is like saying, "Give one hundred to so-and-so and I will owe you." Whenever a person removes money from his own possession at another's instruction, the instructing party can become liable as guarantor. At first glance the guarantor explanation is difficult. In the ordinary case of "give one hundred to so-and-so," the owner fully removes the money from his control because of the other person's request, so the other party is liable. Here, if the animal later dies naturally, what difference does it make whether it died here or there? One cannot say the lender's property was damaged merely because he sent it. The answer is that at least with respect to guarding the borrower became liable once the lender removed his own control at the borrower's instruction. Had the animal simply been lost, the borrower would certainly be liable as custodian. Once he has the status of an unpaid custodian, he can accept borrower-level liability, because an unpaid custodian may stipulate to have the law of a borrower. There are practical differences between the two explanations. According to the agency explanation, the borrower also acquires through the messenger. The lender can no longer retract after the object reaches that messenger, and the messenger must be legally capable of agency. Thus if the borrower said, "Send it to me through a deaf mute, mentally incompetent person, minor, or non Jew," accident liability would not arise through this mechanism. According to the guarantor explanation, liability can arise even through someone legally incapable of agency, because the lender acted at the borrower's instruction. Conversely, the lender could still retract before final delivery because the messenger is not functioning as the borrower's acquisition agent.
