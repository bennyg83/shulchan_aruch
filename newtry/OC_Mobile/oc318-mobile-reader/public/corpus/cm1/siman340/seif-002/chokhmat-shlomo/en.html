Seif 3. One who borrows an animal from another, etc. Note: See the Sema and Shach below, seif 4, regarding their distinction in the laws of a borrower, and the Shach's statement that the issue depends on the underlying dispute. In my work on Bava Kamma, Sefer Milei DeNezikin, page 4b, on the Gemara's statement, 'but the gratuitous custodian and the borrower have already been taught,' I proved from the Gemara's inclusion of the borrower that the Mechaber and those who agree with him are correct that the borrower too can be exempt in that case, because the example can be one where the borrower asserts a robber's claim. However, Rashi there writes that 'borrower' was mentioned only incidentally, implying the view of the Rama and Shach that a borrower is not exempt in that manner. Rashba's Chiddushim there likewise support the Rama. See Rashi carefully and understand this well.
