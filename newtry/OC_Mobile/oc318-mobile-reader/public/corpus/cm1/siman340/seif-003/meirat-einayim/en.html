And the mice ate it, etc. The Gemara's first version speaks this way. The Tur cites the Rosh that even if a cat ate many mice, became overheated, and died, the borrower is exempt because it is considered death through the work for which it was borrowed. Although it was borrowed to chase mice rather than eat them, since its death resulted from that activity it counts as death through work. Terumat HaDeshen appears to treat death from overeating mice as even more clearly work related than a case where the mice gathered upon it while it was chasing them. Likewise, if one borrowed weapons to fight enemies and the city was conquered and the enemies took the weapons, that too is considered loss through the work for which they were borrowed.
