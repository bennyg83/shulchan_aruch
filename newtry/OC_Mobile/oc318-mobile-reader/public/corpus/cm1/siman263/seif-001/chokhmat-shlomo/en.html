Seif 2. If his practice is to return such objects, etc. Note: See the Rosh, who questions Rif for treating the matter as an unresolved question and ruling that one should not return the object in the city. The Rosh assumes Rif had a different textual reading. I do not think this is necessary. Rif can follow our text. In the field, where the scholar ordinarily does take such an object, returning it does not presently degrade Torah honor, so because of the possible mitzvah of returning lost property we rule stringently and require him to take it. Once he reaches the city, the doubt points in the other direction. Perhaps he was never obligated to take it even in the field, and carrying it publicly in the city would then be an unnecessary degradation of Torah honor. Thus he may leave it in the city. The concern that he caused others to rely on him does not apply here for the reason given by the Rosh. This resolves Rif without changing the text.
