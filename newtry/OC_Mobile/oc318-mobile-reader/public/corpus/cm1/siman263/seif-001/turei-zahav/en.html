(Seif 2, rather, he should bring them in from the field.) According to Rosh’s version, this is an unresolved question in the Gemara. Do we say that a complete return is required, and since it is not his way to return such an item in the city he is not obligated at all? Or perhaps he is at least obligated in the field, and once obligated in the field he is also obligated in the city? The Gemara leaves the matter unresolved. Thus the doubt is whether he is exempt entirely, even in the field, or obligated entirely, even in the city. Rosh writes that we rule leniently because the stringent side entails degradation of Torah honor, requiring a scholar to demean himself by the burden of returning the object. The Tur understands that since the exemption rests only on degradation, he is at least obligated in the field, where there is no such degradation. One cannot object that once he obligates himself in the field he should thereby become obligated in the city. Since returning it in the field, where it is less protected than in the city, is independently a mitzvah, as Rosh explains according to Rif, one may say that what he does there is merely beyond the strict law, because he has done some kindness for his fellow. This appears correct. It also resolves Beit Yosef’s objection that the Tur was imprecise in writing that he must return it in the city. Nor can one ask how Rosh knew that Rif had a different textual version. Perhaps Rif had the same version and merely ruled by his own reasoning according to the distinction just stated. The answer is that had Rif written this as his own resolution, he should have mentioned that returning it in the city is prohibited because of degradation, as Rosh does. Instead Rif writes, “therefore he should not return it in the city,” referring back to the Gemara’s unresolved question. That question concerns only obligation or exemption. Accordingly, “he should not return” likewise means that he is exempt, not that it is prohibited.
