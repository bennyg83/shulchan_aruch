In the mitzvah. The Rambam likewise uses this language in chapter 11 of Hilchot Gezelah. The Ran and Nimukei Yosef infer from this reason that the rule is not limited to an animal whose manner of walking in the wild has already been changed, but also applies to other objects, because the reason that he began performing the mitzvah applies to them as well. Others disagree. The Beit Yosef concludes that the Rambam's primary intent is likewise that the rule applies specifically to an animal. His meaning is that since the finder began the mitzvah, if he does not complete it the initial act will cause loss to the owner because he accustomed the animal to walking in the wild. But with other objects that he ordinarily would not tend to, even if he began tending to them he may return and leave them, since his beginning the mitzvah caused no loss to the owner. See there. It is surprising that the Mechaber here simply adopted the Rambam's wording, which can lead to error. Ibid.
