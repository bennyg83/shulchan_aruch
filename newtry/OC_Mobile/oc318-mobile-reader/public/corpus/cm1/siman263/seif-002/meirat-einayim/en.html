Even though he already entered the city with them, etc. This is an unresolved question in the Gemara: once he began the return, must he complete the mitzvah fully or not? The Mechaber quotes Rambam, who rules stringently. Rama cites the alternative view of Rif, the Rosh, and the Tur. In the Derishah I explained that they did not reach their result simply by treating a monetary doubt leniently out of concern for the scholar's honor. The Rosh and Tur object to Rambam that if that were the reason he should at least pay the value of the object from his own pocket. Rather, they follow Rif's textual version, according to which the Gemara's question was only whether such a person is exempt even in the field, since he cannot complete the return properly. In the city itself it is clear that he need not return the object even if he found it in the field and it was ordinarily beneath his dignity only in the city. See there.
Since he began the mitzvah, etc. Rambam likewise uses this language in chapter 11 of Hilchot Gezeilah. Ran and Nimukei Yosef infer that the rule is not limited to an animal whose habits were changed by his striking or moving it, but applies to other objects as well, because the stated reason is simply that he began the mitzvah. Rambam writes the case of an animal because that is the Gemara's formulation and to teach the stronger point that even if he did no more than strike the animal and move it from its place, he must continue tending it and return it. Rashi likewise holds this way. Others disagree. The Beit Yosef, after citing the Nimukei Yosef and Ran's understanding of Rambam and noting their difficulty from Bava Batra 88, offers a reconciliation of Rambam in Darkei Moshe. He ultimately writes, however, that Rambam himself appears to mean specifically an animal. His phrase "since he began the mitzvah" means: since he began, if he does not finish, his initial act will itself cause loss to the owner because he altered the animal's path or behavior. With other objects that he does not ordinarily handle, even if he began the return he may stop and leave them, since his initial involvement caused no loss. It is surprising that the Mechaber here copied Rambam's broad wording without clarification, leaving room for misunderstanding.
