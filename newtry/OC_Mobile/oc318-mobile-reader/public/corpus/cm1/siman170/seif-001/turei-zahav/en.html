(Seif 2: they irrigate in order.) This is difficult, because the Gemara states that regarding drawing water as the river flows naturally, everyone agrees, and the dispute concerns damming the water. Rashi explains that if they do not dam the river but simply draw from it while it continues in its natural course, everyone may draw. Tosafos explain even more broadly that even if the river may thereby dry up, anyone who wishes may draw so long as the river continues naturally. Our master's wording, however, implies that the upper field has priority and may prevent the lower fields from drawing. The Ran's wording is similarly difficult, as he writes that where the water flows naturally everyone agrees that the upper owner drinks first. Moreover, in his abridged rulings our master explicitly writes that where a channel passes along the fields, one owner cannot prevent another from irrigating at the same time. It therefore appears that our master and the Ran mean only that the lower owners have no power to prevent the upper owners from drawing first. The river follows its natural course, and the upper owners may draw as it reaches them. Certainly, if a lower owner wishes to walk upward and draw water and carry it to his own field, he may do so. It is simply not the ordinary practice to draw before the water reaches one's field. The novelty is that even if the upper owner, by drawing first in the ordinary manner, may cause the water to cease before it reaches the lower field, the lower owner cannot prevent him. This is what our master means by saying they irrigate in order and the lower owners cannot object.
