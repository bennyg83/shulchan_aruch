(See there: And the cistern that is near, etc.) The Tur concludes: “He may fill his cistern, and need not close its opening, because of the ways of peace.” This implies that by strict law he would have to close it. Yet immediately afterward we rule that the stronger party prevails. It appears that “the stronger party prevails” applies only to the amount presently needed, as the Tur says, “until he waters first.” Here, however, the person fills a pit with more water than he presently needs, and this extra privilege is granted only because of the ways of peace. Accordingly, the Tur agrees with the opinion cited by the Rama that this law applies specifically when the cistern preceded the irrigation channel, because the owner seeks to fill it with more than his current need. To draw only the amount presently needed, however, he may certainly dig a channel initially in order to bring the water to himself. This appears clear to me.
