They irrigate in order. In the Perishah I explained the Tur's wording, which also clarifies the Mechaber's abbreviated quotation of Rambam. When the Mechaber says, 'the people along the river irrigate in order,' he means where an irrigation channel runs past the fields and each draws from it as the water reaches him. Everyone irrigates in sequence, and the lower owner cannot prevent the upper owner from filling from the channel and irrigating first, even where the nature of the river is that upstream drawing may exhaust the water before it reaches below. The upper owner, however, cannot object to a lower owner's dam once the water has passed the upper field. The Gemara records a dispute. Rav says lower owners irrigate first through damming, because they may tell the upper owners, 'Let the river flow naturally; you have no right to dam it until we irrigate.' A lower dam does not harm the upper owners after water has already passed them, while an upper dam harms those below. Shmuel says lower owners cannot prevent the upper owners because proximity to the source gives the upper ones a prior claim. Since each side has a substantial rationale, the Gemara rules that whoever is stronger prevails. From Rambam, Tur, and Mechaber's wording, 'whoever prevails,' it appears that if middle owners prevail, they can prevent upper owners from damming and also keep water from descending to lower owners until they themselves irrigate. Although according to either Rav or Shmuel the uppermost or lowermost parties have a stronger theoretical claim than the middle, because no final halacha was decided, the middle party can answer each side with the opinion favoring him. To the upper one he can say the halacha follows Rav, according to whom lower position has priority; to the lower one he can say the halacha follows Shmuel, according to whom upper position has priority. Since the Gemara ultimately leaves the matter to strength, strength of legal argument can function like physical strength. Although Abaye said to Rav Shimi, 'You acted for me like two opposing authorities,' and refrained from benefiting from the produce, Rambam and the Tur apparently understand this as pious conduct rather than a strict legal requirement, unlike Tosafos. And the river passes through it in its ordinary course. The Tur says so. In the Derishah I wrote that Rif and Rambam do not appear to require this; even if the channel runs merely beside the pit, they permit diversion into it and filling first for the sake of peaceful relations. The Mechaber follows them. Raavad, Maggid Mishneh, and Beit Yosef explain their view. The straightforward Gemara, however, appears like the Tur. Rama therefore inserted on the Mechaber's words, 'and the river passes through it in its ordinary course.' It is difficult that he wrote this as an explanation of the Mechaber rather than as a dissenting 'some say.' Ir Shushan's presentation of this seif is confused and difficult to understand.
