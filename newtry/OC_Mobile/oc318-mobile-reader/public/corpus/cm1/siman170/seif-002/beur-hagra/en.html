They irrigate in order. This is the case where the water flows naturally. Ran explains that all agree the upper owner drinks first. This is also the plain meaning of the Gemara's phrase, 'all agree,' indicating that one concedes to the other, as it generally does. Rambam and the Tur likewise read this way, unlike Sema and Shach. The Gemara did not answer by saying that the water flows naturally because the obvious question, 'what does this teach us?' would remain. See Rashi, s.v. ve'ashkei.
If one wished, etc. Meaning, where two people seek to irrigate. If all of them come, the middle owner does not acquire priority either way, as stated there. See Tosafot, s.v. kavei. This is why it says, 'if one wished,' etc., and 'another,' etc., unlike Sema.
