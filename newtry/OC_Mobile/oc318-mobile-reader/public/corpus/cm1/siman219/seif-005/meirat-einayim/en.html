He must write that Reuven is the boundary on two sides. Otherwise he acquires only half. The Tur further writes that if the eastern boundary is long and the western short, while the northern boundary is long and the southern short, we divide the parcel with two “heads of a row.” According to the accurate manuscripts, the buyer receives two protruding triangular extensions, one toward the south because the seller also marked the southern boundary, and one toward the west because he also marked the western boundary. The land between those protrusions is not given to the buyer. Some editions of the Tur have a different text, and the Beit Yosef and Ir Shushan follow that version and draw a diagram accordingly. I find that version difficult, because it has no source in the Gemara or poskim. The version I have stated follows naturally from the preceding rule: if one long and one short boundary produces one triangular extension, then two long and two short boundaries produce two. In the Perishah I recorded further questions concerning the competing text and diagram. I also cited my teacher's alternative diagram, which appears to presuppose the first and more accurate version, though I did not fully understand his intended drawing.
