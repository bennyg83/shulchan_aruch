“The eastern boundary is the land from which it was divided.” Meaning that the deed says: “I sell such and such field to so and so. On its west is Reuven, on its north Shimon, on its south Levi, and on its east is the land from which it was divided.” This means that the field that I divided off from the field being sold lies on its east and forms its eastern boundary. The same rule applies if he identified only the western boundary and wrote regarding the east, “the field divided from mine,” because identifying east and west alone can suffice. Or he may write instead, “the land I cut off from it,” meaning the field that I retained for myself and separated from the field sold, which forms its eastern boundary. In either version he did not specify how much he sold and it is also unknown how much he retained for himself.
