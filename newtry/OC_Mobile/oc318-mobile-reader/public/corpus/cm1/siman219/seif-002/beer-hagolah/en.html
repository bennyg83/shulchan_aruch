Gimel) This is the language of the Rambam, chapter 21 of Hilkhot Mekhirah, halachah 15. Rava rules there on Bava Batra 69b that the buyer acquires everything except, etc.
One explanation is that rows of trees are interspersed among the other trees. Another explanation is densely planted palm trees. See Arukh.
Dalet) There in the Gemara. The Maggid Mishneh writes in the name of Rabbenu Shmuel that the excluded area is not enclosed between the two boundary lines on either side, but extends along both of them. If it is enclosed within the boundaries, the buyer acquires everything inside them. Rambam's wording inclines to this view. All the distinctions are explained in the Gemara and Rif. Nine kav is the relevant measure because that is a significant independent field. The same applies to a garden with half a kav, and to every property according to the measure at which it is independently divisible, as explained in siman 171. A stand of palm trees is likewise independently significant.
Heh) There in the Gemara: shuda dedayanei. The Kesef Mishneh explains, following Rashi in HaKotev 85b, that the judges assess the intent of the seller or giver. Rashbam likewise explains that the judges consider whether the seller is known to be generous or restrictive in his disposition.
