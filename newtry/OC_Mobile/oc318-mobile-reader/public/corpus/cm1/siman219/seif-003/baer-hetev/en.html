Like the head of an ox. Had he intended otherwise, he should have written, 'The boundary of Reuven is on the east and the boundary of Levi on the west.' Instead, he wrote that the fields of Reuven and Shimon form the eastern boundary. This certainly indicates that he intended to convey a strip on the eastern side as wide as the combined widths of Reuven's and Shimon's fields adjacent to his field. Since he described a shorter western boundary, we say that he intended to give him land shaped like the head of an ox, meaning diagonally from the end of the long eastern boundary toward the shorter western boundary. The term 'tor' means a row laid diagonally relative to the other rows. Ibid. See Ramban's novellae on this matter.
