He did not buy from the long time as a friend of the schist on the bottom and said that they had expanded to him and had no intention to give him either from the east, but against a short one who had trouble him in the West:
If they belonged to two people, as in this case. If the seller could have written, “Reuven and Shimon,” or separately identified Reuven's boundary on the east and Levi's on the west, but instead wrote that Reuven and Shimon form the eastern boundary, it is clear that he intended to convey land on the east corresponding to the combined width of the fields of Reuven and Shimon. Since he marked only a shorter western boundary, we say that his intent was to give the buyer a triangular extension, a “head of a row,” running diagonally from the end of the long eastern boundary toward the shorter western boundary. The word “tor” means a row arranged diagonally relative to the ordinary rows. See the Perishah.
He acquires diagonally like the head of a row. Rama adds the word “diagonally” according to the alternative opinion, but did not use it in the first opinion stated by the Mechaber. This may be because the Mechaber follows the Rambam, and in chapter 24 of Hilchot Kilayim the Rambam appears to understand “head of a row” somewhat differently. He explains it as the name of a triangular ornament worn by women, from the verse “we shall make for you ornaments of gold,” and therefore any pointed triangular corner of a shape is called a “head of a row.” In chapter 13 of Hilchot Mekhirah, however, Maimonides illustrates this law with four boundaries where one is shorter. I do not know the source of that illustration.
