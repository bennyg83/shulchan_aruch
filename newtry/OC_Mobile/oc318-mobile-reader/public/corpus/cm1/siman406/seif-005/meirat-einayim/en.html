The Sages likewise did not institute appointment of a guardian merely to provide supervision and collect from the orphans after an initial goring. Only after the ox has gored three times did the Sages of the Mishnah institute appointment of a guardian, and if it then gores another three times before the guardian after proper warnings, its damages are collected from the superior property of the orphans. The Tur cites this. The Mechaber omits it, and Rama likewise makes no mention of a guardian. It appears that the reason is that this law is not practically applicable nowadays, since there is no formal muad status in Babylonia, as stated in seif 11. The Tur included it to explain the Gemara, and also for the rare case where the ox became muad in Eretz Yisrael, came outside Eretz Yisrael, caused damage, and the injured party seized it first, in which case it is not removed from his possession. Because this situation is uncommon, the later codifiers omitted it. The Ir Shushan creates a stumbling block for readers by writing that, out of concern for property so that the principal not be consumed, the Sages did not appoint a guardian to their detriment in order to pay the injured party. This is incorrect, because after three gorings they do appoint a guardian, as explained. If he means that the concern of consuming principal applies only while the ox is a tam and payment comes from the body of the ox, whereas after becoming muad the reason no longer applies, Tosafos themselves reject that reasoning. See Derishah.
