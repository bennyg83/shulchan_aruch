One who has a cistern containing collected water or spring water. This refers to one who acquired the water source from the house owner generally, or received it in a division, with an express condition at the time of division that he have access through his fellow's house.
Only during the day. We presume the house owner did not consent to rise from bed at night to open for him.
Each makes a lock, etc. Meaning, each installs a fastening on the cistern door. The Gemara explains that the cistern owner does so to prevent unauthorized people from drawing his water. The house owner does so because of suspicion concerning his wife. If the cistern owner could enter whenever he wished, he might commit adultery with her and would not be caught as a thief merely for entering, because he could say, 'I came to draw water.' Once he knows he cannot draw water without the house owner's key opening the second lock, if he enters at other times he will be caught as an intruder and his scheme will be apparent. Even if he does not actually commit adultery, others may suspect him. Nimukei Yosef writes this. The concern is not theft of household utensils, because the house owner's wife is present and guards them. Rashi explains this point. If he has a separate opening through which he normally enters at night, however, he may use that opening.
