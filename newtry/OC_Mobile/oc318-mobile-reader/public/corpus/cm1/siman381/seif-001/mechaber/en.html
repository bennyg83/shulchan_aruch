The law of five people who sat on one bench. It contains one seif:
If five people sat on a bench and it did not break, and a final person came, sat on it, leaned against the others, prevented them from standing, and the bench broke, the last person is liable even if the bench would eventually have broken under the first five. Since he hastened its breaking, they may say to him, "Had you not leaned against us, we would have stood before it broke." If they all sat down at the same time and it broke, they are all liable. The same applies in similar cases. Rama: Some say an ordinary bench is presumed lent for ordinary people to sit on. If it breaks beneath them, it is comparable to borrowed property that died because of its normal use and they are exempt. Tur, seif 2, in the name of Rosh; Tosafot, first chapter of Bava Kamma. This does not apply if the sitters are unusually heavy compared with ordinary people. If such unusually heavy people sit on it together and it breaks, all are liable. If they sat one after another, then if it would not have broken without the last person, he alone is liable. If it would have broken even without him, he is exempt. If without him it would have broken only after two hours, but with him it broke after one hour, then if he leaned on the others and prevented them from standing, he alone is liable; otherwise, all are liable. All of this is from the Tur in the name of Rosh.
