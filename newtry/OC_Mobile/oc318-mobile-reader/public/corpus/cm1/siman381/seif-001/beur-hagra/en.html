And he leaned, etc., until he could stand. This is the Maggid Mishneh's language in the name of Rashi. See Sema seif katan 2. Rashi understands that even where the bench would not have broken without the final person's presence, liability still depends on his actively leaning upon the others. The Gemara's questions and Rashi's comments are interpreted accordingly. The Rambam, however, cannot be explained this way. It appears that the Rambam had a different text, in which the Gemara asks only whether they could have stood a little longer, and answers that the bench broke while the last person was leaning. Thus, if all sat simultaneously liability differs, but if they sat one after another the last is liable unless the bench would have broken anyway, in which case he is entirely exempt. This reading resolves the difficulties found in Rashi's interpretation. The Tur follows our printed text but limits the Gemara's challenge to the leaning case. The Rambam's reading is the clearest.
And if they sat together, etc. Tosefta chapter 2: five people who sat upon a bench and it broke are all liable; if it broke because of the last person, the last person pays the share attributable to all of them.
And some say, etc. Tosafos, s.v. "for example," unlike Rabbeinu Tam, as the Rosh objects that the sugya does not read naturally according to him. The Rambam and Shulchan Aruch nevertheless follow Rabbeinu Tam.
Then, etc. As above.
He is exempt. The Tur and Nimukei Yosef rule so, following Tosafos' conclusion.
