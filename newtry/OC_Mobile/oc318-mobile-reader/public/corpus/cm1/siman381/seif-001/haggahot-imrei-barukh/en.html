(Rama, seif 1) If it would not have broken without the final person's sitting, etc. Note: See Torat Chaim to Bava Kamma 10b, who disagrees with this rule and writes that the earlier sitters should have stood up when the last person came to sit. Know that this rule is also mentioned in Hagahot Ashri, but it does not initially say that the bench was about to break within two hours, nor is there any indication that those sitting on it were unusually heavy. It may therefore be that Hagahot Ashri holds that with ordinary people, where the owner of the bench does not object to their sitting, the first sitters are treated as having borrowed the bench for their use with the owner's knowledge. The final sitter cannot argue that they should have stood up to prevent the bench from breaking because of him. He had no right to deprive them of the use they had already acquired permissibly. It is as though the owner of the bench himself were sitting there. The Gemara's question, "let him say to them, were it not for you," is interpreted by Hagahot Ashri as applying only to the actual incident, where all of them were like Pappa bar Abba, as Rashbam explains. Persons so heavy have no permission to sit upon a bench that is liable to break, as Tosafot say there: ordinarily it is not lent to Pappa bar Abba. Therefore the final sitter can say, "were it not for you," because the earlier sitters too were sitting without permission.
