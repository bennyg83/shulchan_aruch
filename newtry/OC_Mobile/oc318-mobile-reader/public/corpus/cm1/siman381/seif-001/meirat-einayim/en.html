Five people who sat upon a chair. This is the Rambam's wording in chapter 6 of Hilchot Chovel. The Gemara and Tur speak of a bench.
And he leaned upon them and did not allow them to rise, etc. The Tur cites the Rambam but does not contain these five words, and they are likewise absent from both older and newer editions of Maimonides. In the Derishah I proved that the Tur did not interpret the Rambam as the Mechaber does here, namely that the final person leaned physically upon those already seated and prevented them from getting up. That is Rashi's and the Rosh's explanation. When the Rambam later writes that the earlier sitters can say, "Had you not leaned upon it, we would have risen," his word "it" refers to the bench, not to the people. The meaning is: Had you not come to lean and sit on the bench, we would have stood up. Therefore the last person cannot say, "You should have stood when I sat down." The case is one where the bench broke before they realized he intended to sit there. As soon as he approached and began leaning on the bench, it broke. In some printings the text reads, "Had you not leaned upon us," following the Maggid Mishneh's explanation of the Rambam like Rashi. The Ir Shushan follows that version, but it does not appear correct. See the Derishah.
