If a person obligated to take a Torah oath is suspected of swearing falsely, the opposing party swears and collects, provided his claim is definite. If both parties are suspected, the oath reverts to the defendant, and because he cannot swear, he pays. {Rama: Some say they divide the disputed sum. This appears proper to me, because the burden of proof rests upon the claimant. Some say that when the defendant is suspected of swearing falsely, the opposing party swears and collects only if he did not know of the defendant's status when he dealt with him. Otherwise he does not, for if this were allowed, anyone could transact with a person suspected of false oaths and then swear and collect. Others disagree; see Responsa of the Rosh, rule 11, responsum 1.}
