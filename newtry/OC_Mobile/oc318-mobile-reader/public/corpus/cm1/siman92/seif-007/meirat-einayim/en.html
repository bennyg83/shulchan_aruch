The opposing party takes an oath and collects. Tosafot in the first chapter of Bava Metzia 5a ask why we do not apply here the rule that one who cannot take an oath must pay. They answer first that this case is different because the suspected person would willingly swear, but the court does not allow him to do so, and therefore he does not automatically pay. They give a second answer: If we required him to pay, no one would leave him enough to live on, for everyone could bring him to an oath and take all his property. The Ir Shushan cites this second answer. In my view, however, that explanation does not fit Rama's ruling below that if the other party knew at the time of the transaction that this person was already suspected, the suspected party is exempt without an oath, for otherwise everyone would deliberately deal with a suspected person and then swear and collect. According to that rationale, when the claimant did not know he was suspected, it remains difficult why the opposing party should take an oath and collect. The Ir Shushan discusses these laws at length with explanations that are unnecessary. Examine this carefully.
