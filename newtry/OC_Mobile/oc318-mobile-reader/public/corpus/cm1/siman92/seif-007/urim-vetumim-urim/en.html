One who became obligated, etc. This applies even where his oath obligation arose through a single witness. We do not say that in such a case the opposing party need not swear at all because the claimant has a supporting witness. If that were so, anyone could extract money from a person disqualified from taking an oath through one witness. See what I wrote above in siman 87 at length regarding all the details of this matter in the words of Maharam.
And some say they divide it. Here the ruling is that they divide the disputed sum, whereas below in siman 108, where the borrower died during the lender's lifetime, the rule is that a person cannot transmit an oath obligation to his children and the claimant loses everything. There, however, no oath obligation ever rests upon the defendant, and therefore, since the claimant cannot swear, he loses everything. Here the defendant originally was obligated to swear, but he too cannot swear because he is disqualified. It is therefore proper that both parties bear the loss. The reason we rule that if one who impaired his note died, his heirs collect the entire amount, is that there he committed no fault. Here, by contrast, the claimant is at fault because he is disqualified from swearing. By strict law, even when the borrower dies during the lender's lifetime, the claimant should lose only when collecting from heirs, and that stringent rule too issued specifically from Rav and Shmuel. See Tumim for my lengthy discussion.
And some disagree. See Tumim, where I wrote that there is in fact no disagreement. Raavad speaks of a deposit, while Rosh speaks of a loan. In the case of a loan, Raavad agrees with Rosh. Examine there carefully.
