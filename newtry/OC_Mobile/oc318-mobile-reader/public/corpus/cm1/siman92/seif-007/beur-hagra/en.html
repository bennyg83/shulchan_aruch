One who became obligated to take an oath, etc. See Shach. Shevuot 32b implies that the suspected party must swear. This rejects Maharam's strained explanation that a supporting witness cannot exempt a suspected litigant because otherwise anyone could create liability through one witness. Once the oath obligation arose through the witness, that same supporting testimony cannot remove it. See citations 80 and 86.
And if both are suspected, etc. This follows the view that the oath returns to its original place and Rabbi Abba, whose ruling is accepted. Rava and Abaye follow him, and the mishnayot in Bava Metzia and the discussion in Bava Batra are explained accordingly. Rav and Shmuel are accepted only within their limited case, while Rav Nachman's ruling to divide is not followed. See citation 80.
And some say they divide. This follows Rav Nachman, who acted according to Rabbi Yose, and the law follows Rabbi Yose and Rav Nachman in monetary matters. Rabbi Abba is not a contradiction, because Rav Nachman also accepts his rule where a Torah oath rests on the defendant. Here the division is a penalty because the claimant too is unable to take the oath imposed upon him. The sources show three accepted rules: Rabbi Abba for a Torah oath, Rav and Shmuel in their limited rabbinic cases, and Rav Nachman where both parties are unable to swear. See the extended discussions in Tosafot, Rosh, Mordechai, Baal HaMaor, Ran, and the cited passages. See citations 4, 32, 35, 80, 86, and 146.
And some say that this, etc. Bava Metzia asks that the shepherd should be disqualified. The question matters because, as a robber, he cannot swear and collect; the disqualification is stronger than the later rabbinic disqualification of shepherds. See citation 86.
And others disagree. Tosafot to Shevuot 47a indicate that the rule applies even when the claimant knew the other party was suspected, and Rosh agrees. The shepherd case was answered above in siman 87, seif 14. The first opinion follows Tosafot's second answer in Bava Metzia. See citations 4 and 87.
