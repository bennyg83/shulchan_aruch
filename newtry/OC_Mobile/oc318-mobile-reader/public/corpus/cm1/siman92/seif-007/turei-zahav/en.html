(In the Tur, seif 11) The Rambam writes concerning a bailee, etc. In my humble opinion, the bailee is entirely exempt, though a general ban is proclaimed. One might ask how this differs from a case where both litigants are disqualified from swearing. There, since no rabbinic remedy is available, the matter is left to Torah law and, according to the Rambam, the defendant pays because he is obligated to swear and cannot swear. Why should we not say the same of this disqualified bailee, since the claimant cannot swear in his stead? It appears to me that the Rambam holds that the rule “whoever is obligated to swear but cannot swear must pay” is derived from the verse, “The oath of Hashem shall be between the two of them,” which the Sages expound as “between the two of them, but not between their heirs.” This refers to a case where the heirs say, “We know about fifty and do not know about fifty,” for in the father’s case he would have been obligated to swear and, being unable to do so, would have had to pay, as Tosafot explain in Bava Batra regarding the silver ingot of Rabbi Abba. The Torah imposed payment specifically in such a case, where the claimant asserts certainty. But where the claimant makes only an uncertain claim against a bailee, the rule does not apply. This is a sound distinction, for how can we require payment to a claimant who himself does not know whether anything is owed to him?
