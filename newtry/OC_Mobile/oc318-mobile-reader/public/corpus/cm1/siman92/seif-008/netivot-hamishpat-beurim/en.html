See Shach, seif katan 12, who writes that the guardian must nevertheless take a d'oraisa oath that he did not consume the deposit, and because he is disqualified from taking an oath, the opposing party swears that he misappropriated it and collects. Although the oath imposed on the guardian concerns only whether he consumed it, regarding which the guardian claims uncertainty, why should the opposing party swear that he misappropriated it, when that oath was never independently imposed on the guardian because the opposing party makes a definite claim? The intent appears to be that once the guardian must swear that he did not consume it, he is also required, by gilgul shevuah, to swear that he did not misappropriate it and did not act negligently. Although a guardian ordinarily need not take the guardian's oath where the depositor makes a definite claim, those additional matters become obligatory through gilgul. Since the guardian is disqualified from swearing, the opposing party swears and collects. The Shach further explains the Tur, who states this rule even where the depositor definitely claims that the guardian consumed it, by saying that the guardian must nevertheless swear that he did not misappropriate it from the time it was deposited until it was lost. This is very difficult, because siman 294 establishes that the oath against misappropriation is only through gilgul. If the guardian has no oath that he was not negligent and that the object is no longer in his possession, because the depositor makes a definite claim, there is likewise no oath concerning misappropriation. Ketzot HaChoshen raises this difficulty. It therefore seems that there is a textual error in the Shach. Just as the guardian would have to swear that he did not misappropriate it from the time of deposit until the loss, he must also swear that he did not act negligently during that period. Even if the depositor says definitely that the guardian consumed it, and the guardian says definitely that armed robbers took it in the owner's presence, there remains concern that the guardian previously acted negligently, for example by showing it to the armed robbers or by some other negligence that caused the later unavoidable loss. According to the Tur, the primary oath also includes absence of negligence. Thus, where the Shach mentions misappropriation, “negligence” should also be inserted. Alternatively, even without emending the text, this is necessarily included, because if he must swear that he did not previously misappropriate it, he must likewise swear that he did not previously commit negligence that caused the eventual loss. However, I have already written in siman 72 that the primary rule is that even where the depositor makes a definite claim, the guardian must take the guardian's oath. See there.
