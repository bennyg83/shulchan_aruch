Therefore, if the owner of the deposit claims. The Sema says that “therefore” is imprecise and that the text should simply say, “If the owner claims.” According to what I wrote above in siman 72:27, however, the wording is correct. A custodian's Torah oath does not apply when the owner makes a definite claim that the custodian consumed the deposit. Thus, although the custodian need not take the custodial oath that he did not misuse or negligently guard it, he must still take a Torah oath that he did not consume it. Since he is disqualified and cannot swear, the opposing party swears that he misused it or was negligent and collects. If the owner also claims with certainty that the custodian consumed it, the custodian has no Torah oath and is exempt because he is disqualified. The Tur's wording, “Therefore, if he claims that in my presence he misused the deposit or consumed it,” can also be explained. Even if the claim of consumption is definite, the custodian must swear that he did not misuse it at any time from deposit until loss, since misuse could have occurred at any moment. Because he cannot take that oath, the owner swears that he consumed it. Accordingly, a custodian is exempt from an oath upon the owner's definite claim only where the owner trusts him regarding misuse, authorized him to use the item, the deposit did not remain with him at all, or it is otherwise clear that he did not misuse it, such as where witnesses saw it sealed until the moment of loss. Otherwise the oath concerning misuse remains and brings the other oath through extension. Cited cross-references in the source include: 70.
