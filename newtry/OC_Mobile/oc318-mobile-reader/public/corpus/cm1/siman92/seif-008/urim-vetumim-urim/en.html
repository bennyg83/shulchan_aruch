The opposing party cannot swear and therefore collects. Bach holds that Tosafos, in one answer in Bava Metzia and according to what they wrote in Shevuos, disagree and follow Ramah and Ri cited by Tur, that the opposing party collects without an oath. See Tumim, where I wrote that no decisive proof can be derived from Tosafos.
For he does not make a definite claim. It appears that if one sues a person disqualified from swearing on the basis of a single witness, while the claimant himself does not know whether the claim is true, he likewise collects nothing, for he cannot swear that his claim is true merely on the basis of the witness. See Tumim. It appears that even Ramah and those who follow him, who disagree concerning a custodian, may concede this case. Nevertheless, the matter requires study. See there at length.
Therefore, etc. Sema writes that the word 'therefore' is not entirely precise, and the text should have said, 'If he claimed.' Shach, following his own position that where a depositor makes a definite claim there is no custodial oath, was therefore compelled to explain both Shulchan Aruch and Rambam here in various strained ways. I already wrote at length above in siman 72 that this is not the practical law, and even with a definite claim the custodial oath applies. Rambam's use of 'therefore' may be explained as follows. The reason a custodian ordinarily has no migo that he could have claimed, 'It never happened,' is that this would be a migo merely to avoid an oath, and that principle applies where he swears and is thereby exempt. Here, however, the claimant collects money by swearing, so the issue becomes a migo concerning money, as I wrote above in siman 89 regarding a hired worker. Rambam therefore says 'therefore': because he gave as the reason that the claimant has no definite claim, this indicates that we do not take the migo into account. The general rule is that wherever a disqualified litigant cannot swear, the opposing party swears and collects. Accordingly, even here, if the claimant makes a definite claim, he swears and collects in every circumstance. The word 'therefore' is thus entirely precise.
