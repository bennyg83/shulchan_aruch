Therefore. The Sema writes that the word 'therefore' is imprecise and that the text should have said, 'If he claims.' According to what I wrote in siman 72, seif 27, however, that a custodian's oath does not apply to a definite claim, the wording is correct. It refers back to the opening case, where the owner does not claim with certainty that the custodian consumed the property. Thus, although the custodian is not required to take the ordinary Torah custodian's oath that he did not misappropriate or act negligently, because the owner makes a definite claim that he consumed it the custodian must nevertheless take a Torah oath denying consumption. Since he is suspect and cannot swear, the opposing party swears that he misappropriated it and collects. If the owner also claims with certainty that he consumed it, however, there is no custodian's Torah oath and the suspect custodian is exempt. It follows that when the owner of a deposit makes a definite claim, a custodian will be exempt from oath only where the owner believes him regarding misappropriation, or authorized him to use it, or the like. Otherwise, because he must swear concerning misappropriation, the other oath is included through gilgul. Shach.
