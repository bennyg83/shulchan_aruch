The testimony. This means that the beit din administered an oath asking whether he knew testimony for another and required him to testify. He swore that he knew nothing, and it was later established that he had been present when the event occurred. The case concerns a recent event that he certainly had not forgotten; otherwise he would not be disqualified. Sema. The Ran writes likewise. The Rashba, however, holds that in all circumstances he cannot say, 'I forgot.' The Sema's statements here also appear to conflict with what he writes in siman 34, seif 5 in the gloss. See Responsa Rama, simanim 39 and 40, and Responsa Maharam Lublin, siman 58. Shach. See also siman 81, seif 25, and what I wrote in siman 82, seif katan 31.
The deposit. Meaning that he swore that no deposit had been entrusted to him, and it was then established that it had been. In seif 4 it will be explained that if witnesses testify that the deposit was in his possession at the time he denied it, he is disqualified immediately even without an oath. Here there are no witnesses that it was then in his possession. Sema.
Concerning the past. According to this opinion, certainly one who violated a ban is not disqualified. See siman 34, where all this is explained properly. See Responsa Rashach, additions to part 2, siman 25, and part 3, siman 34; Responsa Maharshdam, Choshen Mishpat, simanim 185 and 228; and Responsa Rabbi Shlomo HaLevi, Choshen Mishpat, siman 21.
