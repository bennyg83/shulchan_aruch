The oath of testimony.
Meaning that beit din adjured him whether he knew testimony for his fellow and he denied knowledge under oath, as the verse states, 'and he hears the voice of an oath ...
if he does not tell,' and afterward it became known that he had been present at the event.
The case is where the event occurred recently enough that he certainly had not forgotten it; otherwise he would not be disqualified through this.The oath concerning a deposit.
Meaning that he swore that the other person had not deposited property with him, and it was later established that the deposit had in fact been made.These are examples of false oaths that establish a person as suspected and disqualified according to the rules explained here.
The precise level of disqualification depends on whether the underlying prohibition is Torah law or rabbinic and whether the falsehood was established by valid witnesses or by some lesser form of proof.
