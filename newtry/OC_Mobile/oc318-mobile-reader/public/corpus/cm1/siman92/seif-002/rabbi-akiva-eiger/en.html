[Sema, subparagraph 7] For example, he swore that he would not eat and then ate. Note: One can explain simply that this example was chosen to teach that not only where he swore that he had eaten and in fact had not, which is a transgression without lashes because a prohibition without an action does not incur lashes and therefore does not disqualify him, but even where he swore that he would not eat and then ate, which does involve an action, the distinction applies. Indeed, this is the basis of the Rivash’s distinction cited in Sema, seif 34.
