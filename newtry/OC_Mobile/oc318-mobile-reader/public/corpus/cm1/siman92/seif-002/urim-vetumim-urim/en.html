That I will not eat, etc. This differs from one who eats forbidden food. That person may treat the prohibition lightly because the food is permitted to other people, and no false oath leaves his mouth. All the more so one who swore that he would eat and then did not, because he committed no affirmative act. See siman 34 for my discussion. According to this view, the same distinction can apply to a communal ban: if the community imposed a prospective ban that no one do a certain act and someone later violated it, he may not become suspected. But if the community imposed a ban regarding a past act, requiring whoever did it to come immediately and accept punishment, perhaps everyone agrees that one who violates that directive becomes suspected. Still, at the moment the ban was pronounced he had not yet violated it. Consider this.
