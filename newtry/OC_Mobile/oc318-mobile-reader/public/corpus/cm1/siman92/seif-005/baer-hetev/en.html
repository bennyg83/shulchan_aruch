An oath. This refers to a Torah oath. If the defendant is merely obligated to take a hesset oath in order to be exempt, the opposing claimant cannot say, 'Since you admitted that you are suspect, and your admission is like one hundred witnesses, I will swear against your will and collect.' The defendant can answer, 'If you believe me, I am exempt without an oath.' We do not make one rabbinic enactment upon another. Rather, the defendant takes a hesset oath and is exempt. It appears that the first opinion agrees with this as well, and this is implied by the Tur and Maggid Mishneh. The Ran too holds that a person is believed to establish himself as suspect, contrary to Tosafot Yom Tov in the chapter Kol HaNishba'in. The matter still requires study. The Bach rules that if the claimant wishes, he may swear and collect. Shach.
