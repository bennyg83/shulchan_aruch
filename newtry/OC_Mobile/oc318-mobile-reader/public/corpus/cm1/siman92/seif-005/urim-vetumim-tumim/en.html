And there is an opinion, etc. The Shach writes that no one disagrees with this ruling. See, however, what I wrote at the end of siman 87, where many authorities disagree and hold that there is a scriptural rule that a person cannot become legally suspect through his own admission. The Rashba’s language also tends that way. He proves from the case of one who becomes suspect through an oath of testimony that we do not say, “Perhaps he forgot.” But what proof is there if a person can become suspect by his own admission? Perhaps the baraita merely teaches that where he admits that he knowingly swore falsely regarding testimony, he becomes suspect and the opposing party may then swear and collect if he wishes, as the Ran indeed explains. It therefore appears that the Rashba does not accept the Ran’s approach. One should not object to the Ran that even if this witness admits he did not forget, perhaps the other witness did forget, leaving only one witness. The case can be one analogous to the silver ingot of Rabbi Abba, where one witness is enough for the relevant effect.
