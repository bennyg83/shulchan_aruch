(Seif 11.) "Nevertheless, a general cherem is imposed." The Sema asks in seif katan 30 why a specific cherem should not be imposed, and his difficulty is valid. It appears that the Rama clarifies this by writing, "and then he is exempt from accepting a cherem." Meaning, the Mechaber's phrase, "a general cherem is imposed," is interpreted here as requiring this disqualified person himself to accept a specific cherem. Although the Mechaber did not intend this, the Rama does. When the Rama says "accepting a cherem," he means that the person accepts it explicitly. There is no need to frame this as a dispute, because the first opinion did not intend to exclude a specific cherem; it only meant that there is a general cherem without the terminology of an oath. This also resolves the Sema's question from siman 82, seif 9, regarding one who is not disqualified, where the claimant may say, "I will not swear," and instead impose a general cherem. That case is different, because he seeks to escape a specific cherem. Here the disqualified party may say: either swear and collect, or release me from the specific cherem, meaning the personal acceptance of cherem, and impose only a general cherem. Above, however, the case itself concerns a general cherem, from which he cannot escape. The Sema's discussion is extremely difficult. His further statement that in a reversed oath the person reversing it must accept a cherem was already challenged by me in siman 87, seif katan 22.
