The Shulchan Aruch requires that the claimant say with certainty that the defendant is suspected because he has witnesses to prove it. This comes from Rashba. Rashba further considers what happens if the claimant says with certainty that the defendant is suspected but cannot produce witnesses. One might think the claimant loses, because according to his own words he cannot place an oath upon the defendant, while he himself cannot swear and collect merely because he says the defendant is suspected. Rashba rejects that. We do not disqualify a person partially, making him disqualified only against this claimant while qualified for everyone else. Likewise, if one witness testifies that he is suspected, we do not say he is disqualified against that witness alone. Therefore, until proper evidence establishes the disqualification, the ordinary oath process remains. This does not contradict Rashba's ruling regarding a claim of prohibited interest, where the claimant says, "You took interest from me," and the defendant is not made to swear because according to the claimant's own account the defendant is already suspected. There the very fact being litigated is itself the act that would make the defendant suspected. If we believe the claimant enough to impose an oath about that event, we simultaneously accept that the defendant is unfit for the oath. If we do not believe the claimant, there is no basis for the oath. In the present case the alleged suspicious conduct is a separate matter, and we do not disqualify a person selectively without proof.
