(Seif 12) “And he produces a note against the orphans.” This differs from the opinion cited below in seif 9 concerning an impaired note, that because he holds a validated note, etc. Here the oath arises primarily because the defendants are orphans, under the rule that one who collects from orphan property must swear. This therefore falls within the rule in siman 108 concerning orphans collecting from orphans: where the borrower died during the lender’s lifetime, a person cannot bequeath an oath obligation to his children. All the more so here, where the claimant himself seeks to collect from orphans and must swear against them, but cannot do so. The case is equivalent to orphans collecting from orphans, as the Rosh writes at the end of Kol HaNishba’in, and is not limited by the principle, “Apply the enactment as stated and do not extend it.” An impaired note is different, because that oath applies even when the claimant is not collecting from orphans. We therefore do not derive it from the law of orphans collecting from orphans, and it remains within the principle that the enactment should not be extended.
