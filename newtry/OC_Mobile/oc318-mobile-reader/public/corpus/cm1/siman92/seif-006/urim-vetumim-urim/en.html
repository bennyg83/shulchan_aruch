But if he said with certainty, etc. Mechaber uses this distinction to reconcile responsa of Rosh and Rashba. Rosh wrote, 'Such a thing should not be in Israel,' while Rashba permits requiring an oath in the synagogue. Mechaber distinguishes a case where the litigant says with certainty that witnesses know the truth. Bach explains that the witnesses come and say, 'We will not testify unless you first impose a ban.' See Tumim. I gave another distinction: whether the litigant himself imposes the oath in the synagogue, or beit din directs its court officer to proclaim a ban in beit din's name.
If he does not testify, etc. See Tumim. The verse-based punishment for withholding testimony does not apply here in the ordinary form; rather there is liability before Heaven, like one witness who suppresses testimony. An exception may arise where the claimant is suspected, the defendant is a partial admitter, and the witnesses know that the defendant too is suspected. Then they may become implicated in the rule that where both parties are suspected the defendant pays.
