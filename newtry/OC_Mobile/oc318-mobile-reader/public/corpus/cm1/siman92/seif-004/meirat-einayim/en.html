Even though he brings witnesses who contradict him, etc. I have already written that the general principle of this law is that we do not establish a person as suspected so long as his conduct can reasonably be explained. Thus, money lent is given for expenditure. Even when the due date has passed, since the money originally came into his hands with permission to spend, we say that by denying the loan he intends merely to delay the lender until he has funds. A deposit is different, because it is not given for expenditure. Nevertheless, where there are no witnesses that the deposit was still in his possession at the moment he denied it, we can still plead on his behalf. Perhaps when the depositor demanded the item he did not know where he had put it, or it had been moved from its place, and he feared that the depositor would require him to take the Torah oath imposed upon custodians. He therefore thought to deny the deposit by saying, 'You never deposited it with me,' thereby avoiding a Torah oath until he found the item and returned it. This is clear.

Provided that there are witnesses, etc. The reason has already been explained. From that reason one can learn that at times a person becomes suspected even without witnesses who saw the property in his possession at the moment of denial. For example, Reuven gave Shimon money to invest for Reuven's benefit. Later Shimon denied receiving anything, and it then became established that Reuven did give him the money. Here there is no basis to say that Shimon merely sought delay until he would have money, as with an ordinary loan. If that were his intention, why deny receipt entirely? He should have said, 'I invested the money for your benefit and have not yet collected it,' since the money was given to him specifically to expend in business for Reuven's benefit. The Rosh writes this in a responsum cited by Darkei Moshe here. Rama writes there that the ruling requires study. In my glosses there I wrote that, in my view, the Rosh's reasoning is straightforward, as explained. See there and examine this carefully.
