[Shulchan Aruch] Whether the disqualification is rabbinic, such as. Note: Even without a public declaration, he is disqualified from taking an oath. So writes Maharam Schiff in the first chapter of Bava Metzia in the sugya of the shepherd, while Knesset HaGedolah here, in the glosses to the Tur, questions this. In my humble opinion, the purpose of a public declaration is for the orderly administration of society. Therefore, with respect to a heset oath, where the opposing party of a suspected person is exempt without an oath, if the suspicion is only rabbinic and no declaration has yet been made, we do not treat him as suspected so as to exempt him without an oath, because that would cause another person a loss. But where he is one who admits part of the claim, and the consequence works to his own detriment, no declaration is required, and we treat him as one who is obligated to swear but cannot swear. See Tosafot, Bava Kamma, at the end of the chapter HaKones, and examine this carefully.
