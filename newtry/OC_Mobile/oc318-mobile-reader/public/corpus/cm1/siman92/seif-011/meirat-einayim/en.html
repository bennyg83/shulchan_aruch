The suspected person became obligated in a hesset oath. Even though the claimant makes a definite claim against him, under strict law the defendant would have been exempt without an oath.
Rav Nachman instituted hesset only for one who is fit to swear. We do not make an additional enactment for the claimant's benefit when the defendant is disqualified from taking that already-rabbinic oath.Nevertheless, a general ban is imposed, etc.
The Tur likewise writes this in siman 90, seif 4, and the Mechaber there appears to imply the same. It is somewhat difficult why a general ban should remain where Chazal declined to create a further oath enactment. The explanation appears to be that a general ban is not the same as compelling a specific person to take an oath or shifting the oath to his opponent.
It is merely a general declaration directed at anyone who wrongfully withholds the money. Therefore it is not considered an enactment upon an enactment in the same sense.
