Thus Rambam. One might ask why this matters. Even if the oath were Torah law, so long as the claimant is uncertain, how could he swear and collect, as Rambam himself writes above in seif 8 regarding a custodian? Sema answers that Rambam gives two independent reasons: first, the claimant lacks certainty; second, the oath is rabbinic. Why are both needed? It appears that above, regarding a custodian, Rambam rules that if the owner says with certainty, 'You misappropriated the deposit in my presence,' he may swear and collect even though the special rolled oath involved is not the original core custodial oath. The reason is that the basic custodial oath is Torah law, and by right the claimant could potentially swear and collect, but cannot do so on uncertainty. Where he has certainty, even the rolled component may support collection. By contrast, these oaths concerning partners and similar relationships are rabbinic from the outset precisely because there is ordinarily no definite claim. Even if one particular rolled issue becomes definite, the claimant still does not become one who swears and collects, because the basic oath itself was never of that category. This is the correct understanding.
