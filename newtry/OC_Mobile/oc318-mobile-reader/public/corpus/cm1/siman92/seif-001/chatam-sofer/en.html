Shach, seif katan 1, "to say, I forgot." Note: his words require study. The Ran there, on page 15a, cites the Rashba's question concerning an oath of testimony: who proves that he acted deliberately, perhaps he forgot? The Rashba leaves the matter requiring study. The Ran answers by distinguishing between a short interval, when one cannot claim forgetfulness, and a long interval. See there. It follows that the Rashba, who left the question unresolved, must hold that one may claim forgetfulness, and found it forced to establish the Mishnah specifically in a short interval. Thus, when the Rashba wrote in the responsum cited by the Beit Yosef and the Sema above, siman 34, he meant only that we do not accept his testimony, because once it is certain that he swore falsely we do not attribute it to forgetfulness or inadvertence so as to accept his testimony against another person. But to disqualify him from taking an oath and thereby extract money from him, the Rashba holds that if some time elapsed between the testimony and the oath he may say, "I forgot," and we do not extract money from him. This is clear. The Ran gives another answer there, that the case is where he admitted that he had testified under oath, and therefore becomes suspect through his own admission so as to disqualify himself from taking an oath that would preserve his own money. See there. Yet this itself requires study: why did the Rashba leave the question unresolved instead of answering in this manner, since the Maggid Mishneh cites the Rashba to this effect in Hilchot Ishut 24:11 concerning one who says, "So-and-so, the sage, ruled my stain pure," and Beit Shmuel likewise cites it in Even HaEzer, siman 115, seif katan 3? One must say that the Rashba holds that since, at least where he would otherwise be obligated in a shevuat heset, he does not become suspect through his own admission merely to gain exemption from the oath, as stated below in seif 11, he likewise does not become partially suspect regarding a Torah oath where the result would be loss of money. This reasoning is written by the Beit Yosef in this siman, chiddush 5, in the name of the Rashba. See also Mordechai, chapter Chazakat HaBatim, page 245d, siman 528, in the name of Raavan, that where one witness testifies against him he may become suspect for an oath through his own admission. Regarding "so-and-so sodomized me willingly," the accompanying witness did not testify that it was willingly, for had he done so the person would become disqualified through his own admission together with the one witness. This is a novel ruling.
