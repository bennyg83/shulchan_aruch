The borrower claimed that he paid and said, 'Let him swear to me,' etc. The Mechaber adopts Rambam's wording from chapter 2 of Hilchot To'en. The phrase 'and he said, "let him swear to me"' cannot refer to one who has impaired his own document, because siman 84 already established that where a document is impaired and contains no credibility clause, beit din itself requires the lender to swear even if the borrower does not demand it.
If the document contains a credibility clause, the lender is believed without an oath even if the borrower asks for one. Rather, the phrase refers to the ordinary rule concerning a documented debt where the borrower claims payment and expressly demands that the lender swear. The particular oath requirement depends on whether the document is impaired, whether it contains credibility language, and whether the borrower himself raises the demand.
