(Seif 14) “The Rambam wrote: if he was disqualified,” etc. Although this can be derived a fortiori from what the Rambam wrote in seif 11, the novelty is that here Rabbi Yeshayah agrees with the Rambam because the oath is rabbinic, as explained immediately above. The difficulty is why the Rambam himself needed to state it. The answer is that one might have thought that because the Sages were sufficiently concerned about partners to impose an oath even upon an uncertain claim, based on the concern that a partner rationalizes taking funds, they would likewise remain concerned when the partner is disqualified and enact that the opposing party swear that he truly suspects him and collect, as Ramah is cited at the end of seif 7 regarding reversal of an oath. The Rambam therefore teaches that they did not enact this.
