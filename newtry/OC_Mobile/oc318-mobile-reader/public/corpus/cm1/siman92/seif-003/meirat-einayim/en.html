Whether concerning rabbinic matters, etc. Each person is disqualified according to the nature of the transgression.
One who violates a Torah prohibition is disqualified by Torah law, while one who violates a rabbinic prohibition is disqualified rabbinically, as explained in siman 34, with practical consequences flowing from that distinction.Suspected of false oaths.
Because these categories of transgressors are listed in the Mishnah and Gemara in chapter Zeh Borer among those disqualified from testimony, the Mechaber counts the various categories of persons suspected of false oaths among the laws of disqualified witnesses.But without witnesses, etc.
A person's own admission does not ordinarily render him wicked or disqualify him from testimony, because a person does not establish himself as wicked through his own statement. Therefore formal disqualification generally requires witnesses or another legally sufficient basis, subject to the special rules concerning one who has already sworn in the present litigation.
