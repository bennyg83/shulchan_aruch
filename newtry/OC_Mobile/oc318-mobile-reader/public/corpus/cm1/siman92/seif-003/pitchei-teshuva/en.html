Whether the disqualification is of rabbinic origin. See Baer Heitev. In Rabbi Akiva Eiger's glosses to the Shulchan Aruch he writes: "Even without a public proclamation, he is disqualified from taking an oath. So writes Maharam Schiff in the first chapter of Bava Metzia, in the sugya of that shepherd. Knesset HaGedolah here, in the glosses to the Tur, is uncertain about this." However, Rabbi Akiva Eiger himself, in his glosses to the Mishnah in the chapter Zeh Borer, Tosafot Rabbi Akiva Eiger, letter 9, distinguishes in this matter. He writes that, at first glance, a person disqualified by rabbinic law requires a public proclamation, as explained above in siman 34, seif 23, but only in order to disqualify him from testimony, whereas to disqualify him from an oath no proclamation should be necessary. This appears proven by the question in the first chapter of Bava Metzia, "let it follow simply because he is a shepherd." Yet the words of Tosafot in Bava Kamma 62, s.v. chamsan, indicate that even with respect to disqualification from an oath, a proclamation is required. He therefore concludes that the rule is as follows: whenever the disqualification would cause a loss to others, the Sages enacted that he is not disqualified without a proclamation; but with respect to his own rights, he is disqualified even without a proclamation. Accordingly, in the case in the first chapter of Bava Metzia the question is proper, whereas in the case in Bava Kamma the rule differs. A practical distinction follows: if one who is rabbinically disqualified, but against whom no proclamation has yet been made, owes another person a Torah oath, the opposing party swears and collects. If, however, he owes only a rabbinic oath, such that failure to swear would exempt him entirely, he must still swear, because we do not deprive another person of his right before a proclamation has been made. See his further discussion there, as well as Tumim above, siman 34, seif katan 22, Shaar Mishpat, seif katan 10, and Birkei Yosef above, siman 34, letters 17 and 27, on this subject.
