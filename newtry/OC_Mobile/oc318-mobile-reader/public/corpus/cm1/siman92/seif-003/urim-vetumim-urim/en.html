But without witnesses, etc. Sema explains: if one person sues another and says he owes him money, while the defendant denies it, we do not say, 'Since he is willing to deny the debt, perhaps he is suspected and should not swear.' Maybe he is delaying payment or thinks he has some outstanding counterloan. Sema therefore emends the wording to 'perhaps a loan.' See Tumim. I offered another explanation that does not require emendation. If one holds an object and claims it is his, and it later becomes clear that it belongs to another, even though he must return it and his claim was not accepted, he does not automatically become suspected if he did not seize it or receive it before witnesses. Perhaps the other person truly owes him money and he therefore held it. On this reading the text should say simply 'a loan,' not 'perhaps a loan.' See Tumim, where I clarified the matter. If he had previously waived all claims through the present day, he still cannot necessarily be forced to swear in a case where temporary self-help is implausible. See there.
