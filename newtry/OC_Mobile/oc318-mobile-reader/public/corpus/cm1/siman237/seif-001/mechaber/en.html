One who is pursuing an item to buy or rent and another precedes him. It contains two seifim.
If a person is pursuing the purchase or rental of land or movable property and another precedes him and acquires it, the latter is called wicked. The same applies when a person is seeking employment with another and someone else takes that position. Some say that if the first person seeks to acquire ownerless property or receive a gift, and another precedes him, the second is not called wicked, because the opportunity is not readily available elsewhere. Rama: A neighboring landowner may precede another and purchase adjoining land even where the formal law of preemption does not apply, because it resembles finding an opportunity. Likewise, if one seeks an article at an unusually low price unavailable elsewhere, another may buy it before the first completes acquisition. Others make no distinction. Rama: The first view is primary. Even under that view, the restriction ordinarily applies to a poor person, not a wealthy one, unless the opportunity is unavailable elsewhere, in which case even one who precedes a wealthy person is called wicked. These rules apply only after the parties have fixed the price and nothing remains but the act of acquisition. If price negotiations are unfinished, because the seller asks one amount and the purchaser offers less, another may buy it, whether the seller is Jewish or non-Jewish. Some write that Rabbeinu Gershom placed a ban on encroaching upon another's rental of houses from non-Jews, and the same applies where custom permits renting the right to lend money to non-Jews.
