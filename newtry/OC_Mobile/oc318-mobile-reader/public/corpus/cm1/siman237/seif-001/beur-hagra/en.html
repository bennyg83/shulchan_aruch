To rent it. According to Tosafos’ explanation of “a poor person turning over a cake.” See Tosafos there, s.v. “a poor person,” and Bava Batra 21b, s.v. “and they distance, etc.” Rabbeinu Tam explains, etc.
Whether movable property. As stated in Bava Batra there concerning distancing, etc. See Beer HaGolah and Rashi there, s.v. “a poor person, etc.”
The same law applies, etc. As stated in Bava Batra there concerning distancing a fisherman’s net, etc. See Tosafos there.
Some say, etc. As stated in Bava Metzia 10a: one who saw a found object, etc. It appears that he is not even called wicked. See Tosafos there, s.v. “one who takes away, etc.,” and the Rosh. Likewise where one fell upon an object, etc. Concerning a gift, HaMekabel states that the rule of doing what is good and right does not create the law of bar metzra.
And one who purchases, etc., and likewise, etc. Anything that cannot readily be found at all times has the status of a found object for this purpose.
Some say, etc. According to their explanation, “a poor person turning over a cake” refers to one seeking to acquire a cake that is ownerless.
Specifically, etc. This is why the Gemara says “a poor person.” Although the incident of Rav Gidel implies that the rule also applies to a wealthy person, that case concerned the purchase of land, which is ordinarily available. Therefore, where the desired item is not readily available, even according to this opinion the rule differs. According to the first opinion there is no distinction, because with an uncommon item even a poor person is not called wicked for acquiring it.
All this does not concern, etc. The Sages were always more concerned with a seller’s loss, as seen in HaMekabel concerning the law of bar metzra: whenever enforcing the preference would cause a loss to the seller, the rule of doing what is good and right does not apply. Our case therefore does not concern such a loss. The same applies when the seller is a non-Jew.
And there is one who says, etc. See Hagahos Ashri, Bava Batra chapter 2, and Or Zarua. These rules apply here as well, similar to what was explained above in siman 156 concerning ma'arufiya.
