One who is pursuing an item to buy it. In the law of a poor person pursuing a cake, once the first prospective buyer tells the second, 'I do not object if you buy it,' there is no remaining basis for complaint against the second under any of the relevant circumstances. If the second then proceeds and acquires it, even by money alone, the first can no longer obstruct him. Even if the first disclosed an intention resembling a protest, perhaps that does not create a claim against the second once he expressly said, 'Buy it.' If there was true coercion together with a formal protest, the matter requires further study.
