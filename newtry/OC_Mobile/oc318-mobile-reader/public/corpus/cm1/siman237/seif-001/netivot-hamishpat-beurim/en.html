Not to encroach on another's boundary in renting houses. It appears that even by strict law a prior tenant can have a form of bar metzra priority, as Taz writes in siman 175. The law of bar metzra in rental can apply even when the property owner is a non Jew. The usual rationale that purchasing from a non Jew drives a lion from the neighbor's boundary does not apply in the same way to rental. Tosafot likewise say regarding Rav Mari bar Rachel that one who held a non Jew's house as collateral can have bar metzra rights because the "lion removal" rationale does not apply. Rabban Gamliel's ban therefore added something beyond strict law: it prohibited increasing the offered rent merely to displace the existing tenant. Before the second year's lease was actually made, another person might otherwise have been legally entitled to offer a higher rental price. Rabban Gamliel banned that conduct as encroaching on another's boundary.
