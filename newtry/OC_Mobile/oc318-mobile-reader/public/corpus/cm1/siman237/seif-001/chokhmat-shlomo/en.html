Seif 1. One who seeks after an item, etc. Note: Regarding the dispute between Moreinu HaRav Avraham Passaler of Krivtof in the Zaleshchik district and the distinguished Reb Leibush Gatisman of Barshtshov: Reb Leibush rented the villages in which Rav Avraham had been residing, and claimed that Rav Avraham gave him permission to rent them. Rav Avraham denied this. They came for judgment before the rabbi of Skala, who ruled that Rav Avraham and his son must swear that they had not sent him to rent the villages. A time was fixed for Rav Avraham to take the oath, but he did not swear by that time. I was asked whether Rav Avraham thereby lost his right. It is obvious that he did not lose his right merely for that reason, and this requires no elaboration. The substance of the ruling, however, was distorted for two reasons. First, since Reb Leibush rented the villages from the landowner, he stands in the place of the landowner and derives his rights from him. Land remains in the possession of its owner. Reb Leibush is therefore the one considered in possession, and the oath should fall upon Reb Leibush the tenant, not upon those who had held the villages until now. This is analogous to the law of a neighboring landowner, where the purchaser is deemed in possession and the oath belongs to him. Second, the law of a neighboring landowner does not truly apply here, because the lease came directly from a gentile. The only possible claim is therefore one of encroachment, which is not a monetary claim but an issur and cherem instituted by the earlier authorities. Where the matter involves only a prohibition, one who denies the allegation is exempt from an oath, as stated in siman 87. Furthermore, because Reb Leibush rented other properties from the landowner together with these villages, the law of a neighboring landowner does not apply. Even if he rented only the two villages, one had previously belonged to Rav Avraham and the other to his son, so against each of them Reb Leibush can claim that he rented another property together with the one in question. The law of a neighboring landowner therefore does not apply. Accordingly, there is no basis for an oath here, and the law is in favor of Reb Leibush. I noted this ruling for myself, but did not send the decision there, because in our many sins there are now many who act defiantly. Examine this carefully.
