To acquire ownerless property. Rabbeinu Tam permits one to pursue and acquire a found or ownerless item even when another person has already been pursuing it. It appears that his reason is that the mitzvah of "love your fellow as yourself" was established only within a sphere resembling "this one benefits and the other does not lose." This positive mitzvah has no stated reward and beit din can compel its observance, just as coercion applies to the trait of Sodom where one benefits and the other suffers no loss. This indicates that the mitzvah does not obligate a person where his own loss is involved, whether spiritual or material. There the principle of "your life takes precedence" applies, and even temporary needs that sustain one's life are included. This is so even according to Ben Petura. His position in the case of two travelers with one flask rests on the prohibition of standing idly by another's blood, not on an unlimited duty to sacrifice one's own life. In matters of temporary livelihood, where there is no direct prohibition of standing by another's blood, one's own immediate needs surely come first. According to the accepted halachah of "your life takes precedence," the command to love one's fellow therefore operates where one gains without causing the other a real loss. For this reason, the law of a poor man turning over a cake, which prohibits another from seizing the opportunity, applies where an equivalent opportunity is readily available elsewhere. There the second person's abstention causes him only limited loss. It does not apply to a genuine find or unique opportunity that is not otherwise available. As to what constitutes a "find" or unusually cheap opportunity, logic suggests that a profit of less than one sixth does not suffice, since deviations under one sixth are normally treated as within market value. One must also judge the matter by the standard of "what is hateful to you, do not do to your fellow." Perhaps only a profit regarded by ordinary people as significant creates the category of a find. Even in matters not governed by the formal laws of ona'ah, one sixth may remain a useful boundary, though there may be a distinction regarding gems and similar goods. This requires further study.
