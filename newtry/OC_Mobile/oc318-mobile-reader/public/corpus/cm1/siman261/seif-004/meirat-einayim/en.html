Although one who sees such conduct is forbidden, etc. The wording "one who sees such conduct" is deliberate. It speaks of one who actually sees the owner throw the purse away intentionally. If one merely finds a purse lying in the public domain, and it has an identifying sign, he must return it, because perhaps it fell from the owner and the owner has not despaired, unless the location has a gentile majority as explained above in siman 259. According to the Rambam, even if the owner is careless with his property, others are not thereby permitted to take it. The Gemara calls it an intentional loss only to exempt the finder from the duty of return or announcement, not to make it ownerless. The Rambam held similarly regarding refuse that is destined to be cleared, in the preceding siman. The Tur disagrees in both cases and holds that these are called intentional losses because they are treated as abandoned property and whoever takes them first acquires. Rama therefore writes, "some say."
