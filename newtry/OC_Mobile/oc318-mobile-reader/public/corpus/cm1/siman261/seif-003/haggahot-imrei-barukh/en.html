(Same Shach) In many places, therefore the Tur's view is primary. Note: The Bach himself addressed this in siman 273. According to his words there, Rambam concedes that where a person's intent is evident from his conduct that he is abandoning the property, as in the case of gathering from the threshing floor, it is ownerless. Only regarding an animal is his intent not sufficiently evident. See Bava Metzia 23b, where an open container is called knowingly abandoned property, and Rashi there. One may also say that according to Rambam there is no concept of ye'ush in knowingly abandoned property. That would explain why Rambam does not distinguish, when one throws his purse into the public domain, between a city with a non-Jewish majority and one without, and likewise when one leaves his cow wandering among vineyards and fields of gentiles. The Gemara's question is then proper. See Ritva's novellae to Bava Metzia.
