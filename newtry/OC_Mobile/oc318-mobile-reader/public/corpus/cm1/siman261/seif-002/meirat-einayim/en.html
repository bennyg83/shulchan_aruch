Because of loss to the land. The animal damages the vineyard. This is derived from the verse "every lost thing of your brother," which includes loss of land, as the Mechaber writes at the end of siman 259.
If it belongs to a non-Jew, he need not return it. The Gemara explicitly says that if the animal was running among vineyards, where there is concern that its legs will be injured by thorns and tree roots commonly found there, then even if the animal belongs to a non-Jew he must return it because the animal of a Jew is being saved from loss. Rambam also writes this briefly in chapter 15, law 2. The Tur and Mechaber omit it. In the Derishah I suggested a reason for the omission.
