Whoever comes first acquires it. Nevertheless, he must return to the Jew the full amount of money the Jew paid the non-Jew. If non-Jewish law expressly provides that only the person who pays acquires, that law governs, as explained in siman 194:2, where several details of this rule are stated.
All these enactments apply even outside Eretz Yisrael. Yehoshua made the stipulations in Eretz Yisrael because at that time Israel was settled there, and he stipulated that people not object to one another's exercise of these rights for the sake of orderly society. The same rationale applies outside Eretz Yisrael.
Some say he may cut, etc., and some disagree. The Tur records another dispute regarding a matter similar to these rules. Rabbi Yehudah says that it is a beit din enactment permitting a person to place manure in the public domain during the manure-removal season and leave it piled there for thirty days so that it is trampled by people and animals. Rif rules unlike Rabbi Yehudah, while the Rosh rules like him. Rama does not mention the dispute here, relying on the Mechaber below in siman 414, where the Mechaber rules without qualification like Rabbi Yehudah. Here the Mechaber omits Rabbi Yishmael's statement, Rama cites it and notes dissenters, while in siman 414 he does not mention dissent. In this he follows Rambam, who likewise rules like Rabbi Yehudah but not Rabbi Yishmael, as Darkei Moshe explains here.
