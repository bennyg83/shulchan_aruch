If there is a known law among the gentiles that ownership is acquired only by the one who pays money, we follow that law, as explained in siman 194, seif 2. See there for several details of this law. Sema. A gentile left several cubits of merchandise in Reuven's shop. The gentile encountered Shimon, who was going elsewhere to buy that merchandise, and told him, 'Go and take from mine that is standing in Reuven's shop.' Shimon went and took some. The gentile then forgot the merchandise that Shimon had purchased. Reuven claimed that he had acquired it because the gentile had left it in his shop. Shimon argued that it had come into Reuven's possession only as a deposit, and wherever it was, it remained in its owner's domain; therefore Shimon acquired it from the gentile. The law follows Shimon. See Responsa Mahardach, bayit 19; Mahari Yaakov Uziel also agreed with him in this ruling. Bnei Aharon, siman 32. Responsa Mas'at Binyamin rules in the name of most authorities according to Reish Lakish, that a gentile acquires from a Jew, and a Jew from a gentile, specifically through payment and not through meshichah. The Rama, in responsum 27, rules according to Rabbi Yochanan that specifically meshichah effects acquisition with a gentile, not payment. See Yoreh Deah, simanim 132 and 146. The Rashach, siman 116, writes that specifically meshichah effects acquisition by a gentile from a Jew or by a Jew from a gentile. For this reason he ruled that if one person bought merchandise from a gentile and paid but did not perform meshichah, and another then bought it and performed meshichah, the second acquires it. Responsa Mahari Weil, siman 209, writes that we are uncertain whether the law follows Rabbi Yochanan or Reish Lakish, and therefore both payment and meshichah are required. See Responsa Maharam Lublin, siman 31. The conclusion appears to me to be that since Rabbeinu Tam, Ri, and the Rosh hold that specifically meshichah effects acquisition with a gentile, and the Beit Yosef in siman 146, the Rama, and the Rashach also rule this way, and according to them the Tur also holds this view, while the Bach in siman 132 likewise writes so, this is the position we adopt.
