There, in the gloss: The intermediate difference. This is the Rema's view. He writes that whoever takes the superior property must pay his fellow money. Rabbeinu then writes that Rabbeinu Yonah likewise ruled that if they own superior and inferior land, one may say, “Take the superior land at its appraised value up to the value of the inferior land, and I will take the balance of the superior land, even though it does not contain the minimum measure, or you may do the same.” Rabbeinu Yonah's wording implies that it is not enough to say, as the Rema does, that whoever takes the superior land must add money. Rabbeinu Yonah holds that the other partner may say, “I want the superior land, but I do not have money to pay the excess. You cannot remove me from the property I desire through a buy or sell claim merely because I lack funds.” Therefore, the claimant must allow him to take the superior land in any event, with the claimant himself paying for the excess. Rabbeinu writes that Rabbeinu Yonah agrees because both maintain, unlike the Ramban, that buy or sell applies where one item is inferior and one superior. One should not ask why Rabbeinu Yonah does not add here, as he did above and as the Mechaber cites in seif 10, that the claimant must say, “If you do not want the small one, I will take everything.” There, the smaller property lacks the minimum measure of a field, and both alternatives offered to the other party must therefore be viable. Here, however, the inferior property does contain the minimum measure of a field. Since many authorities disagree with the Ramban, the halachah follows the Rema, and not the dissenting view cited by the Mechaber. Still, the question discussed above remains: must the party taking the superior property accept payment of the excess even though the remaining portion lacks the minimum measure? The authorities disagree on this, and the practical halachah requires study.
