They reside in it year by year. See Chatam Sofer, Choshen Mishpat, siman 179, concerning two people who owned a house jointly, with upper and lower dwellings. They divided the use so that for every two-year period one would occupy the lower dwelling and the other the upper, and then they would exchange and repeat the cycle. Before the time came to exchange, the upper story burned and became unfit for habitation. The question was whether the loss falls upon the partner whose turn it was to occupy the upper story, or upon both, so that they should now share the lower dwelling. Chatam Sofer rules that a division by time is a complete division with legal effect. According to the opinion that applies bereirah, it is clarified retroactively that during each period that use belonged to that partner. According to the opinion that there is no bereirah, each partner nevertheless acquired a right to the fruits, meaning the use of the property, for his specified period, while the underlying body remained jointly owned. He compares this to Gittin 42a regarding a half-slave, half-free person: if an ox injures him on his master's day, payment belongs to the master, and on his own day to himself, but if the ox kills him, thereby consuming the principal, payment is divided because the body itself was jointly held. So too here. The destruction of the structure itself is a loss of principal and is borne by both partners. But the loss of the use for the two-year period falls upon the partner whose turn it was. When that two-year period ends, if the upper story has not yet been rebuilt, the partner then entitled to the upper dwelling may remove the other from the lower dwelling and occupy the lower dwelling for his own two-year period, until they jointly rebuild the upper story and restore the prior arrangement. This is straightforward, and the discussion of Sema in siman 321 does not alter it.
