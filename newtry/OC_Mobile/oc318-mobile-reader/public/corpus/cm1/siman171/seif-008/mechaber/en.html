If each partner says, 'I will not sell; I wish to purchase the other's share,' or neither wishes to buy or sell and they prefer to remain partners, then, if the property is ordinarily rented, they rent it out and divide the rent. Rama: Either may say to the other, 'Rent it yourself for this amount, or I will rent it for this amount,' under the principle of buy or sell. Wherever the parties can resolve the matter through buy or sell, that method is preferable to the other forms of alternating use discussed here. If the property is not ordinarily rented, and it is a courtyard, shop, or house in which they cannot live together, they occupy it in alternating years, because they cannot both live there due to visual intrusion. Since the property is not divisible and people are not expected to move from one courtyard to another every thirty days, the alternation is yearly. Rama: Some say that a courtyard must be used jointly, because it is appurtenant to each partner's separate house. For that reason, buy or sell does not apply to such a courtyard. This applies specifically to the four cubits required in front of each doorway and to the access route needed for entry and exit. The remainder of the courtyard is subject to buy or sell. See below, seif 17. If one owns two thirds and the other one third, they cast two lots, one for one year and one for two years. The owner of two thirds then uses it for two consecutive years. If the property is a bathhouse, both may enter it every day. The same applies to anything that can be used jointly and is not ordinarily rented, such as a Torah scroll or bedding. One partner cannot say, 'You use it one day and I will use it the next,' because the other may answer, 'I wish to use it every day.' If it cannot be used jointly, they divide its use by days. Rama: If they did not divide its use and one partner used it for several years, the other cannot later demand an equal number of years, because until division each partner is deemed to have used his own property.
