Nevertheless, even if their uses are the same, when their values are not equal there is a dispute whether one may compel the other to take whichever one he chooses, with the recipient of the superior property paying the difference, as the Mechaber concludes at the end of this seif.
We do not listen to him. The Tur explains that the other can say, 'One is enough for me, since each can be used independently.' Meaning, unlike land where a full measure of four amot may be needed for the use to function at all. In the Derishah I wrote that Rashi's wording appears to show that if he gives the other a full choice and says, 'Either take whichever one you want and pay me the difference, or take both and pay me the value of my share in both, or I will do one of those alternatives,' we do listen to him.
Meaning, they divide one against the other. But certainly he cannot say, 'Buy both from me or sell me both,' because the other can say, 'One is enough for me.' Even sequentially, it appears he cannot force buy-or-sell where their uses are identical. This resembles two houses jointly owned where neither is large enough for physical division: each takes one, and one cannot use the buy-or-sell rule simply to dislodge the other from his inheritance.
That one of them take one without adding money. It appears that all agree to this, even those who disagree in seif 10 because one may say, 'I do not want a gift, as one who hates gifts will live.' Here the other can answer: if you do not want the gift portion, pay me the difference, or take the other property instead. The second item is still useful to him, unlike the earlier case where the smaller portion is not fit for his use at all.
Some disagree with this. The Tur explains their reason: a superior and inferior item are like two different species. The Mechaber carefully writes 'with this,' because where both use and value are equal no one disagrees.
