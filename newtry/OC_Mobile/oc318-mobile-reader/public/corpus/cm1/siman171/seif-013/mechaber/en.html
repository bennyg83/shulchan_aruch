Where two jointly owned items do not serve the same use, such as two maidservants, one skilled in baking and cooking and the other in spinning, or two implements used for different tasks, and each partner needs both, then even if the items are equal in value neither may say, 'You take this one and I will take the other,' because each needs both. The same applies where they own a field and a vineyard, neither of which is independently divisible. Even if their values are equal, one may not say, 'You take the field and I will take the vineyard.' Even if he proposes buy or sell with respect to both at once, he is not heard. He may instead invoke buy or sell with respect to one while the other remains jointly owned, or invoke buy or sell successively with respect to each item. If two items have the same use and equal value, and neither is divisible, either partner may certainly say buy or sell, meaning that the items are set against one another and each partner takes one. The same applies if their values are unequal. Rama: There is no distinction whether one partner pays the other the difference in value, or says that the other may take one item without an additional payment while he takes the second, or the reverse. Some disagree with this ruling.
