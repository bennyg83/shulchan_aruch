Seif 13: If he seeks both of them together, he is not heard. The Sema writes in seif katan 31 that this is evident from Rashi's wording. His intent appears to be based on Rashi's statement: “This is not a claim of buy or sell unless he says, ‘Take both of them, or I will take both of them.’” The phrase “unless” implies that the validity of the buy or sell claim depends on this. When the claimant must say, “Take both of them,” as the Sema explains, that claim then assists him regarding the additional payment. Accordingly, Rashi also agrees with the Rema cited by the Tur. The Beit Yosef, however, quotes Rashi as saying, “Rather, he says to him, ‘Take both of them,’” implying that this alone is insufficient, unlike the Rema. In my humble opinion, in every case the claimant need not offer the elaborate alternatives described by the Sema. Even if he says, “Take one corresponding to the other, with an additional payment, either you or I,” and he is content with either one, or if he says, “Take both of them,” his claim is valid, even though he does not say, “Or I will take both.” This follows from the Tur's citation of Rabbeinu Yonah in seif 15 concerning one large and one small property. There, one may invoke buy or sell regarding the additional payment, but must add, “If you do not want the small one, I will take everything.” The same applies here. He may say, “If you do not want one property alone, take both.” The cases are identical, since with one large and one small property the uses are unequal just as here. Just as there he cures the hardship imposed on the defendant by undertaking to take everything, so too here he cures the hardship by allowing the defendant to take both. The Rema's statement that there is no buy or sell claim means only that there is no such claim without combining it with this additional offer. One should not ask why the Rema and the Mechaber wrote only, “If he wishes, he may leave it in partnership,” for they stated a stronger case: the claimant need not give his fellow both alternatives, and even one suffices.
