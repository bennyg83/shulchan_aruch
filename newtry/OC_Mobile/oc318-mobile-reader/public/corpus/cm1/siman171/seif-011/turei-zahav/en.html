Seif 15: Thirty days, he is not heard. Rabbeinu, citing the Rema, discusses this at length and writes that the defendant is granted time unless it is evident that he merely intends to evade the matter, namely, where he did not request time at the outset. Many raise a contradiction within the Rema's words. He first writes that if the defendant says, “You buy,” and then the defendant requests time, time is granted, even though he did not initially request a deferred payment. It appears that only in the latter case, where the claimant explicitly says, “Buy or sell, but give me time,” and the defendant makes no response, is it evident that the request is merely evasive. In the first case, however, the claimant initially made an unqualified buy or sell demand. The defendant responded, “You buy and pay cash,” because he prefers receiving cash to buying the property himself even on deferred terms. When the claimant then says, “Wait thirty days for me,” the defendant did not say, “I will buy and you may defer payment,” because he still prefers the cash that the claimant initially offered. The claimant is now trying to change the terms, and the defendant need not consent. By contrast, in the latter case the claimant expressly offered buy or sell on deferred terms and did not wish to buy for cash at all. In the Rema's text there, the phrase means: perhaps he will go and sell cheaply, because he relies upon this arrangement and may sell his property for far less than its value. The word appearing in the Tur is a scribal error.
