In my view, where one owner has four cubits and the other three, the one with four can say: either take the three cubits as your share and divide with me, or I will take three cubits and as to the remainder invoke gud o agud. This is effectively offering the other the full minimum usable share or buying the whole. But where each has only three cubits, one cannot say, "I will take two cubits and leave one jointly held, then invoke gud o agud on the remainder." The other can answer, "Why should you choose the two? I can do the same." If each takes two cubits, nothing remains with the minimum usable measure. Nor can the first say that he needs only two cubits for spreading produce and wants the remaining cubit jointly held. The second can insist on an equal division of whatever can be divided, and then the residual shared area will no longer be suitable for its intended use. This prevents the proposed division. The case of four against three differs because the three-cubit owner can receive his entire share in a distinct minimum unit without destroying the remaining minimum unit.
