Gloss, seif 1, "Reuven and Shimon." Note: see what Dagul MeRevavah questioned concerning this ruling. In my opinion, the Responsa of the Rosh, rule 5, siman 3, clearly means as follows. Shimon cannot object to Reuven's erecting a partition merely because Shimon would thereby become second in position, since Reuven built on his own property. But Shimon may object for another reason. Originally, when they jointly acquired the place and sat together in a single seating area without a partition, there was no distinction between Reuven's and Shimon's seats. Both were in the foremost seating area and were equal, so neither paid the other any additional value. If Reuven may now erect a partition so that only he occupies the foremost position, his place will increase in value. From the outset they did not enter the partnership on the understanding that he could erect a partition and thereby obtain the foremost seat exclusively. Thus, Shimon cannot object merely because of a loss of honor, but he may object because Reuven's property would gain monetary value at his expense. Study the responsum and you will see that this is its meaning. See my Responsa Chatam Sofer, Orach Chaim, siman 26.
