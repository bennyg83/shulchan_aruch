Dagul MeRevavah notes that Responsa Rosh, siman 7, appears to imply the opposite, namely that the restriction depends on the fact that there was originally a board closing the head of the bench. He also notes an apparent internal tension in Responsa Rosh, klal 3, siman 3. He suggests that in siman 7 the board itself marked the bench as a distinct seating unit, and removing it to create another seat would change the arrangement and diminish the status of those already seated. Where no such board or distinct seating division exists, one may add a seat so long as he does not appropriate common open space, because the open area of the synagogue belongs equally to all and no individual may take it for himself. Chatam Sofer, Choshen Mishpat, siman 26, resolves the Rosh differently. In siman 3 two men sat side by side on a bench without a partition. Reuven wished to install a partition within his own allotted space. Shimon objected that this would make Reuven's seat appear superior and his own inferior. The Rosh's point is that mere honor or prestige is not a legally protected property interest. A person may make a change within his own boundary even if his neighbor thereby receives less honor, unless local custom assigns a monetary value to the superior seat and the partition would actually increase one seat's value while decreasing the other's. In that event there is a real financial injury and the partition may be restrained. Siman 7, however, concerns a different case: many people shared one bench, closed by boards at its ends. The foremost occupant wished to remove the end board and add another seat. There the Rosh objected because the end board was jointly owned by all the occupants. He was therefore altering common property in order to enhance his own status and diminish theirs, even if no measurable monetary loss resulted. With this distinction, the rulings of the Rosh and Rama are consistent. Chatam Sofer discusses the issue again in siman 29 and applies related principles to structural alterations in a women's gallery.
