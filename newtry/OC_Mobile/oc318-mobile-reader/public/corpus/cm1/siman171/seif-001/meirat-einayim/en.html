Whether one acquired, etc. The Tur cites Rambam's wording at the end of this siman. See what I write there and what the Mechaber writes below in seif 9. The phrase here 'or inherited' is also in the Tur. Since there are opinions that distinguish between inheritance and purchase with respect to the rule of buy or sell, as the Tur writes later in seif 33, the ruling teaches that for ordinary partition all agree there is no difference. See the Perishah.
They cannot retract. The responsum concludes that this is not considered a mere kinyan on words. Even in division of a courtyard, whose visual damage the Tur in siman 197 compares to smoke damage for which chazakah does not help, a kinyan nevertheless is effective, as the Tur and Mechaber write at the end of siman 155:1.
Likewise, many who sit, etc. Rama's word 'likewise' is imprecise, because the case does not resemble the one before it. In the first ruling, one cannot object when the other seeks to alter the arrangement after the binding division. In this ruling, one can object. Darkei Moshe does not present them as analogous, but says, 'He further wrote,' because both come from Responsa Rosh.
