Seif 8, gloss: They did not divide, and one of the partners used it, etc. Note: I am uncertain whether this ruling applies specifically to a dwelling that is intended for the partners' own use and not for rental. In such a case, because it is not ordinarily rented, if one partner remained silent while the other used it, he effectively disclosed that he did not presently need his share, and the other may use it alone. But where the property is ordinarily rented for profit, such as property made for rental, perhaps the rule is different. Since it stood to earn rent, a partner who uses it himself may be viewed as having entered on the understanding that he will pay the same rent that an outsider would pay. Even if he did not subjectively intend this, perhaps he must pay because otherwise his use deprives his fellow of the rent that could have been earned. He cannot damage the other by occupying the other's share without payment. This requires study. I am also uncertain whether the claim “I was using my own share” applies only to tangible property, such as a house or object, or even to an incorporeal franchise, such as a lease of the exclusive right to distill liquor, slaughter animals, or similar governmental privileges under which users must pay a set fee to the leaseholder. If two people jointly lease such a right and one conducts much more business than the other, perhaps at year's end he must pay for his use as outsiders would, because there is no tangible object of which he can say, “I used my share,” and additional outsiders would otherwise have generated more fees. On the other hand, perhaps he may still say, “I used my share; you too could have used yours or protested at the outset.” The Magen Avraham in siman 131 regarding Kaddish appears to imply that in such an intangible matter the claim “I used my share” does not apply. The matter requires study.
