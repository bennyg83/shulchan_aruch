House. It appears that this is why the Tur writes that no single house contains a sufficient share for division. If one house did contain enough, they would divide that house and leave the others in partnership, and one could not compel the other to divide all the houses by assigning one house against another. When none of the houses is independently divisible, however, they divide by assigning houses against houses. This requires practical study, contrary to the Bach. See Responsa Rashach, part 3, siman 122. Shach.
In a responsum. The Sema writes that this does not appear to him from the Rosh's responsum, which the Tur cites in seif 28. There the matter depends on the son's will. If he does not wish to remain in partnership with the widow, he cannot say to her, 'Either buy the whole house or I will sell it all and give you half the proceeds.' Rather, the widow is entitled to have the entire shop assigned against the house, with an equalizing payment between them. If he wishes to remain in partnership with the widow, however, she cannot compel him to make such an arrangement. The Taz also agrees. The Shach writes that the Rosh's concluding words, 'all the more so in this case, where the upper story...' somewhat imply the Rama's view.
And the like. This includes promissory notes whose money is to be collected in the future, with respect to which gud o agud cannot be used, contrary to what the Tur and Mechaber write at the end of siman 66. The Rama does not cite the dissenting view there. See what I wrote there in partial reconciliation. Sema. The Taz writes that the Rama relied there on what he wrote here, since this is the primary source for the law of gud o agud.
Rental. The Maggid Mishneh's words show that according to the Rambam there is a distinction. Rental is different because the parties themselves rented the property and pay rent, but they do not ultimately collect a principal sum. Therefore ordinary division does not apply, and gud o agud is used. Thus far the Sema. This resolves the questions of the Beit Hadash and Bach. Shach.
