But from the words of the Rosh in a responsum, etc. See Sema, seif katan 38, who writes: See the Perishah, where I explained that this does not appear to me to follow from the Rosh's words there, through his statement, 'But if he wishes to remain with the widow.' Nevertheless, from the Rosh's concluding words, 'All the more so in this case, where the upper story,' there is some indication supporting the Rama's view. This is easily understood.
It is like a rental, etc. See the glosses to the Darkei Moshe, where I wrote that the words of the Maggid Mishneh prove that, according to the Rambam, there is a distinction between the cases. Rental is different, because they rented the property and pay for its use, but do not ultimately collect money from one another. Therefore a monetary division is not relevant, and the rule of 'buy me out or I will buy you out' applies. This is the language of the Sema. This resolves the objections of the Baal HaItur and the Bach; see there and examine it carefully.
