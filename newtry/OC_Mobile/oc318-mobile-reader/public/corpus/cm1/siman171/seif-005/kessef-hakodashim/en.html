And one of them used it. Rashba rules that if partners did not divide their property and one partner alone used it for a period, the other cannot later demand an equal exclusive period. The reason appears to be that this is the nature of their partnership acquisition: when one uses it, it is as though it becomes retroactively clarified that his share applied to that period alone, similar to the Ran's discussion at the beginning of Nedarim concerning 'I enter my own share.' More fundamentally, Rashba's reason may be that the second partner could have demanded division or an alternating-use arrangement. Since he did not, he allowed his fellow's use to stand under the legal framework then applicable. But if one partner fled under compulsion, or was absent because of an error, or someone acted in his place without full authorization, it appears Rashba would concede that afterward he may use the common property alone for an equivalent period. Beit Yosef implies that Rashba's ruling concerns a case where 'buy or sell' is available and the partners cannot force alternating years. Rama, however, extends the rule even where 'buy or sell' does not apply and the only practical arrangement is alternating years. In that case one must say the absent partner left voluntarily and not through compulsion. If there had already been an alternating-year arrangement, even without formal authorization, it does not appear that one partner's failure to object should be treated as consent to losing his reciprocal year.
