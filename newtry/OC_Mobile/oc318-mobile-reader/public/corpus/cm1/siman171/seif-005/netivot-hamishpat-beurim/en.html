At first glance this is difficult, for in any event it is comparable to two things whose uses are equal but whose values are unequal, discussed in seif 13, concerning which some disagree. It may be that the cases differ. There the discussion concerns superior and inferior property, and the reason is as the Sema writes in seif katan 34, that good and bad property are regarded as two distinct types, because one cannot transform inferior property into superior property. Here, by contrast, with the money added to compensate him one can construct additional rooms so that his portion will equal the other portion. The same applies where one side borders a public thoroughfare and the other side an alley, meaning that one side opens onto the alley, as explained by the Tur. The case is where, through expenditures, one can arrange that this side too will open onto the public thoroughfare. Nevertheless, not all authorities agree with this ruling. According to the Rivash, whose view is explained below in seif 14, there is no law of compelled division in houses at all where expenditures are required, and it appears that this is so even if one party is willing to add the amount of those expenditures; see there.
