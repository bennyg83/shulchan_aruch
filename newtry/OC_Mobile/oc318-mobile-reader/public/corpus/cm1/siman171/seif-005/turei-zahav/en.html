Seif 11: Except for one cubit. Yet this partner insists upon receiving at least his three cubits. On this basis, the responsum of a Gaon cited by Rabbeinu in seif 19 can be explained. There, Shimon says, “Either buy all of it from me or sell all of it to me.” The Gaon ruled that Reuven's two portions have certainly already been removed from the dispute, because they belong to him entirely. Shimon's claim is therefore nullified. Reuven is required only to leave Shimon enough so that, together with the remaining area, there will be the minimum measure of a courtyard, and concerning that combined portion he may say, “Buy or sell.” The Gaon holds, as Rabbeinu writes shortly below, that the rule of buy or sell applies only where there is the minimum measure of a courtyard. This resolves the Beit Yosef's unresolved difficulty. One should not object from what I wrote in seif 4, that when a person owns an adjoining field his portion is deemed clarified. Here the portion of the field was not clarified in any respect. Rather, he now seeks to take all his portions together.
