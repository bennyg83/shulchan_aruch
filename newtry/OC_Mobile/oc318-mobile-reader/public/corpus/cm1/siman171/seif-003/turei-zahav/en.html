Seif 4: His fellow can compel him. The wording implies that the Rema's words in the Tur are understood as follows: if Reuven owns land adjoining the jointly owned field, Shimon can compel Reuven to divide. The Sema quotes the wording of Rabbeinu and explains why Rabbeinu did not cite the Rema's statement. It appears to me, however, that the Rema's words should be explained differently. He speaks of the partner who seeks division. If that partner has another field adjoining the jointly owned field, then, had the jointly owned field contained the minimum measure required for division, they would have been obligated to allot him his portion adjacent to his own field, as stated in siman 174 regarding two fields irrigated by one channel. Since he possesses this right, we say that his portion is known and identifiable. He therefore can compel his fellow to divide even though the latter will not receive the minimum measure for division. This is like the ruling stated shortly below, that if his portion is identifiable, he may be compelled to divide even when there is no minimum measure. Here too, it is considered identifiable, since if there were a sufficient measure they would have had to give him the portion adjoining his field. His portion is therefore deemed clarified. This appears entirely clear to me, without difficulty.
