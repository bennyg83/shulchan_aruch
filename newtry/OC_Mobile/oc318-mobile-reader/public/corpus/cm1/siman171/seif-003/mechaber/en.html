What constitutes property subject to compulsory division? Any property that, when divided among the partners, leaves even the partner with the smallest share a portion that still bears the name of the whole. If the name of the whole does not apply to the resulting portion, there is no right to compel division. Thus, a courtyard is not called a courtyard unless it contains a square area of four by four cubits for each partner, apart from the four cubits allotted in front of the entrances. A field is not called a field unless it contains enough land to sow nine kav. A garden is not called a garden unless it contains enough land to sow half a kav. An orchard is not called an orchard unless it contains enough land to sow three kav. Therefore, a courtyard is not divided unless each partner will receive four cubits, a field unless each will receive land for nine kav of seed, a garden unless each will receive land for half a kav, and an orchard unless each will receive land for three kav. These measures apply in Eretz Yisrael and similar places. In Babylonia and similar places, a field is not divided unless each partner receives enough land for one day's plowing, and an orchard unless each receives thirty-six trees, the amount one worker can tend in one day. An irrigated field is not divided unless each partner receives enough to occupy a worker for one full day drawing water. Rama: If property was subject to division and one partner sold his share to two persons, or bequeathed it to two sons, neither purchaser or heir may prevent division merely because his own resulting share is now below the minimum, since they have no greater right than the person from whom they derive title. If four persons are partners, two have shares large enough for division and two do not, the first two take their divisible shares and the remaining two stay partners. Even if one of those with a smaller share says, 'Give me my share as well,' so that the fourth will be left with less than the minimum, and he says this solely to defeat the division, he is not heard. If three persons are partners in a house, two have divisible shares and one does not, and one of the two purchases the share of the partner who lacked a divisible portion, he cannot thereby defeat the division, although the seller could previously have done so.
