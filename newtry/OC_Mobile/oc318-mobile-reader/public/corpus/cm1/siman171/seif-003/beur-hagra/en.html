If it was... that he does not. As in Bava Metzia 101b. Although Bava Metzia 35a sometimes treats sale or inheritance differently, that is because of the special requirement of "you shall do what is right and good."
If there were... in order to cancel. Bava Basra 12b says that in such a case one compels the other under the rule against Sodom-like conduct, and here all agree.
Three... he cannot. One claimant cannot invoke a rabbinic enactment in a way that would force allocation of a particular boundary where the enactment was intended only to protect against loss. The principle "I do not want the Sages' enactment" applies where the enactment is being used to take from him without corresponding loss, as Ri Baal HaTosafos explains regarding medium-quality land for a creditor. Similarly, one cannot say he will return the document to the first seller or invoke "whatever the first sold to the second," because these are not substantive property rights and a stated desire contrary to the legal structure does not create them.
