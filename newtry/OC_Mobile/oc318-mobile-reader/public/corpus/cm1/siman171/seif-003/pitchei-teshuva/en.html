And the house will suffer a loss, etc. Meaning that the loss exceeds one fifth. See Responsa Chatam Sofer, Choshen Mishpat siman 12, who questions this ruling. Terumat HaDeshen, siman 336, derives it from the sugya of one riding and one leading in the first chapter of Bava Metzia, where the Gemara asks why they should divide if division will cause a loss, and distinguishes a kosher animal that can be used for meat. Although oxen and cows ordinarily sell for more as working animals than as meat, the difference there is only somewhat more than one sixth, as Tosafot indicate at the beginning of chapter HaManiach, and the nearest standard measure above one sixth is one fifth. Such a difference does not prevent division. Chatam Sofer argues that the cases may differ. In the Gemara we are uncertain who the true owner is, so division itself already causes the true owner to lose half. We do not add another loss to his remaining half by making a destructive division. Here, however, heirs inherited from their father property that is by law subject to division. From the outset, then, each effectively inherited only half the house subject to the reduction produced by lawful division. Chatam Sofer therefore suggests that the stated rule applies only to heirs. With ordinary partners, the overwhelming majority of authorities apply either the rule of buy or sell or physical division because each partner can say, "I thought I could tolerate the partnership, but now I cannot." It follows that they originally entered the partnership on the assumption that they would not divide while they could live together. If circumstances later make continued partnership intolerable, one partner should not lose one fifth of his share merely because the other cannot tolerate the partnership; the property is then treated like something not reasonably divisible. Nevertheless, this need for a claim of inability to tolerate applies where one party alone demands division or buy or sell against the other. If both want to separate, they may demand division or buy or sell even without that rationale. No authority ever disputed this, and this is not what Maharshadam or Knesset HaGedolah, siman 171, Hagahot Tur seif katan 50, intended. See there.
