Below. Meaning, there the Mechaber rules without qualification like the Rosh that property rented for a fixed period is not divided. To avoid a contradiction in the Mechaber's rulings, the Sema explains that here the text mentions a field before a courtyard, writing "part of a field or courtyard." This implies a courtyard comparable to a field, where there is no visual intrusion, and the division can therefore be made without a partition. In siman 316, by contrast, the case concerns a house, which ordinarily involves visual intrusion and would require partitioning. The rule there teaches that rented property is not to be divided by erecting a partition. See what the Sema writes further in siman 316. The Shach writes that this answer does not satisfy him. He distinguishes instead as follows: if one wishes to divide because of visual intrusion, it is obvious that the other can be compelled. In siman 316, however, the claimant revealed that he does not object to the visual intrusion but merely does not wish to share with another person, and one cannot compel division of property they do not own. See Responsa Maharashdam, siman 158. The Taz likewise challenges the Sema. In his view, when parties rent a place jointly they certainly intend from the outset not to incur the expense of building a partition in property that will not remain theirs permanently. Thus, anyone who wishes to eliminate visual intrusion would otherwise spend his money for nothing. Accordingly, when the Mechaber says here that they divide, he means division of use of the courtyard itself. In siman 316, when he says that one cannot compel the other to divide, he means that one cannot compel him to contribute to a partition wall.
