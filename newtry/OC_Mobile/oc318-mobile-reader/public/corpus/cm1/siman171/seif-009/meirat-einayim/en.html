Because division does not apply to property rented for a fixed term, etc. This is the Tur's language in the name of the Rosh at the end of siman 316. Here too the Tur comments upon the Rambam's language, which is like the Mechaber's, and expresses surprise: How can they divide by erecting a partition something that is not theirs but merely rented? This appears to indicate that an ordinary division mentioned by the authorities means a physical partition, contrary to what I wrote above. Still, to prevent contradiction between the Mechaber's rulings, one can distinguish. Here he begins, "one who rents part of a field or courtyard," placing field before courtyard. This may indicate a courtyard comparable to a field, where visual privacy damage does not apply, and the division here is therefore without a partition. In siman 316 he speaks of a house, where visual privacy damage ordinarily exists, and therefore teaches that rented property is not divided by permanent partitions. The Tur, however, cites the Rambam with courtyard before field, which suggests an ordinary courtyard requiring visual separation, and therefore objects. Rama cites dissenters here and in siman 316 because of the Rambam's textual formulation as found by the Tur. See what I write there.
