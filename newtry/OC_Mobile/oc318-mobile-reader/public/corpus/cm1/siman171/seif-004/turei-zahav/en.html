Seif 9: If it contains the minimum measure for division. The Sema raises a contradiction between the Shulchan Aruch here and siman 316, and answers that there the case involves visual intrusion. This is untenable, for if so, why does the Shulchan Aruch rule there that one cannot compel the other to divide merely because the house is not theirs? Since the other causes him harm through visual intrusion, he should be compelled. This also refutes the answer of my revered teacher, of blessed memory, who said that there the Rosh did not mean that the claimant himself wishes to live behind a partition, but that he seeks to compel division so that he may later install another occupant, and he cannot impose expenses on his fellow for that purpose. The same difficulty remains: from where would there be any right to compel division where there is no visual intrusion? If the claim is that henceforth there will be visual intrusion, then he indeed may compel him. It appears to me that the Shulchan Aruch can be reconciled as follows. Where partners rent a place jointly, they presumably intended from the outset not to incur the expense of building a partition in a place that is not permanently theirs. Anyone who wishes to remove visual intrusion should not have to spend money needlessly. Thus, when the Shulchan Aruch says here that they divide, it means that they divide the use of the courtyard itself. In siman 316, where it says that one cannot compel the other to divide, it means that he cannot compel him to build a partition at the partners' expense. Accordingly, the ruling here does not disagree with the Tur, who wrote, “It is surprising, how can he build a partition?” Indeed, one cannot compel him to build a partition. Although the Beit Yosef, who wrote that there is no difficulty, did not distinguish this explicitly, the Shulchan Aruch intended this distinction. The Rama also did not understand it this way, and therefore wrote that some disagree. In truth, however, the correct halachic view is as I have written, that one cannot compel his fellow to incur a futile expense.
