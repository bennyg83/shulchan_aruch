His fellow can compel him to divide. The Tur's wording in seif 3 is more qualified: if the case is such that, were there a full measure for division, they would have to give him the portion adjoining his own boundary, for example where both fields lie along one boundary, then even now he can compel division. In the Perishah I wrote that in many cases one need not give him specifically the part adjoining his boundary, as the Tur and Mechaber explain in siman 174. The Mechaber abbreviated and wrote generally that if he has an adjoining field he can compel division, because at the beginning of siman 174 he rules like Rambam that ordinarily one gives a person the share adjoining his existing boundary. Alternatively, one may distinguish this case: the other partner has a share large enough for division and, from his own standpoint, could divide; only because the smaller partner lacks the minimum measure did the law force him to remain in partnership so as not to harm the smaller one. Since the smaller partner can combine his share with his adjoining field, there is no reason to compel the other to remain his partner. The Tur may cite the formulation about what would have happened in an ordinary divisible case merely as an illustration.
