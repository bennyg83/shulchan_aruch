(Seif 3: Enough land for one day of plowing, etc.) The Sema writes that if each party’s share would provide one and a half days of work, laborers can be hired for that period. This is surprising, for the Gemara explains the opposite. It asks what is meant by “one day of plowing.” If it means a day measured by plowing, there may not be a full day’s work at sowing time, because sowing takes less time than plowing. How, then, will he hire laborers for the sowing? If it means a day measured by sowing, there will not be two full days of plowing. Thus, where there is one and a half days of plowing, it may still be difficult to hire workers for the partial day. Why, then, does the Tur state generally that the field is divided whenever each party receives a full day of plowing, implying that division is certainly made when each receives more? It appears to me that if each party’s potential share equals one and a half days of plowing, the field is divided only to the extent that each receives an area requiring one full day. The surplus remains jointly owned. The Gemara’s question does not arise merely because the field contains one day of plowing per share. It asks what each owner will do at sowing time. The Gemara concludes that a day of plowing is also a day of sowing for purposes of this measure. Accordingly, if the field contains one and a half days of plowing for each, they divide the usable one-day portion and leave the remainder in partnership. The loss involved in jointly hiring one worker for the remainder is not as great as the loss that would fall upon one owner if he had to pay a worker for a full day despite having only a partial day’s work. This appears straightforward to me.
