So that they not harm one another by overlooking. The Mechaber follows his understanding in the Beit Yosef that the Tur here speaks of visual privacy damage. In the Perishah and Derishah I demonstrated that the Tur is speaking even of places where such damage does not apply, for his discussion includes the field mentioned at the beginning of the siman, and a field ordinarily has no rule of visual privacy damage, as explained in simanim 157 and 158. In siman 157 the Tur discusses visual privacy damage and the power to compel construction of a separating wall. Here, however, the subject is simply division, so that each party should use his own designated portion rather than the whole property jointly. When the Tur says that they compel one another to separate, he does not necessarily mean to erect a wall, but to establish a recognizable boundary so that one will not mistakenly use the other's portion. Even if each knows his own share, people sometimes cross the boundary without paying attention. The Maggid Mishneh similarly explains the Rambam because the Rambam states this law at the beginning of Hilchot Shechenim, where visual damage is also under discussion. The Tur and Mechaber, however, already separated the laws of visual damage in siman 157. See the Perishah. The following seif somewhat complicates this explanation.
