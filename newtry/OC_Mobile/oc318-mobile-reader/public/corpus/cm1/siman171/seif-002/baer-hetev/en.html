By visual intrusion. The Sema writes that the present rule applies even in places where visual intrusion is not relevant, because it also refers to the field mentioned at the beginning of the siman, where there is no visual intrusion, as explained in siman 157 and at the beginning of siman 158. The issue here is only the legal division of the property, so that each person may use the portion designated for him. The Tur's expression "to separate" does not literally mean that they must erect a partition, but that they must establish a recognizable boundary so that neither uses the other's area. Even if each knows which portion is his, a person may sometimes mistakenly use beyond his boundary. The Taz writes that the Sema is correct that the case does not involve visual intrusion. The beginning of Bava Batra clearly shows that even according to the opinion that visual intrusion is not legally cognizable harm, one must nevertheless erect a ten-handbreadth partition so that an intruder can be apprehended as a thief. The same applies to a field where visual intrusion is irrelevant. But the Sema's assertion that no partition is required, only a boundary marker, does not appear correct. At minimum a ten-handbreadth partition is required. The Maggid Mishneh likewise writes at the beginning of Hilchot Shechenim that where visual intrusion is absent, a ten-handbreadth partition is required. See the related discussion in simanim 157 and 446.
