The language of Rambam, chapter 1 of Hilkhot Shekhenim, law 2. Maggid Mishneh writes that this is disputed in the Gemara and that the halachah is decided there on Bava Batra 13b: where jointly owned property is not legally divisible, the law of "buy or sell" applies. The principal view follows Rambam, that either party may set whatever price he wishes and tell the other, "Either buy at this valuation or sell to me at it," whether the amount is below or above the appraisal of beit din.
Kesef Mishneh writes that Rambam appears to explain "gud o agud" according to Arukh as "draw or I will draw," meaning: I set this amount as the price of your share; either sell it to me for that amount or I will sell you my share for that amount. Rashi explains differently.
Maggid Mishneh writes that the Halachot explain that everything depends upon the party initiating the demand, even if the other partner is poor and cannot afford it. The later authorities agree.
Maggid Mishneh cites the baraita there on 13a: if the father made the property for rental, the rent is divided between them. Rambam understands this to apply so long as they wish to remain partners. But if one of them demands "buy or sell," he is heard even though the property stands for rental. Rashba agrees, and Rabbi Yosef ibn Migash writes likewise, though he leaves it requiring study. Rashbam explains that "made for rental" means the property is large and suited to rental, while "made for himself" means small and suited for personal use; the baraita merely describes the ordinary case, and the father's subjective intention does not control. Tosafot likewise write so.
