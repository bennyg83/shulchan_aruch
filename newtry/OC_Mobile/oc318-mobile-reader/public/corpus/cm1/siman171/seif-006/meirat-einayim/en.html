The father made them for rental, etc. This is the Gemara's language. The Tur writes that some say everything depends upon the father's intent, as the simple wording of the Gemara indicates. Ri explains that it does not depend on his intent. Even if the father made them for personal use, if a tenant can be found, the wealthy heir cannot prevent the poor heir from renting his portion. Conversely, even if the father intended them for rental, if no tenant can be found, the wealthy heir may say to the poor heir: Take servants for yourself and use the bathhouse, etc. According to Ri, the Gemara's phrase that the father made them for rental means that they are large enough and suitable for rental, so a tenant will ordinarily be found. The opposite applies to small properties. The Mechaber adopts the Rambam's language, which follows the Gemara. In the Derishah I discussed how the Maggid Mishneh, Beit Yosef, and Kesef Mishneh understand the Rambam here.
