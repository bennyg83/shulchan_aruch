The father made them for rental. Some say everything depends on the father's intent. Rabbi Yose explains that it does not depend on the father's intent: even if he made the property for his own use, if a tenant can be found one heir can compel the other to rent it. Conversely, even if the father made it for rental, if no tenant can be found, the wealthy heir may say to the poor heir, "Take servants for yourself," and so forth. Sema:
He says to the poor heir, "Take." This applies specifically where one heir is poor. If both are wealthy but do not find it convenient to use the property together, and neither wishes to invoke "buy or sell," they use it during alternating periods or rent it to others. Sema. See the Beurim to seif 8. Nevertheless, the poor heir may rent out his own share independently. See the Beurim:
He must sell his share. The rule of "buy or sell" applies only when one says, "I will buy it for my own use," not when he merely says, "I will sell it to others." But he may certainly sell his own share even according to the Mechaber. The purchaser of that share may then invoke "buy or sell." Likewise, he may borrow money from others, purchase the other share, and retain the property for himself according to all opinions. Sema. See the Beurim.
