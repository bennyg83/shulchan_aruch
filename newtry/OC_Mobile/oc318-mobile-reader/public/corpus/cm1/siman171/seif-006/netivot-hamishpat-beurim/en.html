The father made them for himself. See Mordechai at the end of the first chapter of Bava Batra. If the poor partner seeks to rent out his portion alone, he may rent it. Although the Mordechai later writes that he may not rent it, see Beit Yosef, who cites this, and the gloss printed in the Mordechai has already reconciled the passages.
When he has no money. It appears that the reverse is also true. If the wealthy partner invokes gud o agud, the poor partner can say, "I will buy it and sell it to others." Since there is another person prepared to pay more, the wealthy partner, even if he offers its market value, is effectively saying that the poor partner should sell for less than what it can bring, and he cannot make such a demand. Otherwise the wealthy could pressure the poor, as the Rama writes.
