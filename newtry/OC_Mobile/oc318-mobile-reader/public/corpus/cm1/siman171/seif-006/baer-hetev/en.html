To the poor person. A wealthy person can say to a poor person, 'Either buy my share or sell me yours,' but a poor person cannot make that demand of a wealthy person where he has no ability to buy. If he wishes instead to sell, the matter depends on the dispute of the Rambam and Rosh stated below. See Responsa Raanach, siman 16. Shach.
To others. Meaning, he says: Sell it to me, and I will then sell the whole property together to others. Sema.
His share. The Tur writes in the name of the Rosh that the rule of gud o agud applies only when one says, 'I will buy your share for my own use,' not where he says, 'I will arrange for the whole property to be sold and give you half the proceeds,' merely so that his own share will sell for more. Rather, he must sell his share for whatever price he can obtain. This is also the Rama's intent here when he says that one cannot invoke gud o agud if he lacks funds to retain the property for himself and merely proposes to sell the whole and give the other his share. He may sell only his own portion. If no one wishes to buy his share, that is his loss due to poverty. But if he can borrow from others and buy the property for himself, no one disputes that he may invoke gud o agud through borrowed funds. Thus far the Sema. This is correct, unlike the understanding of Imrei Binah, folio 91b, which expresses surprise only because it did not analyze carefully. See the Bach, who distinguishes in the Rosh's responsum where a large loss would result, in which case he may transfer the matter to others. The Sema's view appears stronger. See Responsa Maharashdam, siman 158. Shach.
Will pressure him. Meaning, when he cannot find another person to take his place, because ordinarily an outsider does not wish to enter into quarrel and dispute between two brothers. Sema.
