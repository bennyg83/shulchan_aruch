Who said to his fellow. See Responsa Rabbi Moshe Rothenburg, Choshen Mishpat siman 19, who writes regarding his fourth case, cited above in siman 111, seif 13: Why should his claim not be effective if he says to both disputants together, "Whichever of you ultimately owns the land, I do not wish to remain in partnership with him. Either buy my share from me or I will buy yours"? This resembles one who owns half a parcel while two other people dispute ownership of the other half and it is not yet known which of them owns it. Could it be imagined that he cannot assert the rule of buy or sell against both, if both deposit the money with beit din and withdraw from the uncertainty? See there.

For less than its value. See Responsa Rabbi Moshe Rothenburg, siman 19, who writes in his case that we find an even greater possibility: the buy or sell claim may indirectly cause the other litigant to receive less than he could otherwise obtain. Although the Rama writes that one cannot demand buy or sell at less than fair value, that means less than the proportional value of the property as a whole. For example, if buyers would pay four hundred for the entire parcel, one cannot offer less than two hundred for the other half under a buy or sell demand, because otherwise a wealthy person could pressure a poor person into selling below value. But suppose a buyer can be found for one half at two hundred, while no buyer can be found for the whole property at four hundred. This commonly occurs because a purchaser lacks enough money for the whole or needs only half. When one partner properly compels the other under the buy or sell rule, valuation is based on the value of the entire property together, because the very basis of the claim is that he refuses to remain in partnership with anyone. The appraisal therefore cannot be made as though each half were an independently marketable parcel. It follows that a valid buy or sell demand can indeed cause the other party to lose the premium that he could have obtained by selling his half separately. See there.
