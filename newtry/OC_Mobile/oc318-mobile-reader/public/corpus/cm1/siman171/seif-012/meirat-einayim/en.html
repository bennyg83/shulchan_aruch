Even in something that as a whole is not fit, etc. The Tur, seif 17, writes: if the entire courtyard contains only three amot, Ramban says the rule of buy or sell applies. The Tur says this does not appear correct, because the other can say, 'Granted that even now it is not useful to me, but I do not wish to buy for money something that is itself too small for my use.' The Mechaber omits the case of an ordinary courtyard because there the rule of buy or sell does not apply in any event when the courtyard is not a self-standing property but merely serves the houses, as the Tur writes and Rama explains in seif 8. The dispute instead applies to a rear courtyard or similar independent property. Nimukei Yosef writes likewise. See the Perishah and Derishah.
Some disagree. We already explained that the Tur is the one who disagrees. Nevertheless, the Tur concludes in the name of Ramah that if the property contains only three amot, since it is not fit for full use but is fit for spreading fruit or a similar limited use, division itself may still be possible: when divided, each receives a portion that remains fit for that same use, and this can still be called a case where after division the original name remains upon the property. Although the Tur then cites the Rosh as disagreeing, that disagreement is specifically with respect to a courtyard in front of houses, where neither division nor buy-or-sell applies for the reason stated above. In other types of property it appears no one disagrees that where each divided share remains fit for the same limited use, division is made. See the Perishah and Derishah.
