We do not heed him. The Tur concludes in the name of the Ramah that if the defendant asks for time, it is granted even though the claimant is prepared to pay immediately. But if the claimant asks for time, it is not granted, and the defendant is not compelled to enter now into the rule of buy or sell. A practical consequence is that during the delay the defendant may obtain money with which to buy, or may find someone willing to purchase his share for more, or if the claimant later brings the money and the defendant then asks time to seek funds, he is given time from that point, not from the first demand. The Ramah further writes that not in every circumstance is such additional time granted. The Tur also cites him as ruling that even if the claimant says, "Let the defendant now acquire from me, effective after thirty days, on condition that if he does not bring me money within thirty days the property will belong to me from now and after thirty days," we do not compel the defendant to accept this, even if the claimant deposits the money immediately with beit din. If the defendant fails to bring money after thirty days, the rule of buy or sell begins anew. He further writes that if one party demands buy or sell and the other agrees to buy or to sell at the agreed sum, the original claimant may still retract and say, "I no longer wish to divide, but will remain in partnership as before," so long as they did not make a valid kinyan. The defendant likewise may retract from buying to selling, or from selling to buying, so long as no kinyan was made. Therefore, if the defendant says, "I do not wish to trouble myself to seek money unless you transfer the property to me now, effective after thirty days if I bring the money within that time," his claim is valid, because otherwise he may sell his assets cheaply to raise funds only to have the claimant retract. But if he does not make this argument, beit din does not advance it for him. It is surprising that the Mechaber and Rama omitted all these uncontested details. I further demonstrated in the Perishah that one may retract from buying to selling or from the entire buy or sell procedure and remain partners, but one may not retract merely to raise the price and say, "Buy at this higher sum or I will buy at that amount.".
