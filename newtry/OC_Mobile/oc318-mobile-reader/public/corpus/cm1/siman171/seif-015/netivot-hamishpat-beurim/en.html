The distinction is whether the request for time was part of the first gud o agud demand. If at the outset the claimant says, "Buy my share, and give me time," then the defendant is not yet required to give a substantive response, and there is no basis to infer evasion. If, however, the claimant first makes an ordinary immediate gud o agud demand and the defendant responds that the claimant should buy, and only then does the claimant say, "Give me time," causing the proceeding to lapse, then when the claimant later renews gud o agud and the defendant asks for time, we do not grant it. The defendant's earlier response showed that he was unwilling to buy at that price when immediate payment was contemplated. His later request for time therefore appears evasive. Tur's phrase, "This applies when at first he said, 'I will buy, and give me time,'" refers to the first demand itself. The following clause means: but if at first he did not say "I will buy and give me time," but merely made a standard gud o agud demand, and only after the defendant responded did the claimant seek time and the proceeding was canceled, then later the defendant is not granted time when the claimant renews the demand. The core distinction is between a demand that was initially non-immediate and therefore never required a response, and a demand that was initially immediate and thereby elicited conduct showing unwillingness. Sema further says that after one retracts and wishes to remain partners he cannot then raise the price. Perishah suggests that after retracting he can raise the price. In my view that distinction is strained. There is no meaningful basis to make the power to raise the price depend on whether the retraction occurred immediately or after a brief interval.
