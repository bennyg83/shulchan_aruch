(Shach, seif katan 5) But Baal HaTerumot, beginning of gate 47, wrote, etc. Note: The Rashba's novellae to Kiddushin 27 cite this view in the name of the Raavad and disagree with it. The Raavad's formulation is cited in Shitah Mekubetzet to Bava Batra 77. Tosafot, Bava Metzia 14a, passage beginning "lien," write that because the purchaser has a deed of sale and the sale is void because the land was stolen, the purchase price is treated like a loan document with responsibility. This is difficult. Even without a document he should collect from encumbered property, because the reason an oral loan with witnesses does not collect from encumbered property is that a borrower ordinarily borrows privately. That rationale does not apply here. The original transaction was a public sale; only because the true owner later dispossessed the purchaser is the purchase price retroactively treated as a loan. There was therefore publicity by virtue of the sale. But if Tosafot follow Baal HaTerumot, this is understandable. I later saw that the Tumim, siman 70, seif katan 10, writes something similar, though in a different form. Still, one can reject the proof. Tosafot in Bava Metzia are discussing Shmuel's rule that the scribe should consult the seller and write enhanced clauses of title, improvement, and produce. The point is that when the true owner dispossesses the purchaser, the purchase price becomes retroactively a loan. Shmuel himself holds that ordinary liens are not Torah law, and according to his position even a loan with publicity cannot be collected from encumbered property without a written document, as appears from Tosafot, Bava Kamma 14b, passage beginning "we learn that a borrower sold." Be aware also of an apparent contradiction within Tosafot. In Sanhedrin 29b, passage beginning "he admitted before two," they ask why a person can retract from ordering a document written when he cannot retract from conveying the land itself. What practical difference does the document make? If it is a gift document there is no lien; if a sale document, even a sale before witnesses allows collection from encumbered property, as stated in Chezkas HaBatim. This implies that every witnessed sale, even without an explicit oral lien, creates a lien, contrary to the Rashba and Baal HaTerumot. Yet Tosafot to Ketubot 25a, passage beginning "write," cite Ri bar Mordechai in the name of Riva that one can retract specifically from a sale document because he does not wish to assume the lien created by the document, implying that a sale before witnesses without a document has no lien. One must say that Ri bar Mordechai interprets the rule that a witnessed seller permits collection from encumbered property like the Rashbam, namely where an explicit lien was undertaken. The Rosh in Sanhedrin also writes like Tosafot there, implying that even without an explicit lien a witnessed oral sale creates a lien. This requires study according to the Shach, siman 39, who explains the Rosh as holding that ordinary liens are not Torah law. If so, why should mere publicity without a document create a lien?
