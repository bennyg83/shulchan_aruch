Tumim questions this from Bava Metzia 32, where the Gemara asks, concerning one who sold stolen land, how the purchaser can collect the principal from encumbered property when the sale document was not authorized to be written. According to the present rule, what difference does that make? Set the deed aside. Even a sale made before witnesses alone should allow collection from encumbered property. In my view there is no difficulty. If one sells his land before witnesses, those witnesses are present and testify orally before us. By contrast, in a deed of sale the witnesses are not now before beit din. If the deed was not authorized to be written, reliance on the writing is testimony from a written statement rather than valid oral testimony, and there is no competent evidence of the sale at all. Perhaps the seller merely gave the property as a gift. Tumim distinguishes differently. He suggests that the rule that one who sells land before witnesses allows collection from encumbered property applies only according to the opinion that liens are d'oraisa. On that view an oral loan ordinarily does not collect from purchasers only because it lacks publicity and purchasers would unfairly lose, so a witnessed sale, which generates publicity, does collect. But according to the view that liens are not d'oraisa, collection from purchasers is itself a rabbinic enactment tied to a written document, and the Sages did not distinguish between loans and sales when the transaction is only oral. This distinction is not reflected in the poskim. Many poskim hold that liens are not d'oraisa, yet the rule that a sale before witnesses permits collection from encumbered property is accepted without qualification.

When the creditor comes to collect from the purchaser, even the dispossession document is ultimately torn. Afterward, even if the purchaser has already left beit din and later returns with witnesses to the dispossession, beit din will write him a dispossession document. Therefore it is necessary that no deed remain in his hands and that there not remain other witnesses to the sale who could later testify again, lest he obtain a second dispossession document.
