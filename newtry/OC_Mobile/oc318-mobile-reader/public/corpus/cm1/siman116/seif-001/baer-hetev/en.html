For one thousand. See the Sema, who asks that this appears to allow the purchaser to recover the full rise in value, whereas siman 115 says that the lender collects only half the improvement. The Bach answers that the case here is where the land was designated as an apotiki. Alternatively, the phrase means that the property appreciated so that half the improvement amounted to one thousand. The Shach notes the matter.
Five hundred. The Tur writes this, but Baal HaTerumot instead says that if the purchaser bought it for one thousand even though it was worth only five hundred, the purchaser may recover one thousand. One can explain that only in such a case can the purchaser say, "You see that from the outset I willingly paid one thousand even though the land was worth only five hundred because it was worth one thousand to me." If instead the property later declined and now stands at five hundred, why should the seller pay more than its value? The Maggid Mishneh writes this plainly regarding a guarantor in chapter 25 of Hilchot Malveh, cited by the Beit Yosef in siman 131, seif 11. The Bach writes there that the Maggid Mishneh was imprecise in speaking of depreciation, because siman 116 shows that if the property depreciated the authorization to seize is written for its original value. The Shach disagrees. The Maggid Mishneh evidently disputes the Tur, and his view is reasonable.
Authorization to seize. It appears that the authorization to collect the improvement is written only against unencumbered property, because improvement cannot be collected from encumbered property, as stated at the beginning of siman 115. However, according to Tosafot and the Maggid Mishneh cited there, a purchaser who buys after the authorization to seize has been issued is subject even to the improvement because its amount is then fixed. Therefore the authorization may be written to permit seizure from such later purchasers. See the Yerushalmi, Gittin, chapter HaNizakin, halachah 2, for an extended discussion of these laws. Shach.
The warranty. Baal HaTerumot writes that where a sale was made only before witnesses and no document was written, we do not say that warranty was omitted merely by scribal error. This is because no written deed existed. Rashbam in Bava Batra 41 and the beginning of 42 implies the same. Shach.
For proof. The Sema explains that although the first purchaser cannot seize from a second purchaser when he has no deed of sale unless the witnesses testify that they never signed any deed, for purposes of collecting from the seller an authorization is written for him. The seller knows the truth as to whether he issued a deed, so there is no concern that the purchaser will collect twice. Moreover, the purchaser caused his own problem by having witnesses testify orally when they had already signed a deed.
