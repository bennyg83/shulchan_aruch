How a writ of seizure is written for a purchaser. It contains 2 se'ifim.
If a field is seized from a purchaser, beit din writes him a writ of seizure describing how it was taken from him for the seller's debt, and he then proceeds against the seller. {Rama: The creditor need not give the purchaser a power of attorney or a written acknowledgment that he seized the property from him. See Beit Yosef, siman 111, citing the Rashba.} If the purchaser bought the field for five hundred and it appreciated to one thousand, the writ of seizure is written for one thousand. The same applies if it was worth one thousand when purchased but only five hundred when seized, or if it was worth one thousand and he bought it for five hundred: in every case, the writ is written for one thousand. Every right the lender has against the purchaser also belongs to an earlier purchaser from whom a creditor seized property when that purchaser proceeds against later purchasers, even if the seller conveyed the property before witnesses rather than by document and did not expressly accept responsibility. {Rama: Some say this applies specifically to a sale. If a gift was made before witnesses, the recipient cannot seize from later recipients without a document, because a gift becomes publicly known only through a document. The rule allowing seizure when the sale was made only before witnesses applies specifically when those witnesses testify that they signed no document concerning this sale and that no other witnesses were present. Nevertheless, beit din writes for the purchaser a record of how the property was seized from him, as proof with which to sue the borrower who sold it to him.}
