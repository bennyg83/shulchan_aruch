Only five hundred. See Baer Heitev, which explains that if the property declined in value and is now worth only five hundred, why should he pay more than its current value? Ketzot HaChoshen, seif katan 2, writes that Tosafot in Bava Kamma 9a, s.v. mishehichzik, who answer the question "let him say, take your land in payment" by establishing the case where the land declined in value, support the Tur's view that where there is a warranty the seller pays according to the price the purchaser paid at the time of sale. Since Tosafot and the Raavad take this view, Ketzot concludes that one should not dispute them. Responsa Beit Ephraim, Choshen Mishpat, siman 18, records that a rabbi objected that Ketzot compares dispossession by the true owner to collection by a creditor. When the true owner recovers his property, everyone agrees that the sale was fundamentally mistaken and void, and therefore the seller reimburses the original purchase price. Nimukei Yosef in the first chapter of Bava Metzia expressly distinguishes these cases. Beit Ephraim attempts to defend Ketzot somewhat, but concludes that although the objection from Nimukei Yosef may be answered, the analogy remains weak and there is sound reason to distinguish the cases. Shaar Mishpat likewise challenges Ketzot on this point. See also below, siman 373, seif 1, seif katan 2.
