The defendant cannot. See the Shach, who cites Rabbi Shmuel that if a defendant appointed an authorized representative and became liable through the representative's admission, the defendant may retract. The Shach rules that the primary law follows Rivash, siman 392, that the admission of an authorized representative is effective, and he proves this from the Jerusalem Talmud's discussion, "Consider a case where he admits part." In my view, if the defendant appointed a representative and expressly placed the words in his mouth, saying, "Make this claim and make this admission," then we say a person's agent is like himself and it is a complete admission, because the admission was made through his agent, who stands in his place. But if the defendant merely appointed someone to argue on his behalf and did not put any admission into his mouth, and the representative then made an admission on his own, that admission is ineffective. The statement that a Kohen Gadol may appoint a representative must mean that he sets out his admission and his argument for the representative to present. Beit Yosef cites Rashba as holding that a defendant cannot appoint a pleader who will invent arguments on his own behalf. See also Rivash, siman 382, concerning a messenger who says, "I betrothed her for my principal," while the woman says, "I was not betrothed." The principal is forbidden to her relatives because of the messenger's statement, since his messenger is like himself, while she is permitted to the principal's relatives. Beit Yosef cites this in Even HaEzer siman 35. This likewise shows that an agent can bind the principal through an admission where the admission falls within the agency actually entrusted to him.
