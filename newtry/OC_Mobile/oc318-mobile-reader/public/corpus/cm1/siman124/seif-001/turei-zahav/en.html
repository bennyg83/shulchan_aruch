(In the Tur, regarding the statement that the High Priest judges and is judged, showing that he may appoint an agent). At first glance this seems to prove the opposite, since perhaps he will also become obligated to swear, in which case an agent cannot take the oath for him. It appears that the questioner asked why the phrase “he is judged” implies that he himself must come to beit din. Why should he be worse than other defendants who may appoint a representative? The answer is that sometimes a person cannot rely entirely on a representative, such as when an oath is imposed. In that instance “he is judged” means that he himself must swear. It follows that a defendant may appoint a representative for the monetary aspects of the claim: he may have the representative conduct the litigation, and if an oath is imposed he swears personally. The same applies where he expressly accepts responsibility for the payment and authorizes the representative to pay on his behalf.
