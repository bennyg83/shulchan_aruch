Shach writes that according to what was explained in Yoreh Deah, beginning of siman 243, namely, that a Torah scholar has an occupation or business sufficient for subsistence but not for enrichment, and whenever free from his affairs returns to Torah study, the present practice of compelling Torah scholars to appear in court is difficult. One must say that, just as there the rule regarding taxes depends upon local custom, so too here. See there. See also Responsa Maharashdam, siman 439, who ruled that even an act of acquisition from the defendant does not suffice to make another his attorney. He further ruled that if the defendant appointed an attorney, who litigated and became liable because he admitted the plaintiff's claim, the defendant may retract. In my opinion this is incorrect. The principal rule is as Rivash, siman 392, that the admission of a defendant's attorney is an admission according to all opinions; his proof from the authorities and from the Yerushalmi is clear. Distinguished women who become obligated to take an oath should not be humiliated by being brought to beit din; rather, they take the oath before the court scribes, Radbaz I, siman 51. This dispute concerns monetary law. In capital matters it appears the reverse: the prosecutor may not appoint an attorney, while the defendant may, Rivash siman 235.
