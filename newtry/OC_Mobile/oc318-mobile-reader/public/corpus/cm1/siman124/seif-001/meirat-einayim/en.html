The defendant cannot appoint an authorized representative, etc. The Tur explains that this is so the judges should not hear false claims through an interpreter. A defendant ordinarily has a presumption that he will not brazenly lie in the face of the person to whom he owes money, as shown by the Torah rule exempting one who denies the entire claim from an oath. If he can plead through an agent, that restraint is weakened. A claimant, by contrast, may appoint an authorized representative because this presumption does not operate in the same way against him. In addition, the claimant transfers the claimed money to the representative, who then stands in his legal place, whereas a defendant transfers nothing. Moreover, Chazal had to provide claimants with authorization so that one could not take another's money and flee abroad. Darkei Moshe adds that even a claimant may use a representative only when the claimant remains at home and sends the representative in his place. If the claimant himself appears in beit din, he may not present his claims through an advocate but must speak himself, as Rambam and the Tur explain in siman 17.
Distinguished women, etc. See the end of siman 96 concerning when women and unmarried girls are summoned to court.
They send the court scribes to them, etc. Perhaps the reason is that the scribes record their claims immediately in their presence and place the writing before beit din. If the claimant wishes, he may accompany the scribes so that she states her claims in his presence and is embarrassed to lie.
They do likewise for a Torah scholar, etc. See siman 7. Here the case is a Torah scholar who is willing to accept the judges' ruling but for whom it is degrading to go quarrel personally with an unlearned person. The qualification that Torah is his occupation matters because other people, whose daily business already brings them into dealings with non-Jews and unlearned persons, ordinarily must attend beit din personally. More plausibly, only one whose Torah is truly his occupation receives this special protection for his honor.
