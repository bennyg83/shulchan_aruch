The defendant, etc. As stated in Makkot 6b regarding those people who came, etc. It is evident that the passage concerns litigants, since it says that they came, etc., and that there was an interpreter between them. See Ran at the beginning of the chapter Shevuat HaEdut. The Rambam rules likewise, as does the Shulchan Aruch above in siman 17, seif 6. This is also the meaning of Bava Kamma 8b, "He cannot say to him, You are not," etc. See Mordechai, chapter HaMafkid.
And distinguished women, etc. As stated at the beginning of the chapter Shevuat HaEdut. If you would say that a woman is not, etc., see Yevamot 100a, where the Gemara says, "Mar said from the first clause," etc.
And similarly, as stated in Shevuot there, one positive commandment and another positive commandment, the positive commandment, etc. Likewise at the end of Ketubot. The Yerushalmi at the beginning of chapter 2 of Sanhedrin says, "Let him appoint a spokesman. Consider a case in which he becomes obligated to take an oath. Can the spokesman take the oath?" This implies that, were it not for the oath, use of a spokesman would be permitted because of the person's dignity. One can answer that they should proceed as stated here, for with a person of great stature even appearing personally in this manner is degrading. See there.
