He shall not collect except with an oath. Here too beit din itself requires the oath.
The case is where the document contains no credibility clause, even an ordinary one, because with such a clause the lender would be believed against one witness, as explained at the beginning of siman 71. All these oaths are rabbinic oaths modeled on Torah oaths, because Torah oaths ordinarily exempt rather than enable one to collect, and because a document creates a lien upon land.If it is still within its term, etc. The Tur likewise discusses whether the presumption that a person does not ordinarily repay within the fixed term removes the requirement of an oath in these cases.
The precise answer depends on whether the uncertainty arises from an actual defect in the document, a single witness, or another factor requiring a collecting oath. The normal within-term presumption is strong, but Chazal sometimes still required an oath where the lender's own document or circumstances created a specific basis for suspicion.
