Tur in the name of the Rambam at the beginning of chapter 14 of Hilkhot Malveh. The Maggid Mishneh writes that in the first chapter of Bava Batra, 5b, if the borrower dies within the term, the lender collects from the heirs without an oath, and Rabbeinu holds that the same applies to all the remaining cases.
Responsa Rosh, rule 68, siman 14.
It is explained there that the witness's testimony was given when the heirs demanded payment from the borrower. The Rosh obligated him because a person cannot bequeath to his children money that he himself could collect only by taking an oath. This was challenged from Shevuot 48b, where the Gemara concludes that this rule was stated only when they seek to collect from heirs. The Mechaber likewise rules below in siman 105. The Bach answers that the incident involved a promissory note against Levi and Shimon, and Shimon died during the lender's lifetime. The lender demanded payment from Levi, and the two debtors had been guarantors for one another. Whatever was collected from Levi could then be recovered by Levi from Shimon's heirs, etc. See there. The Mechaber's ruling here concerns that situation, where the debtor died during the lender's lifetime. He abbreviated because he relied on what was written below in siman 108.
