With an oath. Here too beit din advances the claim that the lender must swear. The case assumes the document contains no ne'emanut clause, not even ordinary ne'emanut. The Sema says that otherwise the lender would be believed against one witness, as stated at the beginning of siman 71. The Shach refers to his disagreement there and writes that ordinary ne'emanut does not overcome one witness. The Sema further writes that all these oaths are rabbinic oaths modeled on Torah oaths, because Torah oaths ordinarily enable the swearer to be exempt rather than to collect, and because a documented debt is secured by land.

His heirs. Nearly all later authorities challenge the Mechaber's ruling here, and even Responsa Rosh itself is difficult. The Ir Shushan and Sema discuss the problem at length. The Bach explains that the borrower died during the lender's lifetime, but the Shach finds that reading strained; moreover, if so, even without a witness or partial impairment of the document the heirs might be unable to collect. For practical law the Shach nevertheless regards the Bach's conclusion as primary, preferring to leave the Rosh and Mechaber difficult rather than invent novel theories inconsistent with the law.
