One witness, etc. See Tumim. I discussed at length the dispute whether, where the borrower himself does not know if he paid and asserts the claim only on the basis of one witness, he can impose the Mishnah level oath upon the lender. See there.
One. Meaning a valid witness. A relative or otherwise disqualified witness does not suffice. Although the Rama holds that a report from such a person can generate a hesset oath through the formula "I was told," against a formal document the special Mishnah oath requires a fully valid witness according to all opinions. See Tumim, against Beit Shmuel.
Only with an oath. Even if the borrower does not say, "Swear to me," beit din raises the requirement on his behalf. Whether a credibility clause can remove this oath was discussed above in siman 71 at length.
Unless he claimed, etc. See what I wrote in Tumim at length concerning an impaired document. The two cases are governed by the same rule.
His heirs do not collect, etc. This ruling requires study, because we generally apply the rule that a person does not bequeath an oath dependent claim to his heirs only to the oath required when collecting from the property of orphans. See Tumim. I discussed at length how to explain the Rosh: here the borrower is still alive and himself wishes to swear that he paid. If the borrower later also dies, all agree that the lender's heirs may collect the document upon taking the ordinary heirs' oath. See there.
