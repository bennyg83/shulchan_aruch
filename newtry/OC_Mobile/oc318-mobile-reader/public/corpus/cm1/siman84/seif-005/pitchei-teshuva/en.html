He shall not collect except with an oath. See Shach, seif katan 7, who cites Kesef Mishneh and explains that this applies specifically where the husband or borrower makes a definite claim, though the Shach rejects that view. Rabbi Akiva Eiger notes that the opinion attributed by the Shach to Kesef Mishneh, that an oath is required only where the borrower makes a definite claim, is also explicit in Tosafot Rid to Kiddushin in the discussion of 'they are his agents and they are his witnesses.' Tosafot Rid writes that before the enactment of the hesset oath, if a debt was sent through an agent, the lender could collect without an oath and was not required to swear in order to contradict the single witness, because that would be an oath taken in order to collect. This shows that a creditor need not swear merely because one witness testifies that the debt was paid when the debtor himself claims only uncertainty. Conversely, Tosafot Rid's reason that the creditor is collecting implies that one who seeks exemption in such a case would have to swear even when his claim is based upon the witness. This is also explicit in the Rosh, Ketubot chapter 9, in the discussion of Avimi son of Rabbah bar Abbahu, 85a, and follows from Responsa Rosh, rule 68 siman 22. The Shach's answer above in siman 56, seif katan 5, beginning 'and now it is resolved,' is difficult in Rabbi Akiva Eiger's view, as he wrote in his gloss there. See also the work Ketubot, siman 56 seif 8.
