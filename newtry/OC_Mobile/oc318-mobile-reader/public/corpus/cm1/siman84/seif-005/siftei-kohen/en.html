One witness testifies, etc. The creditor may collect only after taking an oath. See Kesef Mishneh, chapter 24 of Hilchot Ishut, who explains Rambam as limiting the Mishnah’s rule, that where one witness testifies the debt was paid the creditor collects only with an oath, to a case where the husband or borrower makes a definite claim. His explanation does not appear correct. We rule that even where the claimant’s allegation is uncertain but rests on one witness, an oath is required, for just as two witnesses impose monetary liability even on an uncertain claim, one witness imposes an oath. Rif and Mechaber state this above at the end of siman 75. The same therefore applies here. Rashba’s responsum cited by Beit Yosef above in siman 72, section 24, likewise plainly indicates that according to Rif, even if the borrower’s claim is uncertain, the creditor must swear when one witness testifies that the debt was paid. This is the primary ruling.

His heirs do not collect it. All the commentators have objected to Mechaber’s ruling here, and Rosh’s responsum itself is difficult. Ir Shushan and Sema discuss the matter at length. Bach explains that Rosh and Mechaber speak where the borrower died during the creditor’s lifetime. His explanation is forced, and it raises a further difficulty, for in that event the heirs would not collect even without one witness and without an impaired note. Nevertheless, as a matter of practical law Bach’s conclusion appears correct. One should not invent distinctions that are unsound in halachah. It is better to leave the words of Rosh and Mechaber difficult than to devise strange theories contrary to the law. This is not the place to elaborate.
