So write Tosafos and the Rosh in the chapter HaKotev, and so too the Ir Shushan. See there. According to the Rambam, Rashba, and the other later authorities whom I cited above at the end of siman 83, seif katan 7, however, the creditor is believed here. According to what I wrote there in seif katan 9, that this is a doubt of law and if he has seized the money we do not remove it from him, the same applies here. This is also explicit in the Ramban's novellae to the chapter Chezkas HaBatim. This is unlike the Baal Gidulei Terumah, page 89, who wished to say that according to those authorities, since the creditor does not claim, "I had another promissory note," they too agree that he is not believed. That is incorrect, and he overlooked the cited words of the Ramban. See there.
And the borrower takes a heset oath and is exempt. Even if the note contains a clause granting the creditor credibility regarding the whole debt or part of it, that clause has no effect. Once the note itself is treated like a mere shard, the credibility clause contained in it neither adds nor detracts. So writes the Baal HaTerumot, gate 21, end of part 2, and so rules the Mechaber above at the end of siman 47.
