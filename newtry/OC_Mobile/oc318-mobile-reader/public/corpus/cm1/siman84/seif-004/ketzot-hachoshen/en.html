He thereby admits. Tosafot and the Rosh write that the creditor is not believed through migo because it would be a migo to extract. The Shach says that according to Ramban and Rashba, who accept migo to extract in some contexts, the creditor should be believed even if he does not claim that he once had another document. This is correct. The sugya in Perek Chazakat HaBatim concerning the guarantor who said, "You repaid me," and was answered, "I later took the money back from you," proves the point. Abaye says the result depends on the rule that in land the halachah follows Rabbah and in money Rabbi Yosef. The claimant there did not allege that he once had another document. Yet if one accepts Rabbah's broader rule allowing migo to extract, the migo can work. Therefore there is no basis to limit Ramban and Rashba to a case where he says he had another document. One could reinterpret the question sent to Abaye as precisely whether the absence of a claim to another document weakens the migo, but Abaye's answer indicates that the real issue is the general dispute concerning migo to extract. Accordingly, the Shach's reading is the better one.
