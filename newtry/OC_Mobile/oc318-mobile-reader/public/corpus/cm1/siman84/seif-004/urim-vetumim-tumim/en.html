And the witnesses erred, etc. One must understand the Gemara's statement: "On what do you rely? On this deed? This deed is merely a shard." The Gemara answers that she says, "It was a trust arrangement between us." According to what the Ran writes in the second chapter of Ketubot, the distinction between a condition and a trust deed is that in a trust arrangement the lender was trusted with the deed subject to fulfillment of a condition, and without fulfillment there is no lien. The Ran distinguishes that in a trust arrangement the borrower can simply refuse to accept the money and never become obligated, whereas with an ordinary condition, if the lender fulfills the condition the borrower has no power to prevent or annul the obligation. See there. In short, witnesses are permitted to write a deed subject to a condition, and doing so is not within the prohibition against allowing injustice to dwell in one's tent. If so, why does the Gemara not answer that she says the deed stating one thousand was written subject to a condition, and if the condition was not fulfilled she would be entitled only to five hundred? If the condition was in fact not fulfilled, she has only five hundred, while the witnesses acted properly and committed no wrongdoing, as the Ran writes. The deed would not be a mere shard at all. In my novellae I said that Tosafot's question, as explained by the Maharsha, is difficult: why does a woman who reduces the amount of her ketubah not have to swear, and why are we not concerned that she is acting deceitfully, as we are concerned where she admits payment of less than a perutah? Necessarily, we do not suspect her of deceitfully presenting herself as one who merely reduces the ketubah rather than one who admits partial payment, because by doing so she would render herself wicked for having allowed injustice to dwell in her home. This is explicit in the second chapter of Ketubot. The Derishah likewise writes that she thereby presents herself as wicked, and such conduct is uncommon, as stated in Kiddushin 50. See there, and see what I wrote above in the principles of migo. If so, the Gemara cannot be speaking of a plea such as the one suggested, that the deed was conditional and the condition was not fulfilled. Such a plea would not render her wicked, since there was no wrongdoing in writing the conditional deed. Tosafot's difficulty would therefore remain in full force, because we would still have reason to suspect a stratagem. Nevertheless, although this reasoning is sound, it is difficult for me to create a new legal distinction and say that a woman making such a conditional plea is not treated as one who reduces her ketubah but rather as one who admits partial payment and must swear. If so, the decisors should have stated that one who claims that the deed was conditional and that the condition was not fulfilled must swear because of the concern for stratagem. This requires study.
