He shall be paid only with an oath. See Shach, seif katan 7, who cites Kesef Mishneh and writes that this applies only where the husband or borrower makes a definite claim, but that he does not accept that limitation. In the glosses of Rabbi Akiva Eiger, of blessed memory, on the Shach's citation of Kesef Mishneh that the borrower must make a definite claim: Tosafos Rid in Kiddushin, in the sugya of 'they are his agents and they are his witnesses,' likewise clearly holds this view. He writes that before enactment of the hesset oath, if a debt was sent through an agent, the creditor collected without an oath and was not required to swear in contradiction of the single witness, because that would make him one who swears and collects. This shows that he is not required to take the oath arising from one witness testifying that the debt was paid, necessarily because the claim against him is uncertain. From his reason that the creditor is one who collects, it follows that one who seeks merely to exempt himself in such a case would have to swear even where his claim rests on the witness. This is also the explicit view of the Rosh in Ketubot, chapter 9, in the sugya of Avimi son of Rabbah bar Abbahu, folio 85a, and is likewise evident from Responsa Rosh, rule 68, siman 22. The Shach's answer above, siman 56, seif katan 5, beginning 'and now it is resolved,' appears to me difficult, as I wrote in the gloss there. See there, and see the laws of ketubot, siman 56, seif 8.
