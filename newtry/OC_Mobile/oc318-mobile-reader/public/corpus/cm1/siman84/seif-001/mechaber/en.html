One who admits that part of his document has been paid, one who reduces the amount stated in his document, or where one witness testifies that it was paid. This siman contains 5 seifim.
One who admits that part of his document has been paid may not collect the remainder without an oath, even if the borrower does not demand that he swear. How so? His document is for one thousand zuz. The borrower claims that the entire amount was paid, while the lender says that only part was paid. The lender may collect the remainder only after taking an oath resembling a Torah oath. This applies even if witnesses were present when the partial payment was made, or the lender gave him a receipt for what he paid. We do not say, "Had he paid more, he would have paid before witnesses as he did originally," or "He would have taken a receipt." Even if the lender's accounting is so exact that he combines sums down to less than the value of a perutah, we do not say that such precision proves that he is telling the truth. If the document contains a trustworthiness clause, even if it does not expressly distinguish between full and partial payment, he collects without an oath.
