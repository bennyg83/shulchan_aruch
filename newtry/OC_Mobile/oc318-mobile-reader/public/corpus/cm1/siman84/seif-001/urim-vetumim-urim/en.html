One who impairs, etc. The Sema writes in the name of Beit Yosef that the oath enactment was made when the lender himself impaired his document by admitting partial payment, but if his son later made the impairment the Sages did not enact the same oath. See Tumim for what I wrote. As practical law I agreed with the Shach that there is no distinction at all between a father and son regarding an impaired document. The only difference is that with this oath we do not apply the rule that a person cannot bequeath to his children a claim that he himself could collect only by oath, as explained below in siman 108. See there.
He may collect only with an oath. The reason is that a debtor may be careful about the fact that he paid while not being equally precise about the exact amount or details of payment. The Sages therefore imposed an oath upon the lender so that he too will investigate carefully. Gemara.
Only with an oath. If the borrower claims, "I paid you three times, one dinar each time," and the lender says, "I remember two payments and do not remember the third," the law follows the borrower. If the lender holds a debt instrument or collateral, he must return it accordingly. Beit Yosef and Rashba, cited by the Sema, and this is clear. See above, siman 59.
He is telling the truth. See Tumim. I wrote that if the lender seized property, it appears that we should not place him under ban to force the special oath in the case where the borrower claims repayment before witnesses, nor in the case where the lender carefully aggregates tiny payments one perutah at a time. See there.
Whether regarding all of it, etc. An ordinary credibility clause extends to the entire matter, and in any event we do not impose an oath merely because of uncertainty. See Baal HaTerumot, gate 21, part 4.
