[Shach, subparagraph 1, s.v. And if so] They must swear that their father did not instruct them. Note: In my opinion, Tosafos ask why any creditor who admits partial payment should not be believed without an oath by virtue of a migo that he could have denied any payment. They answer that because the concern is that a creditor is not precise when receiving payment, we do not suspect deliberate falsehood, but only uncertainty as to what actually occurred, and a migo therefore does not apply. If so, here, regarding the oath that the heirs were not instructed by their father concerning an additional payment, they should have a migo that they could simply refrain from saying that their father admitted partial payment.
