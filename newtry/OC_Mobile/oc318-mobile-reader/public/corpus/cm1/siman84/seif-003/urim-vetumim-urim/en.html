Payment between the lines. Sema explains that because the payment was written on the deed itself, it is as though no greater document ever existed. Rosh's responsum gives another reason: with a separate receipt witnessed by others, perhaps once witnesses happened to be available and another time they did not. But writing on the body of the document is always available. If the lender himself cannot write, many others are readily available to write for him. See Tumim regarding the Shach's view that the borrower must admit the notation between the lines was written with his knowledge. I distinguished as follows. If the borrower also holds a separate receipt, then even if the payment is written on the deed, that does not prevent the document from being considered impaired. But where there is no separate receipt and the payment appears only between the lines, the document is not considered impaired, as Rosh writes, and the lender cannot say that the notation was made without the borrower's knowledge. See there. I further left unresolved in Tumim a case where the between-the-lines notation is dated Nisan, but the borrower says, 'I had already repaid you in Adar and trusted you, and only later in Nisan, after I saw you denying that payment, did I force you to write it between the lines.' Does this create the status of an impaired document? The matter requires study.
