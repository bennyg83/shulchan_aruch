A payment is written between the lines, etc. Although Tur records this rule from a responsum of Rosh, it is novel and is not found in any other posek. Had the other authorities accepted it, they would not have remained silent about such a distinction. It appears to me that Rosh, Tur, and Shulchan Aruch speak only where the borrower admits that the notation between the lines was written with his knowledge. Rosh explains in responsum 67:10, also numbered 68:16, that only an oral partial payment weakens the note. Since the creditor trusted the borrower and accepted part in cash without taking up the note or recording the payment, it is possible that the borrower paid everything. The Sages therefore imposed an oath on the creditor. If the payment was written between the lines, however, the creditor is not called one who impairs his note, for he claims only the amount still appearing as due. Just as the borrower caused his payment to be recorded on the note, we are witnesses that had he paid more he would have caused that too to be recorded. Rosh proves this from the question in HaKotev whether a woman who admits partial payment of her ketubah before witnesses must swear. The Gemara considers that witnesses may have happened to be present for the first payment but not for the second. A notation between the lines is different, because making such a notation is always within the parties’ control. Even if neither borrower nor creditor can write, they can easily find someone to record the payment. There is therefore no room to say that circumstances happened differently. Had more been paid, it too would have been recorded. This reasoning applies only when the borrower admits that the entry was made with his knowledge. In that case there is no difference whether the payment was written between the lines, on the back of the note, or in part of the document after the witnesses’ signatures. Although the creditor could cut it off, the notation is nevertheless legally significant, as I wrote above in siman 65, seif 20, seif katan 71. Since recording it is easy, we say that if more had been paid, that too would have been recorded. If, however, the borrower claims that he trusted the creditor regarding the entire payment and that no notation was made on the note, while the creditor later wrote only part of the payment between the lines on his own initiative, the creditor is indeed one who impairs his note and may collect only after swearing. There also appears to be support for this distinction from the Gemara in the chapter Get Pashut, Bava Batra 172a. See there.
