The Sema therefore writes that it is somewhat difficult why the Rama did not note, on the Mechaber's ruling, that some authorities disagree, since the Tur cites Rabbeinu Hai at the end as disagreeing. In my view, however, the Rama's ruling is correct. On the contrary, I find the Tur difficult: where did he find this in the name of Rabbeinu Hai? One who studies the Baal HaTerumot, gate 21, and the Maggid Mishneh, chapter 14 of Hilchot Malveh, cited by the Beit Yosef above, siman 78, will see that Rabbeinu Hai appears to disagree only regarding collection from purchasers. In the cases of a creditor who admits that part of the debt was paid, or where one witness testifies to payment, he agrees. See there.
Unless the borrower says, "Let him swear to me." The Mechaber also writes this below, seif 5, regarding one witness. This does not appear correct to me. Since we rule that within the due date a creditor collects even from orphans, certainly he should collect here. Moreover, if the borrower can require an oath merely by saying, "Let him swear to me," why does the creditor collect from orphans? We should plead for them that perhaps their father would have demanded, "Let him swear to me," since every claim their father could have made is advanced on behalf of the orphans. Rather, it is clear that within the due date one collects without an oath, both where the creditor admits partial payment and where one witness testifies that the note was paid, even if the borrower says, "Let him swear to me." The Sema likewise challenges the Mechaber on this point.
