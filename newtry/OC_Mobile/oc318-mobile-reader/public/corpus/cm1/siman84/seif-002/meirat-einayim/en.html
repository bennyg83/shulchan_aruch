But if it is still within the term, etc. Maggid Mishneh questions the Rambam's ruling, which the Mechaber follows, because the presumption that a person does not repay within the term has been weakened here by the lender's own admission that part was in fact paid within the term. It is somewhat difficult that Rama did not note a dissenting view, since the Tur cites Rav Hai last as disagreeing. One cannot explain Rav Hai as disagreeing only about a demand made on the exact day the term ended, because I rejected that reading in Perishah.
Unless the borrower says, 'Swear to me.' This qualification is not found in the Rambam or in the Tur's citation of him, and it is difficult. In siman 78 it was explained that when a lender sues the borrower himself during the fixed term, even on an oral loan, and the borrower claims payment, Rif and Ri Migash hold that the lender need not swear because a person is presumed not to pay within the term, and the Beit Yosef writes that the Rambam appears to agree. The Rambam's present ruling must therefore apply even where the borrower says, 'Swear to me,' for otherwise even after the term a lender holding a document ordinarily collects without an oath unless the borrower expressly demands one. One can answer that here the presumption is weakened because the lender himself admits a partial payment within the term. Even so, the source for the Mechaber's qualification remains difficult, especially since in the next seif he also requires an oath within the term where one witness testifies that the document was paid and the borrower demands an oath.
