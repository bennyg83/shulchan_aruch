But if it is within its term, etc. Sema asks that Rav Hai Gaon disagrees and requires an oath. He also asks from Mechaber's ruling that if the borrower says, 'Swear to me,' the lender must swear, since this rule is not explicit in Tur and Rambam. The Shach raises the same questions. See Tumim, where I reconciled the rulings at length.
Within its term. Likewise, regarding a handwritten obligation, according to the view that payment cannot be claimed against it, if the creditor admits partial payment he must swear; it is no stronger than a formal deed. But within the term he need not swear. Taz correctly rules this, as I wrote in Tumim. See Tumim also concerning an oral loan still within its term, where ordinarily payment cannot be claimed: if the lender himself admits partial payment within the term, the ordinary presumption against early payment may have fallen away, and the burden of proof rests on the claimant. If there is also concern that perhaps the debt would never become payable at all, the matter requires study.
