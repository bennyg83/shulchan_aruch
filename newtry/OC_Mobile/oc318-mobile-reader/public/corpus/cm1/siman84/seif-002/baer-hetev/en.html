He claimed. The Sema writes that this ruling is difficult. In siman 78 it is explained that when a lender sues the borrower himself, even on an oral loan that is still within its due date, and the borrower claims he paid, the Rif and Ri Migash hold that the lender need not swear because of the presumption that a person does not pay before a debt is due. The Beit Yosef writes there that this also appears from the Rambam and cites these very words of the Rambam. That case must include even one in which the borrower says, "Swear to me," for otherwise, after the due date too, a lender holding a note collects without an oath. Although one could answer that here the presumption has been weakened because the lender himself admits that the borrower paid part of the debt before the due date, and therefore, as to the balance, if the borrower demands an oath the lender must swear, there remains a difficulty from seif 5 below. There, where one witness testifies that the note was paid while it is still before the due date, and the borrower says, "Swear to me," the Mechaber likewise requires an oath. One must therefore say, with difficulty, that the Mechaber holds that impairment of a note or the testimony of a single witness is effective even before the due date at least to require an oath when the borrower demands one. Yet it requires study where the Mechaber derived this. The Shach also challenges the ruling: If the lender must swear when the borrower says, "Swear to me," why do we collect from orphans when the debt is still within its due date? We should raise for them the possible claim that perhaps their father would have demanded, "Swear to me," since we advance for heirs every claim their father could have made.
