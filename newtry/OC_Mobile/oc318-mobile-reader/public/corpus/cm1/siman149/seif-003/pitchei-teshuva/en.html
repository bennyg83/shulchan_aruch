A person who established possession of a mitzvah. Shaar Mishpat writes that Sefer Chasidim, simanim 762 and 763, discusses a Torah scholar who had regularly acquired a mitzvah for a certain sum that he donated to charity. Unsuitable people then arose and offered more charity for the mitzvah. They take precedence because there is greater benefit to the poor, even if their intent is not for the sake of Heaven but for honor. One should not prevent them from acquiring the mitzvah for a larger sum. If so, all the more where someone had held a mitzvah without payment, one who gives money to charity for that mitzvah takes precedence. Responsa Mahari HaLevi, brother of the Taz, siman 21, rules similarly. See there.
