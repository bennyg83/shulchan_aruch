After I converted. See Sema, seif katan 27. The primary ruling follows Taz that he is certainly believed through a migo. Perishah's statement that this is a migo against a chazakah because non Jews are presumed robbers is very difficult. If that were true, then even where he claims, "In my presence the non Jew bought it," which seif 16 says is believed through a migo, the same objection would apply. Evidently this is not considered a migo against a chazakah. Non Jews also purchase property in the ordinary way. Taz's distinction between a robber who repented and a non Jew who converted is itself difficult to understand. The primary distinction appears to be what Sema writes in siman 150, seif 3: where property originally came into a person's hands not by purchase, later chazakah helps only when his legal identity and status have changed. A convert is treated as a newly born person, so conversion is a genuine change of name and legal circumstance. A robber who merely repents has not undergone such a legal change.
