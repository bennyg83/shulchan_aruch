There is an authority... a father... The same applies to the other listed relationships. The rule concerning brothers who eat together, a brother, and a woman who lent money is all learned from father and son, as Rashbam explains. Brothers managing the common estate resemble orphans who attached themselves to a householder, who is treated like an appointed guardian, as stated in Gittin 52a. Adult brothers managing jointly are likewise treated as guardians. Proof that father and son are not exclusive examples appears from Bava Basra 52a concerning one of the brothers, and concerning a woman, and from Bava Basra 173a regarding brothers. This is so even according to the view that formal proof is not required.
