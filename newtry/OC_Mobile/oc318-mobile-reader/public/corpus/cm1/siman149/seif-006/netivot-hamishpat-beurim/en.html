The same applies to other relatives. Here too the primary ruling is like Taz. Even if they are not supported at his table, whenever it appears to beit din that these relatives are not particular with one another, no chazakah arises. This includes father and son when, in beit din's judgment, they are not particular. The requirement that they be supported at one table applies only in an ordinary case where beit din does not know whether they are particular. Rashba and Rivash state this. Likewise, Shach's unresolved question in seif katan 7 concerning lending a place in the synagogue to a son in law concerns only an ordinary case. Where beit din sees that the relatives are not particular, the same rule applies even to other relatives.
