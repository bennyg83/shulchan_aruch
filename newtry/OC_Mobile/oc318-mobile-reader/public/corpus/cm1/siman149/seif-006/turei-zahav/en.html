(Seif 11) One does not establish prescriptive possession against a married woman's property. The Tur cites Ramah that even if the possessor says, “I bought it from you and your husband,” he is not believed, because the husband owns the produce and the wife does not concern herself with discovering who consumes it. This implies that if the husband waived his rights and had no produce, the possessor could claim purchase from both spouses. Yet the Gemara cited above asks from Rav's rule that one does not establish possession against a married woman. According to Ramah that rule cannot refer to a case where the husband has no produce. Otherwise Ramah would disagree with the Tur's nearby citation of Rabbi Yonah that even after the husband writes, “I have no claim,” the wife need not protest, whereas according to Ramah she would need to protest lest the possessor claim purchase from both spouses. One must say that Ramah holds she never needs to protest. His mention that the husband has produce teaches the greater novelty. If he has no produce, the absence of prescriptive possession is obvious: the wife may say she relied on her husband as her heir, while the husband did not protest because he presently had no interest in the field. Even where he does have produce and his silence would ordinarily support possession because another consumes his produce, the wife may still say that she did not care.
