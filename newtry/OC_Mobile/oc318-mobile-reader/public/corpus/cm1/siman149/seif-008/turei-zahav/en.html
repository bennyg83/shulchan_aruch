(Seif 17: after I converted, he is believed.) In our master's text he concludes: this is like a Jew who derives title from a non-Jew, who is believed to say, "I purchased it from you." This appears to mean that even if he says, "I bought it before I converted," he is believed through a migo that he could have claimed he bought it afterward, just as a Jew deriving title from a non-Jew may say, "In my presence the non-Jew bought it from you." Our master did not need to state this explicitly because it is obvious that once he possesses one claim on which he would be believed, we apply a migo based upon it. The Ir Shushan writes likewise. The Sema, seif katan 27, disagrees because the Mechaber says, "after I converted," but that wording is not proof. Nor is this comparable to a robber who later repents, whom our master does not believe. Here, even while he was a non-Jew, he actually acquired the land from the seller; he simply lacked the chazakah necessary for proof. There is therefore no adverse chazakah against him.
