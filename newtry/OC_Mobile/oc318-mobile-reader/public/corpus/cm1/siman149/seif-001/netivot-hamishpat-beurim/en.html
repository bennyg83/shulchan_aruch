His chazakah is effective against the second partner. This is specifically where the property is large enough to be divided, as Taz writes in his first answer. In that situation the partner himself could establish chazakah, and therefore his sharecropper can likewise establish chazakah. But where the property is not subject to division, so that one partner cannot establish chazakah against the other because we can say that they arranged to use it alternately, this one for three years and then the other for three years, the partner's sharecropper likewise has no chazakah. We can say that the partners made precisely such an arrangement and that one partner placed his sharecropper there for his allotted three years. Rashba, cited by Beit Yosef at the end of this siman, makes this clear. He writes that even a brother himself can establish chazakah, all the more so the mother who is not a partner, and although she has no chazakah against Leah her daughter because she is her guardian, she can establish chazakah against the other partner. This proves that the case is one where the partner himself is capable of chazakah. The Rosh's responsum likewise proves it. His reason is that perhaps one partner will admit that he sold his share. If the property were not divisible, what would such an admission accomplish? The purchaser of the second partner's share would simply become a partner in his place, and just as the selling partner could not establish chazakah against the other, neither could the purchaser. The case must therefore concern divisible property, where a partner can establish chazakah. The novelty is that although the possessor claims to have purchased the entire field and his claim fails against the partner whose sharecropper he is, it does not fail against the other partner, and he retains that half. According to Taz's second answer, however, where the partner himself does not make that allegation, there remains a difficulty: the partner should still be believed to say that he never sold, through a migo that he could have made the stronger claim described above.
