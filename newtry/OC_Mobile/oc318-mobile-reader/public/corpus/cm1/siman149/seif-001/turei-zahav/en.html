(Seif 1, gloss: Or he was not with him in the province). I am surprised why the stranger's possession is effective. The partner can say, “I assumed that my partner placed you there because he trusted you not to damage the land.” The Tur cites this very reasoning from Ramah in seif 6. The Mishnah says that artisans, partners, and sharecroppers lack prescriptive possession. The Gemara adds that a sharecropper who installed other sharecroppers beneath him does acquire possession, because an owner would not see strangers descend upon his land and remain silent. No such rule is said of a partner, although the partner appears in the Mishnah before the sharecropper. Necessarily, replacing oneself with another does not create prescriptive possession against a partner. The authorities likewise never distinguish, regarding a partner's lack of possession, between personal use and placement of another. It appears that the Tur speaks where the partner himself would have had prescriptive possession, such as where he consumed the entire property. His conclusion that the Sages did not distinguish means that distinctions exist only among partners, but whenever a partner's own conduct would create possession, one who stands in his place likewise acquires. Alternatively, the absent partner could indeed prevail had he made the specific claim that he thought his partner appointed the possessor. Here he claims only that he did not bother to protest because he had a partner. The ruling rejects that general excuse.
