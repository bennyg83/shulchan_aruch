One who possesses consecrated property. The rationale is alternative on either side. If there are treasurers managing the property, he has no chazakah because a treasurer's failure to protest cannot cause loss to sacred or communal property, like an administrator in seif 24. Ordinary members of the town may not know enough to protest because they assume the possessor is acting for the poor or the institution. If the possessor claims that he bought it from the seven leading citizens and they have died, we respond that such a public sale should have generated publicity. Sema. See Beurim: this applies to the older system where the seven leading citizens could sell only in the presence of the townspeople, which necessarily created publicity. In our times, where communal leaders themselves carry out the sale, they resemble treasurers who have an ownership role, and possession can create chazakah.
