Beit Yosef cites Sefer Agudah as suggesting that no one establishes possession against communal property and the community likewise does not establish possession against others, comparing the community to the household of the Exilarch. Beit Yosef rejects the comparison. Sema explains that the Exilarch's household was exceptional because of its royal power and the fear it imposed. Yet Responsa Maharam of Rothenburg expressly compares community property to the Exilarch's household and says possession does not operate against communal settlement rights because the community is treated as already holding the stronger status. Thus the analogy has direct support in Maharam and cannot simply be dismissed as unique to royal intimidation.
