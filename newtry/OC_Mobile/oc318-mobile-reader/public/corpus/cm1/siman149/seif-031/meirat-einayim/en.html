One who establishes possession in consecrated property, etc., has no chazakah, etc. The Tur explains: there is no chazakah because people do not protest on behalf of consecrated property. If someone descends into it, people assume he entered to collect produce for the poor and will hold it until the poor need it. If he claims, 'I purchased it from the seven good men of the city and they went overseas,' we answer: if so, the matter would have become public. See the Perishah for further discussion.
So that the poor should not lose because the administrator failed to protest, etc. This resembles Rama's ruling in seif 24 regarding an administrator who did not protest. The Mechaber, who there cites without qualification an opinion that chazakah can arise, similarly writes here that if there are appointed administrators, chazakah can arise. Note this consistency.
Some say one who establishes possession against communal property has no chazakah. Chiddushei Agudah writes that one cannot establish chazakah against communal property and the community cannot establish chazakah against others, concluding with the reason that the community is already presumed in possession. Beit Yosef cites this in this siman, seif 12, under the discussion of the house of Rabban Gamliel, where one cannot establish chazakah against them, and simply comments, 'This is not correct,' without giving a proof. It appears that Beit Yosef and Rama reason that Agudah learned the communal rule from Rabban Gamliel's household, but that case is different because they descended from the royal house and inspired exceptional fear, as the Tur writes there in the name of the Rosh.
And he established that the congregation reads, etc., until: a person who established a mitzvah, etc. Darkei Moshe cites both rulings in this siman, seif 13, and writes that the chazakah is one accompanied by a supporting claim; see there.
But if he gave the mitzvah to another without duress, etc. The correct text is 'if he gave,' without the extra letter. Meaning, he himself voluntarily gave the privilege to another. The congregation has no power simply to take it from him and give it to someone else. This is learned from the previous ruling: when the congregation had another Torah scroll even while reading from his, his use constituted chazakah and they were prohibited from changing the arrangement. This is clear.
