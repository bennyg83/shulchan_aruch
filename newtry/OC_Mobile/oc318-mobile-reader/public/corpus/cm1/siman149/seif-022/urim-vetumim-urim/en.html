One who possessed. Seifim 21 and 22 reproduce Rambam, except that Mechaber adds the phrase, 'in the manner of the public report mentioned above.' Later authorities struggled greatly to explain this. Sema's position is that the rumor that the land belonged to orphans defeats the possessor only if it arose before completion of the three years. Thus Mechaber's phrase refers back to the previous seif. He further understands seif 22, where the possessor claims a debt through a migo of purchase, as involving no rumor at all. The Shach correctly objects. In the present case the possessor never acquired three-year possession during the father's life; his only migo is that he could have said the land never belonged to the father. Therefore any rumor that it belonged to the father weakens that migo. He also objects that if seif 22 had no rumor, Mechaber should have combined the cases. See Tumim. I concluded that the Shach is correct against Sema but did not accept the Shach's own explanation. In my view the rumor is that the property definitely belongs to the orphans. The possessor's migo of purchase can still operate because even his present mortgage claim contradicts that rumor. This resolves Sema's question from siman 150. I also concluded that the rumor must have arisen during the father's life; otherwise it is not a sufficiently strong public report. This is what Mechaber intended by adding the phrase referring to the earlier rumor. I used this reading to answer Rambam and Tosafot against the Gemara in Ketubot and raised a strong question on the Shach's view.
