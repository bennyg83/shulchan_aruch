From their father. It appears that even if a rumor arose during their father's lifetime that the property belonged to the claimant, before this possessor had completed three years, the possessor is believed. This differs from seif 20, where the rumor was that the property came into his possession as a pledge, so the rumor itself functions like a protest. Here the rumor is merely that it once belonged to the claimant, which is not a protest, because the possessor can say that it indeed belonged to him but that he later purchased it from him. Shach.
With an oath. The Rosh disagrees, as the Tur and Shulchan Aruch write in siman 150, seif 6, that whatever claim their father could have made, including a claim requiring the claimant to swear, we advance for the orphans even when they themselves have only an uncertain claim. See also siman 133, seif 5, where the Rama writes in a similar case that this appears to him to be the primary ruling. The Sema explains that there the orphans are in possession and the objector seeks to remove from them items normally lent or rented, so the objector must swear. Here, by contrast, the objector is in possession, so the Mechaber holds that the possessor takes without an oath. The Shach writes that the Mechaber relied here on what he wrote in siman 150. Alternatively, here he means only that no special oath of the Geonim is required, of the type imposed on one who collects while holding a pledge. The Taz writes that even the Rama agrees that a rabbinic oath is required here, contrary to the Sema. See there.
