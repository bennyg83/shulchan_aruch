He consumed its produce for the years of chazakah during their father's lifetime, even though a report emerged that it belonged to the orphans, meaning that it had belonged to their father, as explained above. It appears that here he is believed even if the report emerged during the father's lifetime before he had completed three years of chazakah. This is unlike the case above in seif 20, where the report was that the property had come into his possession as a pledge. There the report itself functioned as a protest. Here, by contrast, the report says only that the property had belonged to the father. That is not a protest, because it may indeed have belonged to the father and the possessor may subsequently have purchased it from him. This appears correct. Examine carefully.
And he collects without an oath. Although one who claims against a pledge must swear even when he has a migo that he purchased it, as stated above in siman 72, seif 17, the Maggid Mishneh answers the Raavad's objection by saying that land is not subject to a severe Torah oath, while an oath of hesset was not instituted on an uncertain claim, and here the heir's claim is uncertain. The Beit Yosef cites this, and the Ir Shushan and Sema, seif katan 34, write likewise. This is difficult, because even a Mishnaic oath is imposed in matters involving land, as Rif and Rosh write and as is evident in Ketubot 87b. The Mordechai, Agudah, and Hagahot Ashri in the chapter HaKotev say likewise, and the Sema himself writes this above in siman 95, seif katan 6. The words of Ri of Corbeil in the Mordechai and Hagahot Ashri there, who asks why the oath of "swear to me that you did not collect payment" is imposed when this is comparable to a denial involving land, require study. Perhaps there is a scribal error there, because in Tosafot at the end of Get Pashut, Bava Batra 175a, and in Hagahot Maimoniyot at the beginning of Hilkhot To'en, Ri of Corbeil is cited as asking how an oath of partial admission can apply when one does not swear concerning a denial involving land, and they do not mention the oath of "swear to me that you did not collect payment." See there. It therefore appears that the intent here is different. The oath required of one claiming against a pledge is not a Torah oath even with movable property and is not found in the Gemara; it was stated by the Geonim. Consequently, it applies only to movable property, with respect to which a severe Torah oath can exist, but not to land, concerning which there is no Torah oath. They could not answer simply that this case is different because the heir's claim is uncertain, since the Geonic oath imposed on one who claims against movable collateral that a certain amount is owed applies even where the opposing claim is uncertain, as I wrote above in siman 72, seif 17, seif katan 67. They therefore had to explain that the Geonic oath itself does not apply here. And lest one ask that at least an oath of hesset should be required, since hesset applies even to land, and according to Rambam even after three years of chazakah, as stated above at the beginning of siman 140, they added that hesset was not instituted upon an uncertain claim. In Gidulei Terumah, sha'ar 14, part 1, page 81, columns 3 and 4, the author does not cite the views of the Maggid Mishneh and Raavad and also misunderstood the Raavad. He understood that with respect to produce already consumed, the claimant is exempt from an oath through a migo, and therefore objected that even regarding produce already consumed the Raavad should have challenged Rambam, because a migo does not exempt one from an oath. He consequently left Rambam difficult even as to produce already consumed and discusses the matter at length. All of this is mistaken. With respect to produce already consumed there is plainly no oath at all, because the opposing claim is uncertain both in the principal claim and in the claim underlying the migo, and one does not swear hesset on an uncertain claim, as I explained. Examine carefully. As to practical law, the Rashba in the responsum cited by the Beit Yosef in siman 140, new responsum 5, also agrees with the Raavad that the Geonic oath applies to land as well. This is responsum 939. The same is explicit in Ramban's Chiddushim in Chezkas HaBatim concerning the incident of Rabbah bar Sharshom, and in Nimukei Yosef there. I cite their words below in siman 150, seif 4. This is primary. The Geonic oath is not an independent enactment; rather, the Geonim compared this claimant to those who swear and collect, whose oath is a Mishnaic oath, as is evident from all the authorities and as I wrote above in siman 89, seif 5. There is therefore no reason to distinguish this oath from other Mishnaic oaths, especially according to what I wrote in siman 89, seif 5, that the Yerushalmi provides support for the Geonim. As for the Raavad's question, one could answer that the Gemara's incident involving Rabbah bar Sharshom concerned produce he had already consumed, as the Raavad, Ramban, and Nimukei Yosef write. Yet the plain sense of the Gemara does not imply this, as I explain below in siman 150, seif 4. In my humble opinion the proper answer is that Rabbah bar Sharshom was different because he possessed a document for the very money he sought to collect, as the Gemara implies and as Rashbam and the other authorities explicitly explain. Therefore, even had he not been in possession, he could have sworn and collected from the orphans, including taking property from them. Once he was already in possession, the Geonim agree that he is exempt and need not take an oath while holding a sacred object. I gave the same answer above in siman 89, seif 5, according to the Geonim, regarding the sugya of an artisan in the chapter Kol HaNishba'in and in Chezkas HaBatim. I brought support for their position from the Yerushalmi and concluded that whenever a claimant belongs to the class that swears and collects from the defendant under a Mishnaic oath, if he has already seized the property and also has a migo, he collects without an oath even according to the Geonim. See there, where I discuss this at length.
Without an oath. Below in siman 150, seif 6, the Mechaber cites an opinion that we advance on behalf of orphans every claim their father could have made and may therefore impose an oath even on an uncertain claim. According to that opinion, we must say that here the Mechaber relied on what he writes below. Alternatively, here he means only that a severe oath is not required, namely the Geonic oath applicable to one who swears and collects against a pledge.
