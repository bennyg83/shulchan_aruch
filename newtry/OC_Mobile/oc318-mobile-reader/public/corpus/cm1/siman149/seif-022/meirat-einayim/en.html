And he collects without an oath. I already explained the reason immediately above. The Rosh disagrees. The Tur cites him in siman 150:6, holding that whatever claim their father could have made to require the possessor to swear, beit din advances for the orphans as well, even though their own claim is uncertain. The Mechaber likewise cites that opinion there. See also siman 133:5, where Rama writes in a comparable case that this appears to him the primary ruling. In the Derishah to siman 150 I wrote that the difference may be that in siman 133 the orphans are in possession and the challenger seeks to remove from them articles ordinarily lent or rented, so Rama rules that the challenger must swear. Here the possessor is the one holding the land, and therefore the Mechaber allows him to retain it without an oath.
