Years of chazakah during their father's lifetime. According to the Sema, this concerns a case where no rumor of a mortgage arose during the father's lifetime. If such a rumor had arisen, then even after three years the possessor would not be believed to claim a debt through the migo of purchase, because that would be a brazen migo. The Shach explains differently: the case is one where the rumor was only that the land had belonged to their father, not that it had come into the possessor's hands as a mortgage. In that circumstance the migo of purchase remains effective even if the rumor arose during the father's lifetime, because even where witnesses establish that it was their father's, the possessor can still claim that he purchased it. Shach.
Without an oath. In siman 150 the Mechaber cites the Rosh's dissenting view that for orphans we advance a claim requiring the possessor to swear even where their own claim is uncertain. This differs from siman 133, where Rama follows the Rosh that the possessor must swear because the orphans are in possession and the challenger seeks to remove items ordinarily lent. Here the challenger is the one who is legally in possession of the land, and therefore the possessor may retain it without an oath according to the Sema. The reason there is no severe collateral oath as in siman 72 is that in movable collateral there can sometimes be a Torah oath, and therefore the Sages instituted a severe oath on collateral. Land never gives rise to a Torah oath. The Shach rules primarily like Rashba that even with land one must take the severe collateral oath, because it is a Mishnah oath, and Mishnah oaths can apply even to land.
