Raavad objected that a migo should not allow one to collect initially without an oath, just as one must swear regarding collateral, etc. The Maggid Mishneh answers that there is a distinction: by Torah law no oath is imposed regarding land, and there is no hesset oath here because no one makes a definite claim contradicting the possessor's claim. We do not impose an oath on a doubtful claim. Tosafot likewise write this there, 33a, s.v. 'through a migo that if he wished,' etc.
