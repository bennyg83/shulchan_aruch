Whether he was appointed by beit din, etc. The Maggid Mishneh writes that in the chapter HaMafkid it is explained that one appointed by beit din has no chazakah, even after the minor has reached adulthood. See Lechem Mishneh, who questions this, for it is clear that the Maggid Mishneh refers to the Gemara’s inference from Rav Huna that one does not establish chazakah in the property of a minor even after he reaches adulthood. If so, there is no proof there concerning a guardian, since that rule was stated regarding any person. See what he writes there. It appears clear that the Maggid Mishneh intended the view of Tosafot. Tosafot ask why the Gemara derives the rule specifically from Rav Huna’s statement that one does not appoint a relative over a minor’s property, implying that another person may be appointed and we do not fear that he will establish chazakah over the minor’s property. There are many Mishnayot that speak of appointing a guardian for orphans. The Maggid Mishneh understood the answer to this difficulty as follows. Without Rav Huna’s statement, we could have said that an act of beit din is publicly known. Therefore, when beit din appoints a guardian, the appointment is well known and is not forgotten, and accordingly the guardian cannot later claim chazakah even after the orphans grow up. We would not fear that it might become forgotten that he had been appointed guardian by beit din, because an act of beit din has a powerful public voice and is not forgotten. But Rav Huna said that beit din does not appoint a relative, lest the relative establish chazakah over the minor’s property, even though beit din itself places him there as a sharecropper and it is publicly known that he entered the field by authority of beit din as a sharecropper or guardian. How, then, could he ever establish chazakah? We must conclude, as Rambam writes in Hilchot Nachalot 8:2, that with the passage of many years the matter can eventually be forgotten. If so, the same concern should return with a guardian, for perhaps it will eventually be forgotten that he had been appointed guardian. From this we infer that even an ordinary person, not only a guardian, cannot establish chazakah in the property of a minor. The Gemara is therefore entirely consistent. This is clearly what the Maggid Mishneh intended in explaining Rambam, and all of the Lechem Mishneh’s difficulties fall away. It is straightforward.
