(Seif 21: and he collects with an oath.) This is the reading in the old editions of Rambam, and the Beit Yosef cites it this way. Only in the Kesef Mishneh printing does the text read "from its improvement." The Sema, seif katan 36, agrees with that latter reading, but it appears to me that the old editions are primary. This case is unlike seif 22, where the law says that he collects without an oath. Here he did not establish possession at all during their father's lifetime, only after the father's death. According to his own claim, the orphans are therefore his opposing litigants, and he must take a heset oath. In the later case, however, he was already in possession during the father's lifetime, and according to his own account the heirs acquired no right in the property after their father's death.
