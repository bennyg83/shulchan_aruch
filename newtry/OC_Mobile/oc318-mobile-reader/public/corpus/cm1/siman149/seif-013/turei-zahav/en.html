(Seif 31). Regarding property consecrated for the poor, the Tur concludes that if the seven leading citizens admit the transaction, the possessor has prescriptive possession. Why is possession needed if they admit? It appears that they admit only that the property was sold but do not know to whom. His possession then establishes that he was the purchaser, and their admission is effective despite the absence of the publicity normally accompanying a communal sale. One cannot invoke the end of siman 222, where a seller who says, “I do not know to whom I sold,” is confronted by one claimant saying, “I am the purchaser,” and that claimant is believed. A communal sale is different, because it ordinarily generates publicity, and the absence of such publicity makes the claim weaker.
