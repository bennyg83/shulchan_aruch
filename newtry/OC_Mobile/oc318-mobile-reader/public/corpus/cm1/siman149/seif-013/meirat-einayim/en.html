One who has been established as a robber, etc. The Mechaber omitted the Gemara's rule, also stated by the Rambam and Tur, concerning the household of the Exilarch, that they do not establish possession against others and others do not establish possession against them. It appears that he omitted it because that rule does not apply in our times. The Tur concludes, citing the Rosh, that this was said specifically regarding the Exilarch's household, who descended from royalty and exercised a quasi royal power over Israel. The same rule does not apply nowadays even to powerful people or officials who frequent a royal court. See there.
Or one whose family has been established as people who kill over monetary matters, etc. This is the Rambam's language at the end of chapter 13 of Hilchot To'en, based on the Gemara's discussion: what is a robber who has no presumptive possession? Rabbi Yochanan says, one who has been established as a robber regarding that particular field; Rav Chisda says, a member of a particular family known to kill over monetary disputes. The meaning is that once his family is known to kill anyone who challenges them, people fear protesting even against an individual member of that family, assuming he too is like them. The Rambam's phrase, "although he consumed this field for many years," means that in the case of a family known to kill, every field challenged by them is included. The Tur instead speaks of the possessor himself as one established to kill over monetary matters, and explains that if he is known to kill, he acquires no presumption anywhere, whereas if he is merely known to have robbed this particular field, he may establish possession in other fields. In the Derishah I wrote that although the authorities do not separately discuss one who is an established robber in many matters but is not literally known to have killed, such a person should be treated as one known to kill. Since he knows that people are frantic to protect their property and may resist him, his repeated robbery implies willingness to kill or be killed. The same appears correct according to the Rambam and Mechaber.
