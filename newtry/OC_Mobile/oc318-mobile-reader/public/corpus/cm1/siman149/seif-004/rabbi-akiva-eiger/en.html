[Sema, subparagraph 27] As I wrote in the Derishah. Note: I examined the Derishah, who explains that he is not believed through a migo that he purchased it after converting because this is a migo against the presumption that an ordinary gentile is a robber and does not lawfully acquire it. I have not merited to understand this. If so, why is one who purchased from a gentile believed to say, "The gentile purchased it from you in my presence," through a migo that he could say, "I purchased it from you"? We should likewise say that this is a migo against witnesses.
