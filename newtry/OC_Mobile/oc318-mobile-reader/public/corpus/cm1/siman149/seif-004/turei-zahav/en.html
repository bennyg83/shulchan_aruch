(Seif 9) But if he dug pits. In Hezkat HaBatim the Gemara asks how Rav can say that a married woman must protest when one ordinarily cannot establish possession against her property. Rava answers that the case is her husband, who dug pits, trenches, and caves. The Gemara challenges from Rav Nahman that there is no prescriptive possession for damage. Rashbam explains that where a person uses property destructively, he cannot claim purchase because he did not possess it in the ordinary manner and the owner had no reason to protest. The Gemara answers that Rav Nahman's statement means damage acquires immediate prescriptive rights. Rav Yosef gives another resolution. Rif and Rosh hold that Rav Yosef displaces Rava's answer entirely, while the Tur follows Rava. This is difficult. If destructive, abnormal possession is effective here, why does the Tur rule in siman 141 that continuous cultivation in a place where fields are normally left fallow is ineffective because the possessor did not act in the ordinary manner? It appears that Rif and Rosh are consistent with the Tur's view in siman 141, not with his present ruling. The Tur's distinction requires study. One may answer that digging pits is destructive only from the wife's perspective because she does not desire them; for the possessor it is an improvement, since he needs the pits. Continuous cultivation without fallowing, by contrast, is universally destructive because it impairs future crops.
