And the orphans grew up and left him in that position. This is the Rambam's language. The Tur, citing Ri Migash, writes that it is unnecessary to state that where the guardian began possessing while they were minors, he acquires no presumption even if they later reach majority, because one cannot establish possession in a minor's property. Rather, the novelty is even a guardian over adults, for example one appointed by their father at his death over adult children, or one appointed by beit din, does not establish possession against them. The Rambam did not formulate it this way because he follows his own view that ordinarily, if someone entered the land while the owner was a minor and then held it for three years after the owner attained majority, possession can establish title. Thus, for him there is no special novelty in distinguishing minors who matured from adults from the outset. A non guardian may establish possession in either case when the required conditions are met, while their guardian may establish possession in neither. The Tur and Ri Migash, by contrast, hold that even a non guardian who originally entered during minority cannot establish title merely because the owner later reached majority. Therefore they needed to say that the special rule concerning a guardian is relevant even where the owner was already an adult when the guardianship began.
