The reason is that perhaps the partners divided the use between themselves in this manner, one consuming all the produce for a number of consecutive years and afterward the other consuming it for the same number of years. Since the property cannot be physically divided, they need not both use it each year. Where it is divisible, however, since they could have divided it and did not, possession can establish a chazakah. Sema. See Responsa Ibn Lev, rule 15, end of siman 89. Many people who prayed in a house belonging to the objector acquire no chazakah at all, because they are treated as partners. Maharshdam, Choshen Mishpat siman 353; Raanach, part 1, siman 85; and Responsa Masat Binyamin, siman 4. Whenever land is not subject to physical division, even if its use can be divided by time, the parties do not acquire chazakah against one another. Maaseh Chiya, siman 2. If one partner rented the property to others, that alone does not establish his chazakah. But if the tenants paid rent only to this partner and not to the other, a chazakah is established. The same applies to brothers who are partners, and there is no claim that the other waived his rights merely because the possessor is his brother and poor. Maharit, Choshen Mishpat siman 26. Where two partners owned one house and Reuven possessed the share of one partner who was out of town while living there together with the partner who remained, just as his possession did not establish chazakah against the partner who lived there, it likewise did not establish chazakah against the absent partner. Rashach, part 2, siman 131; see also part 1, siman 55. Knesset HaGedolah.
