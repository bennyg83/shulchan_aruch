One who possessed the property of a minor and said, "It is mortgaged to me, and I have a debt against it," etc. The rules the Mechaber states in this seif and the following seif concerning one who has consumed three years do not naturally belong in this siman, whose subject is whether three years of possession supports a claim of purchase. They concern instead whether another claim may be believed through a migo of purchase, whose proper place is siman 150, as the Tur arranges them. The Mechaber repeats them there in seifim 4 and 5. He follows here the Rambam's order, and also places them here because these two seifim connect directly with the preceding rule. In seif 20 the Mechaber began by saying that where it is not known that the land belonged to the minor's father, possession is effective through the migo that the possessor could say, "It never belonged to your father." Here in seif 21 he adds that in such a case the possessor is believed not only to say, "I purchased it," but even to say, "It was mortgaged to me, the mortgage term ended, and I am continuing to hold it until I recover another debt owed to me," through the migo that he could say it never belonged to the father. The following seif deals with a case where it is known to have belonged to the father but the possessor held it for three years during the father's lifetime. There too he is believed not only to claim purchase but even to claim that it was mortgaged and that another debt remains, through the migo of purchase. From this it follows that the Mechaber's phrase at the beginning of seif 21, "one who possessed the property of a minor," does not mean that he possessed it for three years. Since it is not known to have belonged to the minor's father, even one day of possession makes him the present possessor, and the challenger must prove ancestral title or establish his own three years with a claim of purchase, as explained at the beginning of siman 140. I have explained this at length because the Mechaber's wording here, copied from the Rambam, appears to conflict with siman 150:5, where he follows Rabbeinu Yonah. Here the Rambam's language appears to say that if a public report arose only after the three years, the possessor is still believed to claim another debt through the migo of purchase. In siman 150:5, however, the Mechaber rules that even a report arising after the three years can weaken that migo for a mortgage or other debt, because the possessor may have been afraid to claim outright purchase in the face of the report. The underlying Gemara concerns Rabbah bar Sharshom, who was rumored to be consuming orphans' land. He said that their father had mortgaged it to him and owed him another debt, so after the mortgage term he continued taking produce, intending to suppress the mortgage document and rely upon a migo of purchase. Abaye told him that he could not say it was purchased because there was a public report that it belonged to the orphans. Rashbam, Rav Hai, and the Rambam explain this simply as a report arising within the three years, which destroys the very claim of purchase. Tosafot explain it as a report arising after the three years. According to them, purchase itself would still be believed, but the possessor cannot use purchase as a migo for another debt because he was unwilling to brazenly contradict the public report. Rabbeinu Yonah follows Tosafot, and this is the source of siman 150:5. The Rambam, by contrast, appears to permit such a migo once three years have passed. Some attempt to reconcile the Mechaber by saying that here "I have a debt against it" refers only to the original mortgage debt and a claim that the mortgage term has not yet ended. That is unconvincing, because the same concern about contradicting the public report applies, and the Raavad and Maggid Mishneh explicitly derive the Rambam's law from Rabbah bar Sharshom's additional debt. A forced reconciliation is therefore necessary. Perhaps the Mechaber understood that although the Rambam's interpretation of the Gemara does not itself prove that a migo of this kind is rejected after a later report, it is still logically correct because we generally do not accept a migo involving unusual brazenness. He may therefore interpret the Rambam's final statement, that if a report arose that the property belonged to the orphans he is not believed, as referring to the underlying claim of purchase when the report arose within the three years. The implication that after three years he is believed applies only to purchase itself, not necessarily to using purchase as a migo for an additional debt. Thus seif 22 may be understood as dealing specifically where no such public report arose, contrary to the Raavad's interpretation. The Beit Yosef himself leaves the relation between Rabbeinu Yonah and the Raavad in some doubt. After this general explanation, the particular words are clear: "one who possessed the property of a minor" refers back to the case where it is not known to have belonged to the minor's father, and teaches that not only purchase but even another debt may be asserted through the migo of denying the father's title.

The correct reading is: "and he thereby collects from its appreciation." The printed reading "with an oath" is an error. According to the Rambam, in such a case he collects without an oath, as the Mechaber states explicitly in the following seif, also from the Rambam. The Maggid Mishneh explains the reason: there is no severe oath on land, and the Sages did not institute an oath of hesset upon an uncertain claim by an heir who cannot make a definite allegation. Therefore the possessor is believed without an oath. The same reasoning applies here. The Rambam's text indeed reads "from its appreciation," meaning that he collects from the produce or increase that grows from the land. The Ir Shushan, who follows the erroneous printed text and says "he collects with an oath," is incorrect.
Regarding the public report mentioned immediately above. In the general explanation above I demonstrated that when the Mechaber says "the report mentioned immediately above," he means specifically a report that arose before completion of the three years of presumptive possession. If it arose afterward, he remains believed to claim that he purchased the land. This applies specifically to the claim, "It is purchased and held by me." The Rambam had not stated that rule earlier, so he wrote it here, and the Mechaber copied his language even though he had already stated it in seif 20. But as to being believed to say, "I have another debt against him," through the migo that he could have claimed purchase, he is not believed even if the report that it came to him as a mortgage arose only after the three years, as the Tur and Mechaber state in siman 150:5.
