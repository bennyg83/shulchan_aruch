One can argue that since he has no knowledge at all that the property belonged to the father, this is a case of "the mouth that prohibited is the mouth that permitted," which is stronger than an ordinary migo, and therefore should work even for future produce:
From its appreciation. This is the Sema's reading. One should not read, "with an oath," because seif 22 states that he collects without an oath. The Shach reads, "with an oath," and distinguishes seif 22. There the possessor is believed through a migo that he purchased it. Against a purchase claim the heirs cannot make a definite denial, and no oath is imposed on an uncertain claim. Here, however, the migo is that he could have said, "It never belonged to your father." On that claim the heirs make a definite assertion, so he would have been required to swear. Therefore, even when he instead says, "He owed me money," he must swear. Although on that actual claim the heirs are uncertain, since the underlying migo would itself require an oath, this claim also requires one. Shach:
According to the nature of the rumor. Meaning that the rumor must have arisen before three years, so that it is like a protest, as explained in seif 20. If it arose only after three years, he is believed to say, "I purchased it." But he is not believed to say, "He owed me money," through a migo that he could claim purchase, even when the rumor arose after three years, because this is a migo requiring brazenness. Sema. The Shach writes that the Sema erred, because with property of a minor there is no distinction between within three years and after three years. Here, even if the rumor arose only after three years, he is not believed, since his only migo is that he could have said, "It never belonged to you," and once there is a rumor that it belonged to their father, that migo is unavailable because it would require brazenness. The phrase, "according to the nature of the rumor mentioned nearby," means only that he did not acquire a valid chazakah, because even after three years no chazakah operates against a minor. See the Beurim. Certainly, if the only rumor was merely that the property belonged to their father, the migo remains effective, since he need not definitively contradict the rumor. Even with a chazakah unsupported by a claim, he need not return the land until the challenger brings witnesses that it belonged to his ancestors. Therefore the Mechaber says, "according to the nature of the rumor mentioned nearby," meaning that the rumor was that the property came to him from their father as a mortgage, as in seif 20. Once there is a rumor that it came from their father as a mortgage, even if he says he does not know whether it had belonged to their father, he must contradict the rumor, which is brazen.
