Some say that it is a chazakah. According to this view, if the guardian denies the sale and says, "I never sold it," he must reimburse the orphans for the value of the field that the other person acquired by chazakah. There is no greater negligence or damage than remaining silent and allowing another person to establish a chazakah over orphan property. Otherwise there would be a difficulty from the inference made from Rav Huna: since Rav Huna says a relative is not placed over orphan property, a non-relative may be placed there, and this proves that one cannot establish chazakah against a minor. Why would a non-relative safely be appointed if another person could occupy the orphans' land for three years while the guardian intentionally remained silent, thereby acquiring a chazakah through the guardian's failure to protest? The guardian and outsider could collude to deprive the orphans. The answer must be that the guardian would then be personally liable to reimburse the orphans. He gains nothing by the collusion, because he must replace whatever loss he caused them. This is straightforward.
