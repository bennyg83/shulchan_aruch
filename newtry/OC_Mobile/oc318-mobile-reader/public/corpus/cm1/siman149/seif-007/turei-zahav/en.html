(Seif 12) Reuven sold. Reuven assumes that Shimon placed the other person in his stead. This differs from the sharecropper in seif 26, whose replacement acquires prescriptive possession because the owner should have protested lest the replacement damage the field. A purchaser of three years' produce must return the land after three years in the condition in which he received it, and therefore would appoint only a trusted substitute. A sharecropper, by contrast, does not bear the same responsibility for deterioration. The Tur later says this is unlike an artisan. Many ask that siman 134 itself rules that an artisan who ceased his trade may acquire possession. There is no difficulty. That rule means that possession begins after he ceases being an artisan, or that an item received during his trade remains with him afterward for a significant time. Otherwise he has no possession based on the period when he held it as an artisan. The Tur asks why a robber who repents is believed immediately, even without a later delay. He answers that an artisan's possession began before witnesses under the status of artisan, so the adverse circumstance is established, or if witnesses later see the item in his hands we presume it originally came to him through his ordinary trade. Here, by contrast, no definite adverse circumstance exists, because robbery is uncommon; there is only a reputation that he was a robber.
