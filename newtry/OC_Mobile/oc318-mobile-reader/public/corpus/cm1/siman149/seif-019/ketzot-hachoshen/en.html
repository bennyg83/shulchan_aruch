Rambam says there is no presumption against a minor even if the possessor occupied the land before the minor and continued after he matured. Mishneh LaMelech asks: if the possessor claims that the minor sold the property after reaching adulthood, why should the earlier year of occupation during minority not combine with the later two years, just as a father's one year can combine with a son's two years? That cannot be correct. The three year presumption reflects the period during which a purchaser ordinarily keeps his deed. If the possessor says the sale occurred only after the minor became an adult, he has had the land under a purchase claim for only two years. There is no reason he should already have stopped preserving the purchase deed merely because he happened to occupy the land earlier without any purchase. Maggid Mishneh's case of a father occupying one year and the son two is different. The son's claim comes through the father. The original acquisition is attributed to the father, and beit din advances the father's valid claim for the heir. Here the possessor's proposed sale from the former minor occurred only later. The first year had no connection to that acquisition and cannot be counted. The better reading of Rambam is that the possessor claims the minor sold the land while still a minor and later, after reaching the relevant age, knowingly allowed the sale to stand. Rambam elsewhere says that a minor who sold land and, after reaching the age at which the sale can be ratified, remains silent while the purchaser continues using it, can thereby confirm the earlier sale. If possession against minors were otherwise valid, the possessor could claim such a sale and ratification. Rambam therefore needs the special rule that no presumption is established against a minor. The exact age depends on whether the land came from the minor's father or belonged independently to the minor.
