An individual who owns a Torah scroll, etc. This constitutes chazakah. From Darkei Moshe it appears that a chazakah accompanied by a claim is required. It seems that the claim need not be that he purchased the right. Rather, he says that the community agreed to read from his Torah scroll. This itself is a sufficient claim, for the rule is derived from the case of an eruv that had long been placed in a particular house, and what claim of purchase exists there? The essential requirement is that the practice was established with the agreement of the community and congregation, and was not merely done incidentally without anyone paying attention to whose Torah scroll was being used.
