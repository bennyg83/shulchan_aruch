After the guardians have ceased serving, etc. I do not understand this ruling. If it is known that the guardian delivered to the orphans everything that had been under his control as guardian, it is obvious that he may thereafter acquire chazakah. But if it is not known that he delivered the property, and all that is known is that the period of his guardianship ended, how does this accord with the preceding seif, which states that a guardian appointed by beit din has no chazakah even after the orphans have grown up? Beit din appointed him only while they were minors, since a guardian is not appointed for adults. Thus, when they reached adulthood his guardianship necessarily ended, yet the preceding rule says that he still has no chazakah. It therefore appears that the present case is where it is known that he delivered some of the fields to the orphans because his period of guardianship had ended, but regarding this particular field we do not know whether he delivered it as well. If he then possessed this field for three years, he has chazakah, because we say that had it also belonged to the orphans he would have delivered it to them together with the others, and they would not have remained silent without protesting. But if it is not known that he restored to the orphans the other fields that had been under his control, then even though the formal period of his appointment has ended, he still has no chazakah, as in the preceding rule concerning one appointed by beit din. This requires study.
