The gentile purchased it in my presence. The Tur adds another case: or the gentile showed me a document proving that he purchased it from you. It appears that this applies specifically if he says the document was authenticated, or that he himself recognized the signatures, because otherwise the document is merely a worthless shard. The Beit Yosef cites a responsum of the Ritva stating that the same applies if the possessor claims that he knows the gentile took the property from the Jew in satisfaction of a debt and then sold it to him. He is believed through the same migo. See Darkei Moshe.
Because he could have said, "I purchased it from you." The reason he would be believed if he made that claim even though it is known that the property came to him from the gentile is explained below in siman 150:3. See also the general rule at the beginning of siman 152: all those who themselves have no presumptive possession may nevertheless have descendants who establish possession if those descendants claim, "We later purchased it from the owners," even when they admit that the property originally came to them through a person who lacked presumptive rights. See the Tur and Sema there.
