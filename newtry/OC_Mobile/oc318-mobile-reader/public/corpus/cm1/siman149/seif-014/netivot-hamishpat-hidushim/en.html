Known to belong to a Jew. Meaning that the challenger now establishes before beit din that the property is his. It is then removed from the possessor because an ordinary non Jew is presumed to have taken land unlawfully and does not establish chazakah. Sema:
His consumption is not proof. The Sema explains that the owner is afraid to protest even against a Jew who derives title from the non Jew. See the Beurim. The principal reason is instead that residence by a non Jew is not considered equivalent to the predecessor having lived there for one day. Therefore we do not advance a claim for the Jewish purchaser who derives title from him unless that purchaser himself makes a definite claim, as in seif 16. In truth, the owner is not necessarily afraid to protest. The Sema further writes that if the non Jew, or a Jew deriving title from him, possessed the property for forty years, this establishes chazakah. See the Beurim. It appears that this is specifically where the property remained in the non Jew's possession for forty years, because by then the dispossessed owner despaired while it was in the non Jew's hands, and it later came permissibly to the Jew.
