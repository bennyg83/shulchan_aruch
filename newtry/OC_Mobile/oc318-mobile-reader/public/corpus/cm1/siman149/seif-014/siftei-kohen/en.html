And the field returns to its owners. See the Sema, seif katan 23, citing Maggid Mishneh, who questions how a claim based on what a non-Jew told him can be considered a certain claim. Responsa Rashba, cited by the Beit Yosef above in siman 72, seif 18, and below at the end of siman 180, clearly treats a claim based on a non-Jew's statement as certain. A careful reading of Maggid Mishneh shows that he holds that even with a Jewish witness one does not impose an oath upon a claim that remains uncertain. His statement may therefore have been added only as an additional point. According to our ruling at the end of siman 75 that an oath may be imposed upon a definite claim founded on a witness's report, the same should apply to a report by a non-Jew. In the present case, however, this reason is unnecessary. Since the non-Jew has an interest in the matter, he is certainly no better than an interested Jewish person, concerning whom the gloss above in siman 75, seif 23, rules that no oath is imposed. This appears correct. See also the gloss below in siman 176, seif 36, and what I write there.
