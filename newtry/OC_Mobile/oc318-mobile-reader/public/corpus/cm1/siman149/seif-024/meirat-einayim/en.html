Some say that it constitutes presumptive possession. The Tur explains their reason: a guardian has authority to sell property when necessary for the orphans' maintenance and stands in the father's place. The opposing view reasons that one who purchases from a guardian is careful to preserve his deed, since he knows the land is not the guardian's own. He therefore cannot rely upon the guardian's failure to protest in the same way that he might rely upon the failure of the orphans' father to protest. A guardian is merely an agent, and with every agent the principal may say, "I sent you to improve my position, not to harm it." See the Tur.
