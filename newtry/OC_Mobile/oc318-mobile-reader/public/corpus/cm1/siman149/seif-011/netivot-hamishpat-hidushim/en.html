In the property of a married woman. Even if the possessor says, "I purchased it from you and your husband," he is not believed. The reason is that she relies on her husband to protest, since he consumes the produce during her lifetime and is destined to inherit her. If, however, the husband removed himself from rights to the produce and inheritance, possession of her property can establish chazakah. If one person possessed it during the husband's lifetime, and after the husband's death another possessed it for three years and claims, "You sold it to your husband in my presence, and I purchased it from your husband," he is believed through a migo that he could have said, "I purchased it from you after your husband's death."
