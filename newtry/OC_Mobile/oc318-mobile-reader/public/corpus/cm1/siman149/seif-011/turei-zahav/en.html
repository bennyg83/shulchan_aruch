(See there, concerning the public report). Sema asks from seif katan 50, but there is no difficulty. There we say that the migo is weakened only when it is known that the property once belonged to the challenger, even if it is not known that possession began as a pledge. The public report then directly contradicts the possessor's claim of purchase, and he is reluctant to claim against it because the other's prior ownership is certain. Here there are no witnesses at all that the property belonged to the fathers, as Raavad explains in his gloss on Rambam. The report says only that it was theirs. Such a report cannot defeat a migo. Therefore, if the report arose only after three years, the migo remains effective.
