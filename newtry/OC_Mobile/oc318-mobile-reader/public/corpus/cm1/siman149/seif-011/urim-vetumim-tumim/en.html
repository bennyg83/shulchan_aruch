And if the third year was completed in the son's possession after he reached majority, we do not remove it from him, etc. I am surprised that the Mechaber states this as though there were no dispute. These words come from the Tur in the name of Ri Barceloni, but many major authorities disagree, and Ri Barceloni is almost alone against them. The Maggid Mishneh in chapter 13 of Hilchot To'en writes that Ramban and Rashba hold that possession can be established even while the child is a minor, because the challenger should have protested. According to the Maggid Mishneh, the Rambam adopts an intermediate position: if the entire chazakah begins against the minor it is ineffective, but if the father had already possessed for two years and the son then continued for one, the chazakah is effective even if the son is a minor. This is why the Rambam in chapter 12 states simply that where the father consumed two years and the son one year there is a chazakah, without distinguishing whether the son was an adult or minor. If Rambam, Ramban, and Rashba all hold that where two years were consumed in the father's lifetime the third year can be completed even while the son is still a minor, how can the Mechaber here rule against them without qualification? All the more so where the minor is presently in possession of the field, he can say kim li like these great authorities, pillars of halachic ruling on whom we rely. This requires great study. The Tur earlier in this siman, paragraph 21, rules like the Rambam and not like Ramban, but even against the Rambam's own intermediate position it is difficult to remove land from the current possessor. Nevertheless, one further point appears correct. Suppose the father consumed for two years; after his death the son, while age twelve, consumed for one year; and then after reaching thirteen the son consumed another year. This should constitute a chazakah. Even if the year during minority does not count positively, it should not interrupt the continuity. The minor did continue consuming the field during that year. Thus the qualifying years of possession are still continuous in the practical sense. Once there are three years of consumption during periods in which protest could have been expected, the intervening minor year should not render them nonconsecutive, because there was no lapse in actual possession. This appears to be why the Tur and Mechaber carefully say that "the third year was completed in the son's possession after he reached majority," rather than simply saying "in the possession of the adult son." The wording suggests that the son need not have been an adult immediately when he entered. If he later reaches majority and completes the required year, the earlier minor period does not interrupt the possession. This also explains why the rule is repeated even though above in siman 144 it was already stated that a son's consumption can join with his father's. The present clause teaches the additional point that even if the son's minority intervened, it does not necessarily break continuity, because the family remained in actual possession throughout. This is a genuine novelty and resolves the repetition.
