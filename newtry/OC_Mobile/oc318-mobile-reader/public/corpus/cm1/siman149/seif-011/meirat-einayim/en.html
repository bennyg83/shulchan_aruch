One does not establish presumptive possession in the property of a married woman. The Tur explains that she relies upon her husband to protest, since he consumes the produce of her property during her lifetime and is expected to inherit her if she dies first. According to the Rif, Rosh, and Rambam, who hold that a stipulation can remove the husband's inheritance rights in the circumstances explained in Even HaEzer siman 92, it follows that if the husband wrote a complete disclaimer of any benefit from her property, its produce, and inheritance both during her life and at her death, presumptive possession against her can then be established. The present rule therefore concerns a case where he made no such disclaimer, or where he disclaimed only rights during her lifetime but not inheritance after death. The Tur further writes in the name of the Ramah that even if the possessor claims, "I purchased it from you and your husband," he is not believed. He also writes that if the possessor held the land during the husband's lifetime and then, after the husband's death, held it before the woman for three years, claiming that she had sold it to her husband in his presence and that the husband then sold it to him, he is believed through the migo that he could have claimed to have purchased it from her after the husband's death. The Mechaber omitted these details here and relied upon the laws stated in Even HaEzer. Another reason for omitting the latter rule is that it depends upon a dispute whether one can establish possession after an initial period during which possession could not establish title. See the dissenting opinions discussed in the Derishah and in the Sema to siman 150:4. What the Mechaber writes in Even HaEzer at the end of siman 87, that three years after the husband's death establishes possession, may refer to a case where the possessor did not hold the land at all during the husband's lifetime.
