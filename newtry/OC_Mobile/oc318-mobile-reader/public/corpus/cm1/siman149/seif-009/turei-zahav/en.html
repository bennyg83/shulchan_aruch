(Seif 20: but if it is not known, etc.) One cannot object from siman 146, seif 33, where our master writes in the name of the Rosh that this is not a strong migo because the possessor fears claiming that he purchased it lest the other party have witnesses. Why do we not say the same here, that he fears the claimant may have witnesses that the property belonged to his father? The cases differ. There, the claim that he purchased it is itself a strong claim, because he assumes perhaps he can find witnesses that the claimant sold it to him, as explained there. Here, by contrast, his present claim is inherently weaker because he should have preserved his deed.
