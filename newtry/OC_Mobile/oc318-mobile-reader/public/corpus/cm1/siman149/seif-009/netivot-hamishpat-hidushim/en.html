But if he dug. It is difficult, because from the Mechaber's statement that if he built or demolished there is no chazakah, it appears that even digging does not establish chazakah. The Maggid Mishneh likewise understands the Rambam this way. If so, the Rama should have written this as "some say," as is his usual manner. Sema. The Shach writes that building or demolishing is not comparable to digging pits. See the Beurim. Digging pits establishes chazakah only where the owner himself needs the pits, so that from his perspective the act is an improvement, though it damages the wife's interest because the field is not normally made for pits. This applies specifically where he consumed the produce of the pits for three years, so there is no defect arising from absence of a deed. Therefore it establishes chazakah for the husband, and certainly for another person in a similar case, since he consumed the produce for three years and the activity benefited him. But where the act damages everyone, such as demolition, or improves the property for everyone, such as construction, it does not establish chazakah. This explains why the Rama rules that building and demolition do not establish chazakah even according to his own view, resolving the Sema's question in seif katan 11. The same applies to the case in seif 12 where Reuven sold the produce of his field to Shimon and ordinary consumption does not establish chazakah. If the purchaser dug pits in a field not ordinarily used for such produce and consumed the produce of those pits for three years, it establishes chazakah just as it does for the husband:

Chazakah concerning damages. This depends on the dispute in siman 154 whether three years of possession and an accompanying claim are required. According to the view that they are not required, the law is lenient in treating tolerated damage as chazakah even in the property of a married woman, whether in favor of the husband or another person, because she remained silent and waived her objection. According to the view that three years and a claim are required for a chazakah of damages, such a chazakah is ineffective in the property of a married woman. Therefore the Rama writes this as "some say." In Even HaEzer, end of siman 66, however, he rules without qualification that it is a chazakah, because his halachic inclination is that three years are not required. Sema:
