Sema infers from Rama that a husband who dug pits in his wife's property can create a presumption even if he never wrote that he had no claim in her property and fruits. He takes this as following the Tur rather than Rabbi Meir HaLevi Abulafia. This is difficult because Sema himself writes elsewhere that a husband ordinarily may build and demolish in his wife's melog property during her lifetime because he has a strong fruit interest. If he has the right to dig and alter, her failure to protest cannot create a presumption. This may be precisely why Rabbi Meir HaLevi required a prior disclaimer of rights: only after the husband has removed his normal rights does his digging become conduct she would be expected to protest. Rama's broad statement that digging pits creates a presumption therefore seems to imply the opposite of Sema's view in siman 212. It suggests that the husband has no ordinary right to damage the corpus. Chelkat Mechokek indeed says that even the wife herself may stop him from damaging the body of the land because he owns only fruits. That appears the better rule.
