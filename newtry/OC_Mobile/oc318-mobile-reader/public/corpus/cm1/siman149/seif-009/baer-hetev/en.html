The Maggid Mishneh infers from this that the Rambam holds that even if a husband dug pits, ditches, and caves in his wife's property, he does not thereby acquire a chazakah, as the Tur writes in the name of the Rif and Rosh. If so, the Rama's ruling, 'but if he dug,' which follows the Ramah's view in the Tur, should have been introduced with 'there are those who say,' as is the Rama's usual practice. This requires study. Sema. See what the Taz writes on this point. See the Bach, who writes that building and demolishing is not comparable to digging pits, and see Sefer Imrei Aish, page 87b. Shach.
He dug. Since the Rama states the rule without qualification, it follows that he holds that even if the husband did not write to her, 'I have no claim or dealings with your property or its produce,' digging pits nevertheless establishes a chazakah. See Even HaEzer siman 87, where the Rama does not mention this distinction concerning digging pits. Since that is not the principal place for the laws of chazakah, he abbreviated and did not enter into deciding an issue that depends upon a dispute, particularly since the Rif, Rambam, and Rosh do not hold this way.
