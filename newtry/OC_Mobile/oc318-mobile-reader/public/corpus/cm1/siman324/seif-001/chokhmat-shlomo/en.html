Seif 1, gloss. Some say that all of this applies to a fixed-rent agricultural lease, but with a sharecropping arrangement one may change even to a crop that weakens the soil, while others disagree. Note: The first view is Rashi's. Rashi explains that the mishna cannot be speaking of a fixed-rent lease, because in sharecropping we say, 'Let the land be weakened rather than the owner.' In my humble opinion I do not understand the proof. The earlier case is the reverse: the agreement was to sow wheat, which weakens the soil more, and the tenant instead sowed something less weakening. He then sought to deduct from his obligation because the land had been preserved. There the landowner can properly answer that he preferred greater current profit even at the cost of greater soil depletion, as shown by the original agreement to plant the more weakening crop. But from that case, how can we infer that when the parties agreed to plant a crop that does not weaken the soil, the tenant may deliberately depart from the owner's instructions and plant a more exhausting crop on the theory that it is better to weaken the land than the owner? I do not see the analogy. Divrei Mishpat seeks to defend Rashi based on the end of siman 322 and the Sema there, that under a fixed-rent lease if the tenant wishes not to plant at all, the landowner cannot compel him, because the landlord's agreement was not dependent on receiving produce specifically from this field. If the field fails entirely, the tenant must still purchase the agreed produce from the market. According to that, he says, the Taz's question can be answered. Even so, the statement 'let the land be weakened rather than the owner' was never established for our present direction of change, and I do not see how the earlier case proves it. It is true that the Gemara on Bava Metzia 104 states that from that point onward, rules stated regarding sharecropping do not apply to fixed-rent leasing, and vice versa. This supports Rashi, because otherwise the present mishna could apply to both. Nevertheless, Rashi's particular proof from the phrase about weakening the land still requires study, because the cases are not parallel. Conversely, the dissenting view is difficult in light of the Gemara's statement that this mishna does not apply to sharecropping. This requires study.
