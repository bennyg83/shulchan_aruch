One who leases a field to sow a specified crop and wishes to change it. It contains one seif:
One who leases a field to sow barley may not sow wheat, because wheat weakens the soil more than barley. If he leased it to sow wheat, he may sow barley. If he leased it for legumes, he may not sow grain; if he leased it for grain, he may sow legumes. Some say the reverse: if it was leased for grain he may not sow legumes, while if it was leased for legumes he may sow grain. Tur, seif 1. In Babylonia and similar places, he may not sow legumes because there legumes weaken the soil. Rama: Some say the reverse, that in Babylonia he may sow whatever he wishes. Tur. Some say that all this applies to a fixed-rent lease, but in a sharecropping arrangement he may change even to a crop that weakens the soil. Tur and Rashi. Others disagree and hold that even to a crop that does not weaken the soil he may not change under a sharecropping arrangement. Maggid Mishneh, chapter 8 of Hilchot Sechirut.
