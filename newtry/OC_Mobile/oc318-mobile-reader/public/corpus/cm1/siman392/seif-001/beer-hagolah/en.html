a) The language of the Rambam, chapter 2 of Hilkhot Nizkei Mamon, law 17, from the Mishnah on 21a. This cake contains coals, since it was ordinarily baked upon them.
bet) The Gemara's interpretation on 53a.
gimel) An interpretation according to Rabbi Yochanan, who holds that liability for fire is because it is like one's arrows, on 22a.
dalet) Rashi explains that it is the dog's force and thus a case of pebbles.
heh) The Raavad objects that pebbles produced through an unusual act should at least incur one quarter damages. The Maggid Mishneh writes that he does not know how to resolve this, but perhaps the Rambam holds that once the vessel was set down, the dog's direct force ended, and it is no longer liable for dragging because the stack burned only through the later dragging. The Kesef Mishneh explains that the Rambam's word for dragging means that the dog flung the coal, as Tosafot explain, and therefore it is exempt. In my humble opinion, the Rambam's wording does not support this, and the Beit Yosef cites the Rashba that all the more so if it flung it, Rabbi Yochanan would impose half damages. The Tur likewise writes this and the Ramah cites it in a gloss.
vav) Ibid., law 18. The Maggid Mishneh writes that this follows the sugya on 23a and the reading before Tosafot. There is another explanation based on the reading, 'let the owner of the coal also be liable,' which is our reading and Rashi's.
zayin) Tosafot likewise write there. The reason is that where there is no partner in liability, the dog owner pays only half, meaning one quarter of the total loss, while the owner of the coal pays three quarters, because whenever full payment cannot be collected from one liable party, it is collected from the other, in accordance with Rav Natan on 33a.
