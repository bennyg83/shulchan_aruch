The law of a dog that took a cake together with a coal, ate the cake, and burned a stack of grain. It contains one seif.
If a dog took a cake and went to the cake owner's stack, placed it there, ate the cake, and the coal attached to it burned the stack, the dog's owner pays full damages for the cake and for the place of the cake, and half-damages for the remainder of the stack. If the dog dragged the cake along the stack, burning it as it went, the owner pays full damages for the cake, half-damages for the place touched by the coal, and is exempt for the rest of the stack. Some say he pays half-damages for the entire stack. This applies where the owner of the coal guarded his fire and closed the door, and the dog dug through and took the cake from the fire. If the owner did not guard his fire, the fire's owner is liable for burning the stack, while the dog's owner is liable for eating the cake and for the place where it lay. Rama: Some say that for the place of the cake each owner pays one half, while for the remainder of the stack the dog's owner pays one quarter and the coal's owner three quarters. If the dog threw the cake onto the stack, the dog's owner pays one quarter even for the place of the cake, and the coal's owner pays three quarters.
