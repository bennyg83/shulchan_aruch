And if it was dragging, etc. See Tosafos there, s.v. "and Rabbi Yochanan," and the Rashba's question. This interpretation fits more naturally because it avoids distinguishing between the place of the coal and the rest of the stack. The Rashba explains, however, that the Gemara means merely that Rabbi Yochanan's view can be established even when it set the coal down, unlike Reish Lakish. This is the meaning of "and some say," etc.; see what I wrote in siman 390 seif 5.
(Collected note) And some say that he pays, etc. The Rosh explains so. He writes that Tosafos did not, though Yam Shel Shlomo says Tosafos spoke only according to Sumchus, since Rabbi Elazar's statement was there established as Sumchus. His words are difficult, for if it were not considered his direct force the liability would be half damages, which itself would answer Rabbi Elazar. Tosafos' question can nevertheless be answered in the manner suggested by Yam Shel Shlomo. (End of note.)
The owner of the fire, etc. This is the plain meaning of the Gemara's question, "let him be liable," etc.
And some say, etc. Tosafos, s.v. "and let him be liable," explains so, as stated in siman 390 seif 10.
And if it threw it, etc. This follows the first gloss, "and some say that he pays," etc. All this follows the Raavad and Ramah as cited in siman 391 seif 5. According to Tosafos, however, the question is whether the dog's owner should be exempt from his share; see Tosafos there, s.v. "and let the second be liable."
