In this kind of case, Hagahot HaTur to siman 32, note 56, cites Mahari Ben Lev and other authorities that the rule applies specifically where two other witnesses saw the admission. But if he admitted before one witness, while other witnesses observed him without his knowledge, the statement is not treated as a valid admission. It appears that they follow Baal HaMaor's view that an admission before one witness is ineffective, because when there are not two witnesses the person does not take care to make a legally binding admission. According to that view, what difference does it make that two hidden witnesses observed him if he did not know of them? But according to the Shulchan Aruch's ruling in the following seif that an admission before one witness is sufficient to impose an oath, and if the defendant admits the facts he must pay, how could he now swear falsely when there are witnesses who saw that he admitted? He cannot contradict witnesses. It is therefore obvious that he must pay. With respect, the authorities should not have stated their rule so broadly. Mahari Ben Lev, part 3, is not presently available to me for examination.
