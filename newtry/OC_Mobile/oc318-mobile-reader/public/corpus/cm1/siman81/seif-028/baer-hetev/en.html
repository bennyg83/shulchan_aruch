To him. The Tur writes that a person is presumed to swear only truthfully and with certainty. The Shach concludes that even if the oath was not taken before witnesses and even after the due date, when the debtor would otherwise have a migo of payment, he is still not believed to retract by saying he was mistaken. The authorities indicate generally that once a person swore, he is not believed to undermine the sworn statement. This is contrary to Responsa Maharshdam 74. See also the cited responsa 82 and 164.

The Rama writes in siman 163 that where communal taxation is assessed by estimate, a person cannot later say the assessment was mistaken, but where it is based on an oath he can sometimes say there was an error. The Shach explains that the latter oath ordinarily is not, "I possess exactly this amount," but rather, "I do not possess more than this amount." Even if he presents a list and swears, the oath generally means only that he owns no more than what appears on the list. The Sema's forced explanation there is unnecessary. Mahari Weil itself does not discuss the swearer claiming mistake, but rather the appraisers. Even so, the Shach considers the legal principle correct where the mistake can actually be proven.
