And he swore, etc. Responsa Maharashdam, siman 74, rules that one who swore to pay his fellow and later said, “I reviewed my account and discovered that I was mistaken,” is believed if the oath was not taken before witnesses, or if the due date has passed so that he has a migo that he already paid, analogous to the rule below in siman 126, seif 13, concerning a three-party transfer. This is his conclusion in brief. The ruling is surprising. Baal HaTerumot and the Tur state here that a person is not believed to say, “I forgot and therefore swore, but later remembered that I owe nothing.” Perhaps Maharashdam understood their case as an admission before witnesses where no migo existed. Their plain language, however, does not indicate this. It appears that Maharashdam overlooked these authorities, for otherwise he should at least have cited and discussed them. The principal practical rule is that once a person swore to the debt, he is not believed under any circumstances to claim a mistake. As the authorities explain, a person does not take an oath unless he knows the matter clearly. This also appears from the Rama’s qualification that the rule applies specifically when he admits the underlying matter. See Responsa Maharashdam, simanim 82 and 164.
Likewise, if he claims, etc. The Rama writes below in siman 163, seif 3, that where an assessment is imposed according to valuation, one cannot say that he erred, whereas where the amount is fixed on the basis of an oath, he may say that he erred. There the person does not swear that he owns precisely a stated amount. He swears only that he owns no more than that amount. Even if he submits a written list and swears, the ordinary meaning is not that he swears the list is exactly complete, but that he owns no more than appears on it. The Sema’s strained explanation there in seif katan 28 is therefore unnecessary. Mahari Weil, siman 84, does not actually discuss a person who says that his oath was mistaken, but rather the assessors. Nevertheless, the stated rule appears correct where he can demonstrate the error.
