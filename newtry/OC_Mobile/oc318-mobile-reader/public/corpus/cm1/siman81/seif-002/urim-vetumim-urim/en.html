See Tumim regarding the Shach's distinction between silence from which we infer that they admitted the deathly ill person's statement was true, and silence that might be treated as a waiver. There is also a distinction between a direct claim, such as, 'I have such and such in your possession,' where silence can be treated as admission, and a general statement such as, 'All debts owed to me are paid,' where the creditor's silence need not mean that his own debt is included. He can say, 'Why should I respond when you did not address a claim to me?' Even so, I wrote there that this entire law requires very great study for practical application.
