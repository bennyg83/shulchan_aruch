They remained silent at his testamentary statement. The Shach distinguishes as follows. Where the statement itself could be true, such as, "I have money of yours in my possession," and the other remains silent, silence is an admission. But where the legal effect would rest on waiver, silence does not create waiver. Likewise, where the statement is not addressed directly to him, such as, "All notes held against me have been paid," the other person's silence does not help. Urim VeTumim rules that silence is generally not an admission. See Beurim: silence is considered admission only where a dying person directs that a particular object be given to others in the presence of one who claims an interest in it and that person remains silent. Since the words of a dying person are treated as delivered, this resembles dedicating property in his presence while he remains silent. Otherwise silence is not admission. Taz likewise agrees that silence is not admission.
