(Tur, seif 16: “But if he did not anticipate it.”) The opening case derives its force from the phrase “awake and asleep,” since the speaker would not have used it without reason. Why then does “You are my witnesses” in the later case not itself prove that hidden witnesses were present? The addition “asleep” explains the distinction. Had he said only “awake,” the person making the admission might have mistakenly thought some people sleeping in the room were awake and capable of witnessing. By saying “awake and asleep,” he indicates awareness of the sleepers and implies that other awake people are present. In the later case, when he says merely “You are my witnesses,” he may be addressing those he mistakenly thinks are awake, while actually seeing only sleepers. He therefore need not suspect hidden awake witnesses.
