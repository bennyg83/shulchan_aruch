Seif 4. (One who is in possession.) A person held a document against a government official who had received a fixed sum of money in a transaction involving liquor. The document was written in the holder's name and in the name of another person. The other party claimed that a certain portion of the sum was actually his because he had originally supplied it, although later he received that same amount from someone else as the agent of the document holder. He now claims that he returned that amount to the agent. The document holder claims that at a later time the other party expressly admitted before him that he had not returned it, although he now says that he did. According to the Shach in siman 81, seif katan 12, the document holder could not swear on the strength of that admission alone that the amount represented by the document belongs to him, because even had the admission been made before witnesses, the other party could claim that he was merely jesting, especially where the admission did not follow a formal demand. The other party indeed claimed that the agent had asked him to say that he had not returned the money, although he says he did not actually say so. Nevertheless, the document holder is in possession by virtue of the written instrument as to half the amount, and under the secular courts it would likewise be payable to him. This resembles an admission concerning money already held by the other party. Although the Shach disagrees with the Sema, the Urim VeTumim rules like the Sema that a claim of joking does not apply when the other party is already in possession, though it writes that this distinction applies specifically to money and not to identified objects. Here, however, the instrument is effectively like money. The Tumim explains that the distinction between money and a specific object is based on waiver. Waiver does not operate upon an identified object, whereas with money, where waiver applies, an admission itself can effect the waiver because waiver requires no kinyan. One cannot then claim joking as he could regarding an ordinary admission or transfer. So too here, according to his own admission, the matter is one of waiver requiring no kinyan. Once he waived the claim, the half of the document written in the holder's name remains his. Although the general halachah is not absolutely clear against the Shach, even the Shach did not state his opposition to the Sema so decisively as to justify extracting money on that basis here.
