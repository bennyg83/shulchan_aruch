If he admitted, etc. This is stated in Ketubot there: the case is, etc. See Tosafot there.
Or he admitted. As stated there, the force of a written instrument is strong, etc. All the more so an act of acquisition. See Ketubot 102b, where the case is one in which an acquisition was made from him, and Rashi there. In every place an act of acquisition is treated like a document.
Or, etc. Sefer HaTerumot writes that a three-party transfer is equivalent to a document and an act of acquisition. Moreover, the third party did not sue him, so the defense of jesting does not apply, as explained above. See Baer HaGolah to siman 126:13.
Nor by claiming, etc. This follows from Gittin 14a in the incident involving Ginai. Why did Rav Nachman not assert for him that he admitted only to avoid appearing wealthy, in accordance with Rav Nachman's own view in Sanhedrin? One cannot answer that he said, 'You are my witnesses,' because then he would be liable even without an act of acquisition or a three-party transfer, as stated in Ketubot. Rather, an act of acquisition and a three-party transfer are as forceful as the words 'You are my witnesses' against both the defenses of jesting and avoidance of appearing wealthy. Nor can one say that neither defense was relevant in that incident, because then the need for an act of acquisition and a three-party transfer remains unexplained: an admission itself effects acquisition even where no debt existed, as stated in Ketubot and Bava Batra. Thus the words 'You are my witnesses' were needed to preclude these defenses. See Rashi there.
