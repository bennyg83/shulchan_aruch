An admission before one witness, etc. This is the view of Rosh there and Ramban. As stated on 30b, one admission may combine with another. Although the Gemara there says that the witness told him, etc., regarding this maneh, that is only according to the initial assumption that one loan does not combine with another, and even then only because combination was required. The Yerushalmi to Sanhedrin, on the mishnah at 29a, states that if he intended to transmit testimony to him, it is testimony, referring to the mishnah's case where one says, 'He told me,' etc. Rambam, however, formulated the law in terms of an admission, as stated in seif 9. See Baer HaGolah, seif katan 60. The statement in Bava Batra 41a that an admission is before two is not precise; it means that two are sufficient for writing it, for otherwise an act of acquisition would also require two, which is incorrect. See Tosafot to Sanhedrin 6a. Kiddushin 43a likewise proves that, according to the questioner's reasoning, although one generally requires the words 'You are my witnesses,' even one witness suffices when those words were said. This is a strong proof.
