An admission before one witness. See Shach, seif katan 22, who disputes the Mechaber and holds that an admission before one witness is ineffective. If, however, he admits before two witnesses separately, one not in the presence of the other, the admission is effective. Ketzot HaChoshen asks why we do not suspect that one of the witnesses is lying, in which case only one valid witness would remain regarding the admission and that would be ineffective. This is not difficult. We can say that it is a Scriptural rule that once the Torah allows separate testimonies to be joined, we do not suspect that one of the witnesses is lying. A proof is Kiddushin, where Ran explains that separate testimony is invalid because it resembles capital cases. This implies that if it did not resemble capital cases, the testimony would be valid. Why would we not suspect that one witness is lying, when a single witness to kiddushin has no legal effect? Likewise, where two witnesses sign a document separately, not in one another's presence, the document is valid even for collection from encumbered property, and we do not suspect that one of them lied. It must therefore be a Scriptural rule that once testimony is legally joined, we do not entertain that concern. Tumim, however, writes that the accepted halachah among Israel follows the Mechaber.
