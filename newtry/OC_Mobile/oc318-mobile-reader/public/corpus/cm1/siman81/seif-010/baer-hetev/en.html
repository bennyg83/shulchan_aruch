An admission. The Shach writes that Baal HaMaor disagrees and holds that an admission before a single witness is not an effective admission at all, even where the debtor admits that he made the admission before that witness and said to him, 'You are a witness.' Many poskim disagree with Baal HaMaor. See there, where the Shach discusses the matter at length, explains Baal HaMaor's position, and writes that it appears to him to be the principal view. He concludes: Since many poskim do not agree with Baal HaMaor, I am not worthy to disagree with them. Nevertheless, it appears clear to me that the party in possession may say, 'I hold like Baal HaMaor.' I also found this in the responsum of Maharashdam, siman 37. However, as to the Raavad's view that admissions made separately before two witnesses do not combine unless each witness says, 'He admitted before me and before my fellow,' the Raavad stands alone and I have found no one who agrees with him. Even Baal HaMaor holds that one admission after another combines, even though no additional witness was present. This is also the plain implication of the Gemara in Sanhedrin, which states without qualification that one admission after another combines, and the same appears from all the poskim. The reason is as I wrote above: since he returned and admitted again before another person, it is evident that the first admission was not intended as jesting. This appears to me to be the principal ruling. See the responsa of Maharit, simanim 72 and 73. Thus concludes the Shach.
