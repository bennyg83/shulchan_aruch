To say. The Bach writes that although the Baal HaTerumot states an even greater rule, namely that if the claimant is holding property of the defendant the defendant cannot claim that his admission was made in jest, nevertheless our case teaches that even where the claimant merely holds a promissory note, the admission does not require the words, "You are my witnesses." It appears to me, however, that this applies only to an admission, for we say that the admission is certainly genuine and that he received what he admitted receiving. With respect to a waiver, however, the Tur writes in siman 12 in the name of R. Yeshayah that if the debtor waives while still holding the note, a kinyan is required. Likewise, in siman 241, seif 2, I cited the Baal HaIttur that this is a matter of unresolved law. Shach.
Write it. The reason an admission requires the instruction "Write it" is so that the admission should not become a documented debt against him without his having directed that it be written. By contrast, a waiver or release does not require such an instruction. This is the language of the Tur and Baal HaTerumot. See also siman 39, end of seif 3 in the gloss, and Responsa Maharashach, part 3, siman 98. Shach.
