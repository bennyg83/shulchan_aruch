One who admits, etc. If people told him, "So and so holds two hundred of yours," and he said, "I have only one hundred there," or he volunteered, "I have only one hundred with him," and later sued that person for two hundred, the defendant is obligated to pay only one hundred. If the defendant admits one hundred, he need not swear concerning the remaining hundred, and this is not treated as a partial admission at all. So appears in Beit Yosef according to the emendation of Knesset HaGedolah in siman 32, gloss to Beit Yosef, letter 4. There is no need for what the Shach writes in seif katan 73.
He need not say, "Write it." To say "write" concerning an admission would create the status of a documented loan. Therefore, if he did not direct witnesses to write, they generally cannot transform an oral obligation into a formal document against his will. A receipt is different. See what I wrote in Tumim, for this ruling requires great study.
