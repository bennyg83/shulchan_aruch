Who says. The Sema writes that the phrase 'there is one who says' is used because according to Maimonides, whenever a person admits in the claimant's presence he cannot say that he spoke merely so people would not think him wealthy, even if the claimant did not first demand payment. This does not appear correct. Rather, it appears that the Mechaber saw that Baal HaTerumot disagrees, as cited by Beit Yosef at the end of siman 32. Baal HaTerumot's words imply that even one who admits in response to a demand can still claim that he spoke so people would not think him wealthy. The author of Gidulei Terumah understood Baal HaTerumot this way, and this is also explicit in Baal HaMaor in the chapter Zeh Borer. Nevertheless, as a matter of practical law I have already demonstrated in seif 14 from the Gemara that the principal ruling is like Maimonides: if he admitted in the claimant's presence, even if the claimant did not demand payment, he cannot use this defense. There is also a strong logical basis. How would one admit a debt merely to prevent people from thinking he is wealthy when the very person before whom he admits knows that the admission is false and that no debt is owed? He should have made such a statement before someone else. All the more so, if the claimant demanded payment and he admitted, he cannot use this defense. Shach.
