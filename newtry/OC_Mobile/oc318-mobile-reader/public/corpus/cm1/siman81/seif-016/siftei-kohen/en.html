There is an authority who says. The Sema, seif katan 41, writes that the Mechaber used this formulation because, according to Rambam’s view cited above in seif 14, whenever a person admitted before the claimant he cannot claim that he merely intended not to appear wealthy, even if the claimant had not first demanded payment. This is unconvincing. Rather, it appears that the Mechaber saw that Baal HaTerumot disagrees. In Gate 42, part 1, Baal HaTerumot writes: “If people called him a wealthy man and he responded, ‘To whom do I owe money other than so-and-so?’ he may in all these cases claim that he was jesting or that he intended not to appear wealthy. This follows from the Gemara. Consequently, where one concealed witnesses who heard him admit owing one hundred, or where he admitted voluntarily and witnesses heard him, or where another demanded payment before witnesses and he admitted but did not say, ‘You are my witnesses,’ in all these cases, if he claims, ‘I was jesting with you,’ ‘I intended not to appear wealthy,’ or ‘I never said this,’ he is believed. If he does not raise such a claim, the court does not advance it for him, except in the case of a voluntary admission, as explained above.” Beit Yosef cites this at the end of siman 32. Since Baal HaTerumot writes that in all these cases he may claim either jesting or that he intended not to appear wealthy, it follows that even one who admitted in response to a demand may claim that he wished not to appear wealthy. The Gidulei Terumah likewise understood Baal HaTerumot this way, and this is also explicit in the words of Baal HaMaor in Zeh Borer. Nevertheless, regarding the practical ruling, I have already demonstrated above in seif 14, seif katan 33, from the Gemara, that Rambam’s view is primary. Even if the claimant did not first demand payment, once the defendant admitted before him, the defendant cannot claim that he wished not to appear wealthy. This is also compelling in reason. How could he make a false admission before the very person who knows that the debt is untrue merely so that others would think him less wealthy? He should have made such an admission before someone else. All the more so, where the claimant demanded payment and he admitted, he cannot claim that he merely wished not to appear wealthy.
