He holds the defendant's money. The rationale is that because the claimant knows his own money is presently in the defendant's hands, the claimant would not ordinarily admit liability to the defendant unless it were true. Gidulei Terumah questions Baal HaTerumot's analogy to the case where one claims wheat and the defendant admits barley. Baal HaTerumot himself does not accept Raavad's explanation that the barley admission is disregarded as joking. He holds instead that the claimant's wheat claim is treated as an admission that no barley is owed. If so, the rule there has nothing to do with joking and cannot prove that seizure helps here. The same difficulty applies to the Mechaber, who likewise adopts the admission explanation in siman 88. How then can the Gemara's answer, "when he seized," prove that seizure removes the joking defense? The answer is not obvious, and see the Shach. I address the issue further in siman 88, seif katan 6.
