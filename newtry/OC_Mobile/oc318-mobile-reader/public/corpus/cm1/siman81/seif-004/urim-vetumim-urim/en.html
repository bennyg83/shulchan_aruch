He is holding money belonging to the defendant, etc. See Tumim. This ruling requires study, as I wrote there. At the least I concluded that it applies specifically where he holds money, as the Tur and Mechaber state. According to his own admission he need not return that money because the other party owes him the same amount, so the situation can be treated as a waiver or offset. If he holds the defendant's particular object, which he must ultimately return in kind, it requires study whether possession helps. The principal view is also as the Sema writes, that it is enough if he already held the money before the admission and need not expressly say that he is holding it for this purpose. I also consider the Bach correct that he must actually be in possession at the time of the admission; later seizure does not help. See Tumim.
