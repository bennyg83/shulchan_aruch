For she can say, etc. Meaning that although Ketubot 58b teaches not to explain Rabbi Meir's view simply by saying that a person can consecrate something not yet in existence, but rather as though the husband said, "Your hands are consecrated for what they produce," that works only according to the opinion that a husband may compel his wife to perform her handiwork and she cannot withdraw from that obligation. In such a view, her hands are effectively his and he can consecrate them. But according to us, who rule that he cannot compel her and that whenever she wishes she can say, "I will not be supported by you and I will not work for you," her hands are not his even while he is supporting her. Therefore, even if he expressly says, "Your hands are consecrated for what they produce," he cannot consecrate them. Although the Ran in responsum 35 writes like Rambam, that was not the point for which he was asked there; he cited it merely as proof concerning land of nichsei melog. In his commentary on the Rif, however, Ran writes that Rambam's words were not fully clear to him. The Tur likewise implies a position unlike Rambam, for regarding the husband it does not write that the consecration is effective at all, while regarding the woman it does.
