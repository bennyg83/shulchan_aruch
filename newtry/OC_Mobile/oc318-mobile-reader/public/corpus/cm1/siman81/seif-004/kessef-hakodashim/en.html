(See Shach, seif katan 22.) The Shach writes in Choshen Mishpat siman 81, seif 10, that a kinyan should be made before two witnesses. It appears clear that he said this only where the person against whom the kinyan sudar is asserted denies that the kinyan was made regarding this matter. There the Shach inclines to require two witnesses. He concludes that even regarding an admission, where the need for two witnesses is stronger, a party in possession may nevertheless say, "I rely on the authorities who do not require them." But where the person admits that a kinyan sudar was made, it appears that everyone agrees that it is effective. This is certainly no weaker than saying, "I obligate myself to you for one hundred zuz by a written instrument," where we rule that the person becomes obligated thereby. All the more so, an admission before beit din that he became obligated through a kinyan sudar, which is capable of generating a written instrument and has the force of one for all relevant purposes, is effective. Certainly this applies where he admits that he waived his right through a kinyan sudar, which is a strong form of acquisition, unless he had first expressly retracted from the kinyan before disclosing it in beit din. So long as he used language of admission as above, it is effective according to all opinions.
