Seizes. Meaning the money had already entered his possession as a deposit, loan, or similar arrangement, and he continued holding it when the other party made the admission. Sema.
The defendant. This also assumes that witnesses presently saw the property in his hands, so he has no migo of complete denial or return. Where there are no such witnesses, the law later explains that even without witnesses to the admission he may retain the other party's money up to the amount of his claim through a migo of denial. Sema.
To claim. The Sema explains that since the admitting party knew his money was already in the other's possession, he would not have admitted a debt unless he truly owed it. The Ir Shushan instead limits the rule to seizure at the moment of the claim, reasoning that once the defendant saw the claimant take the trouble to seize his property, he would not respond with a playful admission. The Sema rejects that. The Shach, however, sides with the Ir Shushan and says its reading is better supported by the Rama and Nimukei Yosef. He therefore interprets the Tur and Baal HaTerumot accordingly. The Shach also cites the Bach in the name of Baal HaTerumot that seizure must precede the admission, but he is not convinced. In his view even seizure after the admission can be effective so long as the defendant had not yet retracted his admission. He regards this as the principal law and leaves the contrary sources requiring study.
