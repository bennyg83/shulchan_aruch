If one claims a specified object and the holder replies, 'It is not yours but belongs to so-and-so,' then even if he said this before beit din, it is not an admission that allows that third person to remove the object from his possession. {Rama: Others disagree and hold that it is an admission. Nevertheless, he may later say, 'I erred in my admission; it belongs to others,' through a migo that he could have said, 'I returned it to that person.' He cannot say, 'I erred in my admission; it is mine,' even though he has a migo, because his own admission is like witnesses against him, and he cannot afterward say that he erred even when he has a migo. With respect to an accounting written in his ledger, however, he may say, 'I erred,' even without a migo. See below, siman 126, seif 13.}
