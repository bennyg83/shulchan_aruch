He claimed an object from him, etc. See Tumim. I cited Maharibal and Pnei Moshe, who say that if a person has a deposit in another person's hands and tells the custodian that it belongs to someone else, he can no longer claim joking or that he sought not to appear wealthy. How could he make such an admission when the custodian might hand the item to the third party? I wrote that their ruling does not appear correct. But if he actually tells the custodian, 'Give it to that person,' does not immediately retract, and the custodian has a practical opportunity to deliver it, then he can no longer claim joking or that he sought not to appear wealthy.

An exception is where he said, 'The object belongs to so-and-so,' beit din ruled that it indeed belonged to that person and ordered delivery, and he remained silent. In that case he certainly cannot later say, 'I only intended to delay him,' because he should not have remained silent after the formal judgment.
But he cannot say, 'I erred.' The Shach disputes this ruling. See Tumim, where I established that Tosafot's words are clear. This applies specifically where he first admitted before witnesses. But if he merely admitted privately to himself, Rama below in siman 126:13 holds that he can later say, 'I erred.' See what I wrote in Tumim.
