The Beit Yosef writes that this disagrees with the Raavad's view below in seif 30 that even if a person paid money as "profit," he can say that he intended to joke. The Shach in seif katan 78 answers that the case there is different because the recipient is holding money belonging to the other party. According to the Shach, if this claimant later seized property belonging to his fellow, he could say, "I am retaining it because the money I previously gave you as profit was only a joke." Another distinction is possible. In the Raavad's case, the payer can say, "When I gave you the money, although I called it profit, I was joking and actually intended it as repayment of principal." But here, how could a person jokingly hand over money for no reason at all? According to this distinction, even here if the payer claims, "Those funds were payment of a separate debt," he should likewise be believed according to the Raavad. He could say, "I gave you money that I owed you for another reason." It is possible, however, that even the Raavad would agree here. In his case the lender cannot assert with certainty that the money was profit, because how would he know? His entire knowledge comes from the payer, who said that it was profit and can now answer, "I was joking with you." How then can the recipient retain the payer's money merely on the basis of doubt, when seizure based on doubt is ineffective? Here, by contrast, the recipient asserts with certainty, "You owe me, and this payment was partial repayment of that debt." It is therefore obvious that he may retain the funds, and the debtor's prior admission remains an admission.
