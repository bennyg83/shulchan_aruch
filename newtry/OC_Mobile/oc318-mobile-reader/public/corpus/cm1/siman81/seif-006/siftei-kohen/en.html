He admitted before beit din, etc. See below, seif 22, seif katan 56, concerning an admission before beit din.
The defendant said to them, “You are my witnesses,” etc. Above, seif 8, seif katan 19, I conclude that where the defendant says, “You are my witnesses,” it is effective even outside the claimant’s presence. The Sema cites the Hagahot Maimoniyot, chapter 6 of Hilchot To’en VeNit’an, the Hagahot Ashri, and the Mordechai in Zeh Borer, that with respect to any condition that people stipulate and carry out between themselves, one cannot say, “I was jesting with you.” The Darchei Moshe, siman 32, seif 19, cites this. The Semag, page 182, end of column 3, writes: “Rabbenu Shmuel explained that only in a verbal exchange, where one makes a claim and the other responds, must he say, ‘You are my witnesses.’ But where a condition was established between them before the witnesses, there is no need to say, ‘You are my witnesses.’ Proof is from Kiddushin: ‘If so, one who betroths a woman before two witnesses would likewise have to say, You are my witnesses.’” The printed words “established by a kinyan” appear to be a scribal error. It should read: “a condition established between them in the witnesses’ presence.” This is the reading in the Hagahot Maimoniyot and Hagahot Ashri. Tosafot in Kiddushin 43a state similarly. The principal discussion appears in the Hagahot Ashri to Zeh Borer; see there.
Or the defendant remained silent, etc. Silence is equivalent to an admission. Baal HaIttur, at the beginning of the entry Hoda’ah, writes that the same applies where the lender says, “Write it,” and the borrower remains silent: the witnesses may write a document. Such silence is no weaker than silence at the inception of an admission. His meaning is as follows. The debt is already known as an oral debt. The lender says, “Write it,” in order to convert it into a documented debt, and the borrower remains silent. If silence can create an oral debt where no debt was previously known, then all the more so, where the debt is already known, the borrower’s silence is treated as consent to the writing of a document.
