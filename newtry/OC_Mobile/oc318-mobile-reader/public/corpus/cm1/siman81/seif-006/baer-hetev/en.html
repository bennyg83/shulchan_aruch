That he remained silent. Silence is treated as an admission. The Shach writes in the name of the Ittur that the same applies if the lender says, 'Write it.' Meaning, the oral debt is already known, and the lender says to write it so as to convert it into a debt evidenced by a document, while the debtor remains silent. Such silence is certainly no weaker than silence at the beginning of an admission, where the debt was not otherwise known and the silence creates an oral debt. All the more so here, where the debt is already known, his silence constitutes consent to the writing of a document. Thus concludes the Shach.
