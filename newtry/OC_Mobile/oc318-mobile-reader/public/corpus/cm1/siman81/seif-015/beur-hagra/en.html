According to the opinion mentioned. This is Raavad's view. He explains the Tosefta as discussing one who said, 'You are my witnesses,' yet because he admitted spontaneously he may retract and say that he wished not to appear wealthy. Raavad follows his own position that even this defense must be pleaded by the defendant himself, and he infers it from the Tosefta's phrase, 'he may retract.' See my comments to seif 21.
And some disagree. Ketubot states that if he said, 'You are my witnesses,' etc., and there he admitted spontaneously. The first opinion understands that passage as one where the other party answered yes, which is why the Shulchan Aruch above states that even if he said, 'You are my witnesses,' and the other remained silent, etc. Rambam, who holds that avoiding the appearance of wealth applies only outside the claimant's presence, must explain the Tosefta as a spontaneous admission made outside his presence. Its first clause mentions a demand because ordinarily an admission in the claimant's presence follows a demand. The Tosefta concerns one who did not say, 'You are my witnesses,' but made a formal admission, so he is not believed to claim jesting, only avoidance of appearing wealthy, and that defense applies specifically outside the claimant's presence.
