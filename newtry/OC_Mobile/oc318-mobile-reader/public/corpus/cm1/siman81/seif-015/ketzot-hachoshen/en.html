Even if he said, "You are my witnesses." The Sema understands Raavad to distinguish whether the defendant himself said, "You are my witnesses," or the claimant said it and the defendant remained silent. In the latter case, according to Sema, no one would say the defendant can still claim self impoverishment. Beit Yosef and the Shach read Raavad more broadly, that even where the claimant says "You are my witnesses" and the defendant remains silent, the defendant may still raise self impoverishment. However, where the claimant actually demanded payment and the defendant then formally admitted with "You are my witnesses," he certainly cannot later say he spoke only so as not to appear wealthy, even according to those who sometimes allow that defense after a demanded admission. Here both factors combine: an actual demand and a formal admission. The Tur's objection to Raavad must therefore be read in light of these distinctions. I discuss it further below.
