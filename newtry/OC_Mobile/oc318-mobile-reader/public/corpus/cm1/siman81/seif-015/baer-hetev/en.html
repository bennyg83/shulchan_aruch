And he remained silent. The Sema explains that the case is where the defendant said, 'You are my witnesses,' and the claimant remained silent. He challenges the Beit Yosef and Ir Shushan, who explain that the claimant said, 'You are my witnesses,' and the defendant remained silent. The Shach writes that the Sema's words are not correct. Obviously, the term 'remained silent' naturally refers to the defendant, as written in seif 6 and many other places, because when the defendant remains silent and does not protest we say that he thereby agreed to the claimant's assertion of liability. The expression of silence does not fit the claimant. Although Mordechai distinguishes based on who said 'You are my witnesses,' his reason is that if the claimant said it, that itself constitutes a demand. The Raavad and Tur did not write this and their words do not imply it. The principal view on this point is therefore like the Beit Yosef and Ir Shushan. Thus concludes the Shach. The Taz agrees with the Sema. See there.
