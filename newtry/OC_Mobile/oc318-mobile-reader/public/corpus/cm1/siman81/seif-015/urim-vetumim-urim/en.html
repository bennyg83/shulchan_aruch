And he remained silent, etc. The Shach follows Beit Yosef's explanation: the defendant says, "I owe you a maneh," the claimant says, "You are witnesses," and the defendant then remains silent. In that case the defendant may later say that he admitted only so as not to appear wealthy. But if the defendant himself said, "I owe you a maneh, and you are witnesses," while the claimant remained silent from beginning to end, he is liable and has no defense. The Sema, following the Mordechai, explains the reverse. Only where the defendant says, "I owe you a maneh and you are witnesses," and the claimant remains silent is there no binding admission; but if the defendant says merely, "I owe you a maneh," and the claimant responds, "You are witnesses," while the defendant remains silent, it is a binding admission. See Tumim, where I discuss both explanations. I concluded that the Raavad's language fits the Mordechai and Sema better. See there at length.
He can claim. Raavad, as cited by Baal HaTerumot, gate 42, part 1, explicitly requires the defendant personally to raise this defense. Beit din does not raise it for him. Although Raavad holds the same concerning every defense of avoiding the appearance of wealth and we do not generally rule like him, one should not extend the leniency further. In this special case it is possible that all agree beit din does not raise the defense. The wording of the Tosefta, "he may retract," likewise implies that he must do so himself.
And some disagree. See Tumim. According to the Sema's explanation, where the admission was made entirely outside the claimant's presence and the defendant himself said "you are witnesses," the Tur and Rosh do not necessarily disagree with the first view; the Rama's wording, "since he said 'you are witnesses,'" likewise indicates this. The Rambam is the principal dissenter. Where the claimant was present but had not asserted a claim, according to the Rambam the defense of avoiding wealth does not apply, while according to the Rosh and Tur it can, and they hold that saying "you are witnesses" in the relevant manner may still be ineffective. See there.
