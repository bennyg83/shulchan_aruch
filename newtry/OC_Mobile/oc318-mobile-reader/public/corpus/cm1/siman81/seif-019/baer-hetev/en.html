An admission. According to what is written in seif 17, that where the debtor delivered his handwritten obligation to the person to whom he admitted no oral admission is needed at all, the phrase here, 'even if he did not say, You are my witnesses,' is not precise. Rather, even if he made no oral admission at all, it is effective. The reason the text uses this formulation is that the case concerns a document without witnesses, which at least requires delivery in the presence of witnesses. It therefore teaches that even if at the time of delivery he did not say, 'You are my witnesses,' it is effective. Thus concludes the Sema. The Shach writes: The Mechaber abbreviated the matter here. Know that if a person delivers to his fellow in the presence of witnesses a document stating, 'So and so owes so and so one hundred,' or, 'I owe you one hundred,' and it is not in his own handwriting, this constitutes an admission even though he did not say, 'You are my witnesses,' and he cannot claim either that he was jesting or that he acted only so people would not think him wealthy. This is Rashi's approach. Although Rabbeinu Tam and other poskim explain the Gemara differently, it appears that they do not disagree with Rashi as a matter of law. The same applies if he says orally, 'I owe you one hundred by document.' Since according to Maimonides and the Mechaber in siman 40 one can create an obligation upon himself in this form, it appears that the same wording is effective as an admission. It appears that even those who disagree there would concede in the present context. Consider this carefully. Thus concludes the Shach.
