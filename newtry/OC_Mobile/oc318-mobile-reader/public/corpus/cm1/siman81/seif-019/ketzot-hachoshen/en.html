One who hands his fellow a document. The Shach says the same should apply if he says, "I owe you a maneh in a document." Since Rambam and the Mechaber in siman 40 allow a person to create a new obligation with such language, all the more should it work as an admission. Rav Hai Gaon says similarly. The Shach adds that even Ramban and Rashba, who disagree with Rambam concerning creation of a new obligation, should agree for an admission. Ramban's words in Milchamot do not support this. Rif discusses a dying person who consecrated all his property and then said, "I owe so and so a maneh." Where the creditor holds a validated document, payment is made; where the document is not validated, the dying person's instruction can validate it only if he says, "Give." Baal HaMaor objects that an oral declaration cannot create a lien on encumbered property. Ramban explains that if the dying person says, "I owe so and so a maneh in a document, give it to him," this is treated as though a validated document were produced. The implication is that without "give," even saying, "I owe him a maneh in a document," may still be vulnerable to the self impoverishment explanation. Indeed, according to the Gemara's initial understanding, some such construction is necessary or one could never explain collection from encumbered property. Thus Ramban himself does not treat the phrase, "I owe you a maneh in a document," as automatically equivalent to "You are my witnesses" for purposes of factual admission. Since Rashba likewise disagrees with Rambam concerning creation of a new obligation in that language, there is substantial doubt whether he would validate it here. Because of that doubt, money should not be extracted from the person who made the statement solely on this basis.
