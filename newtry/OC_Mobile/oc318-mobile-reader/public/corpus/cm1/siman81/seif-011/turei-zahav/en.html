(Seif 31.) "The law follows Reuven." The Sema asks that the Rosh makes this ruling depend specifically on the fact that the partners were joint guarantors, as is clear from the Tur. It appears to me that, on the contrary, the Rosh himself requires explanation. Why was this point necessary? Even without it, once they voluntarily paid, they became discharged from their obligation. It appears that the Rosh was troubled by the fact that the circumstances necessarily show that payment was required because of the document. Otherwise, why would both have paid the full twenty-five gold coins when, according to their own account, only one of them admitted the profit? Although the creditor held the document and had a migo enabling him to claim the full amount written there, according to his own account he was entitled only to twelve and a half gold coins, half of the twenty-five. Why did the second party pay another twelve and a half when he did not admit the profit? The Rosh therefore wrote that one of them was compelled to pay the entire amount because he had become a joint guarantor on behalf of his fellow for both principal and profit. The Rosh's responsum concerns that particular case. The Mechaber here, however, deals only with a case where one party was compelled to pay the amount he himself admitted, namely half of the admitted sum, because the claim itself extended only that far according to our ruling that two who borrowed together become ordinary guarantors, not joint guarantors. One must say that when the Rosh wrote that responsum he held that partners become joint guarantors.
