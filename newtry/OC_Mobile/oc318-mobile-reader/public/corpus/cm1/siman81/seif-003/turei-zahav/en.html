(Seif 2: or they remained silent regarding her will.) Mordechai explains that the case concerns a woman who instructed her son to give seven zekukim from her property to her relatives. The son remained silent. After her death he argued that she had never sworn concerning her ketubah, and that his silence was merely to avoid angering her. Mordechai answers that silence at the time of death constitutes admission, even if there are no witnesses and the son himself merely admits that he was silent. I do not understand this. Such silence should not be stronger than one who remains silent when his fellow makes a claim before beit din. The Rama writes in siman 80, seif 1, that if the defendant later gives a reason why he was initially silent, he may change his claim. Here the son gave a sound reason for his silence. Indeed, this very concern, avoiding angering a dying person and disturbing his mind, is cited by our Sages as the reason that a deathbed will was given the force of writing and delivery. Moreover, the Mechaber writes in seif 7 that silence from beginning to end does not constitute admission. This silence at the time of death is no stronger than the case of a healthy person who says, "You are my witnesses," where silence is ineffective. So too here, until she swore regarding her ketubah, the money belonged to the heirs and they had no reason to react to her words. Apparently Mordechai did not accept the principle stated in seif 7. Since that principle is established halachah, money cannot be extracted on the basis of Mordechai's ruling. This appears to me correct.
