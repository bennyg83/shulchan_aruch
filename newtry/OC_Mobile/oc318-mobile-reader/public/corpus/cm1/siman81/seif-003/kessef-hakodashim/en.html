Seif 8. (By way of admission.) Where a person's statement is phrased in the ordinary form of an admission, he should not be able to say afterward that he spoke only in jest or mockery unless the claim fits the recognized halachic form of the defense of jesting. At least where the wording itself indicates that he meant the matter seriously, the defense does not apply. Even if some possible explanation akin to jesting could be imagined, the court does not invent such a defense for him. Therefore one should not refrain on that account from issuing a written confirmation. If the person is absent, his failure to raise the defense of jesting or another explanation proves nothing. If the parties press for a written statement and it is impossible in the local language and legal form to write that the document rests merely on hearing an oral admission, there is support for permitting the signer to keep a private written notation reminding him of the circumstances. This does not create a concern that he is testifying falsely, especially where the written record states the known circumstances showing that the matter was proper.
