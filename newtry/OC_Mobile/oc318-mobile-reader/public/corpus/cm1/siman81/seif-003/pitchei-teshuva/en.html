One who brings him witnesses behind a curtain. See Ketzot HaChoshen and Netivot HaMishpat concerning whether witnesses may testify on the basis of voice recognition. See what I wrote regarding this in Pitchei Teshuva, Even HaEzer, siman 17, seif katan 3, and below, siman 280, seif 1, seif katan 1. See also Responsa Chatam Sofer, Choshen Mishpat, end of siman 2, who writes that even if we say that a person who is not blind may testify by recognizing a voice, that applies when he innocently hears someone and recognizes him by voice. Where witnesses were deliberately concealed and a person was brought before them, however, perhaps another person was introduced who intentionally imitated the defendant's voice. Ramban already writes in his Torah commentary concerning 'the voice is the voice of Jacob' that people can imitate another's voice so as to deceive listeners. In truth, it appears that even an innocent voice recognition alone is insufficient to extract money unless accompanied by some additional recognition, such as touching the person's hand, as with Isaac's instruction, 'Come near, please, and I will feel you, my son.' There is no proof from siman 35, seif 12, in light of the Perishah there. See also what I wrote in siman 55, seif katan 8. He discusses this somewhat at length and concludes that even two witnesses recognizing a person only by voice should not, in his view, suffice to extract money, all the more so one witness whose testimony is to be combined with a witness who saw the event, because a witness may infer rather than know with certainty, as explained at the beginning of the chapter HaIshah Shalom. All the more so where the witness knew in advance that a particular person was about to be brought before him, since he may persuade himself that the voice he hears is that person's voice. See there.
