In a manner of formal admission. Meaning that he said, 'I hereby admit before you,' or any similar expression. Maharam Alshikh and Shach. But mere conversation does not suffice, even if he knew witnesses were present, so long as he did not speak in a formal manner of admission or say, 'You are my witnesses.' Shach is correct, unlike Hagahot HaRosh.

And he pays on their testimony. He can certainly claim that his admission was made so that people would not think him wealthy. But if he instead claims, 'I was joking,' he can no longer retract and say, 'I admitted only so that people would not think me wealthy,' because the formal manner of admission defeats the joking plea. Likewise, if he says, 'It never happened,' he must pay. Joking is unavailable, and if his true intent had been only not to appear wealthy he would remember that and should have admitted making the statement. If he said, 'You are my witnesses,' and the admission was made outside the claimant's presence, there is a dispute among the authorities. Many hold that once he said, 'You are my witnesses,' nothing further can undo the admission. Rosh and Tur, however, hold that even then he can still claim that he admitted only so that people would not think him wealthy. See Tumim for my reconciliation of Tur.
