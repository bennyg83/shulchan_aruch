You are my witnesses. The Shach argues at length that this formula works even when the claimant is absent. He reads Mordechai and Hagahot Maimoniyot as supporting that view and says the Tur's contrary rulings are difficult. In my view Rabbeinu Yitzchak in Tosafot clearly holds that an admission made outside the claimant's presence is ineffective. In Bava Kamma, the Gemara asks whether a thief who admits to the custodian after the custodian already swore is exempt from double payment when the owners later sue. Tosafot derives that an admission to one who is not the true litigant is not a valid admission and asks why the Gemara does not discuss the principal payment itself. This was an admission before an expert ordained beit din, because admissions that exempt from fines require such a court, yet Tosafot still says it does not bind regarding principal when not made to the proper party. Certainly a private admission before witnesses outside the claimant's presence is no stronger. Mordechai himself at the beginning of Perek HaGozel cites Rif and Rabbeinu Yitzchak to this effect. It therefore seems the Shach overlooked that the Mordechai passage in Perek Zeh Borer reflects the same Rabbeinu Yitzchak. The Tur's position is thus well grounded. Authorities who nevertheless validate admissions outside the claimant's presence must distinguish monetary admissions from admissions concerning fines. An admission that exempts from a fine may require the true claimant's presence, while a monetary self admission may operate more broadly. Tosafot, however, appears to connect the two, because if the admission is legally effective regarding principal, it can also count as self incrimination for the fine. The full matter remains difficult, especially from the case of Rabban Gamliel and his slave Tavi. There Rabban Gamliel admitted outside the slave's apparent presence that he had blinded his eye, and the Gemara focuses on whether the admission was before beit din, not on the absence of a claimant. One answer is that a slave's body is effectively already in his master's possession and a complete factual admission concerning him has a special status. The issue requires further study.
