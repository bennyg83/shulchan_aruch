Toward principal. See Baer Heitev. See Responsa VeShav HaKohen, siman 84, regarding two partners who made a complete accounting of their partnership. After the accounting, one partner remained indebted to the other in a specified sum and made a full admission to that effect before witnesses. He later claimed that the accounting had been mistaken, that he in fact owed nothing, and that according to his present calculation the other partner owed him a certain amount. The second partner said that he had forgotten all the accounts, could no longer reconstruct them, and demanded the amount that the first partner had already admitted before witnesses. The responsum concludes that where he admitted before witnesses that he owed his partner that sum, he cannot later say that he erred, even though the other partner does not know whether an error occurred. The second partner must swear only that he does not know of the alleged error. Not only may he exempt himself with this oath of lack of knowledge, because it is comparable to one who does not know whether he ever became liable, but he may even collect from his partner the amount admitted before witnesses; this is not treated as a claim made in uncertainty.
