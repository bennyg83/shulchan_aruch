And he pays on the basis of their testimony. The Mechaber abbreviated and altered the Rambam's formulation. At the beginning of chapter 7 of Hilchot To'en, the Rambam writes that if one made a formal admission and later claimed, "There was never any such transaction," he pays on the witnesses' testimony when they contradict him, because people remember a complete admission. But if he claims, "I intended only not to make myself appear wealthy," he is heard. The Mechaber likewise writes below in seif 14 that one may make that defense even against a complete admission. In the Perishah I demonstrated that although according to the Tur one might initially explain the Rambam as speaking only of an incomplete admission, the Rambam in fact speaks even of a complete admission. Even though the Mechaber has not yet discussed here the defense of not wishing to appear wealthy, he should not have written without qualification, "he pays on their testimony," because beit din itself may assert for him that he intended not to appear wealthy, as the Tur and Mechaber state below at the end of seif 21. One could answer that according to the Rambam beit din does not assert even this defense for him, as the Tur and Mechaber cite in his name at the beginning of seif 21, and therefore the Mechaber writes here simply that he pays, meaning where the defendant himself does not assert that defense. This is strained. More likely, the discussion both before and after this point concerns an admission made in response to a demand, "You have one hundred of mine," and where an admission follows an actual demand the defense that he merely wished not to appear wealthy does not apply, as the Tur and Rama state in seif 21. Although the Mechaber begins here, "If two people said, etc.," without mentioning a demand, he also concludes, "even though the claimant is not with him." The phrase "even though" implies that the principal case is where the claimant is present. He therefore writes without qualification that the defendant pays on their testimony, and merely adds the novelty that even if the claimant is absent, a statement made as a genuine admission rather than casual conversation can still count as a complete admission.
