(Seif 15) “Even if he said, ‘You are my witnesses.’” This is the Raavad’s view as understood by the Tur. The Sema correctly explains that the defendant made the statement and the creditor remained silent. If the creditor says, “You are my witnesses,” and the defendant remains silent, no one says the defendant can later claim that the admission was merely to avoid appearing wealthy. The Beit Yosef surprisingly interprets the Raavad as speaking of the creditor’s declaration, although in siman 32 he himself explains that the Raavad agrees with the Mordechai, who expressly speaks of the defendant. The Tur’s inference from a healthy person is also otherwise unnecessary, since the Gemara already says a healthy person must say “You are my witnesses.” The Tur’s source concerning a dying person appears to have read the Gemara’s conclusion: “A person does not joke at the time of death, and he need not say, ‘You are my witnesses.’” The added phrase teaches that if he does say it, the declaration is effective without further response from the creditor. A dying person needs no declaration to defeat joking, but ordinarily still needs protection against the “not to appear wealthy” explanation; the express declaration supplies it.
