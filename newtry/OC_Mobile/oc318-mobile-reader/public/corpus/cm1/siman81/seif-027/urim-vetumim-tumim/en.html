If he admitted, etc. See Urim for the difficulty I cited in the name of the Shach and the strained explanation I gave there. According to what I established above regarding the Mechaber's view, however, the matter can be explained differently. The Mechaber holds that although a healthy person who made a formal admission can still claim that he admitted merely so as not to appear wealthy, this does not apply where a dying person made such an admission and then died. Therefore, when the Mechaber here says "if he admitted," he means that the statement was made by way of formal admission, which is why he uses the term "admitted" rather than merely "said." The Mechaber also follows the Mordechai's rule that because a dying person does not joke, one likewise does not joke with him. By the same reasoning, if a dying person who makes a formal admission cannot be treated as one who spoke merely so as not to appear wealthy, then one who formally admits an obligation to a dying person should likewise be unable to raise that defense. The law should be the same whether the dying person makes the admission or receives it. This appears to be the Mechaber's intent. Nevertheless, as a matter of practical law it requires study. There is room to distinguish. With respect to joking, people do not normally make sport of someone upon whom the fear of death has fallen, so just as he himself does not joke, others do not joke with him. But the reason we do not advance for the heirs the defense that a dying person admitted merely so as not to appear wealthy is, as I wrote above, that such a defense is wholly uncommon. If another person admits to the dying person and later personally claims that he spoke only so as not to appear wealthy, why should he not be believed? The matter therefore requires study.
