If he admitted on his own before a deathly ill person, etc. The Shach objects that Mordechai says only that he cannot claim he was joking. Just as a deathly ill person does not joke in such matters, others do not joke with him. But a deathly ill person can admit a debt merely so that people will not think his children wealthy. If so, others who admit to him might likewise do so for the same reason. This is a strong objection. One strained answer is that Mechaber follows Rambam above, who holds that in the claimant's presence one cannot in any event say that he admitted merely so that people would not think him wealthy. One might then have thought this is true only before a healthy claimant, who would protest at hearing himself falsely presented as wealthy, but not before one deathly ill, who may not care about such worldly appearances because 'there is no accounting in the grave.' The law teaches that a deathly ill claimant is treated like a healthy one. This remains strained. See Tumim for another, more satisfactory answer: the word 'admitted' here means that he spoke in a formal manner of admission.
