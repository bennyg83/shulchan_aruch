If he admitted of his own accord before a person who was mortally ill, etc. This follows Beit Yosef’s statement above in siman 32, novel ruling 7, that Mordechai in Zeh Borer implies that one who voluntarily admits before a dying person that he owes him a maneh cannot claim that he spoke merely so as not to appear wealthy. In Bedek HaBayit Beit Yosef adds that the same applies where the dying person demanded payment and the defendant admitted, for one does not jest with those nearing death. I examined Mordechai and found the opposite implication. Mordechai says concerning jesting that just as a person does not jest at the time of death, people do not jest with him. He does not mention the defense of avoiding the appearance of wealth. It may therefore be that just as a dying person himself may have made an admission so as not to appear wealthy, another person may use that defense concerning an admission made to him. Beit Yosef’s ruling requires study. This discussion applies according to Rosh and the authorities cited above in seif 14. I have already concluded there, however, in accordance with Rambam, that one who admits before the claimant cannot claim avoidance of appearing wealthy even if both are healthy. If the admission was made outside the claimant’s presence, the defense may apply even where the creditor was mortally ill.
