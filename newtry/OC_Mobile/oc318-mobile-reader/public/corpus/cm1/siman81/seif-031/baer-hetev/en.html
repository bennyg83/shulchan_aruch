Reuven. The Tur quotes the Rosh's responsum: It appears that since Reuven lent to Shimon and Levi as partners, they became mutually responsible and guarantors for one another, and Reuven therefore had the power to demand the entire debt from whichever one he chose. Reuven demanded payment from Shimon, and Reuven swore that Shimon had told him as stated, and they gave Reuven Shimon's share, so Reuven was thereby removed from any claim against Shimon and Levi, and so forth. The Sema asks that this seems to imply that Reuven acquired the right only because he could collect the entire debt from either partner. That fits the Rosh's view, as the Tur writes at the end of siman 77. But according to the Mechaber and Rama there, who hold that one cannot collect the entire debt from one partner while the other has means to pay his share, the Mechaber should have explained here that the ruling applies only where Levi lacks sufficient assets for his share. The Shach writes that these words are astonishing. The Rosh cannot have made this law depend on the partners' mutual guaranty, for it is obvious that Shimon could initially have given Reuven the entire maneh in his possession by saying that it belonged to Reuven, as stated in siman 93, seif 13, where Shimon claims that they owe a debt and he has enough in hand to pay it. All the more so here, where he already gave the money and Reuven says Shimon told him it belonged to him from the profits, and Shimon was believed because the money was in his possession. Rather, the Rosh's statement that they became mutually responsible and guarantors was said only to explain that Reuven could demand the debt from whichever partner he chose. It has no practical relevance to the substantive ruling in that responsum. The present law is therefore correct according to all opinions. Shach.
