The law follows Reuven. The Sema asks that the Rosh's responsum says that because Reuven lent jointly to Shimon and Levi, they became responsible and guarantors for one another and he could collect the entire debt from Shimon. According to Rambam, whose view the Mechaber and Rama follow, the creditor cannot simply choose either co borrower for the whole amount. Why then does the Mechaber cite the Rosh's ruling without qualification? He should have limited it to where Levi lacks assets for his share. The Shach answers that since Levi denies the debt he is treated like one who has no means to pay, and the Baal HaIttur likewise says so. This is correct and accords with siman 77. A difficulty remains concerning profit. Since according to our law Shimon is liable for Levi's portion only as guarantor, an ordinary guarantor does not become liable for future profit without a kinyan, as Beit Yosef writes in Yoreh Deah siman 170 from Mordechai, and as the Shach rules there. According to the Rosh, each co borrower is treated as a borrower for the whole amount, so profit can follow the entire debt. Under our view, however, the secondary liability is only guaranty and should ordinarily cover principal alone. If, however, two people borrow together under a written document, their guaranty can be treated like a guarantor whose undertaking appears before the signatures. Such a written guaranty can bind even where an ordinary later guaranty would require a kinyan. This would explain liability for profit. Yet according to what I wrote in siman 87, seif katan 10, two who sign one loan document are still only oral guarantors for one another, because the document itself records each one's principal half and does not expressly write the mutual guaranty. On that approach the difficulty returns. The answer follows what I wrote in siman 129. A guarantor who joins an iska at the time the capital is advanced can become liable even for the profit, because his guaranty is contemporaneous with the giving of the money and is therefore like guaranty at the time of the transaction. Accordingly, the Mechaber's ruling here is correct and supplies support for my conclusion there.
