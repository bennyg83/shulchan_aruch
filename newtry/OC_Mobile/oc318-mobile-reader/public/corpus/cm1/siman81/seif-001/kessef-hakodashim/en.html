Regarding what I wrote in Choshen Mishpat siman 81, that silence in response to one's mother's statement distributing her garments can be treated as an admission, the reason is only that waiver of the oath connected with a ketubah requires no kinyan, because that oath is merely a rabbinic enactment. Silence can therefore be equivalent to a statement of waiver. But if she distributed more than the amount of her ketubah, then even if he had expressly agreed orally he could retract until a valid kinyan was made. Although the declaration of a deathly ill person is treated as written and delivered, her son's silence is no stronger than his own oral statement. Even regarding an oath, according to what I wrote in Yoreh Deah simanim 228 and 231, even a binding handshake or oath can be nullified when one explains that he was compelled to agree so as not to anger the sick person and disturb her mind. All the more so where he merely spoke generally, and certainly where he merely remained silent. Perhaps the earlier view applies only where she actually gave the garments before him for distribution. In such a case he cannot say that he did not bother to respond, because the property was being actively transferred before him. Nor does the concern of disturbing her mind fully apply, because he could have protested before witnesses outside her presence, placed the goods with a third party, or quietly informed someone that he objected. For this reason, even if she did not immediately transfer the goods, perhaps his silence can still matter because he should have protested at once. Yet perhaps he feared expressing any doubt lest she learn of it and secretly give away the movables so that they could no longer be traced. As to clothing that a husband purchased and designated specifically for his wife, perhaps she has no authority during her lifetime to give it to others, and if she does so the gift is ineffective. The same may be true of nichsei melog that she brought into the marriage. Still, perhaps a widow, whose honor is no longer maintained by a husband, may give away a garment her husband had bought for her if that garment was only an article of honor and she has adequate other clothing. This requires careful consideration.
