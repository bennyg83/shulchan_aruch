If a person admitted on his own, when may he say, 'I was jesting with you'? It contains 32 se'ifim.
If one says to another, 'You have one hundred of mine,' and the other answers, 'Yes,' before witnesses, but the next day, when asked to give it, replies, 'I was jesting with you,' he is believed. He must swear that his statement is true and that he intended to jest. Even if he did not claim, 'I was jesting with you,' but merely said, 'This never occurred,' he is exempt, because people do not remember meaningless words. {Rama: The defense of jesting is available only to an individual who admits, not to a community that admits, because communities do not ordinarily jest. See Rivash 376. If a person tells his wealthy son-in-law, 'Study with your son and I will pay you,' he is exempt. Since the son-in-law is not poor and is already obligated to study with his son, he may say, 'I was jesting with you.' Even if he does not make this defense, we make it for him, although we do not ordinarily advance the defense of jesting for a litigant who did not make it.}
