And he must swear that the matter is as he says, etc. Above at the beginning of siman 32 I wrote that he must also swear that he has nothing belonging to the claimant in his possession. The case concerns an admission made in response to a claim. Even if he swears that when he admitted he intended merely to jest with the claimant, he has still not answered the original claim that was asserted against him, and that original claim plainly remains in issue. Baal HaTerumot, Shaar 42, and the Rambam in chapter 6 of Hilchot To'en explicitly write that he swears he has nothing belonging to the claimant, without mentioning that he must swear that his intention in the admission was to jest. The Rosh, however, cites Rav Saadia as requiring such an oath and writes that this is reasonable, because he should take an oath of hesset just as one was enacted for an outright denial. It appears that both matters are included in one oath: that he holds nothing belonging to the claimant and that his earlier admission was intended as jest. For if he swears only that he presently holds nothing, one can challenge him directly: since he admitted the debt, perhaps he is now swearing falsely. True, the admission itself did not create an obligation and he could retract even if he had not intended to jest, because he did not say, "You are my witnesses," as the Tur and Mechaber state at the beginning of siman 40, where a binding acknowledgment requires either "You are my witnesses," or a written statement, "I owe you one hundred," or an oral statement, "I owe you one hundred by written obligation." Nevertheless, from the fact that he admitted one could infer that he actually owed the money through a prior loan or a fully binding obligation. He must therefore swear that his intention in making the admission was only to jest, because beit din does not assert that defense for him, nor do we believe his bare statement of it without an oath. The Tur and Mechaber did not separately mention an oath that he owes nothing because that is obvious from what they already wrote at the beginning of siman 75 and in many places, that one who denies everything must take an oath of hesset that he holds nothing belonging to the claimant. See the Derishah and Perishah at the beginning of siman 32. The Mordechai, chapter Zeh Borer, writes in the name of Ri that even if the claimant does not expressly demand such an oath, the defendant must nevertheless take it. Darkei Moshe cites this in siman 32:3, and the Beit Yosef likewise cites that text of the Mordechai there, unlike the wording in our editions. It resembles an ordinary oath of hesset that one holds nothing, which the Sages imposed even if the claimant did not say, "Swear to me."

For these are idle words. Meaning, even according to the witnesses it is not known that he actually owes the money merely because of this admission, because he may have intended to jest. The statement is therefore treated as idle speech, meaning words of no operative effect, and a person may forget such words. This differs from the case at the beginning of siman 79, where witnesses testify that he actually borrowed and repaid. Since he unquestionably borrowed, he should not have forgotten that fact and said, "There was never any such transaction." He therefore becomes established there as a false denier.
