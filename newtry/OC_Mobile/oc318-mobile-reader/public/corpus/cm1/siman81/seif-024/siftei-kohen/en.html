And he said, “You are a witness, and you are a witness.”
He cannot say to Levi, “I was jesting with you.” Levi never demanded payment from him, so he cannot say, “Just as you jested with me, I jested with you.” The Tur states this from Baal HaTerumot. Careful examination of Baal HaTerumot, gate 42, part 5, shows that this is not Baal HaTerumot’s own ruling but a responsum of earlier authorities that he quotes without adopting, as I explained above in seif 8, seif katan 19. Baal HaTerumot himself holds that the defense of jesting may apply even to a volunteered admission. The Sema’s statement that Baal HaTerumot concludes that the defense of avoiding the appearance of wealth is also unavailable, even without “You are my witnesses,” because the admission followed Reuven’s demand, is likewise merely the position of that early responsum. Baal HaTerumot himself holds that where one was sued and admitted, the defense of avoiding the appearance of wealth is inapplicable, as I cited above in seif 16, seif katan 41. The present ruling also requires study even according to the authorities who hold that avoidance of appearing wealthy cannot be claimed after a demand. That rule seems limited to a demand made by the creditor himself. Even according to Rambam’s position, which I accepted above in seif 14, seif katan 33, that one cannot raise this defense after admitting before the claimant, the claimant himself must have been present. A person who truly wished not to appear wealthy would not admit before one who knows that the supposed debt is false, for that admission would itself make him appear wealthy. But when he admits to a third party, the opposite is plausible: the third party asks, “Do you owe Levi a maneh?” and the person, wishing not to be thought wealthy, answers yes. To bring the ruling closer to reason, it should be understood as a case in which Levi was present when Reuven asked the question. Under the view of Rosh and the other authorities cited above in seif 14, that even an admission before the claimant permits the defense of avoiding the appearance of wealth unless the claimant himself demanded payment, Reuven’s demand in Levi’s presence is treated like Levi’s demand. According to Rambam, however, once the admission was made before Levi, the defense is unavailable without any need for this additional reasoning.
