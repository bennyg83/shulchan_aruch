Even if Reuven did not say so. Tosafot in Bava Kamma appears to disagree here as well. In the case where a thief admitted to a custodian rather than to the owner, Tosafot says an admission to one who is not the litigant is not an admission, even though it was before an ordained beit din. All the more an admission before witnesses without "You are my witnesses" should not bind when the owner did not himself demand payment. Therefore the possessor can say, "I follow Tosafot and Mordechai," especially since the Shach himself hesitates regarding this ruling. The Tur is also difficult, because elsewhere his rulings suggest that "You are my witnesses" outside the claimant's presence does not work, in accordance with Tosafot, while here he seems to accept an admission made to another person. This requires study. Rama's later case, where beit din publicly announced that anyone holding orphan property must report it and a person then admitted through an agent, is different. An administrator or custodian can stand in place of the true litigants for such purposes. Tosafot itself recognizes that where the custodian is the effective party for the claim, an admission to him can have legal force.
