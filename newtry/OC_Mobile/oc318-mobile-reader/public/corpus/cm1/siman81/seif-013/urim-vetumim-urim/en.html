It is not an admission. The Shach holds that even if the defendant does not himself claim, 'I was joking,' we make that claim for him, because his earlier answer had been negative. See Tumim.
If he remained silent, it is an admission, etc. Beit Yosef in Tur, siman 32, holds that this applies specifically where the other party speaks affirmatively, such as, 'Let the living and the dead be witnesses,' and the defendant remains silent. But if it is phrased as a question, 'Shall the living and the dead be witnesses?' and the other remains silent, there is no admission because he need not bother answering. Hagahot and Shach say Rashi does not imply this distinction. Even in question form, since he had initially answered yes and then remained silent when asked about witnesses, the silence is treated as admission. This is correct.
For he should have anticipated, etc. The same applies where hidden witnesses are concealed in a cemetery and the claimant says, 'Let the living and the dead be witnesses,' and the defendant remains silent. That too is something he should have anticipated, just like the preceding case. Gemara.
