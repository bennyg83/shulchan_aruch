Ketzot HaChoshen writes that voice recognition is effective only in matters of prohibition and not in monetary law, and that witnesses in monetary cases must identify a person specifically by sight rather than by voice. This is incorrect. Regarding identifying marks, Bava Metzia 20 indicates that monetary law can be more lenient than matters of prohibition, as the Gemara says, "Do you compare a prohibition to monetary law?" Thus the name Yosef ben Shimon may serve as an identifying mark in monetary matters even where it would not suffice for a prohibition. If recognition by voice is effective in matters of prohibition, as explained in Chullin and Gittin, then all the more so it should be effective in monetary law. The reason voice recognition is insufficient in capital cases is that capital law requires actual seeing and knowing, whereas monetary law can be established by definite knowledge even without visual observation, as explained in siman 30. See there, and see Sema, seif katan 30. There is a textual error there. It should read, "and he wrote, such as in his presence, according to Rambam," and the word rendered as "that they wrote" is erroneous. There is likewise a textual error in Sema, seif katan 34. It should read, "even so, it is not called an admission," and so forth.
