To retract. The Shach discusses this law at length and challenges it with proofs. See there. He concludes: The result is that one who was sued by his fellow in beit din and admitted the claim cannot retract under any circumstances, except within the immediate time normally allowed for retracting a statement. If he admitted on his own initiative, he may retract and say, 'I erred in my admission,' but he cannot claim, 'I was jesting with you.' If he was sued concerning one matter and admitted a different matter, such as where he was sued for wheat and admitted barley, or the like, he may say that he was jesting. See below, siman 88, seif 12, for further discussion. Thus concludes the Shach.
