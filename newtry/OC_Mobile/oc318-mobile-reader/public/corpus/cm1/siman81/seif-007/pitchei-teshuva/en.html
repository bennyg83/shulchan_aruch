There is an authority who says. See Baer Heitev, which cites the rule that where the creditor holds a document he does not require the debtor’s admission. Responsa Chemdat Shlomo, Yoreh De’ah siman 27, explains that this means the creditor is believed to say that the payment he received was accepted toward profit rather than principal, because the document remains in his possession and the debtor is not believed against it except by virtue of the creditor’s own admission. Where, however, the creditor admits merely that he received an unspecified payment, even in a documented loan the debtor is believed to say that what he gave was intended for principal. Once the creditor admits receipt of money, how can he extract further payment against the debtor’s definite assertion that the payment was on account of principal? See there further.
