The designation, 'You are my witnesses,' is effective only when the claimant first demands one hundred, the defendant answers, 'Yes,' the claimant then says, 'You are my witnesses,' and the defendant remains silent. If the claimant says, 'You have one hundred of mine,' the defendant remains silent, and the claimant then tells the witnesses, 'You are my witnesses,' while the defendant continues to remain silent, his silence has no effect. Silence is regarded as an admission only after he first admitted by saying, 'Yes,' and then remained silent when the claimant said, 'You are my witnesses.' When he was silent from beginning to end, he may say, 'I did not consider it necessary to answer you.' {Rama: If a person arranged with a young scholar, in the presence of a child's father, that the scholar would teach the child, and the father remained silent, his silence is regarded as admission even though he said nothing, because he should have understood the matter and objected. See the beginning of siman 336, and see below, seif 13 and note 3.}
