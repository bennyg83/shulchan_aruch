The rule that it helps... This follows from the statement there: one said to another, "You have a maneh of mine," and the other said, "yes." At first glance this "yes" should not be effective, because without saying "you are my witnesses" an admission is nothing, as stated in Sanhedrin, while once he says "you are my witnesses" even silence suffices.
One who stipulated... As will be explained in seif 13. Meaning, there the law distinguishes between something he should have anticipated and something he could not have anticipated. We learn from there generally that where he should have anticipated it, the stipulation is effective. See Mordechai there.
And although he did not speak. If he expressly spoke the condition, there is no need for "you are my witnesses." That requirement applies to an admission, not to conditions, as Tosafos write in Kiddushin 43a, s.v. "and not." See Baal HaItur.
