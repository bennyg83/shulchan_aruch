[Shach, letter 81] There it concerns a deposit, whereas here it concerns a loan. Note: Urim VeTumim likewise writes that with a loan it is prohibited because of interest. Responsa Shevut Yaakov, part 1, siman 64, strongly attacks the Sema, arguing that it is obvious that with a loan it is prohibited because of interest, and that the Rama’s words in siman 292, whose source is the Mordechai, expressly state that there is no issue of interest because the money was not given to him as a loan, which proves that with a loan it would be prohibited. In my humble opinion, however, the Sema’s words appear correct. In truth, the case in siman 292 where a loan is prohibited because of interest was not structured in a permitted manner. Here, however, the case necessarily concerns circumstances in which taking the return is permitted, for the only reason Reuven acquires no right is that the condition was not stated at the time of the loan but only afterward, making it mere words without a kinyan. It is therefore clear that the discussion concerns a case without an interest prohibition, where the issue is only that it is a verbal undertaking without a kinyan. The Sema therefore properly asks that the obligation should arise by law because the other party used his money, as in siman 292, and here, since it is structured permissibly, the case is comparable to a deposit in siman 292. Examine this carefully.
