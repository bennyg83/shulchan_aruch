To answer you. See the responsum of Maharshach, volume 3, siman 98. The rulings of Maharam Recanati cite Riaz as follows: Wherever the silence can be attributed to the fact that the person was thinking what to answer, we do not say that silence is an admission. Everything is judged according to what beit din can understand to have been the intent of the silent person. Hagahot Ashri in the chapter HaMafkid writes similarly at greater length. See there, and the responsum of Maharam Padua, siman 40, and the responsa of Maharit, simanim 106 and 114. Shach.
