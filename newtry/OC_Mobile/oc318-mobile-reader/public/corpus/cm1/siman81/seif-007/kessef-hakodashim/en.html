Seif 32. (And he may retract.) I wrote elsewhere that a person's handwritten undertaking, without a kinyan sudar, does not suffice after a loan has already been made to obligate himself under an iska arrangement, because the undertaking is conditional. Someone suggested that the act of writing itself should be effective. Although I wrote elsewhere that, through waiver, which requires no kinyan, the lender can remove ordinary liability for loss from half of the fund, that is only where the concern of mere conciliatory words does not apply. Maharshal writes in Yam Shel Shlomo, Bava Kamma, as alluded to by the Shach at the end of siman 81, that liability does not fall away without an act, and there he explains this in terms of mere words said to placate the other party. Therefore, where that concern does not exist, such as where both parties establish a definite condition, the condition may be effective. A written instrument may remove the matter from the category of mere conciliatory words. On the other hand, perhaps he wrote only to placate the other party, because no profit obligation can attach to him merely through a document without a kinyan sudar where the obligation remains conditional. Since the document does not state that it was written after the loan, however, it may itself disclose that the condition was not merely conciliatory. Still, according to the Sema at the end of siman 81, if the time for payment has arrived or the lender has demanded payment, there can be liability for profit. If neither has occurred, then even if waiver transforms one half into a deposit, there is still no present profit obligation. This requires careful study.
