The Rivash explains that where beit din announced that anyone with information should come forward, the announcement is treated as a form of demand, so the defense “I admitted only not to appear wealthy” does not apply. Yet for the defense of joking, a general announcement is not a personal demand. If the person had been specifically demanded before witnesses, joking could be claimed. This is the Rama’s meaning when he says the demand was not made by the litigant himself. In the later case where Reuven tells Shimon, “You owe Levi one hundred,” and Shimon admits, Levi had not sent Reuven as an agent. Reuven spoke on his own. If Levi had sent him, Shimon could say, “Just as you mocked me through your agent, I mocked you through your agent.” Thus neither the Sema’s explanation, which makes everything turn on the claimant personally speaking, nor the Levush’s explanation, which makes a court announcement inherently too serious for joking, is accurate. The decisive distinction is between an unsolicited general announcement and a personal demand conveyed by the claimant or his agent.
