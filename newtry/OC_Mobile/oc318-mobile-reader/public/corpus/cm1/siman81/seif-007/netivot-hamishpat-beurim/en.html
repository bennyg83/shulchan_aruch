One who stipulated with a young scholar that he should study with his fellow's son. See Tumim, who questions this because silence ordinarily does not constitute admission. In my view, where one person acts upon another's property or affairs based on a statement made in the other's presence and the other remains silent, that silence does signify consent. For example, if one says to his fellow, "I am taking your object for this price, and I am now pulling it in order to acquire it for that price," and he performs meshichah in the owner's presence while the owner remains silent, the silence is universally treated as consent and admission. Here too, the case is where the young scholar actually began studying with the other person's son with that person's knowledge. His silence therefore constituted consent according to all opinions. See also Shach, seif katan 20. There is a scribal error in the passage beginning "and also from the words of the Rosh." It should read that where he said, "You are my witnesses," even though they were not before him, nevertheless he cannot later deny the matter.
