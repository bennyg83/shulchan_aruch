For he should have considered this, etc. This ruling too requires study, as I wrote above in seif katan 9. The ruling originates in the Mordechai, which holds that silence from beginning to end is effective as an admission. According to the law we follow, however, the defendant must first say "yes." Even in the case below concerning the living and the dead, the defendant initially said "yes," and therefore his later silence is treated as an admission because he should have considered the matter. But if he remained silent from beginning to end, even there he could still raise the joking defense. Here too the defendant remained silent from beginning to end, because the cases are compared. Accordingly, the Shulchan Aruch's ruling requires study, for it follows the Mordechai's view, which does not appear to accord with the practical law.
