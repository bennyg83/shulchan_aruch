I did not consider it necessary to answer you. See Responsa Maharshach, Book III, siman 98. Piskei Maharam of Rothenburg, siman 427, states in the name of Riaz: “Silence is like an admission. Nevertheless, whenever one can attribute the silence to the fact that the person was thinking what to answer, we do not treat the silence as an admission. Everything depends on what beit din can discern concerning the silent person’s intention, and they rule accordingly.” The Hagahot Ashri in HaMafkid cites Or Zarua at greater length to the same effect; see there. Support for this appears in Bava Metzia 37b, where one opinion holds that the other remained silent, and the Gemara says: “Silence here is not an admission, for he can say, ‘The reason I remained silent before each of them was that I thought perhaps this one is the true claimant.’” See there. See also Responsa Maharam Padua, siman 40, concerning when silence is treated as an admission, and Responsa Maharit, simanim 106 and 114, page 146.
