If a person conducts business with another's money and gives him profit each year, but ultimately claims there was no profit and wishes to count what he gave as repayment of principal, then if he gave him the profit before witnesses, or admits that when he gave it he expressly said it was profit, he cannot now count it toward the principal, and the investor collects the entire principal even though he did not say, 'You are my witnesses.' {Rama: If he can prove that he erred, however, his claim is valid.} But if he gave the money without specifying its purpose, he swears that there was no profit and counts what he gave toward the principal. Some say that even if he gave the profit before witnesses, unless he said, 'You are my witnesses,' he may now swear that there was no profit, and everything he gave is credited toward the principal, even if he gave it at the time as profit.
