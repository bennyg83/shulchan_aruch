If he admits to him. The Shach wrote that, if so, it is evident that this refers to an oral loan, for if it were a loan by deed, the lender would be believed to say that he gave it to him as profits. Even the Raavad, whose view is the "some say" cited by the Mechaber, disagrees only regarding an oral loan, not a loan by deed. It appears that the case is where the profits exceed half of the principal, for otherwise, what difference would it make that this is a loan by deed? He has a migo that he could have said that an unavoidable accident occurred to the half that is a deposit, and he would be believed by that migo. What difference is there between an oral loan and a loan by deed? Therefore the words of the Shach are obscure and require study, for it is difficult to say that the profits exceed half the principal.
If he did not say on his own, etc. One should not ask that this is a case without a claim, and if so how can he plead, "I was jesting," since according to the Mechaber that plea is ineffective without a claim. I have already written several times that where the lender does not assert with certainty, "You owe me," but comes against him only by virtue of his admission, the other can answer, "You do not owe me anything; you became obligated only through your own words," as above in siman 40. In such a case, according to all opinions, he may plead, "I was jesting," etc. One may also say that where there is a reason for doing so, this plea is available even without a prior claim, and here there is such a reason, namely that the creditor should not press him for payment, as in the following seif.
