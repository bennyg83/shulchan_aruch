One who admits, etc. The Shach's view is that if the defendant did not owe anything previously and now seeks to create a new obligation through his admission, then even according to the Rambam he must say, "You are my witnesses." A statement merely phrased as a formal admission is not strong enough to create a debt the way that formula does. I have already written in seif 7 in several places that the formula, "You are my witnesses," is required only to eliminate the defenses of joking and of admitting merely so as not to appear wealthy. Without those defenses, the oral statement itself creates the obligation. For this reason I wrote that the claimant can require the defendant to swear whether he intended to joke, even after the defendant has already sworn that he did not previously owe anything. If so, all the more certainly where the statement was a formal admission and the claimant had demanded payment, so that neither the joking defense nor the defense of not wishing to appear wealthy remains, the defendant becomes obligated through the admission even though there was no prior debt. This is also proven by the Rashbam in Bava Batra 149a concerning the question of a dying person who admitted. Rashbam explains the question as whether even concerning a deposit the person can claim that he admitted merely so as not to appear wealthy. Necessarily the case is one where he did not say, "You are my witnesses," because with that formula even an ordinary loan would become binding. The Gemara resolves the question from the incident involving the admission of Isur the convert. Necessarily, the Gemara knew that Isur did not say, "You are my witnesses," because otherwise nothing could be proven from him. Yet how did Rav Mari acquire the money, when everyone knew that it had never previously belonged to him? Necessarily, because with respect to a deposit the defense of not wishing to appear wealthy did not apply, Rav Mari acquired through the admission even without the formula, "You are my witnesses." The same therefore applies according to our law whenever the admission is phrased formally and there is no concern of joking, as explained above. One can create a new obligation even if no debt existed previously, just as in the case of Isur the convert. This is clear.
