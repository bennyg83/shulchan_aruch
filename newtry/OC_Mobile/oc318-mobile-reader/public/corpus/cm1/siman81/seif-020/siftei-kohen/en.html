On the assumption that he owes him. If the matter were not understood as an existing debt, then even if it were later clarified that he did not owe him, he would nevertheless have to pay where he made a complete admission according to Rambam, or said, “You are my witnesses,” according to the Rosh. We would say that through a complete gift he obligated himself by means of the admission or by delivering the note, as explained at the beginning of siman 40. So writes the Sema. It has already been explained in siman 40, however, that many authorities disagree and hold that one cannot create a new obligation merely by saying, “You are my witnesses.” Even according to Rambam, who holds that he can create such an obligation, it may be that this is true only when he expressly says, “You are my witnesses.” I do not know the basis for the Sema’s equation of a complete admission with that declaration for this purpose. Rambam’s rule that a complete admission is equivalent to “You are my witnesses” may mean only that the debtor cannot claim jesting, but not that he can thereby obligate himself in something he does not owe. Perhaps only the express words “You are my witnesses” suffice for that, as I have written. This is especially so according to what I wrote above in seif 1, seif katan 19, in the name of Maggid Mishneh and Lechem Mishneh, that regarding the claim of not wishing to appear wealthy, even Rambam does not treat a complete admission as equivalent to “You are my witnesses.” I proved there that Maggid Mishneh’s explanation is correct. We thus see that in some respects the express declaration is specifically required. See Maharam of Lublin, responsum 135.
