Presumed to owe. The Sema writes that if the person was not already presumed to owe, then under Rambam even if it later became clear no debt existed, a complete admission could itself create the obligation. The Shach questions this and says perhaps even Rambam would require the formal wording, "You are my witnesses," for a new obligation. In my view the Shach is correct. A complete factual admission can remove the defenses of joking and self impoverishment. But creation of an entirely new obligation requires the specific formal act recognized in siman 40, namely "You are my witnesses," or equivalent language such as, "I owe you a maneh in a document," according to Rambam. The Mechaber's own formulation in siman 40 proves this: he says, "How does one obligate himself? He says to witnesses, Be witnesses for me that I owe so and so a maneh." If a mere complete volunteered admission before the claimant were enough, there would be no need for this formula. The same conclusion follows regarding Issur the convert. Even though the self impoverishment defense did not apply in the same way, acquisition through admission should ordinarily require the formal legal act of admission when the statement is used not as evidence of truth but as a mode of transfer.
