No. The Shach writes that the consensus of the authorities is that beit din does not automatically supply even the defense that an admission was made merely so that others would not think him wealthy. This appears correct, because the Gemara says that if the defendant does not raise the defense, beit din does not raise it for him, and there is no basis to distinguish that defense from a claim of jest. The Shach nevertheless limits this. If the plaintiff specifically says, "You admitted before witnesses that you owe me," then the defendant must raise the appropriate defense. But if the plaintiff merely says, "You owe me one maneh," the defendant replies, "I owe you nothing," beit din asks whether the plaintiff has witnesses, and the witnesses merely testify that they once heard the defendant say he owed the plaintiff, the testimony alone does not establish liability unless the plaintiff himself relies upon that admission. The defendant need not independently claim jest or concealment of wealth, because beit din does not pursue a claim beyond what the plaintiff himself asserted. See the Shach's further discussion.
