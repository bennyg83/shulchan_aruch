Although testimony by concealed witnesses is not valid testimony, the same procedure applies when a person admits voluntarily while witnesses hear him, or when one says to another before witnesses, 'You have one hundred of mine,' and the other answers, 'Yes.' In all these and similar cases, beit din asks the defendant, 'Why do you not give him what he has in your possession?' If he says, 'He has nothing in my possession,' they tell him, 'But you said such-and-such before these witnesses,' or, 'You admitted it voluntarily.' If he then pays, that is well. If he does not make a defense, we do not make one for him. But if he claims, 'I was jesting with him,' 'This never occurred,' or, 'I intended not to make myself appear wealthy,' he is exempt and takes a heses oath, as explained. Some say that even if he says only, 'I owe you nothing,' the judge exempts him and attributes his admission to the common practice of a person who does not wish to make himself appear wealthy, even though he did not make that defense. {Rama: The defense that he did not wish to appear wealthy applies only when he admitted voluntarily. The defense of jesting applies only when the interested party demanded payment from him.}
