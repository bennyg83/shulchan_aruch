If no one claimed anything from him, but he voluntarily made an admission before witnesses, saying, 'I have one hundred belonging to so-and-so,' and when sued later he said, 'You have nothing in my possession; I admitted only so that I would not make myself appear sated and wealthy,' he is believed. There is no distinction in this respect between a poor person and a wealthy person. This applies whether he was healthy when he admitted or was on his deathbed. If the claimant was present with him when he made the admission before the witnesses, he is not believed to say that he admitted only so as not to make himself appear wealthy. Some say that even if the claimant was present at the admission, he is believed to make this defense. Regarding an oath: if the claimant says, 'Give me the one hundred you owe me; if you wish to deny it, here are the people before whom you said that you owe me,' then even though the defendant makes the defense that he did not wish to appear wealthy, he must take a heses oath. Were the witnesses removed from consideration, he would still have to swear in response to the claim. But if the claimant says, 'Give me the one hundred that you said before so-and-so that you owe me,' and the defendant makes the defense that he did not wish to appear wealthy, he need not take even an oath. {Rama: We advance for him the defense that he did not wish to appear wealthy even if he does not make it himself.
