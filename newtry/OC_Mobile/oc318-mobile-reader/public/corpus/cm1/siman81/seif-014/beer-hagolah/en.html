The Rambam there, cited by the Tur. The Maggid Mishneh writes that the words appear correct but he found no explicit Talmudic source. The Kesef Mishneh writes that it is as though the claimant sued him and he admitted, in which case one cannot say, 'A person may admit merely so as not to appear wealthy,' as Tosafot write in Sanhedrin 29b. This may also be inferred from the Yerushalmi cited above in seif 8, which says he intended to transmit testimony 'to him'; the word 'to him' implies that where the claimant is present, the admission is testimony for purposes of preventing the claim that he meant only not to appear wealthy.
The Tur. So writes the Rosh in his rulings on the incident, from the Mishnah's phrase, 'until they say: he admitted before us,' Sanhedrin 29a, meaning 'You are my witnesses.' This implies that where the claimant did not sue him, even an admission in his presence is ineffective until he says, 'You are my witnesses.' The Mechaber likewise writes in siman 32 in the name of Nimukei Yosef, and so write Tosafot, Mordechai, and Hagahot Maimoniyot, chapter 7 of Hilkhot To'en VeNitan.
