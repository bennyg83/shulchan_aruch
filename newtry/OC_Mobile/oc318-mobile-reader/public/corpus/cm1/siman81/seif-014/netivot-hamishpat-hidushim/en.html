That he was a shechiv mera. The Shach asks that below, in siman 255, it is ruled that regarding a shechiv mera we do not say that he admitted merely so as not to appear wealthy. Tumim distinguishes that when the shechiv mera died, we do not plead on his behalf that he spoke so as not to appear wealthy. If he recovered and himself makes that claim, however, it is a valid claim. If he admitted concerning a specific article that it was a deposit, he can likewise say that he did so merely so as not to appear wealthy. A judge who ruled that he cannot advance this claim committed an error in a matter of law and the judgment is reversed:
He is believed to claim. Even if people told him that he was a major creditor and he responded that he was owed only by certain named persons, he can still say that he did not wish to be regarded as a great creditor or a wealthy person. Sema:
Remove this from consideration. But one who is obligated to swear that he owes nothing must also swear, by means of gilgul shevuah, that the admission was made merely so as not to appear wealthy. Shach:
