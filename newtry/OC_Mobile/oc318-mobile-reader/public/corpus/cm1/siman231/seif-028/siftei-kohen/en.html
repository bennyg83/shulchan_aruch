Members of a trade are permitted, etc. See Responsa Ibn Chaviv, ends of simanim 40 and 99; Responsa Rabbi Shmuel Kohen, part 2, siman 109; Responsa Rabbi Avraham ibn Chaim, simanim 98 and 124; Responsa Maharashdam, simanim 299, 398, 406, 407, and 447; and Imrei Emet, page 97, column 1.

Or the approval of all residents of the city is effective, etc. The Rosh writes this in the chapter HaShutafin, adding that members of a trade are regarded as the residents of the city concerning matters of that trade. See there. It appears that he had to write this, and the ruling is both correct and compelled. The Gemara challenges Rava from the earlier teaching that residents of a city may establish prices, and answers that this applies only where no distinguished scholar is present. This proves that even the residents of the city may not enact such regulations without him. Moreover, if the residents of the city possessed greater authority than members of a trade, how could the Gemara challenge Rava from the case of city residents? I therefore rule as the Rama writes. The Sema does not write this, based upon Responsa Mahari Alashkar, and the Kesef Mishneh likewise differs, based upon the Rivash. In my opinion, however, the proof stated above is conclusive. I found this among writings from my youth. See Responsa Rabbi Shmuel Kohen associated with part 2, siman 9; Responsa Rabbi Yosef Beit Levi, siman 29; Responsa Rabbi Eliyahu Mizrachi; Responsa Mahari ibn Lev, part 1, pages 144 and 192, and further at the end of that volume; and part 4 of Mahari ibn Lev concerning parts 1 and 2.
