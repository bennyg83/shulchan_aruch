To return in a proper repentance. Meaning, because he cannot restore the money to its owners, since he does not know for whom he measured falsely or how many times he did so. Although he has some avenue of repentance by spending the money for public needs, as Chazal said concerning robbers and lenders who took interest and do not know from whom they stole or received interest, this still is not a complete and proper restitution. Other transgressions, even very severe ones such as forbidden relationships and idolatry, are sins between a person and the Omnipresent and can be rectified through remorse, confession, and appropriate penitential discipline.
Like one who denies the Exodus from Egypt. One who falsifies measures conceals his conduct so other people will not see him. He does not fear Hashem, because he imagines that Divine providence does not observe human actions. Whoever denies Divine providence thereby denies the Exodus from Egypt, where the magnitude of Divine providence was openly demonstrated to us through all the signs and wonders Hashem performed against Egypt.
