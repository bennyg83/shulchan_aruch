Food essential to life. According to the Rambam and Tur, this law divides into three categories. With essential foodstuffs comparable to wine, oil, and fine flour, a merchant may profit up to one sixth and no more. With items having no food use at all, such as costus and frankincense, he may profit as much as he wishes, even many times the principal. With items used in preparing food, such as cumin, spices, and similar products, he may profit up to double the cost and no more. This is unlike the Beit Yosef's explanation. See there. Ibid.
Upon them. Meaning, all these expenses are included in the principal, and he may earn one sixth profit on the whole amount. It appears that only here, where he sells small quantities little by little, he is allowed compensation for his labor. This is unlike ordinary merchants, as stated at the end of siman 227, seif 28, concerning one who trades on trust. Ibid.
By himself. Meaning, since there is no beit din present to restrain them and the other sellers pay no attention and violate the Sages' rule not to profit more than one sixth, even a person who fears Heaven need not observe the restriction by himself. The Tur explains: Once his own produce is sold out, the others will sell at the higher price. We do not actively cause this one person alone to suffer a loss. There is no need to interpret the case as one where the other sellers are non-Jews. See the end of siman 156, where the Tur and Mechaber state a similar rule concerning giving a Torah scholar priority in selling merchandise. Ibid.
