They should not increase it by more than one sixth. We find that the Sages increased a measure by one sixth. The Torah's sela originally contained twenty gerah, and afterward they made it twenty four, which is an addition of one sixth calculated externally. They should not increase more than that because of the loss to sellers who come from other places. Such sellers may not know that the local measures were enlarged and may sell produce using the larger measures for the price of the former smaller measure. Up to one sixth the Sages were not concerned, both because we find that they themselves increased the sela by one sixth and because an increase up to one sixth ordinarily deprives such sellers only of profit, not of principal. Regarding foodstuffs, ordinary commercial profit is up to one sixth, as the Tur and Mechaber write below in seif 20. Although a merchant who merely buys and resells is still called a merchant, most visiting sellers will ordinarily learn that the measures were enlarged, and even where occasionally they do not, the Sages were not concerned so long as their principal is not lost. The text does not similarly discuss reducing measures, perhaps because such a reduction causes no loss to sellers. The purchasers are usually local residents and themselves know of, and participate in, the reduction, so they are not harmed. Therefore measures may be reduced as the community sees fit.
