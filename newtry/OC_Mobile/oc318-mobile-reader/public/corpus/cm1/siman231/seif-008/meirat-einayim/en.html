To measure with the small measure, etc. Meaning, a small measure such as a log and a large measure such as a kav. When measuring with the smaller unit, the purchaser receives a small excess with each log because exact measurement is impossible. If the seller measures with a kav, the purchaser receives only one excess for six logs, which is worse for the purchaser. The reverse is worse for the seller. In the Perishah I wrote that Rashbam explains this way. The Tur's wording, however, does not appear to me to mean this. Rather, "large" and "small" both refer to a se'ah measure or both to a kav measure, with one local standard being one sixth larger than the other, which is permitted as explained below. The rule is that in a place where the custom is to measure with the smaller se'ah, one may not use the larger one, or vice versa, even if he wishes to deduct or add proportionately. See there and examine carefully.
Three level measures in place of, etc. The amount of a heap is one third beyond the nominal capacity of the vessel. This is also why the following rule says that if the price of a level measure is two dinarim, the amount corresponding to a heaped measure is calculated accordingly, because the heap is one third. The prohibition in all these cases is derived in the Gemara from Scripture, teaching that one must avoid anything capable of causing error or deceit. Therefore, in a place where the custom is to heap the measure, one may not level it even if the purchaser suffers no loss because he gives him three level measures for every two heaped measures. We are concerned that an observer will see him measuring level and assume that this town uses level measures, and later purchase through a level measure at the price charged for heaped measure. The reverse can similarly cause error to the seller.
