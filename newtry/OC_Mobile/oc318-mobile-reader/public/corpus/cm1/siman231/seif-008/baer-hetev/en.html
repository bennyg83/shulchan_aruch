With the small measure. Meaning, a log. The large measure is a kav. When he measures with the small measure, the buyer receives a slight excess with each log and exact measurement is impossible. If he measures with a kav, there is only one excess for six log, which is disadvantageous to the buyer. The reverse is disadvantageous to the seller. Rashbam explains it this way. The Tur's wording, however, does not appear so. Rather, 'large' and 'small' mean that both measures are of the same nominal type, such as a seah or kav, but one is larger than the other by one sixth, which may legally be added as explained below. The rule is that in a place accustomed to measure with a smaller seah, one may not use a larger one, or vice versa, even if he intends to deduct or add proportionately. See there carefully. Ibid.
It is prohibited. The Gemara derives the prohibition in all these cases from a verse teaching that one must avoid anything that can lead to mistake and deception. Thus, where the custom is to heap the measure, one may not level it off even if the buyer will suffer no loss because he will receive three measures instead of two. We still fear that an observer will see him measuring level and assume that the local practice is to use level measures, and will then buy a level measure at the price charged for a heaped one. The reverse can similarly cause error to the seller. Ibid.
