Seif 3. It is forbidden to keep a deficient measure. The prohibition against keeping a false measure or weight in one's house may apply only to an implement intended to be used at some time in commercial measurement. If one keeps an approximate container solely to estimate whether merchandise bought by rough measure was expensive or cheap, and it will never be used in a transaction with another person, perhaps the prohibition does not apply. Household utensils used approximately in cooking are likewise not forbidden merely because they happen to contain slightly less or more than a standard measure. Since they are not intended for commercial measurement, there is no concern even as a matter of special piety. The same may perhaps apply to a rough measuring vessel used only to assess one's own purchase, though this requires careful study. In Ki Tetzei the Torah links false measures with 'all who act unjustly,' suggesting that the Torah prohibition is fundamentally tied to wrongdoing. Where the owner openly discloses the inaccuracy and no one is deceived, the Torah-level concern may be significantly reduced. Rabbinically, however, there can still be concern that one may later forget and use the measure improperly, as the Sages say in analogous contexts. Just as a clear identifying seal can cure that concern there, an unmistakable mark distinguishing the measures may suffice here. Elsewhere I discussed the rabbinic expression 'measure with a rope, whether less or more,' which also suggests that where the variance is disclosed and there is no injustice, the core prohibition is absent. I heard similarly from my relative Rabbi Moshe in the name of Rabbi Shmuel, the av beit din of Tarnopol, that one should make a distinguishing mark. Nevertheless, from the Torah's wording 'you shall not have in your house an ephah and an ephah,' it appears preferable not to keep two differing measures in one home even if both are marked and their differences are disclosed. The better practice is for one person to keep one standard measure and lend it to another. This requires further study.
