[Rama] Meaning, all of them. Note: Responsa Magen Giborim, siman 12, cites Responsa Re'em, siman 57, that Rabbeinu Tam, the Rambam, Ribam, and Rabbi Yitzchak bar Kalonymus hold that where there is profit to one party and loss to another, and the matter is not a protective enactment, unanimity is required and a majority does not suffice. According to the Rashba, Ramban, Rosh, Rabbi Eliezer of Worms, and Rabbeinu Yonah, however, the majority can compel the minority. He further cites Responsa Rashdam, Yoreh Deah, siman 82, that it is not considered a majority unless all assembled together, and then the decision of the majority is binding upon the minority. Rabbi Moshe ben Chaviv writes that if at first the majority assembled and agreed to one view while not everyone was present, then even if they later wish to reconvene with everyone, the minority may refuse, since the agreement was initially made without their knowledge. He further writes there, siman 15, that according to Rabbeinu Tam and those who agree with him, only the minority may object, but the majority who agreed cannot later retract and object. See there, and Responsa Pnei Moshe, siman 4.
