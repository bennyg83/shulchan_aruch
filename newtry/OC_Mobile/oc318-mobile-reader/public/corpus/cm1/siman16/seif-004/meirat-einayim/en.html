If the other says that it is not in his possession, etc. Nothing is stated here where he says, "I do not know whether it contains any right for you." But the rule follows automatically. We tell him: if you do not know, you must inspect your documents. If you find a right belonging to him, show it to beit din. If you do not understand the documents, show them to beit din and the judges will understand and clarify the matter. Rambam writes this in Hilchot To'en chapter 5, cited by the Tur at the end of siman 60: where he claims, "I do not know," meaning, "I do not recognize it," we compel him to produce the document. Rambam further writes there that if he claims the document was lost, a general ban is imposed. If the claimant says with certainty that the document establishing his right is in the other party's possession, the holder must take a shevuat heset that it is not in his possession and was actually lost. The rule is not limited to a claim, "it was lost from me." The same applies if he says, "I never had such a document," while the claimant says with certainty that he does. This is why the Mechaber writes here that if the claimant knows with certainty the document is with him, the other swears a shevuat heset that it is not. The Mechaber therefore omitted Rambam's language at the end of siman 60, relying upon the present ruling. With this one sees that Rosh and Rambam need not disagree here, contrary to the Beit Yosef's implication and the apparent reading of Darkei Moshe there.
