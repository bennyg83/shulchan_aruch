The opposing party. The Sema distinguishes this case from siman 28. Here the ban is directed toward anyone who should produce a document or identify the witnesses, and therefore the opposing party is included. In siman 28, where a general ban is imposed upon anyone who knows oral testimony and fails to come testify, relatives and the opposing party are not included in the ban because they are not legally capable of testifying. Bach distinguishes differently. Specifically with oral testimony the ban does not include the opposing party, but where the relevant evidence is an account written in the opposing party's own handwriting, the ban does apply to him.
