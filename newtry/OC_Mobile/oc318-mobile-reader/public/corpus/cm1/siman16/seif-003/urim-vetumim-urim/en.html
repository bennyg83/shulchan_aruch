One who is opposite him knows them must disclose it. Although we rule that a general cherem imposed upon those who know testimony does not apply to relatives, and certainly not to the litigant himself, that concerns an obligation to testify about the event itself. Relatives and litigants are not legally competent witnesses. Here, however, the demand is only that they identify who the witnesses are or where a document proving the matter is located. For that factual disclosure there is no distinction between relatives and unrelated persons, and even the litigant is included. This is the second explanation in Sema, and it is correct. Bach likewise writes that if the litigant says, "Here is the document, but it has already been discharged," or raises a similar defense, he is believed through the migo that he could have concealed the document. Although the cherem may have compelled him to disclose it, that does not destroy his migo. This principle is clear in many places and also appears from Sema, seif katan 12. See Tumim concerning this.
