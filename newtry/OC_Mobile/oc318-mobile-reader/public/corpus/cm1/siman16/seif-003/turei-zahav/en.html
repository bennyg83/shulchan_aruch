(Seif 4.) "One who says to his fellow, a document," etc. The Beit Yosef, at the end of the cited seif, presents Rambam and the Rosh as disputing one another. It appears to me that they are actually in agreement. Rambam speaks only where the holder of the document says, "I do not know whether you have any right in it," or refuses to produce it; in that case he must at least produce it. From this it follows that if he asserts with certainty, "You have no right in it," he must certainly take a heset oath when the other party asserts with certainty that he does have a right, just as where the document was lost. The responsum of the Rosh cited by the Tur here deals precisely with such a case, where one party says definitely that the other has no right in the document and the other does not trust him. The questioner wished to say that the geonic enactment compelling production of the document includes this case as well, so that no oath is imposed because the matter can be clarified, and we do not impose an oath needlessly, as stated by the Tur in siman 75 and the Shulchan Aruch in siman 84, seif 33. The Rosh answered that this ruling was not proper in his eyes, because one should not cancel the heset oath and force disclosure of the document merely because a person does not want to impose an oath upon another. Rather, he must swear heset that the claimant has no right in his document. This is not considered a case that can be clarified so as to cancel the oath. This appears to me clear and correct.
