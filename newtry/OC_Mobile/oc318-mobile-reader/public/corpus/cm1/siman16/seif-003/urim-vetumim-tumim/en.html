The opposing party is obligated to disclose it. See Urim for Sema and Bach's ruling that if a person knows in whose possession the document is found, he must disclose that information. However, suppose he knows where the document is, but he also knows with certainty that the document is void, was waived, or is otherwise legally ineffective. If he tells beit din where it is and beit din then sees the document, he will not be believed to explain its defect because he has no migo, since the document is in another person's possession. Rosh's reasoning indicates that the ban does not obligate him to disclose it in such a case. Rosh explains that we cannot force a person to feed another forbidden property or enable him to collect money that is not truly his. Here, from the person's own perspective, there is no forbidden withholding. He knows that the document is merely worthless paper. If he discloses it, beit din may extract money improperly, his fellow may consume stolen property, and he himself will suffer the loss. It therefore appears clear that the communal ban does not obligate him to reveal the document in such circumstances.
