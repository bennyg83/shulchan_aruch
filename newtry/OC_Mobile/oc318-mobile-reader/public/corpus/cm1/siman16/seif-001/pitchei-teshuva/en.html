And there is one who says. See Baer Heitev citing the Sema’s rule that whenever the Mechaber writes “there is one who says,” he may be recording a novel ruling even when there is no dissent. Birkei Yosef writes that the Bach, Taz, and Knesset HaGedolah accept this rule in many places. There is no distinction between the formulations “there is one who says” and “and there is one who says,” contrary to Sefer Re’ach Sadeh, which attempts to distinguish them. That work overlooked the Sema’s statements in siman 26, seif katan 13, and siman 35, seif katan 10, where the Mechaber uses “and there is one who says.” Nor is the rule limited to a truly solitary opinion. It may apply even where two or three poskim hold that view, as is evident from the Sema in siman 26. Nevertheless, it is certainly true that the Mechaber often uses “there is one who says” where there is an actual dispute. The rule is only that where no dissenting opinion is found, his purpose may be to flag a novel ruling. Knesset HaGedolah, siman 420, and Re’ach Sadeh itself say this, contrary to Yad Aharon, Even HaEzer, siman 17. Birkei Yosef discusses the matter at length.
