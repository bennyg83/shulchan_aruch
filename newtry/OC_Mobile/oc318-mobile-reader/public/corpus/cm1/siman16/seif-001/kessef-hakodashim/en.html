Seif 2, gloss. Without delay, see Shach seif katan 6. The Shach writes that the Rama abbreviated the source. The phrase "without delay" does not necessarily waive every period ordinarily granted by beit din. Under the rule that the holder of the document has the weaker hand in ambiguous wording, it may merely mean that the party may not evade appearing before beit din. Perhaps one must distinguish the period allowed to prepare claims from the period allowed later to obtain funds for payment, as in siman 100. If the document merely says "without evasion," one can argue that it addresses appearance and not both types of judicial extension. On the other hand, an added expression may indicate a broader waiver. "Without any beit din time" may expressly waive both periods. "Without any delay" may likewise be broad enough, especially if coupled with "without evasion" and "without beit din time." The exact force of each formula depends on ordinary documentary usage. The Urim VeTumim explains the Shach through the opinion in siman 100 that even a written due date does not waive the later time allowed for payment. The Sema there, however, indicates that the limitation concerns only the ordinary due date; an explicit clause waiving judicial delay can be effective. The Urim VeTumim's argument that the debtor may later find a receipt is relevant primarily to defenses against the debt itself, as in siman 16, not to a period needed merely to obtain money for payment. If a receipt would nullify the instrument, no clause can turn a nonexistent debt into a valid one, just as ordinary credibility clauses do not defeat a genuine claim of forgery. But a condition can still waive the procedural time for preparing payment. There is no necessary rule that because one part of the condition fails the whole condition fails. The valid portion remains operative. Since Ritva writes that wording such as "without evasion and without beit din time" can operate regarding claims, either a receipt-type defense is outside his case, or the clause can waive ordinary delaying claims while not defeating proof that the instrument itself was discharged. Thus the Shach's observation that the Rama abbreviated the rule most likely concerns the existence of two distinct periods granted by beit din. The matter requires further study.
