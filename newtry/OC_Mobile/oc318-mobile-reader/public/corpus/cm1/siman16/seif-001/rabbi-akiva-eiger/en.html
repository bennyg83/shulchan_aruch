[Shulchan Aruch] When he was initially sued without evidence and the beit din obligated him. Note: This applies specifically where the beit din has already ruled that an oath must be taken. Before that point, even if both parties have already stated their claims, and the defendant demands that the law be ruled for him while the plaintiff says, "I have evidence," and so forth, time is not granted to the defendant. The statement in seif 1 that "after thirty days the judgment is rendered" refers to other claims that do not concern an oath, such as laws of wills and inheritance. In those matters, even after a ruling by the beit din, time is granted to the plaintiff just as it is to the defendant. Bach.
