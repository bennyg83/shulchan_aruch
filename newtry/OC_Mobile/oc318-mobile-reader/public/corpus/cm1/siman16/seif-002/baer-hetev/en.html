They give. This refers to the claimant. The rule that he is given thirty days, but no more, applies specifically in the stated circumstances. With respect to the defendant, there is no such distinction. Sema.
His oath. The Sema writes that although he may bring witnesses or proof even after the oath and overturn the judgment, as stated in seif 1, he may fear that his proof will not be completely conclusive. Nevertheless, once he brings it, perhaps the defendant will admit the claim, or beit din will impose a stricter oath upon him, or reverse the oath to the other party. This would not help once the defendant has already sworn. The Bach and Taz offer other explanations. See there.
Available. The same applies to one who claims that his documents were lost because of persecution or calamity, and that because of distance he cannot presently bring proof. Sema in the name of the Rivash.
Who says. The Sema writes that whenever the Mechaber found a law that is not also mentioned by other poskim, even when no one disputes it, he often introduced it with the phrase "there is one who says." This usage is evident in many places in the Mechaber's writings.
To answer. See the beginning of siman 20 concerning whether a litigant may return and advance a different claim after he was granted time to present his arguments but failed to do so. Likewise, if a time was fixed and he then claims that he transferred his rights to another, if that person cannot be notified the court rules in his absence. See also the end of siman 18. Sema.
Delay. The Shach writes that the Ritva's responsum cited by the Beit Yosef reads: if the document states that he must pay at the fixed time, without postponement and without demanding any additional court-granted time, the stipulation is valid. Rama abbreviated the matter. See below, siman 129, seif 10.
