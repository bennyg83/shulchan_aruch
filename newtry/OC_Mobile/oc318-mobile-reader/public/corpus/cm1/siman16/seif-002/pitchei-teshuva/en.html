A document that is in your possession. The Sema writes that this means where the court says this to another person who is not the claimant. End of quote. These words were copied in Be'er HaGolah, Mesoret, Ateret Tzvi, and Ma'amar Kaddishin. See Responsa Shevut Yaakov, siman 143, who questions this, since it implies that when the person requesting production is the claimant himself, the holder is not obligated to produce it, whereas the Bach and Knesset HaGedolah explain that with respect to the claimant himself there is even greater reason to require production. He initially considered emending one letter in the Sema, so that instead of 'this means' it should read 'even when,' on the assumption that the printer omitted a letter. The reading would then be: even when he says this to another person, and certainly when he says it to the claimant himself. He later writes, however, that the text is correct without emendation, because the Sema is not commenting on the first clause, which states that he is obligated to produce the document. This is why he writes 'etc.' Rather, he refers to the final clause, where the holder says that he has no document containing any right for this person, in which case we do not compel him. On this the Sema explains that it refers to another person who is not a claimant. With respect to the claimant himself, however, we certainly compel him in every case. This is also clear from the Derishah, and the Sema concludes in this manner. See the Perishah and Derishah there. This will also be cited below in siman 72, seif 17, seif katan 22.
