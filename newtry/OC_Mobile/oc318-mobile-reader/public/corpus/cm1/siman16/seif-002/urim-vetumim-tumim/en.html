And I do not wish to accept his oath, etc. Although whenever a litigant later brings proof he prevails even if the other party has already sworn, perhaps the evidence he expects will not be completely decisive. Even so, as long as the oath has not yet been taken, the other party may admit the claim, compromise, or otherwise resolve it. This is Sema and Bach's explanation. One can sharpen the point further. Perhaps only one witness will appear. The defendant may fear that after swearing, the witness together with the litigant will disqualify him from taking an oath and from testimony, as Tosafot explicitly write in Bava Batra in the case of the silver bar of R. Abba. See also below, at the end of siman 34. For that reason he may refrain from swearing now. Once he has already sworn, however, even if one witness later appears and a second oath becomes necessary, the fear of disqualification no longer adds anything. His first oath itself can already serve as the basis for disqualifying him if it was false. In truth, however, none of this is necessary. Below, siman 87, seif 20, the Gemara applies the verse, "Turn away, please, from the tents of these wicked men," even to the claimant who imposes the oath. Sema explains that the claimant should not cause another person to swear falsely. The claimant can therefore say: "I know he will swear falsely. If I force the oath upon him, I too will be called wicked. I would rather waive the oath for now and wait until I bring proof. Then he will be compelled to pay without an oath, I will not be included among the wicked, and I will still recover my money." The matter is therefore straightforward.
