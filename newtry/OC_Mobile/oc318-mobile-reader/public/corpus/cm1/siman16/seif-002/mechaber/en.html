The thirty-day period is granted specifically where the claimant initially sued without evidence, the beit din required the defendant to take an oath, and the claimant then said, 'I have evidence, but it is not presently available to me, and I do not wish to accept his oath.' He is then granted thirty days. If, however, he initially said, 'I have evidence, but it is not presently available to me,' there is no need to grant a period: when he brings the evidence, the defendant will respond, and until he brings it, the defendant need not answer the claim. If the claimant later says, 'I have no evidence,' the defendant answers the unsupported claim. Some say that the defendant then takes an oath and is exempt, and the claimant is no longer believed if he later produces evidence. {Rama: If the defendant requests time to respond to the claimant's allegation and the beit din sees that this is merely a delaying tactic, he is not granted time and must respond immediately. If the beit din sees that he needs time to consider and attend carefully to the matters between them, it grants him as much time as needed (Beit Yosef in the name of Responsa of the Rashba). If a document states that he must pay without delay, every monetary condition is valid (Beit Yosef in the name of the Ritva).}
