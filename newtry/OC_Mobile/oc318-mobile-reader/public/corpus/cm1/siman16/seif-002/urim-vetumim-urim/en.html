Some say that he swears and is discharged. Tur cites this from Rabbeinu Yeshayah, and no authority expressly disagrees. It closely parallels the Mishnah in *Zeh Borer*: once a litigant states that he has no further evidence, he may be barred from introducing later evidence. Beit Yosef questions the application here. At first the claimant genuinely said he had evidence but that it was not presently available. Only after the court ruled that the defendant need not respond until the claimant produces it did the claimant say, "I have no evidence; let him answer me without it." Perhaps he did not mean to concede that no evidence existed, but only: "What difference does it make whether I presently have evidence? He should have to answer anyway." Bach therefore says that if he expressly says, "I have no evidence; let him answer me without evidence," his words can still be read as Beit Yosef suggests. But if he simply says, "I have no evidence," and the court therefore requires the defendant to answer, that statement is treated as a firm abandonment of further proof and later evidence is excluded. The textual refinement is somewhat difficult, but Bach's substantive distinction is persuasive and requires study.
Without delay. This means that the document fixes a payment date and expressly says that payment is due then without any postponement. When the date arrives, the debtor must pay and cannot demand the ordinary additional time sometimes granted by beit din. Ritva's responsum implies this. Later, in siman 100, there is a dispute whether a debtor who already fixed a due date is nevertheless entitled to the standard court-granted period for payment. According to the views that generally allow such time, the phrase "without delay" must be understood as an express condition excluding later requests such as, "Give me time to investigate a receipt or other defense." Rama therefore properly states the rule in general terms rather than reproducing Ritva's formulation absolutely, because the matter depends on the later dispute. Shach says Rama abbreviated the law; in my view that was appropriate for this reason.
