The Sema writes that Nimukei Yosef states: where a defendant asks the holder of the document being used against him to provide a copy, all agree that the holder may say, "You wish to devise false circumventions against my document in order to cause me a loss," and this is a substantial argument. It appears to me that the Mechaber holds that a claim of forgery is different, because the defendant alleges a defect within the document itself. The Beit Yosef and Rama explicitly distinguish this way in siman 60 and infer it from the Rosh. In my view, however, the Rosh's language supports the opposite inference. He writes that the Gemara objected specifically to recording the defendant's claims next to the claimant's claims, because when his opponent's arguments are constantly available to him and he sees them at all times, he searches for devices to defeat his opponent's truthful words. This concern does not apply to making a copy of the document. The Rosh does not limit this to a claim that the document is forged. It follows that whenever the defendant requests a copy in order to search the document for a valid defense, a copy is given to him. See there. The Shach, however, writes that the Bach agrees with the Mechaber that absent a claim of forgery no copy is provided. See Responsa Mahari ben Chaviv, siman 97.
