It contains a forgery, etc. Sema holds that this wording is not exclusive. Even if the litigant does not expressly allege forgery, he may be entitled to a copy. See Tumim, where I conclude that wherever there is a genuine possibility that the document itself is forged, a copy is given even if he cannot yet articulate the specific defect, because until he sees the copy he cannot know how to formulate the challenge. But where there is no defect apparent in the text of the document and he wants a copy merely to argue that the obligation is void for some separate legal reason, no copy is provided. See there.
