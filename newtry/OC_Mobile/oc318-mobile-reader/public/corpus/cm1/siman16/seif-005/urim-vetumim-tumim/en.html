That it contains a forgery, etc. Sema, seif katan 16, explains at length that Rosh disagrees with Rashba and holds that the rule is not limited to a claim of forgery. According to Rosh, whenever the litigant asserts that he has other substantive arguments concerning the document, he should receive a copy. Even if Sema were correct, there would be little difficulty. Mechaber could simply be deciding between Rashba and Rosh: where the litigant claims forgery, Rosh's view is accepted; for other claims, Rashba's view is accepted. But in truth, the phrase "contains a forgery" is not meant so narrowly. The litigant says: "Give me a copy of the document. Perhaps I will discover a forgery, an erasure, scraping, or some similar defect in the document itself." In that situation Rosh says he should receive a copy. But if he admits that the document itself is entirely valid and contains no defect at all, and seeks a copy only so that he can formulate external legal claims such as a condition, invalidity of the condition, asmachta, duress, or similar arguments, that falls under the separate rule that we do not supply a litigant with his opponent's arguments merely so that he can search for deceptive legal stratagems. What difference is there between giving him another person's arguments and giving him the document solely to help him manufacture such arguments? In both cases his objective is to devise a claim against his opponent rather than inspect a possible defect in the instrument itself. Rosh agrees in that circumstance. Shulchan Aruch therefore writes correctly and precisely.
