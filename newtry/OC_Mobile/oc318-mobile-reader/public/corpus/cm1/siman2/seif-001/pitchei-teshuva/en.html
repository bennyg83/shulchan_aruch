When the people are lawless. See Baer Heitev in the name of the Sema, who writes that this does not necessarily mean the entire populace, but can apply even to a single individual. Urim VeTumim questions this: if only one individual is lawless, what emergency need is there, and why should he be dealt with contrary to ordinary Torah law? He explains that the Sema must mean that if the offender is left unpunished, frivolous people will learn from him and become lawless. Responsa Shaar Ephraim, siman 72, likewise writes that the principle of “making a fence for the matter” applies only where there is concern for corruption of the public because the generation is lawless. Responsa Shevut Yaakov, part I, siman 145, however, writes that in his Mishpetei Yaakov he concluded that the primary rule is that even where an individual is habitually involved in a transgression, or commits publicly a transgression that is common, this too is considered making a fence for the matter, and beit din is authorized to fine him. This is also evident from Responsa Maharam Lublin, siman 138. See also what I write below, siman 52, seif 2, seif katan 1.
