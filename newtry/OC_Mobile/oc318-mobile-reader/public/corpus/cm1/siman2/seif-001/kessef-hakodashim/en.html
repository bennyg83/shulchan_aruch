Where the people are lawless. The Sema, seif katan 3, follows Rav that because of the breach of an individual, punishment may be imposed for the sake of the generation. See Urim VeTumim there. It is clear that this applies only when the individual sinned publicly among those who are lawless, even if he did not himself act in a manner of habitual lawlessness and is not as corrupt as they are. Their wording there likewise implies this. If he acted unwittingly and did not know that a breach would result, because he did not know that he was among lawless people, or if he erred in thinking the act permitted, it is obvious that he should not be punished. Rather, the intentional lawbreakers are punished. Regarding an individual who is himself habitually lawless, Urim VeTumim writes there that he is punished beyond the strict Torah law only where his punishment will serve as a corrective for the generation with respect to the future. But where he is established as an informer, it is clear from the law that punishment may be imposed even without concern that others will learn from him. See there.
