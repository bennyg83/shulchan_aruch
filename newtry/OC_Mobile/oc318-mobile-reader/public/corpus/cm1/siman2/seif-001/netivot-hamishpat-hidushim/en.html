When the people are lawless. This does not mean specifically that the entire populace is lawless. Even a single offender may be fined beyond the strict law as the authorities see fit. When the populace is lawless, however, the authorities may enact safeguards for the entire community and punish one who violates their enactments even if that individual is not otherwise lawless, Sema:
Full testimony. The same applies without formal warning and similar procedural requirements, since this is an emergency measure and they are not judging according to ordinary Torah law, Sema:
They strike him. Here, because they act as an emergency measure to create a protective fence, they may execute the ruling through non Jewish authorities, and there is no need for the wording, "Do what the Jewish beit din tells you," as is required regarding a get. There, the get itself must remain valid. Here, by contrast, they are deliberately acting beyond the ordinary law in order to create a protective fence, Sema:
To confiscate his property. The same applies to other measures, such as placing him under ban, imprisoning him, administering lashes, or publicly disgracing one whose reputation for misconduct is established, provided everything is done for the sake of Heaven. This applies to an ordinary person. With respect to a Torah scholar, however, "cover him like the night," and do not place him under ban publicly, Sema:
