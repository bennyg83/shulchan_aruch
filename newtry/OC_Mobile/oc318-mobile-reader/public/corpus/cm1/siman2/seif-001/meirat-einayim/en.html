Every beit din. Even a beit din outside Eretz Yisrael.
Even if they are not ordained in Eretz Yisrael. See what I wrote at the end of the previous siman.
When the people are lawless, etc. In the Perishah I explained that even if the entire population is not lawless but only one individual is, the judges may punish and fine that individual beyond strict law according to what they see fit. When the people generally are lawless, they may enact protective regulations for the entire community and punish anyone who violates them even if the individual had not previously been known as lawless. This is done to fence the community.
They may adjudicate even death, etc. Meaning, even where strict Torah law would not impose death. Rambam and the Tur write this explicitly. See the Perishah and Derishah regarding Nimukei Yosef.
Even without complete testimony. The same applies without prior warning, because witnesses and warning are required for Torah-law punishment, whereas one who violates communal measures may be punished according to the needs of the hour. Responsa Ramban, siman 279, writes this.
Even without complete testimony. Beit Yosef cites Rashba that in capital matters the judges must be warned to act with the consent of the city's elders and only after great necessity and deliberate consideration.
They beat him through non-Jews. Since the Mechaber does not conclude, 'and they say to him, do what Israel tells you,' as the Tur does, it appears he holds that even the final enforcement may be carried out through non-Jews when the person is violent and defiant, especially when the court is imposing an extraordinary fine rather than an ordinary judgment. See the Perishah.
They have authority to declare property ownerless, etc. The source concludes that they may fine a violent person, place him under ban or excommunication, curse him, beat him, pull his hair, imprison him, and administer an oath that he has not done and will not do the prohibited acts. In all this the judge's intent must be for the sake of Heaven and human dignity must not be light in his eyes. It further says they may flog and disgrace one whose reputation is bad. Beit Yosef and Darkei Moshe cite the Ran that this applies to an ordinary person, but a Torah scholar who sinned should be covered as by night and not publicly excommunicated. This comes from Menachot, where Reish Lakish derives from the verse that a Torah scholar who sins should not be publicly shamed.
Whom beit din appointed over them. The Tur says, 'whom the public appointed over themselves.' According to the Mechaber's wording, the meaning is that the public appointed them as beit din over themselves.
And so. This is the practice. Mordechai cites Rabbeinu Gershom that anyone appointed over the community is like a mighty one among the mighty, and Yiftach in his generation is like Shmuel in his generation; what he does is effective. Rashba likewise writes that each community in its place has power analogous to the Geonim with respect to enactments needed for that community.
Even though there is, etc. Maharik writes that even according to the view that the good men of the city may enact what they wish, this is only for communal needs. They plainly cannot transfer one person's money to another where there is no communal need. Piskei Mahari likewise implies this. The principle that beit din may declare property ownerless in the fullest sense applies to a preeminent authority without peer in his generation.
Or if they accepted upon themselves, etc. Then even a matter that was not previously customary becomes binding merely through their agreement.
And it causes a loss to this one, etc. Darkei Moshe cites Mordechai's conclusion that where it is not a matter of fencing a breach, their enactment is not obeyed. This implies that all agree that for the purpose of correcting a breach, the good men of the city possess authority. Rama therefore may mean that the dissent concerns only the statement that this is the ordinary practice even without an immediate communal need. There is also a textual issue in Mordechai as to whether it says 'not a matter of fencing a breach' or a slightly different formulation. The point requires study.
All the more so if they accepted them, etc. Mordechai in Bava Batra writes that townspeople may not establish measures where there is an expert judge or important person in the city without his consent. The Tur and Mechaber write similarly below at the end of siman 231. Some limit this requirement to penal enactments. Darkei Moshe cites the discussion.
He shall give forty gold coins. Maharam of Merseburg writes that one fined one hundred by the court receives forty lashes; for two hundred, eighty, forty for each hundred. Darkei Moshe cites this as a dispute with Mahari Weil, who says forty gold coins are in place of lashes. The Shulchan Aruch cites only Mahari Weil. See Hagahot Darkei Moshe and siman 420, seif 41.
But in the hands of beit din. Hagahot Maimoniyot, chapter 7 of Hilchot Sanhedrin, tells of one who sat down to receive lashes, but the officer appointed to administer them did not come, and the person was exempted. See there for additional laws of lashes.
