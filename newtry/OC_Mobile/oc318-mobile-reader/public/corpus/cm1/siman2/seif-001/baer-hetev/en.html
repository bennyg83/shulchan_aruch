That the people. The Sema writes that this does not specifically require the entire population to be lawless. Even a single individual who acts lawlessly may be punished and fined beyond the strict law. When lawlessness is widespread, however, the authorities may punish anyone who violates their enactment even if that particular person was not previously observed acting lawlessly, in order to erect a safeguard for the community.
They strike. This implies that even the final enforcement of the judgment may be carried out through non-Jews when the offender is violent, and certainly where the matter is a fine rather than ordinary adjudication. Sema. Meaning, even if the judgment has not yet been finalized before a Jewish court, its enforcement may be completed through non-Jews.
Whom they appointed. Meaning that the community appointed them as its beit din. Sema.
The greatest. The Mordechai quotes Rabbeinu Gershom: anyone appointed over the community is regarded as the mightiest of the mighty, and "Yiftach in his generation" applies to him; whatever he validly does is effective. The Rashba likewise writes that every community in its locale has authority comparable to the Geonim, who enacted many ordinances for all Israel. Thus the Sema.
All of them. Meaning that in such a case even a verbal undertaking is binding, including in a matter for which no prior custom existed. Sema.
For this. The Sema writes that Rama's position requires study. Is the dissenting view that such authority is ineffective even as an emergency measure to safeguard the community, or only that it is ineffective where there is no temporary need, while they agree that in an emergency the rules may be altered? This distinction depends on the text of the Mordechai. See there.
As they see fit. The Sema writes in the name of Maharik that even according to the opinion that the communal leaders may enact whatever they wish, this applies only to communal needs. They plainly may not transfer property from one private person to another where there is no communal need. Nor do we say that beit din may declare property ownerless except with respect to a uniquely preeminent authority in his generation. He further cites Hagahot Maimoniyot concerning a person who sat on the ground to receive lashes, but the appointed official failed to arrive to administer them, and the court released him. See the proof brought there. See below, siman 420. Sema.
