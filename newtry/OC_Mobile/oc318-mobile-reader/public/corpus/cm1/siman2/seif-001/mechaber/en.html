A Beit Din May Administer Punishment as a Temporary Measure. One seif. Every beit din, even one whose judges do not possess semichah in Eretz Yisrael, may, when it sees that the people are rampant in transgression and the needs of the time require it, adjudicate capital matters, monetary matters, and every form of punishment, even without legally complete testimony. If the offender is violent, they may have him beaten by non-Jews. {Rama: They also have the power to declare his property ownerless and destroy it as they deem necessary to repair the breach of the generation (Tur in the name of Rambam, chapter 24 of Sanhedrin).} All their deeds must be for the sake of Heaven. This authority belongs specifically to the leading sage of the generation or to the city leaders whom the beit din has appointed over the people. {Rama: This is the practice everywhere: the leaders of a city have the authority of the Great Beit Din in their city; they may administer lashes and punishments, and their declaration of property as ownerless is effective, in accordance with local custom. Some disagree and maintain that the city leaders have such power only to compel the community regarding an established custom or a measure that everyone willingly accepted. According to that view, they may not change a matter that benefits one person and harms another, or confiscate money without everyone's consent (Mordechai, chapter HaGozel Batra). Nevertheless, local custom is followed, and all the more so where the community accepted them for every matter; this appears correct to me. See Yoreh De'ah, siman 228, regarding communal enactments and bans. The later authorities wrote in their responsa that one liable to lashes should give forty gold coins in place of the lashes (Mahariv, siman 147, and Maharam of Risburg). They did not state this as fixed law; they ruled that way as a temporary measure. The beit din may instead administer lashes or take money as it deems appropriate to the circumstances in order to safeguard the matter. See the gloss at the beginning of siman 425 and seif 5 above.}
