And I am sick with a disease. Fij is re-signed:
The choice is in his hand. According to the wording of the Gemara, Rambam, Rosh, and Mechaber, which does not say "a particular palm tree," the meaning is straightforward. One brother can force the other either to give the designated beneficiary one of the palm trees and receive money from the brother for his share, or to pay money himself and let the other brother contribute his share. According to the Tur's version, where the deceased said, "Give a particular palm tree to so and so," the same wording is difficult. I explained in the Perishah that the choice still remains because ordinarily a person prefers a palm tree or land to cash. If one brother is willing to give the specific palm tree from his share and the other wants instead to undo the division merely because he dislikes the lot he drew, he cannot do so. One might ask from siman 175, where two brothers divided and afterward an unknown third brother appeared, and even if the third brother agrees to take only one half field from each without a new lottery, either brother can void the original division because it was mistaken. The cases differ. There the third brother had an equal legal right in every field from the outset. Here the brothers knew from the beginning that they had to satisfy this devise and nevertheless divided without first setting it aside. They cannot later characterize that omission as a mistake if one of them is willing to satisfy the obligation. Another distinction is that the devisee has a right only in the designated palm tree, not in all the other palms. Even according to the common text, where no particular palm is named, the heirs may give him the inferior one because the recipient has the lower hand. Thus once one brother offers the palm that can properly satisfy the devise, the other cannot undo the entire division.
