One should not object from siman 175, seif 3, concerning two brothers who divided the estate and afterward a third brother, whose existence they had not known, appeared, where either brother may annul the division because it was made in error. Here all the brothers knew that they were obligated to give this person a palm tree or field, and their failure to do so is not regarded as an erroneous division; it is as though from the outset they agreed to satisfy him with whatever could be given. Alternatively, there the newly arrived brother had an equal share with them in the division and had a right to every field just as they did, so only there do we say that the original division was mistaken. Here the deceased said, 'Give such-and-such palm tree to so-and-so,' or, according to the version where he merely said 'a palm tree,' the heirs may give him the inferior one because the recipient has the inferior hand, as stated at the beginning of siman 241 and the end of siman 214. It is therefore as though he had explicitly been given this inferior palm tree. Consequently, if one brother agrees to satisfy him from his own share, the other brother cannot annul the division. According to this, the rule in siman 175, seif 4, where a creditor of their father appears, applies only if the brother whose land was taken does not agree to receive money from the others. If he agrees, the other brothers cannot annul the division. According to the first explanation, however, no such distinction exists. These are the words of the Sema. The Bnei Chayei writes that nevertheless the other brothers can object and say: Since by law the division is void, let us divide again, for perhaps your present share will fall to me by lot.
