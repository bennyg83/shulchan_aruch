Land in another city. Tumim asks that, if so, in Bava Batra 44 concerning one who sold property without responsibility and therefore cannot testify about it, when the Gemara asks that if the seller owns other land the creditor should return to that land, and Rashbam explains that one does not collect from encumbered property when unencumbered property exists, the Gemara could have answered that the seller owns land in another city. If the creditor cannot find property in the present city, he must go elsewhere; but if the seller's testimony is accepted and a field becomes available here for collection, the creditor could not be pushed off to another city. In my view this can be resolved. It is explained here that where the creditor will suffer no loss, the rule that one does not collect from encumbered property while unencumbered property exists applies even when the unencumbered property is elsewhere. Thus, when the creditor seeks to collect from a purchaser who bought without responsibility, while the debtor has land in another city, the purchaser can tell beit din: why should he collect from me when he has unencumbered property? If the reason is merely to spare the creditor the expense of traveling, then sell me the land in the other city at its value there. Certainly, if a purchaser could be found here who would buy that land at the assessed value it has in its location, the unencumbered land would be treated as effectively present here. Accordingly, when this purchaser himself is willing to buy the distant field, it is likewise as though unencumbered property is available locally. The purchaser will certainly do so, because once the creditor collects from him he has no recourse against the seller, having purchased without responsibility. Indeed, an even stronger principle is stated below in siman 116 seif 2: a purchaser without responsibility may tell the creditor, before the creditor collects from him, to sell him the debt instrument through writing and delivery, whereupon the purchaser can himself collect from the debtor if the debtor has property. Only once the creditor has actually collected from him can the purchaser no longer return against the seller. If he buys the debt before collection, however, he becomes the seller's creditor just like any other assignee of the debt. That sale depends on the creditor's consent, and if the creditor refuses to sell the debt and instead collects, the purchaser who bought without responsibility has no recourse. But in the case described here the purchaser certainly can say that he will buy the land in the other city that beit din is obligated to sell, and then the seller has no personal interest in whether his testimony is accepted. Only in the remote circumstance where the expenses of dealing with the distant property exceed the value of the field held by the purchaser would the purchaser refuse to do so.
