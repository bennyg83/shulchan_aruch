And the seller stipulated with him. See Sema seif katan 1 through the words that the creditor will take them. The Tur and Beit Yosef in siman 99 give another reason: what benefit does the seller have from making such a condition? It is only because the purchaser induced him to do so, and therefore it is considered an evasion designed to defeat creditors. There are practical differences between the two reasons. If the land increased in value, under the first reason the creditor has no right to the increase above the purchase price, whereas under the second reason he does. Likewise, if there are additional creditors whose claims are later than this creditor's, then with respect to the field the earlier creditor has priority. But if the sale is void because of the condition, and he is merely collecting money still in the seller's hands, there is no priority among creditors with respect to money and they divide it. Under the second reason, however, the sale remains valid and the creditor collects from the entire field, with his right of priority intact. The plain language of the Tur and Shulchan Aruch indicates the second reason, and Tumim also rules this way.
