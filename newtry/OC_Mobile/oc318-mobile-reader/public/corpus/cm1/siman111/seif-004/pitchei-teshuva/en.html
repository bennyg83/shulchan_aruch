Rather, they divide. See Baer Heitev and Responsa Shevut Yaakov, volume 2, siman 162. Three merchants each entrusted merchandise to the same wagoner to take to market. The merchandise was lost through an unavoidable accident on the way. Their values differed: one had merchandise worth three hundred, another two hundred, and the third one hundred. All three sued the wagoner before the secular court, and a settlement was reached requiring him to pay one aggregate sum to all three. The question was how to divide that payment. Shevut Yaakov initially considers equal division by analogy to siman 104, seif 10. He rejects the analogy, however, on the basis of Tosafot to Ketubot 93a and the Sema's distinction in siman 111, seif katan 18, concerning a gift made to several people. Here the settlement was reached jointly according to an estimation of the amount of each merchant's loss. The payment should therefore be allocated according to the relative value of their losses. In addition, the general rule is that one who claims through a non-Jew is subject to the non-Jew's law, and under the governing law even creditors share according to the amount of their claims. In such a matter the law of the kingdom is certainly binding.
