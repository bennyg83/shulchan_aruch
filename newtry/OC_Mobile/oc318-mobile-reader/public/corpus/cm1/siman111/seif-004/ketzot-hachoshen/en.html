Maharash Sasson distinguishes a gift saying, "on condition that my creditor not collect," from a gift saying, "except that my creditor shall not collect." In the first, if the condition fails the property returns to the son through inheritance, and the creditor can then collect. In the second, "except" is a retention. The father has left in himself a residual right preventing that creditor's collection. Tumim objects that when the father dies the retained right should itself pass to the son, and the creditor should then be able to reach it. In my view the objection is not decisive. Maharik rules that heirs do not inherit an intangible right or mere benefit that lacks monetary substance, such as ordinary discretionary benefit in choosing to whom to give terumah. The father may have retained only a nonmonetary power to block this particular creditor. Such a power does not automatically become inheritable property. The Shach, however, holds that where the benefit is already physically in the heirs' hands they can sometimes acquire it. If so, once the father's retained right ends at death, perhaps the son acquires the free value directly, not by inheritance but like ownerless property. Yet he may have no desire to acquire it if doing so would expose it to the creditor. Moreover, under the view I cited in siman 276 from the Shelah, a mere benefit right is not something another can acquire at all, even when physically connected to property. On that approach the son does not acquire the father's retained blocking power, and Maharash Sasson's distinction stands.
