Another. The Sema writes that the Tur concludes this rule by saying that the first creditor had an after-acquired-property clause. That statement refers to the case where the son later sold the property. In our case, where the son did not sell it, such a clause is unnecessary. For that reason the Mechaber does not state here that such a clause is required. The Bach adds a practical distinction where the son himself borrowed after his father's death: without an after-acquired-property clause, the second creditor collects, while with such a clause the property is subjected to the first creditor. The Shach says this reasoning is sound. See Responsa Mahara Sasson, siman 225, and Responsa Rashach, part 3, siman 83.
