And he has no other son. If there is another son, then at the father's death the creditor can collect. Even if that collection causes the condition to fail and voids the gift, the field returns by inheritance, and the creditor can collect from the recipient's inherited share. See Tumim. I explained at length that if the creditor lacks an after-acquired-property clause and the donor did not make a general gift but only 'from today and after death,' the creditor may have no claim. I also established that for practical law there is no difference whether the clause was phrased as 'on condition that' or as an exclusion, even though an exclusion is technically a reservation rather than a condition.
Another son. The same applies if there are many sons. The creditor can at least collect the portion the recipient would have inherited had the gift been void. Sema.
