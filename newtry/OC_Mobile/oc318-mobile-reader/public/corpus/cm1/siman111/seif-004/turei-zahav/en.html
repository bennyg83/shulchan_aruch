(Seif 19, that he must say to him each time, “I will return it to you afterward.”) This is somewhat difficult in light of siman 42, seif 9, concerning a document dated “after Pesach,” where the Rosh writes that it refers to the first coming Pesach, for otherwise the document would be void. Here too, let us say that the first possible time is intended, since otherwise the document would remain ineffective forever, and for what practical purpose was it written? If his intention was that the recipient retain it forever, he should simply have given it as an unconditional gift. One may answer that there can still be a practical consequence here: in any event, when the recipient dies, the gift returns to the giver, because after the recipient’s death it is impossible for him to return it, and the gift is therefore void. This is unlike the case of a date stated merely as “after Pesach,” where the borrower’s death does not resolve the defect.
