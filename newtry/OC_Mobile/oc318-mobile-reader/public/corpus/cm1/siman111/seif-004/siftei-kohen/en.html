And he has no other son. See the Sema, seif katan 8, through his statement that in our case, since the son did not sell, an after-acquired-property clause is unnecessary. The Bach writes that the practical difference arises where the son also borrowed after his father's death. Without an after-acquired-property clause, the second creditor collects; with such a clause, the inherited property was encumbered to the first creditor. The Bach accordingly reads the Tur as saying, 'The father died, and afterward the son borrowed from the second creditor.' His words are well reasoned. See the responsum of Maharan Sasson, siman 225, and the responsa of Rabbi Shmuel Kohen, part 3, siman 83.
