And he has no other son, etc. The Tur explains that when Reuven, the donor, dies, the right he had retained in the land devolves upon this son because there is no other son. When the creditor later seeks to collect and the original gift is undone by its condition, the land nevertheless returns to the son because he inherited the father's retained right. It is then like a borrower who later acquired property. The Tur mentions "d'akni," but in my view that phrase refers only to the adjacent general rule that after-acquired property can be bound where such a clause was written. In this particular case, because the son did not later sell the land, a d'akni clause is unnecessary. This is why the Mechaber does not require it here.
Another son, etc., until "he collects from him." Where there is no other son, the creditor can collect the entire field. If there were other sons, he could collect only the inheritance share that devolves upon the son who is his debtor, because when the gift reverts to the father each son inherits his proper share. We do not say that because the father revealed an intention that the creditor not collect, he must have intended the other sons to inherit everything. If that were so, even where there were no other sons we would say he intended more remote relatives to inherit instead of permitting the creditor to collect.
