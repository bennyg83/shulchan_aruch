[Shulchan Aruch] And the borrower has no additional property. Note: The discussion in Kesubos 94 makes clear that according to Tosafos, the Raah, and Ritva, even where there is sufficient property for both creditors, the earlier creditor must swear because the second parcel may later be discovered to have been stolen. The later creditor, however, need not swear out of concern that the later parcel might prove stolen and he would then return against the first. See there. Penei Yehoshua explains that for this reason the Mechaber carefully wrote, "and the borrower has no additional property," because in such a case beit din itself administers the oath, whereas concern for hidden payment bundles requires the borrower to demand, "Swear to me." See there.
