(Mechaber, seif 4) One who gave land to his son, etc. Note: One who studies Responsa Rosh, rule 77, will understand that his reasoning is that the father's condition that no lien ever take effect amounts to the father retaining a right in the land. If the son violates the condition, the land reverts to the father. This retained right is inherited by the son upon the father's death. The creditor can therefore claim against the son to the extent of the value of the retained right that he inherited. Since the creditor's lien attaches at least to that retained portion, the condition is thereby violated and void, and the creditor may collect from the entire land. The father's condition was that no lien at all should take effect, but a lien now exists at least on the retained right that fell to the son's inheritance. This also explains why the Rosh specifies that the son wrote an after-acquired-property clause. Without such a clause, the son could have sold or given away the retained right he later inherited from his father, and the creditor would no longer have a basis to invalidate the gift.
