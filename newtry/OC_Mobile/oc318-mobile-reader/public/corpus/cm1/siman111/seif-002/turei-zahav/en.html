(Seif 5, gloss: Some say the same law applies). One should not object that the later clause should then have taught the greater novelty where the gifts were without warranty but equal. It likewise teaches a novelty: even though the last recipient received a larger amount than the first, showing that he was apparently more beloved, the donor nevertheless did not intend that collection begin from him. This also answers the question against the dissenting view, why the first clause did not teach the novelty even where all received equal gifts. Had it done so, the later clause would also have needed to speak of equal gifts, whereas its true novelty applies even where the gifts were unequal. See siman 493, seif 9, concerning this law.
