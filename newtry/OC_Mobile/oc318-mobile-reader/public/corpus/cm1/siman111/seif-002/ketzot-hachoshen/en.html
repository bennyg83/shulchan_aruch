Consecrated property is treated like encumbered property for collection order. As I explain in siman 114, an oral loan can sometimes be collected even from property dedicated for monetary consecration, because such consecration is no stronger than abandonment. Nevertheless, so long as the debtor has free property, one should collect from that property first. This follows even from the law of heirs. Where property passes successively by heir like acquisition, the later acquisition is first exposed. The Tosefta concerning those who take the property of a convert demonstrates that the Sages applied the general collection order even in a setting that is not purchaser like. Therefore, if free property remains, even if it is inferior quality, one should use it before disturbing dedicated property. The Sages did not limit the rule solely to purchasers who gave value. Once the collection hierarchy was established, they applied it generally.
