Therefore, if he gave, etc. The Tur likewise uses the word "therefore." In the Perishah I wrote that it does not connect smoothly with the immediately preceding law. It must have been used because of the latter clause, where the Tur concludes that if he said, "Two hundred to so and so, and after him three hundred to so and so," and a debt is then produced against the estate, collection is made first from the last recipient. The reason is that one does not collect from encumbered property where unencumbered property remains, even if the latter is inferior quality. What remains in the hands of the final recipient is treated as unencumbered property relative to the earlier gifts, because when the earlier recipients received their gifts, the property later given to the final recipient still remained in the donor's hands. In the Perishah I explained that this differs from the rule in siman 104:10 regarding a debtor who owes three creditors, one hundred to one, two hundred to another, and three hundred to the third, while possessing only three hundred, where they divide equally and each takes one hundred. There, each creditor lent money to the debtor, and at the moment of each loan the debtor's property became equally encumbered to the creditor of one hundred as to the creditor of three hundred. In a voluntary gift, however, the proper rule is that the person whom the donor showed an intention to benefit more should receive more. Therefore they divide in proportion to the amounts given.
