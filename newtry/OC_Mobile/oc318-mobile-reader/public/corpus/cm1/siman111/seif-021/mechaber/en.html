If Reuven lent Shimon money orally, Shimon lent Yehudah money under a promissory note, and Yehudah sold all his property, Reuven may seize from the purchasers who bought from Yehudah by virtue of Shimon's note against Yehudah. The law follows Reuven because Shimon admits that he owes him. If a borrower owns no property other than promissory notes against others, then not only may his lender collect from those notes while they remain in the borrower's possession under Rabbi Natan's rule, but even if the borrower dies and the notes pass to his heirs, the lender collects from them. This applies even if the debts represented by the borrower's notes arose only after he borrowed from his own lender, and even if the borrower's debtors acquired property only after the borrower took his loan. The lender collects from those debtors and their heirs under Rabbi Natan's rule. See notes 6 and 40.
