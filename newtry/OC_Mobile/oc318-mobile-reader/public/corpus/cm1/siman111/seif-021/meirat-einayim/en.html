The law is with him, since, etc. The Tur writes that although Shimon could have told Reuven, "I repaid you," the purchasers cannot reject Reuven by claiming that Shimon already paid him, nor is Shimon's admission of his debt disregarded as collusion against them. Ultimately the purchasers must pay Shimon, who is their seller's creditor. Since Shimon can collect from them, Reuven, who is Shimon's creditor, can collect from them under Rabbi Natan's rule once Shimon admits that he owes him. The case assumes that the relevant assets were already in Yehudah's possession when he borrowed from Shimon, or that Yehudah wrote Shimon a d'akni clause. If the assets are movable, they must also have been encumbered to Shimon through agav with land and d'akni. The Mechaber abbreviates because this seif comes only to teach that Reuven can collect even though his own loan to Shimon was oral.
It is obvious while they remain in his possession, for the creditor collects from them under Rabbi Natan's rule, etc. All this concerns documents and assets that remain in the hands of the first borrower's debtors and have not been sold. Therefore the first lender's document need not contain an agav clause for the documents, even though documents are movable property. Since the intermediate lender himself could collect from his debtors while the claims remain in their hands, his own creditor can do the same under Rabbi Natan's rule. Baal HaTerumot states the case with those debtors holding land, but once the conclusion rests on Rabbi Natan there is no distinction between land and movable property.
Even if the documents or obligations arose after he borrowed, etc. All the more so if they existed before the first loan, because then they were already among the borrower's assets subject to the lender. This supports the same formulation above in siman 86.
Even if the debtors of the borrower acquired property, etc. The practical rule is that even where there are two stages of after-acquired property, Reuven can still collect from Yehudah under Rabbi Natan. Baal HaTerumot's wording indicates a case where Shimon had lent Yehudah before Shimon himself borrowed from Reuven, but Yehudah acquired the relevant property only after Shimon borrowed from Reuven. Even though at the time of Reuven's loan the property did not yet exist in Yehudah's hands, Reuven may ultimately reach it through Shimon's claim.
