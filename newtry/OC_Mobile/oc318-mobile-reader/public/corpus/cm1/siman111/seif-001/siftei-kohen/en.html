One who lends to another, etc. See the laws discussed in this siman in the responsum of Rabbi Eliyahu ibn Chaim, siman 100.
But one who lends by written document, etc. The same applies where the borrower admitted before witnesses and instructed them to write a document, as stated by Baal HaTerumot, Gate 61. The opinion cited there that the witnesses must actually have seen the loan, because otherwise perhaps the borrower admitted merely to prejudice others, does not appear correct to me. It is not implied by the Gemara or authorities in many places. Baal HaTerumot himself states the contrary at the beginning of Gate 48 in the name of a responsum of the Ramban. At the end of that discussion he warns that the view requiring the witnesses to have seen the delivery of money was written without sufficient analysis and that Maharam's formulation is precise. The Beit Yosef cites this briefly at the end of siman 39. The Beit Yosef also cites Baal HaTerumot concerning land given to a married woman by another person. Although the husband consumes the produce, his creditor cannot collect from the land or acquire the produce, because the produce is needed for the household and the land has the status of nichsei melog. Where the husband himself gave her the property, however, Baal HaTerumot implies that the creditor cannot collect only if the husband does not retain the produce. Baal HaTerumot states this explicitly in Gate 6, beginning of part 4: where the husband gave his wife land after marriage and before borrowing, she acquired it, the husband does not consume its produce, and his creditor collects nothing from it. This is contrary to the Sema, seif katan 2. Perhaps the Sema contains a scribal error. Alternatively, Baal HaTerumot may mean that when the husband gave her the land but retained its produce, the creditor collects from the produce, while the Sema means only that the creditor does not collect the land itself.
