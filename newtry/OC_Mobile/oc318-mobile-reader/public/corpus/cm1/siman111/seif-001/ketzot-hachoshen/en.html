Beit Yosef cites Baal HaTerumot that a deathbed gift recipient is treated like an heir, so even an oral creditor can collect from the gifted property, and even movables can be reached under the applicable rules. Raavad challenges this from the case, "after him to so and so," which is discussed under the rule that one does not collect from encumbered property where free property remains. The early authorities answer that the case can involve a partial deathbed gift made with a kinyan, which has the law of a gift by a healthy person. Ran asks more broadly how deathbed gifts can be called encumbered property at all. If they were like purchasers, oral loans within term and maintenance of a wife and daughters should not collect from them, contrary to the law that a deathbed gift is weaker than inheritance. He too establishes the case as a partial deathbed gift with kinyan. In my view the difficulty is unnecessary. Even among heirs there can be an order of collection where property passed successively. The Tosefta says that when several Jews seize the property of a deceased convert, the last acquirer is first exposed to creditors, and only if his property is insufficient do we proceed backward. Yet a person who takes the property of a convert is treated like an heir, and oral loans can collect from him. Thus the rabbinic rule of exhausting later property before earlier property can apply even within inheritance type acquisitions. Accordingly, a deathbed recipient can be heir like for oral debts and yet still be treated as later property for purposes of collection order. The Tosefta and the rulings in siman 275 make this clear.
