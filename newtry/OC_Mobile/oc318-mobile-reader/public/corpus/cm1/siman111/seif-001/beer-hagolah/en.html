Maggid Mishneh writes in chapter 18 of Hilkhot Malveh that this is what is stated in Mi SheMet, Bava Batra 157a: collection is from the debtor himself even from the cloak on his shoulders. If he liened property that is not his wife's, while that property is already liened for her ketubah, the creditor's law is analogous to a sale: whatever interest in the property the husband could sell permanently or until she becomes widowed is likewise what a creditor can collect. Beit Yosef cites Baal HaTerumot. He further writes there that if another person, or the husband himself, gave the wife land before the husband borrowed, even though the husband consumes the produce, the creditor cannot collect from it. See below siman 99, seif 25, and Tur Even HaEzer siman 90 for similar laws.

At the end of the first chapter of Bava Metzia, 15b, the halachah is established that omission of a warranty clause is a scribal error, because a person does not throw away his money for nothing.
Tosafot likewise infer this in that chapter, and it is straightforward because every monetary condition is valid.
