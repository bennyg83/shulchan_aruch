The Sema, seif katan 2, cites the Baal HaTerumot that if another person gave land to a woman as a gift, or the husband gave land to his wife as a gift in a case where the husband consumes the produce, the husband's creditor has no right to that produce, because it is designated for the support and prosperity of the household. The Shach asks: either way there is a difficulty. We rule that when a husband gives land to his wife, he does not consume its produce. If the case is that he expressly stipulated at the time of the gift that he would consume the produce, the Baal HaTerumot implies that this is considered a reservation by the husband, and in truth his creditor may collect from it. The Shach is correct that if the husband reserved the produce for himself, his creditor may collect from it. It appears that the Sema meant a gift without any reservation at all. Although we rule that the husband himself does not consume the produce, Tosafot in Perek Chazakat HaBatim write that land is purchased with that produce, and the husband then consumes the produce of the newly purchased land. One might therefore have thought that the husband's creditor could collect from those secondary fruits. The Baal HaTerumot teaches that he cannot, because the primary produce is itself nichsei melog, and the produce of its produce is designated for the support of the household. The creditor therefore has no right to it.
