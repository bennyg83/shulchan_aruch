But he cannot collect from purchasers, etc. Although we rule that the lien of a loan is Torah law even for an oral loan, Chazal enacted, to protect purchasers, that an oral loan cannot be collected from them because it has no publicity. The same applies even to recipients of gifts. Although they did not pay money to the borrower, if he had not received some benefit or satisfaction from them he would not have given the gift, so the gift is treated like a sale for this purpose.
Whether land or movable property, etc. If a husband encumbers property without his wife's consent and the property is already subject to her ketubah, the rule parallels a sale: whatever he could validly sell, permanently or until she becomes widowed, can likewise be subjected to a creditor's lien. Beit Yosef cites Baal HaTerumot at length. Property given to the wife by another person, or land given to her by her husband before he borrowed, may be protected even though the husband enjoys the fruits. See siman 99 and Even HaEzer siman 90.
