He has not thereby forfeited his right. This differs from the rule below at the beginning of siman 147 that if Levi was one of the witnesses to the sale, he can no longer challenge the purchaser by claiming that the field belonged to him. Here Levi possesses only a lien upon the property, and he may say, "I assumed I would be paid in cash or from some other property." There, by contrast, he challenges ownership of the very field that was sold and claims it was his. As for the Tur and Mechaber's ruling in the following seif that Levi had a document upon the sold field and may nevertheless seize it, there too the document does not establish that the house itself belonged to him, but merely that it was encumbered to him. Alternatively, there he performed no affirmative act, but was merely present at the sale. Even the fact that Reuven appointed him over the construction can be explained by saying, "I intended to build it according to my wishes and then seize it." By contrast, signing his own name on the sale document is an affirmative act that weakens his claim, as the commentators explain there.
