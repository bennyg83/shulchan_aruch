This is unlike the rule at the beginning of siman 147, where if Levi was one of the witnesses to Reuven's sale to Shimon he can no longer challenge the purchaser by claiming that the field was his. Here Levi has only a lien on the assets, and he may say, 'I assumed that the debtor would pay me in cash or from another property.' There, by contrast, he challenges the very field sold and claims ownership of it. Nor is this contradicted by the following seif, where Levi held a document against the sold field and may nevertheless later seize it. There too, the document did not establish that the house itself belonged to him, only that it was subject to his lien. Alternatively, there he performed no act beyond being present at the sale. Even if Reuven appointed him over construction, he may say, 'I intended to build it according to my wishes and then seize it.' By contrast, where he personally signed the instrument, he performed an act that weakened his own right. Sema.
