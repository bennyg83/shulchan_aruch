Although, etc.; others disagree. Bava Batra 157a considers whether property acquired after the obligation and then inherited becomes encumbered, and concludes that it does. The Gemara rules that the parties divide in the disputed case. A clause encumbering after-acquired property is not presumed from scribal error, as explained in siman 112:1; it must be written expressly. Since the Gemara groups later sale and inheritance together, both would seem to require express language. The first opinion, however, holds that property passing to heirs is certainly encumbered without an express clause. The Gemara’s rejected proof concerns the view that an oral loan cannot be collected from heirs; according to our ruling that it can, the lien unquestionably applies. The Gemara’s uncertainty regarding inheritance was stated according to Shmuel, who denies collection from heirs. This is also the ruling above in seif 1, at the end of siman 104:16, at the beginning of siman 107, and in siman 112:8.
