And he bequeathed, etc. Those who hold that no after-acquired-property clause is needed against the debtor himself apply the same principle to heirs, because an oral loan can be collected from heirs. The dissenters hold that inheritance is even weaker than heirs' movables, which were not originally encumbered at all. See Tumim, where I defended Ramban, who belongs to the dissenting group. Nevertheless Mechaber and Rama later, at the end of siman 112, rule like the first opinion that no after-acquired-property clause is needed against heirs. Regarding a deathbed gift, however, Bach may be correct that such a clause is required.
