And bequeathed. The heir's domain is treated like the borrower's own domain, because a son stands in his father's place. The Tur and Shulchan Aruch state without qualification at the beginning of siman 107, here in seif 21, and at the end of siman 112 that a son stands in his father's place with respect to despair and change of domain, and we do not regard inheritance as transfer to another domain. The mention here of a dissenting view follows Baal HaTerumot's wording. Sema. The Shach writes that if the borrower acquired the property and then bequeathed it, the creditor collects from it, as is also evident from Bava Batra 157a. This is the primary ruling, and the Mechaber's citation of the dissenting view is surprising. See also the Bach, who expresses uncertainty regarding an inter vivos inheritance arrangement.
