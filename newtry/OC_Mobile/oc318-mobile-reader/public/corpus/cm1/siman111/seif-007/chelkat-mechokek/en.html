Against the excess, it does not reduce. It appears that "against the excess" is not precise. The same should apply if the debt exceeds the excess. There is no reason to distinguish. Since we rule that a creditor acquires only from the time of collection onward, the creditor does not weaken the sons' immediate acquisition of the excess. What difference does it make whether the debt is smaller than the excess or much larger? In either case, the sons first acquire, the debt is paid, and the remaining estate is divided according to the relative ketubah rights. I do not know why the following seif records this as merely "some say," because Rambam appears to hold the same. Although he uses the language "against the excess," Maggid Mishneh's discussion of one ketubah becoming the excess for another indicates this. He cites an authority that if the estate is insufficient for both ketubah amounts, etc., and the Mechaber later rules this without qualification in seif 9. See there.
