The law is with Yehudah, since his document is earlier, etc. This law is essentially the same as the rule in seif 22 concerning a borrower who sold a promissory note to another person. There I explained that the note may already have been in the borrower's possession before he borrowed from his own lender, and therefore no clause for subsequently acquired property was mentioned. That cannot be said here, since it is established that Yehudah lent to Reuven before Reuven lent to Shimon. Accordingly, it is not precise to say here merely that Yehudah's document is earlier and that Reuven encumbered movables together with land to him. We also need Reuven to have written Yehudah a clause covering subsequently acquired property, because Reuven borrowed from Yehudah, later acquired the debt from Shimon, and then sold that acquired right to Levi. In such a case a clause for subsequently acquired property is needed. One might have thought that this is why the Tur and Mechaber repeated this law although they already wrote it in seif 22. But if so, the essential point is missing, since the need for such a clause is not mentioned here. Moreover, even if stated explicitly there would be little novelty, since this was already explained in siman 104. In the Tur, the repetition is understandable because there is a novelty here that the market protection enactment does not apply to the sale of promissory notes. Although the law in seif 22 likewise concerns a promissory note, one might have thought that it speaks only according to the fundamental law before the market protection enactment. Here, however, the Rosh wrote in a responsum that there is no market protection for promissory notes. The Mechaber, although he did not state the distinction between documents and other movables in full, follows the Tur and alludes to it by writing, "the market protection enactment does not apply here," and presents the related laws briefly in sequence.
