His. The Sema explains that each clause is read according to its context. Corresponding to the earlier case where Shimon, the first borrower, sold his promissory notes, the present clause means that he sold them after borrowing from Reuven. Corresponding to the case where Yehudah sold his assets, it means that he sold them after borrowing from Shimon. In that situation each lender's lien had already attached before his own borrower sold the property. This is unlike the Levush's explanation. See there.
Real property. It must be said that this case also concerns one in whose possession the promissory notes already were before he borrowed from each of his creditors. Therefore the Mechaber did not write that the first creditor must also have an after-acquired property clause, because once the debtor wrote that movables are secured together with real property, the notes acquire the status of real property. Sema. The Shach writes that this applies specifically where they seek to collect movable property by means of the notes. If, however, the creditor seeks to collect real property, and certainly if the purchaser of the notes has already collected real property, an after-acquired property clause is unnecessary, as I proved at the end of siman 85; see there. He further writes that if the second debtor owns only real property, no clause securing movables together with real property is needed. Since the note will be used to collect real property, the first creditor may remove it from the lien created by Rabbi Nathan's rule. This is evident from Baal HaTerumot. Certainly, if the purchaser of the promissory note has already collected real property, the seller's creditor may seize that real property from him even if his own note contains no clause securing movables together with real property, because once real property has actually been collected it is treated as real property and may be recovered under Rabbi Nathan's rule. This is explicit in the Ran and Ramban, and in Tur and Shulchan Aruch, siman 85, seif 6; see there. It may likewise be proved from the Gemara, Pesachim 31a, and see Tosafot there in the name of Ri. The same applies when the second debtor pledged movables together with real property: the first creditor does not need such a clause in his own document against the purchaser of the promissory note. It is therefore somewhat difficult that the Tur and Shulchan Aruch state the rule here without qualification, for their case must concern a second debtor who has movable property with which to pay and did not pledge it together with real property. They should have stated this, because this rule is a novelty. This is clear. Shach.
