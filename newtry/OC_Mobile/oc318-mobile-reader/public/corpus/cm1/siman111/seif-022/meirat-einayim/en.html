My husband sold their property. Hashem’s promise is that Hashem’s promise is not to be given to us, and if he has not been given to him, he will not be saved from me, and that he will not be saved from him In his hand, I will give him a blessing from his father, and that he is his father's son, and that he is his father's son, and that he is his father's son, and that's what they are like:
After he borrowed from the lender, etc. The Tur and Baal HaTerumot write the same. In the Perishah I explained that the phrase must be understood according to each clause. Regarding the case where Shimon, the first borrower, sold his own promissory notes, it means that he sold them after borrowing from Reuven. Regarding the case where Yehudah sold his property, it means that Yehudah sold it after he had borrowed from his own lender, namely Shimon, so that each lender's lien had attached before his debtor sold the property. It should not be explained as the Ir Shushan explains, that even with respect to the debtors' sale of their property it is necessary that their sale occur after the first borrower borrowed from his lender, on the theory that otherwise the clause transferring movables with land written by Shimon to Reuven would not help, since without a similar clause written to Shimon by Shimon's debtors, Shimon himself could not collect from their purchasers and this would constitute a double indirect transfer. There is no source for such a rule, for the first lender stands in the place of the second lender for all purposes.
And what he writes, "likewise regarding a document held by the borrower, etc." The Tur also writes this. In the Perishah I explained that "likewise regarding a document" means "or regarding a document," and refers back to the phrase, "or if the borrower's debtors sold their property." In that case Yehudah must have encumbered movables together with land to Shimon, his lender, by the promissory note he gave him. But it is not necessary that both promissory notes contain such a clause merely in order for either document itself to be sold.
A lien on movables together with land. We must also say that the promissory notes were already in the borrower's possession before each one borrowed from his own lender. Therefore the text does not require a clause for subsequently acquired property. Once he wrote a clause transferring movables together with land, the notes are treated like land. Since they were already in the borrower's hands before the loan, the lender has priority and may seize them from one who later purchased them from the borrower without any clause for subsequently acquired property.
