["Or his creditors sold their property. Meaning, their debt documents, and for those an acquisition by agav is required, etc. This is the wording of the Sema. That is the principal ruling, unlike what the Bach wrote, for otherwise the Tur should not have stated the matter without qualification."]
["They sold their movable property, etc. This applies according to the law of the Gemara, or nowadays to debt documents, to which the enactment for market protection does not apply."]
["And likewise regarding a document that the borrower possesses, etc. This refers specifically to the case where his creditors sold their property. The Bach also writes this; see there."]
["A lien on movable property together with land, etc. If the second debtor has only land, no lien on movables together with land is required. Since collection under the document will be from land, the first creditor can extract it by virtue of Rabbi Natan's lien. This is evident from Baal HaTerumot at the end of gate 43, cited briefly by the Beit Yosef in seif 17; examine Baal HaTerumot itself. Certainly, if the purchaser of the debt document has already collected land on the debt, the seller's creditor may seize that land from him even though the seller's document did not contain a lien on movables together with land. Since what was collected is now land, it is treated as land, and the creditor extracts it by virtue of Rabbi Natan's lien. This is explicit in the Ran in Shnei Dayanei Gezerot and in the Ramban cited by Baal HaTerumot, gate 34, end of part 2, and likewise in the Tur and Mechaber above, siman 85, seif 6. See there carefully. This can also be proved from the Gemara in Kol Sha'ah, Pesachim 31a, and see Tosafos there in the name of Ri. The same applies if the second debtor encumbered movables together with land. In that case the first creditor does not need a lien on movables together with land as against the purchaser of the debt document, as is evident from the Ran there and from the Tur and Mechaber above, siman 85, seif 6. It is somewhat difficult that the Tur and Mechaber here state the matter without qualification, because the case must involve a second debtor who has movables from which to pay and who did not encumber those movables together with land. They should have explained this. Examine it carefully, for this is a novel and clear ruling."]
["Together with land. The Sema, seif katan 39, writes that the case must also be one where the documents were already in his possession before he borrowed from each of his lenders. That is why it did not say that he must also write an after acquired property clause, for once he wrote agav, the documents acquire the legal status of land. This also appears from Baal HaTerumot at the end of gate 43, which says that he should write an after acquired property clause, meaning where the documents came into existence later. It appears, however, that this is only when they come to collect movables by means of the documents. Where they come to collect land, and certainly where the purchaser already collected land, an after acquired property clause is unnecessary, as I proved above at the end of siman 85; see there."]
