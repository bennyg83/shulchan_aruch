And he wrote to the first creditor an incidental-acquisition clause. He also wrote an after-acquired-property clause, because the later loan had not yet occurred and a creditor acquires only prospectively. Therefore an after-acquired-property clause is clearly needed. The incidental-acquisition clause is additionally needed because the case concerns movables where neither party owns land. Without incidental acquisition the movables are not subject to a property lien and there is no priority. If the relevant debtor owns land, then because Rabbi Natan's lien applies, the first creditor is treated as though his debtor owns land and no incidental-acquisition clause is necessary. See Tumim. I wrote that in such a case even Rashba agrees. His dispute concerns only movables and whether they can be encumbered through incidental acquisition.
