And he wrote to the first creditor a clause of transfer together with land. Although above the lender could collect movable property found in the hands of his debtor's debtors even without such a clause, because Rabbi Natan's rule gives him the rights of his debtor, here there are two lenders to the same borrower. Without a clause transferring the movables with land to the first creditor, so as to give them the status of land, the two creditors would divide the movables. This follows all the more so from movables already in the borrower's possession before he borrowed from either creditor, concerning which the preceding law says they divide. It appears that here the first creditor received the clause of transfer together with land and the second did not. Otherwise the property would be encumbered to the second rather than the first, as with land acquired by the borrower between the two loans, unless the first creditor had both a clause transferring movables with land and a clause for subsequently acquired property and the borrower later acquired movables or made loans to others, after which he borrowed from the second creditor who likewise received both clauses. In that case too the property is encumbered to the first rather than the second. See Baal HaTerumot, Shaar 43, law 4, in the third discussion and also in the sixth discussion referring back to it.
