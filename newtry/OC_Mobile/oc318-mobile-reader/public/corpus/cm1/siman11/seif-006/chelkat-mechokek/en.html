We remove her from the suspected man upon either of these. This implies that an uninterrupted rumor alone, even without the husband having observed suspicious conduct, is enough to remove her from the suspected man, because with respect to him the rumor arose before the marriage, provided it is a fully established rumor as described above. Rabbeinu Tam, before retracting, indeed held that a woman is removed from the suspected man on the basis of rumor and did not require witnesses to indecent conduct. Rambam, however, chapter 2 of Hilchot Sotah, does not appear to hold that an uninterrupted rumor alone, without witnesses to indecent conduct, requires her to leave the suspected man. See also Responsa Rosh cited by Beit Yosef: a rumor that she secluded herself with him while still married does not prohibit her to him; we do not take account of a rumor arising after marriage when she was not prohibited to her husband, and therefore she is likewise not prohibited to the suspected man.
