Who is in the city, etc. Meaning that he is in the town. Rambam and the Mishnah sometimes call a town a "medinah," and the context here itself proves that this is the meaning. Therefore, they do not establish a later date for him. Even if the messenger tells the neighbors, "Tell so-and-so to come to beit din," we do not rely upon them to excommunicate him if he fails to appear, because perhaps the neighbors did not tell him. They may have assumed that since he was in town the messenger met him while walking and told him personally. Therefore the messenger himself must find him. This is the Gemara's statement, "but if he is in the town, perhaps he found him." The Tur also cites it. Rambam's formulation is essentially the present rule. Rama repeats this at the end of the seif: "likewise, if he is in the city, we do not rely..." Apparently Rama adds nothing new there, but states the same reason for the later case: perhaps the neighbors did not tell him because they assumed beit din itself had already done so. This is the only way I can reconcile Rama's repetition, which otherwise appears puzzling.
They excommunicate him in the evening. Rambam uses this wording in chapter 25. Earlier, regarding one who is present in the city, he likewise writes that he is given only one date and, if he fails to come that whole day, is excommunicated in the evening. The Mechaber altered Rambam's wording there and wrote "the next day." In Derishah I explained that Rambam's intent in both places is merely to exclude writing against him during the same daytime period. "In the evening" means at night, which is already the beginning of the following day. Rambam varies his language for an added point in each case. Where the person goes in and out and receives three dates, Rambam writes "the next day," teaching that even after three dates they do not write against him at the end of the third day itself, but only on the next day. Where he receives only one day, Rambam writes "in the evening," teaching that no additional time is necessary: as soon as that day ends, the excommunication may be written. Since there is no practical difference, the Tur consistently uses the Gemara's phrase "the next day." Perhaps the Gemara uses that phrase because only on the following day is his disgrace publicized and people are informed that he is under excommunication. This also explains why the Mechaber earlier wrote "the next day" but here copied Rambam's wording.
All the more so through a beit din messenger, etc. Although this is an a fortiori inference, the Tur and Rama state it so that one not argue: since he is a beit din messenger, he should have returned and reported, "I fulfilled my mission," and since he did not return, perhaps he never found the person and never summoned him. The law teaches otherwise. See the Perishah for more.
Likewise, if he was in the city. See what I wrote immediately above in seif katan 12 explaining Rama's purpose in repeating this rule.
Likewise, if he did not return to the city until the next day, we do not rely, etc. Meaning that we do not assume that the neighbors summoned him for the established day of the judges' sitting, whether that was the same day or some later court day, such that failure to appear on that day would allow excommunication on the following day.
