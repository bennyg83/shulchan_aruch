If a person is in the city and the beit din's officer went but did not find him, no date is fixed until the officer finds him and informs him. If the person was in a village and normally returns that same day, the officer may tell even one of his neighbors, including a woman, 'If so-and-so returns, inform him that the beit din fixed today as the time for him to appear.' If he fails to come, he is placed under a ban that evening. {Rama: All the more so may this be done through the beit din's officer himself, even if the officer did not return and report that he fulfilled his mission (Tur).} This applies when the person's usual route does not pass the beit din. If his route passes the beit din, he is not placed under a ban until the officer himself informs him, for perhaps the neighbors did not tell him, assuming that he passed the entrance to the beit din, went in to them, and was discharged. Likewise, if he does not return to the city until the next day, the neighbors are not relied upon, for perhaps they forgot and did not tell him. {Rama: Similarly, if he was in the city, the neighbors are not relied upon, for they may assume that the beit din's officer found him and informed him (Tur).}
