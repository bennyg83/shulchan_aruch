If, for example, on Friday or the eve of Yom Tov he says, "I accept the authority of beit din and will obey its judgment," the excommunication can be lifted. I have seen this practice. Rashi speaks of a time when beit din is sitting. Then a person who says, "I will come," yet does not immediately come, remains a refuser. Where the court itself is not sitting and the delay is not caused by him, his declaration of obedience removes the status of refusal. Kol Bo records an enactment attributed to Rabbeinu Gershom concerning the old Ashkenazic practice of interrupting communal prayer when beit din fails to provide justice. A complainant may initially interrupt only in the synagogue he normally attends. After interrupting three weekday prayers without result, he may extend the protest to other synagogues. Even in his own synagogue he may not interrupt Shabbat or festival prayer unless he already interrupted three weekday prayers without effect. This applies in a city with a leading authority or established beit din. Sema.
