Until he comes. For as long as he has not appeared, his conduct remains an act of defiance. Rashi explains so. Kol Bo, siman 117, cited by Darkei Moshe, records an enactment attributed to Rabbeinu Gershom: one who summons another and is met with refusal may not interrupt public prayer except in the synagogue where that person himself prays. If he interrupts three consecutive prayer services there without result, he may then interrupt in all synagogues. He may not interrupt Shabbat or festival prayer unless he has already done so three times. In a communal matter, however, they may interrupt. Wherever it appears that a great authority or beit din once existed, this ban is presumed to have been enacted and is enforced. See there for more.
