Responsa Shevut Yaakov, part I, siman 143, writes that the Shach’s position is not compelling, for reason supports the view of the Be’er Sheva. He also found Responsa Be’er Esek, siman 67, citing several authorities to that effect. The Shach’s reason is that the defendant may say, “Perhaps once I know your claim I will satisfy you.” Shevut Yaakov objects that even once the parties appear before beit din the defendant may do so. If he is a distinguished person for whom appearance in court is demeaning, he can appoint an authorized representative, as in siman 124. Common practice likewise is to summon a person to beit din without specifying the claim, and some support can be drawn from Bava Batra 58b. Responsa Zikhron Yosef, Choshen Mishpat, siman 2, in a responsum of Rabbi Shaul of Amsterdam, disputes this. The passage in Bava Batra proves nothing, for beit din certainly sends messengers to say merely that a particular person summons him to court. If the defendant answers that he will not appear until told the nature of the claim, however, he may indeed be heard. Be’er Esek did not see the Shach, and had he seen him he might have retracted. He further argues that even Shevut Yaakov spoke only where plaintiff and defendant are in the same city, so that the plaintiff’s reason applies, namely, that the defendant can settle even after coming to beit din. But where the road is long and the expenses great, possibly exceeding the claim itself, even Shevut Yaakov could agree with the Shach. Chazal were greatly concerned with needless expense, as seen in siman 13. The author of Zikhron Yosef himself concludes that the matter is difficult to decide, especially because the end of the chapter Zeh Borer appears to support Be’er Esek and Shevut Yaakov, namely, that one need not disclose what monetary claim he has against the defendant. Perhaps he must disclose only whether the claim is monetary or concerns another matter, such as defamation, fines, or the like. That proof may even indicate that the claim need not be disclosed where considerable travel expense is involved, such as traveling from Bavel to Eretz Yisrael. Responsa Chacham Tzvi, siman 169, agrees with the Shach and writes that even if the proofs of Be’er Sheva and the Shach were equally balanced, the law would follow the Shach because he is later, and because where there is an unresolved dispute one cannot compel the defendant out of doubt. Birkei Yosef likewise agrees, explaining that the common practice of summoning a defendant without particulars is no proof, because in ordinary cases the defendant voluntarily comes and raises no objection. If he refuses until told the nature of the claim, he should be heard. Responsa Ramaz, siman 14, agrees, and Ritva to Moed Katan 15a also supports the Shach’s view.
