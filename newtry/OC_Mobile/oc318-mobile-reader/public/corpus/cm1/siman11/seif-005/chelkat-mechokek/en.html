We remove her. Meaning even from her present husband, if she has no children from him. It would take too much space in this work to reproduce the entire sugya and all the commentators, especially since Beit Yosef has already explained them at sufficient length. I therefore rely on the reader to study that discussion first. According to the interpretation of Rabbi Meir in the Sheiltot, witnesses to indecent conduct and witnesses to actual defilement have the same force, so she is removed even if she has children, just as where there are witnesses to defilement. According to the Rosh's interpretation of the Sheiltot, however, one might say the cited baraita's distinction applies only where she has no children. Nevertheless, the Rosh writes that such evidence resembles an open entrance or one witness after warning and seclusion, which likewise implies that it is treated like evidence of defilement. He may therefore understand the Gemara to rule like Rabbi in the case of an uninterrupted rumor, but more stringently than Rabbi in that children do not protect the marriage. Darkei Moshe also appears to understand this way. It is therefore surprising that the Rama writes here that removal applies specifically where she has no children, following the Tur, even though Rabbi Meir's language is explicitly contrary to the Tur. The Rosh too can be explained this way. Since the Sheiltot appears not to distinguish between a woman with and without children, the Rosh may agree that witnesses to indecent conduct together with an uninterrupted rumor are like one witness following seclusion, and that the baraita distinguishing where she has children speaks only according to Rabbi about a rumor that later ceased, a position not accepted as halachah. See Darkei Moshe carefully.
