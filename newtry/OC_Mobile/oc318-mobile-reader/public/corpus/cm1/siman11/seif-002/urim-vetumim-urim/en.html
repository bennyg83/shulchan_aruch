And if one of them is an expert, etc. The Gemara derives that beit din summons a person through a messenger from the verse, "You and Aharon shall be there tomorrow before Hashem." Raavad explains "before Hashem" as before the Divine Presence and the beit din of Moshe. Since the verse mentioned only Hashem and not explicitly "the beit din of Moshe," Raavad infers that mentioning the recognized expert of the beit din suffices; the summons need not name every judge. Sema questions whether this applies only on a day already known to be a sitting day of the court. Perhaps Korach's case involved a specially designated court session. Rama and Raavad imply that the rule applies even without a fixed day. Raavad can maintain that in the wilderness there was no permanently fixed court time at all, because the cloud could rise at any moment and the nation would travel. Therefore the biblical summons itself demonstrates that naming the recognized expert suffices even without a regular court date.
