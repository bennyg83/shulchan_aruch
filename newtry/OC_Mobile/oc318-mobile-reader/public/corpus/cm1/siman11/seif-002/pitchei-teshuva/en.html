The beit din went to another place. See Shemot BaAretz by Maharam ben Chaviv, Rosh Hashanah 31b, s.v. “that woman,” who writes: Although the rule that a borrower must follow the head of the beit din wherever he goes was cited by the Beit Yosef here in siman 11 and ruled by the Rama, the Rambam and Tur omitted it. It therefore appears to me that this rule is not accepted. They apparently hold that the law does not follow Ameimar, but rather Rav Ashi, who challenged him, and that other civil cases are governed like sanctification of the new month. This is also logical, for how could it be that whenever the head of a beit din travels to another city the litigant must follow him, especially since no limit is stated. The Rambam’s view therefore appears correct, that a litigant need follow the head of beit din only to the recognized central court. See Birkei Yosef, letter 2, who cites this and notes that the assertion that the law follows Rav Ashi against Ameimar is difficult, since the plain sense of the passage is that Rav Ashi accepted Ameimar’s answer. Moreover, the Rama’s ruling is not identical to Ameimar’s case. Ameimar spoke of the head of the beit din going elsewhere, whereas the Rama speaks of the entire beit din going elsewhere. In the latter case even Rav Ashi may agree, since the whole court has moved. The Levush likewise uses the Rama’s formulation, as does Maharikash. See there.
