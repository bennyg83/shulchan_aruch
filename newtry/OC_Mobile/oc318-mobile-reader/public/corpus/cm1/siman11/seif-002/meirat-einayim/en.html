They do not write against him a notice of excommunication. "Write" is not literal. Even without a written notice they do not excommunicate him, for we follow the reason of the rule. The exception is where there is a dispute: the beit din messenger says he summoned him in the name of all three judges, and the person denies it. In that case they excommunicate him, but do not write the excommunication document, because that document may entail monetary consequences, as explained above.
And if there is an expert among them, they accord him honor. This is stated there in the name of the Raavad. He learned it from the dispute of Korach, where Moshe was present with the Divine Presence as part of the beit din and sent his messenger to Korach, saying, "Be before Hashem, you and they and Aharon." Since he said "before Hashem" rather than "before us," we see that the summons mentions the distinguished authority and thereby accords him honor. It is not clear from there that on ordinary days, if the summons mentions only the expert judge by name, that alone is sufficient. Yet from Rama's placement of this gloss within the Mechaber's text, it appears that the two rules depend upon one another, and there is no need to summon in the name of all three. The person summoned should assume that all three instructed the messenger, and the expert alone is named merely out of honor. Otherwise Rama should have placed this law after the phrase "when does this apply." Still, this inference is not decisive and requires study.
