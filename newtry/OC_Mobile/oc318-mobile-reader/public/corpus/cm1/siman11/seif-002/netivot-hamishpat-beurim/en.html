And if one of them is an expert. See Sema, who derives from Rama's wording that where one judge is an expert, only the expert's name need be mentioned even on a day not fixed for the court's regular sitting. One cannot object that if this were true there would be no difference between an expert and ordinary judges on a regular court day, when mentioning one judge's name suffices anyway. There could still be an initial-procedure difference: with an expert, out of respect one would mention only his name even initially. Sema's stronger proof is from the structure of Rama's gloss, which is attached to Mechaber's first case concerning a nonregular day; this implies that even then mentioning the expert alone is enough. The 'expert' here need not be an expert recognized by the public. If he were a public expert, Sema's reason that the summoned party should have understood that all three judges were involved would be unnecessary. A public expert can compel a person to appear before him alone, as explained above in siman 3. Therefore even if the person thought only that expert summoned him, he would still be obligated to come.
