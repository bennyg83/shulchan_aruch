How are summonses issued, etc. Maharshal writes in Yam Shel Shlomo, Bava Kamma, chapter 1, siman 43: The Rash rules that in places where there is no beit din, if one person claims money from another and the defendant says, 'Bring me a summons from a place where there is a beit din, and then I will go with you,' the townspeople may set him a time to appear, or conduct the entire matter before them. This is what I found. In Be'er Sheva, page 111a, he rules regarding Reuven, who demands that Shimon select judges with him, while Shimon replies, 'I will not enter litigation with you unless you first tell me what you wish to litigate about,' and Reuven refuses to disclose the nature of his claim at all, that the law favors Reuven. He derives this from the statement in chapter Chezkas HaBatim that a person may refrain from revealing his arguments outside beit din. He adds that the Rosh rules even more broadly in chapter Shevuat HaDayanim that a person need not clarify his arguments unless beit din detects deceit. In my view, his words are incorrect. The case in Chezkas HaBatim, Bava Batra 31a, concerns one who spoke outside court without presenting a particular argument, and then came to beit din and advanced it; he may revise his claim, because a person does not reveal his arguments except to beit din. Meaning, the defendant may say: I did not wish to reveal my defense to you until the litigation. Likewise, the Rosh in chapter Shevuat HaDayanim means that one who claims that he repaid, or the like, need not explain how or with what he repaid. Here, however, the defendant may certainly say: Tell me first what you wish to litigate about, for perhaps once I know your claim I will comply with your demand and need not enter litigation with you at all. So long as the claimant refuses to disclose the nature of his claim, the defendant is under no obligation to go with him to beit din. This appears correct to me.
The beit din sends him, etc. The creditor, not the debtor, pays the cost of the summons. See Responsa Maharshal, siman 106, seif 1.
