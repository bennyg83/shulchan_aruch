But one does not write against him. The Gemara explains the reason: because he must pay a zuz to the scribe. Rashi explains that beit din does not release him from the nidui until he pays the scribe the fee for the written notice of excommunication. Tosafot write that when the document is torn up, he pays the scribe's fee. Shiltei Gibborim infers from Tosafot that they do not hold that the nidui is released orally, but that tearing the written notice itself effects the release. This is strained. How could a nidui imposed orally by a Jewish beit din be released without an explicit oral declaration that the ban is lifted? It therefore appears that Tosafot mean as follows. If he was placed under nidui because of his refusal to obey the court and afterward submits to the law, we do not keep him under nidui merely because he has not yet paid the scribe. He was not excommunicated for failure to pay a writing fee. Beit din therefore releases him immediately. However, if he requests that the written notice itself be torn up so that a document recording his shame and disgrace as one placed under nidui should not remain in existence for many years, then he must pay the cost of the scribe before demanding that destruction. This explains Tosafot precisely.
