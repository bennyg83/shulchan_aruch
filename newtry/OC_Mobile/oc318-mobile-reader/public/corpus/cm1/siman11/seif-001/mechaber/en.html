How, and on Whose Authority, a Litigant Is Summoned. Six seifim. How is a litigant summoned? The beit din sends its officer to instruct him to appear on the appointed day. If he does not come, he is summoned a second time; if he again fails to come, he is summoned a third time. If he still does not come, they wait for him the entire day and place him under a ban on the following day. This applies to one who lives in the villages and comes and goes. A person who is regularly present in the city is given only one date; if he fails to appear throughout that day, he is placed under a ban the next day. {Rama: If the beit din moved to another place, he must follow them; if he does not, he is placed under a ban (Beit Yosef).} The beit din's officer is believed when he says, 'He insulted me,' 'He insulted the judge,' or 'He refused to come to court,' and the person may be excommunicated on his testimony. But a written writ of excommunication is not issued until two witnesses testify that the person refused to come. The officer is not liable for reporting these matters on account of lashon hara. {Rama: One who says that he is unconcerned with the decree of a beit din or a sage is placed under a ban even if he appeared before the court, because by saying that he did not come because of its decree he acts contemptuously. See Yoreh De'ah, siman 334. If he says, 'I will not litigate before you, but before another beit din,' see below, siman 14. One who cannot appear because he must travel far away must notify the beit din, present his excuse, and request another date. If he does not do so, he is placed under a ban even though he was unable to appear (Maharick, shoresh 11). One who formally accepted a panel of two judges and was summoned by them but did not appear is placed under a ban by a beit din of three (Beit Yosef in the name of Responsa of the Rashba).}
