The Shach writes in the name of Maharshal that in a place where there is no beit din, if one person sues another for money and the defendant says, "Bring me a summons from a place where there is a beit din, and then I will go with you," the townspeople may set a deadline for him. He further cites Sefer Be'er Sheva, which ruled in a case where Reuven demanded that Shimon go with him to judgment but refused to disclose the nature of the claim, and Shimon replied, "I will not enter litigation with you unless you first tell me what you intend to litigate about." The ruling was in favor of Reuven. A proof was brought from the chapter Chezkas HaBatim, where it is stated that a person may refrain from revealing his claim outside beit din. The Shach disagrees and rejects the proof. See there, and see Responsa Chacham Tzvi, siman 169.
