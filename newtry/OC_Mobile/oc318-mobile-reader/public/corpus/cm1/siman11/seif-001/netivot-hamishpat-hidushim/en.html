Until two come and testify. See Beurim, seif katan 1. This refers to a case where the beit din messenger reports in the name of the litigant that the litigant explicitly answered that he refuses to come at all. In such a case a writ of ban is written immediately, and therefore the messenger alone is not believed for purposes of imposing a monetary expense. But where the only concern is that perhaps the messenger failed to carry out his mission and did not summon the litigant at all, witnesses are unnecessary even for monetary consequences, because there is a presumption that a beit din messenger performed his mission. Likewise, with respect to all warnings given to a litigant, whenever we do not require the litigant's response we may rely on the beit din messenger alone even for monetary consequences. Only where the litigant's response is required is the messenger not believed for a monetary consequence without two witnesses. The Sema writes that if the plaintiff says, "I will pay the scribe's fee," the writ may be issued on the testimony of the messenger alone, because there is then no extraction of money from the defendant. The writ must record that the plaintiff waived the scribe's fee so that he cannot later claim that fee when the ban is released:
