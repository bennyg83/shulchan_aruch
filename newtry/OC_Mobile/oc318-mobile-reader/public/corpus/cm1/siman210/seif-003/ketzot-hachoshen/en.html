He acquires half. In Perek Mi SheMet, Rav Nachman rules that if one says, "You and a donkey shall acquire," the human recipient acquires half. Rav Hamnuna says nothing is acquired, while Rav Sheshet says the human acquires all. The halachah follows Rav Nachman. The early authorities ask from Kiddushin, where a man betrothed five women including two sisters by saying, "All of you are betrothed to me with this basket." The Gemara asks that even the unrelated women should not be betrothed because the case is like "you and a donkey." It ultimately establishes the case where he said, "Whichever of you is fit for relations with me shall be betrothed." Why is that qualification needed if we rule like Rav Nachman that the valid recipient acquires her share? The answer follows Mordechai's distinction. Where one mode of acquisition is applied to one recipient for two classes of property, and the mode is invalid for one class but valid for the other, we can say that since the acquisition failed in part, it failed entirely. This differs from "you and a donkey," where the donor allocates shares to two separate recipients. The valid human recipient need not lose his share merely because the donkey cannot acquire. In the five women case, the basket is received by one woman on behalf of all of them. The acquisition therefore depends on agency. Tosafot explains elsewhere that one who acquires for another from a transferor must function as agent of the transferor for purposes of placing the property with the beneficiary. If the agency is void with respect to the sisters, one can say the single agency appointment failed entirely, including for the unrelated women. The case therefore resembles one acquisition applied to two legal components, not two wholly independent recipients. This also explains Rashbam's wording that the Gemara's question would appear to support Rav Sheshet. According to Rav Nachman, if the case were truly two independent recipients, the unrelated women should acquire their share. But if agency failure as to the sisters would otherwise nullify the entire agency, only Rav Sheshet's principle, that the valid side can take the whole effective acquisition, would rescue the unrelated women. The Gemara therefore answers that the man limited his statement from the outset to those fit for him. That prevents the agency from ever attaching to the sisters and the unrelated women are validly betrothed. Thus Rav Nachman's rule applies where separate recipients receive separate shares. It does not necessarily save one unitary acquisition or agency that is partly invalid at its inception.
