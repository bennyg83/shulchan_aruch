To one who. The Tur states: just as a person cannot transfer ownership of something that has not yet come into existence, so too he cannot transfer ownership to someone who has not yet come into existence. For this reason, even if the transferor did not retract until after the recipient was born, he may still retract afterward, just as in the law of one who transfers something not yet in existence, stated in siman 209, seif 4. Those who disagree, cited by the Rama here, disagree there as well. It is also explained there that if, after the recipient comes into existence, he takes possession of the property, it is not removed from him. It appears to me that the same law applies here. Sema. The Taz writes that the Sema's words are astonishing and that one must necessarily distinguish between the two cases. See there.
By meshichah. Meaning, the original act of conferring the property upon the fetus through another person was by meshichah. So writes the Tur. The case likewise cannot involve acquisition by sudar, because by the time the child is born the kerchief has already returned to its owner. Nor can it involve chazakah. Rather, it is a case of meshichah, where the property remains in the possession of the person acquiring on behalf of the fetus when the fetus is born, or it stands in a neutral place; alternatively, it is land and the deed remains in the recipient's possession when the fetus is born. See Sema.
His son. But not his son's son, because his mind is not as close to him. For the same reason Nimukei Yosef writes in the chapter Mi Shemet that the child must be viable, for only then is his mind sufficiently close to him. So writes the Sema. See the Shach, who discusses at great length how to reconcile the Rif regarding an inheritance that passes automatically.
Pregnant. Nimukei Yosef writes that this applies only after forty days of pregnancy, because before forty days the embryo is considered mere fluid. The Tur and Shulchan Aruch, which state the law without qualification, appear not to distinguish this. The Beit Yosef likewise writes so. Sema. It appears to me, however, that the Ritva and Nimukei Yosef are correct. The Gemara at the end of Yevamot, regarding terumah and an inheritance that passes automatically, says that within forty days it is considered mere fluid. The Rambam likewise rules this at the beginning of chapter 8 of Hilchot Terumot. The same must therefore apply here. See the Rosh there. The Tur supplies no proof to the contrary, because before forty days it is not called a fetus at all. Shach.
A dangerously ill person. The Shach disagrees and rules that there is no distinction in this law between a person on his deathbed and a healthy person. See there. Mabit, Choshen Mishpat, siman 339, writes that the rule that a gift to one's unborn son is effective applies specifically if the father transfers it, but not if the mother does. See also Mabit, vol. 2, siman 147; Pnei Moshe, vol. 2, simanim 90 and 91; and Responsa Rashach, vol. 3, siman 94. Bnei Chayai.
