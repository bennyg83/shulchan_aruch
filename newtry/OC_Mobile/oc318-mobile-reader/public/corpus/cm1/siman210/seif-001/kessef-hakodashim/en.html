See Ketzot HaChoshen, seif katan 2. In siman 210 Ketzot argues at length against Pnei Yehoshua, although the matter is not conclusively proved from the Rashba's words. The rule is explicit in Gittin 13 and Tosafot there: where the future event is not expected to occur, even Rabbi Meir agrees that it cannot be transferred. Ordinary future offspring, however, appear to be treated as something expected to occur. What Ketzot writes in seif katan 3 against Maharam of Rothenburg from the Ramban is not decisive, because one authority cannot simply be challenged from another. Maharam follows the Raavad and those who agree with him.
