Seif 3. He acquires one half. Note: There are differing opinions on this subject in the Gemara. See also above, siman 209 and siman 13. In my Even HaEzer, siman 120, seif 1, I wrote that even those who disagree do so only where the portion that cannot be acquired has no legal acquisition even rabbinically. But where rabbinic law recognizes the acquisition and only Torah law does not, if it is conveyed together with something that is validly acquired by Torah law, there is room to say that according to all opinions the entire acquisition takes effect on a Torah level. Even according to those who generally maintain that a rabbinic acquisition does not become a Torah acquisition, where it is joined to property acquired by Torah law the entire transaction may become effective by Torah law. See there for the reason, which is sound with the help of Hashem.
