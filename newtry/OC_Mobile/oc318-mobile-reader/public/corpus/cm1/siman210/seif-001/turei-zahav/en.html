(In the gloss: Unlike the dissenting authorities.) The Sema, seif katan 1, writes that these dissenting authorities likewise disagree regarding something not yet in existence. This is astonishing, for the dissenter here is the Rosh, who ruled like Rav Nachman that the expression “when she gives birth” is effective. Yet at the end of siman 212 it is explicitly written in the Rosh’s name that one who says, “When this debt comes into my possession I will give it to charity,” is obligated specifically because of the law of charity. This implies that with respect to an ordinary person it is regarded as something not yet in existence. It appears to me that there is a distinction between the two. Indeed, one must distinguish, because Rav Nachman appears to contradict himself. In Bava Metzia, in the chapter Eizehu Neshekh, he himself ruled regarding the fruits of a palm tree that even after they come into existence the seller may retract. One must therefore adopt the Rashbam’s distinction in Bava Batra, page 143: regarding any fetus, Rav Nachman agrees that it is considered something already in existence, and the property being granted is also in existence; only the wording requires correction, namely, “when she gives birth.” Therefore, the Rosh likewise agrees that in the case of something not yet acquired, the transaction is ineffective even if one says, “when it comes into existence.” This appears correct. In the words of Rabbeinu Yerucham it is written in the name of Rabbeinu Chananel that a fetus has no right of acquisition even by inheritance, but it is explained there that once the child is born, Rabbeinu Chananel agrees with the Rosh.
