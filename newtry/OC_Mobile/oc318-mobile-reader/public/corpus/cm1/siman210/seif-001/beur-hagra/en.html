One cannot, etc. Bava Batra 131a and 141b; Gittin 13b.
Even if he said, etc. In accordance with Rabbi Yochanan there. The wording implies that even an express future formulation does not help, because the Gemara asks, “But our Mishnah, etc.,” and suggests saying “when she gives birth, etc.” This indicates that Rabbi Yochanan follows Rav Huna’s conceptual framework. Rav Nachman’s challenge to Rav Huna likewise shows that according to Rav Nachman the opposite is true. The concluding ruling, “and the halachah is, one who transfers, etc.,” appears to refer to Shmuel and Rabbi Yochanan. Bava Batra 131a and Gittin 13b likewise support this, unlike Rav Nachman’s view. See Tosafos there.
(Collected note) And even. The Tur cites Ramah. His reason is that he rules unlike Ran because the sugyos at the end of Gittin chapter 1 and Bava Batra 131a do not follow Ran, whereas according to Ran a transfer to a person not yet in existence should be all the more effective. Tosafos in Gittin explains this. Rashbam, however, rules like Ran, following his view in Bava Batra 142a that a fetus is treated as already in existence once he says “when she gives birth.” See Hagahos Ashri. (End of note.)
Like those who disagree. This is the Rosh, who rules like Ran and follows Tosafos’ alternative explanation on 141b. See Rosh.
(Collected note) Unlike some who say, etc. Rosh there rules like Ran, as do Rashbam 143a and Hagahos Ashri. (End of note.)
For if not, etc. Even according to Rav Nachman, if the giver dies or retracts before the beneficiary comes into existence, the beneficiary does not acquire. Rav Huna, although he holds that a person can transfer property not yet in existence, agrees that before it comes into existence the transferor may retract, because no acquisition occurs until then. Yevamos 93a and Bava Metzia 66b. For Rav Nachman, a beneficiary not yet in existence is analogous to property not yet in existence according to Rav Huna, as Tosafos writes in Yevamos there.
And he acquired it through meshichah. If the attempted kinyan were a kinyan sudar, the sudar would already have returned to its owner by the time the beneficiary came into existence, as stated at the end of Nedarim chapter 5. Meshichah can remain effective, as stated in Kesubos 82a, specifically when the item remains in a side alley or the beneficiary’s domain, but not in the public domain or the transferor’s domain. See Tosafos there and Yevamos 93a.
(Collected note) If he said “when he is born.” Tosafos, Gittin 13b, appears to imply that even if he did not say this expressly, the same rule applies. The phrase “when she gives birth” merely defines the time acquisition takes effect, with a practical difference if the transferor retracts beforehand. Rosh, Bava Batra chapter 9, siman 5, and Hagahos Ashri rule likewise. (End of note.)
Even if he did not, etc. From the Gemara’s suggestion, “let him say the case is where he said, etc.,” which implies that ordinarily he did not say it, yet the Gemara still raised the challenge from the Mishnah.
Provided, etc. See Rashbam there, s.v. “there are those who disagree, etc.”
And his grandson, etc. Because his affection is not presumed to be as close.
Some say, etc. An automatic inheritance is stronger than an intentional transfer, as stated there 141b and 142a. Rava disputes Abaye and holds that even an automatic inheritance does not vest in a fetus. We do not rule like Rabbi Yosi in Yevamos chapter 7 where the Sages dispute him. Although Shmuel holds like Rabbi Yosi, that is consistent with Shmuel’s own view, which we do not follow here. Certainly an intentional transfer does not work, except that for a shechiv mera the Sages enacted a special rule so that his mind not become distressed. Ramban. The first opinion, however, holds that an automatic inheritance does vest, that Rava does not dispute Abaye, and that the halachah follows Rabbi Yosi that a fetus can acquire. They distinguish the particular rule of Rabbi Shimon bar Yochai where sons are present. See Tosafos, Bava Batra 142a, and Rosh, Yevamos chapter 7. The Yerushalmi, however, limits the rule to a shechiv mera. See below siman 253 seif 26.
