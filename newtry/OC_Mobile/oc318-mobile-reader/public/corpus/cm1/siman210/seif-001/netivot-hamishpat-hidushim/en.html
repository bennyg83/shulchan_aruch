Not like those who disagree. Those who disagree are the opinion cited by the Rama in the gloss to siman 209, seif 4, that even with something not yet in existence, if he said “when it comes into the world,” he acquires it (Sema). See Beurim: this is incorrect, for regarding something not yet in existence Rabbi Akiva concedes that such language is ineffective; only concerning a person not yet in existence is the rule more favorable.
He acquired it through meshichah. Meaning, the initial act of conferring the right upon the fetus through another person was done by meshichah. Kinyan sudar is ineffective because the sudar returns to its owner. Likewise, chazakah is ineffective, even where it was performed by constructing a fence and the fence still exists, because the original act of chazakah has already ended (Perishah).
And if it was his son. The reason is that a person's intent is especially close to his children, and he therefore resolves conclusively and transfers ownership to him wholeheartedly, unlike a grandson. Accordingly, it is also necessary that the child be born viable, for otherwise his intent is not especially close to him (Sema). Even a healthy person may make such a transfer according to this opinion. From this the Raavad proved that, all the more so, a fetus is an heir. The Shach writes that a fetus is not an heir, and that the effectiveness regarding his son is because it is as though he admitted that he transferred it to him through a kinyan made in order to transfer later, or alternatively because the Sages treated one who cannot yet acquire as though he could acquire. See Beurim for the contradiction in his words, and see there that regarding his son it is also necessary that the transferor remain alive and not retract until the child is born.
