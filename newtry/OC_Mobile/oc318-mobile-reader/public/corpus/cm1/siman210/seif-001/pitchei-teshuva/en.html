His son acquires. See Baer Heitev, seif katan 5, citing Mabit I, siman 339, that the rule concerning one's son applies specifically where the father caused the acquisition to vest in him. See, however, Responsa Shevut Yaakov I, siman 111, which appears to disagree with the Mabit and to equate one's mother with one's father for this purpose. The proof stated at the end of that responsum concerning one who guarantees his stepmother's ketubah, from Bava Batra 173 and the verse "I will guarantee him," is difficult to understand, and Responsa Beit Ephraim, Choshen Mishpat, at the end of the relevant siman already questions it. See Shaar HaMelech, Hilchot Mechirah 22:10, who discusses this matter and also cites Mabit II, siman 137, that just as one may transfer to one's son or daughter something not yet in existence, so too there is no distinction between something not yet in existence and a recipient not yet in existence. Shaar HaMelech analyzes this at length and ultimately challenges it from Rambam, Hilchot Zechiyah U'Matanah 6:17, cited in Even HaEzer, siman 51, which explicitly indicates that even with one's son or daughter one cannot transfer something not yet in existence. He concludes that the matter requires study.
