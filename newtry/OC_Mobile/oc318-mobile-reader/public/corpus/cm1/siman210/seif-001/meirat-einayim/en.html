A person cannot transfer, etc. The Tur's wording is: "Just as a person cannot transfer something that has not yet come into existence, so he cannot transfer to a person who has not yet come into existence," etc. For this reason, even if he does not retract until after the beneficiary is born, he can still retract afterward, just like one who transfers something not yet in existence, as the Tur and Mechaber write in siman 209, seif 4. Those whom Rama cites here as disagreeing and holding that if the transferor does not retract before the child is born the child acquires, also disagree in the case of property not yet in existence, as Rama notes in the gloss there. It was also explained there that if, after the property comes into existence, the recipient seizes it, we do not remove it from him according to everyone. In my opinion the same law applies here.
And he acquired it through meshichah. Meaning, the initial conferral to the unborn beneficiary through an intermediary was carried out by meshichah. The Tur says this explicitly. The case also cannot involve kinyan sudar, because by the time the child is born the sudar has already returned to its owner. Nor can it rely on chazakah. Rather, it involves meshichah where the object remains in the recipient intermediary's possession when the child is born, or it stands in a side alley; alternatively, with land, the deed remains in the recipient's possession when the child is born. In the Perishah I explained why chazakah is ineffective.
But if it was his son, etc. The reason is that a person's mind is especially close to his son, and he therefore resolves with his whole heart to confer ownership upon him. His mind is not as close to a grandson. For this reason Nimukei Yosef in chapter Mi Shemet writes that the fetus must be viable; only then is his mind close to him.
Already pregnant. Nimukei Yosef writes that this applies only after forty days, because before then the embryo is considered mere fluid. The Tur and Mechaber state the law without qualification, implying they make no such distinction. Beit Yosef writes this explicitly.
A grandson is like any other person. See siman 247, where the Tur and Mechaber write in seif 3 that if a dying person says, "My property to my sons," his ordinary intent does not include his grandson. Here the novelty is that even if he explicitly confers property upon his grandson, the grandson does not acquire because the transferor's mind is not as close to him.
Specifically with a dying person. The reason is that for a dying person the Sages also enacted that his mere words are treated as though written and delivered, so that his mind should not become distraught from fear that his testamentary instructions will not be fulfilled. The same policy applies here. In the Derishah I wrote that the Tur below in siman 223 cites Rif and those who agree with him concerning the statement, "If my wife gives birth to a male, he shall receive..." and says that it applies specifically to a dying person. Here, however, the Tur states the law without qualification, implying that even according to Rif and his colleagues the present case can involve a healthy person. The reason is that here ownership is conferred through another person with a full act of acquisition. See there and examine carefully.
