Even though he said to him, "Perform work with me," etc. Meaning, even though he hired him generally and did not specify a particular task, so one might think he was hired for anything that might arise.
For example, the river receded, etc. This is the Gemara's and Tur's language. It answers the question that people do not ordinarily hire workers simply to gather found objects, since such opportunities are uncommon.
Some say the same applies if he hired him without specification, etc. See the Derishah and Perishah, where I proved that no one truly disputes this. What was written above, that a worker's find belongs to himself, concerns a worker hired without specification who independently found something for himself. But if he was hired without specification and the employer points out the location of the found items and asks him to gather them, it is as though he had initially hired him expressly for that task. The Beit Yosef likewise writes this. Perhaps the wording is intended to exclude the view of the indexer cited by the Beit Yosef.
