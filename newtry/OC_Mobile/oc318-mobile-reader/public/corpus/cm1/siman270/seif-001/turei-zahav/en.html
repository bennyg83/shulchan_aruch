(Seif 2, those supported at his table.) Rashi explains that the father acquires their finds because of possible resentment. Tosafot write that such resentment applies only to a son whom the father ordinarily supports continuously, for if the father does not support him no one else will. But another person who happens to be supported gratuitously at someone’s table, such as a young orphan, does not reasonably surrender his found articles to the householder. All the more so does this apply to a slave or maidservant, who eat in exchange for their labor. This category also includes a father who has contractually undertaken to support his son at his table. The son’s find belongs to himself because the father provides support as an obligation. This resembles the rule in Ketubot, chapter Na'arah, that a daughter supported after her father’s death keeps her own found articles because her support does not come personally from the brothers.
