Those who are supported at his table, even though, etc. See the Derishah, where I explained that the reason is that the Sages considered it proper to repay good with good. Since he supports him even though he is not obligated to support him beyond six years of age, his found object belongs to the supporter. This differs from brothers who support their sisters after their father's death, for they are obligated to support them. Therefore the sisters' found objects belong to themselves, as the Tur and Mechaber write in Even HaEzer siman 112:2.
And the found object of his daughter who is a na'arah, etc. In the Perishah I explained that na'arah is stated as the greater novelty. Although at that stage the father no longer has authority to sell her as a maidservant, as learned from the verse "and she shall go out free without payment," the Sages nevertheless gave her found objects to her father because of potential animosity. The father still has authority to marry her against her will even to a repulsive or afflicted man. Certainly, with a minor daughter, even if she is not supported at her father's table, her found object belongs to her father, because there are two grounds for animosity: he can sell her as a maidservant and he can marry her against her will. The Tur adds that this is so even if she had previously been sold to another as a maidservant. The Rambam likewise writes this at the end of chapter 17 of Hilchot Gezeilah. The Mechaber and Rama omit it. According to what I explained in the Perishah, no separate practical law emerges, because even after he once sold her as a maidservant and she later left that master at six years, at yovel, or upon bringing signs while still a minor, the father still has authority to marry her against her will. Therefore her found object remains his.
