Adults. The reason is that the Sages deemed it proper to repay good to one who does good by supporting him, even though the father is obligated to support him only until six years of age. This differs from brothers who support their sisters after their father's death, where they are obligated to support them; therefore the sisters' found property belongs to themselves, as the Tur and Mechaber write in Even HaEzer, siman 112, seif 2. See Orach Chaim, siman 366, seif 10. Ibid.
They earned. This is also implied by Tosafot in the chapter HaPoalim in the discussion beginning, 'A person may stipulate through himself...' See there, and Responsa Rashach, part II, siman 211. Shach.
His heirs. Meaning, even if the father died after they had already earned the profit in commerce and the work had been completed, nevertheless, since the profit had not yet come into his possession before he died, he does not transmit it to his heirs. Similarly, the Beit Yosef writes at the end of siman 177 that even regarding a found item which a son found during his father's lifetime, if his father did not claim it during his lifetime and then died, the found item belongs to the son. Sema.
His. The reason the text says merely that it is not his, meaning not the master's, rather than saying that it belongs to the slave, maidservant, and the others, is that the rule differs among the cases. If the father of the minor girl who sold her as a maidservant is still alive when she finds the item, it belongs to her father, not to her. If he is no longer alive, it belongs to her. In any event, it does not belong to the master. Ibid.
A gift. This is likewise stated in the Ritva's novellae at the beginning of Bava Metzia in the name of the Ramban. Shach.
The adult son. We can say that the giver is particular that the gift belong specifically to the son. This differs from a minor, whose property ordinarily is not safeguarded except through the supervision of his father who guards him; therefore, a gift to a minor is presumably given with the father in mind. Sema.
For himself. The reason is that if this person does not support him, others will, because supporting him is a great mitzvah and people are available to do so. Therefore, in such a case the Sages did not institute that his found property belong to the one who supports him. Ibid.
