(But if he died.) This ruling originates in a responsum copied in the second Knesset Mordechai: “Just as a man does not transmit his right in his daughter to his sons, so too he does not transmit the earnings of one son to the other sons. If they say, ‘We labored for the common estate,’ he may answer, ‘You in your own property and I in mine.’” This astonishes me. Regarding a daughter’s earnings, an explicit Mishna at the beginning of Na'arah Shenitpattah, page 46, states that even if she had not yet collected them when the father died, they belong to the brothers. Ran explains that the father acquired them from the moment they came into existence. The rule that a man does not transmit his right in his daughter to his sons concerns only what she earns after his death. If so, the father acquires the earnings of a son supported at his table just as he acquires the son’s found articles. Why should he not transmit those earnings equally to the other sons? Besides this, that responsum contains many further difficulties. It rules like Shmuel in the first chapter of Bava Metzia, distinguishing literally between a minor and an adult, rather than like Rabbi Yochanan, who makes the matter depend on whether the child is supported at the father’s table, although all authorities rule like Rabbi Yochanan. It also states that according to Rabbi Yochanan an adult daughter supported at the father’s table keeps her find because maturity removes her from his authority, contrary to the halacha that even a daughter’s find belongs to her father whenever she remains supported at his table. It therefore appears to me that this responsum is unreliable. The Rama’s ruling may nevertheless be sustained on another basis, namely, Baal HaIttur’s view cited here in Darkei Moshe. He writes that where a son accumulated money for himself during the father’s lifetime, we follow an assessment of intent: since the father did not demand it, he waived it, whether the money came from a find or business profit. This too is difficult in light of the Mishna in Na'arah concerning a daughter’s earnings, which says that even if she had not collected them they belong to the father. This implies all the more where she did collect them. Yet one might have thought the reverse, that once she collected them for herself and he did not demand them, they are hers. We must distinguish that Baal HaIttur speaks only where the son has already accumulated a separate treasury from the money. Only then is it reasonable to say that the father waived his right by not demanding it. The Rama’s ruling here must likewise be limited to that circumstance. He abbreviated where he should have clarified this.
