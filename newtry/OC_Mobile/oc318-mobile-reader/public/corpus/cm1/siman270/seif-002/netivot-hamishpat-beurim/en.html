The same applies if they profited through commerce. The rule in Bava Kamma 87 that compensation for unemployment is given to the sons can be distinguished. Compensation for unemployment is different because they receive it even though they performed no work, since the father is not particular about such proceeds, as the Gemara states. Where they actually work and earn a profit, perhaps it is not regarded as something that came from elsewhere, and the father is particular. Yet there is a difficulty. Since the Mishnah stated only a found object, and nevertheless we say that the son's earnings likewise belong to the father because of potential ill will, why was it necessary in the case of a wife to enact two separate rules, namely that her earnings are in exchange for her support and that her found objects belong to him because of ill will? Once the Sages enacted that found objects belong to him because of ill will, her earnings should be included as well, no less than those of a son whom he supports. Even regarding an orphan whom one supports, Tosafot had to distinguish the case conceptually, and according to their distinction a supported wife resembles a supported son. One can answer that the separate enactment concerning her earnings was needed so that even if the husband says, "use your earnings for your support," any surplus still belongs to him so long as she has not said, "I will not be supported and I will not work."
But not his adult son, and not his daughter who is a na'arah. Chelkat Mechokek and Beit Shmuel are uncertain whether a gift given to a na'arah daughter belongs to her father. Ketzot HaChoshen seeks to prove that the father certainly acquires her gift from Orach Chaim, siman 366, where one may not transfer an eruv through one's son or daughter, even if adult, while they are supported at his table, and may not transfer through a daughter until she reaches full adulthood even if she is not supported at his table. He infers that the father acquires her gifts. His proof is difficult. According to the view that one cannot transfer the eruv through an adult son or daughter supported at his table, which is Tosafot's view in Bava Metzia 12, s.v. Rabbi Yochanan amar, all of the son's acquisitions belong to the father because of potential ill will. Accordingly, just as the supported son's acquisitions belong to the father because withdrawal of support could create ill will, so too a na'arah daughter's acquisitions may belong to the father even where she is not supported by him. Although there is no potential ill will concerning support, there is potential ill will concerning kiddushin because he has the power to marry her to someone repulsive or afflicted, as the authorities explain. Their uncertainty exists even according to Nimukei Yosef's view that the issue is not ill will and that one may transfer the eruv through an adult son or daughter. Even on that view, it can be argued that a na'arah daughter's gifts belong to her father by Torah law because they resemble kiddushin; see their words. Conversely, proof that the father does not acquire her gift can be brought from the first view, which is Ramban's, that one may transfer an eruv through an adult son or daughter even when they are supported by him because the father's right to a child's found objects is only due to ill will, and where the father himself transfers property through the child there is no ill will. If a na'arah daughter's gifts belonged to her father by Torah law, one could not transfer an eruv through her, yet the Shulchan Aruch states generally that one may transfer through an adult daughter, which implies even a na'arah. The Beit Shmuel's words there are also difficult to understand. He writes that since a woman can be betrothed with one perutah, any excess resembles a gift. I do not understand this, for in the case of kiddushin the transaction resembles a gift to her father, since he has the power not to marry her to that man. Indeed, it resembles a sale in which he transfers his daughter to him. A gift given directly to the daughter, by contrast, belongs to her.
