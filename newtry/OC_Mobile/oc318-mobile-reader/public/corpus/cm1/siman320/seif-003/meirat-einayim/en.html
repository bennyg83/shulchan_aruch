Anything that serves as the basic protection of the field, etc. The Tur writes: regarding a sharecropper, anything without which the field is not protected, such as the fence and the ditch surrounding the field, is the owner's responsibility. Anything added merely to make the protection better is the sharecropper's responsibility. The ditch mentioned here appears to mean what is explained in the Gemara and recorded by the Tur and Mechaber above, siman 218, seif 20, concerning one who buys half a field from another. There he writes that one makes a fence, and behind the fence, close to it, a small ditch three tefachim wide, and farther outward a large ditch six tefachim wide, with one tefach between the two ditches, all so that a mongoose cannot jump across. See the Perishah.
The pickaxe with which they dig, etc. The Tur writes that the landowner supplies the principal implements needed for working the land, such as the pickaxe used for digging, the tools used to remove the earth, and the bucket and pitcher used to draw water. Things that are not part of the essential work but merely make the labor easier, such as channels made to conduct water over the field, are the sharecropper's responsibility. The reason is that since the labor itself falls upon the sharecropper, if he wishes to make watering or guarding easier he must do so at his own expense. This explains the Mechaber's wording, copied from Rambam. His phrase, "cleaning out the places where water gathers," means the channels dug by people from the bank of the main river across the fields so that water can flow from them for irrigation, thereby sparing the sharecropper the labor of drawing and carrying water from the large river on his shoulder. According to this explanation, however, it would have been more precise not to say "where water gathers," which sounds like pits dug for water to collect and remain. Some therefore explain that the channels were already dug but became partially blocked with mud and fallen earth, and must be cleaned and cleared.
But in fixed-rent tenancy the landowner has no obligation, etc. The reason is that the tenant must pay the fixed rent in any event, even if the field produces nothing, as explained above. Therefore the owner has no involvement with the field during the term of the tenancy and need not provide or perform any of the items mentioned above. All the more so where one rents a field for a fixed amount of cash, the owner need provide none of these.
And what Rama writes, "and so it appears to me." The Tur likewise writes concerning the second view, "and this is reasonable." See there.
