All twenty-four, etc. As stated there, "the essential rule in the world, etc." Therefore Rambam omitted the mention there of the firstborn animal, etc., because it is included in the general rule, as the Gemara itself says, "the rule is, etc." He also omitted Rav Yosef's second statement.
The axe. This is the hoe. "The implements" refers to the basket. "The bucket" refers to the drawing bucket. "The jug, etc." refers to the irrigation vessel. Bach writes that the text should read "and a leather flask," but this is unnecessary, for Rambam wrote according to the practice of his time, when they did not ordinarily use a leather flask. This is the meaning of "and similar items, etc."
Others say, etc. See Sema. This follows Rashi's approach to the Mishnah there, s.v. "and both, etc.," regarding the latter clause of the Mishnah, and Tosafos 104a, s.v. "hani," who understand Rav Yosef's statement as referring to it. Rambam and Ramah, however, explain the Gemara literally: "these two baraisos, etc.," including the latter clause that both parties provide the reeds, and the same law applies to the other listed implements according to their respective rules. Rav Yosef's statement refers to both.
