He may not uproot. Meaning, uproot together with the roots.
His fellow may prevent him. The reason is explained in the Gemara. Sometimes the landowner prefers that the roots of the crop remain in the soil as fertilizer for the following year. At other times, when the field does not need fertilization, he prefers that the field be cleared and therefore wants the crop uprooted together with its roots and removed from the field. The sharecropper too sometimes needs straw for his animals and therefore prefers uprooting with the roots, while at other times he wishes to avoid the extra labor because uprooting is more difficult than cutting. The general principle is that according to local custom both the sharecropper and the owner have an interest in the crop and straw that grew on the land. Therefore each can say, "I want the roots of the straw," or, "I do not want them." This is straightforward.
Where the custom is to plow. This means plowing in order to turn under the roots of harmful weeds so that they will not grow again when the field is sown.
And he weeded. One might have thought that because he removed the harmful weeds, plowing is no longer necessary. The ruling therefore teaches that the owner can say, "Perhaps you did not weed sufficiently, or perhaps harmful weeds grew again after you weeded."
If he specified at the time of weeding, etc. In the Perishah I wrote that the novelty is that he need not make this stipulation when he first accepts the field. Even if he initially accepted it without specification, if at the time of weeding he states his intention and the other remains silent, that silence is treated as consent. In this way I resolved the Beit Yosef's difficulty with the Tur. See there.
If the custom is to weed, etc. The reason is that the owner can say that seeds of the harmful weeds will otherwise fall to the ground and later sprout, and subsequent plowing will not eliminate them completely. He therefore wants the sharecropper to weed before the weeds produce seed, in accordance with local custom.
