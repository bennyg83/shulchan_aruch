And it is like a written loan. This too explains why the document may be written only with the consent of both parties. Once written, it functions like a written debt and can be used to collect the agreed obligation from encumbered property. People do not ordinarily consent to having such a document created against them without their agreement. See siman 39. In the Perishah I explained that this applies specifically where the document specifies a fixed amount he must pay. The Tur's language proves this. If the document merely says that he will give the landowner one third or one fourth of whatever the field produces, so that the amount is unknown and unquantified, the document cannot be used to collect from encumbered property. The same reason prevents collection of a wife's and daughters' maintenance from encumbered assets. See the Perishah.
The recipient pays the cost of writing. The Tur concludes that this is so even if he leaves the field fallow that year, following agricultural custom. Although he derives no benefit from the field that year, he must still pay the writing expense. The Gemara states this. See the Perishah.
