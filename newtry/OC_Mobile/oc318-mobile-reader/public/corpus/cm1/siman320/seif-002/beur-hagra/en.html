One who receives a field, etc. The distinction between a purchaser and one who receives land to cultivate is explicit in the beginning of chapter 6 of Demai, in the chapter HaMekabel, and in chapter 5 of Peah: one who receives, etc.; when does this apply? When he received it for one half, etc. The distinction appears in many other places as well. See Tosafot there, 103b, s.v. 'and he goes,' etc.

From the time it was written, etc. Meaning, this is why such a document is not written without the parties' consent. The same applies to arbitration documents, as Rashi explains in Bava Metzia 80a, s.v. 'this one chooses,' and to written pleadings, as Nimukei Yosef explains. See siman 80, seif 2. The Tosefta in Moed Katan states that a tenancy or contracting document may not be written during the festival, while Rabbi Yehuda permits it lest another person precede him. Sefer HaTerumot, cited by Beit Yosef in Orach Chaim 545, explains that where the field had already been accepted, writing was permitted so that neither party could retract, for before the document is written both may retract, as explained above.
