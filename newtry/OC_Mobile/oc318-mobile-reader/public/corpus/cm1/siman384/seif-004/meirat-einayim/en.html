The turtle. A quaint of the pyrestone from the mountain:
The correct text is: "and they gave it to the stonecutter." Meaning the craftsman who cuts and squares the stone so it can be fixed into a wall. After he finishes cutting it, the ordinary practice is to give it to the donkey driver, who transports it to the building. The donkey driver gives it to the porter, who carries it up to the wall. The porter gives it to the craftsman standing on the building, and that craftsman gives it to the worker who sets it into the wall. The ruling says that if the stone breaks while still in the hand of any one of them, that person alone is liable even though all of them are contractors on this overall job. But if after the stone has already been set in the building something happens and it falls and causes damage, then if all of them were contractors they are jointly liable, as explained later.
