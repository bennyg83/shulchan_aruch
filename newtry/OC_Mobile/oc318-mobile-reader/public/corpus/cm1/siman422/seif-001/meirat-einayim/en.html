He is not atoned for until, etc. This is the language of the Mishnah and baraita in the chapter HaChovel, and the Rambam writes the same. Rashi, however, explains the statement as referring specifically to pain and embarrassment, and the abridged rulings of the Rosh likewise write so. Accurate editions of the Tur also say explicitly "pain and embarrassment," although some printed editions read only pain.
And he forgives him. The Gemara derives this from Avimelech, where Scripture says, "Now return the man's wife, for he is a prophet, and he will pray for you." It appears that the lesson is that if Hashem had wished to forgive Avimelech directly, He could have done so without Avraham's prayer. Scripture teaches that the wrongdoer must first appease the injured person until he grants full forgiveness, and only then does the latter pray for him.
It is forbidden for the injured party, etc. This too is learned from the incident of Avimelech, for Avraham accepted his appeasement and prayed for him, as Scripture states.

See Orach Chaim siman 606. There Rama writes that if one spread a false reputation about another, the victim need not forgive him. This is from Hagahot Maimoniyot to Hilchot Teshuvah and Maharil. The reason is that a false reputation can damage the honor of the person's ancestors or descendants for generations, and some who heard the slander may never hear of the retraction and forgiveness and will continue to think it true.
