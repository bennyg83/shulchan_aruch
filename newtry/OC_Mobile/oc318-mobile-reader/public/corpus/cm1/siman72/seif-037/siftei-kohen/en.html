A mortgage document. The Bach rules that even a mortgage for a fixed term in a place where the debtor cannot redeem early is not acquired by the first person who takes possession when the convert dies during the term. The Shiltei Gibborim writes that in a place where early redemption is not permitted, the land is effectively sold to the mortgagee until the end of the term, since the debtor cannot remove him by payment. Therefore, upon the convert's death the first person to take possession acquires it. In my view the Bach's ruling is more convincing, because the mortgagee's right remains a security interest and does not become ownership merely because redemption is temporarily restricted.
