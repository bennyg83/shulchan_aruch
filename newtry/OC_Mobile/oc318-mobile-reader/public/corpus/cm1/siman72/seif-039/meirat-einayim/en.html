And the lender was not in his courtyard. If he is in the courtyard, even if it is not intrinsically guarded, it is considered guarded through his presence. The Tur and Mechaber write similarly below in siman 268.
And Levi wishes to swear concerning the pledges, etc. The Tur concludes: Levi wishes to swear concerning pledges held by his partner Reuven, saying, 'His possession is like my possession and my hand is like his regarding all partnership affairs. He holds them as my agent, and I can swear concerning his possession as though I myself held them.' The responsum answers that Levi cannot swear concerning pledges in Reuven's possession. Had Reuven himself claimed that Shimon owed him money and that he was retaining the pledges against that money, Reuven would have been believed through a migo that he could have said, 'It never happened,' or, 'I returned them to you.' But regarding Levi there is no migo. A migo exists only for what is in the claimant's own ability to assert. It does not apply to property in another person's possession, because perhaps that other person would not be willing to lie and say, 'It never happened,' or, 'I returned it to you.' The Mechaber abbreviated the responsum.
