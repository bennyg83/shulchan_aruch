A pledge belonging to a convert. The same rule applies to a pledge belonging to a non-Jew, as Maharshal writes. The Bach says that the following distinction is based on Tosafot and the Rosh rather than Rashi. I do not understand him, because Rashi and Tosafot do not disagree regarding the practical law here. The Tur and Shulchan Aruch may well follow Rashi's explanation. The Maggid Mishneh likewise appears to explain the Rambam in accordance with Rashi, for he says that when the pledge is already in the first person's courtyard, that person acquires it. The difference between the explanations concerns the conceptual route, not the resulting rule of acquisition.

Levi's statement has no effect, etc. Had Reuven claimed that Shimon owed him money and that he was retaining the pledges on account of that debt, he would have been believed by virtue of a migo, since he could have denied the matter entirely or claimed that he had returned them. With respect to Levi, however, there is no migo. A migo applies only to a claim that the litigant himself could have made; it does not arise from a claim that another person could have made, for perhaps that other person would not have wished to lie by denying the matter or claiming that he returned the item. This is the Tur's language from a responsum of the Rosh. The Bach cites Terumat HaDeshen, who distinguishes between witnesses testifying for others, where the matter does not affect them and one cannot know what is in another's heart, and partners who are both released from monetary liability and rely upon one another not to contradict each other, in which case a migo may be invoked. He adduces support from a responsum of the Rashba, from Tosafot in the name of Ri of Chinon, and from the incident of Avimi son of Rabbi Abbahu, where the partners could have denied the debt entirely and therefore were believed to say that the money was received on account of a different debt. He accordingly rejects the Rosh, Tur, and Shulchan Aruch here. All of these proofs, however, are astonishing, because in those cases both parties knew the truth, and therefore the migo was available to both. Here Reuven does not know whether Levi is telling the truth. Why should Reuven wish to lie and deny the transaction when Levi himself may be lying? The principal ruling is therefore as stated by the Rosh, Tur, and Mechaber. This is clear.
