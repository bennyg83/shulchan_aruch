Levi’s words are of no consequence. See Sema and Shach. On this basis Responsa Shevut Yaakov, volume 2, siman 180, discusses Reuven, who received collateral from Shimon without witnesses and lent him one hundred upon an item worth two hundred. When Shimon came to redeem the collateral, Reuven’s wife asserted that she too had lent Shimon one hundred, which Shimon denied. The question was whether she is believed by a migo that she could have denied the transaction entirely or claimed that she had returned the money. He ruled that Reuven may not retain the collateral on behalf of his wife, because Reuven himself does not know the truth except from his wife, while she has no migo because the collateral is held by her husband. He further writes that even if the wife dies and the husband inherits her, he still may not retain the collateral, because he cannot swear from his own certain knowledge that Shimon owed his wife. This is straightforward, and Responsa Maharshal Kohen, volume 2, siman 2, rules similarly. See there.
