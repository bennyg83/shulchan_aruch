If a convert's collateral is held by a Jew and the convert dies without heirs, and another person takes possession, the lender acquires the amount of the debt and the other person acquires the remainder. This applies when the collateral lay in an unguarded courtyard and the lender was not present there when the convert died. If the lender was in his courtyard, or even absent while the collateral was in his guarded courtyard, the courtyard acquires the entire collateral for him. If Reuven held Shimon's collateral, Shimon died, his heirs demand it, and Reuven says, 'I retain it because my partner Levi told me that Shimon owed him partnership money, and Levi is willing to swear concerning it,' Levi's words have no effect because the collateral is not in Levi's possession.
