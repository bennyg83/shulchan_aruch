If the creditor denies it. The Bach writes that even if the pledge was worth less than the debt, the creditor's denial does not constitute a partial admission requiring a Torah oath, because the value of the pledge, according to the borrower's claim, is treated as though already tendered, while the creditor admits the balance of the debt. These words are erroneous. This is the very case of partial admission and denial described by the Torah, since the creditor does not admit that he holds the pledge. The Tur and Shulchan Aruch therefore carefully distinguish the cases. Initially they say that the borrower takes a rabbinic oath, but where the pledge was worth less than the debt they say simply that he “swears” as to its value, meaning a Torah oath. The same conclusion follows from the earlier discussion at the end of seif 17.
