To a Jew. The Tur explains that the body of the pledge still belongs to the Jew. The non-Jew had only a lien upon it, and once it fell from the non-Jew and another Jew found it, that lien terminated. The Shach writes that if the finder returned it to the non-Jew, he must pay the Jewish owner.
The money. The Shach writes that the authorities who cite this law in the name of Rabbeinu Yeruham all misunderstood him. He brings Rabbeinu Yeruham's actual wording, which demonstrates that he speaks only of a pledge, not of money.
The finder. Even regarding a pledge, Beit Yosef and Darkei Moshe cite Maharam that if accrued interest exceeded the value of the pledge so that the non-Jew effectively owned it, the finder acquires it, because a person's property serves as security for his obligations and a non-Jew may look directly to the security. The Sema questions why the Mechaber omitted this. The Shach answers that there is no difficulty, because the Mechaber cites an opinion even regarding money that it should be returned to the Jew, and that opinion plainly disagrees with Maharam. The Rama, however, is somewhat difficult because he rules that money belongs to the finder. See also siman 183, seif 8.
