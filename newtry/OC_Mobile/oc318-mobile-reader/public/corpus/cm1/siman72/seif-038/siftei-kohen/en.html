He must return it to the Jew, etc. If he returned it to the non Jew, he must compensate the Jew. This appears from the Reem, unlike Maharam according to the Bach. In my view, however, one can say that Maharam also agrees; see there and examine carefully.
And there is an authority who says, etc. The Beit Yosef, Darkei Moshe, Sema, and Bach cite this in the name of Rabbeinu Yeruham, Netiv 20, part 2. In my view they all misunderstood Rabbeinu Yeruham, for he is discussing collateral. His text states that when a Jew owned collateral held by a non Jew, the non Jew lent money upon it, it was lost from the non Jew, and another Jew found it, the finder returns it to the first Jew and may not return it to the non Jew, because once it fell from the non Jew his lien ceased when the Jew found it. If the finder says that he wishes to return it for sanctification of the Divine Name, the other Jew may tell him, if you wish to sanctify the Divine Name, do so with your own property. It is plain that the passage concerns collateral that was lost, and the corrupt wording must be corrected accordingly. The old Constantinople edition likewise clearly refers to collateral, not to money. There is a further decisive proof: in Netiv 22 Rabbeinu Yeruham refers the law of a Jew’s collateral lost from a non Jew back to Netiv 20, part 2, and there he wrote only the passage cited above. Thus the reading is certainly as I have explained.
For with money it belongs to the finder, etc. This applies even to collateral. The Beit Yosef and Darkei Moshe cite Maharam that if the interest had already exceeded the value of the collateral, so that the collateral was effectively settled in the non Jew’s hands, the finder acquires it, because a person’s property serves as guarantor for his debt and a non Jew pursues the guarantor, all the more so collateral actually in his possession. The Sema questions why the Mechaber omitted this. There is no difficulty, because even regarding money the Mechaber cites an authority requiring return to the Jew, and that authority plainly disputes Maharam concerning collateral as well. The Sema himself notes that Maharam implies all the more strongly that money belongs to the finder, as the Rama rules. Still, there is some difficulty with the Rama, since he rules that money belongs to the finder, and the same appears in Raavan, siman 112.
