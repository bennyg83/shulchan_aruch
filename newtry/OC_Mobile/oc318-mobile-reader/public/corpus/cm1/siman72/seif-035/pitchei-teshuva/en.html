And they shall take the heirs’ oath. See Baer Heitev, who writes that even where there is an administrator, the administrator must swear. Responsa Shevut Yaakov, volume 2, siman 171, disagrees. He explains that the objections raised from siman 108, seif 6, are not difficult. Here the pledgor seeks to recover his collateral from minor heirs. Even if the relatives and administrators made no claim at all, and even if the orphans themselves said that they knew nothing, nothing could be collected from them until they became adults and took the heirs’ oath. The orphans therefore do not lose any right because of the arguments advanced by relatives or administrators. Even if those persons support the claimant’s account, the property cannot be taken from the orphans on the administrator’s testimony, since he is only one witness, and the relatives are disqualified witnesses. Indeed, the property of minor orphans is generally not adjudicated at all. As stated in siman 290, seif 12, if one has a claim against orphans, the administrator should not plead on their behalf, and if he did so and caused an adverse judgment, the judgment may be reopened. Accordingly, there is no reason to impose an oath or a general ban upon the relatives or administrator for no purpose. By contrast, in siman 108 the administrator is the claimant seeking to collect debts due to the orphans, and therefore he must swear. See there.
