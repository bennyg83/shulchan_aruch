There and there in Baal HaTerumot in the name of Rabbi Yitzchak, and in Responsa Geonim in the name of a responsum of the Rif. Rabbenu Yerucham likewise writes that the borrower claimed the relatives should swear that the deceased instructed that one hundred were in his possession. Rabbi Yitzchak ruled that neither an oath nor general cherem applies to them, because an orphan's guardian must raise every claim the orphan could raise, as we say, 'One who appoints a guardian should appoint one like this,' Ketubot 108b. See siman 156 seif 6 regarding which oath the guardian must take.
Although a minor who becomes an adult need not swear that his father did not instruct him, because a minor is not normally the recipient of such instructions, as below siman 98 at the end of seif 5, here the case concerns intelligent minors who can receive instructions. Shach in the name of the Bach.
