There is room for doubt. The Sema explains the doubt: perhaps because he is holding it only by virtue of Reuven, he does not acquire it. This differs from the case in seif 40 where one Jew repledges the non-Jew's pledge to another, because there the first Jew removes himself from it. The Shach writes that there is no doubt at all. Even surplus located in a protected courtyard can be acquired, as explained above in seif 39, all the more so here where the pledge is already in his hand. Maharshal rules likewise, and this appears primary. The Shach further cites a responsum of Maharam Alshich: Reuven borrowed money from a non-Jew. Shimon found the money and Reuven said to him, 'Give it to me, and I will pay the non-Jew interest in the same manner you pay him.' Since such an arrangement is prohibited as interest, as explained in Yoreh Deah at the beginning of siman 169, Reuven acquires the money and Shimon must return it to him. This differs from a pledge, which wherever it is remains in the owner's domain, whereas money is given to be spent and did not leave Reuven's ownership merely because Shimon deducts the amount of interest that he paid the non-Jew. Although strict law is so, it is appropriate to give Shimon a share of the money because it was found in his possession. In matters of this sort one should not insist on the strict letter of the law where doing so will create conflict; upright parties should compromise. The interest Shimon paid is deducted because it is fixed interest, and whatever he paid to the non-Jew may be recovered from the first Jew as the Rama writes at the beginning of Yoreh Deah siman 169. These are the words of the Shach.
