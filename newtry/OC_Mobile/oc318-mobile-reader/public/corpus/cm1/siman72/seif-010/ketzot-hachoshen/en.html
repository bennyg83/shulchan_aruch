The lender swears first. The order exists because we fear that if the borrower swears first, the lender may later produce the deposited object and expose the borrower as having sworn falsely. If the lender is himself disqualified from swearing, the borrower cannot simply say, "I will swear and then we shall see whether he produces it." The original concern remains. Initially Rava and Shmuel understood the Mishnah as removing the oath from the borrower entirely because of this concern. The conclusion merely says it is enough that the lender swear first. If the lender cannot swear at all, that solution is unavailable. The case therefore resembles one where both parties are suspected and neither can take the required oath, in which case the disputed amount is divided, as Rama rules in siman 92.
