And not to a trustee. See Baer Heitev, through his statement that these communal trustees cannot be compared to a guardian appointed by beit din. See Responsa Shivat Tzion, siman 99. The questioner wished to say that a charity collector is like a communal tax trustee, to whom we cannot apply the reasoning that he willingly accepts liability because of the good reputation he gains. He therefore sought to exempt the collector even for negligence, according to the authorities who exempt a guardian appointed by the father of orphans even for negligence because otherwise people would refrain from accepting the role, as explained in Beit Yosef, siman 290, and Shach there, seif katan 25. Shivat Tzion answers that this is incorrect. Communal appointees are not all alike. One who examines the Beit Yosef will see that the Rashba's reason concerning a communal tax collector is that the office brings him no particular honor or distinction, because people often obtain such positions through maneuvering. This does not apply to collectors for synagogues in our community, who are not appointed through maneuvering but only after synagogue members repeatedly urge a person whom they have chosen. Such an appointment certainly brings honor and establishes a reputation for trustworthiness. Therefore, the collector has the status of a guardian appointed by beit din according to Abba Shaul, since the public relied upon him, and there is no concern that imposing liability will deter people from accepting the office. He must therefore pay if he negligently guards charity funds. See there, and see Netivot HaMishpat.
