The law favors Reuven, since, etc. This is Sema's correct reading. Since he does not contradict the one witness, that witness has the force of two. We therefore have effective testimony that the object came to him as a pledge. In addition there are witnesses who saw the pledge, because he initially produced it before witnesses. He therefore lacks a migo and is not believed to claim otherwise. Rashba disagrees and says that since the witnesses themselves establish that the object came as a pledge, and a creditor acquires a pledge, he is treated as asserting rights in his own property and does not need a migo. Shach agrees with Rashba and sees support from Rambam. Rama in responsum likewise writes that one should not remove property from the possessor against Rashba. In Tumim I discussed this at length and concluded that the principal practical law follows Mechaber and the majority of authorities. Rambam provides no proof for Rashba. Maharik likewise supports this.
And Reuven takes a hesset oath. Although he admits ten and would appear to be a partial admitter obligated in a Torah oath, either he immediately pays the ten, or he leaves the pledge with the other party against the ten, making it an immediate tender. Sema and Shach.
