(Seif 40) “Shimon acquires the entire pledge.” The Sema implies that Shimon’s right rests chiefly on the prohibition of interest: Reuven must have relinquished his interest in the pledge, for otherwise Reuven would effectively be borrowing from Shimon at interest. On that view, if Reuven repledged a gentile’s pledge to Shimon without interest, Reuven would retain the surplus. This is incorrect. The Mordechai, citing Raavan, derives the rule from the case of a convert who died while his pledge was held by a Jew: one Jew acquires to the extent of his debt and another possessor acquires the remainder. Here too, once Shimon advanced his money, the entire pledge becomes his. The basis is not interest but Reuven’s relinquishment of his prior lien when he took Shimon’s funds. The Mordechai later doubts only where Reuven deposited the gentile’s pledge with Shimon and then the gentile died, because a mere deposit remains in Reuven’s domain. Repledging it, even without interest, removes Reuven’s earlier right. The same applies even if the gentile never instructed Reuven to repledge it; the Mordechai reports the facts of the case, not a legal requirement. Nor does the existence of heirs who know of the pledge restore the surplus to Reuven. Shimon acquired the whole pledge immediately upon the gentile’s death. The Bach also disputes the Sema’s requirement of the gentile’s instruction, although he still bases the rule on interest. The Mechaber’s words, which mention no interest, support the explanation given here.
