The law favors Reuven, because, etc. The text should read 'because,' with a causal formulation. The reason is that there is one witness that the object came into his possession as collateral, and it was also seen in his possession before the parties came to litigation. Thus there is both testimony of how it came to him and present sight of it, leaving him no migo that he purchased it or returned it. Although there is only one witness that it entered his possession as collateral, since he himself admits that it is collateral and does not swear against the witness to contradict him, the statement of the single witness is treated in this respect as though two witnesses had so testified. This is clear.
And Reuven shall take and swear. Meaning, if Shimon has no proof that he lent him twenty, so as to collect that amount, Reuven swears and is thereby exempt from Shimon's claim.
And Reuven shall take a hesset oath. A hesset oath is sufficient even though he admits part of the claim, because the collateral in Shimon's possession is equivalent here to an immediate tender. With collateral of this sort, on which the money was originally lent, all agree that it is treated as an immediate tender. See what I write nearby in seif 20, seif katan 69.
