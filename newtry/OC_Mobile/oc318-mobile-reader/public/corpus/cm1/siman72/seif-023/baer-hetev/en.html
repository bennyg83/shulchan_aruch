And since. The Sema emends the text to "because," making the clause explanatory. Since one witness testifies that the item came into the lender's hands as a pledge and the item was also seen in his hands before litigation, this is equivalent to witnesses plus present sighting, so he lacks a migo of purchase or return. Although there is only one witness as to the pledge, the lender himself admits that fact, making the witness's statement equivalent to two witnesses. The Shach writes that the Rashba disagrees and holds that the lender is believed upon an oath. The Rama earlier cites this view in siman 18. In his responsa the Rama writes that although a responsum printed under Ramban's name appears otherwise, money should not be extracted against the Rashba. The Shach further notes that the so-called Responsa Ramban are in fact Responsa Rashba with a printer's error, as Beit Yosef notes in his introduction. It therefore appears that the Rashba himself retracted the former view, leaving no authority who requires return of the pledge. The practical ruling is that the lender swears and collects, as supported by the Rambam and Baal HaTerumot.
Shevuat heset. Because the pledge constitutes immediate payment, this is a case of heilech. The Shach explains that the case may be where the lender has not yet taken the pledge as payment, but since Shimon proves no right in it, it belongs to Reuven and Shimon's claim is merely an oral claim requiring only heset. Alternatively, the lender immediately pays the ten that he admits, which is itself heilech.
