Note: Were I worthy, it would appear to me that when the Rashba says that Shimon claims, "From the profit I earned, you take first," he means the profit that accrued before I lent the money to the gentile, with respect to which you indeed have priority over me. But regarding the excess profit that accrued afterward because of my loan to the gentile until now, the lender argues that he has priority, because the interest increases from day to day. The Rashba rules that although Shimon can repay whenever he wishes, so long as he does not repay, he acquires all the profits retroactively until the time of repayment, and therefore has priority as to all of them. His proof is from the obligation to support one’s wife’s daughter, where although he could divorce his wife, so long as he does not divorce her the obligation exists retroactively. All this concerns the profits accrued until now. But if Shimon now says, "Take the principal and the interest accrued until now, and no further interest shall accumulate," the law is with Shimon. Consider this carefully.
