If it was stolen before, etc. The Tur writes as follows in a responsum: Since we hold that one who lends upon a pledge has the status of a paid custodian, he is liable for theft and loss even though the borrower has already repaid him. The reason for his liability is the benefit of Rav Yosef's perutah, namely that perhaps a poor person will come while he is occupied spreading out the pledge, at which time he need not give charity to that poor person. Therefore, if the pledge was stolen before the time fixed for the borrower to come for it, it was still considered in the lender's custody and the lender was obligated to shake it out and care for it. If it was stolen after the fixed time, it was no longer considered in his custody and he was no longer obligated to shake it out. It is then like an ordinary deposit, regarding which the custodian need not shake it out once the depositor has the right to come and take it and care for it himself. This differs from a pledge, which is not in the borrower's possession so that he can take it and shake it out, because the lender will not allow him to take it. Therefore, the lender must attend to it. See there, and see what I wrote at the beginning of this siman in the Sema in the name of the Rashba.

Even though the borrower had already come and requested his pledge and the delay was caused by the lender, nevertheless the lender does not have the status of a paid custodian, since he derives no benefit from holding it.
