If it was stolen before, etc. The Sema writes that before the fixed time expired the lender was still obligated to air out and care for the collateral. If a poor person arrived then, the lender would be exempt at that moment from giving charity because he was occupied with the mitzvah of caring for the collateral, and he therefore remains a paid custodian. After the fixed time, however, there is no further duty to air it out, since the borrower can come himself and attend to his property, so the special custody ends. See Tumim. I wrote that this reasoning is unnecessary. Even if no airing was required at that moment because the item had been deposited only briefly, so long as the lender's custodial relationship had not ended he remains a paid custodian from the original time he received the collateral, when Rabbi Yosef's saved perutah applied. A single perutah of benefit is enough to render him a paid custodian for the entire period. The exception is the type of case discussed by the Rosh, where at the time the collateral came into his possession the custody was under the special rule of the owner being in service to the custodian. See Tumim. I noted that Ritva appears to disagree with this ruling, and the matter requires study.
He is liable only for negligence. This is where the debt was already fully paid and the lender now holds the collateral only as an unpaid custodian. If the debt has not been paid, he remains a paid custodian up to the amount of the loan, as explained above.
