Go now and return tomorrow. This applies specifically where the creditor actively postpones the borrower until tomorrow. But if the borrower repaid and did not say, "Return my collateral," and the creditor likewise said nothing, and the collateral was afterward stolen or lost, the creditor is exempt because his custodial responsibility had already ended. This is true even according to the view that he is a paid custodian because of Rabbi Yosef's perutah, since the borrower could already have taken the collateral back. See what I wrote in seif katan 4.
If there are no witnesses. If the collateral was stolen or lost and the creditor does not know whether the loss occurred before the agreed termination time or afterward, at first glance this resembles seif 32, where we do not extract the money from the borrower. The same should apply here. However, Nimukei Yosef in Perek HaShoel writes that where one borrowed for half a day and then rented for half a day, and one does not know whether the accident occurred during the borrowing or rental period, this is treated as, "I do not know whether I repaid you," because liability had already attached and only later may have ended. By contrast, if he first rented and afterward borrowed, the doubt is whether liability ever arose. Therefore here, where the creditor was initially a paid custodian and later became an unpaid custodian, the doubt resembles "I do not know whether I repaid you," and the creditor should be liable for the excess. Nevertheless, according to what I wrote in siman 344, that even where he first borrowed and later rented he can be exempt, the same exemption applies here.
But according to the one who says he is an unpaid custodian. Beit Yosef writes that under that view the creditor is a paid custodian only because he holds the collateral to collect his debt. Once the debt was already paid and that reason disappeared, he is plainly exempt from theft and loss. The Shach says this wording is imprecise. If the creditor were paid custodian merely because he holds the collateral for collection, he should be liable even for value above the debt. Moreover, the sugya in Perek HaUmanin shows that ordinary lenders on collateral are not paid custodians merely because they hold the pledge for collection. In this particular case, however, Beit Yosef's wording is exact. The Rosh's case is Reuven, who rented barrels from Shimon and gave collateral for the rental charge. He later returned the barrels, paid the rental, and demanded the collateral. Here holding the collateral for the charge is itself a source of paid custodial benefit, like an artisan who holds the object for his wage. Tosafot asks why a lender on collateral is not a paid custodian because of this benefit and answers that had he not lent, he would not need to hold the collateral. An artisan, by contrast, gains the wage from the work and therefore benefits from holding the object for collection. Tosafot similarly asks concerning a renter and answers that ordinary renters all hold the rented property by nature of the rental, so that fact alone is not special benefit. But here the person rented out his barrels and received a separate pledge for the rental fee. This is like an artisan holding for wages. He therefore is a paid custodian while the charge remains unpaid. Once he is paid, that benefit ceases.
He is liable only for negligence. The Shach holds that even according to the view that the lender is otherwise an unpaid custodian, to the amount of the debt it is as though he expressly agreed that loss of the collateral means loss of the debt and therefore as though he expressly accepted payment liability. Even after he has received the money, if the collateral is stolen he must refund the corresponding amount. In my view Beit Yosef and Rama are correct. If part of a pledge is stolen while enough value remains to secure the full debt, the creditor can still collect the debt from the remainder, as Mahari ibn Lev rules. If so, once the borrower has already repaid, the money in the creditor's hand is at least as good as the remaining collateral. If the actual collateral is then lost, the creditor can apply the money already held toward the debt. Payment should not be worse than a surviving pledge. Therefore he is only an unpaid custodian after repayment.
