Through negligence. The Sema writes that this is specifically where the lender already received his money. If he has not, he loses the amount of his loan, as stated in seif 2. The Shach says this is not compelled. The Rama may mean only that the lender is liable for the excess value solely if negligent, while still agreeing that even if he had already received the money he must return it, as though he had expressly stipulated that if the pledge were lost he would refund what he received. Responsa Maharanach questions why the lender is not a borrower of the item, since once the loan is paid retention of the pledge is no longer needed for the loan. The Shach answers that one is not a borrower unless he has permission to use the item. Where he may not use it, even if it is no longer held for the depositor's benefit, the Gemara and authorities show that he is not a borrower. The same appears from the law of an artisan who says, "Bring the money and take your item," at the beginning of siman 306.
