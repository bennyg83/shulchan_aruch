If it was stolen before, etc. The Tur, citing a responsum of the Rosh, explains that a lender holding a pledge is a paid custodian because of Rabbi Yosef's perutah: while occupied with airing the pledge he may be exempt from giving charity to a poor person. If the pledge was stolen before the time fixed for redemption, it was still under the lender's control and he was obligated to air it. If it was stolen after that time, it was no longer under his control for this purpose and he was not required to air it, like an ordinary deposit that the bailee need not air once the depositor may come take it and care for it himself. A pledge differs during the loan term because the borrower cannot take it to air it, since the lender will not release it, and therefore the lender must attend to it. After the term, it is like an ordinary deposit. See above, seif katan 10 and 36.
But according to the view that he is a custodian, etc. The Beit Yosef writes that according to this view the lender is a paid custodian only because he holds the pledge to collect his debt from it; once the debt has already been collected, that rationale disappears and he is plainly exempt from theft and loss. This wording is imprecise. According to the opinion that the lender is merely an unpaid custodian, he is not a paid custodian against the amount of his money merely because he holds the pledge for collection; otherwise he should be liable even for the pledge's excess value. The Gemara and authorities also show that holding it for collection is not itself the reason for paid-custodian status. Rather, the Beit Yosef means that, with respect to the amount of the debt, his acceptance is treated as though he stipulated that if the pledge is lost he will not collect the debt. Once he has already collected the debt, he is therefore exempt. This is not conclusive, however. One may say that an undertaking that loss of the pledge will entail loss of the debt is equivalent to an undertaking of personal liability, so that even after collecting he must restore the money. It is possible that the Rama means only that he is not liable for the excess value except through negligence, while conceding that he must return the amount collected. This may also be the Levush's view. Thus, even according to the opinion that he is ordinarily only an unpaid custodian, he must return the money; the practical difference concerns only the excess, for which liability exists only according to Rabbi Yosef. Consider this carefully.
