If it was stolen before, and so forth. The source is a responsum of Rosh. A person rented barrels and gave collateral for the rental. After he returned the barrels, he asked for his collateral back. The owner asked for additional time to return it and fixed a date. The collateral was stolen during that interval. Rosh rules that if the theft occurred before the agreed return date, the holder remained responsible for spreading and airing the collateral and was therefore a paid custodian through the perutah of R. Yosef. After the agreed date, that duty ended and so did the paid-custodian status. This seems to contradict Ritva's principle cited above that later spreading and airing creates paid-custodian status only where the original transaction itself began as a mitzvah. Here the original transaction was a rental for payment, not a mitzvah loan. Beit Yosef senses the difficulty and refers to Nimukei Yosef, Raavad, and Rashba, who say that once a perutah benefit creates paid-custodian status it can continue for the full custodial period. But why did Rosh need the new spreading-and-airing reason after the barrels were returned? Before that point the collateral itself had already been held in a paid relationship. The answer is that a new custodianship began after the rental ended. Once the renter returned the barrels and demanded his collateral, the original rental-based custody ended. The owner then asked the renter to leave the collateral with him for an additional fixed period. That was a new deposit. It was no longer shemirah beba'alim tied to the reciprocal barrel relationship, and the previous status did not automatically carry over. During this new period, Rosh therefore needed the separate obligation to spread and air the object in order to create the perutah of R. Yosef. This also avoids the problem that the original exchange of barrels and collateral might have involved reciprocal custody. Accordingly, Rosh can still agree that one initial perutah is enough for the duration of a single custodial relationship. He needed a new basis only because a new custodianship arose after the original one had ended.
