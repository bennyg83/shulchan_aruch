Shimon was negligent. See Shach, seif katan 156, through his statement that no proof can be brought from there because it also says, 'or he gave it to him as a gift corresponding to the debt,' and it is obvious that if he gave a complete gift corresponding to the debt, the debt is extinguished. Necessarily, that wording was only an example. See Responsa Beit Ephraim, Choshen Mishpat, end of siman 36, who questions the Shach. He writes that the Shach proceeded too quickly and understood 'or he gave it to him as a gift' to mean that the lender obligated himself to give the borrower a gift equal to the debt, and therefore wrote that it is obvious that the debt is extinguished, from which he inferred that the case must involve an incomplete gift. In truth, that is not the implication at all. The opposite is possible: the borrower may have given an actual gift to the lender equal to the debt. See there.
