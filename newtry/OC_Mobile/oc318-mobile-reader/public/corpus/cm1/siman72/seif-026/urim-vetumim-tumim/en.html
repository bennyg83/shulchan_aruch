Shach correctly observes that Nimukei Yosef derives this from the worker whom an employer tells, "Take what you produced as your wages." So long as the worker has not actually removed the goods with the employer's acquiescence, the employer can retract. Shach asks from Mechaber below in siman 336, who follows Rashba that where the worker's wages are already represented by property in his courtyard, the courtyard can acquire it immediately. If so, why may the debtor retract here? The answer is that Nimukei Yosef's implication works only one way. If even a worker, who normally expects immediate payment, can be told that the employer was merely delaying him until actual transfer, then certainly a debtor's statement concerning collateral can sometimes be only a postponement. But the converse does not follow. Rashba's reason for immediate acquisition by a worker is special. A worker normally removes his wages promptly because his livelihood depends on them. If the employer directs him to specific goods in his own courtyard, the circumstances indicate genuine intent rather than delay. Collateral is different. Even if the debtor says, "Keep the pledge as yours," he knows the creditor may not rush to sell or consume it. The statement can therefore still be a temporary postponement while the debtor hopes to find money and redeem it. There is an even better explanation from Shitah Mekubetzet. The Gemara distinguishes a worker's wage claim from a claim for stones or materials partly because delay of wages implicates bal talin. The employer is therefore especially motivated to effect payment promptly and completely. That factor does not exist in an ordinary collateral settlement. Accordingly, even if a worker's courtyard can acquire immediately under Rashba, the collateral case remains analogous to ordinary goods where actual transfer or clear acquisition is required. There is no contradiction in Mechaber.
