Collateral of a Jew. If someone took possession of the convert's collateral during the convert's lifetime and retained it until after the convert died, the result appears to depend upon the dispute between Rashi and Tosafot in Kiddushin concerning Mar Zutra's taking possession of a convert's slave just before the convert's death. According to Rashi, the slave was an adult and Mar Zutra took hold of him while the convert was alive so that the slave should never be ownerless for even a moment. The convert departed through death while Mar Zutra entered ownership while alive. According to that approach, here too the collateral would never be without an owner and the possessor could acquire it. According to Tosafot, possession during the owner's life would not help once the original owner dies. Yet even according to Rashi there may be a distinction. There Mar Zutra took hold of the slave himself, the very object of ownership, so the slave was never a moment without a master. Here the person took only the collateral, not the underlying debt. Upon the convert's death the debtor can acquire the debt itself, and possession of the collateral may not prevent that.
