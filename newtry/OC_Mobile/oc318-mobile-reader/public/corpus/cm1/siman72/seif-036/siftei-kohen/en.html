A pledge belonging to a Jew. Tosafot, Nimukei Yosef, Maharshal, and the Maggid Mishneh write that even where the pledge was taken after the loan, the deceased owner may still redeem it with money, and therefore another person does not acquire it merely by taking possession after the creditor's death. This follows their view that a creditor does not acquire a pledge as complete ownership. According to what I established above, however, a pledge taken after the loan is acquired by the creditor as a genuine proprietary interest, even regarding unavoidable loss, as though sold to him for the debt. Consequently, if the creditor was a convert who died without heirs, the first person to take possession may acquire that interest. If the pledge was held by a non-Jewish creditor, however, the same does not follow, because a non-Jew does not acquire a Jew's pledge in that manner. See below in siman 278.
"He has no inheritance in the way that he is given to a man."
