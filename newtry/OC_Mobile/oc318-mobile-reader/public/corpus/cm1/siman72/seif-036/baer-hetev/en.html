Tosafot, Nimukei Yosef, Maharshal, and Maggid Mishneh write that the same applies even to a pledge taken after the time of the loan, because the debtor could still redeem it with money. They appear to follow their view that a creditor does not acquire such a pledge as a complete proprietary acquisition. According to what the Shach wrote above in seif 2, however, a pledge taken after the loan is fully acquired by the creditor even for liability for unavoidable loss, as though it had been sold to him for the debt. Accordingly, where the debtor is a convert who dies without heirs, the holder acquires it. If the pledge was held by a non-Jew, however, the holder does not acquire it, because a non-Jew does not acquire a Jew's pledge, as stated in seif 38. Perhaps even Tosafot and those who agree with them would concede the law where the pledge was taken after the loan and there is no other property from which to collect, for then the pledge is fully acquired by the creditor. Alternatively, even where there is no other property, although the creditor acquires the pledge even regarding unavoidable loss, since it has not yet been finally awarded to him and the Jew could redeem it if he had money, it remains the debtor's property. The matter requires study. These are the words of the Shach.
