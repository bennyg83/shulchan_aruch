And Levi brought witnesses. Meaning, witnesses who recognize the object as Levi's and testify that Levi deposited it with Reuven. This therefore has the status of witnesses plus current observation.
Or Shimon admits it to him. Meaning, Shimon admits that he knows the object in Reuven's possession was deposited there by Levi. Shimon therefore stands in place of witnesses plus current observation. The Shach disagrees with this law. In his view, a purchaser does not stand in the place of witnesses plus current observation, because the purchaser is a witness who himself needs an oath. The seller could say, "I returned the object to Levi, and the purchaser is lying when he says he bought it from me. Perhaps he stole it from you." Had the purchaser not claimed purchase, he himself would need to swear. A witness who requires an oath is not a valid witness.
And if he does not claim that it was purchased in his presence. Meaning, he does not say, "It was sold to me in your presence." Sema. Where Shimon admits that it belongs to Levi and that Levi deposited it with Reuven, the market protection enactment does not apply, because Shimon caused his own loss by buying despite knowing it was deposited property. Although Levi must reimburse the purchase money, Urim VeTumim remains uncertain whether witnesses plus current observation can extract the money from Reuven, because perhaps that evidentiary structure works only to recover the physical object, not to extract money. In my view there is no doubt: Reuven must return the money.
