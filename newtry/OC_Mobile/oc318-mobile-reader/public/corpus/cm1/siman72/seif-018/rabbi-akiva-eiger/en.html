[Shulchan Aruch] With an oath while holding a sacred object. Note: In the Derishah below, siman 107, seif 11, it is stated that this applies specifically when he seeks to collect from the lender himself, who contradicts him with a definite claim. But when he seeks to collect from heirs, since they do not make a definite claim, he collects after a heset oath. See there. In my humble opinion, these words are astonishing. From the Rambam and Shulchan Aruch here, who write, “even if the borrower died first,” it appears that even from the heirs he cannot collect without an oath. Likewise, the Ramban’s discussion of the incident of Rabbah bar Sharshom makes explicit that even against heirs a stringent oath is required. The same is true in the case of the goats that ate barley, where the Gemara states that he swears, even though the opposing party can claim only uncertainty. This requires analysis.
