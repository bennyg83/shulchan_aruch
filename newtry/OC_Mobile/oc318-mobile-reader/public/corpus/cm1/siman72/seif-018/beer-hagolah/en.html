y) So writes the Rambam in chapter 8 of Hilkhot To'en, from the Gemara's answer: Rava can tell you that a slaughtering knife, meaning a butcher's knife, is different, because people are particular when it becomes nicked, Bava Metzia 116a.
k) From what the Rif writes there: wherever an item was lent or rented before witnesses, even if it is not ordinarily lent or rented, if there are witnesses that it is presently in the holder's possession and he says, "I later purchased it from him," he is not believed. The Rif brings proof from the chapter Chezkas HaBatim, Bava Batra 46a.
l) These are the words of the Rosh there.
m) Like the statement Rav Huna bar Avin sent: items ordinarily lent or rented, etc., there. So too writes the Rambam there.
n) The Maggid Mishneh writes that this is straightforward, because the entire sugya there concerns a case where the claimant saw the item in the holder's possession. So too writes the Ramban, and this is also proved in the chapter Chezkas HaBatim, 46b.
s) See seif 23 and what is written there. Reuven claimed against Shimon, "I have sefarim in your possession." Shimon answered, "I became guarantor for you, and when the lender demanded payment I delivered the sefarim to him so that he could hold them as collateral and collect from them." It was ruled that even if the books are treated as items ordinarily lent or rented, they are no longer in Shimon's possession. Reuven therefore cannot remove them from the current holder until he repays everything that holder lent to Shimon upon them, because of takanat hashuk. Rashba in a responsum, cited by the Beit Yosef, from siman 8.
o) Meaning, he became obligated to him for some other reason, not that he lent money against this item. This is explained at the end of the gloss.
p) This refers to the immediately preceding clause. It means Shimon does not claim that he knows the item in Reuven's possession was purchased outright; rather, he claims only that Reuven gave it to him as collateral and that he lent money against it.
