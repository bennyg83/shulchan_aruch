There were witnesses that it was stolen and that it was worth more, etc. The Sema writes that even if there are no witnesses that it was worth more, but the lender admits this, nevertheless we do not say that the lender is believed to say that he was not negligent by virtue of a migo that he could have said it was not worth more, because this would be a migo involving brazenness. He could not have brazenly denied a matter that the borrower knows with certainty, unlike the question whether it was stolen through negligence, concerning which the borrower makes only an uncertain claim. In my humble view, however, since the Tur and the Rama write immediately in the following seif, "and there are witnesses to this, or the lender admits it," but do not write the same here, it appears that here witnesses are specifically required. Otherwise the lender is believed by migo, for this is not a migo involving brazenness, as I prove below in siman 75, seif 7, seif katan 21; siman 81, seif 23, seif katan 58; siman 93, seif 2; siman 110, seif 2; and siman 357, seif 1. See there carefully. Moreover, here he would in any event be believed by a migo that he could have said, "I do not know how much it was worth," as above in seif 12, which involves no brazenness. Although Tosafot in the chapter HaShoel, Bava Metzia 97, write that in such a case this is not a migo because a person does not willingly present himself as uncertain, since doing so makes it appear that the claimant who speaks definitely is telling the truth while he himself says, "I do not know," and the Ran writes similarly in Kol HaNishba'in concerning the ingot of Rabbi Abba, nevertheless, since he could in fact have claimed "I do not know," because it is possible that he truly did not know, then if he instead made the definite claim that it was not worth more, that claim would not be considered brazen, since the borrower could think that perhaps the lender does not really know and is therefore speaking this way. The Bach's further statement that here, according to all opinions, we do not apply a migo to avoid a more stringent oath, as discussed below in seif 17, is likewise imprecise, as I explain below in seif katan 66. Therefore, according to the Rosh, Tur, and Rama below at the beginning of siman 296, who do apply a migo to exempt from an oath, here too, if there are no witnesses that the pledge was worth more, the lender is believed with a hesset oath that he was not negligent, by virtue of a migo that he could have claimed that it was not worth more. This is why the Tur and Rama specifically wrote that there are witnesses. See carefully.
According to the opinion that he has only the status of an unpaid custodian, etc. We rule this way, because due to uncertainty we do not extract money from one who possesses it, as explained above in seif 2. According to the opinion that he has the status of a paid custodian, he must claim that the loss occurred through an unavoidable accident.
The lender shall swear like any unpaid custodian that he was not negligent with it. He must also swear that he did not make unauthorized use of it and that it is not in his possession, as explained below in siman 295. This oath that it is not in his possession is a Torah oath, for since the pledge had been in his possession and he would have been obligated to return it, by this oath he exempts himself from paying the amount by which its value exceeded the debt, as explained below in siman 294, seif 2.
