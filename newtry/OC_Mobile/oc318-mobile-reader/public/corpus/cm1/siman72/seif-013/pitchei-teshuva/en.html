And he swears, etc., and includes, etc. See Responsa Veshav HaKohen, siman 79, who writes that according to the rule stated above in seif 3, if the lender returned the pledge to the borrower as a deposit and it was then lost with the borrower, the lender remains liable while the borrower is only an unpaid custodian. Suppose in such a case the borrower sues the lender and says, 'I lent you one sela upon it, and it was worth two,' while the lender says, 'It was worth only five dinarim.' We should not simply have the lender swear and be exempt, because there is concern that after the lender swears, the borrower may produce the deposited item. Therefore, the borrower must swear first that the item is not in his possession, and afterward the lender swears that it was worth only five dinarim and is exempt. This follows only Rashi's explanation and the explanation of Rabbeinu Chananel and the Rif of the concern that the other party may later produce the deposit. According to the reason that the pledge may deteriorate, mentioned by Tosafos in Bava Metzia 34b and Shevuos 43b, that concern does not apply here, because the lender is swearing only that the item was not worth more. On this basis Veshav HaKohen properly explains the Mishnah's wording, 'The one with whom the deposit is located should swear, lest this one swear and the other produce the deposit.' Why does the Tanna use this formulation rather than simply ask who swears first, and why does he call it a 'deposit' rather than a 'pledge'? He also thereby resolves the words of Maharil cited by the Rama at the end of Yoreh Deah siman 217, who derives from this Mishnah that one who vowed not to accept a deposit is also forbidden to lend on collateral if he says that he intended both, because the term 'deposit' can encompass both situations.
