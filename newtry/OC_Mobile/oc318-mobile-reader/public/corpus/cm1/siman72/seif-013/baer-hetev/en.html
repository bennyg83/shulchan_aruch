Witnesses. The Sema writes that even if the borrower has no witnesses that the pledge was worth more but the lender admits that it was, we do not believe the lender's claim that he was not negligent through a migo that he could have denied the higher value, because that would be a brazen migo against a fact the borrower knows. By contrast, the borrower's claim of negligence is uncertain. The Shach rejects this as not constituting an improper brazen migo, citing several simanim. He adds that the lender could in any event have claimed that he did not know the value, as in seif 12, which involves no brazenness. Therefore, according to the Rama at the beginning of siman 296, who recognizes a migo to exempt from an oath, when there are no witnesses to the greater value the lender is believed with a shevuat heset that he was not negligent through a migo that he could have denied the value. That is why the Rama specifically required witnesses.
Who says. We follow that view because money is not removed from a possessor on the basis of a doubt. The lender must nevertheless swear that he did not make unauthorized use of the pledge and that it is not in his possession, as stated in siman 295. Shach.
