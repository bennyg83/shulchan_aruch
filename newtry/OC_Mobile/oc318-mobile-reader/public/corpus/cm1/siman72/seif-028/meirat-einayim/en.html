And this one swears that he sold it to him. Baal HaTerumot concludes regarding this: although one of them will thereby necessarily come to take a false oath, the halachah follows the first Tanna of Ben Nanas, who says that both parties swear and collect from the employer. See the Perishah, where I explained why the Sages instituted oaths in this manner, even though the two oaths contradict one another, rather than instituting oaths like those in the case of two persons holding a garment, discussed below in siman 138.
And if they wished to reverse their oaths. Meaning, one party wishes to take upon himself both his own oath and the reversed oath of the other, and the other agrees that he may swear and collect. It appears to me that 'reversal of an oath' here has its ordinary meaning. The oath originally imposed upon a person in order to be exempt is transferred to his opponent, who then swears and collects. The rule is thus as follows. Reuven says to Shimon: 'You are required to swear that the item in my possession was sold to me and thereby be exempt. Swear also the reverse of the oath imposed upon me, namely that it is not held by me as collateral, and I will give you three dinars as you claim.' Or Shimon says to Reuven: 'Swear your oath that it is collateral in your possession, and also swear the reverse of my oath, namely that it was not sold by you to me, and I will give you four dinars for the collateral as you claim.' They may proceed this way because of concern that one of them might formulate one oath deceptively. If they disagree about reversing the oaths, meaning that neither is willing to take both oaths, the ruling concludes that if they do not agree, each takes his own oath. Since neither wishes to take both oaths and collect, each takes the oath incumbent upon him and is thereby exempt. This appears to be the proper explanation. Other explanations were before me, but they did not seem correct. Examine carefully.
