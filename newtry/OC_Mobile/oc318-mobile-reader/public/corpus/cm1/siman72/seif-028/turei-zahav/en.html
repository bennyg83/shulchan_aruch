(See there: nevertheless we learn, etc., until: and at the time of the loan he loses the handle, etc.) I found written: "I do not understand. Why should a loss occurring at the time of the loan be more significant than a loss occurring later? If he expressly stipulated, then even beyond the value of the collateral the lender bears the loss, and this is true at the time of the loan and all the more so afterward. If he did not stipulate, then even when the loss occurs later he loses only up to the value of the collateral, and all the more so at the time of the loan." The rabbi's words are therefore extremely difficult. This is the end of the citation.
