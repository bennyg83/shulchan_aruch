And they take the heirs' oath. See Baer Heitev, who writes that even if there is a guardian, the guardian must swear. See Responsa Shevut Yaakov, part II, siman 171, who disagrees and writes that the question raised by the later authorities from siman 108, seif 6, is no difficulty. Here, the person who gave the pledge is seeking to recover his pledge from the heirs. Even if the relatives and guardians made no claim at all, and even if the orphans themselves said that they know nothing, nothing could be extracted from the orphans until they grew up and took the heirs' oath. The orphans' position is therefore not weakened by claims made by their relatives or guardians. Even if those people state facts supporting the claimant, property is not extracted from the orphans on the testimony of the guardian, who is only one witness, while the relatives are disqualified witnesses. Indeed, in general we do not adjudicate against the property of minor orphans. As explained in siman 290, seif 12, if someone has a claim against orphans, the guardian should not plead on their behalf, and if he does plead and liability results, the judgment is reversed. See the Sema there. Why then should we administer an oath to the relatives and guardian for no purpose, or even impose a general ban upon them? This differs from siman 108, where the guardian seeks affirmatively to collect debts owed to the orphans; there he must swear. This is the substance of his words. See there further. His words will be cited below in siman 108, seif 6, seif katan 6.
