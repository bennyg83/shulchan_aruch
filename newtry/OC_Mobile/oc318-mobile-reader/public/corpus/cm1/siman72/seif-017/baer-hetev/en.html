Regarding the principal. The Shach writes that the same law applies where the parties dispute the amount by which the pledge depreciated: the lender must take an oath while holding a sacred object, contrary to the Bach. See seif 2 and seif 27.
With a sacred object. The Bach suggests that although the Rosh recognizes a migo to exempt partners from an oath in siman 93, that applies only to exemption from the oath entirely. Where an oath is required in any event, a migo does not reduce its severity. The Shach says this is incorrect and points to the Rosh at the beginning of Kol HaNishba'in, citing Ri Migash, which shows that there is no such distinction. The reason here is instead that the lender is not asserting ownership of the physical pledge. See the beginning of siman 296.
Money. The Sema explains that an object which ordinarily must be returned in specie makes the lender resemble one who is extracting property from the borrower, so the lender must take a more stringent oath as one who swears and collects. Money, by contrast, according to the lender's own position remains in his hands as payment of the debt. See also the Sema and Shach's discussion of the Ran.
First. We do not apply here the principle that one cannot bequeath to his children money that he himself could collect only through an oath, as in siman 108. This property is already in the father's possession to satisfy the debt. Beit Yosef nevertheless writes that the heirs must take the heirs' oath that their father did not instruct them otherwise. The Sema explains that this is so even according to the Rosh and Tur, who ordinarily instituted the heirs' oath only when they seek to collect from the borrower's heirs. The Shach adds that even according to his ruling in siman 108 that heirs need swear against the borrower only if the borrower says, "Swear to me," that is limited to a document, where their father himself would not automatically have needed an oath. With a pledge, where the father would have needed an oath in every case, the heirs too must always take the oath that their father did not instruct them otherwise. See siman 108, seif 12.
Collects. Meaning upon an oath while holding a sacred object. Shach.
Teacher. Meaning that the teacher is retaining the item for some other debt. For tuition itself he need not return it, as stated shortly. Sema.
Migo. Terumat HaDeshen 321 writes that he likewise does not lose his status as possessor. This matters in a doubt concerning a document, where possession is effective even without a migo, as stated in siman 42, seif 8, or in a dispute among major authorities or another doubt of law. Shach.
Deposit. The Shach writes that the Rama did not formulate the distinction precisely. Only with a loan may a person generally retain money in his possession and say simply that he owes the claimant nothing. With a deposit, the custodian cannot seize the deposited property and swear in general terms, because who appraised it for him? The same applies to a pledge. Rivash 396 says this and adds that even where the claimant owes him wheat from a loan, he cannot simply seize it without valuation. See Responsa Maharanach 113. Even with reciprocal loans, where one person has superior and medium land and the other only inferior land so that the rule is that each collects from the other, as in siman 85, seif 3, the first cannot merely swear generally that he owes nothing. This is where the one seeking to make the general denial possesses superior and medium land and the claimant has inferior land. In the reverse case he plainly may make the general claim that he owes nothing.
Lost by accident. The Sema explains that if the custodian claims an unavoidable accident, the opposing party must be certain that no such accident occurred in order to deny him. But if the custodian denies that any deposit was ever made, he thereby concedes that no accident occurred, for saying "you never deposited it" necessarily means "it was not later lost by accident," and the other party may swear generally that he owes him nothing.
According to law. Rivash explains that this means after valuation by beit din, as stated later in siman 73, seif 15. Shach.
