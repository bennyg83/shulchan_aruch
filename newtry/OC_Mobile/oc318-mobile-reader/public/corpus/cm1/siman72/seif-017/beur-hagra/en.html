(Collected note) While holding a sacred object. This is the view of the Geonim. They were challenged from the Yerushalmi concerning a worker who held a pledge and collected without an oath, and from Bava Batra 33a, where the migo appeared to spare an oath. Ramban and Ran distinguish between an artisan and a creditor holding a pledge. Rambam and the Shulchan Aruch in siman 89, seif 5, however, explicitly require an oath while holding a sacred object. The resolution is that the cited sugyos follow the view that a migo can exempt from an oath, a position rejected by the Rosh, Ran, and other poskim. The Shach explains that where, without the pledge, the claimant would not be believed even with an oath, the migo cannot dispense with the oath, whereas in the other cases he would have been believed with an oath independently. This fits the Yerushalmi's worker case. The ruling therefore follows the Riem and Rambam that a migo cannot exempt from an oath, as in siman 93, seif 2, and the beginning of siman 296. Rambam, chapter 13 of Hilchos Malveh, halachah 3, expressly explains that because the creditor's claim does not concern ownership of the pledge itself, even without witnesses he cannot use his migo to avoid the claimant's oath. Maggid Mishneh explains similarly. The Bach's objection is likewise not persuasive, since even where only a shevuas hesses would otherwise be due, a migo does not remove the special oath of one who collects. The law concerning heirs who claim through a purchase also shows this. Accordingly, the Rama's language at the beginning of siman 296 requires study, since the accepted law follows the Geonim regarding a pledge. (End of note.)
