There in the name of the Geonim. So too the Rambam there. The Maggid Mishneh writes that all the Geonim agreed, because the lender does not claim ownership of the collateral itself. We do not say he should take only a shevuat hesset through a migo that he could have claimed, 'I purchased it,' because we do not use a migo to exempt from an oath, as the Rambam writes there. As for the Sema's statement in the name of the Ran that where the borrower seeks to extract the item from the lender, the lender is believed up to its value with a shevuat hesset, it appears clear to me that the Ran did not write this as practical law. His wording likewise indicates this, and this view is contrary to all the poskim. I further believe that what the Tur cites in the name of the Raavad, that the lender takes only a shevuat hesset, should read Rabbi Ephraim, for the Raavad did not object to the Rambam on this point in chapter 13. He only wrote that the Rambam's wording contains an error, as the Maggid Mishneh and Bach note. This view is indeed found in Rabbi Ephraim, cited by the Ran and Beit Yosef at the end of siman 89, where he concludes that this certainly appears correct, except that one should not dispute the early authorities.
