He has not lost the migo. The same applies if he seized before witnesses and the item was seen in his possession. If the authorities dispute the law, he has not lost his status as possessor for purposes of saying, "I rely upon the authorities supporting me." Shach.
He may swear in general terms. This applies where there is no practical difference in the mode of payment. But where there is a difference, for example he must pay the other party from superior land while the other owes him only from intermediate land, he may not swear merely in general terms. Likewise, if he holds another person's deposit whose value requires appraisal, he may not swear generally, because who has appraised it? Rather, he must state the facts accurately before beit din: "I have a deposit of yours, but you owe me such and such." Shach.
