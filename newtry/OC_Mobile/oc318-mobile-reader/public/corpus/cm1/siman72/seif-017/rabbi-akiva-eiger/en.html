[Shulchan Aruch] Up to its value, upon an oath. Note: The great Rabbi R. G. of blessed memory writes as follows: Even if he has a supporting witness, that witness does not exempt him from the oath, because he is believed only by virtue of a migo. See siman 87:6 in the Rama. I, in my poverty, did not merit to understand this. There the rule is different, because so long as he has not sworn there is no migo. Here, by contrast, had he claimed that the object was purchased, he would not have been required to swear; an oath was imposed only because he is coming to collect, as the Rosh writes. This should be no stronger than a Torah oath, from which a supporting witness exempts him. This requires study.
