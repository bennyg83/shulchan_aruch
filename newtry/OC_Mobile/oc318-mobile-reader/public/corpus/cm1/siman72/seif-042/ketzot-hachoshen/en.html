With a hesset oath. The Sema writes that although a claim concerning movable collateral ordinarily requires a severe oath, land is different because there is no severe oath concerning land. The Shach objects that a Mishnah oath can apply even to land. In my view the answer follows what I wrote in seif katan 21. Where a migo supports the principal monetary claim, even if an oath would otherwise follow from the resulting legal posture, the migo can also exempt from that oath. The reason movable collateral requires an oath even with migo is that if witnesses came and established the pledge but did not know the amount, the creditor would still swear and collect. Rashba explains this because a creditor acquires a movable pledge. Since even independent witnesses would leave an oath on the amount, the migo does not remove it. With land, however, a creditor does not acquire the collateral in the same way, as shown by the law that movable collateral is not canceled by Shemittah while land collateral can be. Therefore the migo goes to the principal money itself rather than merely to an amount within an acquired pledge, and it can exempt from the stronger oath, leaving only hesset. Rambam rules similarly regarding an artisan who says, "I fixed the wage at two," requiring a severe oath. Rashba, however, holds that if witnesses establish the artisan relationship but not the wage, the artisan does not swear because an artisan does not acquire the object as collateral. Perhaps Rambam disagrees and holds that an artisan does acquire pledge rights, as Rabbeinu Yerucham writes. The sugya in Bava Batra saying an artisan collects only through migo can still be reconciled as I explained in seif katan 25.
