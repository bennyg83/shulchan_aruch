"And he said, 'Damn' admits a little. “And I shall not swear to the land I am the depressant of a lot of Jenners and the land is nothing but a mortgage and so on, as did the S.C. at the end of his words and what he wrote in the window is not true.”
The lender is believed upon taking a heset oath. The Sema explains that although the lender also has a migo that he purchased the land, this case concerns possession of land for three years, and Torah oaths are not administered regarding land, only a heset oath. Years ago I found this difficult, because even Mishnaic oaths may be administered in connection with land, as is explicit in the Gemara and authorities and as the Sema himself writes in siman 95. I initially thought the reason was that the lender takes payment from the produce of the land, and distinguished this from the Sema's explanation; my teacher and other great scholars agreed. Upon further examination, however, the Sema may mean that because no Torah oath applies to land, the Geonic oath imposed upon one who asserts a claim against a pledge likewise does not apply. The same explanation is required below in siman 149:22 according to the Rambam and Mechaber. It is not correct to answer merely that he takes from the produce, because he is still seeking to collect and should require an oath. Nevertheless, the Raavad holds that the Geonic oath applies even to land, and this is also explicit in the Rashba, Baal HaTerumot, Ramban, and Nimukei Yosef. That is the primary view, as explained below in siman 149:22.
And he takes the hundred that he claims from the produce of the land. The Sema asks why the ruling speaks of collecting from the produce rather than from the body of the land while consuming the produce subject to deduction, as in an ordinary mortgage, and says that Baal HaTerumot does not mention the produce. He did not examine the language carefully. Baal HaTerumot uses the same language in recounting the incident of Rabbah bar Sharshom and expressly states that he collects from the produce of the land whatever he claimed. There is no difficulty in why he does not collect the land itself, because the case is one in which even the lender concedes that the borrower may redeem the land immediately. See also simanim 149:21-22 and 150:6.
