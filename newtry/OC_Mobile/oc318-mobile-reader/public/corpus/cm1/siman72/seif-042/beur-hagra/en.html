(Collected note) And he swears a Torah oath. Had he denied everything, he would swear only a shevuas hesses and his oath would be to retain possession, like all defendants who swear and keep their property. He recovers his land before swearing, as the Shach, seif katan 82, explains: because the possessor lacks a chazakah, he must return it, and thereafter his monetary claim resembles an oral claim. (End of note.)
(Collected note) He is believed... Although with a movable pledge the creditor who collects from the pledge swears while holding a sacred object because he is taking payment, here he collects from the produce already in his possession. This is like seif 17 in the gloss, "specifically where he seized an object, but..." Money and produce are alike once he has already taken payment from them. The special oath applies only to a movable pledge that the creditor cannot retain against the borrower's will. This is why the Gemara speaks of "the produce of the land," contrary to the Sema. Rambam and the Shulchan Aruch likewise write in siman 149, seif 22, that he collects from the produce. Since he is already collecting from the produce, if the father were alive he would owe only a shevuas hesses. After the father dies, no shevuas hesses is imposed against an uncertain heir's claim, in accordance with Tosafos and as stated in siman 133, seif 5. This is unlike Maggid Mishneh's distinction between land and movables, and unlike the Sema, because rabbinic oaths can also apply to land, as learned from HaKosev. Rather, because the claimant already possesses the land and seeks satisfaction from its produce, he is not regarded as taking property. The incident of Rabbah bar Sharshum proves this. Tosafos asks that he should at least swear and answers that no shevuas hesses is imposed on an uncertain claim. Yet seif 17 states that one who is taking property must swear even against an uncertain claim. Thus the case here must be treated as retaining, not taking. See siman 150, seif 6. (End of note.)
