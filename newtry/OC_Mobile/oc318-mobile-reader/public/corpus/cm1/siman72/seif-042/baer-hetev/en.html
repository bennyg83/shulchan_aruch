And he shall swear. This means a Torah oath. Although Torah oaths are not ordinarily taken concerning land, as the Tur and Shulchan Aruch state in siman 95, seif 9, this case is different. Since the lender has not yet held the land for three years, it has not left the borrower's ownership, and because the lender has no mortgage deed his claim is like an oral monetary claim. Alternatively, since both parties admit that it is a mortgage and the dispute concerns only the money, this is not considered a claim concerning land. The Rama likewise writes in siman 95, seif 3. The Shach agrees with this latter explanation and writes that the Sema's first explanation is incorrect.
Heset. The Sema writes that although one who claims against a pledge up to its value must swear while holding a sacred object even when he also has a migo of purchase, as explained above in seif 17, here he has held the land for three years, and no Torah oath is taken regarding land, only heset. The Shach writes that for many years he found the Sema's words difficult, because even a Mishnah oath is taken concerning land, as explicit in the Gemara and authorities and as the Sema himself writes in siman 95, seif katan 6. The Shach initially thought the reason here was that the lender takes from the fruits of the land, and his teacher and other great scholars agreed. Upon reconsideration, however, he explains that the Sema means: because no Torah oath applies to land, the Geonic oath imposed on one who claims against a pledge likewise does not apply. The same understanding is required in siman 149, seif 22, according to the Mechaber. The explanation that he takes from the fruits is not correct, since ultimately he is collecting and should require an oath. This is also shown by the Raavad's gloss to chapter 14 of Hilchot To'en and by the Maggid Mishneh and other authorities. Nevertheless, the Raavad there holds that the Geonic oath does apply even to land, and this is also explicit in a responsum of the Rashba and appears from the Ramban and Nimukei Yosef. This is the primary view. See below siman 149, seifim 21 and 22, and siman 150, seif 4. These are the words of the Shach.
