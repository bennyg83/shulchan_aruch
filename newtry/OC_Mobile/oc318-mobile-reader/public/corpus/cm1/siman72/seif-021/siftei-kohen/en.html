He is not believed to say that it was purchased, etc. It appears that this applies where there are witnesses who saw the object in his possession, because the law of these objects is like that of objects normally lent or rented. But where there are no witnesses who presently saw it in his possession, since he is believed to say, "This never occurred," or, "I returned it," he is likewise believed to say, "I purchased it," or, "I hold it as a pledge," by virtue of that migo. This appears correct to me.
And if he possessed them for three years, etc. This wording appears to include an animal as well, so that three years of chazakah are effective even for an animal. The Sema writes that the Mechaber relied on the ruling below in siman 135 that three years of chazakah do not establish ownership of an animal, following the Rambam and Baal HaItur. Below in siman 135, however, I write the opposite: the primary conclusion of the Gemara and poskim is that three years of chazakah are effective even for an animal, and that this is also the view of the Rambam, Baal HaItur, and the Mechaber, as appears from the Mechaber's words here. See there.
And he shall take a hesset oath that he purchased them from him or that he delivered them to him for his debt. Meaning that they were given in payment so that they should become his property. But if he claims, "I have held them as a pledge throughout the three years," he must swear while holding a sacred object. The Bach writes this.
