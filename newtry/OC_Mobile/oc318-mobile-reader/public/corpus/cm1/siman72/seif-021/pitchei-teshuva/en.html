Until his fellow gives him releases. See Responsa Shevut Yaakov, part II, siman 170, concerning Reuven, who held in escrow a document against Shimon. Shimon demanded return of the document, and Reuven answered that he was withholding it because long ago he had paid Shimon a debt on another document, but Shimon did not return that earlier document and claimed that it had been lost. Reuven therefore demanded a release and receipt regarding that earlier document. Shimon answered that he had in fact returned it. At first glance, the law appears explicit in the Rama to siman 72, seif 17, from Responsa Mahariv, that one cannot seize property merely to obtain releases, because we do not say a migo from a monetary claim to a non monetary matter. Upon closer examination, however, Shevut Yaakov writes that this law is not sufficiently clear to extract from the party in possession where he has a migo. One can argue that all the more so a migo from money to a non monetary matter should be effective. In addition, in the present case, since the primary dispute concerns an escrowed document, the migo is from one document to another document, which is effective like a migo from one monetary claim to another, as the Shach concludes in siman 82 in the laws of migo. Furthermore, Shimon admits that the prior debt is no longer owed and merely claims that he returned the earlier document. What harm is there in requiring him to give a release? Shevut Yaakov therefore concludes that Reuven may withhold the escrowed document until he receives the releases, and he ruled this way in practice. In part III, siman 143, he discusses a case where Reuven orally sued Shimon for one hundred gold coins. Shimon responded: It is true that I owe you, but I have certain rights and documents in your possession in which I have rights, and I am withholding your money until you return my rights. Shevut Yaakov writes that Shimon may certainly withhold the money and say that Reuven holds a document in which he has rights, by virtue of a migo that he could have denied the debt. He had already proved this in part II, siman 170, and at the beginning of his treatise on migo. See there.
