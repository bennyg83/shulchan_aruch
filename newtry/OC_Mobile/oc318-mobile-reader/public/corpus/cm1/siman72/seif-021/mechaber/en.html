A possessor's presumption permits a claim up to an item's value only for something that cannot move independently and ordinarily remains under its owner's control, such as movable property, a young servant unable to walk, or an animal kept under a shepherd's watch. Since it was protected by its owner and is now held by another, the possessor is believed up to its value. For adult servants and animals not entrusted to a shepherd but wandering and grazing independently, the possessor is not believed to say, 'I purchased it,' or, 'It is collateral.' When the owner brings witnesses of ownership, the servant or animal is returned, and the owner swears that he neither sold nor pledged it. Possession is ineffective because they move from place to place on their own and remain legally in the owner's domain wherever they are. If the possessor held them for three years and has witnesses to that fact, he is believed upon a heses oath that he purchased them or that the owner delivered them as payment of a debt.
