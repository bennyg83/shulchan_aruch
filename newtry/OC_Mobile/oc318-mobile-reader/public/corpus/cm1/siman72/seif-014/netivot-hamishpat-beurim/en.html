The borrower swears against the witness. See Shach, seif katan 59, who identifies this as an oath of the Mishnah. Tumim objects that the creditor has only a lien in the collateral, so when the creditor asserts a claim against the borrower supported by one witness, the borrower should take a d'oraisa oath. In my view Shach is correct. Since the creditor cannot collect the debt from the collateral without satisfying the governing rules, while the borrower seeks return of the collateral, the borrower is in substance swearing in order to recover property held under the creditor's lien. The collateral is treated as acquired by the creditor to the extent that he need not return it until paid, as explained above in siman 66 and below in this siman. The borrower therefore swears in order to extract the lien from the other party's possession and has the status of one who swears and takes. This is evident from the converse. Where the oath is imposed upon the borrower simply to defend against collection, it is only a shevuat heset, as Shach writes at the end of siman 71, and he is treated as one who swears and is exempt. Here, because his oath is needed to recover the collateral, he is one who swears and takes.
