For the borrower does not know, etc. If the borrower asserts with certainty that the collateral was not lost through unavoidable accident, the borrower is exempt even where he has no migo. See what I wrote above in seif katan 15.
The borrower is believed, etc. He need not swear at all; only a general ban is proclaimed against one who lies. Shach.
And if there is one witness, etc. This refers to a case where the lender himself does not claim certainty. If the lender claims with certainty and one witness supports him, he is exempt without an oath, because without the witness the lender would have been required to swear, and a supporting witness exempts him from that oath. Shach, and this is clear.
The borrower swears against the witness. The Shach holds that this is only a Mishnah level rabbinic oath, and therefore the borrower may, if he wishes, return the collateral and later bring a new claim that will generate a Torah oath. In Tumim I wrote that this oath is immediately a Torah oath. See there.
