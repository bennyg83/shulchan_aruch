The borrower swears. He takes the Mishnah oath and recovers the collateral. This applies specifically where the borrower is suing the lender for return of his collateral and the lender does not presently seek to collect anything. The borrower is then one who swears and collects. But if the lender is the claimant and comes to collect from the collateral, the borrower swears a Torah oath. Since the lender has only a lien upon the collateral, the borrower swears a Torah oath and is exempt according to the view that collateral is not considered an immediate tender. See Beurim, seif katan 28.
