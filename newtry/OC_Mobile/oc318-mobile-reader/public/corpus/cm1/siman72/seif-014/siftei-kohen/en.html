For the borrower does not, etc. It follows that if the borrower makes a definite claim, saying, "I know that it was not lost through an unavoidable accident," the lender collects nothing by means of his oath. Rather, the borrower takes a hesset oath and is exempt. See what I wrote above in seif katan 14.
The borrower is believed, etc. The lender may impose upon him only an unspecified cherem, as explained above in siman 59. This also appears from Responsa Rashba, siman 976.
And if there is one witness, etc. This is where the lender himself does not know. But if the lender makes a definite claim and has one witness supporting his version against that of the borrower, then, since even without the witness the lender would have sworn and collected, the single witness now serves as a supporting witness and exempts him from the oath. He therefore collects without an oath, as I write below in siman 87, seif 6, in the name of Maharam. See there.
The borrower swears against the witness and takes the pledge. This is the oath of the Mishnah applicable to those who swear and collect. If the lender wishes, however, he may first return the pledge and afterward require the borrower to take a Torah oath because of the single witness, according to our ruling in siman 75, seif 23 that an oath may be imposed on an uncertain claim when it is supported by a witness, as I explain below in siman 121, seif katan 54. This appears to me from the Responsa Rashba cited by the Beit Yosef in his twenty fourth additional note concerning a borrower of an object, and the same applies here to a lender. See carefully.
