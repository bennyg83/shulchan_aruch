The borrower swears against the witness. The Shach says this means the special oath of one who takes money. If he wishes, the creditor may first return the pledge and then sue the borrower on the basis of the witness, compelling a Torah oath. He follows Responsa Rashba, where Shimon borrowed an object from Reuven, the object was lost, Shimon said it was worth a sela, one witness said it was worth only a shekel, and Reuven did not know. Rashba says Shimon swears against the witness and collects, which is indeed an oath by one taking money because Shimon seeks to recover value. Our case is different. Here the pledge is already in the borrower's possession and belongs to him. He is not extracting anything through the oath. He says in effect, "I do not know whether I owe you." The creditor has one witness supporting the claim. The borrower therefore takes the ordinary Torah oath contradicting that witness and is exempt. The pledge is not treated as money he is collecting, because it is his own property already in his possession. Accordingly, the oath here is a full Torah oath without any difficulty.
