I am certain. Meaning, I am certain that its depreciation resulted from negligence.
And he swears a shevuas hesses. The oath of bailees applies only where the owner of the deposit does not know what occurred. See the Shach. The case is where the pledge is not worth more than the debt.
(Collected note) And he swears a shevuas hesses... The oath of bailees applies to a claim of uncertainty. With a definite claim, the case is governed by the ordinary rules of monetary claims. See Tosafos Bava Kamma 107a, where the poskim accept the explanation of the Ri, as stated at the beginning of siman 87. (End of note.)
(Collected note) A pledge... and he swears a shevuas hesses... The Sema writes that the Torah did not obligate him to swear, etc., and Beer HaGolah cites him, but this is mistaken. If so, why does seif 30 say, "the same law applies if it depreciated..."? There would be no Torah oath and no rule of one who is obligated to swear but cannot do so and therefore pays. See Sema, seif katan 38, and siman 75, seif 14. (End of note.)
(Collected note) And he is exempt. He collects his entire debt, yet needs only a shevuas hesses because his claim concerns the body of the pledge. This is the meaning of "and he is exempt." (End of note.)
And if he admits. As explained above in seif 2. Certainly where there was negligence, he is liable even beyond the amount of the debt, even according to the opinion that a lender is a gratuitous bailee in that case.
And if there is a dispute. Everything follows the principles stated above: the oath is imposed upon the party in possession.
(Collected note) And if there is... This refers to the immediately preceding clause, "and if he admits..." (End of note.)
(Collected note) For the pledge... See the Shach's question from siman 87, where a pledge is ruled not to be "here it is" payment, and Rambam, chapter 4 of Hilchos To'en, says so even where money was lent upon it. He overlooked that here the lender gives the pledge to the borrower in satisfaction of the debt, as explained above, and then claims an additional five. The same applies below where he asks an additional ten. See the Sema there. Although the Shach disagrees that the creditor can be compelled to take the pledge as payment, he concedes that the case can be where the creditor agrees to do so. See his seifim ketanim 119 and 122. (End of note.)
(Collected note) And he deducts. It should read "and he credits," meaning he credits the borrower five from the value of the pledge and retains the remainder for himself. (End of note.)
He is believed with an oath. With an oath while holding a sacred object.
(Collected note) He is believed with an oath. The purpose here is only to explain why he is believed. All these oaths are merely shevuas hesses, as stated subsequently and at the beginning of the siman. Since the parties do not dispute the loan at all, only the condition of the pledge itself, only a shevuas hesses is needed. This is contrary to the Shach's lengthy discussion here. (End of note.)
A shevuas hesses. Regarding the phrase "I do not know," the Taz emends it to "I do not know whether it depreciated," meaning whether there was any depreciation at all. Otherwise he would be one obligated to swear who cannot swear and therefore must pay. See seif 12.
(Collected note) I do not know how much... Even though he admits some depreciation, this is not the case of one obligated to swear who cannot do so, because the amount he admits is satisfied by returning or crediting the pledge itself. The pledge is a deposit in his hands, as the Mishnah says, "Who swears? The one in whose possession the deposit is..." This counts as "here it is," as Maggid Mishneh explains. See siman 87, Beer HaGolah, seif katan 70, and the proof from Bava Metzia 5a. (End of note.)
