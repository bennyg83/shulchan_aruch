(Shach, seif katan 68) And they also saw it now in his possession, so he has no migo. Note: See Mishneh LaMelech, Hilchot Malveh 13:3, who explains that in such a case the lender is not believed to collect even with an oath. The Rambam's statement that if there are witnesses that this object is held by him as a pledge refers to witnesses who saw the delivery of the pledge but did not see it presently in his hand. The lender then has a migo that he could have claimed, "I returned it." The Rambam follows his own view that one who claims return must take a severe oath. Consequently, there is no migo with respect to the oath itself. This is why the Rambam needed his rule that a migo to exempt from an oath is not accepted only where there were no witnesses at all and the lender has a migo even regarding the oath, because had he claimed purchase he would not have had to swear. According to the Mishneh LaMelech, this is also the Mechaber's view here and below in seif 23. Nevertheless, the Rambam's wording, "witnesses that this object is a pledge in his hand," fits the Shach's interpretation better. According to the Mishneh LaMelech, who says that the witnesses did not see it presently in his hand, the phrase "a pledge in his hand" is unnecessary. Wherever a claimant has a migo that he returned the property, he is believed even if the witnesses do not know that it came into his hands as a pledge.
