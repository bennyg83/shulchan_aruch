(Seif 20) “Judgment favors the owner of the utensil.” The Beit Yosef explains that a possessor is not believed through a migo beyond the content of his actual claim. He admits that the utensil is not his but only a pledge, so how can his possession become stronger than his own admission? Even if he says, “I originally lent you more upon it, and although the amount exceeds its appraised value I prize it and wish to keep it,” the rule is difficult. The Gemara says a pledge-holder may claim only up to its value, while one view here allows more than its value. The Gemara can be read as dealing with a case where both parties’ asserted loan amounts remain below the object’s value; there the holder prevails up to the full value. It does not address a claim exceeding value based on special personal attachment. Nevertheless, this ruling appears limited to an object requiring expert appraisal, as indicated by the phrase “according to the appraisal of experts.” For an object whose price is fixed and commonly known, one cannot claim more than its value. See seif 35 below and my further difficulty concerning the claim “it is dear to me” in siman 109, seif 2.
