He shall not pay. The Shach and Taz agree that if an ordinary sword is never worth nearly so much, the custodian had no reason to anticipate that this particular sword was extremely valuable and is exempt from that excess. He must pay only the ordinary value of an average sword. Specifically where he should have anticipated that an item might be worth that much is he liable. This will be explained in the laws of custodians. If he informed the borrower that the object belonged to a gentile, the borrower is liable even for extraordinary claims made by the gentile, provided it can be proved by clear evidence that the claim arose because of this very loan, as in siman 131 concerning one who guaranteed a gentile on behalf of another Jew. See Beurim, seif katan 24.
