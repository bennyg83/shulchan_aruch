He is a paid custodian. In the glosses of Rabbi Akiva Eiger to Shulchan Aruch it is written that if the pledge was delivered before the loan, he does not become a paid custodian until the loan is actually made, as stated below in seif 52. See Knesset HaGedolah, Hagahot Beit Yosef, entry 20, in the name of Maharashach, part II, siman 169, who distinguishes between an ordinary paid custodian who receives direct compensation for guarding and a pledge holder considered paid only because of Rav Yosef's perutah benefit. In the latter case, if he can truthfully say, 'What more could I have done?' he is exempt even without an express stipulation. Mishneh LaMelech, chapter 10 of Hilchos Sechirus, law 1, challenges this distinction, while Ketzos HaChoshen discusses it. Responsa Chatam Sofer, Choshen Mishpat, siman 16, appears to agree with Maharashach and extends the same reasoning to custodians treated as paid only by presumed benefit, such as enhanced reputation or having been selected over others. Such indirect benefit does not obligate the extraordinary level of guarding applicable to direct payment. He finds support in Bava Metzia 93a and suggests this is the view of the Raavad cited by the Rosh. See there, and see what I write below in siman 303, seif katan 4.
