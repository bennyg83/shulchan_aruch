Even by merely granting permission. See Shach, seif katan 31. His words are somewhat difficult. At the beginning he questions Rama because according to the opinion that a creditor holding collateral is only an unpaid custodian, permission to use it should not automatically make him a borrower. Although Sema in siman 264 writes that permission can raise a custodian by one level, here everyone agrees that at least up to the amount of the debt the creditor is a paid custodian, so perhaps permission should raise him to liability for unavoidable accidents at least to that amount. Shach, however, follows his own view there that this "one level" principle is not compelling. According to the view that he is an unpaid custodian, the Mishnah does not prove that permission to use makes him a borrower, and he may remain exempt from theft and loss. Shach then explains the word "perhaps" in Rashba. Since Rashba himself holds that a custodian of a lost object is a paid custodian, one might think he must hold that permission to use produces borrower liability, so why speak only tentatively? Shach therefore reads the Mechaber's "perhaps" as reflecting the uncertainty. Yet the matter remains difficult. The loan already occurred. Why should prior benefit prevent the creditor from becoming a borrower when later given free permission to use the borrower's object? If Reuven lends an object to Shimon for a period and during that period Shimon lends an object to Reuven in a case without shemirah bebaalim, or first gives Reuven a gift, Reuven can still become a borrower of the later object. It should make no difference whether the earlier benefit was a loan of an object or a loan of money. The true uncertainty therefore concerns acceptance. If the borrower merely grants permission and the creditor remains silent, does that silence constitute acceptance of borrower status? The same uncertainty applies to an unpaid or paid custodian who is granted permission and remains silent, as Tumim also writes. If the custodian expressly protests when permission is granted, he certainly does not become a borrower. It further appears that the case assumes the borrower expressly said, "Use it free of charge." If he merely said, "Use it," one could argue that he meant for payment. Although siman 363 rules that "reside in my courtyard" ordinarily means gratis, Beit Yosef in Yoreh Deah siman 166 writes that where a borrower tells a lender to reside in his courtyard we construe it as for payment, because we do not presume people to act wickedly by arranging ribbit. The same reasoning applies here. Sema in siman 246 also explains that "reside there gratis" is inferred specifically where one person benefits and the other suffers no loss. Movables depreciate through use, so the owner suffers loss and the case resembles "eat with me," where payment is due.
