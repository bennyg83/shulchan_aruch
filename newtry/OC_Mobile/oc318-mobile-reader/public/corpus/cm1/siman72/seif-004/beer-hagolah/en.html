In my humble opinion, this is so even though the borrower does not receive every benefit, since the owner lent him the object because of the loan. Nevertheless, the Beit Yosef accepts the responsum's reasoning that he becomes a borrower. Even without permission to use the collateral he would be a paid bailee over it, and once permission to use it is added, his status is elevated as though all benefit were his. The Ran writes in Responsa, at the end of siman 20, concerning Reuven who lent Shimon a sefer and Shimon left another sefer with Reuven. Both books were taken from them through circumstances beyond their control, and afterward the sefer Shimon had given Reuven as collateral was returned to Reuven. The Ran ruled that Shimon is not liable for the unavoidable loss because Reuven received a mitzvah benefit by lending him his sefer. Reuven must return Shimon's sefer, and Shimon is exempt because the loss from his hand occurred through unavoidable circumstances.
