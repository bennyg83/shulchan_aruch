[Shulchan Aruch] He is a paid custodian. Note: If the collateral was delivered to him before the loan, he does not become a paid custodian of the collateral until he actually lends the money. See below, siman 32.
He is a paid custodian. Note further that Mishneh LaMelech, Hilchot Sechirut 10:1, writes in the name of Maharashach that in this case we do not apply the rule that the depositor may say, “I gave it to you so that you should guard it with superior care.” Where the custodian receives no payment except the benefit of Rabbi Yosef’s perutah, he may say, “I guarded it as ordinary people guard.” See Mishneh LaMelech there, who disagrees and derives his position from Responsa Maharikash. In my humble opinion, Maharashach’s view is clearly proven by Tosafot, Bava Kamma 57a, s.v. Kegon Sheta’anu. According to Rabbah, who exempts a custodian for theft occurring at the time when people ordinarily enter their homes, Tosafot implies that according to Abaye the Gemara’s objection is resolved. Chiddushei HaRashba explicitly gives this answer to Tosafot’s difficulty, namely that Abaye is the one who challenges Rav Yosef, and Abaye follows his own view that one is liable even where the theft occurred at the time when people ordinarily enter their homes. See there. That passage concerns a custodian of a lost object, who is a paid custodian solely because of Rabbi Yosef’s perutah, yet Tosafot and the Rashba make clear that according to Abaye he is liable even at the time when people ordinarily enter, like any other paid custodian. This is a clear proof in my humble opinion. One may also infer this somewhat from the Sema, siman 263, seif katan 35.
