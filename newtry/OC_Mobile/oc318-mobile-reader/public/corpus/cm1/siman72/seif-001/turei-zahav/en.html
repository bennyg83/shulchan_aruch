(Shulchan Aruch, seif 1) “Because of suspicion.” The Beit Yosef asks about the Gemara’s dispute concerning a lender who needs the pledge, which Rashi explains as needing to use it while deducting the value from the debt. He answers that there the parties stipulated this from the outset and the loan was made on that basis, whereas here the lender acts without the owner’s consent. At first glance this implies that the stipulation must be made specifically at the time of the loan, as the words “from the outset” suggest, and so the Sema understands. But this is difficult. The Beit Yosef concludes only that use without the owner’s consent is forbidden. If a post-loan stipulation were ineffective, use would remain forbidden even with consent. Moreover, a lender on a house may occupy it while deducting rent, even if the deduction arrangement was made after the loan; no authority requires that it be stipulated at the loan’s inception. The Mishna in Bava Metzia likewise discusses a lender who may not live in the borrower’s courtyard, and Tosafot allow occupation with deductions even though the discussion concerns a post-loan arrangement. It therefore appears that “from the outset” means before beginning to use the pledge. The Beit Yosef’s words that “he lent on this understanding” describe the Gemara’s case as proof that a prior stipulation removes suspicion, but the same applies if the stipulation is made after the loan and before use. This is also the Rama’s intent in saying, “if he stipulated with the borrower initially,” meaning before use. A further question arises from the Mishna permitting one to rent out a poor person’s pledge because this is like returning a lost object. Regarding found property that must be sold, the Tur cites the Rosh that the finder may take it for himself, and the Beit Yosef explains that one returning a lost object is not suspected. We must say that the comparison is only to permit rental, not to remove all suspicion. The Mishna concerning deposited produce that is deteriorating supports this: Rabban Shimon ben Gamliel permits sale through beit din because it is like returning a lost object, yet sale through beit din is still required. Likewise here “he may rent it” means to others, because renting it to himself would create suspicion.
