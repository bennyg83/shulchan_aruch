Like. The Sema notes that the text carefully says "like interest," because actual ribbit exists only where the principal loan itself is increased.
Without. Ordinarily the owner is pleased with such use, and the user is like one returning lost property. The Tur concludes that with other items whose depreciation is substantial, one may not rent them out without the owner's knowledge. Sema.
Suspicion. The Sema explains that people may suspect him of using the pledged item as a benefit of the loan without deducting the value of the use. The Tur brings proof. This also answers why, in siman 267, seif 22, a finder may appraise a lost article for himself without beit din and is not suspected. Here there is a special concern that the lender's true intent is to use the pledge without deduction. The Shach distinguishes that there the finder has already performed the mitzvah of returning the lost item by bringing it into his house, so he is not suspected of theft, whereas here the lender is only now purporting to act beneficially when renting it to himself, so there is room for suspicion.
Permitted. This is only where the value of the use is deducted from the debt; otherwise there is a prohibition of ribbit. The Shach nevertheless questions the Rama's ruling where the borrower expressly stipulated permission, because according to the Tur there may still be suspicion among those who know that the item is pledged but do not know about the stipulation.
Hand. From that point onward, even if the books are lost through unavoidable accident while in his possession, he is liable. If the parties dispute whether they were damaged through his study, he is not believed merely by oath to exempt himself; rather, the pledgor swears as to the amount of depreciation and collects. This applies only when witnesses saw him study from them. Otherwise he is believed upon oath through a migo that he could have said he never studied from them, since a person does not make himself a robber and invalid witness by his own admission. The Sema further attempts to reconcile the Rama here with his ruling in Yoreh Deah 172:1. The Shach and Taz disagree and regard the two rulings as distinct opinions, noting that the present discussion does not primarily concern the prohibition of ribbit.
Mitzvah. At the end of siman 292 the Rama rules that where books are deposited with a Torah scholar, he may read and copy from them because they were deposited with him on that understanding. That is limited to a deposit. With a pledge, even a Torah scholar may not use the book because of concern for ribbit. All this applies where the book came into his possession with the owner's knowledge. To enter another person's home and read his books without permission is forbidden even for a Torah scholar and even without concern for ribbit, because the owner may object that his book could be torn. The Rama rules likewise in Orach Chayyim 14. The Shach discusses the Rama's statement there that a tallit, tefillin, and similar mitzvah objects may be taken even without the owner's knowledge, noting that this does not appear consistent with the primary authorities.
