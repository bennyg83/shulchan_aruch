(Shach, seif katan 9) In the passage beginning "and also that of chapter HaSholeach," etc.; if so, with a pledge it is acquired by him immediately when the Sabbatical year begins. Note: This requires study. If the due date arrived before the beginning of the Sabbatical year, the Shach's lengthy discussion was unnecessary, because at the due date the lender can certainly collect the pledge in his possession for the debt through beit din, and he may therefore execute judgment for himself and acquire the pledge for the debt, as the Shach reasons. But if the debt does not become due until during the Sabbatical year itself, by what right can the lender make an acquisition in the pledge without the borrower's consent? If the reason is fear that the debt will be lost through shemittah, the Shach himself writes in siman 73, seif katan 34, that such a case is not comparable to one who is dissipating his property. Even where a person is dissipating most of his property, the majority view is that neither the lender nor beit din can compel the borrower before the due date even to provide a pledge. All the more so, the lender cannot make the pledge his own.
