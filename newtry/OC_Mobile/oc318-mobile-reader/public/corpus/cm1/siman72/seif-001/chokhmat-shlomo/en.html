Seif 4. One who lends on collateral and gives the lender permission to use it, etc. See Sema and Shach citing Ran regarding one who borrows a sefer and gives another sefer as collateral. Ran writes that he is not treated as a borrower with all benefit on his side because the owner derives the benefit of the perutah of Rav Yosef. This is very difficult in light of Bava Metzia 94b. The Gemara says that a borrower, although all benefit is his, pays only principal. It then asks whether all benefit is truly his, since he must feed the animal, and answers where the animal stands in pasture. It asks that he still must guard it, and answers where there is a city watchman. A second answer says not that all benefit is his, but that most benefit is his. The Gemara therefore establishes that an ordinary borrower need not receive literally every benefit. At most, most of the benefit must be his. Even according to the first answer, the Gemara merely identifies a particular case in which all benefit is his; it does not deny that in ordinary cases, where he bears food and guarding expenses, he remains a borrower liable for accidents despite not having all benefit. Thus the mere fact that the lender gains some incidental benefit does not remove the borrower’s status. The reciprocal-loan case of “lend me and I will lend you” is different because each party gives the other a substantial corresponding benefit, so even most of the benefit may not belong to the borrower. But where the only benefit to the owner is the small perutah of Rav Yosef, why should that be stronger than the borrower’s ordinary expenses of feeding and guarding, which do not prevent liability for accidents? Especially with a sefer, where there may be no other benefit to the owner beyond that perutah, the distinction is difficult. This Gemara therefore requires substantial analysis.
