He authorized me to study from them. In my view the case is one where Levi merely says that Shimon authorized him personally to study from the books. If, however, he says that Shimon told him Reuven had authorized any person who lent money upon them to use them, he is exempt, even without a deduction from the loan, as explained above. More fundamentally, if Reuven authorized Shimon to use the books and Shimon in turn authorized Levi, Levi should be exempt. The Ir Shushan writes that Shimon has no power to extend the permission to another and that Levi is therefore one who uses the property without authorization. I disagree. Since Shimon himself had a lawful right of use, his authorization to Levi is effective at least where such delegation is within the ordinary scope of the permission and does not increase the risk. Liability arises only where the original permission was personal or where the second use exceeds its terms.
