If the borrower admits that the ring in the money changer's possession is equal in value to his own, etc. This entire seif concerns a case in which it is known that the ring presently in the money changer's possession is worth more than the debt, and the lender admits that the lost ring was at least no worse than that ring. Therefore, in the first case, where the borrower likewise says that the lost ring was not worth more than the existing ring, the lender pays the excess over the debt according to the appraised value of this ring. In the middle case, the lender is one who admits part of the claim, because he too admits that the lost ring was worth more than the debt. In the final case, where the lender says, 'I do not know,' meaning that he does not know whether it was worth more or not, yet at minimum it was worth as much as this ring, the rule is that one who cannot swear must pay.
