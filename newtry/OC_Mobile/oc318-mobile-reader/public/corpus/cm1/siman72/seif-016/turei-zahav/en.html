(If witnesses testify that he guarded it, Shimon is exempt.) Why then does the borrower not remain liable for the debt, as seif 2 rules when a pledge is lost through unavoidable accident? The word “accident” here is not a complete unavoidable accident. It is the opposite of negligence mentioned earlier: the witnesses say he guarded properly, so the theft was accidental in the sense that he was not negligent. In that case, although a lender on a pledge is a paid custodian, he loses the amount of his debt. If there was a true unavoidable accident, however, the borrower must pay the entire debt. The Bach says that even when witnesses establish a complete accident the borrower is exempt from the debt because the creditor himself does not know what happened, like one who says, “I lent you, but I do not know whether you repaid me.” This is incorrect. Even where a creditor is uncertain whether he was paid, clear witnesses establish liability. The exemption in siman 59 applies when only the instrument supports the creditor, not when witnesses establish the facts. Here too, if witnesses establish a complete accident, or if Reuven trusts the creditor and the creditor swears to it, the borrower certainly must pay the debt.
