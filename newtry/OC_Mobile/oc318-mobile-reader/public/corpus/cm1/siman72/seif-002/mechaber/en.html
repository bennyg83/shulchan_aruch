One who lends upon collateral is a paid custodian, whether he lent money or produce and whether he took the collateral at the time of the loan or afterward. {Rama: It makes no difference whether he expressly accepted the collateral for its full value or accepted it without specifying.} Therefore, if the collateral is lost or stolen, the creditor is liable for its value. If it equaled the debt, neither party owes the other. If the debt exceeds the collateral, the borrower pays the difference. {Rama: Some say that if the lender expressly accepted the collateral for the entire debt, then even if he accepted a sickle handle for one thousand zuz, loss of the collateral cancels everything.} If the collateral exceeds the debt, the lender pays the surplus. If it is lost through unavoidable accident, such as armed robbery, the lender swears to the accident and the owner pays the debt in full. {Rama: Some say a lender upon collateral has only an unpaid custodian's liability in this respect: if the collateral exceeds the debt, the lender does not pay the surplus but merely loses his loan. This is indeed evident as the Rosh's view in several places, and money is not removed when the law is doubtful. If it was lost or stolen through negligence, everyone agrees that he must pay. See also siman 40.}
