(Ibid.: There is no distinction among books.) The Sema explains the Rama’s ruling in Yoreh Deah that one may lend on books as applying only when use was stipulated at the time of the loan. I do not understand this. An express stipulation at the loan is more problematic, for the Beit Yosef cites the Rashba that if the lender stipulated from the outset that he could study from the books, this is fixed interest. There is no contradiction: the Rama there follows the Mahari Weil and the Agudah and does not accept the Rashbam and Raavyah. Here the subject is not interest but whether unauthorized use constitutes misappropriation. The Sema’s statement that even a Torah scholar faces a prohibition with a pledge because of suspicion of interest is difficult. The glossator suggests that “suspicion” is a scribal error for “concern,” as copied by the Shach; then there is no difficulty, because interest remains forbidden even with the borrower’s permission. I also do not know what compelled the Sema to distinguish a deposit from a pledge for a Torah scholar. Whoever permits use of deposited books by a Torah scholar should likewise permit use of pledged books, apart from the separate law of interest. The Rama in siman 292 follows an authority who stated the law for a deposit, and the same reasoning applies to a pledge. At the end of siman 292 I proved from the Mordechai that one who holds a Torah scholar’s books is a paid custodian by virtue of the benefit of studying from them. Even the Rama, who disputes the Mechaber, appears to agree when the scholar lacks comparable books of his own, for only then does he derive actual benefit. The same should apply to a pledge. In any event, the Mechaber already rules that every lender holding a pledge is a paid custodian.
