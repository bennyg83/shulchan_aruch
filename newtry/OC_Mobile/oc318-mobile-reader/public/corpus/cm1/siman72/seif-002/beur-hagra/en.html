(Collected note) Whether he took the pledge after... This is unlike Rashi, who holds that Rabbi Yitzchak's acquisition makes him liable even for unavoidable accidents. The Gemara sought to establish the dispute of Rabbi Eliezer and Rabbi Akiva, and the Mishnah, according to Rabbi Yitzchak. In Bava Kamma 36b, one who seized an animal becomes a paid bailee, not an owner liable for accidents. Likewise an artisan who acquires the improvement in the object remains only a paid bailee. We do not find that he may use the object or is liable for accidents. The passage in Makkos 16a can be explained where he actively destroyed the pledge. See Tosafos there and Ramban in Milchamos at the end of HaDayanim. Rambam also follows this view, as in siman 97, seif 14. The Shach explains otherwise, that where the creditor unlawfully took the pledge the Torah made him more fully responsible. This is a sound explanation, especially according to the Rif's reading. Still, Rambam's language is best understood like Ramban. This also resolves Tosafos' question in Bava Metzia 115, s.v. v'chayav. It supports the Rif and Rambam's ruling concerning fulfillment of the positive command. Rambam's wording in chapter 3 of Hilchos Malveh, halachah 4, nevertheless requires substantial study, as Maggid Mishneh already notes. The Shach's reading appears from that passage, but the overall law still requires Ramban's distinction. (End of note.)
