Seif 5. One whose property was taken as collateral by communal trustees, etc. See the Shach citing Ir Shushan that even if the community already paid the ruler, the debt does not become a debt owed to the community. At first glance this contradicts the rule in siman 4 concerning when the community is considered in possession against an individual. There the distinction is that before the community has appeased the king, it is considered in possession, but once it has already paid the king it is no longer considered so. That seems to show the opposite: after the community pays, the debt is now owed to the community itself and not to the king, rather than treating the community merely as one collecting the king’s debt. It therefore appears that the cited ruling cannot be correct in that broad form. Once the community has already paid the ruler, the communal trustees should at least be treated as paid custodians of the collateral. This also explains the Sema’s reading of the gloss. The Shach explains that the custom mentioned there concerns a case after the community has already paid the ruler. But if payment itself creates the relevant responsibility by law, no special custom would be needed. The custom must therefore concern the different circumstance identified by the Sema. The matter still requires study.
