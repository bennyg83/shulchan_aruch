See Shach, seif katan 51. The Shach rules in siman 72 that where the depositor does not know that the custodian is himself uncertain how much was deposited, the custodian is one who is obligated to swear but cannot swear, and therefore pays. Urim VeTumim explains there that the special enactment made for a robbed claimant does not apply to collateral, while a deposit may be treated more stringently. His language implies that a sealed and tied package is more lenient because the custodian could reasonably be unaware of its precise contents. Where the item was not sealed, there is greater reason to expect knowledge. This is especially so with loose money that the custodian was permitted to use, for he may have exchanged the coins and therefore should know the amount. The more plausible it is that he actually knew, the stronger the rule becomes; with a sealed package the case is correspondingly more lenient.
