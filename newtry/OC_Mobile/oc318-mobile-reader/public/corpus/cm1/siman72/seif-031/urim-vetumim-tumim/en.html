Yet in siman 291 Rama rules from Maharam in Mordechai that a depositor who returns a deposit to the depositor's wife is exempt. Sema distinguishes that a borrower receives all the benefit and therefore bears a stricter duty of return. A lender on collateral likewise has a strong proprietary relationship to the pledge. Shach finds this distinction strained and also questions Bach's distinction between a wife who regularly manages the household and one who does not. The real reconciliation is different. Maharam's rule regarding return to the depositor's wife is connected to the principle that a depositor ordinarily entrusts property with the understanding that the custodian may rely on his own household members. But that principle alone does not necessarily mean that return to a family member counts as direct return to the owner. Rather, the first custodian has delivered the object to a legally competent second custodian whom the owner is ordinarily willing to trust. This is not negligent transfer. Maharam himself follows Rambam that an unpaid first custodian who properly transfers the deposit to a reliable second custodian can be exempt even though the technical return to the owner has not yet occurred. That explains the deposit case. A paid custodian or borrower is different. If he transfers the object to the owner's wife, the technical obligation of return may still not be complete, while the wife ordinarily has only the status of an unpaid custodian. The first custodian has reduced the level of protection from paid or borrower-level responsibility to ordinary unpaid custody. That can itself be negligent and leave the first custodian liable. Thus the difference is not that the wife is or is not considered the owner. It is the continuing custodial level. Even according to authorities who dispute Rambam regarding a first custodian's liability for a second custodian, the first custodian can plead kim li where the transfer was to the owner's own wife and no unusual risk existed. And the concern that people might manipulate the rule by transferring to their own relatives is much weaker when the recipient is the owner's spouse. This reconciles Rama's rulings cleanly.
