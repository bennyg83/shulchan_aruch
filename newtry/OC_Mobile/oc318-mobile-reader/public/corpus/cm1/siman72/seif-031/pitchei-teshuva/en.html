Shimon is negligent. See Shach, seif katan 156, through the words, “and there is no proof from there, for he also wrote there: or he gave him a gift corresponding to the debt.” It is obvious that if he gave him an absolute gift corresponding to the debt, the debt is discharged. The Shach therefore says that this was stated merely by way of example. See Responsa Beit Ephraim, Choshen Mishpat, end of siman 36, who questions the Shach. He writes that the Shach, in the course of his discussion, understood the phrase “or he gave him a gift” to mean that the creditor obligated himself to give the debtor a gift equal to the debt, and therefore wrote that such a gift plainly discharges the debt and that the passage must speak of something less than a complete gift. In truth, however, that is not the implication of the wording at all. The reverse is possible: the debtor may have given the creditor a gift equal to the debt. See there.
