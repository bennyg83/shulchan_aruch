Shimon is negligent. The Rama rules below that, just as one who deposits generally does so with the custodian's wife and children in mind, a custodian who returns a deposit to the depositor's wife is exempt. Elsewhere the Rama cites an opinion that one who returns a borrowed article to the borrower's wife remains liable if it is lost. The Rosh likewise distinguishes a deposit from a loan or pledge: a borrower or pledgee must return the article to its owner, as shown by the Mishna concerning one who sends the borrowed cow through another person. Thus, delivering a pledge to the owner's wife is not necessarily a valid return. Some authorities distinguish between an ordinary deposit, which household members are expected to receive, and an article delivered for the personal benefit of the borrower or pledgee, which must be restored to the principal himself. The proper ruling follows the nature of the original transaction and the owner's established practice, not a single rule for every form of custody.
