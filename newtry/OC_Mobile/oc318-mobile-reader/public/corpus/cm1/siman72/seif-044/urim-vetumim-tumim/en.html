This follows Rambam and those who agree with him, as Mechaber rules below in siman 92: a person disqualified from oath who claims a deposited object was lost through unavoidable accident can sometimes be exempt rather than automatically paying. According to Ramah and Rabbenu Yeshayah, however, inability to take the required custodian oath causes payment. That can change the present case involving a gentile. If the gentile custodian would, under Torah monetary law, have had to swear that he was not negligent and had not misappropriated the collateral, but he cannot provide an acceptable Jewish oath and is treated as one unable to swear, he can be monetarily liable according to those authorities. The practical inability to collect from him in gentile court does not necessarily erase the underlying debt. If Shimon, the Jew, owes money to that gentile, Reuven may be able to reach that obligation through R. Natan's rule and collect directly from Shimon. See below in siman 86 for the separate question whether R. Natan's rule applies where a Jew owes a gentile who in turn owes another Jew under Jewish law. This can have practical relevance even today where the gentile is liable under Jewish law but not under the external legal system.
