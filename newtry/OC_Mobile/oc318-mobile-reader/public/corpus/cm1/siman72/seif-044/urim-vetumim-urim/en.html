Shimon is exempt. What practical difference is there to the gentile whether Shimon borrowed or not? In any event the gentile is exempt because the pledge was burned and he is not liable for unavoidable accident. Why should Reuven care whether Shimon profits from the event? See Tumim. I wrote that if the pledge was lost in a manner where the gentile would be liable to the Jew under Torah law, but is exempt only because the secular courts will not compel him, then Shimon may indeed be liable. He may owe Reuven through Rabbi Natan's lien, because the gentile is legally obligated to Shimon and Shimon is obligated to Reuven. The secular court's refusal to enforce does not necessarily change that Torah-law relationship. See below, siman 86.
