From paying. Even without the loan the pledge would have remained in the non-Jew's house. Moreover, Shimon performed no act upon the body of the pledge that would place it in his legal possession and create liability. Reuven also cannot demand that Shimon pay him the amount of the debt the non-Jew had lent him. Why should Shimon pay him? If the pledge burned and under the non-Jew's law his lien thereby ended, what concern is that of Reuven? It is from Heaven that Shimon happened to be favored. These are the words of Maharam, cited by the Shach.
