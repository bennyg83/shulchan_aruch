Shimon acquires the entire pledge. See Tumim. I agreed with the Sema in the following case: if the gentile instructed the first Jew to pledge the object with another Jew, and then the gentile died, even if he left descendants who wish to redeem it, the first Jew cannot demand, "Return my pledge to me," because he completely withdrew from the pledge. But if the first Jew repledged it to the second Jew on his own initiative, and the gentile died leaving descendants who wish to redeem it, the law follows Rama in Yoreh Deah 169. By customary practice, the second Jew must return it to the first Jew if the first wishes, just as when the gentile is alive. This applies to the gentile's descendants. But with other heirs, there is a distinction, because inheritance by other gentile heirs is not Torah inheritance. See there.
