According to Rambam. He reads the wording "objects that are ordinarily..." precisely.
According to the Rif and Rabbeinu Tam. Bava Metzia says that because it becomes damaged, etc., and "this too, since..." implying that even without actual damage there are important objects not ordinarily lent or rented because of their value.
Provided that it is... and all this... Tosafos prove this there and in Shevuos 46b from the case where he produced a book of aggadah, implying all the more so other books. Bava Kamma 114b discusses one who recognizes his utensils and books and bought them back. Why should he be believed if these are objects commonly lent or rented? It cannot be answered merely that we do not presume ordinary people are thieves, because he is not accusing the possessor himself of theft and there are many possible thieves. See siman 357, seif 1. Tosafos therefore establish the case where the possessor is not accustomed, etc. See there.
