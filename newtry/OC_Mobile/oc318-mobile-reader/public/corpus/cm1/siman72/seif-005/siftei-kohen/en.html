The person whose property was pledged must pay the tax and redeem it, even if he was not expressly warned to do so. Where he retains the item as security for his own money or receives compensation tied to custody, the analysis may differ. The public office itself does not create paid-custodian status.

Nor does the trustee, etc. The Mechaber's categorical ruling that the communal trustee is not a paid custodian requires study. The Rashba calls the matter doubtful. His conclusion that he does not see a clear basis for liability from Abba Shaul's rule concerning a guardian appointed by beit din means only that one cannot prove definite liability from that source; it does not remove the doubt. There is also a strong reason to deem him a paid custodian according to Rav Yosef: he performs a mitzva and benefits from the coin he need not give a poor person while occupied with it. The Rashba never fully rejects that reasoning. His novellae in Gittin state expressly that an administrator is a paid custodian through Rav Yosef's benefit, and that concern that people will refuse appointment does not apply where the appointment enhances his reputation for reliability. The distinction from a guardian of an ox is that the latter is appointed merely to prevent damage and gains no reputation for trustworthiness. The Rashba further explains that Abba Shaul's special statement concerning appointment by beit din rests on the public trust and reputation conferred, while Rav Yosef's benefit would apply even to one appointed by the orphans' father. A communal trustee may lack the special reputation rationale, but the mitzva rationale remains. A Geonic responsum calls public tax trustees unpaid custodians, though it may follow Rabbah rather than Rav Yosef. Therefore the Mechaber's absolute ruling requires study. If the owner of the pledged property seized payment from the trustee, it should not be removed, because he may rely upon Rav Yosef. Likewise, when two parties deposit an item with a stakeholder until a fixed time, and neither may retrieve it during that period, the stakeholder may be a paid custodian through the duty to air and preserve it; if one party seized payment from him after loss, it should not be removed.
