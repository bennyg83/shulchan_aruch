(Seif 18) “There are witnesses who saw it now in his possession.” See siman 297, where the Tur writes that even if witnesses see a identifying mark on a utensil in his possession matching the borrower’s utensil, so long as they do not actually recognize it he may say, “I purchased it,” through the migo that he could have said, “I returned it.” The glossator notes that this is not compelled by the Tur and that siman 134, seif 2, suggests the opposite. The Tur in siman 297 means only that while the father was alive we would not compel him to show the object to the witnesses, because before they saw it he could claim purchase; for orphans we do not advance that claim. In any event, this requires study in light of the nearby rule that if the object is publicly known to be in his hands there is no migo because he fears lying. Here too perhaps he fears that the witnesses who know its marks will identify it and testify.
