But not the administrator. The Shach asks that the administrator should be a paid custodian because of the perutah of Rav Yosef. See Beurim, seif katan 20, for a proper answer. If the administrator is paid for acting as administrator, he is a paid custodian according to everyone. Charity collectors who take collateral are unpaid custodians. The Shach writes that a shalish who holds an object for both sides is a paid custodian because he cannot hand it to either party, must air and care for it, and thereby receives the benefit of the perutah of Rav Yosef. See Beurim, seif katan 19, where the primary law is that he is only an unpaid custodian.
Unless there is a local custom. The Shach explains that the custom is one in which community leaders first advance the tax from their own funds and then collect from individuals, taking collateral from those who do not pay. In that situation the leaders themselves are the lenders. But an administrator does not become a paid custodian merely because there is a custom, because a custom cannot uproot the underlying law in that manner. See Beurim, seif katan 28: if the actual custom is that the leaders themselves must pay, and the community or administrator undertook the position on that understanding, they are liable to pay.
