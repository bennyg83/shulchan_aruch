Seif 27. He did not air it properly. If a wool garment was held as collateral for more than a year and the lender neither spread it out nor removed it from the chest, causing a measurable deterioration established by experts, the lender cannot answer, "You were in town and should have spread it yourself." The end of siman 292, as explained by the Tur, applies even to airing a garment, and the Shach in siman 72:3 and Urim VeTumim there likewise indicate that the borrower cannot ordinarily be expected to take back collateral for this purpose, since the lender did not trust him with the pledge. The lender also cannot say that the borrower should have offered substitute collateral whenever airing was needed, or provided security and temporarily received the garment. The borrower may have reasonable grounds for not proposing an exchange: perhaps the lender would refuse a substitute, perhaps the borrower had no present use for the garment and saw no need to take it, perhaps he was embarrassed to seek repeated exchanges, or perhaps neither party wished to trouble a third party. Where they do not trust each other, an exchange cannot safely be made without such a third party. At the outset the lender accepted responsibility to preserve the garment, knowing that substitution every month might not be practical. Even where the parties use a third party who customarily gives the pledged garment to the borrower for each festival on condition that it be returned afterward, the lender still bears the duty to air it, because people may be unwilling to trouble the third party merely for routine airing.
