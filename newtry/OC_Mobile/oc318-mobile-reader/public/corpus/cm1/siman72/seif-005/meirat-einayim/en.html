One from whom collateral was taken, etc. See the Beit Yosef and Darkei Moshe, which quote the question as saying: 'One from whom collateral was taken, etc., and he warned him several times, and it was stolen, etc.' The Mechaber omitted the warning because the response itself indicates that the same law applies even without prior warning. See the language there, part of which I cite below.
Neither the community nor the trustee has the status of a paid custodian. The responsum states: 'It is obvious that the community is exempt, because those who pay the tax do not owe one another anything such that the community could be viewed as taking collateral for their debt. Rather, each individual pays his own share to the ruler, and the community merely appoints officers to collect it for the ruler or for a creditor who lent money on its account, etc.' At first glance, it appears that the point is not merely that the community does not have the status of a paid custodian with respect to paying the excess of the collateral's value over the tax. There is also a practical difference according to the view that an ordinary lender on collateral is only an unpaid custodian. Ordinarily, even according to that view, the lender at least loses the amount of the debt corresponding to the value of the collateral. Here, however, the community loses nothing at all, and even the tax for which the individual was distrained must still be paid. Otherwise Rama should not have remained silent without noting that according to the 'some say' opinion in seif 2, which we follow, there would be no practical difference in this ruling. The Mechaber simply writes that the community has no status of paid custodian and does not add that even the value of the collateral is not deducted from the tax. Perhaps this is because the rule that the amount equal to the collateral's value is deducted from the debt derives from a responsum of the Rashba, and the Rashba rules like the Geonim that a lender on collateral is a paid custodian. Once we are taught that the community here does not have the status of an ordinary lender on collateral because the community and its agents are not the ones personally taking collateral for their own debt, it follows automatically that the taxpayer must still pay his tax, since there is no basis to exempt him. In any event, it appears obvious that if they already warned him to redeem the collateral and he did not do so, he must pay the tax amount. As for the statement that the trustee likewise lacks the status of a paid custodian, see the responsum. It explains that these community trustees are not comparable to an administrator appointed by beit din over orphans who failed to guard an orphan's ox and it went out and gored, concerning whom some hold the administrator liable. There, the administrator is willing to accept liability because of the good reputation he thereby earns, for administrators are appointed over orphans only when they are known to be trustworthy and pious. Community trustees are different. See there, and see the Darkei Moshe glosses for the wording.
