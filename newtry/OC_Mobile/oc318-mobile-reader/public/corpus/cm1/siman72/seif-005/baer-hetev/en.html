To the trustee. The Sema writes that although ordinarily the lender at least loses his debt to the extent of the value of the pledge, here the community loses nothing. Even the tax for which the item was pledged must be paid. At the very least it seems obvious that if the owner had already been warned to redeem it and did not do so, he must pay the tax. Regarding the statement that the trustee does not have the law of a paid custodian, see the responsum, which explains that these communal trustees cannot be compared to a guardian appointed by beit din for orphans whose ox was not properly guarded and caused damage, concerning whom some authorities impose liability. There the guardian accepts the responsibility because of the good reputation it gives him, since beit din appoints a guardian only when it knows him to be trustworthy. This does not apply to communal trustees. The Shach writes that the Sema misunderstood the Rashba's responsum. The Rashba never intended to compare this to the orphan's ox, for there the guardian was negligent, and even an unpaid custodian is liable for negligence. Rather, the Rashba was discussing a guardian appointed by beit din according to Abba Shaul, who must take an oath. See there. The Shach further writes that the Mechaber's categorical ruling that the trustee is not a paid custodian requires study, for the Rashba himself writes that the matter requires analysis, and the other authorities likewise clearly do not incline to rule this way. Therefore, if the person whose property was pledged seized assets from the trustee, they are not removed from him, because he can say, 'I rely on the opinion that rules in my favor.' It further appears to me that if two people deposited an item with a third party until a fixed time and it was stolen or lost during that period, the third party is a paid custodian according to Rav Yosef and those who agree with him, because he was required to air it out and shake it since neither party could take it during that time. Thus, if one of them seized assets from the third party, they are not removed from him. These are the words of the Shach.
Custom. The Shach writes that this wording is imprecise. It is obvious that such a custom has no force, because only an established custom or enactment can override the ordinary halachah, not a defective custom that contradicts the law. This is as stated in Terumat HaDeshen at the beginning of siman 342, that only regarding taxation do we refrain from scrutinizing a custom even when it is defective, but not in other matters. The Rama's own wording in siman 163, seif 3, also indicates this. The wording of the Rashba's responsum, however, is proper. His intent is that if the custom is that the community pays the tax and then collects from each individual, so that the individuals become debtors to the community, the community thereby becomes a paid custodian. The Rama's words must be understood accordingly. These are the words of the Shach.
