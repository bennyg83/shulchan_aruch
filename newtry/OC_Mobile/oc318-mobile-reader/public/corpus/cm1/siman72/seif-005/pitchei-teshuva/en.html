And not to a trustee. See Baer Heitev through the statement that these trustees are not comparable to a guardian appointed by beit din. See Responsa Shivat Tzion, siman 99. The questioner wished to say that a charity collector resembles a communal tax collector, and therefore it should not be said that he willingly accepts liability in exchange for the good reputation he gains. Accordingly, he would be exempt even for negligence according to those authorities who exempt a guardian appointed by the father of orphans even for negligence, lest qualified people refuse such appointments. Shivat Tzion rejects the comparison. The Beit Yosef shows that the Rashba's reason regarding a communal tax collector is that the office brings little honor, since such collectors are often appointed through maneuvering and pressure. That is not true of synagogue charity collectors in his community, who are appointed only after the congregants repeatedly urge trusted individuals to accept the position. Such appointment is an honor and public testimony that the person is trustworthy. He therefore has the status of a guardian appointed by beit din according to Abba Shaul. Since the community selected him, there is no concern that imposing liability will cause qualified persons to refuse, and he must pay if he negligently failed to guard charity funds. See there and Netivot HaMishpat.
