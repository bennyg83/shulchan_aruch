Seif 14, in the gloss. The lender says, “It is true that you paid me, but I do not know how much,” and the borrower is believed, etc. A proof can be drawn from Bava Batra concerning uncertainty whether a tree preceded the city or the city preceded the tree. Here too, even according to the lender’s own account he must ultimately return the collateral and has only a monetary claim against the borrower. The borrower is therefore considered the party in possession with respect to the disputed amount. See also my fifth-edition responsa, siman 42, where I wrote that for this reason, when the parties come to litigation, although ordinarily a defendant need not first deposit the disputed money with beit din, in a dispute over collateral he may be required to deposit it. In either event the collateral itself must ultimately be returned, so requiring deposit does not create a pointless indignity to beit din. Examine this carefully.
