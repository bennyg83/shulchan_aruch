See Baer Heitev, who writes that if a Jew pledges to another Jew a non Jew's collateral and pays him interest, etc. See Responsa Chatam Sofer, Choshen Mishpat, siman 216, who writes that it is even more obvious that the same applies to a wicked Jew who lends another Jew at fixed interest. Such a substantial benefit renders the lender a paid custodian. This is certainly true in our times, when unfortunately the borrower cannot practically recover the forbidden interest through judges and would not even think to sue for it. But even if our authority were strong enough to compel the lender to disgorge the interest, the fact that the lender holds and uses the interest money until it is recovered through litigation itself renders him a paid custodian.
