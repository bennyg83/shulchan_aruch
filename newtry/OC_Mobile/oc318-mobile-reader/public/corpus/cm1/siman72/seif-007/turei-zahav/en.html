(Ibid.: Shimon claims that Reuven owes him.) Shimon wishes to acquire Levi’s object because Reuven says he purchased it from Levi and Reuven acted in concert with Shimon. The ruling teaches that Reuven has no credibility once the object is no longer in his possession; Shimon must return it to Levi. Reuven cannot say, “I returned it and then purchased it from Levi,” because there are witnesses and current observation. These are the Rashba’s words in the Beit Yosef. The Rama omits the requirement of current observation. In truth, the Rashba’s words are difficult, for why is observation needed? It cannot be merely to deprive Reuven of a migo through Shimon’s possible claim, “You sold it to me,” or “You sold it to Reuven,” since no such migo is recognized when Reuven and Shimon are acting together. The glossator explains that the author is invoking the Rosh’s responsum later in this siman: no migo arises from a claim lying in another person’s power, because perhaps that person would not agree to lie.
