According to the value of the collateral, as explained. Meaning, above in seif 2 the Mechaber ruled like the Geonim that the lender becomes a paid custodian and is liable for the full value of the collateral. But these laws also apply according to the view that he is only an unpaid custodian, because he nevertheless loses from his debt the amount equal to the value of the collateral, as explained. Therefore the Tur states these laws according to all opinions, unlike Eir Shushan, who makes this rule depend on the ruling that the lender is a paid custodian.
And a shekel was worth a shekel; a shekel is half a sela, and a sela is four dinars. This refers to the shekel mentioned in the Gemara. The Torah's shekel, however, is four dinars, for the biblical shekel was doubled, as Rashi explains on the Torah. See Perishah.
He swears first. Meaning, we do not first have the borrower take a hesset oath that the collateral was worth the amount of the debt, because we are concerned that after the borrower swears, the lender may produce the collateral and contradict him, causing a divine name used in the oath to have been desecrated. The Tur and Mechaber write this later in seif 15. There it is explained that even if the borrower believes the lender's statement that the collateral was lost, the lender must nevertheless swear whenever the borrower himself must swear.
That it is not in his possession. Below in siman 295 it is explained that the Sages of the Mishnah instituted this oath for custodians out of concern that the custodian may have set his eyes on the object. It is a stringent oath taken while holding a sacred object. The Tur and Mechaber state this nearby in seif 15.
