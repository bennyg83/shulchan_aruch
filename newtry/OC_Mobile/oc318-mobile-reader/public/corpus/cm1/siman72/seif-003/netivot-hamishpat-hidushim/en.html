The Shach writes that their entire dispute is according to those who rule like Rabbah and Shmuel, making the lender a paid custodian because of the benefit of holding security for his money, and treating loss of the collateral as though he had agreed to lose the debt. That rationale does not apply where the collateral is temporarily in the borrower's own hands. But according to those who rule like Rav Yosef, the lender remains a paid custodian. Likewise, a paid custodian who deposits the item with its owner remains liable. Therefore the borrower may say, "I rely upon Rav Yosef," regarding the amount corresponding to the debt. Even if the borrower borrowed the collateral back from the lender for temporary use, the borrower does not thereby become a paid custodian merely because he benefits from the use. Likewise, the lender may not charge the borrower rent for using the collateral even when it was taken not at the time of the loan. See Beurim, seif katan 15.
