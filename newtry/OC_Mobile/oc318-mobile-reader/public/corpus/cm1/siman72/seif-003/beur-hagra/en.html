One who lends... See Bava Metzia 35b, "sometimes..."
(Collected note) One who lends... As in Bava Metzia 115a concerning a pillow, etc., and Rashi there. Although that case concerns a pledge taken after the loan, which the creditor acquires, this distinction matters when the pledge was formally returned, not where it was merely deposited with another person. Mordechai there explains this, and this is the meaning of, "since it was given as a deposit..." (End of note.)
(Collected note) For this reason... Ordinarily we presume the object remains lawfully in her possession and do not presume theft. Even if it had been in her possession, she would have had to return it, contrary to the Sema. See Even HaEzer, end of siman 73. Here, however, Reuven died. If he had fully returned the pledge to her, she could have held it for her kesubah, and even if she later gave it to Shimon, he could not seize it from her. Mordechai says this explicitly. This is the meaning of the phrase that Reuven died during the festival, based on the Gemara's rule that the pledge does not become ordinary movable property in the hands of the creditor's heirs. See the Sema. Rashba's responsum cited by Beit Yose in Even HaEzer 73 appears at first glance to support the Sema, but this is not certain. His alternative reason based on market regulation also differs. See Beis Shmuel there. (End of note.)
Reuven who gave... According to the Rif, liability as a paid bailee arises because the creditor performed the mitzvah of lending. Here he did the other person no such favor. According to Tosafos, liability arises because he accepted the pledge in exchange for the money, which would also apply here to the extent of the pledge. Because of this uncertainty the law follows the principle stated above. One cannot invoke Rav Yosef's perutah benefit from guarding the pledge, since here he believes the pledge is in the possession of a non-Jew and need not spread or air it, as stated at the end of siman 292. See the Shach.
