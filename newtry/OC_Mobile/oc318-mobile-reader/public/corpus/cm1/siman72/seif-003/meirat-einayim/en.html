The lender is liable, etc. The phrase 'it still remains in the lender's domain' means that the borrower is only an unpaid custodian for the lender and is therefore exempt for theft. According to the first opinion in the preceding seif, that the lender is a paid custodian, the lender must pay the amount by which the collateral's value exceeds the debt. According to the 'some say' opinion above, when it says here that the lender is 'liable,' it means that the lender loses the amount of his debt up to the value of the collateral. The Ir Shushan was not precise in presenting this law only according to the view that the lender becomes a paid custodian. The proof is that the Mordechai, the source of this law, follows Ri, who holds that the lender does not become a paid custodian. Examine carefully.

The law favors Shimon. The responsum states that the woman cannot seize the items for her ketubah because they are still in the lender's domain. The same is true that they do not become movable property in the hands of the borrower's sons, and the lender may collect from them. See the Derishah for the view of those who disagree.
He becomes a custodian over it. The Ran writes in responsum 19 concerning Reuven, who lent Shimon one book, and Shimon left another book with him as collateral.
