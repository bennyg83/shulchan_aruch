If a lender upon collateral later deposits it with the borrower and it is stolen or lost, the lender remains liable. Although lost in the borrower's possession, it was given as a deposit and therefore remained under the lender's responsibility. For this reason, if Reuven borrowed from Shimon against his wife's garments, Shimon lent them back until after the festival, Reuven died during the festival, and fear of the ruler caused the wife to deliver all her property, including those garments, to Shimon, who returned everything except them, Shimon prevails. Because the garments came back to Reuven only as borrowed property, they remained Shimon's collateral. {Rama: If Reuven gave Shimon collateral to pledge with a non-Jew, but Shimon gave the non-Jew different collateral and retained Reuven's, Shimon becomes its custodian as though he had lent money upon it (Maharam Padua, siman 59).}
