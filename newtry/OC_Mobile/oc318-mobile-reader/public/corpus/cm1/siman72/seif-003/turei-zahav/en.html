(Seif 4) “He is a borrower and is liable.” The Sema wonders why the Mechaber rules categorically, since the Beit Yosef cites another responsum of the Rashba saying that even after using the item he is not a borrower, because he did not receive the entire benefit; the permission was granted only after he had lent the money. A responsum of the Ran likewise suggests that, because of doubt, money cannot be extracted: the borrower is exempt from the debt to the extent of the pledge, and the lender is exempt from any excess value. I am astonished that this was treated as a dispute. The Rashba’s responsum saying he is not a borrower concerns a loan made specifically on condition that the lender may study from the books. He is therefore only a paid custodian. The responsum cited in the Shulchan Aruch concerns an ordinary loan, after which the borrower gratuitously permitted use; the lender is then a full borrower, since the loan had already been made and all present benefit is his. The Ran’s case is likewise one in which, when lending a silver cup, the owner stipulated that he also be allowed to borrow and use a woolen garment. Even there he is only a paid custodian because he does not receive all the benefit. The distinction is straightforward.
