For this reason, Responsa Shevut Yaakov, part II, siman 180, discusses Reuven, who received a pledge from Shimon without witnesses and lent him one hundred upon it, although the pledge was worth two hundred. When Shimon came to redeem it, Reuven's wife said that she too had lent Shimon one hundred and he denied it. The question was whether Reuven's wife is believed by a migo that she could have denied the matter or claimed that she had returned the pledge. Shevut Yaakov rules that the husband cannot seize the pledge on behalf of his wife, because he personally does not know the truth except from her statement, while she herself has no migo because the pledge is in her husband's possession. He adds that even if the wife dies and the husband inherits her claim, since he cannot swear that he knows with certainty that Shimon owed his wife, he cannot seize or withhold the pledge. This is straightforward. Responsa Maharash Kohen, part II, siman 2, likewise writes this. See there.
