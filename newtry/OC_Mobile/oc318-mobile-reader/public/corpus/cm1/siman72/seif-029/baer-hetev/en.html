Definite. If the borrower did not assert the matter with certainty, the lender would swear that the pledge was lost by unavoidable accident and collect his debt, as stated above in seif 14. Shach.
And is exempt. Through a migo that he could have said, "I owe you nothing," or, "I paid you," as the Tur writes. This implies that if he lacks such a migo, for example because the loan is evidenced by a document or witnesses and he stipulated that repayment must occur before witnesses, the lender swears and collects his debt. This is contrary to the Sema's explanation in seif 2 that without a migo the borrower would merely need to take a stringent oath. The Shach rejects that because if there is no migo, why should the borrower escape payment at all, and in any event the category of a stringent oath is inapplicable here except to Torah oath cases or those who swear and collect. Baal HaTerumot clearly supports the Shach's interpretation.
