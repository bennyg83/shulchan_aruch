(Collected note) When does this apply... only, and there is no... The language implies specifically where only the lender is believed. This requires study because it appears to contradict the end of seif 12, "and if the borrower trusted him..." See the Sema there. (End of note.)
(Collected note) In order to collect. Meaning where the lender reversed the oath onto the borrower. One might otherwise think that the Mishnah's phrase "who swears..." refers only to its opening clause; the phrase is repeated in the concluding clause to teach that the rule applies throughout the Mishnah and even there the lender cannot reverse the oath, despite the borrower's willingness. (End of note.)
(Collected note) Even if he trusted... The Mishnah was established where the borrower trusted the lender, yet Rabbi Ashi still says, "this one swears..." Tosafos may imply otherwise, but Sefer HaTerumos and the Ran are primary because the entire Mishnah is read consistently. (End of note.)
