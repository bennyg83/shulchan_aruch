In order to collect. Sema explains a case where the lender says he does not know the value of the pledge, so he is obligated to swear and cannot swear, and therefore must pay, except that one witness supports him. The borrower must then swear to contradict that witness. See what I wrote in siman 87. Bach explains instead that the lender reversed the oath onto the borrower, who now swears and collects. This too is correct.
Even if he trusts him. We ourselves do not rely on the borrower's trust, because perhaps after the oath the pledge will be produced and Hashem's name will be desecrated. If the borrower says that he himself was present when the pledge was lost, that is different. We do not suspect him of lying about a circumstance he personally observed, and there is no later concern. The Gemara likewise implies this by not establishing the case in that manner.
And Hashem's name will be desecrated. Rashi explains that the later exposure would disqualify him for testimony and oath. On that explanation, even imposing a ban on the borrower is problematic, because if the pledge later appears and it becomes clear that he accepted the ban falsely, he too may be disqualified. Rabbeinu Chananel and other authorities explain instead that the concern is a purposeless oath. Under that explanation, one may require him to accept a ban. Shach agrees with Rabbeinu Chananel and says that most authorities do as well.
