Everyone who is in the market is equal. Dovze is entitled to feel that his eyes have given him a fortune in the market:
How much it was worth, in order to collect. In the Perishah I explained how there can be a case in which the borrower swears and collects. For example, the borrower claims, 'You lent me one sela and the collateral was worth two,' while the lender says that it was worth more than the debt but he does not know how much. There is one witness who testifies that it was worth only five dinars. Without that witness the borrower would collect without an oath, as stated in seif 12. Here, however, the borrower swears against the witness and collects one sela from the lender. See there. See also Rama's gloss in seif 14 concerning a similar form of payment, and see another case stated by the Mechaber in siman 73, seif 15, where one swears and collects.
