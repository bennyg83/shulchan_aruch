(Seif 30) “Shimon must pay.” I do not understand the ruling. If it refers only to the third dinar, which he must pay while swearing that the pledge was not worth more than three dinars, what is the concluding permission to impose a ban directed against? He pays only the one dinar he admits, and swears regarding the rest. It cannot mean merely a ban against one who needlessly forced him to swear, for that applies to every oath and is discussed in siman 87, seif 22. The following phrase, “The same law applies if the pledge depreciated,” indicates that the cases share one rule, and there the borrower apparently pays the entire amount claimed by the creditor. The Sema explains that the oath about value and the oath that the pledge was lost while in Levi’s possession arise from one claim. Since Shimon cannot take the latter oath, he is treated as unable to take the former and must pay the creditor’s entire demand. This has no basis. If a claimant presents two demands and the defendant loses one, why should he lose the other when he would have prevailed had it been asserted alone? Siman 354 and the sugya in HaShoel contradict such a principle. The correct ruling is that Shimon pays only the single dinar he sought to avoid through the claim of accident, and swears regarding the remainder.
