Whenever the lender must swear that the collateral is not in his possession, he is not heeded if he says, 'I will pay whatever the borrower claims and will not swear,' because he may have set his eyes upon the object. If it is a standardized item readily available in the market for the amount he offers, he may pay without swearing. This applies when only the lender must swear and no oath rests upon the borrower. If the borrower must swear to its value in order to collect or be exempt, the lender can never avoid swearing that it is not in his possession, even if the borrower trusts him, even if he offers payment, and even if the item is readily replaceable. We fear that he may produce the collateral after the borrower's oath, causing desecration of Hashem's Name.
