To collect. The Sema writes that this can occur where the borrower claims, 'You lent me one sela, and the pledge was worth two,' while the lender claims that it was worth more but does not know how much. The lender has one witness that it was worth only five dinarim. The borrower then swears against the witness and collects one sela from the lender. In seif 14 the Rama states a similar case regarding repayment. See below siman 73, end of seif 15. The Shach writes that another case is where the lender reverses the oath onto the borrower, as the Bach also writes. This passage proves that a person who is liable to swear and has one witness assisting him is nevertheless not exempted from the oath, and if he cannot swear he must pay. These are the words of the Shach.
Be profaned. Alternatively, the concern is that the oath will be taken in vain, as Rabbeinu Chananel explains, rather than Rashi's explanation that the person would become disqualified from testimony and oaths. According to this, the lender can say, 'I will not swear that it is not in my possession, since such an item is readily available in the market; instead, you accept a ban upon yourself as to how much it was worth.' According to Rashi, this is not so, because even one who violates a ban becomes disqualified from testimony and oaths, as stated in simanim 34 and 92. The end of siman 75 likewise supports Rabbeinu Chananel's interpretation. The Gidulei Terumah asks: if an oath is required to determine the item's value, how can this be an item of known and readily available market value? One can simply see what it sells for. This is no difficulty. The case may be where an object of this type is commonly sold for five dinarim, but there are more important specimens worth two selaim. We nevertheless fear that when the lender produces the pledge the borrower will be forced to admit it is his, for example because everyone recognizes it as the borrower's or because his name is written on it. Therefore the lender must swear that it is not in his possession. Other resolutions are also possible, and the matter is clear. These are the words of the Shach.
