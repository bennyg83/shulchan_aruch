See Responsa Shevut Yaakov, part III, siman 140, concerning Reuven, who seized several hundred from Shimon and claimed that Shimon owed him the entire amount as compensation for the trouble and effort he had expended on Shimon's behalf in non Jewish courts over a year or two, and that Shimon had promised to pay for all his effort. Shimon replied that Reuven should specify all the work and effort and beit din would appraise the proper fee. Reuven answered that after working for a year or two he no longer remembered every detail, but knew with certainty that his total effort justified the entire amount seized and was willing to swear to that effect. Shevut Yaakov responds that similar cases had come before him many times and they had been unable to decide them according to strict Torah law, because the claimant himself could not clarify how much was owed, no fee had been fixed in advance, and a possessor cannot simply appraise his own labor at whatever amount he wishes. They therefore did everything possible to bring about a compromise acceptable to both parties. Where agreement could not be reached, Shevut Yaakov proposed a method based on Talmudic and halachic cases where one pays the reduced value of barley, namely one third less than full value, as in Bava Kamma 20a and Choshen Mishpat siman 391, and similarly siman 341, seif 4. The possessor should swear in the following form: 'I swear that according to the value of the effort and labor I expended for so and so, if beit din knew the full extent of my efforts they would appraise at least such and such an amount as my fee; and because beit din has ruled that one third is to be deducted, I take only two thirds, which certainly is owed to me.' His beit din agreed with him and they ruled this way several times. He suggests that this may be comparable to the rule in Choshen Mishpat siman 12, seif 5, that a judge may sometimes render a ruling resembling compromise where circumstances require it. End of quote.
