(But if it is an object ordinarily lent or rented.) Siman 133 establishes that witnesses must also have seen the object in the possessor’s hands. The Sema says current observation is required only when the possessor is alive and makes a certain claim; with heirs, the fact that the object is ordinarily lent or rented is sufficient to extract it. With respect, that is incorrect. The Rosh at the end of HaMekabel proves that current observation is required even against heirs. Hagahot Ashri states expressly that if witnesses have not now seen the object in his possession, he is believed through the migo that he could deny the transaction or claim return, and if he dies or sells it, we advance for the heir or purchaser the claim that it was purchased, as though the possessor were alive. The Sema’s proof from siman 296, where heirs are treated differently when the claimant identifies the object, is misplaced. There the item was proved to have entered the father’s possession as a deposit, so the original owner’s possession remains the baseline and a purchase claim is considered uncommon. Here there is no proof how the item entered the possessor’s hands. People do sell items that are also ordinarily lent or rented. Such a possessor cannot rely on his possession alone, but a migo of return gives credibility. Therefore witnesses and current observation are required even against heirs. The authorities abbreviated here and relied on siman 133, the source of the rule. Maharshal likewise states plainly that this case includes current observation.
