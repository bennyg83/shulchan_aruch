The law favors the owner of the object. The Beit Yosef explains that even though the possessor has a migo that he could have claimed ownership by purchase, we have not found that migo grants a person greater credibility than the claim he himself actually makes. Since he admits that the object is not his and is merely held by him as collateral, why should we allow him to retain another person's object against that person's will? See there.
And some say that the law favors the possessor. In the Perishah I explained their reasoning. One who lends on collateral for an amount greater than the collateral's market value presumably intended from the outset that if the borrower did not redeem it, he would retain it in satisfaction of the amount lent, because the object was especially desirable to him. The possessor's claim here is that this was indeed his intention from the beginning. Just as his possession enables him to claim against the object up to its value, it likewise enables him to say credibly that this was the understanding under which he made the loan.
