And the heir claims. See Beer HaGolah and the Shach.
(Collected note) And he claims... Meaning a claim of uncertainty. If the heir asserted a definite claim, he would swear and collect the debt, as stated in seif 14. (End of note.)
(Collected note) The defendant swears... This follows the Rosh in chapter 3 of Bava Batra, siman 17, that the defendant can be made to swear even against an uncertain claim where only a shevuas hesses is involved, unlike Tosafos. This is also the ruling in siman 133, seif 5 in the gloss, contrary to the Shach, who overlooked the Rosh's explicit words. Even where the possessor claims that he purchased the item and has three years of chazakah, the Rosh still requires the defendant to take a shevuas hesses. The oath here is because we advance for the heirs the claim that the pledge was returned, as in Bava Batra 70, so the defendant swears that it was not returned. We do not advance a claim of unavoidable loss, certainly according to the first opinion in siman 108, seif 4, and even according to the latter opinion we do not advance a doubtful claim that would make the opposing party pay. Tosafos and Rambam disagree with this entire approach, as stated in siman 133, seif 5. See siman 150, seif 6, where both opinions are recorded, and siman 297, where the Shulchan Aruch rules without qualification that no oath is required. The Rama did not add a gloss there. See what I wrote in siman 133 regarding the Ra'avad and Beer HaGolah. (End of note.)
