And there is a pledge. Shach explains that this applies even if the pledge is not mentioned in the written document, so long as it is known that there was a pledge. See Tumim.
And the heir claims the pledge was lost through unavoidable accident. Meaning he claims uncertainty, not that he personally knows an unavoidable accident occurred. If he claimed definite personal knowledge, how could he swear that the pledge was not returned when his own claim is that it was lost? See Tumim. According to the Shach's explanation, however, and as I wrote in the next seif katan, the borrower's oath that he did not return it does not refer back to the case of alleged unavoidable accident. It is therefore possible that the heir says with certainty only that his father instructed him that the pledge was lost through accident, not that he himself knows the facts. Even the father would have been able to collect only after the appropriate oath, and since he died without swearing, the heir cannot extract. There is therefore no difficulty.
That he did not return it. The Shach asks that this is an uncertain claim, and one does not take an oath on an uncertain claim. He answers that the cases are alternatives: if the heir claims with certainty that it was returned, the lender must swear. See Tumim, where I asked that according to the opinion that where there is a document we accept a migo even to extract, because a document is somewhat like property already collected, the heir should be believed through a migo that the pledge was lost by unavoidable accident, and the lender should be able to collect on his document after swearing. Therefore the case must be that the heir claims with certainty that his father instructed him that the pledge was returned. According to this, the two matters can in fact belong to one case. The heir says: according to your own statement that it was not returned, it must certainly have been lost by unavoidable accident; and if you deny that, then it was certainly returned to you. The lender therefore swears that it was not returned. As for unavoidable accident, even the father himself would have been believed only with an oath, and he died without taking that oath. The matter is therefore resolved.
