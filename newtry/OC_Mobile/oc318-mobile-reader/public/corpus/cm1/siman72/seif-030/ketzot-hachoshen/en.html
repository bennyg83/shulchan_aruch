Bach writes that Reuven nevertheless cannot collect the amount of the loan based merely on Levi's oath or on witnesses, because Shimon does not make a certain claim. The Shach is certain that with witnesses Reuven can collect, and is uncertain where the matter rests only on Levi's oath, particularly where Reuven ordinarily deposits with Levi. Tumim writes that even on Levi's oath Reuven collects his debt, because Reuven is only a custodian and once Levi swears, Reuven collects. In my view, the Mechaber's language, "Shimon is exempt," implies at minimum that Reuven does not collect. The reason becomes clear from the first clause. Where Reuven does not ordinarily deposit with Levi, Shimon must pay because he can say to Reuven, "I trusted you." Yet one might ask: even if Shimon cannot collect because he himself cannot swear, Levi can swear. Since Reuven gave or pledged the collateral to Levi, from that point the debt should effectively belong to Levi, for a pledge can be sold, as explained in siman 66, seif 8. Why should Levi not swear and collect? The answer is that when collateral is lost through unavoidable accident and the original lender is exempt, only that lender can still collect the debt, because he actually lent the money. A purchaser of collateral does not acquire the underlying personal debt, only the collateral. If the collateral is lost, his money is lost. If Levi had acquired the debt itself through a three party transfer, he could indeed swear and collect. But where he acquired only the collateral, loss of the collateral means loss of his own investment. Accordingly, even if witnesses establish the unavoidable accident, Reuven does not collect if he already completely transferred the collateral to Levi by gift or sale. Reuven has removed himself from the collateral relationship, and Levi cannot collect because he acquired only the collateral, not the debt. This is like principal returned to the owner in the case of consecrated stolen property. If, however, Reuven merely pledged the collateral onward to Levi, then if the collateral is lost from Levi through unavoidable accident established by witnesses, Levi collects his debt from Reuven and Reuven can still collect his own debt from Shimon, because Reuven never fully divested himself. The same applies where Levi swears and Reuven ordinarily deposits with him. The Mechaber's phrase that Shimon is exempt is therefore best understood in the gift case mentioned in the first clause. There, once the collateral is lost, neither Reuven nor Levi collects. This is clear.
