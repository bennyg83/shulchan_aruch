And he cannot swear, so he pays. Meaning, he pays the entire claim, namely two dinars. Although he could swear the oath of partial admission, since the item had originally been in his possession and its value was seen, and merely because he cannot swear that it is not presently in his possession and that he was not negligent there would ordinarily be no reason to make him pay more than its actual value, nevertheless all three oaths arise together from his present claim. Since he cannot swear them all, we treat him as though he cannot swear even the partial-admission oath that the collateral was not worth more than three dinars, and he must therefore pay two dinars. It appears that even according to the view that where a secondary oath is attached through gilgul to a primary oath and he cannot swear one of them we do not say he must pay both, as the Tur and Mechaber write in siman 75, seif 15, everyone agrees here because the three oaths all arise from one integrated matter and are treated as one. This is how the Tur and Mechaber's wording appears to be understood. In Perishah, however, I wrote that it seems more likely the case is one where Shimon also does not wish to pay Reuven the third dinar, because he assumes with certainty that Levi was not negligent. He would therefore ordinarily be exempt from that third dinar, but to exempt himself he is obligated to swear that the loss was unavoidable. Since he cannot swear this, he is one who is obligated to swear but cannot and must therefore pay everything. Even if, after hearing the ruling, he now wishes to pay the third dinar and swear concerning the balance, because at first he refused to pay and swear, we apply the rule of one obligated to swear who cannot swear and must pay. The Tur and Mechaber's phrase, 'he owes only one dinar,' does not mean that he is willing to pay that dinar; rather, he says: 'Even according to your account that there was negligence, I would in any case owe at most one dinar.' For this reason the Tur concludes, 'he must further swear,' etc., 'to be exempt from the dinar,' implying that he does not wish to pay the third dinar. See Perishah for additional proofs.
The same law applies if it depreciated, etc. Meaning, although there is no oath that the collateral is not presently in his possession, since the collateral is before us and merely lost value, and he can swear the partial-admission oath, nevertheless, because he refuses to pay the one dinar he admits was lost in value, saying perhaps the depreciation occurred through unavoidable circumstances, and he cannot swear to that unavoidable accident, he has the law of one who is obligated to swear and cannot swear, as explained above.
If there are witnesses, etc. The same applies if Shimon believes Reuven that it was lost through unavoidable accident.
And it was lost from him through unavoidable accident, Shimon is exempt. Reuven cannot say, 'Had it remained with you it would not have been lost.' Whenever the later event is a true unavoidable accident, something uncommon, we say, 'What difference does it make whether here or there?' Only concerning theft or loss, which are common and were preceded by some deviation amounting to negligence, do we say that had he not deviated the loss might not have occurred. See below siman 291 and Perishah here.
If Reuven ordinarily deposits with, etc. Then he cannot say that the second custodian is not trustworthy to him concerning his oath.
If Levi was negligent, etc. The Tur writes similarly nearby in seif 25, and the Tur and Mechaber below in siman 291 rule that although we say every depositor deposits with the understanding that the custodian may entrust the object to his wife and children, if the custodian gives the deposit to his wife or son and they are negligent, he himself must pay because the property was entrusted to him. It appears that even Rambam, whom the Tur and Mechaber there in seif 24 cite as holding that the negligent wife or son is liable rather than the husband who delivered it, would agree here where he transferred the item to Levi, an entirely separate person. Even if Reuven ordinarily deposits with Levi, the custodian should not have done this, and for this purpose it is considered negligence. Therefore the Tur and Mechaber state the rule here without qualification.
