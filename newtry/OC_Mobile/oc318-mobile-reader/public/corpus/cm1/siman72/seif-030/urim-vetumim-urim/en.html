And it was lost through unavoidable accident while in Levi's possession. This follows Shimon's own claim by which he seeks complete exemption even from the value exceeding the debt. The Mechaber holds that the lender is a paid custodian, so only unavoidable accident would exempt him for that excess. According to the Rama, where the excess is treated as under an unpaid custodian, theft or ordinary loss would likewise exempt him.
And he cannot swear, so he pays. The Shach explains that "he pays" means only the one dinar that he admits, despite his claim of unavoidable accident, while as to the other dinar that he denies he swears and is exempt. All the other commentators explain that he must pay both dinars claimed by the borrower. See Tumim, where I proved from the Shulchan Aruch and Baal HaTerumot that their explanation is correct, against the Shach. I discussed the reason at length. The oath Rav Huna requires a custodian to take, that the item is not presently in his possession, is a Torah oath. Since Shimon cannot swear it because he does not know whether the collateral remains in Levi's possession and Levi may have coveted it, Shimon is one who cannot take the required oath and therefore must pay according to the borrower's claim. Levi's willingness to swear does not satisfy Reuven, who can say that he does not trust Levi on oath.
The same law applies if the collateral depreciated, etc. Here the preceding reason cannot apply because there is no need to swear that the item is not in his possession, for the collateral is physically before us. One must therefore explain, as indeed Baal HaTerumot implies, that the depreciation occurred while the item was with Levi, and Shimon does not know how or to what extent it depreciated except from Levi's report. Reuven can therefore say, "I do not trust Levi on oath," and Shimon must pay according to Reuven's definite claim. This is correct. It is better to accept a slight pressure in the wording than to say, as the Sema does, that because Shimon cannot swear that he was not negligent he must pay the entire depreciation even though he is personally able to swear to that fact. That proposition has no basis in law. See Tumim.
And it was lost through unavoidable accident. According to the Rama, the same applies if it was stolen or lost under the opinion that the lender is only an unpaid custodian for value exceeding the debt. There is no issue of one custodian who transfers to another, because the loss is established by witnesses, and the same is true where the original owner explicitly trusted the second custodian. Shach, and this is clear.
And Shimon is exempt. Meaning exempt from his custodial liability for the collateral, because there are witnesses to the accident or because he transferred it to someone with whom he customarily deposits property. Once he is exempt as custodian, the borrower must of course repay the entire loan to the lender. The lender held the collateral only as a paid custodian, and if an unavoidable accident occurred the loan obligation plainly remains. The Shulchan Aruch therefore states only that Shimon is exempt from custody, from which the borrower's duty to repay follows automatically. I do not understand why the Shach saw even a slight reason to write that this requires study.
Shimon is liable. The Sema holds that although the Rambam rules below in siman 291 that a custodian who entrusts property to his wife or children is exempt even if they cannot pay, the reason is that we treat the depositor as having implicitly authorized the ordinary household transfer. But where the custodian transfers it to another person, even one with whom he commonly deposits his own property, if that second custodian cannot pay, the first custodian remains liable according to all opinions. The Shach objects that the reverse is more plausible: if anything, one should be more inclined to hold the first custodian liable for transfer to his wife or children, lest every custodian evade liability by handing property to his family and allowing it to be lost. He therefore explains that the Rama's reference to siman 291 points to the opinion there that exempts the first custodian even in a transfer to another custodian. See Tumim. I wrote that there may be a distinction between an unpaid and a paid custodian. For a paid custodian there is room to say that the first custodian certainly remains liable. See there.
To pay Reuven. Meaning only one dinar, provided Shimon swears that the collateral was not worth more than three dinars. The one dinar Shimon pays Reuven can then be recovered by Shimon from Levi, because Shimon paid on Levi's behalf. Tur and Shach.
