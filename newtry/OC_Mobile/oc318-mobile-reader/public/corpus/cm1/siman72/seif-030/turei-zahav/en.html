(Ibid.: Hagahot Ashri writes at the end of HaOmanin.) The passage until “although we rule like Rav, who holds that he must swear,” is difficult. The Gemara there says that the creditor must always swear, by extension, that the pledge was worth no more than a sela. Even if the creditor must pay because the pledge is no longer in his possession, the borrower does not trust him about its value. But no authority says that the creditor must swear merely because the borrower thinks the creditor has not followed the accepted ruling. Examine Rashi there carefully.
