His son becomes a paid custodian. See Shach, seif katan 36. His words are very difficult, as Tumim notes. A son stands in place of his father. Just as the father does not become a newly paid custodian merely because the collateral remains with him many days after the loan, neither should the son become one for that reason. The principal basis for paid custodial status is Rabbi Yosef's perutah. According to one who rejects that rationale, the creditor loses only the amount of his money under Shmuel's implied condition. Ketzot HaChoshen seeks to exempt the son because the father's death was an unavoidable event and the son never expressly accepted liability. This is incorrect. The reason Shmuel treats the loan as though the creditor expressly accepted loss of his money if the collateral is lost is that the creditor is presumed willing to do so in exchange for the benefit of not having to pursue the debtor repeatedly for payment, as Shach writes in seif katan 25. That same benefit applies to the son who succeeds to the creditor's position. Hagahot Ashri's statement that Shmuel's rule does not apply to collateral taken after the loan is explained by Tumim, seif katan 11, as referring only to the portion of the debt exceeding the collateral, where asmachta concerns arise. Up to the value of the collateral Shmuel's rationale certainly applies. If the creditor sells or gives the collateral to another, that transferee is governed only by Shmuel's rationale and not by Rabbi Yosef's perutah, because the transferee's duty of airing and care did not originate in a mitzvah imposed by the Torah.
