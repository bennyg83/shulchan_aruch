It appears that the Rosh follows his own view, namely Rabbah and Shmuel's theory that the pledge arrangement is treated as though the lender explicitly agreed that loss of the collateral cancels the debt. If so, the debt is fully offset against the value of the collateral, as I wrote in seif katan 7. The case then resembles a lender who says, "I do not know whether you repaid me," while the borrower is also uncertain. In an oral loan the borrower is exempt, as explained in siman 75. But according to the Mechaber, who treats the lender as a paid custodian, each party has a separate claim. The lender collects the debt, and the borrower has a separate claim for the lost collateral. A claim of, "You have money of mine in your hands," does not by itself defeat the lender's collection except through a migo, as explained in siman 24. Here the borrower is uncertain and has no migo. Why then do we not collect? It appears that we nevertheless do not extract from the borrower because the case resembles the Mishnah in Perek HaShoel: rented for half a day and borrowed for half a day, and the borrower says, "I do not know." That is treated as, "I do not know whether I became liable," and he is exempt. Yet there is an independent monetary uncertainty, as shown by the Mishnah's rule of division according to Sumchus, and Sumchus applies only where there is a genuine independent monetary doubt. In such a case possession can help even one who pleads uncertainty, as Tosafot writes in Perek HaShoel. Therefore, because the borrower already possesses the money, we do not extract from him.
