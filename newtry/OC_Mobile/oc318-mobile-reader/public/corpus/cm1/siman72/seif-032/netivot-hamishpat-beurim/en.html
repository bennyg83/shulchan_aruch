Ketzot HaChoshen asks that according to the Mechaber, who holds that one holding collateral is a paid bailee because of the perutah of Rav Yosef, and that the rule is that each party separately collects from the other, the lender should be considered as one who says, "I do not know whether I became obligated to you," with respect to the borrower's counterclaim. This is incorrect, because with collateral the rule that each party separately collects does not apply, as I wrote above. Moreover, even in another matter, where the borrower treats his counterclaim as repayment, it is like a doubt concerning repayment that arose before the doubt concerning the loan. The Shach in siman 75, seif katan 27, explains that if the lender is also uncertain he does not collect. See there. This is stronger than an ordinary claim of "I have something of yours corresponding to your claim," which is believed only by virtue of a migo, and consequently does not help where both claims are uncertain because no migo applies. A claim concerning collateral is not a weak claim, because it is known that the borrower's property was in the lender's possession, as I wrote above. What Ketzot writes that this is derara de'mamona and seizure therefore helps is likewise incorrect. Seizure in derara de'mamona helps only where the possessor claims ownership of the object itself, as I wrote in the general rules of seizure. See there.
