(Netivot HaMishpat, seif katan 46) It is a doubt of repayment preceding the doubt of the loan. Note: Here there is no doubt of repayment that precedes the loan. If the collateral was lost before the loan, the borrower is still obligated to repay the loan. Since the debt certainly became effective, the case is like one who says, 'I do not know whether I have a counterclaim against you.' Nor is the lender considered one who says, 'I do not know whether I was repaid,' according to Ketzot HaChoshen's reasoning in this law. In such a case each collects from the other. For this reason Ketzot HaChoshen also disagrees with the Shach in siman 75. In my opinion, even if we accept the Shach there, one can say that his ruling applies only in that case because the doubt arose before the loan, so there is reason to say that the claim itself arose from a separate matter. Nevertheless, if the lost deposit was in fact worth more, no loan obligation ever took effect against the borrower for the later money he received, because he already had a monetary claim against the lender for the deposit equal to the amount he later received. But where the doubt arose only after the loan, the lender is like one who asserts with certainty, 'The debt you owe me was not repaid.' A later doubt caused by loss of the collateral does not extinguish the loan debt that had already certainly taken effect.
