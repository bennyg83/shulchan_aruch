Since he cannot swear, he must pay. Raavad objects because partial admission requires an admission in a definite measured or counted amount. If the defendant says only, "Whatever you left is what you will take," his statement may include one perutah or one hundred, and at the time of admission the amount is not fixed before beit din. The later measurement does not repair the original defect because the admission already emerged in a form exempt from the Torah oath. The Shach explains that if he says, "I do not know how much it was worth, but at least one perutah," that is different because his admission is capped at the definite minimum of one perutah. This creates difficulty from seif 16 concerning a ring, where the lender admits that the lost ring was worth the same as another ring held by a money changer, while its actual value still requires expert appraisal. At the moment of the admission beit din does not yet know the amount. The answer is that he has admitted a definite referent: "the value of this ring before us." Even though experts have not yet translated that object into money, the admitted amount is objectively fixed. By contrast, saying, "Whatever you left is what you will take," points to no definite object or amount before the court. Therefore, if the comparison ring is not actually before beit din when he admits, but is merely somewhere in the money changer's house, the admission again lacks a definite measured amount and should be treated like the first case.
