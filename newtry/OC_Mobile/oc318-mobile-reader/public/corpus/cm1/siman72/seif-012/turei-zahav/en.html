(I do not know whether it depreciated.) The word “how much” is a scribal error; the text should read, “I do not know whether it depreciated.” The glossator notes that the author likely deleted “how much” to avoid the rule that one obligated to swear who cannot swear must pay. Yet no deletion is necessary, because there is no partial admission creating such an oath: the borrower owes the creditor far more than the amount of the alleged depreciation, and the pledge remains merely a deposit securing the debt. Until beit din formally takes possession, it remains the owner’s property, with the creditor only a paid custodian. If it is lost through unavoidable accident the borrower still pays the debt. The Gemara likewise treats a deposit as “here it is” even when it is not physically before the court, because wherever it is, it remains in the owner’s domain. The same is true of a pledge.
