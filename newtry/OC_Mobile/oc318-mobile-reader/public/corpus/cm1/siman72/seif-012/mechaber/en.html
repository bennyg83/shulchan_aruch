If the borrower says, 'You lent me one sela upon it, and it was worth two,' while the lender says it was worth only one, then if the borrower believes that it was lost, or witnesses establish the loss, the lender takes a heses oath and is exempt. If the borrower does not believe the loss and no witnesses establish it, the lender swears that it is not in his possession and includes in that oath that it was worth no more than the loan. If the lender partially admits, saying it was worth five dinars rather than the two sela claimed, he pays the admitted extra dinar and swears it was worth no more than five dinars, including that it is not in his possession where necessary. If he immediately tenders the amount by which it exceeded the loan, or says, 'I had property of yours equal to the excess value,' he takes a heses oath and is exempt if the loss is believed or witnessed. If the borrower claims one sela lent and two sela value, and the lender says he does not know its value, the lender swears it is not in his possession and that he does not know it exceeded the debt, and is exempt. If the loss is believed or witnessed, a heses oath of ignorance suffices. If he says, 'I know it exceeded the debt but do not know by how much,' he is obligated to swear but cannot, so he pays one sela; he may impose an unspecified ban upon anyone claiming falsely. If each claims money from the other, the borrower claiming one sela lent and two sela value, and the lender claiming only half a sela value, the borrower takes a heses oath that it was worth at least one sela, while the lender swears it is not in his possession and that it was worth no more than one sela. If the loss is believed or witnessed, the lender too takes only a heses oath concerning the value.
