In the first two clauses, trusting the lender does not help because a paid bailee cannot be released from his statutory oath merely through the borrower's trust, according to the Rif. (Collected note) The borrower said, "a sela..." I do not know... This is also learned from the Yerushalmi there. From this we likewise derive the law in seif 11, just as that case parallels the opening clause here. (End of note.)
(Collected note) And he includes... This follows Rambam that in gilgul we do not apply the rule that one who cannot swear must pay; rather he takes the oath that he can. So too siman 344, seif 4. Certainly where the principal oath is only rabbinic, inability to swear does not create payment, as in siman 75, seif 14. See siman 93, seif 13, and siman 298, seif 2. According to the first opinion in siman 75, seif 15, however, no additional oath is rolled on at all; he merely takes a shevuas hesses. See the Tur, Beer HaGolah, and the gloss there. The phrase "rather he shall swear..." means a shevuas hesses and therefore it can be reversed. See also siman 94, seif 9. According to the second opinion in siman 75, seif 15, where the principal oath is of Torah law, inability to swear would create payment, but here, since the oath is only rabbinic, even that opinion agrees. Tosafos in siman 94, seif 2, holds that no oath on an uncertain claim is added through gilgul at all, which reaches the same practical result as the first opinion in siman 75. (End of note.)
(Collected note) That he does not know... Although with a deposit the Sages enacted a claimant's oath analogous to the law of a robbed person, as in siman 90, seif 10, and siman 298, seif 1, they did not enact it for a pledge. See the Shach. (End of note.)

(Collected note) And if he responded... The Yerushalmi, as Ramban explains. This is not treated as a claim concerning something outside his expected knowledge. See siman 75, seif 19, the dissenting opinion, and the unqualified rulings in simanim 90, 292, and 388. This also differs from Tosafos and other poskim who hold that where he could not have known, inability to swear does not create payment. See what I wrote in siman 90, seif 10. (End of note.)
