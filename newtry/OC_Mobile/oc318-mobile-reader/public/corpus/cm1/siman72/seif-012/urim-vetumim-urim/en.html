And the case is where it was lost through negligence. The same applies if the collateral was taken after the time of the loan, because the Shach concluded above that in that case the lender is liable according to all opinions even for value above the debt. Tosafot explained the Mishnah, according to Rabbah, as discussing collateral given at the time of the loan and then lost through negligence, because the phrase "one who lends upon collateral" ordinarily implies collateral given at the time of lending.
And he swears that it was not worth more. This is a Torah oath of partial admission, whereas the additional oath that the item is not in his possession is rabbinic. The Tur therefore treats the former as the principal oath. The Rambam states the order in reverse. The Sema and Shach write that there is no practical difference. See what I wrote above in Tumim.
And he includes, etc. For the difficulty from simanim 75 and 94, see my lengthy discussion below in siman 94, which is the proper place.
I know, etc. This constitutes an admission of at least one perutah, because less than a perutah is not treated as meaningful value for purposes of saying that the collateral was worth more. Since he does not admit the remainder, he is considered to deny it. There is thus an admission of one perutah and a denial of two silver maah. As to the difficulty that the claim is not one defined by measure, weight, or number, see Tumim, seif katan 25, at length. Why, if the lender has no idea whether the collateral was worth more, do we not allow the borrower to swear and collect according to those authorities who applied the enactment for a robbed claimant to deposits? The Shach answers that this enactment was not applied to collateral according to any opinion, because the lender already has a strong incentive to guard the item carefully, since he loses up to the amount of his debt if it disappears. See what I wrote below in siman 90.
And he pays, etc. Even according to the many authorities who do not apply the rule that one who cannot swear must pay where he had no reason to know, a lender holding collateral is expected to know its value, because he ordinarily estimates it carefully before lending against it and must know whether his debt is adequately secured. Shach. See Tumim, seif katan 27, and below siman 94, Tumim seif katan 2, where I offered another resolution.
And if the borrower trusted him, etc. Here, since the lender himself must at least swear a hesset oath regarding the value, the borrower may choose to trust him, and there is no concern that one will thereby cause the other to take a false oath. See Tumim for the reason.
He too shall not swear, etc. This means that the lender swears first and afterward the borrower. Otherwise, after the borrower swears, the lender might produce the collateral and expose the oath as false.
