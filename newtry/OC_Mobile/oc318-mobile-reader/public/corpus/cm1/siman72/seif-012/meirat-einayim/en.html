The Tur and Mechaber write nearby at the end of seif 15 that when the borrower must swear how much the collateral was worth, the lender can never avoid swearing that the collateral is not in his possession, etc., and that case applies even where the borrower trusts him, as Rama writes there. One may distinguish that here, since in any event the lender must take a hesset oath that the collateral was worth no more than a sela, if it were actually in his possession he would produce it rather than place himself under an oath. According to this, one must say that here too the lender first takes this hesset oath, and afterward the borrower takes his own hesset oath. See the Derishah, where I offered additional resolutions.
