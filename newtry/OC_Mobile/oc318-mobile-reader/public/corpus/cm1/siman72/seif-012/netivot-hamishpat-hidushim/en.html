And he does not know how much, so he is one who cannot swear and must pay. Although the claim is not initially expressed as a precise matter of measure, weight, or number, the Shach explains that because we cannot obligate him in more than the value of one perutah, his admission is equivalent to an express admission of at least one perutah. The rule that "whatever you left you may take" can later be measured objectively, but when the admission is made before beit din the judges do not yet know the amount, and therefore it may not qualify as a precise admission. This will be explained, God willing, in siman 88. Although the defendant may not have had reason to know the value, many decisors hold that with a pledge he should know, because a person carefully assesses the value of a pledge. The Shach writes that even if he had no reason to know, he is still liable. Only where the claimant admits that the defendant truly did not know is the defendant exempt.
