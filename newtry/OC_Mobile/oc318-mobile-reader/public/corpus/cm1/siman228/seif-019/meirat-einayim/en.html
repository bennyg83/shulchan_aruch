It is forbidden to mix lees, etc. Seifim 19 and 20 follow Rambam in chapter 18 of Hilchot Mekhirah. In the Derishah I explained Rambam and the Maggid Mishneh, whose presentation is obscure and mixed. I demonstrated that Rambam holds it forbidden even to mix the lees of that same barrel back into its wine when selling it by measured portions, unless he sells the entire barrel together, in which case he may include the lees. For this reason Rambam holds that the purchaser must accept a certain amount of lees. If the seller were permitted to mix them in at the time of measuring and nevertheless refrained, the buyer would not have to accept them afterward because we would say that by not mixing them the seller waived that right. That is the alternative opinion Rama cites at the end of the siman. Accordingly, the Mechaber appears self contradictory. In seif 15 he writes that the lees of that very barrel may be mixed in during measurement, following Rosh and Tur. Here he rules without qualification that mixing lees is forbidden, following Rambam. The Tur presents these views explicitly as a dispute. Rama too, at the end of the siman, says, 'some say, rather, it is permitted to mix his own lees,' implying that according to Rambam even this is forbidden. We must therefore say that the Mechaber follows his interpretation in Beit Yosef of Rambam, namely that Rambam forbids mixing the lees of one barrel into another wine, but agrees that the lees of the same barrel may be mixed back while measuring. This is why the Mechaber in seif 15 permits that practice. In the Derishah, however, I rejected this interpretation of Rambam and showed that the Tur did not understand him that way. The Mechaber also omitted Rambam's following statement that if the seller pours all the wine from one vessel to another he may put its lees into it. Perhaps he omitted it because, according to the Beit Yosef's reading, Rambam already permits mixing the same barrel's lees even when measuring portion by portion, and all the more so when transferring the entire barrel from the seller's vessel to the buyer's. It also follows from the later law that one who sells oil without specification must allow for one and a half log of lees, which according to the Beit Yosef is because mixing is permitted. Examine this carefully.
