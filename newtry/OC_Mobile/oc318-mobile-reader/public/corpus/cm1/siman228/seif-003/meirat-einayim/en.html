Because her tears are readily brought forth. The Gemara in HaZahav explains that a woman is naturally tender, so even slight distress may bring her to tears, and Hashem is especially attentive to tears. The Gemara cites the teaching: although the gates of prayer may be closed, the gates of tears are not closed, as it says in Tehillim, 'Hear my prayer, Hashem, and give ear to my cry; do not be silent at my tears.' Rashi explains that although the beginning of the verse is a request, 'do not be silent' can also be read as a statement of confidence regarding the future: I trust that it is not Your way to remain silent in the face of tears. The Ir Shushan has a textual error, writing a phrase that should read 'at my tears.'
