Even new produce with new produce. In the Perishah I wrote in the Rosh's name that this means even where there is no obviously poor quality produce. So long as the produce comes from two different fields, one is ordinarily somewhat inferior to the other. The rule therefore means that one may not mix a small amount of inferior produce into a large amount of superior produce. For the same reason one may not even mix new produce from one field with new produce from another, because it is impossible that they are exactly equal in quality. Only a merchant is permitted to do so, because everyone knows that merchants ordinarily mix stock together, as the Tur and Mechaber write below in seif 16.
And it goes without saying, new with old, etc. Rama emended the Mechaber according to the Tur's version. The Mechaber's original language follows the Rambam, who follows the Rif. Their version of the baraita was that an ordinary purchaser in the market is actually pleased to buy new produce because he can store it and age it, even though old produce may sometimes be more expensive because it is better for immediate eating. This is explicit in Rif and Rambam. I discussed the matter at length in the Derishah.
