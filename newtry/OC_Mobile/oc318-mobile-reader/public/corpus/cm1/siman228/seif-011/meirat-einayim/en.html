Only between the winepresses. The Tur writes: although soft wine is better for drinking than hard wine and commands a higher price, because when one puts a little hard wine into soft wine during the winepress season it improves and preserves the soft wine, mixing is permitted then. Outside the winepress season it is not. Soft wine, however, may always be mixed into hard wine, because it is better to drink and improves it. This is the Rosh's view. Rashi disagrees and holds that soft wine may not be mixed into hard wine. This may explain why the Mechaber and Rama did not add the word "always." According to the Rosh and Tur they should seemingly have written that soft into hard is always permitted. Perhaps they held that this is self-evident and there is no reason to distinguish soft into hard outside the winepress season. See the Derishah, where I explained the basis of the dispute between Rashi and the Rosh.
One may never mix water, etc. It appears that the word "never" is misplaced. In the Tur it does not refer to the prohibition of mixing water that follows, because the Tur concludes that during the winepress season water may be mixed into wine everywhere according to local practice. The permission between the winepresses applies universally; only the quantity of water depends on custom. Rather, "always" refers to the preceding case of mixing soft wine into hard wine: although hard into soft is permitted only between the winepresses, soft into hard may be mixed always. Although the Mechaber changes the wording and writes in the following seif, "in a place where the custom is to mix water during the winepress season, it is permitted," it appears that his intent is still the same as the Tur's. Otherwise, the ruling would be obvious: if the custom explicitly permits it, of course it is permitted. Even if one interprets the Mechaber differently, the word "never" cannot properly introduce an absolute prohibition of mixing water, since during the winepress season the law itself depends upon custom. The correct explanation is therefore as I wrote: "always" refers backward. If the taste is recognizable, one may mix hard wine into soft wine even outside the winepress season. The Mechaber uses similar language in seif 14 concerning mixing water into wine, writing that in a place where tasting is customary, it may always be mixed. The Tur writes likewise.
