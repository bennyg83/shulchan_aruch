There is a name for Dr. McMahon and G-d, and it is written that the 'sexual sign' is the most pronounced signs such as a snail and a wide-ranging religion, and that these are the signs that I will give it back to them, and that there are important signs such as my father's religion, and his weight, and that I will return to them, and so that there are no such signs such as my father's
The Maggid Mishneh writes that it appears from the Raavad's words that he holds this even if the claimant gave a highly distinctive sign, such as a hole beside a particular letter, etc. Although signs of this type are valid by Torah law, as stated above, with a known deceiver we remain concerned, and this is learned from Torah law itself, there in the Mishnah. I saw that in Rambam the Raavad comments on the phrase 'it is his': 'This applies where the sign is not absolutely distinctive.' This implies that according to the Raavad, if the sign is absolutely distinctive we return the object even to a deceiver. The Bach likewise writes so.
