(Seif 25, to use the money.) The Tur writes: “Likewise, one with whom orphans’ money was deposited may not use it, and he is an unpaid guardian.” Why does he specify orphans? One may answer that one might have thought orphans are different, based on the Gemara’s case in which Bei Rachava held orphans’ money and Rav Yosef thought he could use it. Rosh explains that although a regular depositary may not use deposited money, as stated in siman 292, Rav Yosef initially thought this was comparable to proceeds of a lost article, because of the benefit Rachava rendered to the orphans who depended on him. Abaye told him that it is like the lost money itself, since he exerted no effort regarding it. This is also the Tur’s intent. Yet this interpretation of the Gemara is strained. If so, Abaye should not have needed to teach Rav Yosef the distinction between money found as a lost article and proceeds obtained by selling a lost article, for Rav Yosef knew that distinction and merely thought this case resembled the proceeds. Nimukei Yosef explains that Rachava held money that had been found, and only afterward was it discovered that the money belonged to orphans. It remained in his hand because returning it to minor orphans is not a proper return, since their custody is like an unguarded place. Abaye then taught him the distinction between proceeds of a lost article, for which the finder exerted himself, and the lost money itself, for which he did not. It appears that Rambam explains the Gemara the same way, and therefore did not separately record the case of orphans’ money, since it is simply the rule already stated. This resolves the Sema’s question in seif katan 7 why Rambam omitted the case cited by the Tur. It remains difficult from siman 292, seif 7, where a depositary is not treated as a borrower unless he actually used the money, whereas here Rambam treats the finder as a borrower even before use. We must answer as Rosh writes in the name of Raavad, and as Nimukei Yosef explains in Elu Metziot concerning Rabbi Tarfon’s rule, “What should he do with the money?” A deposit is different because the owner can return and demand it whenever he wishes. The depositary is afraid to touch it lest the owner suddenly arrive. With proceeds of a lost article, however, for which he exerted himself and sold the article, and whose owner is unknown, he does not fear immediate demand and may use the money. Yet here the money is known to belong to orphans. If it consisted of proceeds of a lost article, such as movables he found and sold after effort, Rachava would seemingly have been permitted to use it and would be a borrower according to Rambam. Why is this not like siman 292, since the owners are known? One may answer that it is still considered unknown in the relevant sense, because the money will remain with him for a long period. According to this, if the orphans have a guardian and the finder fears that the guardian may suddenly demand the money, then even proceeds of a lost article do not render him a borrower. This appears to be Rambam’s view, consistent with his ruling that a guardian of a lost article is a paid guardian.
