Therefore, if they were lost through circumstances beyond his control, etc. See Sema, seif katan 38, who says that his level of custodial liability is raised by one degree. Tosafot in Perek HaKones, Bava Kamma 56b, Perek Elu Metziot 29a, and Perek Shevuat HaDayanim 44a, as well as the Rosh there and at the end of Perek HaMafkid, rejected this reasoning. Nevertheless, the Semag and Rabbeinu Yerucham also rule here that with respect to money received from the sale of a lost item, he is only a paid custodian. Maharshal likewise rules this way in Perek HaKones, siman 9. Their reason is that since the finder of a lost item is an unpaid custodian, we are not compelled to interpret the Mishna as addressing unavoidable accidents. Rather, the Mishna teaches that with respect to the proceeds he is a paid custodian. Why should we depart from the Mishna's plain meaning? The Tur's intent must also be this. Perhaps the Sema intended the same, although his language does not indicate it. Examine this carefully.
