Therefore, if the money was lost through an unavoidable accident, he must pay. The Rambam writes this and follows his own ruling that every guardian of a lost object has the law of a paid custodian, as his language was cited above in seif 16. Therefore, with these proceeds, which he is permitted to use and benefit from, his level of responsibility increases by one degree and he becomes liable even for unavoidable accidents. For this reason the Rambam and the Mechaber conclude that if he found money itself, where he is forbidden to use it, the money remains under the ordinary law of a guardian of lost property, namely as a paid custodian. From this we learn that Rama's dissenting view cited there, that a guardian of a lost object is only an unpaid custodian, must hold that when one finds an object and sells it, so that he is permitted to use the proceeds, he is only a paid custodian. Although he may use the money, so long as he has not actually used it he is not treated as a borrower liable for unavoidable accidents. The Tur writes this explicitly. Perhaps Rama relied upon what he wrote regarding a guardian of a lost object in seif 16. Nevertheless, he should have stated the rule here as well, so that one would not mistakenly say that he has the law of a borrower because all the benefit belongs to him if an advantageous business opportunity arises. It is strained to say that Rama omitted the rule because he does not accept the dissenters' view for this very reason. This requires study.
