To pay. So writes the Rambam, following his own view that every guardian of a lost object has the status of a paid guardian, as stated in seif 16. Therefore, with money received from the sale of a lost object, which he is permitted to use and benefit from, his liability rises one level and he is liable even for unavoidable accidents. He therefore concludes that if one finds money that he is forbidden to use, it remains subject to the ordinary law of guardians of lost property, who according to him are paid guardians. From this we understand the Rama's concluding statement that some disagree and hold that such guardians are only unpaid guardians. According to them, with proceeds from a lost object that he is permitted to use, he becomes only a paid guardian, because until he actually uses the money he is not considered a borrower liable for unavoidable accidents. The Tur states this explicitly. The Rama may have relied on his discussion of the law of a guardian of a lost object in seif 16. Nevertheless, he should have written it here as well, so that one not mistakenly say that he has the status of a borrower merely because all potential benefit is his if an opportunity to purchase merchandise arises. This requires study. So writes the Sema. The Shach writes that the theory of raising his liability by one level was already rejected by Tosafot in Bava Kamma 56b, Elu Metziot 29a, and Shevuot 44a, and by the Rosh there and at the end of HaMafkid. Nevertheless, the Semag and Rabbeinu Yerucham also rule here that with proceeds of a lost object he is a paid guardian, and Maharshal likewise rules in the chapter HaKones, siman 9. Their reason is that since a guardian of lost property is ordinarily an unpaid guardian, we are not compelled to interpret the Mishnah as discussing liability for unavoidable accidents. Rather, its teaching is simply that with the proceeds of a lost object he becomes a paid guardian. Why remove the Mishnah from its plain meaning? The Tur's intent must also be understood this way. Perhaps this is also the Sema's intent, although his wording does not appear so. Shach.
