The correct reading is: large male geese and chickens, he tends to them, etc. The word "males" does not appear in the Rambam or Tur, but it follows from Rashi's explanation of the Gemara and must be understood this way. If they were females, the law was already explained in seif 22. Since they lay eggs, he sells the eggs and feeds the birds from the proceeds, just as with an animal that produces enough to pay for its upkeep.

From that point onward he sells them through beit din. This is likewise the Rambam's language. He does not write here that the finder should assess them and retain them in partnership, as he wrote at the end of seif 22, because these are males and produce no profit. What partnership would there be in them? It appears that even in seif 22, where he appraises the animal to himself as a partnership, and in seif 21, where he appraises the tefillin to himself, the Rambam also requires appraisal by beit din. If so, it is somewhat difficult that the Rambam did not state this explicitly there.
