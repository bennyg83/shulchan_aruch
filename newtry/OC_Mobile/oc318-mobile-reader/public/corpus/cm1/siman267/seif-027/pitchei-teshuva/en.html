And he claimed. See Baer Heitev, which states that if the owner claims, "I saw that you lifted..." See Taz, who disagrees with the Sema and writes that the Sema's distinction is difficult. Why should this differ from the case where the finder says, "I returned one of them to you," where there is likewise a direct dispute between claimant and respondent, yet the Sages were concerned that imposing an oath would deter people from returning lost property? Indeed, there is a stronger argument there, because the finder can avoid the problem by returning the object before witnesses, yet the Sages still waived the oath. All the more so here. Taz therefore concludes that whenever the object was found under circumstances giving it the legal status of lost property, the finder is never required to swear. The Tur and the abridged rulings of the Rosh likewise state that even if the owner claims, "You found two purses for me and they were tied together," or the finder says, "I found two and returned one," no oath is required. Levush rules similarly. It is plausible that these words also stood in the Shulchan Aruch and were omitted in printing, as implied by Tumim to siman 88.
