One says that its length is such, etc. The Rambam writes in chapter 13, law 7: 'One gave its length and width, and the other described its borders; it is given to the one who gave its length and width.' This is the Rambam's explanation of the Gemara, and the Maggid Mishna writes likewise. The Kesef Mishna writes that this is difficult, because the measure of the borders appears to be the same as the garment's length, which the Rambam already discussed. He suggests that describing the borders means giving both their length and width. This is not conclusive, for the borders may not be included in the garment's stated length. It is possible that if one gives the length and width of the borders, the garment is not awarded to the one who gives the general length and width. This requires study. It appears that everything depends on the judge's assessment and the manner in which such garments are made.
