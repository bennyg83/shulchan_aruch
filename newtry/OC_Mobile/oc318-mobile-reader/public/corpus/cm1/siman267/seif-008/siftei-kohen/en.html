If two came, etc. Shiltei Giborim writes: It appears to me that if one says to the other, 'Either you swear that it is yours, or I will swear that it is mine,' we listen to him. Likewise, if they agree to divide it between themselves, we listen to them, and if one admits to the other, we accept the admission. It appears that if one says, 'I will swear and you shall not,' and the other says the reverse, the item is deposited and not awarded to either.
One brought witnesses, and the other brought witnesses and identifying marks, etc. The Darkei Moshe writes this in the name of Hagahot Mordechai at the beginning of Perek Shnayim Ochazin. In my opinion, Hagahot Mordechai there discusses only the law addressed by the Gemara and cited by the Tur in the following seif. This law is not mentioned there at all. Perhaps, since the first claimant does not know the identifying marks, the circumstances demonstrate that his witnesses are false, unless the matter is such that he would not have been expected to know them. This requires study.
