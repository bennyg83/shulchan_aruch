(Seif 27, and it is impossible.) The Sema, seif katan 30, writes that if the owner claims, “I saw you,” and so forth, the finder must swear. This is astonishing. Why is this different from the owner claiming, “You returned only one of them,” where there is likewise a dispute of claim and denial, yet the Sages were concerned that people would refrain from picking up lost articles lest they become subject to an oath? The same concern applies here. The finder does not know that the owner sees him. If you require him to swear whenever the owner later claims, “I saw you lift both,” every finder will fear that the owner may later make such a claim and will refrain from picking up the object to return it. This is all the more compelling than the case of “I returned one,” because there the finder can protect himself by returning it before witnesses, yet the Sages still exempted him from an oath. It therefore appears to me that whenever the circumstances give the object the legal status of a lost article, the finder is never subject to an oath.
