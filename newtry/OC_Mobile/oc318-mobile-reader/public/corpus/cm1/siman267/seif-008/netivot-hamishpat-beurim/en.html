And this claimant has witnesses and an identifying mark. See Shach, seif katan 6, who leaves this ruling unresolved. In my humble opinion, the reason is as Tosafot write in Bava Kamma 72, s.v. ein lecha bo, that migo is ineffective where two pairs of witnesses contradict one another because a migo is no stronger than witnesses, and two witnesses have the force of a hundred. Thus, although a migo is normally effective, it cannot prevail where contradictory witnesses neutralize one another, for that would be like a migo against witnesses. The same applies to identifying marks. Since they cannot prevail against witnesses, they do not prevail even when two sets of witnesses contradict each other. The case must concern two pairs who contradict each other, with one pair testifying concerning a time earlier than the time addressed by the other pair. We then leave the item in the possession of the second claimant based upon the presumption that whatever is in a person's possession is his, and presume that he certainly acquired it from the other party.
