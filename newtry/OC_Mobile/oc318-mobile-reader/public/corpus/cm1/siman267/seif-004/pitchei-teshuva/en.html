And he claimed. See Baer Heitev, who writes that if he claims, "I saw that you lifted it," etc. See Taz, who disagrees and writes that Sema's ruling is difficult. Why is this different from one who claims, "I returned one of them to you"? There too the claimant and defendant disagree, yet the Sages were concerned that people might refrain from returning lost property. Indeed there is an even stronger argument there, because the finder could protect himself by returning it before witnesses, and nevertheless the Sages were concerned. All the more so here. Taz therefore concludes that whenever a person finds an object in circumstances that give it the legal status of a lost object, he is not subject to any oath. See what I wrote above in siman 88, seif 32, seif katan 11. Taz's reference to the case of claiming, "I returned one of them to you," means the Tur's ruling that even if the claimant says, "You found two purses for me and they were tied together," or the finder says, "I found two and returned one to you," he need not swear. The same appears in the abridged rulings of the Rosh, Gittin, chapter HaNizakin, siman 4, and in the Levush. It is quite possible that the same wording originally appeared in Shulchan Aruch and was omitted in printing. Tumim above, siman 88, seif katan 22, also implies that his text of Shulchan Aruch contained it.
