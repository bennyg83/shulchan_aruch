Until he gives distinct identifying signs. In the Derishah I cited the Maggid Mishneh's classification of signs. A hole beside a particular letter or a comparable unique mark is a fully distinct sign, treated in other contexts like testimony. Length or shortness, exact measure, weight, or place is an intermediate sign and is ordinarily called a distinct sign without qualification. This is what the Rambam and Mechaber mean here. By Torah law a lost object is returned on such a sign, as stated in seif 7. Color such as white or red is not considered a distinct sign at all. The law is derived from "until your brother seeks it," which the Sages interpret to require inquiry into whether the claimant is truthful and not fraudulent. By Torah law, once he gives an ordinarily distinct sign we do not suspect fraud. Originally this was sufficient for an ordinary claimant unless he was already known to be fraudulent. For a known fraud, the Sages required proof. Once fraud became widespread, they enacted that an ordinary claimant must either bring witnesses that the object is his or bring proof that he is not fraudulent, after which the Torah rule returns and the object can be restored on the basis of distinct signs.
