Without an oath. See Baer HaGolah. See Beit Ephraim, Choshen Mishpat, siman 59, who writes that the ruling of siman 267, derived by the Maggid Mishneh from the rule that a finder does not swear, requires study. That exemption was stated regarding the found object itself, where the claimant says that more was found. But regarding the finder's expenses, it may be that his law is like any person who incurs expenses on another's property: where one party knows and the other does not, the one who knows swears and collects. There is no reason to exempt him from an oath. Nor can one say that he should be exempt because of an enactment encouraging people to return lost property, lest they refuse to involve themselves. He can protect himself by producing witnesses or by having the expenses assessed by beit din. He cites Responsa Mahari Weil, siman 148, concerning one who spent money to ransom another from danger, who must swear and collect, and Responsa Maharik, siman 173. This shows that even where the act is itself restoration of a lost person, the Sages did not waive the oath, even when the owner's claim is uncertain. See there, and see Nachalat Tzvi on this matter.
