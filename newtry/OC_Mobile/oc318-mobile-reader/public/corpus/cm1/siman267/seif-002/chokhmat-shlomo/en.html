Seif 21. One who found tefillin may appraise their value and keep them. Note: Sema writes that this is specifically true of tefillin. In my opinion that limitation is difficult. Siman 72 states that when an item of the same type is readily available in the market, one need not swear that the actual object is no longer in his possession. If the owner cares about the specific physical object, why is no oath required there? If he does not care, why may the finder here not appraise the object and leave its value? It is strained to answer that the Sages did not institute an oath for such a preference while nevertheless forbidding substitution at the outset. The matter requires study.
