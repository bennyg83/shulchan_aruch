Some say that with a distinct sign it is returned, etc. This opinion does not deny the explicit baraita and poskim that once fraud became widespread they enacted that the claimant be told, "Bring witnesses that you are not fraudulent and take it." Rather, they hold that even an established fraud receives an object upon a fully sufficient sign such as exact length, size, measure, or weight. They understand that originally an ordinary person could receive an object even upon a weak sign such as color. Therefore, even today, if he brings proof that he is not fraudulent, or if he is known to be a Torah scholar, the object can be returned upon ordinary intermediate signs, as explained in the following seif. The dispute depends upon interpretation of the Gemara, which I discussed in the Derishah. This "some say" also disagrees with the preceding seif's statement that an established fraud must bring witnesses that the property itself is his.
