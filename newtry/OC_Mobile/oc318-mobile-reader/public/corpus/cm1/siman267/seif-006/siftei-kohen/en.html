Some say that with a distinctive identifying mark, etc. See Sema, seif katan 9, who writes that this view holds that even a completely fraudulent claimant receives the item upon giving a distinctive identifying mark. Everything written in that seif katan is incorrect. The Rosh explicitly indicates that originally an ordinary person did not receive an item on the basis of color, being long or short, but only through a mark of measure, weight, or the like. A fraudulent claimant, and today even an ordinary claimant, receives nothing through such marks, but only through a mark that is conclusive in other areas as well, such as a hole beside a particular letter. According to the Rosh, such a mark is sufficient even for a fraudulent claimant. All this is explicit in the Rosh and in the Gemara in Perek Elu Metziot, folios 27 and 28, and Perek Shnayim Ochazin, Bava Metzia 18. Examine them carefully. The Bach likewise explains the Rosh this way. It appears that the Sema followed the wording of the Tur and Rama, who, after citing the Rosh, wrote that measure and weight are distinctive identifying marks. Their intent, however, is only that these are sufficient marks in the law of lost property when the claimant is known not to be fraudulent. They exclude color and being long or short, which do not help even if witnesses establish that he is not fraudulent, as is evident from the Rosh and Maggid Mishna. No authority can hold that measure or weight suffices for a fraudulent claimant. We rule like Rav Nachman in monetary law that the garment itself is announced, as all the authorities write. Rav Nachman must therefore interpret the Mishna's case, 'he stated the lost item but did not give its identifying marks,' as one who gave nonconclusive marks but not conclusive marks, while a fraudulent claimant receives nothing even if he gave conclusive marks, as the Gemara explicitly states and as explained in seif 5. Under every interpretation, measure and weight are at least conclusive marks, while color and being long or short are no marks at all and do not suffice even for one known not to be fraudulent. It therefore follows explicitly that a fraudulent claimant receives nothing on the basis of measure or weight according to any authority. The Sema's words require study.
