He assesses their value and places it upon himself. Meaning, he may keep the tefillin for himself, account for their value, and if he wants, use them to fulfill the mitzvah of tefillin. The owner will not object, because tefillin are readily available for purchase from scribes. Rambam concludes in chapter 13, law 13, "they are made solely for their mitzvah." This wording implies that other items, even readily available ones, may be beloved to a person because he is accustomed to that specific item, whereas with tefillin a person ordinarily does not care whether he fulfills the mitzvah with this pair or another, so long as both are presumed kosher. The Tur gives only the Mechaber's wording. The Tur also writes there, "likewise produce that has begun to rot, and anything similar, he sells immediately." It is surprising that the authors of the Shulchan Aruch omitted this.
