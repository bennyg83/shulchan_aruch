He then wonders why the authors of the Shulchan Aruch omitted the law of produce that has begun to rot. In my opinion, there is no difficulty. Such produce is sold because the burden of caring for it exceeds its benefit, and this case is certainly included in that broader rule. The Rambam, chapter 13 of the Laws of Theft and Lost Property, and the Semag, positive commandment 74, folio 153d, state explicitly: Any item whose care is burdensome is cared for for three days, and thereafter sold through beit din; likewise produce that began to rot and similar items are sold through beit din. Since the Mechaber writes in seif 24 that any item whose care exceeds its benefit is sold, he did not need to state separately the obvious and more readily included case of produce beginning to rot. This appears correct.
