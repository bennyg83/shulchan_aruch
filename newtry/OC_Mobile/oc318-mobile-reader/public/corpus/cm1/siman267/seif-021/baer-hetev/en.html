And he sets them aside. Meaning, he may retain them for himself and place them on his head, fulfilling the mitzvah of tefillin with them if he wishes to acquire them for himself, because their owner certainly does not object, since tefillin are readily available for purchase from scribes. The Rambam concludes: 'They are made solely for their mitzvah.' From this wording it appears that with other objects, even if readily available for purchase, a person is nevertheless attached to an object to which he is accustomed. Tefillin are different, because a person ordinarily does not care whether he fulfills the mitzvah with these or with others, provided the others are presumed kosher. So writes the Sema. The Shach explains differently: since tefillin are made only for their mitzvah and for no other use, a person is content that they be sold and used for a mitzvah. This differs from other objects.
