Like a paid custodian. The Gemara records a dispute. One view treats the finder as a paid custodian because he must tend to the lost object by airing garments, spreading them out, and otherwise caring for each item according to its needs. While he is occupied with the mitzvah of tending to it, if a poor person asks him for charity or bread he is exempt because one engaged in one mitzvah is exempt from another. This benefit is his compensation, and therefore he is liable for theft and loss like an ordinary paid custodian. The other view disagrees. Since it is uncommon for a poor person to approach him at the exact time he is engaged in caring for the lost object, the potential benefit is too remote to make him a paid custodian. He therefore has only the liability of an unpaid custodian.
As we rule regarding a pledge, and the same applies here. See there. Regarding a pledge equal in value to the money lent, the Tur and Rama rule that according to all opinions the lender is treated as a paid custodian, because at the time of the loan it is as though he explicitly accepted responsibility for theft and loss up to the amount of the debt, though not for unavoidable accidents. Rama's intention here is not that the two cases are identical in every respect. Rather, just as regarding the value of the pledge beyond the debt there is a doubt and we do not extract money, so here, regarding the entire value of the lost object, we follow the same practical rule. At the end of siman 72 the conclusion is that because of the doubt we do not extract, and the custodian has the law of an unpaid custodian regarding the excess. The same practical result applies here.
