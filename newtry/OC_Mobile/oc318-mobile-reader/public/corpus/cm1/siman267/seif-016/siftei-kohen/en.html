Accordingly, the finder who guards the lost item is exempt from payment, but if the owner of the lost item seized payment, it is not taken from him. Maharshal, Perek HaKones, siman 9, entered between great mountains and sought to decide that the finder is fully an unpaid custodian. He argued from the rule that one prohibited by a vow from benefiting another may nevertheless return the other's lost item, and from the rule that one may cover blood with earth from an idolatrous city because commandments were not given for benefit, and similarly regarding a shofar. See his lengthy discussion. I examined his words carefully and found no substance in them. The case of one prohibited by a vow is different, as the Gemara in Perek Ein Bein HaMudar, Nedarim 33, says that Rav Yosef's peruta benefit is uncommon. The Rosh explains that it is uncommon for a poor person to appear at that exact moment. The Ran likewise writes that it is uncommon for a poor person to come request charity from him at that time. Therefore we do not say that he may not return the item because he benefits, since such an uncommon benefit is not included in the prohibition of benefit. Nevertheless, because it is possible that a poor person will come and he will benefit, he is a paid custodian and is liable for theft or loss. Maharshal's proof from the earth of an idolatrous city and from a shofar also overlooks Tosafot at the end of Perek Shevuat HaDayanim. Tosafot ask why one prohibited by a vow from benefiting another may blow a shofar for him for a mitzva, and why one prohibited from benefiting a spring may immerse in it for a mitzva, since according to Rav Yosef he benefits by becoming a paid custodian. They answer that guarding a lost item or collateral is different, because during the many occasions on which he handles it, spreads it out for its benefit, and puts it away, he may repeatedly be exempted from giving charity. In the cases of the shofar and immersion, however, he can arrange to blow or immerse at a place and time when no poor person will arrive. We therefore maintain that it remains an unresolved question of law whether the finder is a paid or unpaid custodian, as all the later authorities write.
