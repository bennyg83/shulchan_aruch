A finder of lost property is only an unpaid custodian. See Shach, seif katan 14, who gives the reason that the exemption from giving a perutah to a poor person is uncommon. It appears that even if the finder actually did once benefit from the exemption of Rav Yosef and thereby saved a perutah, he does not become a paid custodian. The Gemara and poskim make no such distinction. The reason is that when he initially accepted custody, he did not do so for compensation. Since the benefit was not expected or common, the custody was not undertaken in exchange for it. A later incidental benefit does not retroactively turn the arrangement into paid custody. This differs from siman 185, seif 1, where if the owner sends him a gift during the custody he can become a paid custodian. There the gift was consciously sent because of the custody, so it is as though he accepted compensation for guarding. Here the exemption from a poor person's perutah occurs automatically and not because the owner intended to pay him for the guarding.
