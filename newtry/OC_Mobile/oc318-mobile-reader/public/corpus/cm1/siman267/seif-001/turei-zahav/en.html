(Seif 1: a garden and a ruin.) In our master's wording it says that by returning the item there "he has fulfilled" the obligation. This after-the-fact wording is difficult, just as we analyze the phrase "he has fulfilled" regarding the blessing "HaKel HaKadosh" at the end of the first chapter of Berachot. Rambam likewise concludes here that "he has fulfilled the mitzvah," also sounding after the fact. Yet the Gemara derives the rule directly from Scripture. It appears that they understand the Gemara's phrase, "The Torah included many forms of return," to mean that restitution has different levels: some are proper initially and others are sufficient only after the fact.
