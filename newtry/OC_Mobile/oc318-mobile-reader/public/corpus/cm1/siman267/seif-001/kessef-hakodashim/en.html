See Shach, seif katan 14. In siman 267 the Shach answers the questions of the Yam Shel Shlomo against the view that one who guards a lost object is a paid custodian. One can add that in the case of a person forbidden by vow to benefit from the owner, and in the other cases discussed there, the finder can undertake that even if he becomes exempt from giving a perutah to a poor person while occupied with the mitzvah, he will nevertheless give that perutah to the same poor person or seek another poor person and give it to him. In this way he receives no actual economic benefit from returning the object. There is no concern that voluntarily doing so makes him a fool for performing an obligation from which he was exempt. On the contrary, it is proper to be stringent with oneself in this way. By contrast, for purposes of determining whether the finder is a paid custodian, the mere fact that the law gives him a possible exemption from giving that perutah is itself a benefit that can classify him as paid, even if in practice he chooses to give the money anyway. His voluntary payment does not erase the legal benefit available to him. This requires further study.
