Seif 8, gloss. Note: See my Even HaEzer, sixth edition, siman 1, where I concluded that this ruling concerns non-distinctive identifying marks. With a truly distinctive mark, the object is given to the person who provides the mark according to all opinions. There, seif 16. Whether there is a distinction between an exceptionally strong identifying mark or witnesses establishing ownership, and an ordinary intermediate mark, see my Orach Chaim writings, Hilkhot Tefillin, siman 39. For matters relevant here, including whether a guardian of lost property is a paid guardian and his obligations of spreading out and airing the object, see my Choshen Mishpat, fourth edition, siman 301, responsum to the community of Hrymailiv. Seif 16, gloss: Some say that a guardian of lost property is only an unpaid guardian. See Shach and Lechem Mishneh at the beginning of chapter 7 of Nedarim. According to Lechem Mishneh's distinction, when the finder is carrying the lost object home and it is stolen or lost, he would be only an unpaid guardian; only afterward, when it rests in his house and he becomes obligated to spread and air it, would he become a paid guardian. This has practical significance. See, however, my Nedarim writings where I challenged that distinction and discussed the Ran, Shach, and Rambam at length.
