He is obligated to tend to the lost object, etc. The Tur explains: If he knows the owner, he must tend to the item until he brings it into the owner's hand. If he does not bring it directly to him but places it in the owner's garden or ruin, he fulfills the mitzvah even without the owner's knowledge, provided it is guarded there. This differs from a thief, robber, and the four types of custodians who return entrusted property. They ordinarily must restore it to its place with the owner's knowledge. If they return it without informing the owner and it is then stolen or dies from hunger, they may remain liable. With lost property Scripture repeats "you shall surely return them," teaching that even return without the owner's knowledge is sufficient when the item is placed in a guarded location. Rashi explains this in Bava Metzia 31a. The Mechaber uses the Rambam's shorter formulation, but his closing statement that the owner's knowledge is unnecessary applies also to the opening clause.
He returned the lost object in the morning, etc. The ruling mentions morning because the owners are then usually at home, entering and leaving, and will see the object. Therefore, even if he places it in a location that is not fully guarded, it is treated as though he delivered it into the owner's hand.
