He is liable, etc. Bava Kamma 56a: "he said to him, it means into his garden, etc." This follows Rambam's approach, which rules like Rav Yosef, as stated above. Likewise Bava Metzia 31a: "you shall return them, from this I know only, etc." According to Tosafos, who rule like Rabbah, he is liable only with the law of an unpaid bailee. See Tosafos to Bava Kamma there, s.v. "be'hahu." This is also how they explain the passage in Eilu Metziyos, etc.; see there 27a, s.v. "lo" and s.v. "ela."
(Collected note) In a guarded place. Bava Metzia 31a: "what are the circumstances? If, etc.; rather, etc." And there 32a, Rav said, "a stable, etc., and it is not guarded, etc."
But if, etc. Bava Kamma there: "not from the place, etc."
If he returned, etc. There. Although according to Rav Yosef these explanations are unnecessary, the law is nevertheless correct because the Amoraim do not dispute that point. Maggid Mishneh.
But with a living animal, etc. See Tosafos there, s.v. "le'olam, etc."
(Collected note) When does this apply, etc. Rambam's reading there is: "he said to him, that case concerns a living animal, etc.," and not "I concede to you," which implies that the answer refers to taking it from his house. He also establishes it as a case where it was returned to the same place, like the first clause. According to our reading it is difficult, for then why in the first clause does the Gemara force two separate qualifications? The language also implies that it did not retract its first answer. Moreover, "I concede" implies that it was known that the statement concerned a living animal. How was this so obvious?
He saw, etc. This is implied by the Mishnah in Bava Metzia 30b.
And he need not, etc. The Gemara there 31a.
