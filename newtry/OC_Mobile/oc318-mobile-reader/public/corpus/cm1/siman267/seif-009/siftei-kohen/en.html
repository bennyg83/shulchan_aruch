The first supplied identifying marks, etc. This applies even to distinctive marks, as the Rosh writes. His wording implies even the most conclusive marks, and Maharshal in Chokhmat Shlomo likewise indicates this. Maharshal writes, however, that Rashi does not appear to agree, but that is not correct. Rashi writes: 'Even if you say that identifying marks are of Torah authority, the teaching states a greater novelty, namely that even here witnesses are superior.' His meaning is as follows. If identifying marks are not of Torah authority, then even the most conclusive mark is effective only by logical inference, since nothing about identifying marks is stated in the Torah. It is then obvious that witnesses, who are explicitly stated in the Torah and are also logically persuasive, are superior. The novelty is that even if identifying marks are of Torah authority, such that even an ordinary significant mark derives from Torah law and one might have thought it equivalent to witnesses, we are taught that witnesses are superior. Since witnesses are superior to an ordinary significant mark, they are also superior to the most conclusive mark, for once identifying marks are of Torah authority there is no legal distinction between the levels for this purpose. Examine this carefully.
