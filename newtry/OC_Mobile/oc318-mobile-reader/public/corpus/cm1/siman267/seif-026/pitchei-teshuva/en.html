Without an oath. See Baer HaGolah. Responsa Beit Ephraim, Choshen Mishpat 59, questions the rule derived by Maggid Mishneh from the enactment that a finder does not swear regarding the amount of the found property. That enactment concerns the object itself, when the owner claims that more was found. With respect to expenses incurred by the finder, however, the rule should seemingly resemble other cases where one person incurs expenses for another's property: where one party knows the amount and the other does not, the knowledgeable party swears and collects, rather than collecting without an oath. Nor should one exempt him from an oath merely because otherwise people will refuse to involve themselves in returning lost property, because expenses can be documented by witnesses or assessed by beit din. Beit Ephraim cites Mahari Weil 148, concerning one who spent money to ransom another from danger, and Maharik 173, both requiring the claimant to swear and collect. Thus even where the expenditure itself constitutes returning a lost object, indeed rescuing the person's body, the Sages did not waive the oath merely because the other party can plead only uncertainty. See also Nachalat Tzvi.
