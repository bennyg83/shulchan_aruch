Something he never studied before. Rambam likewise writes this in chapter 13, law 13. The Maggid Mishneh comments that Rashba writes in Ramban's name that this rule was stated only regarding a Torah scroll, Prophets, and Writings. One who is accustomed to them need not handle them at all, while one unaccustomed may pull them back and forth and there is concern he might tear them. Nowadays, however, when Gemara is written, a person who reviewed his chapter one hundred times and one who has never seen it are alike in being permitted to touch and handle it, because it requires deeper thought and he may learn from it initially. Although Rashba states this regarding a borrower, the Maggid Mishneh says the same likely applies to a lost item, but leaves it requiring study. Nimukei Yosef cites Rashba's words in the laws of lost property without entertaining any distinction between lost property and a borrower. It is surprising that neither the Mechaber nor Rama mentioned this here, though Darkei Moshe cites it.
