Something not within his possession, etc. See above, siman 209:5, and what I wrote there in the name of Responsa Maharashdam; Responsa Rabbi Avraham ibn Chaim, siman 66; Responsa Rama, siman 78, pages 140 and 147; Responsa Maharashdam, simanim 265, 283, 288, and 373; and Responsa Rabbi Shmuel Kohen, part 2, siman 11.
And he said, 'This field,' etc. The Sema explains that because he specified and identified the property and the inheritance comes automatically, it is not treated as fully comparable to something not yet in existence. This is also the Rosh's language in the chapter Mi SheHayah Nasui. His meaning is that if one says, 'This field that I will purchase,' the transfer concerns something not yet in existence even though he said 'this,' as is evident from Bava Metzia 16a and many other passages. This is unlike the Ir Shushan, who writes that some say the law applies equally if he says, 'This field, when I purchase it,' and if he says, 'This field, when I inherit it.' See Responsa Mahari ibn Lev, part 1, siman 63, pages 87 and 88.
