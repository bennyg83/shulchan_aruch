(Seif 3: He need not return it.) The Sema, seif katan 7, expresses surprise and writes that otherwise there is no reason why he need not return it. This is difficult. In Bava Batra, in the chapter Mi Shemet, it is explicitly stated: “They sent from there: A son who sold his father’s property,” and so forth. The Gemara says that this is a difficult monetary law for which no reason is known, and it could establish the case only under another ruling there. Why did it not explain the statement in its straightforward sense, that the son need not return the money even if it has not been consumed, and that this itself is the novel rule that is difficult in monetary law and has no reason? Editor’s note: Does this resolve the matter according to him? It remains difficult, and necessarily so. Since he comes by virtue of his grandfather, why should he be in a better position than the person through whom he derives his claim, when his grandfather himself was obligated to pay? It appears to me that according to the Sema there is no difficulty. Where the money has not been consumed, it is obvious that the son must pay, because we rule that an oral loan may be collected from heirs when their predecessor left them property subject to collection. It is therefore impossible to explain the statement sent from there in that manner. This is clear.
