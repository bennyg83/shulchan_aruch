And Levi gave Shimon the deed that was in his possession. See Responsa VeShav HaKohen, siman 48, who writes that the delivery of the deed is not essential; that was simply what happened in the particular case. In truth, even if the third party did not hand the deed to Shimon, Shimon acquired retroactively. This is also implied by Levush at the end of siman 54, who writes that the arrangement is comparable to one who sells his field effective immediately and the creditor tells the debtor, "When you have the money I will return it to you." If the debtor fails to pay at the stipulated time, the sale stands from the original date on which the field was sold. See there further for his explanation of Beit Yosef at the end of siman 54 and his disagreement with Gidulei Terumah on this matter. In siman 49 he further raises a doubt concerning Reuven, who rented a house to Shimon for one year and, during the rental term, sold the house to Shimon with the sale to take effect after the year. During that year Shimon sold the house to Levi. Do we say that because Shimon's purchase was not to take effect until after the year, and before his own acquisition took effect he sold the house onward, Levi does not acquire, just as one who says, "This field shall be acquired by you when I purchase it" does not transfer ownership? Or perhaps, since he established in another responsum, siman 47, cited below, siman 260, seif 4, that according to most poskim rental for a day is treated as a sale for that day in all respects, then once the house was rented to Shimon for the year, the later sale to him was set to take effect immediately upon expiration of the rental. The house is therefore considered sufficiently his even during the rental period that his sale to Levi during that period is valid. He writes that there appears to be proof from the Tosefta at the end of the chapter HaMafkid: one who rents a cow from another and it is stolen, and so forth, with the result that double or fourfold and fivefold payments are paid to the second party. He discusses the Tosefta at length and concludes from it that if one rents an item for a year and afterward sells that same item to the renter with the sale to take effect after the year, then if the purchaser sells it to a second person during the rental term, that sale is effective. He nevertheless concludes that practical application requires further study, because the early authorities did not cite this Tosefta, among other reasons.
