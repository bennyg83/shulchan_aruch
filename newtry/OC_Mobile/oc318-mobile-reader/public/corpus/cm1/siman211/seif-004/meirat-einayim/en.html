There is an authority who says, etc., until "rather, I inherit from my father's father." The Mechaber presents this law with the phrase "there is an authority who says" because in his view it is not entirely analogous to the previous case. There the son sold property that had never yet come into his possession, whereas here Reuven sold his own property lawfully while his father had not yet collected from him the debt Reuven owed. Examine this carefully.
And Reuven and Yaakov died. Meaning, Reuven died first. If Yaakov died first, Reuven acquired his father's estate while still alive and his father's debt against him was extinguished through merger in the inheritance, leaving the property in the purchaser's possession. This is obvious.
