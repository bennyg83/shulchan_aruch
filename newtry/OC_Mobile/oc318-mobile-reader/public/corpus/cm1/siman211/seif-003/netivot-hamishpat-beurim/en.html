And he need not return the money. Sema, seif katan 7, explains that the money had already been spent. Taz says the buyer then has an oral loan that can be collected from the heirs. In my view this is not because it was a debt owed by the grandfather who later died. The money was spent only after his death for burial, so no obligation could have been imposed upon the deceased. Rather, the grandson says, “I come through my grandfather,” and as heir he has the obligations associated with burying the decedent. The son had sold “what I will inherit from my father,” the sale was void, and the buyer’s money remained his. That money was then used for the burial, an expense for which the heir was responsible. The resulting loan is therefore the heir’s own obligation.
