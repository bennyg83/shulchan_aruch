A person who has a deposit in another's possession may transfer it by sale or gift, because a deposit remains in the legal possession of its owner and is presumed to exist. If the depositary denies holding it, the owner cannot transfer it, for it is like lost property that is not in his possession. A loan, however, was given to be spent and therefore is not presently in existence, so it cannot be transferred except through a three-party declaration. If the loan is evidenced by a promissory note, the creditor may transfer the note by writing and delivery, since there is a tangible document through which its lien may be acquired. Rama: Reuven borrowed from Shimon and gave him an unconditional deed of sale over his field effective immediately, depositing it with Levi on condition that Levi deliver it to Shimon if Reuven failed to pay by the appointed date. Before that date Shimon sold the field to Yehudah. When the date arrived, Reuven did not pay, and Levi delivered the deed to Shimon. Shimon's sale is valid even though it preceded the due date.
