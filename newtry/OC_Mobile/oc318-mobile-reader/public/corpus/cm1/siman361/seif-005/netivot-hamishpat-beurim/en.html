When taken by force it is not called a change of possession. This is difficult from siman 368, where one who rescues property from Jewish robbers after the owners despaired can acquire it, and from siman 181 concerning a caravan attacked by raiders, where one who saves the property can acquire for himself after despair. In both cases the property left the owner through force, yet later possession can still matter. The distinction is this. If the owner despairs only after the object has already reached the robber's hands, that is ye'ush of stolen property. The robber has already become obligated in restitution. Ye'ush does not by itself cure that obligation, and other people may be barred from acquiring because the stolen object is needed for the robber's restitution duty. But if the owner sees the armed force approaching while the property is still before him and despairs because he realizes he cannot save it, that ye'ush occurs before the robber takes possession. It resembles ye'ush of a lost object. If another person rescues the object before the robber takes it, this is exactly like rescuing from a lion, river, or fire. The owner's despair already operated while the object was outside the robber's possession. Even if the rescuer takes it only after the robber physically seized it, the object had already acquired the legal status of a lost object through prior ye'ush. The robber certainly becomes a wrongdoer because his threatened taking caused the despair, but the underlying object had already become subject to lost-property ye'ush before entering his hands. Thus one who later saves it need not return it. The robber's own payment obligation can remain under the law of causation because his coercive act caused the owner's loss.
