(Netivot HaMishpat, seif katan 1) According to this, it is understandable, etc., that he should acquire to his own detriment. Note: I did not understand his reasoning. Acquisition caused by theft applies only to liability for unavoidable accidents, and that is to the thief's detriment. But acquisition according to value is a full acquisition that makes the stolen object the thief's. The Gemara's question is: since the Torah said not to do this act and he nevertheless stole, why should a subsequent change be effective to acquire the stolen object? This question applies equally to a change that occurs by itself. Why should such a change make the stolen object his, when the change by itself, absent the theft, would accomplish nothing?
