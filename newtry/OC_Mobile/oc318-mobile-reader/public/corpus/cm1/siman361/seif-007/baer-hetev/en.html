Property. The Mechaber writes generally "property" because nowadays even heirs' movable property is subject to collection by a creditor and by one whose property was stolen. The Tur states this explicitly in simanim 341 and 7. Sema.
From minors. Beit din adjudicates a claim based on witnesses to theft even against minor heirs, as the Tur and Mechaber write in siman 110. So too the Itur, Sha'ar 14, cited in Darkei Moshe, siman 28, seif 19. Certainly, if the stolen object itself is extant, it is removed from the minors, as the Tur writes here. Ibid.
Are believed. They have a migo that they could have said, "We returned it to you." Even according to the view that one who robbed before witnesses must return before witnesses, that applies to the robber himself, but his sons are believed. This is only where the adult sons themselves make the claim; we do not advance for them an uncommon claim. So writes Rabbeinu Yerucham, netiv 26, part III. The Shach writes that this follows the Rosh, but the Ramban in Milchamot holds that we do advance for heirs a claim of purchase through the migo that the father could have claimed he returned it. He explains our case as one where the object was presently seen in the heirs' possession, so we do not advance an uncertain purchase claim because the father himself would not have been believed in that situation. If the heirs make a definite claim and the object was not seen in their hands before litigation, so that they could have claimed it was returned, they are believed through migo. The Nimukei Yosef cites this in the names of the Ramban and Re'ah, and the Ritva rules likewise. The Rosh and Ramban follow their respective views whether beit din advances an uncommon claim for heirs. Since that matter is itself a legal doubt, the same applies here, and money cannot be extracted from the heirs due to doubt. The Darkei Moshe and Sema did not note the dissenting authorities. All the more so according to what I wrote in siman 104, where the Ramban's view appears primary. The Shach further writes that even if they say, "Our father told us he settled accounts with you," they are believed, as above, siman 297, in the gloss.
Are not. The Sema explains that a migo of "we returned it" is a more brazen claim than saying, "we know our father settled accounts with you." The Shach disagrees and writes that such a migo is valid, as shown in siman 81, seif 23; the end of siman 82 in the rules of migo, letter 6; and siman 93, seif 2. The Sema was led astray by thinking that according to the dissenting view they are not believed even where they could have claimed return. That is not so. See the Shach's proof, and Responsa Maharit, simanim 9 and 112, and Responsa Rashdam, simanim 198 and 200.
