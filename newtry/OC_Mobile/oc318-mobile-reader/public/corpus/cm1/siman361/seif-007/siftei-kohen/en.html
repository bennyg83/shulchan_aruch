But if there were witnesses that he robbed it, etc. The Sema and Levush explain that the heirs cannot use a migo of 'we returned it' because that would be more brazen than saying that their father settled accounts with the claimant. This is incorrect. We do recognize such a migo, as explained above in siman 81, at the end of siman 82, and in siman 93. They misunderstood the alternative opinion. That opinion is the Tur's, drawn from the Rosh, who expressly says that the case is one where it is known not only that the property reached the father's hands as robbery but also that it remains in the heirs' hands, so neither the father nor the heirs could have claimed return. Where there are merely witnesses that it came to the father, but no present sighting, the heirs are believed through the migo that the father returned it or that they returned it. Tosafot, Baal HaMaor, Ramban, and Maharshal rule likewise. The alternative opinion therefore comes only to explain that the heirs' assertion that their father settled accounts is accepted through a migo, not as an independently superior claim. See the cited responsa for applications.
