And even if it remained in his possession, etc. Although I wrote in siman 137 that partners commonly object to one another's possession, nevertheless, because it is apparent and known that this item had belonged to the partnership, it remains presumptively owned by both of them. These are the words of the Sema. He wrote nothing there, and it appears that he intended the Tur's statement in seif 13 there, that it remained in his possession for a long time. This follows Tosafot at the beginning of Bava Batra, that after a long period partners commonly object, so the possessor may claim that he purchased it. I do not understand the Sema's statement, 'nevertheless, because it is apparent,' for what difference does its former partnership ownership make once partners commonly object? Consider a case where it was known to have belonged solely to the other party: the possessor is believed to claim that he purchased it. All the more so should this be true where it belonged to both of them. The fact that he is a partner does not prevent his objection. Moreover, a wall likewise belongs to both parties. It therefore appears to me that the Tur speaks of a long period during which partners do not commonly object, whereas Tosafot speaks of an even longer period during which they do commonly object, as I wrote above in siman 157. Examine carefully. See Responsa R. Avraham ben Chaim, siman 122; Responsa Mabit, part I, siman 73, second series, siman 90, p. 143, and part II, siman 149; and Responsa R.
