He exchanged money. Baer Heitev cites the Sema's question why the Rama records three opinions here but in siman 209 records only the view that when one transfers an existing thing together with something not yet in existence, the existing thing is acquired and the future thing is not. The Sema understands that the final opinion here is the primary halachah: the view that nothing is acquired compares the case to “acquire, you and the donkey,” whereas we rule like Rav Nachman that in that case the competent recipient acquires his share. Rama merely copied all three views from the Mordechai but accepted Rabbeinu Simchah's final view, which explains his ruling in siman 209. Noda BiYehudah, however, in simanim 25 through 28, treats the matter as a doubt in law because some poskim rule like Rav Hamnuna that nothing is acquired, and therefore property cannot be extracted from one in possession. In siman 29 he offers another reconciliation. Based on Chelkat Mechokek and Beit Shmuel, there is a distinction between a transfer containing separate subjects and one expressed as a single indivisible whole. If one says “you and the donkey,” the competent recipient can acquire independently. But if the transfer is one integrated transaction, such as kiddushin of a group that includes two sisters, or where a single purchaser intends to acquire a good and defective element together, failure of one part may invalidate the whole. Thus, where money and movable property are transferred together, all three views are possible, depending on whether the conveyance is one unit and whether the purchaser would want one without the other. By contrast, where present property is transferred together with future property, the future item is not yet before him and is known to be presently incapable of acquisition, so there is less reason to infer that the present transfer depends upon the future one. Noda BiYehudah applies this to a gift document covering “all objects and movable property,” noting that money is certainly included among objects. If the final kinyan was made “on all the foregoing,” everything may be a single integrated acquisition; if each transfer was framed independently, the analysis differs. If the document is phrased as a past-tense admission that the transfer already occurred, however, there is no defect, because an admission is binding and one can presume that the money had been validly acquired agav land. Elsewhere Noda BiYehudah nevertheless treats even transfer of present property together with future property as a legal doubt due to the authorities who follow Rav Hamnuna. He further distinguishes future property that will certainly come into existence from assets that may never be acquired. Where one gives all present assets together with “whatever I may acquire for the rest of my life,” the present assets are certainly acquired even according to Rav Hamnuna, because there is no reason to assume the donor made the present gift contingent upon assets that may never exist. He supports this from the Tosefta cited by Baal HaTerumot. See also Noda BiYehudah, second edition, simanim 45 and 47, Beit Meir, Even HaEzer 40:3, and Responsa Chatam Sofer, Choshen Mishpat, simanim 101, 106, 173, and 186.
