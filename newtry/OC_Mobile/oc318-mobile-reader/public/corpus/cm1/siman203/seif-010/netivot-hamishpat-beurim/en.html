And some say that the entire acquisition remains effective. It appears that the dispute concerns only kinyan sudar. Where land is acquired by chazakah, the acquisition of one item does not depend at all upon the other. Cancellation of one does not cancel the other, and the principle "since he acquired this he acquired that" has no application. With kinyan sudar, by contrast, the transfer of the cloth effects both acquisitions together, and the principle of "since" can operate. This may answer the Mordechai's question from acquisition of movables agav land, because that case can be where the land itself was acquired by chazakah. However, according to the view that the entire acquisition remains effective, there is still a difficulty from Bava Metzia's challenge to the opinion that coin can be acquired by exchange, based on the case of one standing by the threshing floor. Even according to the view that coin is not acquired by exchange, why could he not transfer the money together with another item by kinyan sudar, so that by the principle "since he acquired this he acquired that" he would acquire all of it?
