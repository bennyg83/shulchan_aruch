If one exchanges land for land, movable property for land, or land for movable property, once one party acquires the item transferred to him, the other becomes obligated to deliver the item exchanged for it. Rama: If money is exchanged together with movable property, or money together with land, some say that because chalipin does not acquire money, the entire acquisition is void. Others say the entire acquisition is valid. A third view holds that it is valid regarding the land and movable property but void regarding the money. Mordechai, chapter Mi Shemet. The same question applies when a kinyan is made over something for which acquisition is effective together with something lacking tangible substance, which is merely a kinyan devarim. If two people make a kinyan with each other to exchange all that they own, some say the kinyan is ineffective. Rivash, siman 263. Others say it is effective, but each keeps his own property and the other pays him the excess value, because their primary intent was to profit rather than literally exchange all their property. Terumat HaDeshen, siman 311. See above, siman 209, seif 2.
