He exchanged money and movable property, etc. This means that Reuven gives Shimon his kerchief and says, "Take my kerchief and in exchange give me money and movable property." Rama records three opinions. We must explain why here he cites all three, while in siman 209:4, concerning a transfer that combines something not yet in existence with something already in existence, he states only that the existing item is acquired and the future item is not. The Mordechai in Mi SheMet records the three opinions together. In a case where one person transferred land, movables, and money in one kinyan sudar, some authorities compared it to "acquire, you and the donkey" and held that just as the money is not acquired, neither are the land and movables. Others held that everything is acquired, because once the kinyan takes effect upon the land and movables it also takes effect upon the money. Rabbi Simchah held that although the money is not acquired, the land and movables are. Otherwise, why does the Gemara require the special rule of agav for movables acquired with land? They should simply be acquired because the kinyan takes effect upon the land. One cannot answer that this is like "acquire, you and the donkey," because a donkey is wholly incapable of acquisition, whereas money can be acquired through lifting, meshichah, or courtyard. Since Rabbi Simchah gives a reasoned refutation of the first two positions and is cited last, Rama in siman 209 states only his conclusion. Here, because this is the primary source discussed by the Mordechai, Rama reproduces all three views, while hinting that Rabbi Simchah is primary by placing him last. Another explanation is that the first view, which says nothing is acquired, rests upon the opinion that in "acquire, you and the donkey" nothing is acquired. We rule like Rav Nachman that the human recipient acquires his share even though the donkey cannot acquire, as the Tur and Mechaber state at the end of siman 210. This is why Rama in siman 209 says simply that the item already in existence is acquired, just as in "acquire, you and the donkey." The view here that even the money is acquired through the kinyan applies specifically to money already in existence, which is capable of other methods of acquisition such as lifting and meshichah. It can therefore be argued that the kinyan sudar extends to it once it validly applies to the other property. A future item, by contrast, is not presently capable of any acquisition at all, so no such extension is possible. See Terumat HaDeshen, siman 173, who questions whether in such a mixed transfer we say that because the kinyan fails as to one item it fails as to all, and Darkei Moshe's discussion. I explained elsewhere why the Mordechai and Mahari appear to compare this to the opinion that "acquire, you and the donkey" fails entirely even though the law follows Rav Nachman. Possibly, since there is a Talmudic dispute, we leave the money with its prior owner and say no acquisition occurred. Ritva also asks why the Gemara challenges according to the opinion that nothing is acquired when we rule like Rav Nachman. See also siman 212, seif 3, concerning a reservation for oneself and another, where the rule of extension is not comparable to the present case.
