And they become obligated, etc., and if, etc. The Mishnah concerning inferior coins and superior coins.
Some say that even, etc. There is a textual omission here. Tosafos, the Rosh, and other poskim explain “inferior coins” as coins not completely invalidated, but not freely accepted in commerce. They similarly explain an asimon as not generally circulating. Such coins are considered produce relative to superior coins but currency relative to ordinary movable property. See Tosafos 44a, s.v. “the gold,” and the Rosh. The Mishnah’s statement that currency does not acquire movable property applies to all the currencies enumerated there; even inferior coins are currency relative to an asimon. The Gemara’s anka and anigra refer to coins wholly invalidated, and therefore those can both effect and be acquired by chalipin. By contrast, inferior coins and an asimon neither effect nor are acquired by chalipin because they retain the status of currency. This is where the Tur and other poskim disagree with the Rambam. The Rambam interprets inferior coins as wholly invalidated and therefore as ordinary movable property for all purposes, like anka and anigra. He similarly explains asimon as lacking a coin image and thus as ordinary pieces of metal. According to him, the Gemara’s rule that currency neither acquires movable property nor participates in chalipin applies only to gold, silver, and copper currency. Coins that are merely not freely circulated are not separately discussed by the Gemara and remain within the category of valid currency. According to the other poskim, however, such coins are movable property relative to superior coinage, so they can acquire superior coinage as money, but not through chalipin. This is the correct reading of the Tur and Maggid Mishneh, and explains the Rama in siman 195 seif 2, who specifies a coin “completely invalidated.” He deliberately changed the Rambam and Shulchan Aruch’s broader phrase that it is “like produce for every purpose,” because the Rambam follows Rabbeinu Tam, who permits exacting chalipin even with produce. The Rama, however, follows Tosafos, the Rosh, and Tur, that produce cannot effect chalipin at all. Coins are different because they are utensils, and even after invalidation a fully invalid coin can qualify as such. This is why the Rama in siman 195 seif 2 distinguishes an animal from a completely invalidated coin. All this is unlike Rabbeinu Tam and the Rambam, as explained above.
