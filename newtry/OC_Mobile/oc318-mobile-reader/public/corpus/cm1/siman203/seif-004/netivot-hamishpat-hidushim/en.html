The same principle applies if one sells movables and stipulates that the purchaser will give in exchange an item not yet in existence. Once the purchaser performs meshichah on the movables, he becomes personally obligated to provide the future item when it comes into his possession. Every meshichah of movables obligates the purchaser to provide what he agreed, and a personal monetary obligation can attach even regarding an item not yet in existence. But if an employer promises a worker a particular object as wages, since the worker does not acquire the employer's body through his labor, his labor is treated only like payment of money for the object. Therefore, if the employer says, "This object will be yours as your wages," it is like payment of money and the employer who retracts from giving the object and wishes instead to pay cash is subject to "He Who exacted punishment." If he merely says, "I will give you this object as your wages," no acquisition attaches to the object at all and he may discharge the worker with money:
He did not acquire. Nevertheless, he is subject to "He Who exacted punishment," like one who paid money. Sema:
