(Sema, seif katan 19) Unlike something not yet in existence, regarding which, etc. Note: At first glance there is a proof for this from Gittin 43a, where the Gemara seeks to prove that one who sells his slave's right to a future penalty does not effect a sale because it is something not yet in existence. It seeks proof from the baraita of 'one born in the house,' arguing: if a slave can be sold for his future penalty, is there any slave who cannot be sold for that penalty? See there. This is difficult, for even if the future penalty alone cannot be sold because it is not yet in existence, one could sell it simultaneously with an existing object worth a perutah, so that the acquisition would take effect upon the future penalty through the fact that it takes effect upon something already in existence.
