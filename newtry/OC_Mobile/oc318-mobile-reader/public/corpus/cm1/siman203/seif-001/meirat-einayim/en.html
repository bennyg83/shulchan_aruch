All movable property acquires other movable property through chalipin, etc. We derive this from the verse in Megillat Ruth, "Formerly in Israel, to confirm every matter, a man removed his shoe." From the comparison to a shoe we derive that the object used to effect chalipin must specifically be a utensil, as explained above in siman 195. But as to what can be acquired through chalipin, even produce is included from the phrase "to confirm every matter," as the Tur and Mechaber write immediately below in this seif.
If the transferor is not particular to know the value of the object, etc. Meaning, afterward the other party gives him the full value of the object. Otherwise, if this itself were the complete payment, it would be difficult, because even without the law of chalipin he would acquire, as the Tur and Mechaber write at the beginning of siman 199 concerning one who had money in hand without knowing its number. See the Perishah.
And produce as well, even though one cannot use it to effect, etc. Anything that is neither a utensil nor a living creature is included under the name "produce." See above siman 195. In the Perishah I wrote that the Tur and several Geonim hold that only ordinary kinyan sudar, which is not given as equivalent value, cannot be effected with produce. But barter transactions in which the exchanged produce represents value can be effected with produce. See there. Rama cites this as a dispute in the gloss at the end of this siman.
Except for coin, etc. The reason is that when parties make an exchange using coin, the purchaser and transferor principally rely upon the image stamped on the coin, and that image may be invalidated by the king. It is therefore like letters, which neither effect nor are themselves acquired through ordinary chalipin.
Rather, even if he is particular to know, etc. This refers back to the beginning of the seif, where it says, "It need not be stated that if the transferor is not particular, etc." The Mechaber now concludes that even if he is particular to know the value, etc.
His cow is acquired by the owner of the donkey, etc. Consequently, if the cow dies, or increases or decreases in value after the other party pulls the donkey, the death, appreciation, or depreciation of the cow occurs in the domain of the donkey's owner.
