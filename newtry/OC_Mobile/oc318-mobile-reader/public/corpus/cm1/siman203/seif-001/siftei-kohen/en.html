The benefit of discretion is not money. Hagahot Maimoniyot, Hilchot Mechirah chapter 5, and the Baal HaItur write that the benefit of choosing the recipient of priestly gifts is not monetary property and cannot be transferred by barter or agav. They therefore reject the explanation that Rabban Gamliel transferred the tithe as movable property by agav and follow Rav Pappa. This is difficult, for the anonymous Gemara in Kiddushin 27a expressly adopts the agav explanation regarding land sold and movable property given as a gift. Rav Pappa may also agree that this benefit can be acquired by agav, or he may agree that Rabban Gamliel used agav while merely adding that the case could also be explained by another person's intent to transfer. Furthermore, even if the benefit is not money regarding priestly gifts, it may still be treated as property for barter and agav. Barter is ineffective for priestly gifts because the Torah requires giving and barter resembles sale. This appears clear, unlike the Rama, Hagahot Maimoniyot, and the Baal HaItur in his section on acquisition. Indeed, the Baal HaItur himself writes in his section on leasing that mere rental did not acquire the tithe without agav, proving that he understood Rabban Gamliel's act as agav. His discussion there also requires analysis, for renting the place itself should acquire as a courtyard, as stated above in siman 198, seif 5. The Mishna in Bava Batra 84a likewise states that a prudent purchaser rents the object's place, apparently even without agav. In any event, the Baal HaItur's own words in the laws of leasing prove that the benefit of discretion can be transferred by agav. This is the clear law. See also siman 67, seif 35 in the gloss and Yoreh Deah 160, seif 4 in the gloss.
