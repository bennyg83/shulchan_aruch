And the discretionary benefit. Shach writes that even if discretionary benefit is not considered monetary property in the ordinary sense, it can still be sufficient for barter and incidental acquisition. He argues that the only reason barter does not work for priestly gifts is that the Torah requires them to be given and barter resembles a sale. I find this surprising. Discretionary benefit is not even inheritable, as Maharik, Maharam Padua, Rama, and the Shach himself rule. If one cannot transmit it by inheritance, all the more it should not be transferable by an ordinary acquisition. Inheritance is generally the broader and easier mode of succession. Therefore, if discretionary benefit is not monetary property, it should not become transferable merely through barter or incidental acquisition. See also what I wrote in siman 275.
