One from whom they made a kinyan, etc. Rashba writes this according to the view that “I will give” is not merely a kinyan devarim, because the kinyan creates an immediate monetary obligation. By contrast, a kinyan to execute a release document does not itself create an obligation; it merely concerns removing a lien, and according to all opinions that is a kinyan devarim. But an undertaking to return legal rights is a monetary obligation. All this follows the view that such a commitment is not a kinyan devarim. The Rashba agrees with this there, while in siman 245 the Rama rules like the first opinion that it is a kinyan devarim.

Some say, etc. Rabbeinu Tam holds that an animal is also called “produce,” following the Gemara’s straightforward implication that anything not a utensil is called produce. The baraisa regarding exchanging a cow for a donkey is different because the parties are particular about value. The rule that produce cannot effect chalipin was said only regarding a kinyan analogous to kinyan sudar. See Tosafos 46b, s.v. “and produce,” and 47a, s.v. “redemption,” as well as Bava Kamma 70b. Ri’s answer there is forced, and the simple reading of the Gemara supports Rabbeinu Tam, that the item is acquired through chalipin.
(Collected note) Some say, etc. This is the principal view, for the Tosefta cited by the Rosh at the beginning of Bava Batra states that one may exchange land for land, as stated in seif 10 and siman 157 seif 2. Certainly produce should be no worse. See Responsa Rashba, siman 1226. (End of note.)
