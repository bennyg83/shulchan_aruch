Benefit. So write Hagahot Maimoniyot, chapter 5 of Hilchot Mechirah, and the Baal HaItur, that the right of discretionary benefit, tovat hana'ah, is not considered money and cannot be acquired by exchange or by acquisition together with land. They reject the answer that movables may be acquired together with land and instead follow Rav Pappa. The Shach writes that this is difficult, because in the first chapter of Kiddushin the anonymous Gemara explicitly accepts that answer concerning land by sale and movables by gift. It appears that Rav Pappa too agrees that tovat hana'ah can be acquired together with land. It is also possible that he agrees that the incident of Rabban Gamliel involved movables acquired with land. In any event, there is no difficulty, because a transfer by another person's intent is different. It appears further that even if tovat hana'ah is not money, as stated in siman 350, nevertheless for exchange and acquisition with land it is treated as property. Only gifts to Kohanim are different, because the Torah uses the term "giving," and exchange does not help there since it resembles a sale. This appears clear. See the Shach's lengthy discussion, the Rama in siman 87, seif 35, and Yoreh Deah siman 160, seif 23 in the Rama.
