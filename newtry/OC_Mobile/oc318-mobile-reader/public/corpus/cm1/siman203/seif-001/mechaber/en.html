All movable property may be acquired by barter, but coin is not acquired by barter. It contains ten seifim.
All movable property may acquire one another through barter. This is certainly so when the transferor does not insist on knowing the precise value of the item he receives in exchange, as in an ordinary kinyan sudar, which is a complete acquisition effective for land, movable property, animals, and other property. Rama: Except for documents; see siman 66 above. Produce, although it cannot itself serve as the instrument of a sudar acquisition, may be acquired through barter. Coin is the exception: it is neither acquired by barter nor may it serve to acquire another item by barter. A kinyan also cannot take effect upon something intangible, such as an undertaking to walk with a person to a particular place or merely to divide a jointly owned courtyard. Rama: See siman 157, seif 2. These are merely agreements concerning future conduct, with no object upon which the acquisition can take effect. Rama: A right of discretionary benefit is not regarded as money and cannot be acquired by barter. See siman 245 concerning one who says, 'I will give something to so-and-so,' and whether that is an ineffective kinyan of words. If one made a kinyan obligating himself to write a release, it is a kinyan of words, because a release document is only a relinquishment. If he made a kinyan obligating himself to restore to the other party all rights he holds against him, it is not merely a kinyan of words. Even where the parties carefully appraise the value of each exchanged item, so that the exchange resembles a sale for money, barter still acquires. Thus, if one owns a cow and another a donkey, they appraise each, agree to exchange them, and the owner of the cow performs meshichah on the donkey, the cow immediately belongs to the former owner of the donkey wherever it is, and neither may withdraw. Rama: Some say that when the exchanged items are appraised against one another, even produce may serve in the barter. Others disagree.
