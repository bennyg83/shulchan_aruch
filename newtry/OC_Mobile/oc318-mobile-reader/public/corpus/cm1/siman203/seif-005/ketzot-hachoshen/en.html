Nimukei Yosef writes that if one pulls an object from another and agrees to pay with a kor of new wheat, he must deliver exactly what he stipulated. My distinguished brother asked from Ran, who rules that if one hires a worker and promises a kor of wheat as wages, he need pay only money, because wheat requires meshichah. The distinction is as follows. The Mishnah that gold acquires silver means that one party becomes personally obligated for the silver. Currency itself is not acquired by barter. When the Mishnah says silver does not acquire gold, it means that even if he promises gold in exchange for silver, no obligation to deliver that gold arises merely through payment, because gold is treated as produce relative to silver and still requires meshichah. Thus money does not create an obligation to deliver produce, even where the produce is unspecified. Labor compensation is comparable to money and therefore likewise does not bind the employer to deliver the promised produce. But where one has already pulled produce and, in exchange, obligates himself to deliver different produce as its price, the personal obligation does attach. Although produce cannot itself be used for barter in the formal sense, a person can create a personal obligation to deliver produce because responsibility for that obligation rests upon him. Accordingly, money and labor do not by themselves obligate delivery of produce, whereas receipt of produce can generate a personal obligation to deliver other produce. This reconciles Nimukei Yosef and Ran.
