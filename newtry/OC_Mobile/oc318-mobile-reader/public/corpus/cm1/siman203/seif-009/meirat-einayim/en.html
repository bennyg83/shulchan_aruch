That he acquire it from now. He carefully writes "that he acquire it," because in an ordinary sale there is a way to acquire coin, as explained above: if Reuven pulls Shimon's movable property, Reuven becomes obligated to Shimon for the value of those movables. But to acquire coin as a gift, the available methods are only through agav land, or by renting or buying the place where the money is located, in which case that place becomes his courtyard and his courtyard acquires the money for him. For this reason he also writes "that he acquire it from now," meaning immediately, without having to perform meshichah on some other object in exchange. It is also possible that the phrase "from now" refers back to what was written immediately before: now that it has been explained that coin is not acquired through chalipin, it follows that it can be acquired only through agav, etc. The first explanation appears primary. See the Perishah for further discussion.
Provided that those coins still exist, etc. And the deposit-holder has not denied holding them. This refers to the acquisition agav land discussed immediately before. If he denied possession, or if the money is held by him as a debt, one cannot say that the courtyard or land should acquire it for the recipient, because there is no specific existing object here to acquire. It was also explained above at the beginning of siman 123 that strictly speaking one cannot write a power of attorney for such a claim except by force of an enactment. That is not the subject here, which concerns one who wishes to transfer full ownership to another under ordinary law. This is the meaning of "see above siman 123 in the laws of authorization." Alternatively, the reference may allude to what is written there that according to the Rosh one may write a power of attorney even for an oral loan by the law of the Gemara, as explained there in the Sema and Perishah.
But he can transfer a debt owed by a Jew. Rama deliberately writes "owed by a Jew," because if it is owed by a non-Jew there are different rules, as the Tur and Mechaber write in siman 126, seif 22.
