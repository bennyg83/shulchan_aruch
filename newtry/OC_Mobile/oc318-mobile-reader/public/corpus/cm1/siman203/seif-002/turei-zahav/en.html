(There, in the gloss: One from whom a kinyan was made that he would execute, etc.) The words of the Rama require study. In responsum 1033 of the Rashba it is written that the Geonim dispute the matter. Some say that a kinyan to “give” or to “do” is a mere kinyan concerning words, while others say that it is not a mere kinyan concerning words unless he says, “I will divide with you.” Accordingly, whenever a kinyan was made from him that he would return all rights that he possesses, it is a full kinyan. But where a kinyan was made from him that he would execute a deed of release, it is necessarily a mere kinyan concerning words, because a release is only the removal of a lien. If a kinyan was made from him that he would remove at a later time a lien that rests upon another, this is nothing more than words. End quote. This is the view that a kinyan to give is not a mere kinyan concerning words, but a full kinyan. On that basis, the Rashba writes that a kinyan to return rights is effective. Yet in siman 245, seif 2, the Rama rules in the name of Mahariv that a kinyan to give is a mere kinyan concerning words, whereas here he writes that a kinyan to return rights is effective. Still more difficult are the words of the Sema in siman 12, seif katan 20, who writes that if a kinyan was made from the claimant that he would waive and forgive, it is not a mere kinyan concerning words. This implies that he understood the Rama here to mean that a kinyan to execute a deed of release is a mere kinyan concerning words, but that a kinyan by which the release itself is presently effected is a full kinyan. This certainly is not so, for the responsum of the Rashba cited above makes clear that the same applies where he undertakes to effect a release, and it remains a mere kinyan concerning words. See below, siman 245, seif 3, regarding the practical ruling. It appears to me that the Rama’s ruling here is correct, for the Rashba so concludes. It is possible that the Rama understands the Rashba’s statement, “Accordingly, whenever a kinyan was made that he would return rights, it is a full kinyan,” to apply according to all opinions, even according to the view that a kinyan to give is a mere kinyan concerning words. Thus the Rama may hold that a kinyan to give is ineffective, but a kinyan to return rights is a full kinyan, as stated. This is readily understood. The words of the Sema, however, require study, for one cannot say that he disagrees with the Rama. Had that been his position, he should have brought proof concerning this very case.
