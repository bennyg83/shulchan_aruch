If the stolen property underwent a change, such as one who stole a lamb, carried it out while violating Shabbos, and it later became a ram, I was uncertain whether he must return the animal itself. Ordinarily a thief acquires the body of stolen property through a qualifying change and thereafter owes only its monetary value as of the time of the theft. But for the moment of theft the principle that a more severe punishment precludes monetary liability may apply. In my view, the victim nevertheless takes the stolen object itself even after the change. Acquisition through change is derived from the verse requiring return of "the stolen item that he stole": if it remains like what he stole, he returns it, but if it changed he owes money. Since the Torah imposes a monetary obligation after change, it follows that the Torah places the object in the thief's domain. The acquisition through change is imposed even against the thief's will. Only acquisition through despair requires the thief's willingness, whereas change acquires involuntarily, just as liability for unavoidable loss is imposed involuntarily. Where the more severe punishment rule prevents the thief from becoming obligated in the command of restitution, however, there is no enforceable duty of return. Even when stolen property remains intact, beit din cannot compel the thief to fulfill restitution in that situation; rather, the victim comes and takes his own property, as Ketzos HaChoshen writes in siman 350. Since there is no obligation of restitution and no duty to pay money, the thief correspondingly does not acquire the stolen object through change. A proof is that in the case of one who stole and slaughtered an animal on Shabbos, the Gemara says only that he is exempt from payment. If change itself transferred the animal to the thief despite the exemption, the Gemara could have stated the greater novelty that the body of the animal became his. Rather, when the monetary liability that would work to the thief's disadvantage never arises, the corresponding proprietary acquisition that would work to his advantage does not arise either.
