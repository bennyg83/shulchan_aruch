Rather, he dragged it out, etc. The Tur, following the Gemara, writes that he dragged the purse from the owner's domain into the public domain, placed his hand against the threshold within three handbreadths of the ground, and dropped the purse into his hand, so that the theft liability and Shabbat liability arose simultaneously. If he had not removed it from the owner's domain into the public domain there would be no Shabbat liability. If he had not brought his hand against the threshold, there would be no monetary acquisition through theft, because pulling from the owner's domain into the public domain without lifting does not acquire. Meshichah acquires only in an alleyway, a jointly owned courtyard, or the buyer's own domain, as explained in siman 197. Therefore he must drag it from the owner's domain and let it fall directly into his hand as soon as it leaves the house. His hand is treated as his own domain for acquisition and at the same time is in the public domain for Shabbat. The statement that his hand is within three handbreadths is not exclusive. The same applies if it rests directly upon the public ground or anywhere within three handbreadths. If his hand were higher than three handbreadths, he would have to lift the purse three handbreadths while it was still in the owner's domain in order to place it into his hand, and he would acquire it through lifting before the Shabbat prohibition of carrying out arose. If his hand is a short distance from the threshold but within three handbreadths, there is a dispute whether the liabilities are treated as simultaneous, because within three handbreadths is treated as joined. But when his hand is actually connected to the threshold, everyone agrees they arise together. See the Perishah.
