And they were lost. This is the Gemara's conclusion there, “and the halachah is,” etc., showing that the halachah follows Rava. Rava's return of them was an act beyond the letter of the law, as the Baal HaMaor explains.
There. This is the Rambam's language, indicating that he follows the first view in the Rama. Thus the Rama writes, “However,” etc., unlike the Maggid Mishneh; see Bach.
But, etc. At that moment he stole it, as stated in Bava Metzia 43a concerning one who takes an article, with the reason given there. So too here. The text therefore says “but if he took it,” rather than “but if he did not break it,” since both cases involve taking. The plain sense of the Mishnah concerning one who enters through a tunnel and breaks the article also indicates that it was broken while he remained in the tunnel. The Gemara's challenge is specifically according to Rav, whereas according to Rava it is straightforward. Likewise, when it says “By God,” etc., how did Rava know that Rav held this? Perhaps Rav referred to one who took and then broke it. This is the Ramban's explanation.
Some say, etc. This is Rashi's view there, s.v. “mistabra”; see Tosafos there and the Baal HaMaor. Ramban writes that Tosafos's difficulty does not arise, because Rav speaks even where he took it, whereas Rava rejects Rav's rule in a case of taking. The Shach's words are difficult, because the Maggid Mishneh explicitly records this dispute. As for the objection that the death liability still applies, also raised in the Perishah, the case can be one involving a karmelis or the like, and “afterward” means after his capital liability ended. See Baal HaMaor, who states this explicitly.
(Collected note) But if he stole, etc. The Rambam does not state that he stopped to rest between lifting and carrying it out, as in Kesubos 31a, because he rules like the latter version there, or because there he could have acted otherwise; see Tosafos there. He omits the distinction there concerning small objects because in any event the case necessarily involves large objects, as stated in Bava Batra 86a. In small objects he would not acquire them, as Tosafos explain there. Nevertheless, there are other circumstances in which liability would arise, and the Rambam does not generally enumerate them here.
