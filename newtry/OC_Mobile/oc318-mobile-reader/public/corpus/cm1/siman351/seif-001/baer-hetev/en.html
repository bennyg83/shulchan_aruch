Spitzer. According to S. S. S. K.C., the column is still another matter of deprivation, and it is such that it was a mishabilitated or broken in his hand and slaughtered on the Shabbat for theft and prohibitions. A:
Dragging it. The Tur's wording, based on the Gemara, is as follows: he was dragging it and removing it from the owner's domain into the public domain, and he brought his hand close to the threshold of the house within three handbreadths of the ground and dropped it into his hand, so that the prohibition of theft and the Shabbat prohibition took effect simultaneously. The reason is that had he not removed it from the owner's domain into the public domain, there would be no capital liability for carrying on Shabbat. On the other hand, had he not brought his hand to the threshold, there would be no monetary liability for theft, because one who pulls an object from the owner's domain into the public domain does not acquire it unless he lifts it. Meshichah effects acquisition only in an alleyway, in a jointly owned courtyard, or in the acquirer's own domain, as the Tur and Shulchan Aruch write in siman 197. Sema.
