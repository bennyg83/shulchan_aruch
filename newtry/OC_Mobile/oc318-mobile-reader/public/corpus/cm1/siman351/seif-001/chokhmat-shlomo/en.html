Seif 1, in the gloss. "But if they were subsequently lost, he is liable." Note: See the Shach, who writes that this requires study, for one could say that since the liability to death still rests upon him, he should be exempt from payment. It appears to me that there is a proof for his position from Shevuot 17a. There the Gemara says, "If he remained there, he is already liable to karet," until Abaye says, "What is the difficulty? It can be found in such a case." I find this difficult according to Abaye even without this, because in the third chapter of Ketubot he holds that according to Rabbi Nechunya ben HaKanah, even those liable to death at the hands of Heaven are exempt from monetary payment. Why, then, does the Gemara challenge? It could say that the teaching follows Rabbi Nechunya ben HaKanah, and there would be a practical distinction if he tore another person's silk garments while performing the forbidden service. Because he had already remained there, he had become liable to karet earlier, and he would now be liable to pay. But for the act of service he is performing now, the death liability begins now, and he is exempt from payment. Although Tosafot independently raise another practical distinction, why did they not also raise this one? One cannot answer that exemption from payment applies only where a new death liability can attach, whereas here, since he is already liable to karet, no further death liability can attach and therefore it cannot exempt him from payment. That is not so, for we rule that one who commits a capital offense unwittingly is exempt from payment even though no death penalty is actually imposed upon him. Since the prohibition itself carries a capital liability, he is exempt. Certainly the present intentional act should be no worse merely because he is already liable to karet, just as in an unwitting case. Accordingly he should be exempt from payment, and this would itself create a practical distinction. Why, then, does the Gemara challenge, and why does Abaye not answer in this manner according to his own view? The fact that Tosafot did not raise this point proves, apparently, not like the Shach but like the Rama. Accordingly here as well, since he is still remaining there and has already incurred karet, he is exempt from payment even without a newly arising death liability, which is why the Gemara asks how the case can be found. Examine this carefully. See also my novellae to the chapter Merubah, written in 5613, where I brought a proof from the words of the Rosh in support of the Shach. However, in the sugya of "pluck a fig" I cited Tosafot there, whose words indicate that they agree with the Tur. See there and examine carefully. Also see Shach, seif katan 1, where he challenges the Rama. In my work on Orach Chaim, Hilchot Shabbat, siman 358, I discussed the Shach's words and brought a proof for him from Tosafot in the seventh chapter of Shabbat, page 70. I concluded there that with respect to unwitting capital offenses, even the Shach agrees with the Rama that afterward he is liable. See there and examine carefully.
