(Netivot HaMishpat, seif katan 1) Rabbi Shimon ben Yehudah said that a change does not effect acquisition. Note: It is in the name of Rabbi Shimon. See there. Rava concludes that perhaps Rabbi Shimon ben Yehudah said this only regarding dye, because it can be removed. Accordingly, there is no longer a question against the view that prohibited work on Shabbat is rabbinic from Rabbi Shimon's position, because perhaps he follows Rava and holds that a complete physical change does effect acquisition. Even according to Abaye, the Sages can hold in accordance with Rabbi Shimon that change effects acquisition. To resolve the author's question, one can say that the damage is not recognizable through the beginning of the slaughter. Since the animal has not yet thereby become halachically fit, the prohibition of benefiting from prohibited Shabbat labor does not yet apply, and the completion can be performed by a minor, deaf-mute, or mentally incompetent person, whose act does not carry the status of Shabbat desecration. This is even better than labor performed inadvertently on Shabbat, which is permitted according to Rabbi Yehudah.
