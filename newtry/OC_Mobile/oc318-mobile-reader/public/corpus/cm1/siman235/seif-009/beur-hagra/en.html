When does this apply, etc., but, etc. Bava Batra there and Gittin 65a.
Or from his other inheritors. “His father” is not literal. The reason is that he did not toil to obtain such property and it came to him automatically. The Gemara says that sometimes money jingling before him tempts him, unlike property acquired through his own efforts. The same applies to a gift, because if he had not exerted himself and provided benefit to the donor, the donor would not have given it, as stated in Megillah, Bava Metzia, and elsewhere.
Or it was given to him, etc. As stated there, 149a: a gift of a shechiv mera is like inheritance, etc.
Twenty complete years. In accordance with Rava in the name of Rav Nachman there, that time within the twentieth year has the same status as before that time. Although the Gemara discusses Rava’s statement further, it concludes there and in Niddah that within the time is like before the time.
And he produces two hairs. There, from Rabbi Zeira’s challenge based on the incident in Bnei Brak, etc.
Even though he produced, etc. As above, age twenty is specifically required. The Gemara says that he is examined for kiddushin, etc., and for selling inherited property, etc., meaning that the signs do not permit such a sale before age twenty. Rashbam explains likewise there.
And he understands, etc. As above, the case of Gidul bar Menashya concerned his own property. The Gemara likewise infers from the incident of the person under twenty who sold his property that the case specifically concerns property belonging to him himself.
Perhaps, etc., because, etc. This is the Gemara’s explanation there.
Whether movable property. This wording is superfluous, as explained above in seif 8.
Even if he is not, etc. This follows the Gemara’s discussion of Rava’s ruling concerning one who was within the relevant age. The exception there was specifically because the individual was mentally incompetent. On this reading there is no need for the strained explanation offered by Tosafos there.
