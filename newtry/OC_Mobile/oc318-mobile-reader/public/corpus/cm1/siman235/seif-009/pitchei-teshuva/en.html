Rabbi Akiva Eiger, responsum 147, initially writes that according to the Sema, who holds that even a minor who has not reached business maturity is liable for money borrowed for his maintenance, there appears to be a clear proof from Beit Yosef, siman 128. One who supports an actual minor orphan, where the minor could not have been expected to stipulate terms, may recover from him once he grows up if the support was furnished as a loan. Rabbi Akiva Eiger then rejects the proof. There is a distinction between lending money to a minor for maintenance and actually feeding him on credit. When money is lent, the minor's need has not yet been satisfied at the moment of the loan and his personal obligation may not attach. But when the provider directly spends his money on the orphan's necessities on the basis of a loan, the orphan may later be obligated to reimburse the expenditure. See further below, siman 246 seif 17.
