His sale is not a sale. Rishmei Sheelah, siman 54, discusses a fifteen-year-old orphan who had produced two hairs and understood business dealings. He rented for six consecutive years a house inherited from his father. A guardian appointed by beit din objected that he had not known of the lease. At first glance the lease might appear invalid because Shulchan Aruch here states that inherited land cannot be sold before age twenty, and elsewhere the Sema notes that a lease for a term is a form of temporary sale. Rishmei Sheelah distinguishes the cases. There the very body of the premises is transferred to the lessee for the term; here, the arrangement is an ordinary lease for income while the underlying land remains the owner's. Shach, siman 313, makes a similar distinction. Moreover, the restriction on a person over bar mitzvah who understands commerce but is under twenty is itself a rabbinic safeguard. There is no clear basis to extend it to ordinary leasing, especially according to Bach's view that the maxim “a lease for its day is a sale” is itself rabbinic. He therefore concludes that the orphan's lease is fully valid.
