And he paid money and took possession of the land. In the Perishah I wrote that it appears mere chazakah of the land may be enough to leave the field in the minor's possession, like chazakah by an adult, based on the rationale that one may acquire a benefit for a person outside his presence. Still, perhaps the rule was stated only once he had already paid, because then the minor has separated from the money. So long as he has not paid, minors commonly retract because they are attracted to cash. I already wrote that even after he paid and took possession, although the seller cannot retract, the minor himself can retract because the transaction may be a liability rather than a pure benefit. Do not object that if so, a minor should also be able to acquire movable property by renting the place in which it lies, because one may acquire a benefit for him outside his presence. Rambam, Tur, and Mechaber write below in seif 6 that a minor cannot acquire in that manner. The reason is stated there: renting a place is no stronger than acquisition through his courtyard, and a minor's courtyard itself does not acquire for him.
