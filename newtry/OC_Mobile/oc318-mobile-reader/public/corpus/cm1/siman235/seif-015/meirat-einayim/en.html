A minor who borrowed from others, etc. Since the Tur and Mechaber write simply "a minor" and do not specify one who has reached the age of mature childhood, unlike siman 96, and since they also cite dissenting opinions here that are not cited there, it appears that there is a distinction. If a minor expressly borrows for commercial purposes, everyone agrees that he must have reached the age at which his commercial transactions are valid. Below that age, even transactions with his own money are ineffective. Once he reaches that age, all agree that a loan made for his business must be repaid, since with movables his commercial law is like that of an adult. Here the case is a younger child who claims that he borrowed for food. According to the first opinion, if his statement is true and the money was for subsistence, he must repay even before the age of commercial capacity. According to the second opinion he is exempt because below that age he is legally like a complete incompetent, even if the borrowing was truly for his food. The third opinion distinguishes further and requires independent proof that he borrowed for subsistence. We do not rely solely upon the child's assertion. In the Perishah I offered another way to reconcile the Tur, namely that this siman is the primary source and therefore records all opinions, while siman 96 cites only the Rosh's final view. That explanation does not resolve the Mechaber, who rules unqualifiedly in siman 96 like the final opinion but here cites the dispute without decision. Therefore the first explanation is preferable.
