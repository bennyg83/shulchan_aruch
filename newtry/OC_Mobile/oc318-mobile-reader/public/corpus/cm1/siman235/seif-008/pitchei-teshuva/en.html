Mabit, I, siman 122, infers from Rambam's wording, followed by Shulchan Aruch, that since they do not say simply that the transaction is void and both parties may retract, the enactment was made for the young seller's protection. Until age twenty the seller may withdraw, but so long as he does not withdraw the sale remains operative and the buyer cannot independently rescind. This accords with Maggid Mishneh and with the language of the Gemara. Rambam's statement that inherited land “is not sold validly until twenty” therefore means that it is not irrevocably binding against the young seller, not that it is automatically void from inception. Maggid Mishneh similarly writes regarding land purchased by a minor that the minor can withdraw whereas the seller cannot, and comparable one-sided protections appear elsewhere. Rashba and Ritva appear to disagree, but Mabit considers Rambam controlling in jurisdictions that follow him. Mabit adds that in his case the orphans had sworn to uphold the sale. Since they were already adults for the laws of vows and oaths, they could not withdraw without violating their oaths. Even according to Rashba, who otherwise allows the purchaser to withdraw because the youthful seller can withdraw, once the seller is barred by his own oath, the purchaser likewise loses that derivative right. The conclusion is even stronger where the purchaser also swore to pay. Mabit further argues that even according to Rashba and his students, if the deceased's widow, acting as guardian with her sons' reliance, participated in the sale, the sale may be valid for that additional reason.
