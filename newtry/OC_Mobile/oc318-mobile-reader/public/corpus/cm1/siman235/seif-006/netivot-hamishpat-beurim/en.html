The kinyan shall not be... Rambam writes: "A proof is that courtyard and four cubits do not..." His intent appears to be as follows. One might otherwise say that the Sages, when they instituted transactions for young minors, gave them the entire commercial legal capacity of adults. Rambam therefore points out that a minor's courtyard and four cubits still do not acquire for him because a minor lacks agency. If the only problem were that a minor's acts ordinarily have no effect, that would not prove anything. Chazal already intervened and gave efficacy to the acts of young minors. If they intended to make them fully like adults in commerce, then courtyard and four cubits should also work rabbinically, and written instruments should likewise be available. Since Chazal did not equalize all acquisition modes, and Gittin speaks specifically of drawing produce from orphans, it is possible the enactment recognized meshichah but not every other kinyan mode whose scriptural formulation includes "man," such as kinyan sudar. See siman 243, seif 19. A female minor is somewhat different. We see that Chazal derive monetary acquisition of a found object from gittin by analogy, as Tosafot in Bava Metzia 10 explain. Because her courtyard can function in gittin, there is a stronger basis to treat her like an adult in related monetary contexts. This is also Rambam's purpose in saying that we do not make a kinyan from a minor because there is no deed for a minor. Meaning: just as Chazal did not create adult documentary capacity for him, they likewise did not institute adult kinyan-sudar capacity. Had they fully instituted kinyan sudar, it would ordinarily carry the consequences of a deed as well. The absence of the latter shows they did not institute the former.
