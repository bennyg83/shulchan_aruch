To pay their father's debts. Netivot HaMishpat writes that the same authority exists to pay the heirs' own debts, citing Rashba. He nevertheless suggests that beit din may sell inherited property to satisfy debts, but if a young heir himself merely needs to sell for some other purpose, beit din should not appoint a guardian to sell for him, because a guardian is not ordinarily appointed for adults. On that approach, a person under twenty who wishes to sell inherited land has no direct solution except first to borrow money and encumber the property, as permitted in the Rama later in this siman, after which beit din may collect the land for the creditor through judicial appraisal. Radbaz, I, siman 173, indicates otherwise. He discusses the principle that beit din does not appoint a guardian for adults and records a dispute among the Rishonim whether, if beit din nevertheless did appoint one and the adult accepted the arrangement, the guardian's acts are valid. Rosh holds that the appointment is ineffective, Rashba holds that although beit din is not obligated to make such an appointment, if it does and the adult agrees, what the guardian does is valid, and Ran records both views without deciding. Radbaz infers from Rashi and Rambam's language that they follow Rashba, especially where there are both minor and adult heirs and the guardian necessarily administers the minors' share as well. In the present context even Rosh may agree, because Rosh's concern is ordinarily an adult heir who objects that a guardian may cause him loss, whereas here the adult heir himself desires the appointment so that a sale can be made for his benefit. Mabit, I, siman 122, likewise writes that even where the heirs were not yet twenty, a sale made together with the widow who was their guardian may stand. If a guardian may sell the property of actual minors with beit din approval for recognized needs, then certainly mature youths over thirteen, who possess greater legal capacity, may sell inherited property with a guardian and especially with subsequent approval of beit din. The concern that a young person is overly attracted to cash is reduced when a guardian participates. Mabit further writes that the usual requirement of public announcement for sales of orphan property applies to actual minor orphans; it need not invalidate such a sale by older heirs. The matter nevertheless requires study.
