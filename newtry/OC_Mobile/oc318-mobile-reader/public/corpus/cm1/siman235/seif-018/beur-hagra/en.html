A mute person, etc., or, etc. As stated in Gittin 71a: are you speaking of a mute person, etc. Although we do not rule like Rav Kahana in every respect, a mute person is examined. The Gemara also challenges from the baraita that concerning a deaf person they followed gestures only for movable property. By contrast, where a mute person's understanding is verified even for a bill of divorce and by his handwriting, the same applies to land, which is no more stringent than a bill of divorce. This explains the phrase: in the same manner, etc.
Or he may write, etc. On this point all agree with Rav Kahana, as stated: are you speaking of a mute person, etc.
