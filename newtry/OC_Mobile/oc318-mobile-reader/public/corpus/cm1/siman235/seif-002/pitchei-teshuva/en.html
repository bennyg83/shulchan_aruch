This ruling does not follow Rabbeinu Chananel, cited by Tur in seif 4, who holds that a minor's gift is valid even where he has a guardian, because in the law of gifts the guardian's presence makes no difference. Maharam Alshich, siman 101, rules in a case before him that a gift made by a nine-year-old orphan to his grandfather from property held by a guardian was valid. First, he regards the primary view as that of Rabbeinu Chananel in the name of Rav Hai Gaon, which also appears to be the view of Rif, Tosafot, Semag, the later Geonim cited in Hagahot Maimoniyot, Rosh, and Tur, all of whom hold that a gift is valid even when the minor has a guardian, unlike Rambam. Second, he argues that even Rambam required the guardian's consent only because the minor may mistakenly believe that the recipient has done him a significant favor, or may be influenced by flattering speech. In the case before him, everyone knew that the grandfather had benefited the orphan and raised him like a father, and the orphan himself stated that this was why he made the gift. Rambam would therefore agree that the guardian's approval was unnecessary. Maharam Alshich further suggests that even according to Rambam the restriction may apply only to the gift of a healthy person. A deathbed gift takes effect only after death, and at the moment it takes effect the giver no longer has a guardian, so it is as though no guardian existed from the outset. Moreover, an ordinary gift depends on the giver's act of acquisition, whereas a deathbed gift does not operate through an ordinary kinyan. A further reason is that a guardian's authority exists for the orphan's own welfare. During the orphan's lifetime the guardian may prevent a gift to preserve the orphan's assets, but once the gift is a deathbed gift the objection would benefit only the heirs, and the guardian was not appointed for their benefit. The matter nevertheless requires study in practical halachah.
