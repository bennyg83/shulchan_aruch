A minor, deaf-mute, mentally incompetent person, and intoxicated person: when they may sell movable property, and the law of one who purchases on Shabbat or Yom Tov. It contains twenty-eight seifim:
A minor younger than six cannot effect a transfer to others at all. From age six until majority, if he understands ordinary commerce meaning that he was examined and found competent, and after age ten anyone who is not mentally incompetent is presumed competent. Tur. his purchases and sales are valid and his gifts are effective, whether large or small, whether a gift of a healthy person or a gift made on one's deathbed. This is by rabbinic enactment, and it applies to movable property. Some say that since the rule is only rabbinic, if the minor's act was improper, such as selling a small estate in which the daughters had acquired support rights, the sale is ineffective. Responsa Ran, siman 49. With land, however, he may neither sell nor give until he reaches majority meaning a boy of thirteen or a girl of twelve who has developed the signs of maturity. Rambam. This is true even of land he received as a gift or land his guardian purchased for him. If he gives such land away, even by a deathbed gift, the gift is ineffective even though the land itself had been given to him and even though he understands commerce. Nevertheless, as long as the minor wishes to uphold what he did and neither his relatives nor beit din annul the transaction, if the purchaser consumed produce from the land he bought, he need not repay what he took. Responsa Ramban, siman 2. If the minor inherited a debt document from his father, it is treated like movable property and he may sell or give it to another.
