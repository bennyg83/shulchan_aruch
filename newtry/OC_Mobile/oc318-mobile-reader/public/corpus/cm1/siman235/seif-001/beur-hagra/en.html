Meaning that they examined him, etc. From the statement “according to his sharpness.”
And after ten. The Gemara there counts the stages from six through ten, each according to his ability, implying that after ten no further maturity test is required. This is the Tur’s view in the name of the Rosh. The Rambam, however, holds that so long as the child remains a minor he must possess sufficient understanding, because the term pe'utos applies throughout minority. The Gemara mentioned only the beginning age, according to ordinary development. This is why the Rambam and Shulchan Aruch say here “from six until he reaches majority,” and below in seif 8, “a minor who reached majority, etc.” See Beer HaGolah.
Provided he is not mentally incompetent. As stated in Bava Batra 155b: “No, there it was foolishness, etc.”
Whether, etc. The Gemara in Gittin there appears to apply this also to buying and selling, unlike Rav Huna Gaon, who limits it to the amount necessary for the minor’s livelihood. All the poskim disagree with him, for the Gemara says that his gift is effective in either case. The end of Kesubos chapter 6 also indicates this.
Some say, etc. As in the statement concerning “who is a cunning wicked person, etc.” The Sages did not enact their rule in such a case.
But with land, etc. Meaning even land that belongs to the minor himself, as explained below in seif 9. Bava Batra relates the case sent by Gidul bar Menashya. The Rif explains that it concerns the minor’s own land, because land inherited from his father may be sold only at age twenty. The Gemara’s case of one under twenty who went and sold his property likewise shows that it concerns his own property. The minor must also have produced two pubic hairs. Otherwise he retains the status of a minor until the later age discussed in Niddah and Yevamos.
Even if it is land that was given, etc. As above, the rule concerns property that is his own. Property inherited from his father remains restricted until age twenty, as explained below in seif 9.
And if they gave, etc. The rule that a minor’s gift is effective was stated only concerning movable property, as the Mishnah concerning pe'utos expressly discusses movables. The Gemara’s question whether their gift is effective asks whether a gift follows the same rule as buying and selling. Rashi explains it concerning movable property. The statement in Bava Batra that “his gift is a gift” concerns one who is already thirteen, as Rashbam explains there.
Nevertheless, all this, etc. His express waiver is effective, as stated in seif 3. The statement that orphans are not capable of waiver refers to an implied waiver that would ordinarily be presumed from an adult. If they waive explicitly, it is equivalent to a gift. Even a mistaken waiver can be effective in such a setting, as stated in Bava Metzia 66. Ramban writes that all the more so, if the minor expressly permits another to consume the produce of land, this is like giving him the produce as a gift.
And if he inherited, etc. With movable property there is no distinction between property that is his own and property inherited from his father. The Mishnah states the rule of pe'utos without qualification, unlike the law of land in Bava Batra. A proof also appears at the end of Kesubos chapter 6 concerning property left to her by her father, whether by inheritance or by a gift of a shechiv mera, which has the status of inheritance as explained below in seif 9.
Its law is like movable property. As indicated by the teaching concerning the three kinds of documents, etc., and by the Mishnah concerning property, etc.
