A debt instrument has the law of movables. In siman 66 I asked that a debt instrument is acquired only through writing and delivery. How then can witnesses write a transfer document for a minor, when witnesses ordinarily do not sign a document unless the transaction was made by an adult? Rosh answers in a responsum that the rule, "Witnesses do not sign unless the transaction was made by an adult," applies specifically to a sale of land, because a minor cannot sell inherited land and the Sages did not validate such a sale lest he destroy his inheritance. A debt instrument, however, is treated like movable property. Since the Sages validated a minor's transactions in movables under the rules for minors of sufficient understanding, the witnesses may properly sign a transfer of a debt instrument even though the transferor is a minor. Rambam appears to disagree. He writes that one does not perform a sudar kinyan with a minor because sudar kinyan is like a document, and witnesses sign only the document of an adult. The Shulchan Aruch likewise says that witnesses who see a minor's kinyan should not write a document. This suggests that even where the underlying transaction has some legal effect, the formal evidentiary document itself is not written for a minor. Rambam says similarly in the laws of divorce that a female minor cannot appoint an agent to receive her get even though her courtyard can acquire it, because appointment of a receiving agent requires witnesses and testimony is not taken concerning a minor. His language indicates that even oral testimony can be limited where the legal act itself requires formal witnessing. There is therefore difficulty with Rosh's broad statement that a debt instrument can be transferred by a minor simply because it is movable property. A further issue arises according to Rabbeinu Tam, who holds that delivery of a document must occur in the seller's presence. A minor is legally like one who is absent for many acquisition purposes. How then can delivery of a debt instrument work for him when acquisition requires both writing and delivery? Knesset HaGedolah raises a comparable concern regarding a three party arrangement with a minor recipient, because a minor is like one absent. Rosh, however, appears to hold that delivery can work even outside the seller's presence. Darkei Moshe cites Ritva that writing and delivery of debt instruments may be ineffective for a minor because these are formal verbal mechanisms instituted only for adults. The practical result is therefore doubtful. If the minor already collected the debt from the borrower, we should not remove the money from him for the purchaser, because the validity of the minor's transfer remains subject to substantial doubt, both regarding the writing under Rambam and regarding delivery under Rabbeinu Tam and Ritva. Rashba likewise expresses uncertainty whether debt instruments may have a fixed special legal status. Thus possession should remain with the minor who collected.
