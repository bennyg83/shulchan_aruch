g) Like Shmuel, who says, "My father admits to me," etc., there 52a. Although Rav Pappa challenged him and the discussion concludes with a difficulty, Rabbeinu Chananel writes in the name of the Geonim that a statement is rejected only by a decisive refutation, not merely by a difficulty. A difficulty means that we do not know how to answer the statement, but the statement may still be correct and have an answer. The Rivash writes in siman 169 that the Rashba likewise rules so. The reason is that although generally we do not advance for heirs a claim their father himself could not have made, here it is possible that had their father been alive he would have brought proof, etc. Moreover, the document is issued in his name, placing the burden upon the brothers to bring proof.

y) From the Yerushalmi, from the incident of Bar Ziza, cited by the Rif in the chapter HaKones. The Sema writes that the same applies if a household member possesses a signed promissory note against the homeowner for a certain amount. The homeowner cannot say, "I sent you to redeem it and you retained the document"; rather, the household member may collect the amount from him or his heirs. The Bach writes that this is clearly only where it is known that the household member has property of his own, like the sharecropper in the case of Bar Ziza, who ate at the table with the members of the household. But young servants or dependents known to own nothing of their own, who also eat at the homeowner's table, are not believed without proof to say the property was theirs.
