A Woman Who Conducts Business Within the Household. One seif. If a woman conducts the household's business and documents for purchasing land or servants, or debt documents, are written in her name, and she says they belong to her, she must prove through witnesses that she had separate property, even if she holds the documents. The witnesses need not testify specifically that these documents are hers. Likewise, if movable property is found in her possession, she bears the burden of proof. {Rama: This applies even to a widow and even when she holds the documents. If she did not conduct the household's business, documents in her name belong to her; documents in both spouses' names belong to both. An ordinary wife is presumed to conduct household business. If a woman wrote that property in her possession belongs to others, and it is known that she had property independent of her husband, she is believed and he cannot claim it. Some say that although a woman who conducts household business is not herself believed when documents are in her name, if she dies we advance for her heirs the claim that they were hers, as explained shortly regarding brothers (Responsa of the Rashba, siman 879, and Rivash, siman 169). See Even HaEzer, siman 86.} The same law applies to one brother who conducts business for the household and claims that documents in his name are his because property fell to him from his maternal family, he found it, or received a gift: he must bring witnesses. If he dies leaving orphans, the other brothers must prove their claim. {Rama: This applies to property not known to have belonged to the brothers. For property known to have been theirs, the orphans bear the burden of proof.} If the brothers maintained separate food or any separate affairs, the other brothers bear the burden even during his lifetime, because he may have saved from his own allocation. He is believed through an alternative defense even if he says it came from his maternal grandfather's estate. {Rama: This applies when the documents are in his name. If they are in the brothers' or father's name, he cannot claim them; even after his death, the orphans bear the burden. These rules concern a wife or brothers. A household manager who conducts the owner's business is presumed to own property deposited by him or found in his possession, unless it is known to belong to the owner, even if the manager is not wealthy and had no known property apart from the owner's. If he dies, it is given to his heirs.}
