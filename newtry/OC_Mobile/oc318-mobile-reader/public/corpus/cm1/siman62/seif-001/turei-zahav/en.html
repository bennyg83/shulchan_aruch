Seif 1, in the Shulchan Aruch. "If it is known that she had money." The Sema asks from siman 56, which implies that this applies even when it is not known, and therefore interprets our case as one where the woman died. This is extremely difficult, because death is not mentioned here at all. Moreover, what difference would death make? At the time the document was written she was believed through a migo. The original difficulty does not exist. We treat it as established that whatever property is in a married woman's possession belongs to her husband, as Rashbam explains in Perek Chezkas Habatim, page 52, that property is not removed from the father's possession, and the same applies to a woman's property in relation to her husband. Therefore, the migo here cannot operate against this established presumption. The earlier case, however, concerns a woman who says, "This money came into my possession from my husband, but I know that my husband possessed money belonging to others, and I wish to give it to the person to whom it belongs." In that case her migo is effective. Although Even HaEzer, siman 86, states that she is not believed to say that an item belongs to a particular person, that refers to her claim that the particular person gave her that very object, in which case she is certainly lying against the presumption. Here, however, she admits that her husband placed it in her hand, but says only that it belongs to another person. In such a case her migo is effective. This appears to me clear.
