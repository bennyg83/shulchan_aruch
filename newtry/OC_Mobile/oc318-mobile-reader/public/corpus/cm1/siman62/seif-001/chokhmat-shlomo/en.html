Shach, seif katan 1. The Shach concludes that where a wife conducts the household business, even if she has a migo she is not believed to claim that particular property is hers. His words initially seem contradicted by Tosafot, who write that where she claims she saved the money from her household allowance, she can even say that the property fell to her from her maternal grandfather and is believed through a migo. This shows that a woman conducting the household business can sometimes be believed through migo. The difficulty is only apparent. The Shach’s reason is that the migo stands against an established presumption that everything in her possession belongs to her husband. That applies where there was no prior uncertainty. Once everything she possesses was presumptively her husband’s, a later claim that it is hers by inheritance is a migo against a chazakah and is ineffective. But where the parties are already disputing whether she may have saved from the household provisions, there never was a firm prior presumption that everything in her hands belonged to the husband. From the outset it was known that she might have accumulated savings from the household allowance. There is therefore no migo against an established chazakah, and Tosafot can properly hold that she is believed through a migo. Examine this carefully.
