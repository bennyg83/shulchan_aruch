A found article. The Shach writes that it follows from here that if one of several brothers or partners finds a lost article, it belongs to him alone. This is the primary ruling, contrary to one who ruled in practice that they divide it. According to this, the Rama's ruling in siman 176, seif 12, that a partner who steals or robs must share with his partner is difficult, for the Yerushalmi compares the two cases. Thus the Shach.
Proof. The reason is that since the document was written in his name, logic indicates that the money was his. During his lifetime, however, we say that if it truly belonged to him, it is impossible that he would be unable to bring proof that he possessed separate property. After his death, by contrast, a son does not know his father's affairs so thoroughly. For this reason, the Darkei Moshe writes in the name of the Rashba that if she died leaving movable property, we do not say that the brothers must bring proof, since there is no factor assisting the heir in maintaining possession of it. Thus the Sema. The Taz challenges this from Responsa Maharam concerning a widow who remarried and brought books into the marriage; see there, where he concludes that money may not be extracted from the possessor, who can say, 'I follow the authorities who rule in my favor.' The Shach writes that a definite claim by the brothers does not help, even if the documents are in their possession; see there.

His. The Sema writes that not only do we not remove a house or deposited property from the son's possession, but the son may even recover it from the homeowner or his heirs by virtue of this presumption that whatever was found belongs to him. The Rama's words, 'but a member of the household,' do not refer only to documents written in his name, but to everything mentioned above. The reason appears to be that specifically with a wife or brothers, who have equal control over the property, we fear that one might have emboldened himself to claim improperly that all of it is his. A household member, however, has no connection to the property except because the homeowner trusted him, and once he trusted him, he trusted him fully. Thus the Sema. The Shach gives here, briefly, all the laws that emerge from this siman; one who wishes to master them should see there.
