For example, grapes standing ready to be harvested, etc. The dissenting opinion is that of the Tur and those who follow him, who rule like the Sages that whatever remains attached to the ground retains the legal status of land. Rama cites their view here against the Mechaber, although in siman 95:2 he did not comment on the Mechaber, who there also rules according to the Rambam. The Tur there writes that the Rosh and Rabbeinu Chananel disagree. Further, in siman 301:5 the Mechaber rules without qualification that one who entrusts to another something attached to the ground is governed by the law of land, even grapes ready to be harvested, and there he does not distinguish whether they still need the ground, unlike siman 95. Yet here he writes without qualification that grapes ready for harvest have the law of movables. We must explain that the Rambam and Mechaber's ordinary case of grapes ready for harvest concerns grapes that still need the ground somewhat. Therefore the Mechaber rules in siman 301 that they are land specifically for the obligations and oaths of custodians. For acquisition by money, deed, or possession, and for overreaching, however, the Rambam regards them as movables even though they still need the ground somewhat, because they are destined to be harvested today or tomorrow. This is why the language here precisely says, "They are like movables for acquisition and are subject to overreaching," implying not for every law. Siman 95 too deals with custodians, and therefore produce that still needs the ground somewhat is treated as land. Since the Rambam there began with the Mishnah's language, "He claimed grapes ready to be harvested," he had to explain that in the Gemara such wording means produce that no longer needs the ground at all. If it still needs the ground somewhat, no oath is imposed. His statement there that such produce is "like land for every matter" is not literal, since here he treats it as movable for acquisition and overreaching. It means for all custodial matters, such as custodians' oaths, partial admission, and the like. Rama here cites the dissenters, who hold that even produce needing the ground no longer at all is still legally land so long as it remains attached, following the Sages and Rabbi Eliezer. From Rama's silence in siman 95 it appears that where the produce needs the ground not at all he accepts the Rambam that it is movable, while if it still needs the ground somewhat he follows the Rosh and treats it as land even for acquisition and overreaching. I saw that the Ir Shushan explains the dissenters here by saying that even if the produce no longer needs the ground it is acquired through agav because anything standing to be detached is treated as detached. Perhaps he wrote this to explain why Rama cites the dissenters here but not in siman 95, but this is entirely inaccurate. The dissenters disagree also regarding overreaching and say that the produce has the law of land and is not subject to overreaching, where no rationale of agav applies. Moreover, the present case can involve one who wishes to acquire only the produce and not the land. The Rambam himself writes at the end of chapter 3 of Hilchot Mekhirah that dry flax standing in the ground and no longer needing it is acquired together with land by agav like other movable property. Thus Rama could not have meant that the dissenters explain this case through agav. In addition, where one transfers land merely in order to transfer movables with it, everyone agrees that he must say explicitly, "Acquire the land and acquire the movables with it," as appears below in siman 202. Rama therefore should not have written this without explanation had that been his intent. See my further explanation here and in siman 95. I also cited the Maggid Mishneh, who writes that according to the Rambam the distinction between grapes that still need the ground somewhat and those that do not is specific to custodians, not to other laws. The Rambam and Mechaber's language in siman 95 does not prove otherwise, for it may mean that when the Gemara says grapes ready to be harvested are like movables, that is because such grapes are assumed not to need the ground, whereas other attached produce that still needs the ground is land. Examine this carefully.
