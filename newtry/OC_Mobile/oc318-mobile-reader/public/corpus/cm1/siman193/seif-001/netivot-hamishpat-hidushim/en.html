They are treated as movables for purposes of acquisition. The Sema asks that in siman 95, regarding oaths, the Mechaber distinguishes between produce that still needs the ground, which is treated as land, and produce that no longer needs the ground at all, which is treated as movables. In siman 301 he states generally that it is treated as land, while here he states generally that it is treated as movables. He explains that the Rambam and Mechaber's ordinary phrase, "grapes standing to be harvested," refers to produce that still needs the ground somewhat. Regarding custodians it is treated as land, while for acquisition and overreaching it is treated as movables. Therefore simanim 301 and 95, which concern custodians, treat it as land, while here, concerning acquisition and overreaching, it is movables. Produce that no longer needs the ground at all is movable property for all purposes. Rama's gloss here that it is treated as land refers even to produce that no longer needs the ground at all, following the Tur. Since Rama did not gloss similarly in simanim 95 and 301, the Sema infers that Rama generally follows the Mechaber. Practically, according to the Sema: produce that no longer needs the ground is movable property for all purposes; produce that still needs the ground somewhat is land for custodians but movables for acquisition and overreaching. The Shach disagrees and concludes: regarding custodians, since it was entrusted for guarding and not for detachment, even produce that no longer needs the ground is treated as land. Regarding oaths, acquisition, and overreaching, produce no longer needing the ground is movables. Produce still needing the ground somewhat is land for all purposes. Regarding creditors it is treated as movables even when it still needs the ground somewhat, because creditors do not rely upon it since it can be detached, as explained in siman 116. The Taz agrees with the Shach's practical approach.
