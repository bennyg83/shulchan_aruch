Grapes. The Sema discusses this at length and asks from siman 301, seif 5, where the Mechaber writes without qualification that one who delivers produce to another for safekeeping, even produce standing ready to be harvested, is governed by the laws of guardians, without distinguishing whether it still needs the ground, as he distinguishes here and in siman 95. The Sema answers that there it still needs the ground somewhat and is therefore treated as land regarding guardians. Siman 95 likewise concerns guardians. When that siman concludes that such produce is like land "for every matter," this is not literal, since here regarding acquisition and ona'ah it is treated as movable property. Rather, it means for every matter of guardianship, such as the guardian's oath and an admission in part. These words are not correct. The matter is simple: there is no contradiction between Rambam and the Mechaber, for they follow the approach of Rabbi Yosef Halevi, as explained above in siman 95. Therefore, in siman 301, where the produce was entrusted for safekeeping, even if it no longer needs the ground it is treated as land. Here and in siman 95, where it was not given for safekeeping, it is treated as movable property when it no longer needs the ground. There is more to answer regarding the Sema's other comments, but there is no need to elaborate, since I already concluded in siman 95, seif 2, that the principal view is that of the Tur, which is also the view brought by the Rama as "some say." The Rama's failure to gloss there can be explained because in any event a rabbinic oath remains, and he did not enter into that distinction. The Taz writes similarly. See there. The Bach also asks at the end of this siman that the Tur here appears not to distinguish at all, treating attached produce as land everywhere, whereas in simanim 115, 257, and 394 he rules that fully ripened produce no longer needing the ground is not treated as land, while in siman 301 regarding a guardian's oath he rules the opposite. This too is no difficulty. The Tur only says here that it appears attached regarding acquisition and ona'ah because regarding an oath the Sages treated it as attached. In siman 115, regarding a creditor, the rule differs because the creditor does not rely upon the produce since the purchaser can harvest it. In siman 394, where an animal consumed fully ripened produce, the case differs either because it is now detached or because valuation together with the land is different. In siman 257 the donor did not intend to include it. But siman 301 concerns an oath, so acquisition and ona'ah are comparable to an oath. This is the correct ruling. See Responsa Maharashdam, siman 76. Shach.
