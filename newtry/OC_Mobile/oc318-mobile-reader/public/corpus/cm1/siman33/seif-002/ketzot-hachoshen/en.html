His grandson is disqualified. Bava Batra 128 says that Mar son of Rav Ashi permitted testimony concerning one's paternal grandfather, but the halachah does not follow him. Kiddushin and Yevamot discuss Rabbi Yehudah's special rule that a father is believed to identify his son as disqualified. The baraita limits this to a son who has no children, because the Torah's special credibility of "he shall recognize" applies to his son but not to his son's children. This creates a difficulty for Mar son of Rav Ashi, who holds that a grandfather is not a relative for testimonial purposes. Why would the father lack ordinary credibility concerning the grandson? Rashi says the Sages ordinarily do not believe a father to disqualify his son because he is a relative. Rabbi Yehudah overcomes that through the special verse of recognition. If a grandson is not considered a relative, ordinary testimony should suffice there. The difficulty is mitigated because Rashi's language itself was questioned by the early authorities: even apart from relationship, one witness is ordinarily insufficient to establish such a disqualification. Thus the limitation of "recognition" to the son does not necessarily prove that the father would otherwise be a disqualified relative to the grandson.
