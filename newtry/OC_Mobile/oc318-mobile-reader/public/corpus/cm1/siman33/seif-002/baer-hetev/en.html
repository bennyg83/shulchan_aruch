Rabbinically. The Shach disputes Rama and writes that Rambam never intended to say that maternal relatives are disqualified only rabbinically with respect to kiddushin. On the contrary, Rambam holds that if kiddushin took place before such relatives, the woman is not betrothed at all and requires no get. Rambam commonly calls laws derived through the thirteen hermeneutical principles "from the words of the Sages" even though they have Torah force. See the Shach's extensive discussion. I have demonstrated that his proofs are unconvincing and that Rama's words are correct. There is, however, a difficulty from Rambam, Hilchot Issurei Biah 14:14, where he rules that twin brothers conceived before conversion but born after conversion incur the prohibition of a brother's wife. With converts, paternal kinship is not legally recognized, so their relation can only be maternal, yet Rambam writes that they incur karet. This appears inconsistent with Rama's statement that maternal kinship is only rabbinic. One must therefore say that Rambam holds testimony follows the law of chalitzah and yibbum, where brotherhood is specifically paternal, derived from the brotherhood of the sons of Yaakov. Likewise, regarding testimony the Torah says, "Fathers shall not be put to death," etc., indicating that the Torah specifically focused on paternal kinship, while the remaining disqualifications are only supported by an asmachta. This is also somewhat implied by the Sema, seif katan 7. Examine carefully.
