These are the disqualified relatives: brothers, whether maternal or paternal, are first-degree relatives to one another; their children are second-degree relatives to one another; and their grandchildren are third-degree relatives to one another. A third-degree relative is always fit to testify for a first-degree relative, and certainly for a second-degree relative, whereas two second-degree relatives, and certainly a second-degree relative and a first-degree relative, are disqualified. Thus, a father and his son's son are disqualified because father and son are first-degree relatives like two brothers. A father and his son's grandson, who is four generations removed from him, are fit because they stand as third degree to first degree. The same pattern applies through women: two sisters, or a brother and sister, whether paternal or maternal, are first-degree relatives; their sons or daughters are second-degree relatives to one another; and their grandchildren are third-degree relatives to one another (Tur, siman 6, in the name of Rabbeinu Chananel). Some say that a third-degree relative is disqualified from testifying for a first-degree relative. {Rama: This is the proper ruling. Some say that this disqualification is only rabbinic, although other disqualifications are by Torah law; this affects witnesses to kiddushin, as explained in Even HaEzer, siman 42. Others say that maternal relatives too are disqualified only by rabbinic law.}
