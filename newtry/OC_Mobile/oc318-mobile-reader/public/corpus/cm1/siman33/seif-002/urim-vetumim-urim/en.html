Brothers, etc. The Sages derive this from the verse, "Fathers shall not be put to death through sons." The "fathers" refers to brothers, and their sons are excluded from testifying for one another. Therefore brothers are first degree relatives to one another, since they are termed "fathers" by the verse, and their sons are second degree relatives, termed "sons" in the verse. Gemara.
And it goes without saying that a third degree relative to a second degree relative is qualified. Lest one think that because the generations have not become so distant the relative should still be disqualified, the ruling teaches otherwise. Sema, following his approach. See what I write below.
A third degree relative to a first degree relative is disqualified. This is Rabbeinu Tam's view. There is a dispute within his position whether the disqualification is Scriptural or rabbinic, since it is possible that the derivation from the extra wording of the verse is only an asmachta. Sema.
The practical difference concerns witnesses to kiddushin. If kiddushin was performed before witnesses who are third degree relatives to a first degree relative, a get should be required, because perhaps the Sages imposed the disqualification only stringently and did not intend to uproot the kiddushin entirely.
Some say that maternal relatives, etc. The same applies to relatives through marriage. Rambam states this explicitly, and Rama relied on the fact that it is all the more obvious from the law of maternal relatives. Shach holds that if kiddushin was performed before such witnesses there is no concern for the kiddushin at all. See Tumim, where I conclude that one should be stringent and require a get. I discuss Rambam's words there at length.
