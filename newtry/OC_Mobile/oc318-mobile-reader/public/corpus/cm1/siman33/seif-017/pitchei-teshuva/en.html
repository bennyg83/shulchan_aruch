Who are related to one another. See Responsa Rabbi Akiva Eiger, siman 54, who questions a case where two witnesses were related to each other through marriage when they saw the event and later ceased to be related. Is this considered 'disqualified at the beginning'? One may argue that at the time of seeing, if they had not intended to testify, either one could have gone alone to beit din and testified as a single witness. Even if they intended to testify, according to the Rosh and those who require disqualification only upon testimony in beit din, until both actually testified each remained capable of giving single-witness testimony. Their joint testimony would have failed only when both testified. Since they had not yet done so, perhaps each retained the status of a valid individual witness, and after the relationship ended they could testify together as two witnesses. Rabbi Akiva Eiger found only Hagahot Ashri, Shevuot chapter 4, siman 15, stating plainly the opposite, that this is considered disqualified at the beginning. Hagahot Ashri even adds that if their wives were already dying at the time, this still is not considered a qualified beginning. Rabbi Akiva Eiger finds this difficult and says Tosafot there is no proof, because 'related through their wives' may mean related to the lender and borrower. He later brings support for Hagahot Ashri from Sotah 24 regarding a yevamah awaiting levirate marriage. It seems that Rabbi Akiva Eiger's doubt should apply specifically to monetary witnesses, because each witness alone was initially capable of serving as one witness and creating an oath obligation. For witnesses to kiddushin and similar matters, however, one witness accomplishes nothing without the second. If they were related at the time, the testimony therefore began in disqualification without doubt. Yet this would undermine his proof from Sotah, where a single witness likewise has no effect regarding the relevant pre-marital testimony, so the comparison to monetary law remains difficult. Chatam Sofer, Choshen Mishpat, siman 37, records that his father-in-law Rabbi Akiva Eiger presented this doubt to him. Chatam Sofer writes that he had independently developed the same basic reasoning in his novellae to Shevuot, but disagreed with extending the doubt to witnesses of kiddushin. He also questions the source of Hagahot Ashri. He adds that one practical case arises where a woman claims maintenance and clothing and the man denies the kiddushin, and she invokes the witnesses to the kiddushin. If they were related through their wives at the time they saw the kiddushin, they were certainly disqualified from the outset, because one witness in kiddushin has no effect. The same applies to divorce and similar cases. As to witnesses who are related to the judges, see Sema, seif katan 26, Shach, seif katan 16, and Noda BiYehudah, Even HaEzer, siman 72, partly cited in Pitchei Teshuva there, siman 11, seif katan 16.
