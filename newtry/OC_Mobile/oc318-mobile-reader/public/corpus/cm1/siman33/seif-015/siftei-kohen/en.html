If he claims that he paid. The presentations in Beit Yosef and Rabbeinu Yerucham are confused regarding the Raavad. They imply that he distinguishes between two statements and one statement, but he makes no such distinction. Rather, where a person testifies about himself, the concept of testimony does not apply to that portion at all. A person is not deemed a disqualified witness concerning himself so that we would invoke the rule that when part of testimony is void, all of it is void. Here, by contrast, it is ordinary testimony of which part is void, so the whole testimony is void. See the Rosh at the beginning of Makkot. It seems Rabbeinu Yerucham and the Beit Yosef thought the Rosh's first answer also remained according to the Raavad, but this is not so. See Rivash, siman 339, and below, siman 34, seif 26.
