See Baer Heitev and Shach below, siman 37, seif 18, seif katan 32, who cites many authorities holding that even a monetary disqualification requires that the witness be qualified both at the beginning and at the end. He concludes that the possessor may say, 'I rely on those authorities,' and Baer Heitev cites this there. Chatam Sofer, Choshen Mishpat, siman 36, writes that there are three categories in the rule requiring qualification at both beginning and end. First, disqualification by kinship unquestionably requires qualification at both stages, as stated in Bava Batra 128a. Second, disqualification because of a monetary interest is treated leniently by many authorities, for three reasons: the witness can remove his interest at any time; the disqualification is not universal as kinship is; and initially he was not a disqualified witness but a litigant, becoming a witness only when he removed his interest, so his 'beginning' as a witness was already in a state of qualification. Other authorities disagree and require qualification at both stages even for monetary interest, and the Shach rules that the possessor may rely on them. Third, disqualification because of sin is not explicitly discussed in the Gemara, but the Shach in siman 34, seif katan 33, assumes that the rule of qualification at beginning and end does not apply. From the arrangement of his words, Chatam Sofer suggests that this may have been said only regarding a transgression not involving monetary rapacity. See there and Pitchei Teshuva to siman 34, seif 29, seif katan 46.
