(In the Tur, this siman, seif 6: therefore, two men married to, etc.) Many have challenged this, and Maharshal likewise writes in responsum 80 that the Tur was imprecise, because the husband of the elderly woman could testify even for the daughter of her son herself. The Mishna disqualifies only one's stepson, as the Tur writes in siman 108, and it is likewise written nearby that the husband of one's maternal grandmother is valid. To avoid, Heaven forbid, attributing error to the great Tur, it appears that his words are exact and teach a major novelty. The Gemara states that Rabbi Yose says only the stepson himself is disqualified, and similarly only the brother-in-law himself. Concerning a brother-in-law there is a dispute. The Rif explains that one is certainly disqualified for his brother-in-law's son, because that son is the husband of his mother's sister, which all agree is a disqualifying relationship. Thus, when the Gemara limits the disqualification to the brother-in-law himself, it means that the brother-in-law's sons by another wife are valid. Rashi explains that it excludes the sons of one's brother-in-law who were born to one's wife's sister, and the Rosh agrees. Although the witness is disqualified for that son, the disqualification is not because he is the son of the witness's brother-in-law, but because he is the husband of the witness's maternal aunt. The practical difference is that if he were disqualified on account of being the brother-in-law's son, the brother-in-law's son-in-law would also be disqualified. The Gemara therefore teaches that the disqualification is because he is the husband of the witness's maternal aunt, with the result that the latter's son-in-law is valid. The Tur explains this so that one not mistakenly infer that, just as in the case of a brother-in-law only his son-in-law is valid but his son is disqualified, so too when the Mishna says 'his stepson alone,' only the stepson's son-in-law is valid but his son is not. The Tur therefore writes that the stepson's son-in-law would be valid even without the Mishna's special wording, because he is related only through two marital links and is first with second. According to my father the Rosh, two applications of 'a husband is like his wife' are not combined. In the case of the brother-in-law himself, by contrast, one could have said that the Mishna teaches precisely that two marital links are not applied even in first with second, making him valid. The Tur therefore first states that the grandson is considered first with second. Since they are permitted to testify for each other by reason of the two marital links according to the Rosh, we learn that the Mishna's phrase 'his stepson alone' refers to the stepson's son, who is valid. This is the Tur's intent.
