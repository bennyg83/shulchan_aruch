Since the judgment has been concluded. See Baer Heitev, who cites the Shach's explanation that he will certainly maintain his original statement, etc. See Responsa Noda B'Yehuda, Even HaEzer, siman 27, who writes that even according to the Shach, who explains the reason as the tendency to maintain one's prior statement, the case still requires that judgment have been concluded. The Shach's disagreement concerns only a case where judgment was concluded but the person had not yet testified. Conversely, where he already testified but judgment had not yet been concluded, he is later qualified to testify. See there. I also mentioned this briefly in Pitchei Teshuva to Even HaEzer, siman 17, seif 3, seif katan 19. In the glosses of Rabbi Akiva Eiger to Shulchan Aruch, on the Shach's words that he can no longer be sued except on the basis of this witness, it is written: The Ra'ah explicitly writes in Kesubos 22, s.v. amar Rav Huna amar Rav, in the name of one opinion, that witnesses who were disqualified when they testified and were later rendered qualified are not believed with respect to that same testimony, because they are regarded as interested parties in the testimony once they have already testified. End of quote.
