(The Tur further writes in seif 21.) 'But his wife's paternal grandfather is valid for him.' The Beit Yosef correctly asks that he is certainly disqualified, since he is first with second through one marital link. A further proof is the Tur's phrase 'and his son's daughter-in-law,' which is the reading found in some texts and refers to his wife's paternal grandfather. There is also proof from the Yerushalmi, which asks whether Moshe may testify for Pinchas's wife. This implies that it was obvious that Aharon was disqualified for her. One cannot say that the Yerushalmi was asking two questions at once, for it would then have formulated the second as an 'if you say' inquiry. Rabbeinu Tam likewise proved from this Yerushalmi that Moshe is plainly disqualified for Pinchas, since the question was not asked about that case. Nor can one say the questioner asked both issues simultaneously, for the respondent, who answered that Moshe was valid, should then have said emphatically that even Aharon was valid, just as the Talmud reasons in this manner in the chapter Elu Tereifot concerning a rib and half a rib that were removed. Maharshal wished to validate one's wife's paternal grandfather because the generations are separated. In my humble opinion this is impossible, for the Tur writes that Rabbeinu Tam validates third with first in relation to the latter's wife. He should have stated the greater novelty that even first with second is valid, since that case too involves separated generations. The responsum of the Rosh cited by the Beit Yosef in siman 17 also states that in third with first we do not apply even one marital equivalence, based on the Yerushalmi, implying that in second with first it is applied. That discussion too concerns separated generations. A further strong proof is the Tur's statement that one's stepson is disqualified for him, but not the stepson's son or son-in-law, although they are disqualified for his wife. This explicitly teaches that the stepson's son-in-law is disqualified for the wife, namely that one's wife's paternal grandfather is disqualified. One cannot say 'they are disqualified for his wife' refers only to the stepson's son, because the Tur uses the plural. Rather, the text must be emended to read: 'his wife's paternal great-uncle is valid,' exactly paralleling the Yerushalmi's question of Moshe regarding Pinchas's wife, where Moshe is valid, while the wife's paternal grandfather is disqualified. This appears to me the primary ruling. Do not ask why the husband of one's maternal grandmother, who is first with second through one marital link, is valid, while the wife's paternal grandfather, also first with second through one marital link, is disqualified. A wife's direct ancestors are psychologically closer to her husband, because she descends from them, than the husband is to his wife's son from another man. This is the intent of the Sages in saying 'his stepson alone,' and it is a straightforward distinction.
