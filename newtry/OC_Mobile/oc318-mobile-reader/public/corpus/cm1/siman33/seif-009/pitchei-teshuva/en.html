If the borrower comes to exempt himself. See Sema, seif katan 24. See Responsa Neot Deshe, siman 67, concerning a case where Shimon produced a document in Reuven's handwriting, authenticated by Levi and Yehuda, who testified concerning both its authentication and the substance of the transaction. The document stated that Reuven sent Shimon to a certain official to obtain a required writing for him, and that when Shimon brought that writing from the official Reuven would pay him one hundred gold coins as his fee. The document also contained other terms and conditions. Shimon now demanded his fee, claiming that he had already delivered the official's writing to Reuven. Reuven replied that he had already settled with Shimon and paid him, and that he had not taken back the document because when he originally signed it only two lines were written on it, whereas everything now appearing in the document was false, because the arrangements were ultimately made differently. Since it had not been a complete document at the time, he had not bothered to retrieve it. Beit din asked Shimon why he had delivered the official's writing to Reuven before receiving his fee. Shimon answered that Levi and Yehuda had guaranteed Reuven's payment. Indeed, even before traveling to the official he had not trusted Reuven at all, and only because of Levi and Yehuda's guarantee had he gone. The document had never been in Shimon's possession, but had remained with those guarantors, and now, when he needed to litigate, he took it from them in order to sue Reuven. He also claimed that Reuven's alleged settlement was false. Their arguments continued at length. Neot Deshe ruled that the document Shimon produced was merely a worthless shard, because Shimon admitted that Levi and Yehuda were his guarantors, and a guarantor is disqualified from testifying. Even relatives of a guarantor are disqualified regarding all claims arising between borrower and lender. See siman 33, Sema seif katan 24. Consequently, since the witnesses on the document were disqualified, the document is worthless, and Reuven may contradict any of its contents as he wishes. All of Shimon's claims are therefore merely oral claims, and Reuven may swear and be exempt. Even if Shimon now wishes to release Levi and Yehuda from the guarantee so that they will no longer have a financial interest, this is ineffective. First, from the wording of the question it appears that Levi and Yehuda were guarantors of the type known as arev shlof dotz, concerning whom siman 129 explains that the creditor has no claim against the borrower as long as the guarantors have assets. If so, Shimon initially has no claim against Reuven at all while Levi and Yehuda have property, and they themselves are therefore Reuven's opposing parties. In such a case there is no room for Shimon to release them from the guarantee. Second, even if Levi and Yehuda were ordinary guarantors, releasing them would still not help, as explained in siman 121, seif 9: even if a borrower releases an agent from an oath so that the agent will no longer be financially interested in his testimony, the release is ineffective because we may say that he testifies for the borrower in return for the benefit of being released from the oath. Third, even in a case where removal of an interest does render a witness qualified, he must testify again before beit din after the interest has been removed, as explained in siman 33, seif 15. In the present case, therefore, this document is at minimum void because it was executed when the witnesses were financially interested. The claim thus becomes an oral claim. Although Levi and Yehuda may orally testify regarding Reuven's denial of the particular terms between the parties, Reuven is believed with an oath when he further claims that he already settled with and paid Shimon. We do not say that Shimon, as holder of the document, swears against the claim of payment, because the document has been voided. All of this is straightforward. See there. In siman 68, Neot Deshe further writes that even if Levi and Yehuda were not financially interested when they signed, such as where they did not become guarantors before Shimon traveled to the official, but only later, when he brought the official's writing and refused to give it to Reuven until his fee was paid, at which point they became guarantors and thus financially interested, the document is nevertheless disqualified. Since they are presently interested, we are concerned that they wrote the document only now and intentionally antedated it, unless witnesses saw the document before Shimon returned from the official, while Levi and Yehuda were not yet guarantors. This is explicitly explained in Bava Batra 159, that with a financially interested witness we require witnesses to have seen the document before he acquired the interest.
