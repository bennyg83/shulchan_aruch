(In the Beit Yosef, beginning: converts have no kinship, etc.) See the end of seif 5, and it requires study. I do not know what uncertainty my father of blessed memory had here. An explicit Gemara in Yevamot 97b, cited by the Rif and Rosh at the end of the first chapter of Yevamot, teaches that twin brothers conceived before conversion but born after conversion neither perform chalitzah nor yibbum, yet each is liable for relations with the other's wife. Rashi explains that the liability is karet because she is the wife of his maternal brother. Their status as maternal brothers is thus explicit, and they are plainly disqualified from testifying for each other, since they are actual maternal brothers. The Gemara's statement that a forbidden relation is prohibited to everyone, etc., concerns brothers whose birth too occurred before conversion. Their prohibition is rabbinic, lest people say that they came from a more stringent sanctity to a lesser one, as explained in the second chapter of Yevamot. Twins born in sanctity, by contrast, are prohibited to one another's wives and liable to karet by Torah law as full maternal brothers. This is precise and clear.
