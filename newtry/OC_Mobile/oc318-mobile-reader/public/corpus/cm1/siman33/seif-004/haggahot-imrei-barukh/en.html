(Netivot HaMishpat, seif katan 1) In my humble opinion there is no difficulty, etc., for their testimony is nothing, etc. Note: According to his reasoning, it requires study where one person has a single claim against two people, sues one of them, and the witnesses testify before beit din in that defendant's presence. Can the claimant then sue the second defendant on the basis of that testimony? One could say that the first testimony cannot obligate the second, since it was not given in his presence. Yet taking the testimony a second time in his presence may also be ineffective, because once the witnesses gave full testimony against the first defendant they are naturally inclined to maintain their earlier words and cannot excuse an inconsistency by saying that, because the first testimony was given outside the litigant's presence, they had not been precise. Their testimony was given in the presence of one of the litigants, and as to that litigant it was validly received. This question extends to a creditor collecting from purchasers where testimony was received in support of the creditor but the seller was not present. The seller can say that he wishes to litigate with the creditor, as stated in Bava Metzia 14a. Since he too is a litigant, no ruling should be entered against him on testimony taken outside his presence. Yet if the witnesses testify again, one might argue that they are predisposed to uphold their first testimony. If so, strict law would appear to prevent collection from the purchasers, because the seller cannot be obligated. This could resolve Tosafot's question there in Bava Metzia. See also Ketubot 94a concerning two brothers, and Shitah Mekubetzet there, where the students of Rabbeinu Yonah cite the Ramah that testimony heard before the first litigant may later be repeated before the second. Those words indicate that even where the witnesses cannot retract the testimony already accepted in the presence of the first litigant, they may nevertheless testify again against the second in his presence. At first glance one can answer that the witnesses are not considered interested merely because they testified earlier, since they can simply refrain from testifying again, for beit din does not ordinarily compel particular witnesses to volunteer. They also have a migo that they could remain silent, and could say that they no longer remember. The Itur's concern that witnesses are inclined to reinforce their earlier words may apply only where their first testimony was rejected and the court ruled contrary to it. In such a case they may actively seek to testify again in order to vindicate the testimony that was previously nullified. But where their testimony was accepted as to one litigant, they have no personal interest in lying when testifying again, because their earlier words already stand and require no reinforcement.
