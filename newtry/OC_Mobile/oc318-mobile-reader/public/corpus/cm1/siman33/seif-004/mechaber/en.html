If two women are second-degree relatives to one another, their husbands may testify for one another, because we do not apply the rule that two husbands have the same status as their wives to second-degree relatives. {Rama: Nevertheless, ideally they should not sign the same document together (Terumat HaDeshen, siman 226).} If the women are first-degree relatives, such as where one man married a woman and another married her daughter, or if the men are married to sisters, the husbands are disqualified from testifying for one another, because the rule that two husbands have the same status as their wives does apply. It also applies between a first-degree and a second-degree relative. Thus, just as a man is disqualified from testifying for his wife's sister's son, he is disqualified from testifying for the husband of his wife's sister's daughter. He may, however, testify for the son whom his wife's sister's husband had with another woman. Some say that the rule concerning two husbands does not apply between a first-degree and a second-degree relative. {Rama: This appears to me to be the proper ruling.}
