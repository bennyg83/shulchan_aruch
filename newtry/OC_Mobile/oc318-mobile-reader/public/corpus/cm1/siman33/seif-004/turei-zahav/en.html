(Seif 16: there is no distinction whether the borrower, etc.) The Nimukei Yosef brings two explanations. According to Rashi, both the borrower and guarantor denied the loan itself. Relatives of the guarantor came to testify, and Rav Huna son of Rav Yehoshua disqualified them, because when the borrower has no assets the creditor proceeds against the guarantor. It is therefore as though the document had been written against both borrower and guarantor, and since the witnesses are disqualified regarding the guarantor, they are also disqualified regarding the borrower. The Raavad explains that their claims concerned repayment: borrower and guarantor admitted the loan, but the borrower claimed, 'I repaid you,' which would release the guarantor. The creditor brought witnesses before whom the borrower had admitted that he had not repaid, with the result that the guarantor would become liable. Since the Nimukei Yosef brings both explanations, it appears that they disagree. In my view, they differ as follows. According to the Raavad, the witnesses might be valid in Rashi's case, because there was never a joint liability of borrower and guarantor until valid witnesses established that this borrower borrowed, at which point the guarantor would become liable. Here the witnesses are relatives of the guarantor and are therefore treated as nonexistent, so there was never any established partnership of liability between borrower and guarantor, since neither admits that the borrower borrowed from the creditor. In the Raavad's case, however, both admit the loan, and it is therefore reasonable to say that just as the witnesses are not believed regarding the guarantor, they are not believed regarding the borrower, because the two are already clearly joined in liability. The Tur, followed by the Mechaber, rules like Rav Sheshet that in both cases they are not believed. This is the meaning of 'there is no distinction.' The Sema, seif katan 11, did not examine the Nimukei Yosef cited above, who expressly writes that both denied the loan. How then could the Sema write that perhaps the guarantor did not deny it? I have also seen other explanations of the Tur's phrase 'there is no distinction,' but none properly accounts for the difference between Rashi and the Raavad except what I have written with Heaven's help, and this is the correct explanation.
