And they testify concerning him that he borrowed, etc. Their testimony benefits the guarantor. Even if the borrower would deny the loan, the guarantor may be trustworthy and would not deny his obligation, but through their testimony the borrower himself becomes liable. The same rule applies even if they testify that the borrower repaid, thereby exempting the guarantor as well. Since they are relatives of the guarantor and their testimony affects him, whether for benefit or detriment, the entire testimony is invalid. Concerning why we do not divide their statement and accept only the part unrelated to the guarantor, see Tumim, seif katan 11. See also Tumim, where I write that if the lender holds a document and these relatives testify that it was paid, their testimony is certainly invalid.
Relatives of the murder victim, etc. Since they receive no financial benefit from the testimony, a desire for vengeance against the murderer is not considered a disqualifying interest. Therefore even the victim himself may testify, and if he is not a tereifah the murderer may be executed on the basis of his testimony. But if the victim remains alive and the testimony concerns payment for lost work and medical expenses, the relatives certainly may not testify because they have a monetary interest. Sema. See Tumim, where I conclude that the victim himself and relatives of the victim's heirs are qualified according to all opinions, while the status of the victim's own relatives is disputed.
