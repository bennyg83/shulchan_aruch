There is no difference whether the borrower comes to exempt himself, etc. Meaning, not only where the borrower says, 'I never borrowed,' is it obvious that the witnesses are disqualified for him because they are relatives of the guarantor and their testimony benefits the guarantor. We can say that the witnesses know the guarantor is an honest man who will not deny a guaranty he undertook and will admit it to the lender. His admission can obligate himself but not the borrower. By testifying that the borrower borrowed, the witnesses place the borrower ahead of the guarantor, for collection must first be sought from the borrower. Even where the borrower admits that he borrowed but says that he later repaid, so that this claim would exempt the guarantor as well, and the witnesses' testimony that he did not repay gives no direct benefit to the guarantor, they remain disqualified regarding the borrower. The reason is that if the borrower has no assets the creditor will proceed against the guarantor. Thus every testimony against the borrower is effectively testimony concerning the guarantor. I wrote another explanation in the Derishah.
They may testify concerning the murderer. The reason is stated there: once the victim has died, what benefit do his relatives derive from the killer's death? Rama's conclusion that relatives of one who was struck may testify regarding matters from which the injured person derives no benefit is likewise from the Mordechai. It further writes that even the victim himself, while still alive and not a tereifah, may testify, since a person is believed to say, 'So-and-so violated me sexually.' It appears that the wording 'the murdered person himself' teaches that at the time the murderer is actually adjudicated the injured person has already died, but at the time he testified he was still alive and was not a tereifah. His testimony may then be accepted to execute or exile the offender, because he has no benefit from the execution or exile. If he remains alive, however, it is obvious that his testimony cannot be accepted to impose payments for loss of work, medical expenses, and the like, since he has a direct financial interest. Rama abbreviated this because capital and exile cases do not apply among us and cited the law of relatives of a murdered person only to teach by analogy the law of relatives of an injured person.
