If the borrower comes to exempt himself. See Sema, seif katan 24, and Responsa Neot Deshe, siman 67. Shimon produced a document in Reuven's handwriting, authenticated by Levi and Yehudah, who also testified to the substance of the transaction. The document stated that Reuven sent Shimon to a certain nobleman to obtain a needed writing and promised him one hundred gold coins upon delivery, with additional conditions. Shimon claimed he delivered the writing and demanded his fee. Reuven answered that they had already compromised and settled, and that he had not taken back the document because when he originally signed it only two lines were written and the later additions were false. Shimon replied that Levi and Yehudah had guaranteed Reuven's payment, that he relied on Reuven only because of their guarantee, and that the document had remained in their hands until he took it for litigation. Neot Deshe rules that the document is worthless because Shimon admits Levi and Yehudah were his guarantors, and a guarantor is disqualified as an interested witness. Even relatives of a guarantor are disqualified regarding disputes between borrower and lender. Since the witnesses to the document were disqualified, Reuven may deny its contents and Shimon's case becomes only an oral claim, against which Reuven may swear and be exempt. Shimon cannot cure the defect by now releasing Levi and Yehudah from their guarantee. If they were guarantors from whom the lender must first collect, Shimon may have had no direct claim against Reuven while they had assets, making them actual parties to the dispute. Even if they were ordinary guarantors, releasing them does not necessarily remove the interest, similar to the rule that releasing an agent from an oath does not make him a competent witness because the benefit of the release itself may motivate his testimony. Moreover, where removal of interest does help, the witness must testify anew in beit din after the interest has been removed. Here the deed was executed while they were interested, so it remains void. Their oral testimony may still address factual details not affected by the interest, but Reuven's additional claim that he already settled and paid is believed with an oath, because there is no valid deed against him. In siman 68, Neot Deshe further writes that even if Levi and Yehudah were not guarantors when they signed, but became guarantors only later when Shimon returned with the nobleman's writing, the document is still suspect once they are presently interested. We may fear they wrote it later and antedated it, unless independent witnesses saw the document before they became guarantors. This is explicit in Bava Batra 159.
