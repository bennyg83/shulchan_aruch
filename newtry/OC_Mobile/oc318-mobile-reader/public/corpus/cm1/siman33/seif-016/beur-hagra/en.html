(Anthology) There is no distinction, etc. See the Rosh there, who cites Rashi and the Raavad. It appears at first that the Raavad disputes Rashi, and Rabbeinu Yerucham likewise writes that according to the Raavad, witnesses to a loan are believed because we divide their statement, since these are two separate statements. This cannot be correct, however, because the Raavad as cited by the Rosh makes no such distinction. His reason is that a litigant is not treated as a witness concerning himself, as the Shach also writes. Nevertheless, according to the Rosh's first resolution, and according to what is written in seif 15 concerning disqualification and in the gloss concerning relatives, which does not follow the Raavad's resolution, one must say that there are other witnesses that he became a guarantor in response to a denial. The Ritva and Rivash, however, follow the Raavad. See siman 36.

(Anthology) The relatives of the murdered person, etc. This follows the second resolution of Tosafot, Makkot 6a, at the end of s.v. nirba, in the name of Ri Bekhor. According to the first resolution, however, the rule must be limited to the murdered person himself, as Tosafot write there, s.v. ela me'ata. All the more so according to the Rosh's explanation there in the name of the Raavad, in seif 13, that a person is considered his own relative. Rivash likewise writes that this applies only to the litigant himself, whereas his relatives are disqualified and invalidate the testimony. According to that view, this ruling does not stand. See siman 36, seif 1, in the gloss concerning a communal enactment.
