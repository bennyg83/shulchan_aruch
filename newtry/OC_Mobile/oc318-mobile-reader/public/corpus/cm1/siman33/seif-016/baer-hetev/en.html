Murdered. Even the victim himself, so long as he is alive and is not a tereifah, may testify, for he is believed to say, "So-and-so sodomized me." The Rama abbreviated here because capital and exile penalties are not practiced among us, and he mentioned the relatives of a murder victim only so that one may derive from it the law concerning relatives of one who was beaten. Thus writes the Sema. The Shach discusses at length whether a tereifah may testify in monetary cases even though testimony must also be capable of hazamah. See there, where he challenges Darkei Moshe and the Sema, who wrote that according to the view requiring testimony capable of hazamah even in monetary cases, a tereifah is disqualified from testifying. This is incorrect, for monetary law is entirely unlike capital law. In capital cases hazamah cannot apply to witnesses who are themselves tereifot, because they can say that they intended to kill a person already considered dead. Likewise, where the tereifah is the witness, the law of retaliating against those who later make a false hazamah cannot apply. None of this is relevant in monetary cases. Although the witness is a tereifah, the monetary testimony remains subject to hazamah, because if he is shown to be a false witness he must pay. See there at length.
