(The Tur further writes in siman 31: such as her son and daughter, etc.) It is difficult why he did not mention her sister herself. It is also difficult why he says 'until he marries her,' for it is obvious that after marriage he is disqualified, and the Tur has already written this many times. It appears that the Tur follows the Rambam, who likewise mentions the husband of her sister. There the difficulty is resolved, for the Rambam writes: 'But if the testimony concerns the relatives of his betrothed, such as the husband of her sister, they do not disqualify him until he marries her.' This implies an initial, preferred disqualification. The Tur means the same. First he speaks of testimony for the betrothed herself, where the disqualification is absolute. Regarding her relatives, he is free from absolute disqualification until he marries her, at which point he becomes fully disqualified even for her relatives. It follows that before marriage there is at least a disqualification ab initio, even concerning the husband of her sister. That he is permitted to testify for her sister herself is known from the rule concerning her son and daughter. This appears correct to me.
