(The Tur further writes in seif 19.) 'And her mother's husband, and his son-in-law.' It is obvious that this means his own son-in-law. If, as the Beit Yosef explains, it referred to the son-in-law of his mother's husband, why did the Tur not also count his son, as the Mishna says, 'their sons and sons-in-law'? Similarly, when the Tur later writes 'and his daughter-in-law,' it refers to his own daughter-in-law, not to his father's wife, contrary to the Beit Yosef's explanation. This is simple: in the clause listing a woman who is disqualified for him, the Tur lists those disqualified through her and includes his son-in-law; in the clause listing a man, he lists those disqualified through him and includes his daughter-in-law. Some editions read 'and her daughter-in-law and her son's daughter-in-law,' but that is not primary. The plain reading of our text is correct, and the Beit Yosef departed from it without cause. The Beit Yosef's further question, why the Tur did not mention the daughter-in-law of one's grandson, whom Rabbeinu Tam disqualifies, is also imprecise, because the Tur wrote that the rule 'a husband is like his wife' is not applied in such a remote relationship.
