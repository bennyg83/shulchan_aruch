A man with his wife, etc., and therefore he is not, etc. Meaning, only one generation upward or downward is treated as the same person. But a second generation, which is third degree relative to her, is not treated that way. Although for first degree with second degree we say that husband and wife are one legal unit, as taught in the Mishnah, even second degree with second degree does not extend through a third generation because the generations have become separated. Therefore the Yerushalmi validates the wife of Pinchas, and the Gemara validates Mar bar Rav Ashi regarding his paternal grandfather. An explicit Mishnah says "his stepson alone," but not his stepson's son, as Rashi writes there. This is what the Tur writes in seif 23, "but the father of his father-in-law, etc." The Beit Yosef and Darkei Moshe there strained to explain this; see there.
(Collected note) Not her son, etc., and not her daughter, etc. Yerushalmi there, halacha 7: "we learned, his stepson alone. What of the wife of his stepson? What of the husband of his stepdaughter? A wife is like her husband and a husband like his wife. The mother-in-law of the wife of his stepson is like his stepson, and the husband is like his wife, etc." See there. (End of collected note.)
(Collected note) And not her father, etc. All these resemble the first four clauses. Her father and mother are explicit there, for even their brothers are disqualified, as stated in the Mishnah and Gemara regarding the brother of my father-in-law, etc., and the brother of my mother-in-law, etc. (End of collected note.)
