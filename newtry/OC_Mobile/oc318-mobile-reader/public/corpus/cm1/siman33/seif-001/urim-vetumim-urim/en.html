Except for a friend and an enemy, etc. Regarding judging, a person's mind may not tolerate finding merit for an enemy or fault in a friend. Testimony is different, because witnesses merely report what they saw and heard, and Jews are not suspected of lying because of friendship or hatred. Sema, and this is obvious. See Tumim concerning Maharshal's view that an absolute enemy, such as one whose opponent seeks nearly to kill him, is disqualified from testifying because such hatred creates a real suspicion that he will seek revenge by false testimony. I wrote that he has no clear proof from the Gemara and that we should not create a new disqualification from reasoning alone. I also concluded that household servants are qualified to testify, as is the common practice, and I found support for this from Rambam. Their fitness to serve as judges, however, requires study, because they may fall within the categories of friend and enemy. The Radbaz likewise appears to treat them as such. A wedding attendant is qualified to testify concerning the very matter in which he served as attendant. So I concluded in Tumim, unlike Knesset HaGedolah, who remained uncertain.
