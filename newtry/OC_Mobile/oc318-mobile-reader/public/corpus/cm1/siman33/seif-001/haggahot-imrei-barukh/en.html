(Shach, seif katan 1) I am astonished at the Maggid Mishneh who wrote, etc. Note: This requires study. Since the Shach himself upholds the reading in the Rambam that kiddushin by money are Torah law, it would seem that relatives through the mother's family should likewise be called disqualified by Torah law. Yet the Rambam's language in Hilchot Edut chapter 13 points in the opposite direction. He writes that those disqualified by Torah law are only relatives from the father's family. His earlier phrase, "from the received tradition," and his later statement that all other relatives are disqualified "by their words," clearly indicate that maternal relatives are not included among those disqualified by the Oral Torah, but only rabbinically. That is why he calls their disqualification "by their words." Similarly, in Hilchot Ishut he calls kiddushin by money "from the words of the Sages." See also the Rambam's own terminology in his Commentary to the Mishnah, Kelim 17:12; Tosafot Yom Tov, Ohalot 7:9; the final chapter of Sanhedrin, mishnah 3, concerning the greater stringency of the words of the Sages; and the other places he cites.
