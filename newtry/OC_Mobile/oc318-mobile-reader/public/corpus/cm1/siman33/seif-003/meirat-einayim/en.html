Any woman to whom you are disqualified, etc. These general rules refer to the relationships discussed immediately above: first degree with first degree, first with second, or second with second. Those are disqualified according to everyone. Until now the discussion had not yet addressed marital disqualification arising through one's spouse. The rule therefore teaches that any woman to whom you are disqualified because of one of the preceding relationships also disqualifies you to testify for her husband, because in one such marital link we say that husband and wife are one legal unit. The same applies in reverse: if you are disqualified to the husband through one of those relationships, you are also disqualified to his wife. This is accepted by everyone. But where the relationship is already more remote, such as the son of the brother of one's wife's father, and the question concerns that relative's wife, this creates second-degree relation combined with two marital links. There we do not automatically say that each husband is as his wife.
And according to those who disqualify third degree with first degree, etc. Meaning specifically in the closer relationships above, where the generations are not greatly separated, do we apply the principle that one husband is as his wife. In the more distant case of third degree with first degree, even a single marital link is not automatically treated that way. So writes the Rosh in Responsa, rule 57, siman 3. See Derishah.
