(There: nor for her father.) The Sema, seif katan 15, validates testimony concerning her paternal grandfather. I am astonished, for the Tur writes in siman 66, seif 15, in the name of the Rambam, that a relative in the second degree is disqualified through the rule that a husband is like his wife. He writes that one may not testify for the husband of his wife's niece. According to the Sema, one would be valid even for the wife's niece herself, since the wife's niece is first with second in relation to the wife, making the witness first with third in relation to her husband. Rather, it must be that the husband is treated exactly like his wife even in a case of first with second. Below, in connection with the Tur's words in seif 21, we will cite further material relevant to this. The Rambam did not mention even her paternal grandfather because his purpose was only to teach that affinity through marriage can itself create disqualification, as stated nearby. We therefore know automatically that the same applies even to his father-in-law, since the father-in-law is first with second in relation to his wife. See below, in the explanation of the Tur's words in seif 21, for more on this. After death, as regards them, the witness was originally unrelated. Accordingly, if it was a partial gift of a person on his deathbed, which requires an act of acquisition as stated in siman 250, he would be disqualified according to all opinions, because the testimony affects his own interest, since the donor can no longer retract afterward. This appears correct to me.
