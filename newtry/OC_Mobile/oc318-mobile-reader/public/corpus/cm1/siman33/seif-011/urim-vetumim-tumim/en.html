A convert who converted, etc. Beit Yosef is uncertain whether brothers who were conceived before conversion but born after conversion may testify for one another. Shach objects: we rule that they are prohibited to one another's wives as brothers. Thus they are Torah brothers, so how could one think they may testify for one another? Shach's objection is strong. Still, one can defend Beit Yosef according to Rambam, who holds that maternal-relative disqualification is rabbinic. The Sages disqualified maternal relatives as a decree because of paternal relatives. That makes sense among born Jews, where paternal kinship exists as a legal category. For converts, however, paternal kinship from before conversion is not recognized at all. Since there is no paternal-relative category to confuse with, perhaps the Sages did not impose the parallel maternal-relative disqualification. On the contrary, they may have left the rule distinct precisely to differentiate converts from ordinary Israelite lineage.
