Who converted. The Sema writes that the Beit Yosef was uncertain where their conception was not in holiness but their birth was in holiness, such as where their mother converted while pregnant, whether they too are called ordinary converts and have the status of a newborn child, or not. Darkei Moshe cites this and writes that both conception and birth must be outside holiness. Nevertheless, if one of them was born as a gentile and converted, that suffices, and he may testify even for his Israelite brother whose conception and birth were in holiness. The Shach writes that, in his view, there is no doubt at all, for Yoreh Deah siman 269, seif 4, explains that twin brothers conceived outside holiness and born in holiness incur karet for relations with a brother's wife. It is therefore obvious that they are disqualified by Torah law. The Taz writes likewise. According to what I wrote in seif katan 2, however, testimony is different. See there. It therefore appears that there is no difficulty at all. Examine carefully.
