(In the name of Maharshal in the Beit Yosef, beginning: all who are disqualified to judge, etc., through: and he ruled like the Rabbis, etc.) Tosafot in Makkot 6a, beginning 'If so, the victim should save him,' write that the victim is a litigant, or alternatively that he is an enemy, and an enemy is disqualified from testimony. Their intent is according to Rabbi Yehuda, who holds that an enemy is disqualified. According to his own position, capital cases could not be judged because of the presence of the victim or the person subjected to the forbidden act. This is simple. The work Bnei Shmuel wished to infer from Tosafot that all agree that an enemy of such intensity is disqualified, but that is incorrect. He himself writes that according to the explanation based on the victim being a tereifah, perhaps every enemy is valid. See there.
