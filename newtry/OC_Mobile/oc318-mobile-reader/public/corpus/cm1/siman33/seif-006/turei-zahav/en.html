(The Tur further writes.) In seif 6: 'But according to Rabbeinu Tam, who disqualifies first with third, etc.' One may ask why the Tur did not simply write, 'But according to Rabbeinu Tam, he is disqualified.' It appears that he intended to exclude the Semag's view that third with first is disqualified only in the case of one's brother's grandson, but not one's own great-grandson, because the generations are greatly separated. The Tur disagrees. He derives this from the Rif in the chapter Zeh Borer, who writes that Mar bar Rav Ashi validated a paternal grandfather, considering him first with third, but the halacha does not follow him, because grandfather and grandson are second with first. If separation of generations were legally meaningful, why would the Gemara treat Mar bar Rav Ashi as having erred in the simple classification of a grandfather? Perhaps he too regarded grandfather and grandson as first with second but validated them because the generations are separated, disqualifying only ordinary first with second, such as Reuven and Shimon's son. Rather, separation of generations is not legally significant. Consequently, when we reject Mar bar Rav Ashi and disqualify even first with third, we do so even where the generations are separated, namely in the case of a great-grandson. This is why the Tur writes that according to Rabbeinu Tam, who disqualifies first with third, he disqualifies even here, notwithstanding the separation of generations. This resolves Maharshal's challenge that Mar bar Rav Ashi validated even a paternal grandfather, and although we do not follow him, Rashbam explains that all descendants remain disqualified through all generations, which seems astonishing: Mar bar Rav Ashi validates even a paternal grandfather, yet we would disqualify through the fourth generation. The Rif's wording likewise implies that Mar bar Rav Ashi thought a paternal grandfather was first with third and therefore validated him, but the halacha does not follow him because father and son are first, making a paternal grandfather first with second. The views of the Halachot Gedolot and Rabbeinu Tam regarding first with third are discussed in Zeh Borer. Thus even a fourth generation is valid only according to the Rif, but according to Rabbeinu Tam they are disqualified. In my view, Rabbeinu Tam understands Mar bar Rav Ashi's mistake to have been that he considered separation of generations sufficient for validation, and therefore validated a paternal grandfather. The halacha rejects him, because separation of generations helps only where the relationship is through marriage, such as the wife of one who is third with first, as the Tur later writes in Rabbeinu Tam's name, but not in blood relationship. Since the Gemara rules that the halacha does not follow Mar bar Rav Ashi and that separation of generations is not significant here, even a fourth generation is disqualified because it is still first with third. Although the Semag validates a fourth generation even according to Rabbeinu Tam, it appears proper to rule stringently like the Tur on the authority of his father the Rosh, that third with first is disqualified even in the case of one's great-grandson.
