And there is one who validates. Taz holds that in a partial deathbed gift, where the deathly ill person could not fully retract and the testimony concerned him at the time of observation, a relative is disqualified because the testimony began while he was interested. Maharit questions this, comparing it to financial interest that can later be removed. I agree with Taz. In cases where a townsman later removes his financial interest, the testimony being offered after removal can be separated from the former interest because the relevant proposition, such as 'this property does not belong to the challenger,' was always testimony directed against the outsider. But where the very content of the testimony is that the relative himself sold, gave, or became obligated, the testimony necessarily concerns the relative. If it was observed while the witness was disqualified, later death does not cure the defect. The examples of relatives of a murder victim and a seller who identifies the buyer are different because the testimony can be divided: one can testify that the defendant killed a Jew without needing to testify legally about the deceased relative's rights, or the seller can be believed by a special credibility rule not based on ordinary witness status. Likewise, saying a third party has no title can remain testimony against that third party even though the relative once benefited incidentally. But where proof of the heir's liability depends on proving the deceased relative's own loan, sale, or gift, the original disqualification remains. Ketzot HaChoshen's distinction between sales and loans based on the principle that assets are guarantors is not convincing. After death the personal obligation of the deceased has ended in any event, and the heir becomes the relevant obligor with respect to inherited assets. Evidence valid against the heir can therefore establish the necessary lien. The real distinction is whether the substance of the testimony itself necessarily testified about the relative while the witness was disqualified.
