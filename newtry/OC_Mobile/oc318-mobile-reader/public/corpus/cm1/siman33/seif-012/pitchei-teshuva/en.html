Even though she left him children. See Rashbam to Bava Batra 128a, who explains the case of 'his daughter died' as one where she left him no children, in accordance with the Mishna in Zeh Borer. Rabbi Akiva Eiger, in his glosses to the Gemara there, questions this, since it is Rabbi Yehudah who says so, whereas the first Tanna holds that even if she left children the witness is valid, and that is the halacha. Knesset HaGedolah and Birkei Yosef had already raised the same difficulty. See Tumim, seif katan 7.
Since the judgment has already been rendered. See Baer Heitev's citation of the Shach, who explains that a person will certainly maintain his earlier statement. Noda BiYehudah, Even HaEzer, siman 27, writes that even according to the Shach, whose reason is that a person tends to maintain his prior statement, it is also necessary that judgment already have been rendered. The Shach disagrees only with requiring a prior ruling where the person had not yet testified. But if he testified while disqualified and no judgment was yet rendered, once he later becomes qualified he may testify anew. Rabbi Akiva Eiger further cites the Raah in Ketubot 22, in the name of some authorities, that disqualified witnesses who testified while disqualified and later became qualified are not believed to repeat that same testimony, because after having testified they are considered interested in maintaining their prior words. See also Maharit, volume 2, Even HaEzer, siman 37; Brit Avraham, Even HaEzer, end of siman 105 and Choshen Mishpat end of siman 2; Shaar HaMelech, Hilchot Ishut 9:12; and Chatam Sofer, Even HaEzer, volume 2, siman 67.
