Judgment was already rendered. The Shach writes that there is no reason to distinguish whether judgment was already rendered. He explains Baal HaItur's case as one where a witness originally testified while his relationship was unknown, the court ruled on that testimony, and only afterward the witness became unrelated. He cannot now testify again because he will naturally maintain his original story. I have already shown in siman 28 that we do not generally say a person is disqualified from retestifying merely because he wishes to maintain his prior words. Baal HaItur himself allows witnesses who testified before an unqualified court to repeat their testimony before a proper court. Rosh likewise rules that where a document was invalid because one signer was a relative or disqualified, the qualified signers may testify again from memory before beit din. Rama codifies this in siman 36 and Shulchan Aruch in siman 45. The Shach himself holds there that the same applies even after they first testified orally together with the disqualified witness. Thus the reason must truly be that judgment has already been rendered. Terumat HaDeshen, cited in siman 268, establishes that once a legal result was fixed, later developments do not automatically reopen it. He derives this from the case of a doubtful heir and a yavam, where once judgment was rendered the later availability of an alternative "whichever way you look at it" claim does not reopen the matter. The idea resembles legal abandonment of the prior claim after a completed judgment. Here too, when the witness was a relative the court's decision at that moment was correct. His later removal of the relationship does not reopen a judgment that was lawfully final then. There is also another possible rationale: testimony requires beginning and end in qualification. Once he first testified while related, his testimony has already reached its end in disqualification, and later removal of the relationship does not repair that original testimony.
