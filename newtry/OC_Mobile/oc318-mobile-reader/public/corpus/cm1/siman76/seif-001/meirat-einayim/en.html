One who borrowed from two people, etc. See the Derishah. There I wrote that these laws are derived from the case of two people who deposited property with one custodian, mentioned in the Gemara and stated by the Tur and Mechaber below in siman 300. Although the Tur writes there that only where they deposited one without the other's presence must the custodian, as a matter of law, give each claimant what he claims if each makes a demand, and even if they do not demand it he must do so to satisfy his obligation before Heaven, whereas if they deposited simultaneously, even without a document, he is liable only to satisfy his obligation before Heaven even when they demand, and if they do not demand he is exempt even before Heaven, the present case of a loan is different. Since at the time of the loan they informed the borrower that one was lending one hundred and the other two hundred, and borrowed money is given for expenditure, he should have paid close attention, for that was the reason they informed him. With a deposit, by contrast, even if they informed the custodian when depositing, he can say that he did not pay close attention because a deposit is not given for expenditure. He assumed that each depositor would later take back the bundle he had placed there, and that the bundles became mixed without his knowledge. See the Derishah.
After each one takes an oath, etc. We do not say that the borrower is one who is obligated to take a Torah oath because he admits part of each claimant's demand, namely that he owes each of them one hundred, and is unable to swear that he does not owe each another one hundred, so that he should pay. The reason is that he certainly does not owe each of them an additional one hundred, but only one of them, and he can swear to that fact. The second one hundred is money that, because of his negligence, he will ultimately pay gratuitously to one of them. The Sages therefore enacted that neither claimant may collect more than one hundred except upon taking an oath. See the Perishah, where I wrote another reason as well.

All of them hold that in such a case, where both lent to him and informed him of their respective amounts, he must give each two hundred if he wishes to satisfy his obligation before Heaven. They disagree only where merely one of them lent to him and did not make a demand, as the Tur and Mechaber state below in seif 3, for the reason I will explain there.
