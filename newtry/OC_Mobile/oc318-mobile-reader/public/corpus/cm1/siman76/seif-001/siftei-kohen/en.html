When they come to claim from him, etc. If they do not make claims against him, he need not give two hundred to each; that obligation exists only if he seeks to satisfy Heaven, as stated below. Whenever the authorities distinguish between “they claim from him” and “they do not claim from him,” a definite claim is considered a claim, whereas an uncertain claim is treated as no claim. There is no distinction whether the claim was made initially or only later. This is clear from the Gemara in HaMafkid, Baal HaTerumot, gate 39, and the other authorities.
He must give each one two hundred, etc. The Tur writes that this appears to be the Rosh’s view. I do not know the Tur’s source. The Rosh, following Rashi and Tosafot, writes that whenever two people deposited in one another’s presence, it is as though they deposited together in one bundle. The Tur cites this below in siman 300. The same reasoning could apply to an oral loan. Ramban’s ruling that in an oral loan the borrower must give each claimant two hundred follows his explicit view in Baal HaTerumot, gate 38, part 2, that deposits and loans are equal in this regard, and his view that exemption exists only when they actually deposited in one bundle, following Rif and Rambam. Thus, according to Ramban, the same rule naturally applies to a loan. The Tur must distinguish a loan from a deposit, but the source for such a distinction requires study.
After each one swears, etc. Each takes an oath while holding a sacred object. We do not say that the borrower admits one hundred to each and is uncertain regarding another hundred, so that he should pay without an oath as one obligated to swear who cannot swear. Since he knows with certainty that he owes only two hundred in total to both claimants, he is considered to advance a definite claim regarding that limitation. Baal HaTerumot writes this in the name of Rambam. Nimukei Yosef in HaMafkid and the Maggid Mishneh in chapter 8 of Hilchot She’elah write similarly in the name of Rashba.

And the balance remains deposited until Eliyahu comes. Since each claimant makes a definite claim, one of them is certainly dishonest. Therefore the disputed money remains deposited and is not divided unless both agree to divide it. If each says, “I know about one hundred and do not know about the other hundred,” the borrower gives each one hundred and thereby fulfills even his duty toward Heaven. In a written note, which is treated as a single bundle, and where neither makes a definite claim, he is exempt even toward Heaven according to all opinions, as is clear from the Rosh in HaMafkid, the Tur here and in siman 300, and the Rama there at the end of seif 1. The third hundred likewise remains deposited, because each claims part and there is concern for dishonesty, as explained in siman 365, seif 2, according to Rabbeinu Yerucham. But if neither makes any claim at all, the third hundred is divided between them because no dishonest claimant is present, as follows from Rambam, Rabbeinu Yerucham, and the Mechaber in siman 365, seif 2.

And the person holding the note may, etc., without authorization, etc. The Tur, following Ramban in Baal HaTerumot, explains that because the lenders became partners in one loan and one lien, relied upon one another, and trusted one another with possession of the note, which is held by one of them, the custodian or borrower was not negligent. The Tur adds that the holder can collect the entire debt without authorization. In my opinion, that final point is not essential. The same ruling applies even where the note is written so that one lender cannot collect the entire debt without authorization. In deposits, all agree that where the money was delivered in one bundle the custodian is exempt, yet the Tur and Mechaber rule in siman 77, seif 9, that one of two depositors cannot collect the entire deposit. The true reason is that by becoming partners and entrusting one another to some degree, they accepted the risk between themselves, so the custodian was not negligent. Ramban’s own words make this explicit. He writes that in a written loan the borrower gives each lender one hundred and the remainder stays deposited until Eliyahu comes, because the funds were always in one bundle. Although the borrower knew which lender contributed two hundred and which one hundred, they became partners in one loan and one lien, relied on one another to some degree, and trusted one another with the note itself. Ramban then adds that according to those who permit each lender to claim the entire debt, this conclusion is all the more obvious. Thus, the ability to collect the entire debt is only an additional consideration, not the foundation of the rule. Rashi, Tosafot, the Rosh, the Tur, and other authorities likewise hold that when two depositors deposit in each other’s presence, the custodian is exempt because they revealed that they did not suspect one another of later claiming the whole amount. Even though neither could collect the entire amount without authorization, the exemption remains. The Mechaber therefore properly stated the rule without the Tur’s additional phrase. The Rama’s insertion of that phrase is unnecessary and emphasizes a secondary point rather than the principal reason.
