One who borrowed, etc., but if, etc., since, etc. As stated there in the reconciliation between one deposit and another: in the first case it is as though he deposited with him separately. The same applies to a loan, as Sefer HaTerumot writes in the name of Ramban at length.
Others who are called A. Like the snails and servants of all the swearers of Hashem’s promise that if I were alone, I would like to give him a swear to him as the S. C.C. and I would like to say:
And there is one who says, etc. As stated in the Gemara there, that the case is specifically one who seeks to fulfill his obligation toward Heaven. The first case is comparable to an oral loan. Shach writes that no one disputes this. It appears that the opposing view is Ramban's opinion in seif 3, which requires none of the distinctions advanced by Sema and Shach. Yet in siman 300 the ruling is stated without qualification, implying no dispute, and the gloss then cites the first view of seif 3.
