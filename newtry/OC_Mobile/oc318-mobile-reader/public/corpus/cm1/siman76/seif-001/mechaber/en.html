One who borrowed orally from two people, one hundred from one and two hundred from the other, and each claims two hundred. This siman contains 3 seifim.
If a person borrowed from two people at the same time, one hundred from one and two hundred from the other, and they informed him which person's money was the one hundred and which person's was the two hundred, but when they come to collect each says, "The two hundred were mine," and the borrower does not remember which is entitled to one hundred and which to two hundred, he must give each two hundred, according to the Tur's presentation of the Rosh, after each takes an oath. If, however, it was a documented loan and they made a single document against him for three hundred, informing him that one had one hundred and one had two hundred, he gives each one hundred and the remaining one hundred remains deposited until Eliyahu comes. Rama: Since they became partners in a single loan and the one holding the document could collect without authorization from the other, the borrower was not negligent and had no duty to remember which share belonged to which. See below, siman 77, seif 9, regarding these laws. Some say that with an oral loan, if neither lender actually demanded the additional amount, the borrower is obligated only if he wishes to fulfill his obligation toward Heaven.
