To claim from him. Whenever the authorities distinguish between "he claims from him" and "he does not claim from him," the distinction means a definite claim versus an uncertain claim, not whether the plaintiff spoke first or only afterward. The Gemara, Baal HaTerumot, and other authorities make this clear. Shach.
To give. The Sema notes that in siman 300 the Tur and Shulchan Aruch say that where two people deposited items simultaneously, even without a document and even if both make definite claims, the custodian is obligated only to satisfy Heaven, while without definite claims he is exempt even from that. He distinguishes a loan because loan money is given for expenditure, so the borrower should have kept careful track, especially since the lenders identified their respective amounts. A deposit, by contrast, is not for use, and even if they identified their portions the custodian could reasonably assume that each would later take what he deposited; the bundles may have become mixed without negligence. The Shach says the Tur must be understood this way, but questions the source of the distinction.
That he swear. He must swear while holding a sacred object. We do not treat him as a partial admitter who owes each claimant one hundred and is uncertain about another hundred, which would ordinarily trigger payment where he cannot swear. Because he knows with certainty that he does not owe both claimants more than the total sum, he is regarded as making a definite claim in that respect. Baal HaTerumot in the name of Rambam. Shach.
And they informed him. Meaning even though they informed him. Shach.
And to this one. See siman 222, seif 2. Where the defendant swore falsely, the Sages penalize him and require payment to each claimant. The Shach says the same should apply here to a loan: whenever he committed a transgression, the penalty applies.
Deposited. Because each claimant asserts a definite claim, one of them is certainly lying. Therefore the disputed amount is not divided unless both agree. If each says, "I know one hundred is mine and do not know about the other hundred," he gives one hundred to each and thereby fulfills even his duty toward Heaven. See siman 300. The third hundred remains held because each claims part of it and there may be a deceiver, as in siman 365. If neither makes a definite claim, however, the third hundred is divided because no deceiver is established. In siman 222 the Shach cited many authorities that "held" means held by the defendant himself, although he must take a shevuat heset that he does not know, even if he would prefer to deposit the third hundred with beit din. Whether he remains liable toward Heaven depends on the dispute at the end of siman 300. According to the Shach's conclusion there and in siman 222, most authorities exempt him even toward Heaven because he was not negligent. The same applies here.
Authorization. The Tur's phrase "without authorization" is not necessarily literal. Even if the document is drafted so that neither creditor can collect without authorization from the other, the law may be the same. As proof, with a joint deposit all agree the custodian is exempt, yet siman 77 says one of two depositors cannot collect the whole without authorization. The reason is that once they acted together and relied partially upon one another, the custodian was not negligent. The same applies here, as the Ramban in Baal HaTerumot indicates. Perhaps this is why the Mechaber simply states that in a documented loan he is exempt and omits the Tur's phrase. The Rama's insertion of the Tur's language may therefore be unnecessary. Shach.
Who says. The Sema explains that the Mechaber uses the phrase "there is one who says" although no explicit dissenter is found because the rule was not expressly stated by other authorities. The Bach and Taz object and identify Baal HaTerumot in the name of Ramban as an opposing view, but the Shach says that is incorrect because Baal HaTerumot explicitly states that in this case there remains an obligation toward Heaven.
Heaven. See the Shach's comments above to siman 75, seif katan 19.
