And he informed him, etc. If he did not inform him which money belonged to whom, the deposits are treated as one combined bundle in the custodian's hands, comparable to the rule for documents.
And when they come to claim from him. The essential distinction in this siman is whether the claimant asserts certainty or uncertainty. A definite assertion is called "claiming from him," whereas an uncertain assertion is treated as not making a definite claim. Shach, and this is also implied by the Gemara.
He must give, etc. See Tumim, where I distinguished between a deposit and a loan according to the Rosh and explained how the Tur derived this from the Rosh.
Two hundred. Even if he says "here you are" concerning the one hundred that he admits, so that there is no Torah oath on the remaining hundred. Had only one claimant sued him for two hundred and he responded, "One hundred, here you are; as to the other hundred I do not know," he would be exempt. Here, however, he admits that he owes money to both claimants and that one of them is entitled to two hundred, so his position is worse and he must pay each one. See Tumim for the reason.
After he swears, etc. Even where he did not tender the admitted hundred immediately, so that in an ordinary case his response would be "fifty I know and fifty I do not know" and one who cannot swear would pay without the claimant's oath, here the second claimant must still swear. The custodian knows with certainty that he does not owe both claimants together more than the total amount of money deposited, and he is prepared to pay that total. Nothing remains in his hands that he knows belongs to them beyond that amount. Therefore we do not apply the rule that one who cannot swear must pay. Ramban, Nimukei Yosef, and Maggid Mishneh.
And he informed him. Even if each lender informed him of his amount, so that one might think he should have remembered who lent one hundred and who lent two hundred, the ruling teaches that since both loans were recorded in a single document, he did not necessarily focus on retaining that distinction. Sema and Shach.
He gives this one a maneh, etc. But if a person swears falsely and thereby causes this uncertainty, he may be obligated to pay each claimant fully. See below, siman 222.
And the remainder shall remain deposited. This is because one claimant is certainly making a false claim. If no claimant is lying, the disputed amount is divided. Shach. See what I wrote below in siman 138.
It shall remain deposited. See below, simanim 222 and 300, whether he may retain it himself or must deposit it with beit din.
And that one can, etc. This reason is stated only for additional clarity. Even without it, once both obligations were written in one document and the document is held by one claimant, the debtor has effectively trusted the holder regarding distribution. This is implied by Ramban in Baal HaTerumot, gate 39, part 2. The Mechaber therefore omitted the proposed reason, because the ruling does not depend upon it. Shach.
He is obligated if he wishes to fulfill his duty toward Heaven. Seizure by a claimant does not help. Shach. See what I wrote above in siman 28.
To fulfill his duty toward Heaven. Under enforceable human law he is exempt. He gives each claimant one maneh, and they divide the third maneh. Shach.
