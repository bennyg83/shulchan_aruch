Exempt. This differs from seif 1. There, because he borrowed from two people, he should have recorded the separate debts so as not to confuse them, and he was therefore somewhat negligent. Here it did not occur to him that he would forget which person lent the money and mistakenly think it was the other. Maadanei Melech, Gidulei Terumah, and the Sema discuss the distinction. The Sema proposes that in the earlier case each lender can say, "We heard from you that you knew what each of us lent and relied on you to return each one's money," whereas where only one of them lent and the borrower truly does not know which, such a claim is unavailable. The Shach finds this strained, especially because the issue turns on definite versus uncertain claims as explained above, and notes that in siman 300 the Sema himself uses the former rationale.
And some say. In siman 300, seif 3, the Rama states the first view without qualification. The Shach finds it difficult that he did not do so here, because there is no apparent basis to distinguish a loan from a deposit. Baal HaTerumot expressly equates them, and the Tur here says that the Rosh's words indicate exemption even from the obligation toward Heaven, although the Rosh's original discussion concerned a deposit.
