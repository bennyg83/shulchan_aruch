Even from the duty toward Heaven. This differs from seif 1. There, because he borrowed from two people, he should have recorded the matter so that he would not confuse them, and he was therefore somewhat negligent. Here, he did not anticipate forgetting who had lent him and confusing that lender with another. Ma’adanei Melech in HaMafkid and Gedulei Terumah explain this way. The Sema offers another distinction based on what each lender could infer from the borrower’s statements, but that explanation is strained. The distinction between definite and uncertain claims, explained above in seif katan 2, does not support it. Indeed, the Sema himself adopts the explanation stated here below in siman 300, seif katan 12.

And some say that to satisfy Heaven, etc. Below in siman 300, seif 3, the Rama rules without qualification like the first view. It is difficult why he did not add here that the first view is primary, since there is plainly no distinction between a loan and a deposit in this respect. Baal HaTerumot, at the end of gate 39, explicitly equates them. The Tur also states that the Rosh appears to exempt here even from the duty toward Heaven, although the Rosh discussed only a deposit.
