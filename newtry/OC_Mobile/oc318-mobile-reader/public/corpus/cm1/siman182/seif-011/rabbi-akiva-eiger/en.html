[Shach, letter 6] This is what appears correct in my humble opinion. Note: The Taz wrote likewise. According to this, the Shulchan Aruch's phrase "since he purchased it with his money," which is the language of the Rambam, is readily understood. It concerns a case where the agent did not notify the seller that he was acting for another, and the agent still has the principal's money. The law is that the principal can compel the agent to correct the distortion by selling the property to him with full warranty. The reason given is that the agent purchased it with the principal's money. Had the agent purchased it with his own money, he could merely return the principal's money to him. The Bach, however, wrote that even where the agent did not notify the seller, the purchase is valid only between the agent and seller, not between the principal and agent, and the agent can return the principal's money. He therefore had to explain the phrase "since he purchased it with his money" in a strained manner. See there.
