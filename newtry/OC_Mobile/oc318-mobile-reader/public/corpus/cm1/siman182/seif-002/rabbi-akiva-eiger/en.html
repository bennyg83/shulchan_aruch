[Rama] And specifically where the agent. Note: See Responsa Maharit, part 1, siman 116, which is uncertain whether the principle that there is no agency for a transgression applies to a rabbinic prohibition. See Mishneh LaMelech, chapter 2 of Hilchot Rotzeach, halachah 2. If the agent acted inadvertently, see Shach below, siman 348, seif katan 6. It also requires consideration whether agency is effective where the agent is an apostate who sins defiantly. According to the rationale that "the words of the Master and the words of the student, whose words should be obeyed?" that rationale applies here as well. According to the rationale that the agent had the option whether to act, it is possible that the principal is liable, because why should the agent not perform the act, along the lines of Tosafot's reasoning regarding an inadvertent agent. See Mishneh LaMelech, chapter 4, halachah 5 of Hilchot Malveh, regarding a non-Jewish agent; Shitah Mekubetzet in the name of Mahari Abuhav; Sema, seif katan 2, who makes the rationale depend on the agent being able to say, "I thought..."; and below, siman 388, Shach, seif katan 66.
