Shach, beginning of seif katan 1, "specifically." Note: see the responsum of Maharit cited by the Mishneh LaMelech, Hilchot Rotzeach 2, where the matter discussed by Noda BiYehudah, first edition, Even HaEzer, siman 75, is explained. As a matter of law, it appears to me that if the principal specifically instructed the agent to divorce in a prohibited manner, such as only against the wife's will, she is not divorced because there is no agency for a transgression. If he specifically instructed him to divorce only with her consent and the agent divorced her against her will, he deviated from the principal's instructions and she is not divorced. But in the case of the Noda BiYehudah, where he appointed him simply to effect a divorce and was not particular, if the agent can appease her, well and good, and if not, he may divorce her against her will. The agent was therefore not appointed as an agent for a transgression, but only as an agent to divorce. As for the prohibited aspect, the principal gave him permission but did not appoint him as his agent for that act. This resembles the law of substitution, from which we derive that one who says, "Whoever wishes to substitute may substitute," is effective. See what the Noda BiYehudah himself wrote there at the end of siman 82, letter 20. This differs from Ketzot HaChoshen in this siman, and resolves most of the objections of the gaon Rabbi Yitzchak in Noda BiYehudah, siman 76. Concerning agency for a transgression generally, see Shach below, end of simanim 348 and 388, and above, end of siman 32.
