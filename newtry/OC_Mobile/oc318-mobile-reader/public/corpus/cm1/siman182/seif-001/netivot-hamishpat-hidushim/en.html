Where the agent is subject to the prohibition. The Shach disagrees and concludes that the halachah follows Rav Sama: the sender is liable only where the agent resembles a courtyard that passively receives against its will, such as where the agent did not know that the object was stolen. Otherwise the sender is exempt even if the agent himself is not legally subject to the prohibition. He further cites Nimukei Yosef that where one acts through a minor who lacks an independent legal hand, the sender is liable because the minor resembles a courtyard that passively receives. The Shach challenges this from the Mishnah that one who sends fire through a deaf mute, mentally incompetent person, or minor is exempt. See Beurim: this is a dispute among the early authorities. Ritva holds that a minor cannot serve as an agent to effect a legal transaction, but where the act is already accomplished and agency matters only to impose punishment or liability upon the sender, for example where one tells a minor to cut the sidelocks of an adult, the sender can be liable even through the minor. Where legal agency is required to complete the underlying transaction, however, an adult agent is required. Even according to the opinion that ordinarily an act performed by an agent in violation of instruction remains legally effective while the sender escapes liability, theft is different. The acquisition of theft exists principally to impose accident liability, which is itself part of the penal consequence. Therefore there is no agency for the sender where the agent remains free to choose whether to perform the wrongdoing. Likewise, one who sends fire through a deaf mute, mentally incompetent person, or minor is liable according to Ritva if he explicitly commands them to cause damage. Tosafot in Bava Metzia 10 hold that a minor can never serve as an agent.
And all his acts are valid. If the intermediary is a deaf mute, mentally incompetent person, minor, or gentile, who cannot act under ordinary halachic agency, the agency is void and the principal does not acquire even if the intermediary later hands the item to him and the principal himself performs meshichah, because that meshichah was not performed before the seller. See Beurim: where a seller sells an object to an intermediary, even a gentile, and writes or says to the purchaser through him, "Go, pull it and acquire," the purchaser acquires when he performs meshichah. Likewise, if the purchaser sends purchase money through a gentile or such a legally disqualified messenger, the purchaser acquires when the seller receives the money, because the acquisition is completed by the seller's receipt rather than by treating the messenger as a formal agent.
