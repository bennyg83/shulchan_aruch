His law is like that of any ordinary person, etc. The Tur and Mechaber wrote similarly above at the end of siman 109 regarding beit din that sold property through an agent. But if beit din itself sold the borrower's assets to pay a creditor, the ruling there is that even the slightest pricing error can void the sale, because the buyer too relied upon beit din and did not investigate the value independently. That is not so when the buyer or seller deals with an agent of beit din. This is unlike the Ir Shushan, who writes that a purchaser never relies upon the seller any more than he relies upon beit din when beit din itself is the seller. The Ir Shushan follows his own ruling in siman 109 according to the Rambam, but Rabbeinu Yonah and the Rosh disagree, as the Tur records and Rama cites there. Therefore he should not have made the present rule depend upon the law of a direct purchase from beit din. Most authorities do not rule that way regarding a direct beit din sale, while most do agree that a purchaser dealing with the agent cannot void the transaction on this ground.
