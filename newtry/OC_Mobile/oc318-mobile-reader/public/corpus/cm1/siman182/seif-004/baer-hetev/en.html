Although the principal makes a definite claim and the seller, who is in possession, is uncertain, we nevertheless apply the rule that the burden of proof rests upon the claimant, as the Tur and Shulchan Aruch write in simanim 223 and 224. There is also a reason for this. Presumably the agent stipulated with the principal that he should remain his agent even if he deviated, so that the agent would not become liable to correct his agency, since he receives no compensation for serving as agent. This differs from siman 185. Sema. The Shach writes that, in his view, the Ramah speaks only where the deviation is not sufficient to void the sale. In such a case we presume that the principal authorized him as agent whether he acted correctly or deviated, because he ordinarily appointed him to act as though he himself were acting. But the Ramah does not refer to a case where an item worth one hundred was sold for one dinar, for a person is not presumed foolish enough to stipulate that even a gross deviation should remain valid. See further siman 185, seif 5.
