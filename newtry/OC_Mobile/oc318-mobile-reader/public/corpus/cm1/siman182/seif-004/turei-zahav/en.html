Seif 9: This is deemed a deviation. The Gemara leaves unresolved whether an agent instructed to sell a certain quantity who sells it in parts has deviated because the owner did not want numerous documents and wished one sale, or whether the division benefits the owner because he may not need to sell the remainder. The Rif rules that the agent deviated, while Rav Hai rules that he improved the transaction. Rav Hai relies on the Gemara's later conclusion that when the owner said “to one,” without explicitly excluding two, the agent may sell even to one hundred buyers. Rashi's text supports him. The Rif had a different text: if the owner said “to one,” that is restrictive; if he spoke generally, the agent may sell even to one hundred. Tosafot reconcile the Rif in two ways. First, the earlier deviation concerned the owner's burden of preparing two deeds, whereas later the agent brings prepared deeds. Even then, saying “to one and not two” is restrictive, and according to the Rif even “to one” may be restrictive because the owner may not wish to litigate with multiple purchasers. Second, the later case involves multiple purchasers in a single deed. Rabbeinu appears to accept the first textual tradition, that only “to one and not two” is restrictive, while “to one” alone is not, provided the sale is contained in one deed. His statement that this is according to the Rif refers only to the basic rule that the unresolved question is decided against the agent, unlike Rav Hai, not to every textual detail. The Rambam's language suggests another distinction. When the owner says, “Sell me two seah,” and the agent sells one seah first, a later sale of the second seah requires a separate transaction and creates publicity and burden. But when the owner says, “Sell me a field,” the mandate naturally refers to one transaction. If the agent sells portions to many purchasers simultaneously, that is still one sale and may be valid even to one hundred, because “a field” means as much of it as he can sell in that transaction. Nevertheless, if the owner expressly said “to one,” that wording is restrictive even within a single transaction. The practical halachah follows the Rif's text and the Mechaber: an express instruction to sell to one is restrictive, in accordance with Tosafot's second resolution and Rabbeinu's understanding of the Rif.
