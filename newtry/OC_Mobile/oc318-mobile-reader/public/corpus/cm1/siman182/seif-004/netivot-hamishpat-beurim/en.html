The seller can. See Shach, seif katan 4, who writes that ordinarily he appointed him, because a person is not presumed to act foolishly. At first glance this is difficult. Whichever way one understands the case, the ruling is problematic. If it concerns items to which the laws of ona'ah do not apply, then even a substantial error is effective, for the Shach himself writes that the principal effectively authorized him to act as the principal himself would. If it concerns items to which ona'ah does apply, then even if the principal authorized him "whether for benefit or for detriment," and even if he expressly made him an agent, a major distortion should not be effective. The agent is no better than one who says, "on condition that there is no claim of ona'ah," which is ineffective, as the Shach himself writes in the preceding seif katan. This requires study.
