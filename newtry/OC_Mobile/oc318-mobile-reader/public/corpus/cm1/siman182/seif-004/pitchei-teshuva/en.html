And all of his acts are effective. See Responsa Mayim Chayim, Choshen Mishpat, siman 18, concerning the following case. Reuven gave an item to Shimon to sell for twenty seven gold coins. Shimon went to the market and sold it to Levi for exactly that amount, informed Levi that the item belonged to Reuven, received the money from Levi, and delivered the item to him. It was afterward established by witnesses that before the sale between Shimon and Levi had been completed, Reuven had already sold that same item, which was still in Shimon's possession, to Yehudah, by kinyan sudar or by situmta. Reuven's sale is certainly equivalent to revoking Shimon's agency, but the scholars were uncertain whether such a revocation is effective, so that Shimon's sale to Levi is void and Levi must return the item to Yehudah, or whether such a revocation is ineffective and everything Shimon did remains valid, leaving Reuven's sale ineffective. He replied that in his view, if Levi did not know of the revocation and withdrawal of authority, Levi need not return the item to Yehudah. Although Reuven can revoke Shimon's agency, if he did not notify either Levi or Shimon of the revocation, the revocation is ineffective. This resembles the rule in Shulchan Aruch, siman 122, seif 3. According to the Shach, seif katan 11, it is clear that in our case even if Shimon the agent knew of the revocation, so long as Levi did not know, the revocation is ineffective. See there:
