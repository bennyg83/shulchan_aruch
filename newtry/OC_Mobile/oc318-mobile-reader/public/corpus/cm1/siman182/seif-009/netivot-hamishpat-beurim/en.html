This is considered deviating from instructions. See Sema, seif katan 77, who explains the reason as proliferation of documents. This follows Rosh, who says the phrase "I did what was better for you" is not literal, and that the main concern is additional documents. According to this, in movable property, where that concern does not exist, if one told an agent to sell a kor of wheat and the agent sold half a kor, the sale should stand. The implication would be so even if the principal said "a kor for thirty." This is difficult. Siman 200, seif 7, states that where a seller himself says "a kor for thirty," he may retract even in the last se'ah because the wording reveals that he needed the entire price and intended no sale to be completed until the whole kor was sold. If the principal could undo his own partial sale on that ground, he should likewise be able to undo a partial sale made by his agent. Agency should not make the principal worse. It therefore appears that agency is indeed different, because the rationale "I did what was better for you" can apply. Ran writes that the agent's partial sale is effective only where he can also sell the remaining half. This resolves the issue. When the owner sells personally, if each se'ah were immediately acquired he risks that the buyer may stop halfway, leaving the owner without the full price he needs. He therefore withholds intent to complete each partial sale until the whole kor is sold. When an agent sells, however, the principal can prefer that each partial sale become effective immediately, because the agent continues seeking a buyer for the rest. If the remaining portion cannot be sold, the first sale can also fail under Ran's rule. Thus the principal gains flexibility with no corresponding risk. The agent can therefore say, "I did what was better for you." This also resolves Rosh's question from the cloak case. There too the buyer must be able to complete the principal's desired transaction. If completion is impossible, the deviation defeats the agency. Therefore, even in movable property or in land transferred by chazakah where no additional document is needed, the partial sale is effective only in the circumstances Ran describes. I will explain below that the primary rule is that the transaction can fail even where the proliferation-of-documents concern is absent.
