The agent takes it for himself. Meaning, it remains in his possession as though he purchased it without warranty, and no new kinyan is required. The Tur concludes that Raavad writes this rule for every agent who distorted the principal's instructions: if the principal wishes to uphold the transaction, it remains effective and the agent must correct the damage he caused. When Chazal say that if an agent departs from the principal's instructions the transaction is void, that applies when the principal does not seek to uphold it. If the principal wants the transaction, the agent must cure the distortion. Rambam and Rosh distinguish. If the agent informed the seller that he was acting as an agent, and the principal can prove that he did not authorize him to act whether for proper execution or distortion, then the sale is void. The principal has no claim against the agent; rather, the agent recovers the money from the seller. If the agent did not tell the seller he was acting for another but bought without specification, they hold that the seller cannot be forced to unwind the sale. The Gemara's case can be one where the seller was not informed, so the seller can say, 'I have no dealings with the principal,' and the agent must therefore correct the distortion. Alternatively, the seller was told that he was dealing with an agent, but the principal cannot prove that he did not authorize the agent even in the event of distortion. The seller can then say that the agency included such authority, so the sale stands. The agent himself, however, admits he was not authorized to distort and must compensate the principal. This is Rambam and Rosh's position, as the Tur cites them, and corresponds to the distinctions the Mechaber states above in seifim 2 and 4. The Mechaber abbreviated here where he should have explained them.
Since he purchased it with his money. Rambam writes this. In the Derishah I explained that it excludes a case where the agent purchased with his own funds. Then he need not correct the principal's loss, for this is like one sent to purchase a specific commodity who instead bought it for himself with his own money: the transaction stands, though he is called deceitful. Here too, he would not have to cure the principal's distortion.
