General rule. There are three principal approaches. Where the agent disclosed the agency, the sale is entirely void. The agent may therefore satisfy the principal by returning the money and cannot compel the principal to accept the land with the agent's warranty. Conversely, the principal cannot compel the agent to accept warranty unless neither the agent nor the seller has money to return. Where the agent did not disclose the agency, the sale remains valid. The Ran may hold that the principal chooses either repayment or the land with the agent's warranty, and the Rambam appears to agree. According to the Raavad, even without disclosure the principal may rescind if he wishes. According to the Rashba, disclosure makes no difference: the sale between agent and seller always remains valid, and the agent's obligation to the principal is either to transfer the property with warranty or return the money. The Rama's presentation is difficult. He first cites the Ran concerning an ordinary undisclosed purchase, then cites as a dissent the Rosh's view that compulsion applies only where the agent lacks money, although the Rosh addresses disclosed agency. There may be no disagreement at all: the Ran could agree with the Rosh where disclosure occurred, and the Rosh could agree with the Ran where it did not. In practice, the Rosh's approach appears correct, especially since the Rambam agrees and the Ran likely does as well.
