The transaction is void. This applies specifically where the other party was informed that he was dealing with an agent. Ran proves that the relevant case in Kiddushin concerns an error by a disclosed agent. But if the agent did not reveal his agency, the transaction stands so long as the error does not reach the amount of ona'ah, or once the period in which one normally shows the merchandise to an expert has passed. That period is measured from the agent. When the agent did not reveal that he was acting for another, his legal position is like one who purchased for himself. The agent cannot say, "I immediately delivered the object to the principal and was therefore unable to show it to an expert." That is no excuse. It is like a person who buys for himself and immediately gives the item to another, and then allows the normal inspection period to pass. The buyer is deemed to have waived the claim because he should have inspected before transferring it. Likewise, between the agent and principal, once the agent delivers the object and the principal keeps it beyond the inspection period, the principal is deemed to have waived any claim against the agent arising from that defect.
