This was explained in the Tur, Yoreh Deah, etc. There the Mechaber writes that the produce consumed is recoverable through beit din, while the Tur, both here and there, writes that it is rabbinic interest and cannot be recovered by judicial process. The Beit Yosef explains that when Ravina ordered the produce returned, according to Tosafot and the Rosh, he did not do so because it was fixed Torah interest but because the waiver was made in error. He therefore concludes that the Tur's statement that the produce is not judicially recoverable means only that it is not recoverable as interest, while it can be recovered because of mistaken waiver. In the Derishah I explained that this reading is strained. How could the Tur state in both places without qualification that the produce is not recoverable if, in fact, it is recoverable on another legal basis? Although the Rosh says regarding Ravina that the produce was recovered because of mistaken waiver, he says so only when explaining Tosafot's approach. The Rosh himself appears to follow Rav Ashi's different approach. There, in the chapter Eizehu Neshekh, the Rosh cites Mar son of Rav Yosef, who said that in a mortgage arrangement in a place where redemption is permitted, if the mortgagee consumed produce beyond the amount of the debt, we do not remove it from him, and Rav Ashi followed that rule even against minor orphans. The Rosh's discussion indicates that Rav Ashi disagrees with Ravina. In my view, the Rosh holds that in the case under discussion there is no mistaken waiver at all, because the transaction began as a sale, not as a loan. Mistaken waiver applies where the underlying arrangement began as a loan. Proof appears from the Gemara's discussion of one who lent against a field, where Rav Nachman ruled that both the land and the produce are returned. The Gemara challenges him from the case of one who sells the future fruits of a palm tree, where if the buyer already seized and consumed the fruit we do not take it back, and answers: There it is a sale, here a loan. This supports the distinction stated.
