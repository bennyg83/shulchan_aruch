In this way they did not lose their right. Rivash's language is precise: they did not lose the right they had in the land because of a debt or lien. A beit din or guardian who earlier sold orphan land may later foreclose that same land for a debt, because at the time of sale they may have expected to collect the debt elsewhere. But if they later challenge the buyer by claiming that the land had actually been stolen and never belonged to the estate, Rivash says the judge or beit din can lose that right after having themselves transferred the field. See Tumim. Sema reconciles Rama's two glosses this way. I offered another explanation of Rama, because Darkei Moshe does not actually cite Rivash at this point and the marginal attribution appears mistaken. There is therefore no scribal error in Rama's text.
