Likewise, if Levi testified in a document in which, etc. The Maggid Mishneh writes that the Rambam intended here the law stated in the Yerushalmi. Another person sold his own field, and in describing its eastern boundary wrote that the field now disputed by Levi belonged to the present possessor. Levi signed that deed as a witness. From this law it follows all the more so that if Levi himself sold his own field and described the disputed field as the possessor's eastern boundary, or if the possessor sold his field to another and described the disputed field as his own eastern boundary and Levi signed as witness, Levi loses his right to challenge. The Maggid Mishneh states all this. The Mechaber therefore abbreviated by copying the Rambam's wording, and Rama likewise did not need to add the other cases. Similarly, if the possessor had designated the field as a boundary in the name of the challenger, the challenger retains his right. All of this follows by stronger inference from the rule stated here.
And he did the field after he did not lose his right, but he did not lose his right, he said, “I am not afraid of Hashem’s wrath, and I am not afraid to give him an ad from my father’s father, and I will not give him my father’s hand, and I will give him a call to him.”
They did not lose their right. The Rivash writes there that they did not lose whatever right they had in the land by reason of a debt and lien. He repeats this at the end of the responsum. It appears to me that he is precise in speaking specifically of a right arising from debt and lien. Since the body of the land did not belong to beit din or to the guardian, they acted properly in selling it while assuming that their debt could be collected elsewhere. But if beit din later sought to challenge and seize the field on the ground that it had been stolen from them, the law differs. Rivash writes in siman 292 that a judge loses such a proprietary claim once he has already caused the field to be transferred to another person. Rama cites that ruling in the following gloss, seif 4. This distinction reconciles the two rulings of the Rivash. The Mechaber similarly wrote above in siman 111:6 that they may afterward collect "their debt," which is precisely the point. He repeats this in siman 226 in the laws of warranty. Although siman 111:17 also says that an ordinary person who signed as a witness does not lose his lien, beit din and a guardian are mentioned to teach that even though they actually sold the very field subject to their lien, this does not demonstrate a waiver of that lien. Rama's phrase here, "a guardian or beit din who sold orphans' property in such a case," should not be understood as referring to their having signed a boundary designation, because the Rivash did not discuss that. Rather, "in such a case" refers to the fact that despite having taken affirmative action and sold it, they did not lose a debt or lien. The Ir Shushan's interpretation is therefore incorrect.
