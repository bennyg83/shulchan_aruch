There. The Maggid Mishneh cites the Mishnah: if he made it a boundary marker for another, he lost his right, as Rashi explains. The Yerushalmi adds that even if the deed was to another person and the challenger signed as a witness, he loses his right. The Halakhot record this. Rambam cites the Yerushalmi's case, and all the more so the Mishnah's case. Beit Yosef writes in seif 2 that the Gemara there on 30b says this applies only when the marker was for another person, but if it was for himself he does not lose his right, because he can say: had I not acted this way, he would not have sold it to me. One might ask why he did not issue a moda'ah, but the answer is that publicity spreads through acquaintances. Rif and Rosh write likewise. Sema in the name of Darkei Moshe.
