Later. In the Derishah I proved that this applies even if he did not make both claims immediately in one statement. Even after he first claimed, "I admitted only one furrow to him," he may later add, "I subsequently repurchased even that furrow from him." Sema. The Shach writes that the same applies to heirs: we advance on their behalf the claim that perhaps their father later repurchased it. But if it is known that he did not repurchase it, we do not claim for the heirs that the document was forged, because such a circumstance is uncommon. Tosafot in the second chapter of Ketubot state this. According to what the Shach established in simanim 68 and 297, however, we advance all plausible claims for heirs, and the same should apply here. The Rosh also appears to limit the contrary rule specifically to the view of Ri, which we do not follow. See also siman 100 and Responsa Mahari Beit Levi 2, pages 9 and 12. Shach.

Sema. If the possessor signed the deed by which the property was sold to the challenger, he does not thereby lose his right. Maharashdam, Choshen Mishpat 223. If the challenger claims that he signed by mistake, and circumstances support the possibility of error, there are grounds on both sides. Rashach I, 126. See also siman 109 there, Responsa Rosh 70:4, and Responsa Maharit I, 93. Knesset HaGedolah.
