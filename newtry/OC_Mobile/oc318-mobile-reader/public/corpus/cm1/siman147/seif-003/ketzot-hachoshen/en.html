Rama rules that if a document of admission in the challenger's handwriting is not independently validated, the possessor can say, "I later repurchased the land," through a migo that the writing is forged. This applies even when he is not presently holding the handwriting as a separate item. So long as the land itself remains in the possessor's hands and the handwriting is not independently established, the forgery plea supplies the migo. Once the handwriting is independently verified, the migo disappears and the possessor cannot use a later purchase plea merely against the written admission. The rule follows the ordinary distinction between a document whose authenticity rests only on the party's admission and one whose authenticity is independently established.
