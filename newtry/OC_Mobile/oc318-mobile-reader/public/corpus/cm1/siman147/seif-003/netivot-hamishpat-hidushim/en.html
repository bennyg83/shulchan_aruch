The minimum measure of a field. As explained below in siman 171:3. Sema.
I later repurchased it. Even if he did not make this claim together with the original claim concerning one furrow, but first claimed only one furrow and afterward added, "I later repurchased that furrow as well," the explanation is accepted. Sema.
If his handwriting is not verified. He is believed through the migo that he could have claimed forgery. Nevertheless, if the admission concerns a furrow smaller than nine kav, he is not believed because that migo stands against the principle that a "field" is not ordinarily less than nine kav. Sema. For orphans, we advance the claim that the property was later repurchased through the migo of forgery, and likewise may advance a forgery claim itself. Shach. See there: the Rosh writes that here we certainly do not advance a forgery claim where both seller and purchaser contradict it.
The furrow is in his possession and he established chazakah over it. See Beurim: the case is where the challenger first recovered it from the possessor before making the boundary description, and the possessor knew that the challenger had so described it. Then the challenger's later chazakah claim can be effective. But if the description was made before he recovered the furrow by beit din, and neither beit din nor the possessor knew of that deed, the challenger cannot later say, "I repurchased it." The reason is that the land came into his hand through error, and the possessor can also say that because he did not know of the deed until now, he had no reason to protest.
