One who sells a garden, etc., even in a place where the custom is not to fence gardens. The Maggid Mishneh writes that perhaps the Rambam's view is that anyone who sells property to another intends that the purchaser remove every harm that comes to the seller by reason of the sale. This resembles the rule in chapter 1 of Hilchot Mekhirah, stated below in siman 218:20, that one who sells half a field must leave space for the fence, ditch, and outer ditch. The Rambam may have derived this law from there. According to that explanation, even if the garden purchased is surrounded by other gardens still belonging to the seller, the seller himself may compel the purchaser to fence so that no harm results from him, or at least so that the purchaser not be regarded as a thief because of produce crossing the boundary, just as in the cited case.
