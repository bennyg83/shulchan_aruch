There is an authority who says, etc. Rashi writes this explicitly regarding Rava's challenge to Rabbi Elazar: neither this one nor that one should make such a mark. See there. It is difficult to understand how the second approach, that of the Ri, explains this passage. According to it, what is Rava's challenge that neither should make a mark? If neither makes one and the wall falls into one party's domain, that party can claim that it is his. The Ateret Zekenim writes that according to the Ri one must say that even regarding a courtyard wall, if it falls into one party's domain it belongs to him and he may assert ownership. If so, the first clause of the Mishna regarding a courtyard should also have required a distinguishing mark for this reason. The Tur explicitly writes this in siman 157 in the name of the Ri. All of this is contrary to the Beit Yosef. See further in Ateret Zekenim, page 88, column 3.
