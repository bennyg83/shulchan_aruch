One who had. The commentators ask how one neighbor can obligate another without his consent. They explain that Rav Huna treats the benefitted neighbor like one who enters another's field without permission where the field is normally planted, who must pay for the benefit. Bava Metzia 101a. Thus if the surrounded owner later stands and builds the fourth side, everyone agrees that he pays proportionately because he revealed that the enclosure benefits him. The incident of Runya likewise shows that compulsion arises only after such demonstrated benefit. The Mishnah can be understood as dealing with ruined structures, which the Yerushalmi says are normally rebuilt and therefore resemble a field normally planted.
His fellow then stood. Whether the enclosing owner or the enclosed owner, following Rashi and as explained below.

He fenced two sides. The Gemara's dispute between enclosing and enclosed property still imposes payment specifically when the fourth side completes the benefit, as Rabbi Yosi says; Bava Kama 20b likewise emphasizes the fourth side.
Provided that... but... for. The Yerushalmi indicates that one charges only the basic cost of the wall, not the full value of every possible use, unless the circumstances show that the neighbor can actually use it. A wall built along the neighbor's wall may not be usable by him for beams unless permission was given, in which case that permission itself can establish a use right.
All this. This follows Rashi and Rambam.
But if he fenced. This follows Tosafos in Bava Kama 20b.
Collected note: All this concerns... The Tur derives from Ramban that where the outer owner fenced from the outside, the case must be one where fencing was not customary. Otherwise the inner owner would have to build his own boundary anyway and would receive no legally chargeable benefit from the outer wall. In a place with no fencing custom he cannot postpone payment by saying he intends to fence separately later, because such a statement is merely evasive. If he himself later fences between them, his action reveals benefit.
If it is. Otherwise he cannot be charged, since the wall provided him no useful protection and the outer owner could still compel a separate internal fence.
