According to the extent of the loss he caused him. See Baer Heitev, and see Noda BiYehudah, Second Edition, siman 24. A question was posed concerning Reuven, who authored a commentary on Seder Nezikin and Kodashim and contracted with Shimon the printer for a fixed price per sheet to print those two sedarim with Rashi, Tosafot, and Reuven's new commentary beneath. Ordinarily, after each sheet is printed the typesetters dismantle the type and reset it for the next sheet. Shimon, however, owned many letters and left the type standing, removed only Reuven's new commentary, and used the standing type to print copies for himself of the two sedarim with Rashi and Tosafot. Reuven argued that since the cost of setting the type had effectively been paid for through the printing arrangement, Shimon should not enjoy that typesetting without payment, and should reimburse Reuven for his share, as one who benefits from another's labor, comparing it to the laws of a water channel serving fields and one who encloses his fellow on three sides. Shimon answered that the letters were his own and he could use them as he wished. The Noda BiYehudah first explains that if Shimon undertook the printing as a contractor for a fixed amount per sheet, with Shimon himself responsible for the typesetters, printers, paper, and all other expenses, Reuven has no claim. Shimon may say that he agreed to that price with the expectation that he too might benefit from the standing type, and it is Shimon, not Reuven, who paid the typesetters. If, however, the original calculation expressly allocated a stated amount for typesetting, another amount for presswork and paper, and the balance for the use of the type and ink, so that Reuven was in substance paying the typesetters, then the parties' arguments arise in full. Reuven's analogy to the water channel is not exact, because there the law concerns parties who all presently require irrigation, not one who has already dug for himself. Nor is the case exactly like one who enclosed his fellow on three sides, because the Gemara, Bava Kamma 20b, grounds that liability in the additional expense caused by the inner owner. Here, the expense of typesetting did not increase at all because Shimon later printed additional copies. Even though Shimon's willingness to incur further printing expenses might show that he values the typesetting, that alone does not necessarily shift to him the original setting costs. According to the Shach in siman 158, seif katan 4, following the Bach, even a manifestation of benefit obligates only for the additional expense actually caused. According to the Sema, seif katan 19, the inner owner may sometimes pay his proportional share, but there too he caused at least some additional expense; where he caused none, his later conduct does not by itself create liability for the whole cost. After further consideration, however, the Noda BiYehudah concludes that Shimon must pay his proportionate share. One who resides in another's courtyard, even where the courtyard was not ordinarily rented, must according to the cited opinion in Shulchan Aruch 363:7 pay the full value of his benefit if he caused even a slight loss. Here Shimon causes Reuven a substantial loss, because had Shimon not printed competing copies, buyers would have been more readily available for Reuven's edition. This is certainly so according to the majority view cited there. Even according to the Ramah discussed by the Nimukei Yosef, who limits payment to the owner's actual loss, the loss here is substantial and may exceed the typesetting expense. Shimon therefore must pay for the benefit he received from the standing type, proportionate to the number of copies he printed. See there.
