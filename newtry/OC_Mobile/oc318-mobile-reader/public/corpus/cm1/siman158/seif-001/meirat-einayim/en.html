When they come to divide, they compel one another, etc. The Mechaber states this generally and relies on siman 157, where he explained that if a shared courtyard is large enough for compelled division, either may compel the other to divide. If not, both must consent to division, but once they made a kinyan they may compel one another to build the partition. The same applies here to a garden. The minimum garden division is an area in which each can sow half a kav, as the Tur writes below in siman 218 and the Mechaber in siman 171:3.
Even where there is no known custom between them. But if the established custom is not to build partitions between gardens, we follow the custom. The Tur says this explicitly. This differs from two partners in a courtyard, as the Tur writes in the Rosh's name at the beginning of siman 157, and Rama rules there.
But in an ordinary field, etc. Ramban explains that a garden ordinarily contains crops all year and therefore suffers greater harm from visual exposure, unlike a field. He gives additional distinctions, which I cite in the Perishah.
