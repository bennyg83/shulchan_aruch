But in a valley. If a wall had stood and fell, whether one can compel rebuilding appears to depend on the dispute between Rashi and Tosafot concerning the Gemara's statement that a fallen wall is different. According to Tosafot, the reason is that in a courtyard people commonly conduct private uses and therefore privacy damage is more serious. In a valley, where the concern is primarily the evil eye while crops stand, one would not compel rebuilding after collapse, just as one does not compel building above four cubits. According to Rashi, however, the reason a fallen wall is different is that the parties had already agreed to remove the visual harm permanently. Shitah Mekubetzet, in the name of Ri, explains that their original consent is presumed to continue. On that approach, the same may apply in a valley, and after collapse each can compel rebuilding. Rashba's responsum cited by Beit Yosef at the end of the siman also implies that the rule of a fallen wall remains operative even after the sugya's conclusion.
