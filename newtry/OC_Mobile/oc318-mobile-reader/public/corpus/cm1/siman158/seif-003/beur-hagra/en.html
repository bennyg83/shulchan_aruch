The required height. Some say. A garden and a valley also involve visual intrusion, as stated at 2b, 'A garden is different,' etc., and therefore four cubits are required, as stated at 6b. The first view holds that this was said only at the preliminary stage of the discussion. According to the conclusion, which explains 'likewise' as referring to unhewn and dressed stone, it does not refer to four cubits. See there. They require ten handbreadths, as stated at 6b. They were compelled to this reading by the mishna at 5a concerning a courtyard wall.
(Supplement) The required height, etc. Rambam writes likewise in a responsum, but this is very strained, and Raavad and Ramban object. Rambam's words can be explained differently. Tosafot ask why the Gemara challenges from 'likewise in a garden,' for perhaps that clause too concerns a case where they voluntarily agreed. That answer is available only at the preliminary stage, according to Abaye. Under Rava's conclusion, which makes 'likewise in a garden' an independent rule and applies 'the place where people customarily fence' to a seller, the Gemara raises no challenge. The conclusion that a seller is liable even where local practice is not to fence cannot be due to visual intrusion, for why would he differ from partners? Rather, it is to prevent him from being suspected as a thief. Partners do not require such protection, since until now they did not suspect one another. See Baer HaGolah, seif katan 2. (End of supplement.)
