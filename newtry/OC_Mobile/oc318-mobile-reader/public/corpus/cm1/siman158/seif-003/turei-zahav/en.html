(At the end of this siman the Tur cites the Ramah concerning measurement of the enclosure of the inner field by mathematical calculation). It appears that the Ramah teaches us not to err as follows. One might say that the portion of the outer owner's fence directly opposite the inner field benefits both parties equally, in length and width, and they should therefore share that portion, while the remaining outer corners should be paid by the outer owner alone. This cannot be correct, for the Gemara says that the inner owner causes the outer owner an additional enclosure. Under the suggested calculation there is no additional enclosure, only one common extent of fencing. The Ramah therefore explains that this is insufficient. The outer owner also loses through the corners, because the entire area of the inner field causes the outer perimeter to expand around it, just as any inner object placed within an outer one necessarily expands the outer boundary. This is the additional enclosure caused by the inner owner at the corners. To calculate its measure, the Ramah says to compute all the fields geometrically. Divide the outer area into small square units, each one cubit by one cubit, and do the same for the inner square. Then determine how many such units are represented by the inner field relative to the outer, whether one third, one quarter, or another fraction. One might ask why the outer area must also be calculated; why not calculate only the inner field and deduct that amount from the outer? The answer is that such a calculation would not tell us what proportion of the expense the inner owner must pay, namely the measure of additional perimeter he causes. Necessarily, the outer area must likewise be divided into small units, and if the inner units constitute one third or one quarter of the outer area, the inner owner must pay that same fraction of the expense incurred by the outer owner. This is why the Ramah writes that payment follows the benefit received, and this is what he means by the measure of the enclosure.
