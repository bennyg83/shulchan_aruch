And they impose a general ban. See Baer Heitev, who writes that when one comes to collect he must swear even where the opposing claim is uncertain. Beit Ephraim, Choshen Mishpat, siman 59, writes that this may apply specifically to the amount he paid, because otherwise anyone could claim to have paid an inflated amount with no one able to contradict him. But where the issue is only whether he actually lent money on the object, a general ban should suffice. Baer HaGolah's statement that because he must swear how much he paid he should include in the oath that the money was lent on this object is not necessarily correct, because there may be cases where the amount paid is independently known or admitted by the other party. See there, and Biur HaGra.
