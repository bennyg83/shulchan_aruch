reish) This is the Mechaber's view in the Kesef Mishneh there. He quotes the Maggid Mishneh that in such a case the person holding the object is believed with an oath, and writes that he does not understand why an oath is required when the other party is not making a definite claim, so that only a general ban should apply. In my humble opinion, the Maggid Mishneh's ruling stands, because since the holder must swear how much he paid, he must include in that oath that the owner explicitly told him, 'Lend me money upon this item.'
