If a creditor was owed one hundred zuz by a thief, and the thief stole an object and delivered it to him, then also paid another hundred, the stolen object returns to its owner. We tell the creditor to pursue the thief for two hundred, because the extra payment was not necessarily made solely because of the object; just as he trusted him initially, he trusted him later. This applies when the object was given without explanation. If the creditor explicitly lent one hundred against that object, it is collateral protected by the marketplace enactment, and a general ban is proclaimed to verify that this was the arrangement.
