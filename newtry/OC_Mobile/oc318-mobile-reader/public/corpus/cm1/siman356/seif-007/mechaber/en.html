If stolen property was pledged, whether the pledge was for more than its value or for less than its value, the owners must pay the pledgeholder the money he advanced and then pursue judgment against the thief, unless the thief was publicly known as a thief, as explained above. Rama: Likewise, if an artisan receives utensils for repair and pledges them to another, the owners must reimburse the pledgeholder his principal, but not the interest accruing on it, because the market-protection enactment was not made for interest. Even according to the view that an artisan acquires an interest in the improvement he creates in a utensil, we do not say that the utensil therefore belongs to the artisan rather than the owner, because in any event it must now be returned to the owner by dina d'malchusa, since present governmental law requires the return of every stolen object even after ye'ush and change of possession. See above, siman 306, seif 2. In a place where the custom is to reimburse the interest as well, the owner must also pay the interest. Terumat HaDeshen, siman 370; see also siman 136 there.
