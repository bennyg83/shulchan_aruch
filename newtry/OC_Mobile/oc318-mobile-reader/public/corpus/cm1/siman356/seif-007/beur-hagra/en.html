He pledged it, etc., for more, etc. See Beer HaGolah. The ruling is inferred from the Gemara's conclusion, “the halachah in all of them,” except, etc. The exception teaches that all the listed cases, including a pledge exceeding the object's value, fall under the market enactment, even though that particular detail was not itself disputed. A notorious thief is not listed because that case was not included in the statement under discussion; see Beit Yosef. His money. Meaning that even the excess advanced must be repaid to him, as Rashi explains regarding a sale, unlike the Maharshal. The initial sugya clearly treated a pledge for more than the object's value as outside the enactment, and the final rule reverses that and equates sale and pledge.
