If he bought from a notorious thief. The Rambam follows his own views. He holds that the market protection enactment was not made for one who buys from a notorious thief, and he also holds that despair together with change of possession does not transfer full ownership so as to eliminate the owner's monetary claim. It prevents recovery of the actual object, but the purchaser must still pay its value to the owner. If he bought from one who was not known as a thief, however, even where the owner had not despaired, the owner must at least reimburse the purchaser what he paid under the market protection enactment. Therefore the law is formulated specifically regarding purchase from a notorious thief.
