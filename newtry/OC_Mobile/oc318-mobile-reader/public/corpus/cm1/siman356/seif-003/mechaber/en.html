If the owners despaired, whether before or after the thief's sale, the purchaser acquires through despair and transfer of possession. Some say that despair after the sale does not suffice, because the despair must precede the transfer, and this should be followed. The purchaser need not return the stolen object, but if he bought from a notorious thief he pays its value; if the seller was not notorious, he returns neither object nor money. Rama: The owners must reimburse the purchaser's price because of the marketplace enactment, and according to this view any excess value is paid to the owner. Others hold that despair and transfer of possession acquire completely, so no money is owed.
