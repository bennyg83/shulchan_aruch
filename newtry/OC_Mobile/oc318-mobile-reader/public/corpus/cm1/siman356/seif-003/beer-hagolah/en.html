chet) Ibid., law 3, following the verified reading cited by the Maggid Mishneh. The Raavad's objection was based on a corrupt reading caused by a copyist's error, which is the reading reproduced by the Tur, as the Kesef Mishneh and Beit Yosef explain. The verified reading cited by the Maggid Mishneh is the reading in our books. It is then clear that where there was despair by the owner together with a change of possession, the purchaser certainly acquires the object. Nevertheless, if he purchased from a notorious thief, although he acquires the item and need not return the object itself, he must return its value. If he purchased from a thief who was not notorious, then because of the market regulation he need not even return the money. The Bach proves from several sources that the purchaser returns the money, but deducts from the value of the object that he gives the owner the amount that he himself paid the thief, because of the market regulation. So appears in the Rambam.
tet) Ibid., seif 3, as written there in the name of the Maggid Mishneh.
yud) The Rama's words explain the version of the Rambam quoted by the Tur. In my humble opinion, they do not fit the version copied by the Mechaber, concerning which the Maggid Mishneh testified that it is the verified and authentic reading. The Sema writes that the Rama, for brevity, derived his view from the Mechaber because it may be said that the Beit Yosef and Mechaber also agree with this law, but they discuss a purchaser who did not buy the item below its value. In Perishah he writes that Maharam's principle, that the excess is returned, resolves several difficulties in the Tur and Rambam. The Kesef Mishneh discusses at length the rejection of the version quoted by the Tur, and this is not the place to elaborate.
