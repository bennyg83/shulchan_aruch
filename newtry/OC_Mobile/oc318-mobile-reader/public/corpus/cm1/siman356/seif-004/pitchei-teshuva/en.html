As a gift. See Baer Heitev in the name of Shach, who calls Rama's ruling an error. Responsa Knesset Yechezkel, siman 96, defends Rama and Sema. He concludes that where one purchases from a notorious thief and knew at the time of purchase that the object was stolen, and under contemporary law every stolen object must be restored so that despair of recovery does not transfer ownership, Rama ruled correctly that the thief need not reimburse the buyer. Shevut Yaakov, volume 3, siman 181, also defends Rama. He distinguishes land, where documents normally include liability even if not written explicitly, from movables, which are not ordinarily acquired by deed and for which there is no presumption of implied warranty. Similarly, with land where the document expressly excludes warranty, the money may indeed be viewed as a gift. He concludes that this is clear in practical law, though in my opinion his words require study. Netivot HaMishpat and Divrei Mishpat also reconcile Rama in other ways. See there.
