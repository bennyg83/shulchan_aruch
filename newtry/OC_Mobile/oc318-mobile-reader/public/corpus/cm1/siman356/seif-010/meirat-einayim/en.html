This view holds that the original owner must first reimburse Shimon, the second purchaser, the full one hundred and twenty gold coins because of the market protection enactment, since Shimon did not know that Reuven, the first purchaser, had bought from a notorious thief. Shimon then recovers the entire one hundred and twenty from Reuven, because Reuven did know that the seller was a notorious thief. See the Tur, who rules differently. The Tur writes that the owner takes the article from the second purchaser without paying him, the second purchaser recovers from the first purchaser everything he paid, and the first purchaser then litigates with the thief for what he paid. In the Perishah I explained the reason. Strict law does not require reimbursement even to one who bought from a nonnotorious thief. Reimbursement exists only because of the market protection enactment. In a case like this the enactment was not made, especially since the thief is identified and the purchaser can recover from him directly.
