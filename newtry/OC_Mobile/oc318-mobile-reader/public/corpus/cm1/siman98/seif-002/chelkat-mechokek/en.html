I have no vow or oath against you. Although the present case concerns a divorced woman, and a vow ordinarily applies specifically to a widow because the Sages refrained from administering oaths to widows in beit din, if he had written only, "I have no oath against you," he could argue that he exempted her only from a severe oath but not from a vow. Nimukei Yosef, citing Ritva, writes that generally, if one person owes another an oath and the creditor exempts him from it, he is exempt from every vow as well, and conversely. It therefore appears that if a husband writes to his wife, "I have no vow against you," all the more so he cannot require an oath, which is more severe. Nevertheless, he might argue that when he exempted her from a vow he meant only if she became a widow, while if he divorced her he can still require an oath.
