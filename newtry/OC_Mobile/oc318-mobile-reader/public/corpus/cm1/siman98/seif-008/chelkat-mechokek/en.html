Or she admits, etc., and is not believed to say, etc. One should ask why she is not believed by a migo. Responsa Rosh, rule 50, siman 7, likewise writes that even where there are no witnesses there is no migo, but does not explain why. In Beit Yosef to siman 118 the same responsum appears in another version. There, a woman was exempted by her husband and after his death, when she showed the heirs some of his documents, she said, "My husband told me to tear these." The heirs said that he had never made her credible for such a claim. If she then tore the documents she was liable, because she no longer had a migo. The meaning is that the heirs protested before she tore them and said they were not obligated to believe her. Since their protest was legally valid, she had no right to damage the heirs' property merely because she claimed to be telling the truth. According to this, our text can also be understood. Once the heirs have authority to object, she may not destroy their documents without their knowledge, because the documents were not deposited with her; immediately upon the husband's death the estate passed to the heirs. See Responsa Rosh, rule 79, siman 8.
