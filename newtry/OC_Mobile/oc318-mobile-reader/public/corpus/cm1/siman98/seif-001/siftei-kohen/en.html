Who wrote all his property to his sons. This applies even where he was a person on his deathbed. See what I wrote above in siman 81, seif 2, in the gloss. This is also explicit in Baal HaTerumot, Gate 59, which is the source of this ruling, and it is likewise demonstrated by the Gemara and the authorities cited in Even HaEzer, siman 106. This is contrary to the Beit Yosef. See also the end of that siman in the Beit Yosef.
If the debtor admits. The Sema explains that he admits he has not yet paid, but claims that the creditor waived the debt. It appears that the Sema means that the debtor argues that, since the creditor did not demand payment for many years, he must have waived it. If, however, the debtor claims that the creditor expressly waived the debt, it is obvious that he is believed through a migo, since he could have claimed, 'I paid you.' Once the debt was waived, the creditor would no longer care about retaining the document, as explained in siman 81, or according to the corrected reference, siman 82. See what the Bach wrote on this matter, though his words are astonishing.
