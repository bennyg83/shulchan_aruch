(Seif 7) “Perhaps it will deteriorate.” One might ask that when the creditor collects land we should likewise fear that he will not improve the field and will allow it to deteriorate, because he knows his note is forged and that the land will not remain his. Similar concerns appear in siman 109, where an authorization to seize land must state that beit din recognized it as property of the deceased; otherwise the authorization is invalid, because the heir expects to recover his land and will not improve it. Likewise, in siman 145 uninterrupted but abnormal consumption is not a prescriptive possession, because the possessor does not use the land in an ordinary manner and does not care about loss to land that is not his. The answer is that we do not fear an uncommon deterioration here when the creditor holds an apparently valid note. The concern about movables is different, because he will certainly dispose of them, as people ordinarily do. One might say that he can later restore their value, but since the movables certainly will not remain intact in his hands, the collection must occur without any risk of loss. This also explains why, regarding land, he may consume the produce even though perhaps he will later lack funds to repay it after the borrower proves the note forged. Since the note appears valid, the Sages feared only destruction of the borrower’s principal, as occurs with movables, not ordinary consumption of produce.
