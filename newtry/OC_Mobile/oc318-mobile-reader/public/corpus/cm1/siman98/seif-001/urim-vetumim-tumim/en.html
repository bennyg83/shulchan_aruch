And he remained silent, etc. If this creditor expressly accepted the division and said that he was satisfied with it, Baal HaTerumot, sha'ar 59, part 1, law 2, still holds that this does not necessarily extinguish his lien. "Acceptance" there does not mean that he expressly forgave the debt, as Gidulei Terumah understood. Everyone agrees that an actual waiver is effective and does not require a kinyan. Rather, he merely says that he is satisfied with the distribution. Razah explains that the creditor can mean, "I have no objection to your giving these assets to your children, but my debt is not cancelled and my lien remains on the property." Even this requires study. Razah does write that explanation, but Tosafot on Bava Batra 132b, in the passage beginning "it follows," expressly give a different reason why the wife's acceptance does not waive her ketubah. They say that she can claim that she agreed only to please her husband. Rashbam gives the same explanation. They do not use Razah's broader theory that acceptance of the distribution does not touch the existing lien. The rationale of pleasing one's husband is unique to a wife and does not apply to an ordinary creditor. It may therefore be that when the Gemara says this is among the leniencies of a ketubah, it refers only to the view that mere silence causes the wife to lose the ketubah. According to the first Tanna, who requires both a writing and acceptance, the rule is not a special leniency of a ketubah and could apply equally to other creditors. This is also suggested by the Rambam's commentary to Peah, chapter 3, mishnah 7. There he understands the case of one who writes property to his wife as involving a person on his deathbed rather than a healthy person. This reflects his view, following the Rif, that because the relevant question was unresolved, the wife still collects her ketubah. In his Mishneh Torah he later ruled differently. Nevertheless, in the commentary he writes that if the husband produces a document showing that she accepted the distribution, she loses the ketubah. If, according to that approach, an ordinary healthy person's distribution would place a ketubah and other debts on equal footing, explicit acceptance should affect an ordinary creditor as well. See also below, siman 111, seif 14. Where a creditor sees the debtor distributing all his property without leaving anything over and actively participates or accepts, the circumstances may show that he waived his right. The reason given there is that otherwise he would have assumed he could collect from what remained. Accordingly, the present rule requires study, as Gidulei Terumah also observes.
