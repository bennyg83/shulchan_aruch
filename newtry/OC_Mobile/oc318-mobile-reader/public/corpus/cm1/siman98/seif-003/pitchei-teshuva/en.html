And afterward, if he has proof, it shall be returned. Shaar Mishpat writes that this is difficult. Since the case here concerns a document that was authenticated in beit din, even if the debtor later brings witnesses after collection, he should not be able to recover the money, because the matter would then be two sets of witnesses contradicting one another, as explained in siman 46, seif 37, in the gloss. Therefore, the Rambam and the Mechaber must be speaking where the document was authenticated through other witnesses who never signed any document, and the later witnesses testify that this document is forged; in such a case the later witnesses are believed. Alternatively, the debtor later brings witnesses that the creditor was searching for someone to forge a document. In that case authentication is ineffective, as explained in siman 63, seif 1, and even the creditor's seizure is ineffective, as the Sema writes there. But if the document was authenticated by witnesses who said, "This is our handwriting," and the debtor claims that he will bring witnesses that this is not their handwriting and the document is forged, then even though his claim appears to be mere pretext, the money is deposited with a third party, as in the case of a violent person.
