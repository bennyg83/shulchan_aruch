Beit din sets him a period of time, etc. The period is determined according to what beit din considers necessary, taking into account whether the witnesses he says he must bring are near or far, readily available or difficult to locate, and similar considerations. The Sema writes this in seif katan 7. In seif katan 8, however, the Sema writes that if the creditor is forceful, beit din initially grants the defendant ninety days where his claim appears to have substance. This is difficult. If the claim has substance, the court should simply grant whatever period it considers necessary, even more or less than ninety days. The Shach also appears troubled by this in seif katan 3. The Sema wrote this in order to explain a responsum of the Rif cited by Baal HaTerumot, sha'ar 3, part 7, law 2. It states that where the creditor is powerful, the money is placed with a third party and the defendant is told, "If you do not bring proof by such and such a time, the money will be given to the creditor." It then says that if the court sees substance in the defendant's claim, it sets him a period of ninety days and removes nothing from him until that time arrives. The commentators have already wondered why ninety days is specifically relevant. If beit din sees genuine substance in the defense, even twelve months may be granted when circumstances require it, as appears from the Gemara. It therefore seems that there is a scribal or printing error. The correct text likely read "the days he needs," meaning whatever period beit din sees as necessary. This phrase may originally have been abbreviated and was mistakenly expanded by a printer into "ninety days." Such errors are common in printed books.
