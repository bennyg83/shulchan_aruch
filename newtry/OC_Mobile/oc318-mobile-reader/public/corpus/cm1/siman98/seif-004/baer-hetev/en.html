Time. Beit din grants whatever period its judges deem necessary. The Tur's reference to thirty days means the ordinary rule where beit din cannot determine how much time is actually needed. This is what Rama means by "ordinarily." Sema.
A third. If the borrower's claim has substance and the lender is a powerful litigant, they initially grant the borrower ninety days and do not remove anything from him until that period arrives. Beit Yosef writes likewise in the name of Baal HaTerumot. Thus writes the Sema. The Shach writes that the Sema's words imply that these ninety days are granted immediately when the borrower alleges that the document is forged. This is not correct. Plainly, the ninety days referred to are after the thirty days already granted before an authorization to seize property is written, as stated in the Gemara. According to the Sema, Baal HaTerumot would contradict the Gemara.
