And witnesses are in such-and-such a place. Meaning, this is what the borrower claims: that he has witnesses there.
They set him a time to bring his witnesses. The Mechaber does not specify how long because it depends on beit din's assessment of what is reasonably needed. The Tur's thirty days is the default where beit din cannot make a more precise assessment. Thus Rama concludes, "ordinarily they give him thirty days."
They place it in the hands of a third party. This applies where there is not real substance to the borrower's words but he is raising contrived arguments. With an ordinary lender he would have to pay immediately. Where the lender is forceful, however, the money is placed with a third party and the borrower is given thirty days to bring proof. If he fails, the third party gives it to the lender. By contrast, where the borrower's claim has genuine substance and the lender is forceful, they initially give the borrower ninety days and do not remove anything from him until that period expires.
