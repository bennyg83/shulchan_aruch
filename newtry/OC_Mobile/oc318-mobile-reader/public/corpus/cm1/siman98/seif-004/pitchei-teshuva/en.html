And afterward, if he has proof, he shall return it. Shaar Mishpat asks: since the case here concerns a document already authenticated by beit din, even if after collection the borrower brings witnesses, he seemingly cannot recover the money, because this would be two sets of witnesses contradicting one another, as explained in siman 46:37 in the gloss. One must therefore say that the Rambam and Mechaber refer to a document authenticated by other witnesses who themselves never signed that document, and later witnesses testify that it is forged; in such a case the latter witnesses are believed. Alternatively, the borrower may bring witnesses that the lender was seeking to procure a forgery, in which case authentication of the document is ineffective, as explained in siman 63:1, and even the lender's possession does not help, as the Sema writes there. But if the document was authenticated by witnesses who said, 'This is our handwriting,' while the borrower merely says that he will bring witnesses that it is not their handwriting and is forged, then even if his claim appears to be mere harassment, the money is placed in the hands of a third party, as in the case of a violent litigant.
