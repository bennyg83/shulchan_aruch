And we also administer an oath to her heirs, etc. Above in seif 1 the Mechaber writes simply that he cannot administer any oath to her. The Gemara in chapter HaKotev clearly shows that an ordinary exemption covers the oath of one who impaired her document and the other oaths in the mishnah, all the more so an oath concerning guardianship, which is imposed upon her and based on a doubtful claim. Here, however, when he exempted only her, her heirs and those coming in her right must swear the oath of one who impaired the document and an oath regarding guardianship if they themselves acted as guardians. Likewise, even without impairment, if he claims against the heirs, "I paid you," they must swear if he says, "Swear to me." In a case of impairment, beit din administers the oath even without his claim. Sema at the beginning of siman 84 rules that if the son impaired the document he does not swear, because the enactment was made concerning the original party, not his son, citing Beit Yosef in the name of Baal HaTerumot and the Yerushalmi. Yet here the Mechaber rules that impairment applies even to heirs. Bach explains that when the Tur says he cannot administer any oath to her, that means if he exempted her from guardianship after she had already become a guardian but not from impairment, while if he exempted her at the time of marriage he exempted her from impairment but not from guardianship. This would split the Tur's words into different cases, but the explanation is not convincing. Nor is Bach's objection to the Rosh compelling. The Rosh's words are clear: if he exempted her from impairment, all the more so he exempted her incidentally from guardianship. The Gemara asked only about a case where he exempted her from guardianship alone but not from impairment. Since most people who write an exemption at marriage have impairment in mind, the same applies if he exempts her after marriage when she has become a guardian: absent qualification, he exempted her from all oaths, because impairment is common. This is unlike Bach.
