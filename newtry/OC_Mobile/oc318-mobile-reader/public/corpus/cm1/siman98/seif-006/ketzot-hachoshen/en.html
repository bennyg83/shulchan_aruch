A deposit document is ordinarily payable immediately because the deposit itself must be returned in its existing form and requires no period in which to obtain money. Ran writes, however, that if the custodian admits negligence and the object no longer exists so that he must pay money, the resulting obligation has the law of an ordinary loan and the debtor is given the normal time to pay. Shiltei Gibborim questioned this from Mordechai, who says that a transaction does not change merely because the parties later change their language, such as where a fixed term loan is verbally converted into an iska. There is no difficulty. Once the deposited object is gone through negligence, the legal subject itself has changed. The custodian no longer owes return of the original object but owes money damages. That monetary liability is naturally treated like an ordinary loan for purposes of the time allowed for payment.
