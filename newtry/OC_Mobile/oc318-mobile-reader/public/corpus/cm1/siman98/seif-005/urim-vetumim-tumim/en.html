Under nidui for ninety days. The Sema writes that after thirty days the nidui is repeated, and after sixty days a more severe cherem is imposed, as explained above in siman 100. He follows Baal HaTerumot, sha'ar 3, part 8, who applies the rules stated in Moed Katan. The Rambam, however, in Hilchot Sanhedrin, chapter 25, records the Moed Katan procedure generally, yet in chapter 22 of Hilchot Malveh he simply writes that this debtor remains under nidui for ninety days and does not mention a cherem at all. The Tur and Mechaber likewise say only nidui and later speak of releasing the nidui, without mentioning a more severe ban. This strongly suggests that they distinguish between a person who simply refuses the authority of beit din and one who claims that the creditor's document is forged. Where a litigant defiantly refuses to obey and there are no assets available for immediate execution, progressively stronger sanctions are necessary to break deliberate resistance. Here, however, the defendant does not absolutely reject the court. He claims that the document is forged. Although he is not believed without proof, he may know personally that it is false. Moreover, he has assets and the process will ultimately lead to an adrachta against them. There is therefore no need to impose the harsher cherem without purpose. The Ran in the chapter Elu Megalchin writes a related distinction regarding the rule that after sixty days one imposes cherem. Some explain that this applies to a person who does not come to court at all, whereas one who appears but does not obey the judgment is treated differently, and after ninety days the court proceeds to write an adrachta. The authorities here can similarly distinguish levels of defiance. A person who comes to beit din and says without any substantive basis, "I simply will not pay," is worse than one who fails to appear, because he openly displays contempt for the court. Such a person may be subjected to repeated nidui and cherem. By contrast, one who appears and asserts forgery, although the claim does not presently defeat the document, is not necessarily acting with that degree of contempt. The Rambam, Tur, and Mechaber therefore speak only of nidui for the ninety-day period. This appears clear.
