They write against him a petichah. A written notice of excommunication is called a petichah. Some explain that it bears this name because it is the first writ. If the debtor still does not pay, later documents follow, namely an authorization to seize property, an appraisal, and a final vesting order, as the Tur explains in the coming simanim and as the Mechaber states below in seif 9.

One day, etc. The reason is that he had already appeared initially before beit din, and beit din had already granted him thirty days to bring his evidence. Therefore at the end they grant him only one additional day.
And some say two days. The dispute depends upon variant readings of the Gemara concerning the court sessions held on Monday and Thursday. According to the opinion that grants one day, the text is read to mean that when he fails to appear on the Monday at the end of the ninety day period, they send for him on Tuesday, he comes on Wednesday, and on Thursday he stands before beit din. According to the opinion that grants two days, the reading is that they send for him on Monday and Tuesday, he comes on Wednesday, and on Thursday, toward evening, he appears before beit din. So write the Maggid Mishneh and Beit Yosef.
