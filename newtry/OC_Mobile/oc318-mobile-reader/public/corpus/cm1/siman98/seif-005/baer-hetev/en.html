Writ of excommunication. Meaning a written notice of excommunication. The Gemara states that during the first thirty days they do not descend to his property, because perhaps he is working to obtain money to borrow. During the middle thirty days they likewise do not descend to his property, because perhaps he failed to find a loan and is now trying to sell. During the final thirty days they still do not, because perhaps a prospective purchaser who wishes to buy his property is working to raise the money. This reason alone would not justify waiting ninety days, for siman 100 explains that a debtor is ordinarily given only thirty days to obtain money and pay, after which an authorization to seize his property is written immediately. Here the case is different because he claims that he does not owe the money and that he has witnesses proving the document forged. Although we do not accept his assertion and place him under excommunication, his claim is sufficient to prevent seizure of his property until after ninety days. After sixty days he is placed under a ban, and at the end of ninety days an authorization to seize his property is written. Sema.
