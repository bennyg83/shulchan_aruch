Ninety days. Ninety days are granted because during the first thirty we say he is trying to obtain money on credit; during the second thirty, after failing to find a lender, he is trying to sell his property; during the final thirty, the purchaser is trying to gather the necessary cash. Thus the Gemara. Sema writes that although below in siman 100 a person who comes to beit din and asks for time to seek funds receives only thirty days, here the debtor denies the document and says the debt is forged. Although he is not believed, that claim still gives enough basis to allow ninety days. This explanation is unnecessary. There, he is not even placed under excommunication, and therefore only thirty days are granted. Here he is excommunicated immediately. We assume that he is actively searching for money because a person does not willingly remain under excommunication, and therefore we wait ninety days before issuing an order of foreclosure. This is straightforward.
Ninety days. Sema holds that after thirty days the excommunication is renewed, and after sixty days a more severe ban is imposed. See Tumim. I wrote that Rambam, Tur, and Mechaber do not appear to hold this. Since he claims the document is forged and he has property, he remains under excommunication throughout the ninety days, and if he still does not pay, the court then issues the foreclosure order.
One day, etc. Beit din ordinarily sits on Monday and Thursday. The creditor complains in court on Monday. According to one view the court sends its messenger on Tuesday, the messenger travels all Tuesday, the debtor returns at the end of Wednesday, and on Thursday morning appears before beit din. According to the view requiring two days, the court sends immediately on Monday, the messenger travels Monday and Tuesday, the debtor returns Wednesday, and by the end of Thursday appears before beit din.
