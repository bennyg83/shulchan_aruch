And if she is still alive, etc. At first glance this implies that even if the husband exempted her from an oath, once she sold her ketubah she still must swear, because the exemption applied only if she did not sell. There is no evident reason for that, and Bach already noticed the problem. See what he writes here and below in siman 105, where he distinguishes that perhaps the exemption was only from a claim of uncertainty, whereas if the purchaser asserts with certainty that she was paid and she denies it, she was not exempted from that. This distinction is not clear, because a clause of credibility can be effective even against a certain claim and even where she impaired the document.
