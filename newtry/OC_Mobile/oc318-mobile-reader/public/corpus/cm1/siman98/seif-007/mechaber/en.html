We stated that if the debtor does not return at the end of ninety days, a writ of authorization is issued against his land. But even after ninety days, as long as he says, 'I will now bring proof and invalidate the document,' the creditor is not placed in possession of movable property. The creditor might consume it, and the debtor may then bring proof and invalidate the document; if the movable property can no longer be found, what will the debtor recover? This applies even if the creditor owns land, because that land might deteriorate or be devastated and decline in value. Although a writ of authorization is not issued against movable property, it is placed in the hands of a third party, and beit din grants an appropriate period. If the debtor impairs the document within that time, it is impaired; otherwise, the property is delivered to the creditor. See note 80.
