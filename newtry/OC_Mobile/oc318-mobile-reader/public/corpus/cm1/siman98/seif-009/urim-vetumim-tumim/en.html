And they tear it, etc. The Sema is surprised that the Rambam and Mechaber do not write here, as they do later concerning an adrachta against encumbered property, that the adrachta itself must record that the debt document was torn. The Lechem Mishneh raises the same question. There is no difficulty. In the present case beit din already knows the specific unencumbered property available for collection and immediately places the creditor into that property in satisfaction of the debt. There is no reason to fear that the court will forget to tear the debt document. Once the court itself gives land as payment of the debt, why would it leave the original document intact and permit a second collection? An adrachta against encumbered property is different. At the time it is issued, beit din may not yet know whether any recoverable property exists, where it is, or in whose hands it lies. The document merely authorizes the creditor to search and locate property from which collection might ultimately be made. There is therefore genuine room for uncertainty whether the original debt instrument was already torn, since collection has not yet occurred and may never occur. In that setting the adrachta should expressly record that the original document was torn. Whenever possible, we minimize assumptions that a beit din acted incorrectly. There is a chazakah that judicial acts are performed properly. Therefore, where collection was completed directly before the court, no additional recital is needed.
