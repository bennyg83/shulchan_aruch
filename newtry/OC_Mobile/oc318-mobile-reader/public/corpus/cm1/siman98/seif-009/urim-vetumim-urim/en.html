Until the bidders cease. Rambam holds that a thirty-day appraisal period is required specifically for orphan property and encumbered property. For a creditor collecting the borrower's own property, the court need wait only until, in its judgment, the bidding has ended. Many authorities disagree and hold that the same thirty-day appraisal applies to an ordinary creditor and to encumbered property alike. See below, siman 103.
And they tear the debt document. It appears that beit din makes its formal tear in the document, but the document nevertheless remains physically intact enough to be preserved, and the creditor remains responsible to keep it. The reason we do not collect movables is that the borrower may later find witnesses who invalidate the document. We foreclose land because the creditor will not suffer irreversible loss if that later occurs. But if the borrower later brings witnesses who say, for example, "We never signed such a document," or another ground that invalidates the document, how could he prove it if the document had been completely destroyed? The document must still be available for identification and comparison. Therefore "they tear it" means they perform the formal beit din tear while preserving the document in the creditor's hands sufficiently for later evidentiary challenge. This is clear.
