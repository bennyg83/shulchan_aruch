Beit din. The Sema explains that this means three appraisers who value the pledge, as the Mechaber himself explains in seif 15, and an ordinary panel suffices. The Shach concludes that with a mortgage of land the lender cannot compel redemption before one year, because an unspecified land mortgage ordinarily lasts a year. It would also be unreasonable for the borrower's position to be weaker than the lender's: if the borrower cannot remove the lender before a year, the lender likewise should not be able to force the borrower to redeem earlier. The Shach therefore rejects the Beit Yosef's ruling from Baal HaTerumot to the contrary.
Wait. Since the lender could, if he wished to lie, claim that he bought the pledge, beit din merely advises him to sell it in the presence of witnesses so that the borrower will know the sale price. This is the Rambam's wording. Rivash explains that valuation by beit din is still required; the witnesses are additionally useful because the lender may sell for more than the appraisal and thereby avoid a later oath.
