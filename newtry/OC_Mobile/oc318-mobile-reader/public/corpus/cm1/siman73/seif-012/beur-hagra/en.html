And if the borrower... As at the end of Bava Batra in the case of the judge who seized, etc.: a person's property serves as guarantor for him. A pledge likewise serves only as security, not as absolute payment, as Rashi explains. Rashi infers this from the Gemara's statement, "the handle was lost," implying that absent loss the borrower remains liable. Sefer HaTerumos, sha'ar 49, seif 5, further proves it from the end of HaOmanin, which says that one who lends on a pledge is a paid bailee. This implies that if the pledge is lost through unavoidable circumstances, the borrower must still pay. Otherwise the pledge would be like a designated apotiki, where if the property is swept away the creditor cannot collect from other assets, as stated in Gittin 41a. See Sefer HaTerumos.
