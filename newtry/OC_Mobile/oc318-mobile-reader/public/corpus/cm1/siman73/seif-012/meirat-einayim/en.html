He may sell it through beit din, etc. This is also the wording of the Tur. In the Perishah I wrote and demonstrated that at first the Tur adopts the implication of the Gemara's language, from which it appears that the sale must be through beit din, and then explains what that means. Some say that it means an actual beit din, in order that they notify the borrower, etc., while the Rosh explains that "beit din" is not literal, but means three appraisers who assess how much the pledge is worth. We may explain the Mechaber's words here in the same way. He first writes that it is sold through beit din, adopting the language of the Gemara, and later, in seif 15, he concludes that the sale through beit din means before three ordinary persons who appraise it. In seif 14 he also explains that selling through beit din does not mean immediately, because some hold that one must wait thirty days after making the demand. This is clear.
