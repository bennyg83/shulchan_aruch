If he lent upon a pledge, etc. The Beit Yosef understood Baal HaTerumot to mean that a mortgage of land without a fixed term is treated like a pledge of movables, so that after thirty days the lender may compel sale, subject to local custom. The Darkei Moshe, Sema, and Bach followed him. This is not the meaning of the source. The Ramban was asked only whether a lender holding mortgaged land can compel the borrower to redeem or sell it, and he answered that the borrower cannot say, "You relied upon the land; take it for your debt." Although the Ramban says that movables may be sold after thirty days, he never says this of land. Land may instead be governed by the rule that an unspecified mortgage lasts a year. The Raavad likewise distinguished movables, which may be sold after thirty days, from land, where local custom governs and, absent Jewish custom, one may consult gentile practice only because our law is not explicit. The Rashba and Maggid Mishneh also indicate that a lender cannot compel redemption of land as he can with movables. Thus, even according to the Ramban and Rosh, who allow compulsion concerning land, it may be only after a year. The authorities cited by the Beit Yosef do not establish a thirty-day rule for land. The correct distinction is therefore between movable pledges, saleable after thirty days, and land, for which one must wait a year or follow an established custom where the law is not otherwise fixed.
