(Seif 14) “He cannot sell it before one year.” The Mordechai, Bava Kamma, states in the name of Rabbi Yitzhak bar Peretz that the creditor may not be compelled to sell the pledge after the year has passed. The Rama cites it in siman 369. The novelty here is that the creditor need not grant an additional thirty days after demand. The Sema says the novelty is that even where the pledge belongs to a gentile and produces interest, the creditor must wait. But the Mordechai’s case expressly involved books, not a gentile’s pledge. The rule is simply that, according to local custom, the pledge may not be sold before a year. This custom limits sale of the pledge, not an oral demand for repayment from other assets. Since an ordinary loan is for thirty days, the creditor may demand payment after that period even though he may not yet sell the pledge. The Rama carefully says, “he cannot sell it,” not “he cannot demand payment.” We should not extend the custom beyond its express scope.
