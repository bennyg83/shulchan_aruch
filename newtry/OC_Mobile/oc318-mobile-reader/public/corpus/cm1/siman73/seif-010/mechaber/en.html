If a person holds a promissory note against another that is payable at a future date, and before the due date he comes to beit din and says, 'I have found property belonging to so-and-so, and I fear that if it comes into his possession he will conceal it from me and I will have no means of collecting my debt,' then, if the judge sees a reasonable basis for his claim that he will be unable to collect when the debt falls due, it is a mitzvah for the judge to restrain the property until the note's due date. The same law applies when a loan was made for a fixed term and, during that term, the lender sees the borrower dissipating his property while owning no land, or sees that the borrower wishes to travel overseas. If the lender demands payment or a guarantor, his demand is granted. {Rama: The same applies wherever beit din considers it proper to restrain the defendant's money, as ruled in Piskei Maharai 64. From this developed the practice of attaching funds even where the need is not so great, as stated in Terumat HaDeshen 85. If the defendant is from another city and beit din considers it proper to attach his money located in the claimant's city, the defendant must go and litigate in the claimant's city to release his money; see above, siman 14. But if beit din sees that the defendant will obey the judgment in his own city and is not a violent man, his money is not restrained even though it is in the claimant's city. Instead, the claimant must follow the defendant, as stated in Mahariv 34 and 155. When beit din considers it proper to restrain the money, it must notify the defendant, as explained below in siman 106. If a person lived in the same city as the lender, left it, and left property in that city, the claimants may compel him to litigate with them there, so that every borrower should not borrow and then travel overseas. Beit din must first notify him, as stated in the responsum of the Rashba and below in siman 106; see also note 116 and source 6.}
