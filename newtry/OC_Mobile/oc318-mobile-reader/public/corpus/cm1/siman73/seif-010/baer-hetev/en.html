The document. The Sema writes that it appears this applies specifically when he has a written instrument in his possession, for even if the borrower disputes his claim, the creditor can swear on his document and collect. Therefore the court may restrain the funds. But when he has no document, since the borrower can take a hesset oath and be exempt, even if the loan was made before witnesses, for we hold that one who borrows before witnesses need not repay before witnesses, no restraint is imposed. The Shach writes that it follows that even an unverified document is sufficient to restrain the funds until the creditor verifies it and collects upon taking the required oath. See the Bach. Since these laws depend upon local custom and upon the judges' assessment of the circumstances, I have been brief.
Squanders. If he is not squandering his property, then even if his financial position deteriorates from day to day and he will be unable to pay at the due date, even from movable property, the creditor may not restrain his assets before the due date. This is the ruling of Maharshal, and it appears to be the principal rule in practice. Many authorities in any event disagree with this law, and their view appears prima facie to be the primary implication of the Gemara. Although the later authorities have accepted the Mechaber's ruling and the custom has spread accordingly, the novelty should not be extended beyond its stated case. See Responsa Mahari ben Lev, part 1, rule 17, siman 102; Mabit, part 1, simanim 33, 133, and 213; Maharashdam, simanim 57, 151, 158, 386, 392, 408, and 457; Maharashach, part 3, siman 58; Mabit? [Responsa M.A.], simanim 51 and 89; and Maharanach, siman 25, for various laws of attachment. Since these laws depend on local custom and the judges' assessment, I have not elaborated. Shach.
