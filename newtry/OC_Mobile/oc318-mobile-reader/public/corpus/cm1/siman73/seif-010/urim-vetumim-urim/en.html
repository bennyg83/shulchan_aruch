One who holds a document, etc. The wording specifically speaks of a document, because then the borrower cannot plead total denial or payment. With an oral loan, where he can plead payment, the lender ordinarily cannot restrain assets before the due date. Sema. The same should therefore apply to a handwritten obligation containing a credibility clause. Shach writes that even if the document has not yet been authenticated, but the lender asks for time to authenticate it, the court may meanwhile restrain the debtor's assets. Rosh states this in the first chapter of Bava Kamma.
He is wasting his property. Meaning that he is dissipating it improperly. Such a person is acting wickedly by endangering another person's money, and beit din may therefore preserve the creditor's rights. But if he is not wasting his assets through misconduct and they are simply declining day by day because of difficult circumstances or similar causes, beit din has no authority to restrain them before the due date. Shaarei Shevuot, and the Shach agrees.
