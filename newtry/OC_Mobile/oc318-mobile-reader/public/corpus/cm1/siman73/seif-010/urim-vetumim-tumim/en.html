And they shall give it to him as payment. Rosh and Tur distinguish that if the debtor expressly swore to pay in money rather than in equivalent value, giving collateral does not fulfill the oath. If he failed to pay cash before Shabbat, he has violated the oath. Mechaber omits this distinction. One reason is that Rosh's responsum appears to follow R. Yitzchak bar Shmuel's approach to oaths against mitzvot, where everything turns on whether the mitzvah object was explicitly named. According to the alternative Tosafot approach, the decisive factor is active versus passive violation and the structure of kolel. A general oath to pay over a range of days can take effect on weekdays, but an active prohibited act on Shabbat does not necessarily become permitted merely because the oath also covered permitted times. Ramban in responsa also disagrees with aspects of Rosh's distinction. This likely explains Mechaber's omission. Still, once Mechaber allows appraisal and payment through property on Shabbat, one may ask why cash itself cannot be transferred through a gentile or by granting the creditor the room in which the money rests so that acquisition occurs through the courtyard. Conceptually that is not obviously more severe than transferring appraised property. The practical details therefore require further study.
