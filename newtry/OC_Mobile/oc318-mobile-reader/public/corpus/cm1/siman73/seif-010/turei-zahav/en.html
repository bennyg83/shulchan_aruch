(I was asked) about one who swore to pay ten gold coins and added, “If I do not pay, I will give ten gold coins to charity as a penalty.” He paid only five, and argued that the full penalty applied only if he paid nothing. At first glance he should owe the entire penalty because the undertaking covered the whole debt, like one who must provide a pledge worth the entire debt when the due date falls on Shabbos. On reflection, he owes the penalty only proportionally. In Bava Metzia, one who undertook to pay one thousand if he left an entire field fallow and left only one-third fallow was assessed proportionately. Although that particular ruling is limited by the law of asmachta, it shows that “if I do not perform” may be apportioned. Here the penalty was phrased negatively: “If I do not pay, I will give a penalty.” He may say that the full penalty meant failure to pay anything, while partial failure creates a proportional penalty. By contrast, if he said, “I swear to pay one hundred on this date, and if I violate the oath I will pay the entire penalty,” the oath covers every part of the payment, and any breach triggers the whole penalty.
