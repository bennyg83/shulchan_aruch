And he fixed a time for him, etc. At first glance, it appears that this refers specifically to a term fixed at the time of the loan. If the term was fixed only after the loan, a kinyan would be required, just as with an undertaking of credibility or a stipulation that repayment be made only before witnesses, as explained above in simanim 70 and 71. See there. There is, however, room to distinguish. In those cases the lender seeks to extract money, whereas here the borrower seeks merely to retain the money in his possession until the agreed time. This is somewhat comparable to the lender having waived collection for that entire period. The matter requires study.
Whether the borrower died. See the Perishah, where I wrote that this is not comparable to a partnership or a lease, regarding which the Tur and Mechaber state in simanim 176 and 329 that the other party may remove the heirs. There the asset itself is still extant, and one may also say: Your father was trusted by me to exert himself in commerce or labor, but I do not trust you to do so. A loan is different, because the money is no longer extant, having been given for expenditure for a fixed term, and when that term expires the heirs will pay the lender his money. They are also presumed capable of paying. Furthermore, in a partnership or lease, the owner gave the property to the father partly for his own benefit and may say: I entrusted it to your father because I relied upon him not to damage it and to give me my profits, but I did not entrust it to you. An ordinary loan, by contrast, is for the borrower's benefit, and his children inherit his right. It is therefore comparable to a borrowed cow that was in their father's possession when he died, where the heirs may use it throughout the entire period of the loan, as the Tur and Mechaber state below in siman 341:3.
