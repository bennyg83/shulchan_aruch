And fixed. The Sema writes that at first glance this seems to mean only if the due date was fixed at the time of the loan. If it was fixed afterward, a kinyan should be required, similar to a stipulation of trustworthiness or 'do not repay me except before witnesses,' as discussed in simanim 70 and 71. One might distinguish that there the lender seeks to extract money, whereas here the borrower seeks to retain the money until the agreed time, which is somewhat like the lender forgiving collection for that period. The matter requires study. The Shach writes that to him it is obvious that this indeed constitutes waiver. Moreover, the Sema's comparison to 'do not repay me except before witnesses' does not support him, because in siman 70, seif 3, we rule that such a condition is effective even after the loan without a kinyan. Regarding an ordinary stipulation of trustworthiness, the Shach likewise concluded in siman 71 that no kinyan is required even when made after the loan. All the more so here. Even according to one who would require a kinyan there, this case is different because it is a waiver, which requires no kinyan, as stated at the beginning of siman 241. The Maggid Mishneh, chapter 7 of Hilchot Malveh, concerning a mortgage in a place where early redemption is permitted, also supports this. These are the words of the Shach.
His heirs. This differs from partnership and tenancy, where the Tur and Shulchan Aruch write in simanim 176 and 329 that the other party may remove the heirs. There the property remains intact, and he can also say, 'I trusted their father to exert himself in the business or labor, but I do not trust you.' A loan, by contrast, is given to be spent. Alternatively, since the ordinary term of a loan benefits the borrower, his children inherit that right, like a borrowed cow in their father's possession, which the heirs may use for the duration of the borrowing, as stated in siman 341, seif 3. These are the words of the Sema. The Shach writes that the qualification that the heirs must be as financially reliable as the father appears neither in the Rambam nor Tur nor any other authority, and the Beit Yosef does not mention it. In his view, even if the heirs are not as wealthy, the lender cannot demand payment before the due date, which is why the Rambam and Tur state the law without distinction. Even according to the Mechaber, if the heirs provide adequate security or a sufficient guarantor, it appears that they need not pay early. The Mechaber only means that if they are not financially reliable like their father the lender may seek to restrain their assets, comparable to seif 10. Even that requires study, because in seif 10 the judge sees a real danger that the debt will become uncollectible, which is not necessarily true here. These are the words of the Shach.
He swears. The Sema explains that no distinction is made in the obligation to swear between denial of the due date and denial of the principal sum. The Shach writes: if the claimant says, 'Today is the due date and you owe me one hundred,' and the defendant replies, 'I owe you only fifty, payable in ten days,' he immediately takes a heset oath regarding the date, and after ten days takes a Torah oath regarding the admitted partial debt. The Shach writes that he has seen this ruled in practice. See the Taz, who at length challenges certain judges who held that such a case is not an admission of part at all, and refutes their reasoning.
