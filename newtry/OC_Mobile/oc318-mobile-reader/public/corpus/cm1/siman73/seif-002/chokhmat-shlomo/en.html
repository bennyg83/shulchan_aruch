Seif 3. One says five days remain in the term, and the other says ten days remain, etc. I was uncertain about a partial admission where, at the time of the admission, the defendant could not yet say “here it is,” because the admitted amount was not due until five days later. When those five days pass and the time arrives for the oath, he then says, “Here it is.” Do we follow the time of the original admission, when the Torah-oath liability was created without an immediate tender, or do we say that because no oath could yet be demanded at the moment of admission, the legally operative admission occurs only when the five days expire, at which point his immediate tender exempts him? A proof can apparently be drawn from the Gemara’s discussion of “here it is.” The Gemara asks from the Mishna’s distinction involving vessels and land. One could have established the case where the defendant admits that a parcel of land was given or mortgaged only for a limited period and claims that the term has not yet ended for several more days, while denying the remainder. At the time of his admission he cannot return even the admitted portion immediately, because according to his own claim it remains legally in his possession until the term ends. Were this movable property, such an admission would appear to create an oath after the days expire. Yet with land there is no oath because land is excluded. If we regarded the original admission as decisive, the Gemara could have used precisely such a case to show a distinction between land and movables. Its failure to do so suggests that where the admitted amount is not yet due, no Torah-oath obligation is finalized until the due date. If at that later time he maintains the admission and immediately tenders the property, it is then a valid “here it is” and he is exempt. This appears to be the implication, although the matter still requires study.
