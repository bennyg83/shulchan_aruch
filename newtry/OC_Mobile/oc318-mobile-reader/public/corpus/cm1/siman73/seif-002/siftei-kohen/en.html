And he fixed a time for him, etc. The Sema initially suggests that this refers only to a maturity fixed at the time of the loan, because an extension granted later might require a kinyan, like a grant of credibility or a stipulation not to repay except before witnesses. He then suggests a distinction, because here the borrower merely retains money already in his hand for the additional period, which resembles a waiver by the lender. In my view it is plainly a waiver. Moreover, even a stipulation not to repay except before witnesses is effective after the loan without a kinyan, as explained in siman 70, seif 3, and I have likewise concluded in siman 71 that an ordinary grant of credibility does not require a kinyan even after the loan. All the more so here, because waiver requires no kinyan. The Maggid Mishneh in chapter 7 of Hilchot Malveh, concerning a mortgage in a place where removal is permitted, also supports this.
If his heirs are as financially established as he was. This condition appears neither in the Rambam, Tur, nor any other authority, and the Beit Yosef does not mention it. In principle, even if the heirs are not as financially established as the deceased borrower, they cannot be compelled to pay before maturity. The Rambam and Tur state without qualification that this applies whether the lender or borrower died. Even according to the Mechaber, if the heirs offer adequate security or a sufficient guarantor, they should not have to pay before maturity. At most, his statement excludes a case in which their lack of means creates a genuine danger that the debt will not later be collectible, analogous to seif 10 below. Even that comparison requires study, because there the judge actually sees that collection will otherwise become impossible.
And he says, today is the end of the term, etc. If the lender claims, today is the end of the term and you owe me one hundred, while the borrower responds that he owes only fifty payable in ten days, he takes a heset oath immediately and after ten days takes the Torah oath that then becomes due. I have seen authorities rule this way.
