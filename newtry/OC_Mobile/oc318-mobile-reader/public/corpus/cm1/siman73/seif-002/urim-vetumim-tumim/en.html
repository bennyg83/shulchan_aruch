Shach says that if the claimant demands one hundred immediately and the defendant admits fifty but says even that fifty is due only after ten days, the defendant now takes hesset on the ten-day delay. When the ten days pass, he then takes a Torah oath on the other fifty he denies. I do not understand why the Torah oath should be postponed. The defendant presently admits a real fifty-zuz obligation, albeit payable in ten days, and denies the other fifty entirely. That is already a classic partial admission. If one says that an admission of a debt not yet due is not counted as an admission for the Torah oath, then the same reasoning should still apply after ten days only if the original pleading is reassessed. Some dayanim therefore say no Torah oath ever arises. I do not think that is correct. The better view is that the defendant should take the Torah oath immediately, because the admitted fifty is a definite monetary admission and the other fifty is a definite denial. A different case is where the claimant himself says the entire hundred is due only in ten days and the defendant admits fifty. There the claimant's own theory says no amount is yet payable. The oath should therefore be administered when the payment date arrives, since before then perhaps the defendant will admit the balance and no oath will be needed.
