Seif 15. And they shall appraise it. If the borrower tells the lender, "If I do not pay by a certain day, sell the collateral for whatever you can get," this does not necessarily authorize a sale without appraisal. The holder of a document is at a disadvantage in interpreting ambiguous language. The phrase "for whatever you can get" can refer merely to selling immediately without waiting the usual thirty days, and need not waive appraisal. This resembles other cases in which apparently expansive language is interpreted narrowly. Although siman 61:6 rules that a clause authorizing collection without appraisal is ineffective in certain contexts, collateral delivered voluntarily at the time of the loan is different. The restriction there is connected with the prohibition against taking collateral outside beit din, so wording dispensing with appraisal can be understood as applying where beit din is unavailable. A pledge given at the inception of the loan is not subject to that same concern, and an explicit waiver of appraisal can therefore be effective.
