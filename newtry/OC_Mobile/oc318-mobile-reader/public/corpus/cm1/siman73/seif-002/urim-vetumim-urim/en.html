And he fixed a time for him. Sema is uncertain whether, after the loan, if the lender grants an extension of the term without a kinyan, he may later retract from that extension. The Shach treats it as obvious that he cannot retract. Granting time is like partial waiver: what difference is there between waiving the entire debt and waiving the right to collect it until a later date? The Shach is correct. This is straightforward in an oral loan. But with a written loan, according to what I wrote above in siman 12 and below in siman 241 that waiver of a written debt is ineffective without return of the document, perhaps extending the term is likewise ineffective. However, I explained in siman 12 that the reason waiver does not work while the deed remains outstanding is that the creditor's failure to return the document shows he did not truly intend complete waiver. That reasoning applies where he waives the debt entirely, because he could have returned the deed. With an extension of time, he cannot return the deed because the debt remains in force. Therefore the extension may be effective according to everyone. This still requires study.
If his heirs are financially established. The reason is that a creditor may restrain a borrower's assets if he sees him dissipating them. So too, part of the lender's implied condition was that he did not lend on the assumption that impoverished heirs would succeed to the borrowed funds. The Shach questions this reasoning. One should not depart from the words of the Mechaber because we rule that property can be restrained where necessary. But if the heirs are financially established, the lender cannot take the money from them before the fixed term. This resembles a borrowed cow: the heirs may continue using it for the entire borrowing term. All the more so with a monetary loan.

He takes a hesset oath. Suppose the lender claims one hundred payable immediately, and the borrower admits fifty payable within ten days. The Shach says the borrower takes a hesset oath immediately that the term for the fifty is ten days, and after ten days he takes a Torah oath concerning the remaining fifty he denies. In Tumim I disagreed. He should take the Torah oath immediately. Taz also appears to hold this. But if the lender claims one hundred payable within ten days and the borrower denies fifty, then he does not take the Torah oath until the ten days arrive. Before then, even according to the claimant, the debt is not yet due. Perhaps during the intervening period the matter will be resolved.
