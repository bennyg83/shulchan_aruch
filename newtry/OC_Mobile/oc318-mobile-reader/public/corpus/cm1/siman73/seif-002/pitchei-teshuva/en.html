And he fixed a time for him. See Noda BiYehudah, second edition, siman 14, concerning one who compromised with his creditors and wrote notes promising payment “when Hashem expands my boundary.” Some wished to define that phrase by the statement in Chullin 84a concerning the verse “when Hashem expands your boundary,” and the scale of expenditures appropriate to one’s means. He answers that this is mistaken. The measure of “expansion” depends upon the time and country and is left to the judges’ assessment. See there. See also Responsa Amudei Or, siman 102, concerning one who owed a fixed sum, lost his financial standing, and wrote: “I acknowledge that I owe the bearer one hundred rubles, and when Hashem expands my boundary I will pay him.” At that time he owned houses but had no ready cash. He later died and the houses descended to his heirs. The holder of the note demanded payment, and the question was whether the heirs were liable, since perhaps the condition was never fulfilled in the debtor’s lifetime. After lengthy discussion, the responsum concludes that the heirs must pay, bringing a clear proof from Beit Meir, Even HaEzer, siman 90, seif 1. See there.
