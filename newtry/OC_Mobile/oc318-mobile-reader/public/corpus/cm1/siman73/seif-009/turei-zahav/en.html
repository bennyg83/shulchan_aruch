(Seif 8, gloss) “Nevertheless he must pay because of his oath.” This does not contradict the earlier gloss at the end of seif 7 that when the debt is extinguished by law, the oath is extinguished too. There, nonpayment results from Hashem’s decree that the Sabbatical year cancels the debt, or from the creditor’s waiver. The oath was only not to withhold a debt that remained due. Here the obstacle comes from the debtor, who seeks to invalidate a document because of a defect. The oath obligates him not to create that invalidation. This does not include an oath made in factual error, where he believed at the time that he owed money; that is plainly an erroneous oath. The case is one where he knowingly undertook an obligation that he later has legal power to cancel. The oath prevents him from exercising that power. The Sema says he was not otherwise liable but voluntarily accepted liability through the oath. That is difficult, since a person can create a monetary obligation even without an oath, as explained in siman 40.
