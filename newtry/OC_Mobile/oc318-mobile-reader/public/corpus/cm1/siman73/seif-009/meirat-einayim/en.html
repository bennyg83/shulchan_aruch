To pay on Rosh Chodesh Adar. The same applies to any month whose Rosh Chodesh consists of two days. The novelty is that although the first day technically belongs to the preceding month of Shevat, and he swore simply to pay on Rosh Chodesh Adar, we follow ordinary usage, in which people refer equally to both days of Rosh Chodesh by the name of the incoming month. Since this produces a doubt concerning a Torah obligation, we rule stringently. But one who swears to pay on Rosh Chodesh Nisan, which is one day, is not required to pay at the beginning of the day, because in ordinary language the entire day is called Rosh Chodesh. All this is stated by the Rashba in the responsum there.
