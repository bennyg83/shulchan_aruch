And they shall appraise it. Magen Avraham in Orach Chaim siman 306 objects to Mechaber. Rosh and Tur say only that the debtor can give collateral on Shabbat. They do not clearly authorize a full appraisal and transfer as payment. The need for a mitzvah justification exists even for collateral because ordinary commercial pledging is itself restricted on Shabbat. All the more so if the object is actually transferred as payment. Magen Avraham's objection is sound. Tosafot at the beginning of Shabbat distinguish the principle that one may sometimes commit a minor prohibition so another person can fulfill a mitzvah. That leniency is strongest where the other person was not negligent. Here the debtor himself failed to arrange payment before Shabbat. There is also a legal difference between merely handing over collateral and effecting actual payment ownership. Rama in Even HaEzer 29 says that if a man gives an object as collateral and explicitly grants the woman a monetary interest in the body of the object, kiddushin can take effect. That shows a transfer can be structured through the object without full sale formalities. Magen Avraham therefore suggests that any appraisal needed should be done through gentiles. Why not simply annul the oath? Because the oath may have been made on the creditor's authority. Even where a public-interest vow can sometimes be released for a mitzvah, here the creditor himself may object: if the oath is released, who knows when payment will actually occur? He has a concrete interest in insisting on performance now. Thus release is not necessarily available without his consent.
