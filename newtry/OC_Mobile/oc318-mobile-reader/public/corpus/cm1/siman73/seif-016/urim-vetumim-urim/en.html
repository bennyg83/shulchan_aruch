He may not buy it for himself. The reason is suspicion that people will say he bought it below value. Rivash therefore holds that before a beit din of recognized experts, who investigate carefully and whose acts have full judicial authority, even the lender himself may purchase it. Sema asks from the beginning of siman 72, where use of a pledge is likewise prohibited because of suspicion, yet no exception is stated for three experts. He answers that the case is uncommon. According to Sema's own explanation there, however, the suspicion is not that he uses the pledge at an unfairly low rental but that he uses it without deducting anything. If the arrangement has publicity through three people, even laymen can testify that proper deduction occurred; if there is no publicity, even experts do not help. See my earlier discussion at length. Taz likewise says that there three laymen suffice, though for another reason. Maharatz objected, but according to what I wrote there is no difficulty, because the suspicion there differs from the suspicion here.
Some say, etc. The Shach challenges Rivash on this point, and he is correct. Rivash disagrees with most authorities. This question is not dependent on the earlier rule that three laymen may suffice. Even with an ordinary beit din there can still be suspicion regarding a judge's purchase.
