For himself. The Sema writes that at the beginning of siman 72, concerning rental of a pledge, the opinion that three expert judges permit the transaction was not mentioned because expert judges are not ordinarily convened for a rental. The Taz disagrees. On the contrary, there even an ordinary beit din is effective, for the Rama states there that if the parties stipulated in advance it is permitted, and we established there that before the creditor uses the pledge a stipulation is sufficient, even if made before witnesses. The gloss there answers that a stipulation with the litigant himself is different, because he knows and waives his rights. But where beit din acts in the owner's absence there is concern for suspicion that the creditor will use the item too harshly with no one supervising him. If he rents it to others, however, he supervises them so it will not be damaged. This suspicion does not apply to a sale. According to this, renting it to himself is forbidden even through beit din, which is why the Shulchan Aruch and Rama did not mention it. The Shach disagrees with the ruling itself and writes that Tosafot and the other authorities clearly imply that even through an expert beit din he may not take the pledge for himself, and even after the fact such a transfer is not valid. This appears to me the principal rule, for when the Gemara says simply that he sells through beit din, it means expert judges, as explained above, and nevertheless Bava Metzia, chapter HaMafkid, says that he sells it through beit din but does not sell it to himself.
