When does this apply? With an oral loan, etc. This qualification also applies to the Torah oath mentioned above in seif 2, when the lender claims that today is the end of the term.The lender takes a hesset oath. Rambam likewise writes this in chapter 13 of Hilchot Malveh. The Tur, however, merely says that the lender swears, without specifying hesset.
Above, in siman 72, seif 17, it was explained that when a lender swears concerning collateral in his possession, even though he has a migo that he purchased it, he nevertheless swears while holding a sacred object. One must distinguish that there the borrower denies the amount of the obligation, whereas here they do not dispute the amount of the loan but only the time of payment, and hesset suffices. Although seif 2 explained that denial of the term and denial of the money are equivalent regarding an oath, that was specifically for the rabbinic hesset oath, where the Sages did not distinguish.
