Heset. The Sema writes that although in siman 72, seif 17, a lender swearing regarding a pledge in his possession must swear while holding a sacred object, there the borrower claims that he never owed so much. Here, where they dispute only the time of payment, a heset oath suffices. The Shach calls this strained. If the reason a lender on a pledge must swear while holding a sacred object is that he does not claim ownership of the body of the pledge, or because a migo does not exempt from an oath, those reasons apply equally here. Rather, the Tur intentionally writes merely that 'the creditor swears,' meaning each situation follows its own proper oath. A lender on a pledge must swear while holding a sacred object because he is not claiming the body of the pledge. A creditor holding a promissory note, however, is not asserting a claim for which the document itself serves as proof, so he takes only a heset oath. These are the words of the Shach.
