The lender shall swear hesset, and so forth. Shach says that where collateral is involved, the lender should instead take the formal oath required by the Geonim, because his claim concerns money secured by the collateral rather than ownership of the object itself. That would be persuasive if we had a clear reason why the underlying timing dispute is not already a Torah partial-admission matter. But as long as the distinction between immediate and delayed payment is treated as insufficiently defined monetary value to generate a Torah oath, it is likewise difficult to impose the Geonic formal oath merely because collateral happens to secure the obligation. If the disputed timing value is not sufficiently concrete money for the Torah partial-admission oath, it should not automatically become sufficiently concrete to trigger the special collateral-collection oath. Therefore Mechaber's hesset formulation can stand, though the entire classification of the timing dispute requires further study.
