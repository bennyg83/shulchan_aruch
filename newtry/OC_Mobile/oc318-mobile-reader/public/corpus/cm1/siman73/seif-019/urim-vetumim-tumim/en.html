Shach argues that no one should dispute the rule because the Gemara expressly says that one who lends on a field and stipulates, "If I do not pay within three years, it shall be yours from now," avoids asmachta. If Rashi disagreed, one would have to say the Nedarim sugya conflicts with Bava Metzia and that Rashi follows Nedarim. But Mechaber below in siman 207 expressly codifies the field case. One can answer Rashi more narrowly. Land is not subject to ordinary ona'ah. Therefore even if the property is worth substantially more than the debt, the condition may not look like an exaggerated penalty. A movable pledge, however, is subject to ona'ah and can involve a clear disproportion. There the forfeiture looks more like a penalty and can remain asmachta despite the words "from now." Ritva and Shitah Mekubetzet discuss such a distinction. But this still does not reconcile Mechaber. Below in siman 207, seif 14, Mechaber rules broadly that a present "from now" acquisition can remove asmachta even in strong asmachta cases where the other party is not already holding the property. All the more so should it work where the creditor already holds the pledge. The difficulty is even greater where the debtor says, "The excess value shall be yours." Shach explains that mentioning the excess makes the arrangement look expressly penal and therefore asmachta. Rosh indeed says that a clause mentioning the surplus resembles the penalty "if I leave the field uncultivated I will pay from the best property." That makes sense within Rosh's system, where even a present formula does not cure every severe asmachta. But Mechaber follows Rambam that "from now" can cure asmachta broadly. It is difficult to say Rambam would make a special exception merely because the clause is phrased as a penalty, since the Spanish sages' standard mechanisms for avoiding asmachta were designed precisely for penalty obligations. Therefore Mechaber's distinction here also requires study.
