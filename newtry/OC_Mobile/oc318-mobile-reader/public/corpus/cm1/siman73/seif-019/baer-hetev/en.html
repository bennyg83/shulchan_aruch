An article. The Sema writes that 'an article' is not literal. The same applies to money held by him as a deposit and still intact. Only with a loan, which is given to be spent, does the language of waiver apply, as explained at the beginning of siman 241. The Shach writes that this is not actually explained there, but the Sema follows his own interpretation there. The Bach writes similarly here. Their words appear correct specifically where deposited money was bundled and sealed. Otherwise, since the custodian may use it, and even if the depositor demands it before it has been spent he need not return those same coins but may give other money in their place, as stated in siman 292, seif 7, in the name of Tosafot in Bava Metzia, chapter Elu Metziot, it resembles a loan and the language of waiver is applicable. Chacham Tzvi, in his gloss to the Taz, challenges the Shach sharply and writes that he made a major error, for Tosafot proves the reverse: according to our ruling that such a custodian is a paid custodian, if the depositor demands the money before it was spent, the custodian must certainly return the depositor's own money and not other coins.
