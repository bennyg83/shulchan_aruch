But when one has an object in another’s possession, etc. The Sema writes that “an object” is not literal; the same applies to money held as a deposit and still extant. Only a loan, which is given for expenditure, is subject to the language of waiver. He says this is explained below at the beginning of siman 241. It is not actually explained there, but follows his own interpretation in seif katan 6 there, and the Bach writes similarly here. Their view appears correct only where deposited money was tied and sealed. Otherwise the custodian may use it, and even if the depositor demands it before it was spent, the custodian need not return those very coins but may return other money, as I write below in siman 292, seif 7, in the name of Tosafot in Eilu Metziot. It is therefore comparable to a loan, and the language of waiver does apply. So it appears to me.
