And if through fear of the lashes he regrets, and so forth. Tosafot Gittin 35 ask why a nazir who drinks wine can ever receive lashes. Perhaps he will later ask a sage to annul the nezirut retroactively, making the warning uncertain. Tosafot answer that the existing nezirut has a chazakah of continuing. Maharsha adds that if the court knows the nazir intentionally violated his vow, a sage should not annul it merely to save him from punishment. Otherwise every warning would remain uncertain because the offender could later erase the prohibition. This seems to conflict with Rosh's rule here that beit din may annul the earlier oath so the debtor can avoid punishment and fulfill his later obligation. There is support for Maharsha in the Tosafot passage itself, but the matter is not decisive. One can distinguish vows made on the authority of the public. Even such vows can sometimes be annulled after the person has already violated them for a mitzvah need. That creates enough possibility to make a later warning uncertain even if a sage would ordinarily refuse to help an intentional violator. But a simpler approach is that beit din may indeed annul a vow even though the result is that a prior act no longer receives lashes. A nazir can still rationally choose not to annul. If he observed nezirut faithfully for a long period and then failed near the end, accepting punishment preserves the spiritual value of the prior observance and addresses the violation. Annulment would erase the entire nezirut retroactively and treat the preceding abstention as having no mitzvah status. A sincere person may therefore prefer the punishment to annulment. That is enough for actual lashes to exist in practice, while Tosafot can still ask that the legal warning is uncertain because perhaps this particular person will choose annulment. This reading also fits the responsa discussing the issue. The special case here is even stronger because repayment of a creditor is itself a mitzvah. Beit din has an affirmative reason to facilitate annulment of the earlier oath so the debtor can pay. Therefore Rosh's ruling need not be rejected because annulment also removes the disciplinary consequence.
