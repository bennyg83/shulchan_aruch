One who swore to another. See Sema, seif katan 18, and the Shach’s comments in Yoreh Deah 232, seif katan 28. Noda BiYehudah, siman 30, section 13, writes that the Sema’s statement in the name of Terumat HaDeshen, that where the claimant does not admit the oath the monetary law is that of an ordinary document without an oath, is astonishing, because Terumat HaDeshen states the opposite: his discussion concerns only the prohibition arising from the oath, whereas monetarily the obligor may not contradict the written admission and say that he never swore. The Sema copied the Darkei Moshe, and Noda BiYehudah concludes that a printing error entered the text. The words “if the claimant does not admit” should read “if the claimant admits.” If the claimant admits that no oath was taken, there is plainly no concern for the oath, and the monetary issue is governed independently. If the claimant does not admit this, Terumat HaDeshen leaves it unresolved whether the obligor may so plead. Rama below, siman 207, seif 19, rules that an admission that one swore is effective even if in fact no oath was taken. The Shach in Yoreh Deah noticed that the Sema’s wording conflicts with Terumat HaDeshen but did not note the Darkei Moshe or the apparent printing error. Noda BiYehudah also questions the Taz’s understanding of Rama in Yoreh Deah 232, seif 12, as though a person is believed against the claimant by giving an explanation for why he wrote that he swore. Rama did not intend to disagree with Terumat HaDeshen in that case. As to the Sema’s further statement that if one wrote in his own hand that he actually swore he cannot excuse himself, the Taz disagrees and holds that the word “actually” adds nothing. A convincing explanation for why he wrote so is effective, and without such an explanation even the omission of that word would not help him. Tumim agrees. Responsa Beit Ephraim, Choshen Mishpat, siman 24, applies this to a case involving a written undertaking not to rent houses. Such an undertaking is merely a kinyan on words and is ineffective by itself. The fact that the document says that he swore is also not decisive where that is the standard form used by the scribe. This is especially so when the document speaks in the past tense. Even according to the Sema, the Taz holds that a satisfactory explanation is accepted, and there can be no greater explanation than that the document followed a standard formula. Beit Ephraim further distinguishes between a case in which the written obligation itself could validly have been undertaken and a case where everyone knows that no actual oath was made and the only basis for liability would be the oath itself. In the latter case he is believed to say that he merely signed the standard form. See there, and see also Responsa Chatam Sofer, Yoreh Deah, siman 227, and Pitchei Teshuva there, siman 236.

Misgeret HaShulchan and Maamar Kaddishin cite Responsa Avodat HaGershuni, siman 7, concerning Reuven, who deposited a debt document with a third party, in a form not subject to asmachta, providing that if he failed by the fixed date to supply merchandise he had undertaken to provide, the third party should give the document to the other party without objection. The date arrived and the other party did not demand the merchandise. Later he demanded that the third party release the document to him. It was ruled that Reuven was exempt because the merchandise had not been demanded at the appointed time. The responsum concludes, however, that where the failure to demand was itself with the other party’s consent, it follows that he remains obligated to accept the merchandise even after the fixed date.
