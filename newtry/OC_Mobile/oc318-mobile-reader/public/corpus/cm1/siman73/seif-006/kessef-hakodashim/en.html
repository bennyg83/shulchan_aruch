Seif 19. Forgiveness does not apply to a specific object. I heard from my father-in-law, apparently in the name of one of the great Sephardic authorities, that forgiveness is ineffective regarding a specific identified object because such language is even weaker than the defective formulas discussed at the beginning of HaKotev. This requires great study from Kiddushin 15, where the Gemara proves that a Hebrew servant's body is acquired because if it were not, the master could simply say before two people or a beit din, "go free." The Gemara found it difficult to avoid the proof by saying no beit din was present. If forgiveness of a specific acquired object could be strengthened by a kinyan sudar, the case would be easy: even an impoverished person can receive some object from another and use it for the kinyan, and with a Hebrew servant there is no obstacle of "whatever the servant acquires belongs to his master" in the relevant form. At the beginning of HaKotev a kinyan does cure defective wording. The Gemara's proof therefore indicates that simple forgiveness of title to a specific movable or land is weaker still and may not be made effective merely by a kinyan. This requires very careful study.
