(Seif 6) “And siman 232.” This refers to Terumat HaDeshen 302. A prisoner wrote and signed that he had sworn to give Shimon whatever Shimon later claimed, without excuse. After release he said the writing was compelled because Shimon controlled all his property. Terumat HaDeshen was uncertain where Shimon says, “I know you truly swore,” for an admission is like one hundred witnesses. The Rama in Yoreh Deah 233 rules that one who publicly admitted or wrote that he swore may later deny the oath if he gives a plausible reason for the earlier admission, and certainly if in truth he never swore. Thus even the Terumat HaDeshen’s doubt is resolved in favor of the explanation. The Sema’s statement that the words “I actually swore” cannot later be explained is surprising. If the writer gives a credible reason why he was forced to write that phrase, it too may be retracted. If witnesses write only that he admitted before them that he swore, an explanation of the admission is effective. If they testify that he actually swore before them or gave a formal handshake, no phrase such as “actually” is needed; their testimony establishes the oath.
