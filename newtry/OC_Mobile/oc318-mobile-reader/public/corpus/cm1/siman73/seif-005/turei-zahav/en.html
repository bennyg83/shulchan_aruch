(Seif 5) “Reuven swears.” Why does the creditor not take the items in payment? A debtor’s property secures his debt by Torah law, and beit din should descend to the assets and give the creditor what is due. The Sema calls it a sale and says the debt cannot be collected until the oath is released. That is astonishing. A debtor cannot extinguish another’s debt through his own oath. Even a sale made in violation of an oath is legally effective. The responsum’s point is not that beit din lacks power to collect, but how to minimize violation of the conflicting oaths. If he or beit din, acting as his agents, sells, he actively violates the first oath; if he does not sell, he passively violates the second. The ruling prefers inaction. He is disciplined until he regrets the first oath and obtains release, enabling him to fulfill the second. If the debt preceded the oath, the oath never takes effect against the mitzvah of repayment. One who refuses to pay is compelled until he complies, as with any positive commandment. The Rosh’s discussion of punishment does not imply that the debt is suspended. If he never regrets, beit din still collects forcibly. A difficulty remains because the Rosh’s responsum compares the case to conflicting oaths about eating a loaf and says to remain passive, whereas his rulings and Yoreh Deah 238 say one should eat, preserving the first oath and avoiding a new violation. The Tur’s conclusion can nevertheless be justified by the same principle: once the second oath was uttered falsely, its violation already exists; selling would create a fresh violation of the first, so he should not sell. The Shach’s distinction between an oath concerning one debt and an oath concerning all debts is incorrect where the oath includes only prohibited nonpayment. The glossator develops two alternative textual explanations of the Rosh, but the practical point remains that an oath made before the debt can take effect through inclusion of permitted possibilities, whereas an oath made after an existing debt cannot cancel the duty to pay.
