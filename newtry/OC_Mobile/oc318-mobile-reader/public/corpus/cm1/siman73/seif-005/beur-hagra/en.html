Reuven swears... As stated in Kesubos 86a, where Rav Kahana said to Rav Pappa, etc. Shevuos in several places and Nedarim 16a state that one cannot swear, etc.
And they strike him. Kesubos there, as above.
And if... Shevuos 29a discusses one who swore to eat a loaf. The Yerushalmi there, cited by the Rosh, says that we tell him to eat it, because it is preferable, etc. The same applies here: he may not sell.
And they strike him. This too is a vain oath for which lashes would be due, except that today lashes require ordained judges. See the Shach, seif katan 16.
And if as a result... Then the second oath is not necessarily vain, because if the first oath were annulled, etc., as stated there 27b. See Yoreh Deah 238:17, where the Rashba is uncertain regarding this point. The comparison is not exact, because the Gemara says there that although there is no liability, an oath still exists. Even there the Gemara initially considered that the later oath might have no effect at all, whereas here the Gemara says at 29b, "the oath has already issued..."
