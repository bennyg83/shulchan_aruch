Some say that even if he moved his residence, etc. Even if the borrower expressly said in his oath that he would bring payment to a particular place, and the lender later moved elsewhere, with no relevant difference in distance or difficulty, Maharik holds that the borrower must still fulfill the oath. Mentioning the original place was not considered a strict condition. The Shach disagrees. In Tumim I concluded that the Shulchan Aruch and Maharik are correct in the following case: at the time of the oath the lender did not live in the place where the loan was made but lived elsewhere, and the borrower swore to bring payment to that location. If the lender later moves to another place and there is no meaningful difference between the two cities in distance, travel, or other circumstances, the borrower must follow him and pay there. Even had no place been mentioned in the oath, the borrower would have had to bring payment to the lender wherever he lived. Mentioning the place merely clarified that he would not have to pursue him to some remote country. But if the lender originally lived in the borrower's own place and the borrower specifically swore to bring payment to a named different city, then without the named place he would have had no duty to follow the lender at all. The only basis for imposing travel is the oath, and we can enforce only what his lips actually expressed: that place and no other. All the more so if there is some meaningful difference between one place and another. In that case he is not obligated to follow him elsewhere.
