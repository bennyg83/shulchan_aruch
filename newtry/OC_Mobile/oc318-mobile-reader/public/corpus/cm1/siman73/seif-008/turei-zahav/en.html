(Seif 7: A pledge on that day.) The Beit Yosef permits appraisal of the pledge on Shabbos to determine whether it covers the debt, so that the debtor is free of his oath. The Sema further permits measuring an object on Shabbos when needed for the mitzvah. This is difficult. The Gemara asks, “Do we tell a person to sin so that another may merit?” Why should others perform appraisal or measurement so that this debtor can fulfill his oath? The creditor should instead extend the time until Sunday. Moreover, if rabbinic restrictions may be waived, why not permit payment in cash, which involves only handling money? Were it not for the authority of the Beit Yosef and Sema, I would say the Rosh intended only delivery of an unappraised pledge, which involves no full prohibition. A responsum of the Ramban permits taking a pledge on a festival and implies that appraisal was not permitted: if given as actual payment, appraisal would have been needed. The Ramban later says that release from the oath is preferable where possible.
