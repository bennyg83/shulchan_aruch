The responsum of Rosh itself says that the lender claimed the agreed date meant payment no later than that time, not payment only at that exact moment. Once the creditor has demanded payment, the debtor must take reasonable steps before Shabbat to satisfy the oath. The demand can of course occur before the precise due moment. It is impossible to require the creditor to make his demand at the exact instant of maturity. If, however, the creditor never demanded payment, neither beforehand nor on the due date, there is generally no oath violation merely from nonpayment, as Sema explains. A debt that is not presently demanded does not always require unsolicited delivery. Taz's objection, that the creditor obviously could not demand on Shabbat itself, is therefore no objection. A prior demand is sufficient.
