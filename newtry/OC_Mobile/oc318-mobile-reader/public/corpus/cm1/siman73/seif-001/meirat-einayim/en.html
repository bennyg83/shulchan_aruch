See the beginning of siman 341 and Orach Chayyim siman 307.However, if the borrower protests that a document should not be given to the lender, etc. The Tur cites a responsum of the Rosh concerning Reuven who lent Shimon one hundred for a fixed term, Shimon made a kinyan and subjected his property, and afterward protested before witnesses that they should not prepare a document.
Rama is discussing the same case, where a kinyan was made and the witnesses would ordinarily write a lien against the borrower's property, as explained at the beginning of siman 39. The Tur writes there that the borrower may retract and protest that they not write the document, but if he does so he must repay immediately. This is likewise the intent here: although ordinarily the lender must wait thirty days, in this case he need not wait.
But if the loan was made before witnesses without a kinyan, it is evident there that the borrower cannot undo the loan merely because no document was written. Although the Mechaber in siman 39 cites the Rosh's view and then another opinion, namely Rashba, that even if the borrower stands and protests, the witnesses may write the document against his will, Rama cites this law here to teach that according to those who permit the borrower to protest, once he does so and the lender asks for a document or immediate repayment, we listen to the lender.
The Levush's explanation, which extends this to an ordinary private loan with no initial stipulation for a document, is not correct. A person may lend another for thirty days on trust without witnesses or writing, and the lender cannot later compel him to provide even a handwritten acknowledgment merely because he now wants written proof.
