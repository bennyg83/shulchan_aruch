(Shach, seif katan 10) It appears to me, etc. Note: It requires practical study whether the lender himself, who claims that the agreed term was longer than thirty days, is believed. Even if he has a migo, this depends upon the dispute among the authorities concerning a migo used to extract property. The Rambam's wording implies a case where the borrower claims that a term was fixed and the lender denies that any term was fixed. The Rambam therefore concludes that the lender must swear that he did not fix a term. This conflicts with the Mechaber's formulation, which speaks even where the end of the term has already arrived. The Rambam's intent as stated here is also clear from his words in halachot 5 and 6. Their implication is that even with a written document, where the lender himself admits that a term was fixed and the parties disagree only about the number of days, the borrower swears.
