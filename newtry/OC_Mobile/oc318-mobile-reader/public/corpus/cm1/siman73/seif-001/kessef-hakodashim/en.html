Seif 2. One witness. From siman 73 it appears that where one witness testifies that payment is due immediately and the debtor claims it is due only ten days later, the debtor is subject to a Torah oath. This shows that although merely preventing another from using money is generally exempt from damages in human court, a creditor may nevertheless compel timely payment and need not allow his money to remain withheld. Beit din compels payment when due even though a temporary delay, with intent to pay later, is less severe than outright denial. There is room to say that the benefit of having the money for those ten days need not itself equal two silver ma'ot for the Torah oath, because the debtor is presently withholding the entire sum. The fact that he will pay it later does not make the present denial a partial denial. Still, the time value must at least equal a perutah for beit din to entertain a coercive monetary claim. Yet the law of conspiring witnesses, where the value of an extension of the due date is appraised, seems at first glance inconsistent with this. If withholding the entire principal until a later date were itself treated as the full amount, the conspiring witnesses might have to pay the principal immediately and recover it when the original debt would mature. Perhaps, however, because the debtor could in practice delay payment and force collection proceedings, the measurable damage remains only the value of the extension. The matter requires further study.
