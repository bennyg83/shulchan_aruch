Custom. Where there is an established custom, it is followed whether it shortens the ordinary thirty-day period or extends it. In seif 14, regarding sale of a pledge, it will be explained that a Jew's pledge is treated according to the custom governing a non-Jew's pledge held by a Jew. The Sema writes that the same appears to apply here. The Shach writes that he disagreed there with the Rama regarding sale of a pledge. All the more so here, we do not follow non-Jewish custom when there is no known Jewish custom, but apply the ordinary rule that an unspecified loan is for thirty days. These are the words of the Shach.
To demand payment from him. Only for this purpose do we say that an unspecified loan is for thirty days. Nevertheless, the principle that a person does not repay before the due date does not apply to such an unspecified loan, as the Tur and Shulchan Aruch state at the end of siman 78. See there. Sema and Shach.
Borrowing. A borrowed object must be returned in its existing form, whereas money lent is given to be spent, so the borrower requires time to obtain money with which to repay. See the beginning of siman 341 and Orach Chaim siman 307. Sema.
Objects. The Sema discusses this at length and holds that the rule applies specifically as in the Rosh's responsum, where the borrower made a formal kinyan. In that case it is presumed that the lender advanced the money only on condition that the borrower execute a written document. The Sema challenges the Levush, whose language implies that the same law applies without a kinyan. The Shach cites his teacher, who writes that the Levush understood the kinyan in the Rosh's responsum merely as a fact of that particular case, whereas the reasoning of the answer implies that the rule applies even without a kinyan. The same can be said above in siman 39. The Bach later agreed. Thus, the Rama's words are properly understood literally, since he did not mention a kinyan. These are the words of the Shach. The Taz likewise agrees with the Shach, while R. Tzvi Ashkenazi writes in his glosses that the Sema's words are correct and explains their reasoning. See there.
