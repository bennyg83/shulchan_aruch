However, if the borrower protests, etc. The Sema explains at length that the rule applies specifically where, as in the Rosh's responsum, the borrower made a formal acquisition, for then it is presumed that the lender advanced the money only on condition that a deed be written. He criticizes the Levush, whose wording implies that the same law applies even without an acquisition. My teacher wrote in the margin of his Sema that the Levush understood the acquisition in the Rosh's responsum merely as the facts of that incident, whereas the reasoning of the responsum applies even without it; the same may be said above in siman 39. The Bach later agreed. The Rama's omission of any acquisition is therefore to be understood literally.
