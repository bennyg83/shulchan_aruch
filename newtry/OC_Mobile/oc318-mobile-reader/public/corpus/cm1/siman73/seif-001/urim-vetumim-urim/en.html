Whether the loan is evidenced by a document, etc. Sema writes that one might have thought that where there is a written loan and the lender took the trouble to prepare a document, the term should be longer than thirty days. The Gemara teaches the opposite logic: because he troubled himself to formalize the debt, one might have thought he is entitled to thirty days, whereas in an oral loan perhaps even thirty days would not be granted. The law teaches that the standard term is thirty days in either case. Taz likewise writes this. It is straightforward.
But the question is just a halo, and the transmitter lies in her eyes, and from above, it seems that if he has taken such a thing as a cold to the valley in which he should be placed in the valley in which he is depleted to the same thing as his time, and he has seen a sign:
The lender protests that he should not give him a document, etc. If the original agreement was that the borrower would provide a written instrument, and afterward the borrower refuses to provide it, then even without any kinyan the lender can plainly demand immediate repayment of his money. Sema agrees, despite having mentioned a kinyan in his opening language. Anyone who reads him carefully sees that the kinyan was not essential. The Shach therefore criticized him unnecessarily. But if the lender originally made an ordinary loan without requiring a document and only afterward asks the borrower to give one, Sema correctly holds that he cannot compel him to create a formal deed. If the lender asks only for the borrower's handwritten acknowledgment, Sema tends to say he likewise cannot compel him because there was no original condition. In my view he can compel him. Refusing such a memorandum causes the borrower to bring suspicion or a curse upon himself, as explained above in siman 70, while he suffers no genuine loss by writing it. Why should the lender not be able to require this minimal protection?
