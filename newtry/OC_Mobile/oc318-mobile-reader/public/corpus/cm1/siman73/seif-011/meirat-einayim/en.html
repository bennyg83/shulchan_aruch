And claims arose against the field. The Tur includes this law together with the preceding law concerning one who says, "I fear that the borrower will dissipate his property," in a single paragraph, while the Mechaber states it separately. It appears that the reason is that where one purchased a field and claims then arose against it, the purchaser can compel the seller to use the purchase money to acquire land even if beit din does not see evidence that the seller is dissipating his assets. The reason is that this resembles a mistaken transaction, and also because the purchaser's money is still intact in the seller's possession. This differs from an ordinary lender and borrower, where the lender is not heeded unless beit din sees a credible basis for his concern and finds it proper to take precautions. The Baal HaTerumot likewise states this distinction. It is also precise in the Mechaber's wording: above he mentioned a credible basis, while here he did not. This is clear.
