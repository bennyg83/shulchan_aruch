My father-in-law, of blessed memory, establishes Rava's ruling as referring to an item without a fixed market price, because if it has a fixed price its law is explained in siman 183, where the parties divide the excess. He entered this distinction unnecessarily. Here the case is that the item was sold there at a higher price because all sellers in that city sold at that rate. In siman 183, by contrast, the agent received more than other sellers in that same place, so the excess is merely a gift. That is why the cases differ. Here the rule applies in every circumstance. This appears simple to me.
