It sold for more than the appraisal. The excess belongs to the pledgor, as the Tur and Mechaber explain in seif 16, because the appraisal establishes only that it not be sold for less. See also siman 114.He shall swear that it is as he says. He is not treated as a robber merely because he sold property that was not his so as to render him untrustworthy for an oath, because people do not regard such conduct as full-fledged robbery for this purpose.
So writes Rivash.If he does not know how much it was worth. The Tur uses the same wording. This does not mean that he admits it was worth more but does not know how much, for then he would be one who is obligated to swear and cannot swear, and the opposing party would collect without an oath, as explained in siman 72, seif 12.
Rather, he does not know its value and it is possible that it was not worth more than the amount he says he received for it. Although in siman 72 it was explained that in a comparable case the lender takes a hesset oath and is exempt even if the collateral was lost through negligence, because it is like saying, 'I do not know whether I borrowed from you,' here is different because he actively acted negligently by selling his fellow's collateral without permission.
