Asmachta. The Mechaber's words require study. Many authorities disagree with this ruling, and it appears to me that the primary rule is that with a pledge consisting of movable property there is no issue of asmachta even if he did not say, 'Acquire it from now.' Shach.
Who says. The Shach writes that it is surprising that the Mechaber cites this rule in the name of 'some say,' for in his opinion it is an explicit Gemara and is agreed upon by all the authorities. See there at length. The Sema writes that even according to the Mechaber, this is specifically where the debtor says, 'Acquire the entire pledge from now,' in which case there is no asmachta, as stated in siman 207. But if he tells the creditor to collect his debt from the pledge, without saying 'from now' regarding that portion, and says only regarding the surplus that the creditor should acquire it from now, then since the authorization to collect the debt by himself is an asmachta, the surplus is also subject to asmachta. See the Shach for the reason: 'from now' is effective because the transaction appears to be a sale, as Tosafot, the Rosh, and the authorities explain. Their words clearly imply that where it does not appear to be a sale but rather a self-imposed penalty, it remains an asmachta. Here, since he said that the creditor should collect his debt from the pledge and the surplus should be a gift, this is plainly a penalty and does not resemble a sale at all.
To retract. Initially he appointed him only as an agent to sell it, and a principal may revoke his agent's agency. So writes the Sema. The Shach writes that even if the debtor wrote, 'If I do not repay you by such and such date, you shall have authority to sell,' and formalized this by a kinyan, he may still retract and prevent the creditor from selling. If the creditor sells after the debtor has protested that he not sell, the sale is void because the agency was already revoked. This is stated in Responsa Rashba. See also siman 182, seif 2.
