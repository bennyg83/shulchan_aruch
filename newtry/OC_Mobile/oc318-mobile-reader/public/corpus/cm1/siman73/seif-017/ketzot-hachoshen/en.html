He acquires it from now. The Shach concludes that with movable collateral there is no asmachta even if the creditor did not expressly say, "Acquire it from now." At first this seems contrary to Rif, Rambam, Tur, and Shulchan Aruch concerning a gentile who lends to a Jew on chametz, or the reverse. They require an express "from now" clause. Without it, the forfeiture is treated as asmachta even though the chametz was actually pledged. One could answer that Rabbi Yitzchak's rule, which is the foundation of the Shach's position, applies specifically between Jews. In the gentile and Jew cases the special pledge acquisition may not apply. Yet the Shach himself elsewhere writes that even where Rabbi Yitzchak's full acquisition is absent, because the creditor has a lien on the object and it is physically under his control, the arrangement can be sufficiently serious to remove asmachta. Beit Yosef cites students of Rashba similarly. If so, the chametz rulings remain difficult, for they still require "from now." Therefore the primary basis should indeed be Rabbi Yitzchak's rule of acquisition of movable collateral, and the Shach's broader formulation needs study. The requirement of "from now" in the chametz cases shows that mere physical possession plus ordinary lien is not always enough to remove asmachta. Where Rabbi Yitzchak creates an actual acquired interest, however, the result may be different.
There is an authority who says. The Shach wonders why the Mechaber presents this rule in the name of only one authority, since the Gemara in Bava Metzia states it expressly. Mordechai and Hagahot Maimoniyot, however, report that Rashi disagrees in a responsum regarding a pledge. This is not contradicted by the Gemara. Rashi can explain the sugya differently. The Mishnah says that if the borrower fails to pay within three years the land belongs to the lender, and the Gemara establishes the case where he said, "Acquire it from now." Rashi explains that this is not an asmachta because there was a complete sale from now at the reduced price, while the buyer separately agreed to accept repayment and return the property if the money was repaid within three years. This is the same method used by the Spanish sages to avoid asmachta, namely first creating a complete obligation or acquisition and then creating an independent present waiver if the condition is fulfilled. According to Rashi, the Mishnah therefore does not prove that ordinary collateral language by itself escapes asmachta. This explains why here the Mechaber cites the rule as one opinion, since Rashi can disagree, while in siman 207 the Mechaber states the Mishnah's rule without qualification because everyone agrees where there was a true present acquisition.
