If one lends another money against collateral on condition that, should the borrower fail to redeem it by a specified time, the lender will collect the amount of his debt from the collateral and the excess will become a gift to him from now, this is an asmachta. If he stipulated that, should the borrower fail to redeem it by the specified time, it will belong to the lender absolutely, this too is an asmachta. If the borrower said, 'Acquire it from now if I do not redeem it by the specified time,' some say this is not an asmachta. If the borrower claims that the words 'acquire it from now' never left his mouth, the lender is believed upon taking an oath. See above, siman 207, regarding the laws of asmachta, and note 111. If a person holds another's collateral and, when the due date arrives, the borrower tells him, 'Go and sell it,' and he sells it, the borrower's later claim that he intended only to put the lender off cannot invalidate the sale; the sale remains valid. {Rama: As long as the lender has not yet sold it, however, the borrower may retract and tell him not to sell it. Similarly, if the lender pressed the borrower for payment and the borrower said, 'Let the collateral be yours,' the borrower may retract.}
