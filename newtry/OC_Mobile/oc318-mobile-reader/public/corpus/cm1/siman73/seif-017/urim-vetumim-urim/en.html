And the excess shall be a gift from now, etc. Sema asks: if he said 'from now,' why is this an asmachta? He answers that the borrower did not say that the lender should acquire the entire pledge from now, but only the excess over the debt. Since the acquisition of the amount corresponding to the debt is an asmachta, the entire arrangement is treated as an asmachta. Shach answers instead that it resembles a penalty, and with a penalty even 'from now' may not cure the asmachta. See Tumim, seif katan 19. The matter requires study under Mechaber's position.

As practical law it is an unresolved dispute among the early authorities, and money cannot be removed from the possessor. One must say that the borrower did not use the language 'on condition that,' despite the opening of the seif, because several major authorities treat 'on condition' like 'from now' for asmachta. See siman 207:14 in Rama.
Some say, etc. Most authorities agree that there is no asmachta concern in this case. Mechaber's formulation as 'some say' therefore requires study, as I wrote in Tumim. One cannot simply say the practical law follows Rashi, because here Rashi is virtually a lone view.
