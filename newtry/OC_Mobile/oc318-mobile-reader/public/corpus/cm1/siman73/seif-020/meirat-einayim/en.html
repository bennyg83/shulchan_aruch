This wording does not imply, etc. The Tur writes that the borrower's claim that he understood the lender's words to mean he should pledge it at interest is merely an unexpressed thought and has no legal effect. He should not have relied on his own assumption that the lender would reimburse the interest. From the Tur and Mechaber we learn that if he explicitly told him to pledge it at interest, then clearly the borrower must pay the interest, and so the Mechaber rules in Yoreh Deah siman 169, seif 6. This is unlike the Levush. Maharshal likewise rules this way and questions the Kol Bo's citation in the name of Rabbi Hezekiah that the one who pledged it must pay the interest unless he told the non-Jew before any interest accrued that the collateral was not his but belonged to another. Such a statement appears unnecessary, because ordinarily the non-Jew relies upon the collateral itself. All this is specifically where the lender delivers the collateral into the non-Jew's possession, so the non-Jew relies on the collateral. Without collateral it is plainly prohibited, because the non-Jew is lending to the second Israelite and the second to the first, making the interest pass directly from borrower to lender. Rashba's responsum cited by the Beit Yosef concerns this latter case.
