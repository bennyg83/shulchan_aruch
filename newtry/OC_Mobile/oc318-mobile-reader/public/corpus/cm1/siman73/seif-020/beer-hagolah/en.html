It’s also in his name that he remembers
He explains there that what he says, that he understood from the lender's words that he should pledge it for interest, is merely an unspoken thought and has no legal effect. He should not have relied on his own understanding until the lender expressly instructed him. If the lender explicitly told him to pledge it for interest, the borrower must pay the interest, as written in Yoreh Deah siman 169 seif 17, provided that the gentile's recourse is only against the collateral and not personally against Shimon the lender, as explained there.
