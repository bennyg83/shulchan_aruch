This wording does not imply, etc. The Tur writes that the lender’s claim that he understood the agent would pledge the article to a non-Jew at interest is merely an unexpressed thought, and unexpressed thoughts have no legal effect. He should not have relied upon his assumption that the borrower would reimburse the interest. From the Tur and Mechaber it follows that if he expressly instructed him to pledge it at interest, the borrower plainly must pay the interest. The Mechaber rules this way in Yoreh Deah 169:26. The Sema adds that this is unlike the Levush. My teacher, the great scholar, wrote in the margin of his Sema that the Levush is correctly constructed and also clearly agrees that when the lender tells him to pledge it at interest, it is as though he expressly stated that he would pay the interest, for the Levush writes precisely as do the Tur and Mechaber. This is correct. The Sema and Bach also cite Maharshal’s objection to the Kol Bo in the name of Maharam. I explained in Siftei Kohen, Yoreh Deah 169:80, that there is no difficulty. See Responsa Maharam Alshich, siman 19; Responsa Mabit, part 1, siman 186; and Responsa Maharash Kohen, book 3, siman 101.
