Shach disagrees. Baal HaTerumot says only that according to authorities who think a creditor can never compel redemption of a land mortgage, Ramban proves that the creditor does have some eventual right to demand redemption. That does not show the same thirty-day term as movables. Land mortgages have their own rule: an unspecified mortgage normally lasts a year. Baal HaIttur and Shitah Mekubetzet citing Ramach explicitly distinguish the cases. Movable collateral can be forced to redemption after thirty days, but a land mortgage cannot necessarily be forced within the first year. This is logical. Many authorities give the borrower an especially strong position in a land mortgage. It would be strange to say the opposing view not only allows creditor enforcement but gives the creditor a stronger right than the borrower, allowing him to terminate within a year while the borrower cannot. Rashba's theory above in siman 67 complicates the matter because he says even one day of nonredemption can classify a mortgage as nonredeemable for certain laws. Under Rashba, the phrase "an unspecified mortgage is a year" may refer principally to places where redemption is contractually suspended. Even so, Rashba's position itself is disputed, and Mechaber elsewhere does not consistently follow it. For practical halachah the borrower can therefore say kim li like the authorities who preserve the one-year term and resist forced redemption during that period. There is a possible reconciliation for Beit Yosef and Rama. They may be speaking of a land mortgage without deduction of produce value, where the creditor receives no agreed economic benefit from holding the land. In such a case the annual mortgage term may be less significant, and after thirty days either side can demand settlement more readily. The classic one-year rule concerns a mortgage with deductions, where both parties entered a continuing produce arrangement. There the lender, like the borrower, should ordinarily remain bound during the term. A Sura-type mortgage is stronger still and resembles a fixed lease. Most authorities do not permit the lender to compel early redemption there. Thus the thirty-day rule should not be applied indiscriminately to every land mortgage.
