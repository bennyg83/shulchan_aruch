Some say that even after the loan has fallen due, the lender must wait thirty days after demanding payment before selling the collateral. {Rama: He need not demand payment in beit din. It is enough to demand before witnesses that the borrower pay him; he then retains the collateral for thirty days and sells it. Even a private demand between the two of them is effective if the borrower admits it. Some say that in a place where the custom is that one who lends to a non-Jew may not sell the collateral before a year has passed, the same rule applies to a Jew who lends another Jew money against collateral, for in this matter we follow local custom. If a person gave collateral to charity for what he owes, the charity administrator may sell it under the same law as an ordinary person who lent against collateral.}
