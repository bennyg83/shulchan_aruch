Even though the loan period passed, etc. This refers to an ordinary loan without a specifically fixed due date. If a fixed due date was set and passed, whether he is nevertheless given an additional thirty days depends upon the two opinions cited by the Mechaber below in siman 100:2. The same legal issue governs both cases. Sema.
Even if he demanded payment privately, etc. Witnesses are needed only against false denial. If the debtor admits that he was demanded to pay, why should witnesses be required? A litigant's admission has the force of one hundred witnesses. This resolves the apparent inconsistency in the Rosh's responsa, where sometimes he implies that the demand must be before witnesses and elsewhere that a private demand suffices. If the debtor does not deny the demand, a private demand is enough; if he denies it, witnesses are required. Sema.
We judge the same way among Jews. If the non Jewish custom of waiting applies specifically to collateral bearing interest, but not to interest free collateral, then among Jews the same custom applies only to a lawful profit bearing arrangement such as iska or the like. An ordinary interest free loan need not be delayed. See Shach. This is correct, unlike the Sema.
Among Jews. Here it appears that absent the non Jewish custom the lender could sell the collateral immediately after thirty days, while below in siman 369:8 it appears that absent a non Jewish custom permitting sale after a year he could not sell even after a year. The Sema gives a forced distinction. The principal resolution is the Shach's: there the case is one in which the lender never demanded payment at all, so without a custom permitting sale without demand he could never sell. Here the lender has demanded payment, so absent a contrary custom he could sell after thirty days. The Taz adds that if the lender already waited a full year and then demanded payment, he need not wait an additional thirty days after that demand. Both rulings are correct.
