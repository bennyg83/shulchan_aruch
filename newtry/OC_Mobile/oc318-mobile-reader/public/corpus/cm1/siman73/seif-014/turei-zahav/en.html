(Seif 16) “By expert beit din.” The distinction between an expert court and lay judges is explained by the Beit Yosef in the name of the Rivash. Experts are considered the sellers, whereas laymen are merely appraisers, leaving the creditor to sell on his own and creating suspicion. The Sema asks why the Rama at the beginning of siman 72 says that an expert court is effective to permit the creditor to use the pledge while deducting its value, and answers that three experts are not commonly assembled merely for rental. This is unconvincing. There, even lay judges should suffice, because the Rama permits use where the parties stipulated beforehand. As shown there, a stipulation made before use is enough, and even a stipulation before witnesses suffices. The glossator distinguishes a stipulation with the owner, who knowingly waives objections to the agreed use, from a court arrangement made without him. In the latter case the owner may suspect excessive or damaging use, and the court will not supervise continually. Rental to others is different because the creditor himself supervises the property. This suspicion does not apply to a completed sale. On that approach, even an expert court cannot authorize the creditor to rent it to himself, explaining why the Shulchan Aruch and Rama do not mention such permission.
