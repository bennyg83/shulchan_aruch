They appraise it and give it to him as payment. Without an appraisal and giving it to him expressly as payment, the borrower has not fulfilled his oath, because collateral remains in the owner's domain and was given only as security. Chazal permitted the appraisal on Shabbos so that he could fulfill his mitzvah, since this is considered a matter of Heaven. The Rosh concludes that it is no worse than arranging a child's match on Shabbos or hiring someone to teach him, even a trade, provided no sum of money is mentioned.
From this it appears that others may participate in the appraisal so that he can fulfill his obligation, just as arranging a child's match is done through others. It also appears that if the item requires measuring, it may be measured on Shabbos, analogous to measuring a belt for medical purposes or measuring a mikveh, which are permitted because they are matters of Heaven.And the Sabbatical year arrived in the interim, etc. See what I wrote above in siman 67.For whenever he is exempt under the law of payment, etc. This is unlike Rama's ruling in the next seif that one who swore to pay another must pay even where he was not otherwise legally obligated. There he knew he was not obligated and voluntarily accepted the obligation through an oath.
Even if he claims that he did not know he was exempt, we do not say the oath was mistaken, because one who swears is presumed to deliberate carefully. Here, however, he swore only to pay his existing debt, and the exemption arose afterward, comparable to a later waiver by the lender. In such a case we say he did not swear with that later circumstance in mind.
