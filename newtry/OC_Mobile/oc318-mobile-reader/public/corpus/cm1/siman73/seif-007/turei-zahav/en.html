The wording suggests liability depends on demand, and the Sema infers that even one who swore to pay on a certain date does not violate the oath until the creditor demands payment. This is difficult. A document maturing on Shabbos requires the debtor to provide a pledge that day, although the creditor surely does not demand payment on Shabbos. Also, the Beit Yosef cites the Rashba that if one swore to pay a first creditor in Adar whenever demanded and later swore to pay another then, the second oath can take effect until the first creditor demands. This implies that without the express condition “whenever you demand,” the first obligation exists even without demand. Maharalbach likewise cites the Rashba that one who swore to pay on a fixed date violates the oath if the creditor is in town and payment is not made, even without demand. The Rosh’s responsum here must involve an express condition, “Whenever you demand within the period, I will pay.” Otherwise the phrase would add nothing, since demand could occur at any moment before the fixed end.
