One who swears... is obligated. His intent was that the debt be paid by that time, and we follow the person's intent, as stated in Kiddushin 62 and as explained at the end of siman 61. The same applies to an oath, as in Bava Kamma and the case of one who vowed, etc.
He must. Money's worth is equivalent to money, as stated in Kiddushin 8a and elsewhere. Although repayment is ordinarily prohibited on Shabbos, that prohibition is rabbinic because of "pursuing your affairs," and in a case of a mitzvah it is permitted. This is no less than arranging a betrothal for a child, etc. The oath takes effect because the underlying prohibition is only rabbinic, as stated in Nazir 4a and Tosafos there, and in Shevuos 24a and Tosafos 23b.
They appraise it and he gives it... Giving a pledge is not itself payment, as shown in Shabbos 148a, "if he does not trust him..." whereas actual payment is prohibited there. See the Sema. Likewise Kiddushin 8b says, "There is no maneh here..." although betrothal and acquisition can occur with a perutah.
One who swore... Shemittah cancels the debt by operation of law and is as though the creditor forgave it. See Nedarim 63b and Gittin 36b, where Rava says that beit din may declare property ownerless. See Rashi there.
