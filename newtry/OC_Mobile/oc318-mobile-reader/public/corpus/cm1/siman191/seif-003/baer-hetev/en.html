In general. The Sema writes in the name of Maharshal that the rule that instruments intended merely as evidence do not themselves effect acquisition applies where acquisition is ordinarily made by deed. Where acquisition is made by money, however, even if a deed is also written, so that money alone does not complete the acquisition, the evidentiary deed can join the money and the acquisition is effective. See Responsa Rashach relating to part 2, siman 24.
