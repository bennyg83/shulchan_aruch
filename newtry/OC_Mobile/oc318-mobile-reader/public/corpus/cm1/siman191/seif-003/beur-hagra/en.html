Our documents, etc. Meaning that even though they do not say “I hereby sell” or “I hereby give,” but rather “I sold” and “I gave,” they are instruments of acquisition and not merely evidentiary documents. Thus in Gittin 40b: “Our Rabbis taught: one who says, ‘I gave, etc.’” and all these cases are by document. It must be for acquisition, for if it were merely an admission, why would a document be needed? Likewise the verse states, “I have given the money for the field, etc.”
And if he wrote for him, etc. This is unlike the Rashba cited by the Beis Yosef here and the gloss at the beginning of the laws of sale, who hold that even an evidentiary document effects acquisition. Their proof is from Bava Batra 51a, “Remove the money from here, etc.” But this is not conclusive, for the Gemara should then have said that the case is in a place where documents are not ordinarily written. Rather, one must explain as Tosafos does that they were in a place where documents are ordinarily written, and it may be that their documents were themselves instruments of acquisition.
