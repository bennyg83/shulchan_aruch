He does not acquire until he gives him the money. Maharit, Even HaEzer siman 95, cites Mahari ben Lev's doubt whether, if they stipulate that the deed alone shall acquire, it works even where the seller is not selling because the field is inferior, just as money without a deed can work in that special case in siman 190, seif 8. He also asks whether money without a deed might work in a sale of inferior land even without express stipulation. Maharit resolves that money alone does not work. When the obstacle is a deed without money, the seller ordinarily lacks confidence before receiving the price, and one can say that with an inferior field he fully resolves to transfer. But when the obstacle is money without a deed, the buyer is the one who lacks confidence. In a sale of an inferior field he is even more particular, because his money leaves his hand while he receives not even a document. Maharit leaves unresolved whether an express condition makes the deed alone sufficient. In my view Maggid Mishneh and Beit Yosef's categorical ruling that the condition works is not displaced by Maharit's doubt. His proofs are not compelling for our halachah. From the beginning of HaOmanin, where Rabban Shimon ben Gamliel says that the parties are taught a method preventing retraction, one cannot prove that they should instead be taught to stipulate that the kinyan alone is sufficient, because even after any kinyan the sale can still be canceled when the seller repeatedly demands the unpaid price. They must therefore be taught to convert the balance into a loan. Nor is there proof from kinyan sudar, since we rule that kinyan sudar does work without payment. Ran at the end of Mi SheHayah Nasui likewise does not prove otherwise, since his statement that they converted the price into a loan need not be exclusive. If the seller converted the price into a loan, the deed certainly works without present payment. Maharit further rules that if the buyer swore to uphold the purchase effected by deed alone, this is equivalent to an express stipulation.
