Our deeds. See Nachalat Shivah for the form of a deed of sale customary among us. See Responsa Beit Ephraim, Choshen Mishpat, siman 6, who writes: I have long wondered about the wording of our deeds of sale, in which we write, "We, the married couple, have sold," and so forth. What legal force is added by the wife's participation in such a sale? She has no ownership interest in the body of the land, but only a lien for her ketubah. Even if a creditor purported to sell property subject to his lien, the sale would be ineffective, because we rule that a creditor acquires the debtor's property only from the time of collection onward. The statement that one "purchased from the wife" means that he first acquired from her the release of her right and lien against that land, as explained by Rambam, Hilchot Ishut, chapter 7, halachah 17, cited by Beit Yosef in Even HaEzer at the end of siman 90 in Bedek HaBayit, who writes that "he purchased from the wife" does not mean that she sold the property to him, but that the removal of her lien is termed a purchase from her. Although Tosafos in Ketubot 95a indicates that the concept of sale applies to her, that can be explained as referring to nichsei tzon barzel, since those assets are at least designated for her. Although her sale of nichsei tzon barzel is ineffective on its own, it becomes effective when the husband consents. But the current practice is to write this language in every deed of sale, even when the husband sells his own land over which the wife never had possession and against which she has only a lien. In that case, the language that she "sells" is difficult to understand. He discusses this at length and ultimately explains it on the basis of Rambam, Hilchot Mechirah, chapter 10, who writes that just as a slave who buys or sells may have the transaction ratified, so too a wife who sells or gives away property, whether her husband's property or nichsei tzon barzel, if the husband wishes to ratify the transaction he may do so. It follows that the wife's sale can be effective even with respect to the husband's property, even if she does not conduct the household's business, provided that the husband later consents, and even verbal consent suffices. Accordingly, the wording of our deeds is sound: the term "sale" properly applies to the wife as well, because the husband did not annul her act but instead ratified it, and her sale therefore becomes a valid sale. These are his words. See Nachalat Tzvi for what he writes on this. Beit Ephraim further discusses the customary wording of our deeds, which states that both husband and wife accept liability, as in the clause, "And the couple said to us, the liability of this deed," and so forth. He considers whether, if the purchaser is later dispossessed of the land, he may collect reimbursement from the wife. He cites Beit Shmuel, Even HaEzer, siman 93, seif katan 83, who writes that according to the current practice of writing a liability clause and making a kinyan first from the wife and afterward from the husband, this itself proves that the liability clause in the deed applies only to the husband and not to the wife. If the liability applied to both, they could make the kinyan from the husband first, as explained there in Shulchan Aruch, because where the wife accepted liability she cannot claim that she acted merely to please her husband. Rather, the kinyan made with the wife serves only to prevent her from later challenging the sale and to remove her own lien, not to impose general liability upon her, unless that is explicitly stated. The Taz rules likewise. According to this reasoning, in our deeds, where liability is expressly imposed on the wife as well as the husband, she too should be obligated to reimburse the purchaser. The Taz there, however, questions this, because the clause may have been written without her actual consent and merely as an embellishment of the deed. Beit Ephraim discusses the issue at length and concludes that the matter requires great study. In his view one must investigate the local custom, because we have not heard of collecting reimbursement from the wife in such a case. See there carefully:
