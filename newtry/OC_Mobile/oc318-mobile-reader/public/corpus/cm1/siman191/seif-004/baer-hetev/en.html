With money. The Darkei Moshe also writes this in the name of the Beit Yosef, but I searched and did not find that the Beit Yosef wrote so. On the contrary, at the end of simanim 190 and 192 he explicitly cites Tosafot that with money one acquires even without saying "from now." So too Tosafot in Yevamot 93, Ketubot 82, and Kiddushin 63 state that money acquires without "from now." Shach. Since, if the sale did not take effect, the money would have to be returned to him, it is regarded as though still extant. Nimukei Yosef in the first chapter of Bava Metzia says this explicitly. One can answer with difficulty that "the money was lost" means that it was lost in a manner in which he can no longer recover it from the other party. So too the Ran in Kiddushin, chapter HaOmer, the glosses at the end of HaIshah Shenaflu, and Mordechai in HaOmer. The Rama himself makes this distinction in Even HaEzer at the beginning of siman 40, based on the Maggid Mishneh in the name of Ramban and Rashba, whom the Beit Yosef cites there. Shach. If we emend the text to read "with the deed and the money," it is well explained. The latter clause, that if he did not say "from now" he did not acquire, can then refer to a place where both money and deed are required and the deed was lost. The Darkei Moshe indeed says explicitly "and the deed was lost" and does not mention the money. In the first clause he states the greater novelty that even if the money too was lost, he nevertheless acquired because he said "from now." This is the Sema's conclusion. See what the Taz writes on this.
Possession. The same applies to acquisition by exchange. This is stated by Tosafot in Yevamot, chapter HaIshah Rabbah, and Kiddushin, chapter HaOmer, and by the Ran and the Rosh at the end of chapter 5 of Nedarim, and by the Tur and Shulchan Aruch in siman 195. This is contrary to Mordechai in HaOmer and the Ran, who write that exchange is like monetary acquisition and does not require "from now." See also the end of siman 197. Shach.
