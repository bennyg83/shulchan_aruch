He acquired, etc. From Kesubos 110a concerning a place where they give, etc. If it were otherwise, he should have claimed that the purchase money had not previously been given to him. Likewise Bava Batra 6a, “Come and hear: from four cubits and upward, etc.” Why not? There too it is analogous to a sale. And there, 154b, “that I sold to you and not, etc.” If the document is authenticated, according to all opinions he is not believed. This appears at first glance to apply even in a place where documents are first written. The Rashba writes likewise, though the proof can be rejected.
He sold to him, etc. As stated in Gittin 74a and Kiddushin 60b, where they challenge from “This is your get on condition, etc.” Rabbi Yochanan answers, “That case is Rabbi’s view, etc.”
(Collected note) He sold to him, etc. See Tosafos, Bava Metzia 16a, s.v. “acquired, etc.” (End of note.)
Or with money. None of the poskim wrote this. See Kiddushin 59a: “Likewise one who says, etc.; it did not come, etc.” There the case is where he did not say “from now.” See seif 18.
The same law applies to an act of acquisition, etc. All these resemble a document and are unlike money, because money must be returned, as Tosafos writes. The same is found regarding meshichah in Kesubos 82a and a kinyan sudar at the end of chapter 5 of Nedarim 48b, “and furthermore, the sudar, etc.” The Rosh rules likewise there. This explains Yevamos 93a: “This field, etc., is acquired from now.” Even according to the view that a thing not yet in existence can be acquired, “from now” is still necessary, because the ordinary acquisition of land is by kinyan sudar, document, or chazakah; money does not acquire where documents are customarily written. See Tosafos there. Since the poskim state the matter without qualification, one must say that regarding other acts of acquisition Rav and Rabbi Yochanan agree with Shmuel.
