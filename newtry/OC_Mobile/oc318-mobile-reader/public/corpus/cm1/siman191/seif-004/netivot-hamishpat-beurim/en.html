Or the money was lost. See Sema, seif katan 9, who explains the case as one where there is no way to collect the money again. Taz objects that since the money was consumed under the status of kiddushin, the woman is nevertheless betrothed. Ketzot HaChoshen says the case is where the money was lost through ones and she is legally exempt from returning it. In my view the issue depends on an earlier dispute. Ran at the beginning of HaOmer in Kiddushin writes that even though the money no longer exists when the kiddushin takes effect, she originally committed herself by means of the money given to her; this reasoning works even if it was later lost through ones. Rashba, however, explains that even though the money is no longer in existence, because if the acquisition does not take effect she would have to return it, we view it as though present and she is betrothed through the benefit of that monetary obligation, like kiddushin through benefit of a loan. According to Rashba, if the money was lost in a manner for which she is exempt, there is no such benefit and the kiddushin does not take effect. Ketzot questions whether retraction makes her liable for ones as a robber. This is certainly incorrect. Even one who buys movable property with money and then retracts is exempt from accidental loss of the money. Tosafot in Bava Metzia 43 say that, were it not for the special rule that money must be guarded underground, he could say, "Your money burned." Maharit likewise writes that he can be exempt even from negligence. If he actually used the money, however, it unquestionably became a loan and he must repay it. Mechaber here speaks where the money was still intact and was then lost.
