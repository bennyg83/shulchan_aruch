Or on pottery, etc. Although pottery and a leaf are materials on which the writing can be altered, and for that reason a promissory note may not be written on them even for immediate collection, as the Tur and the Mechaber write above at the beginning of siman 42, there is a distinction. There we derive from Scripture that the document must be fit to endure for many days. A deed of acquisition, by contrast, need not endure; it is sufficient that it effect the acquisition at that moment.
"My field is given to you," "My field is sold to you." Meaning, or if he wrote, "My field is sold to you," each according to its context. Rabbeinu writes below in siman 245 at length about acquisition of a field given as a gift through a deed, because that is its proper place. With a gift there is no distinction between inferior and superior land; in every case the recipient acquires through the deed. The Mechaber and the Tur mention it here only incidentally because of the law of "my field is sold to you," and they employ the wording of the baraita.
Even if there are no witnesses upon it at all. Witnesses were instituted only against false denial, so that the party cannot deny the matter, as the Tur and the Mechaber write in siman 189.
