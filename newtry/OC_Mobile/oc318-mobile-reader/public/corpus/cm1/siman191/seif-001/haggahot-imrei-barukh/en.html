(Mechaber, seif 1) Once the document reaches his hand, he acquires. Note: Beit Meir, Even HaEzer 133, leaves this ruling in difficulty. See the Bach here, cited by the Shach above in siman 42, seif katan 2, who explains that the case concerns a document written in the grantor's own handwriting, which has the force of witnesses. The Sema, seif katan 1, appears not to understand the case as involving the grantor's handwriting, because if it were his handwriting the document could not be forged. The Bach writes that the Sema must understand the case as one with signed witnesses but without witnesses to delivery. On that reading, the objections of the Makneh and Beit Meir disappear. See also Tumim, siman 68, seif katan 8, who explains that Tosafot in Gittin 10b, passage beginning "a shard," when saying that it is valid by the grantor's admission because his admission is like one hundred witnesses, must be discussing a document signed by the grantor himself.
