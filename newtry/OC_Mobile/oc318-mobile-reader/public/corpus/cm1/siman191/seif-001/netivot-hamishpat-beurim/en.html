Ketzot HaChoshen writes that just as the paper itself must belong to the transferor, because the Gemara regarding gittin requires "the transferor's document," so too the scribe's fee must be paid by the transferor, like a get. Since we rule that the buyer pays the scribe's fee, Ketzot concludes that this works only for an evidentiary deed. With a conveyancing deed the seller must pay, or the buyer must transfer the scribe's fee to him. In my view only the paper and ink must belong to the transferor, because that is what "the transferor's document" means. The requirement that the husband pay the scribe's fee for a get is derived from "and he shall write," meaning the husband must cause it to be written. Nimukei Yosef similarly says that the husband pays because the Torah says "and he shall write." That verse does not apply to sale or gift. Therefore, there is no requirement that the scribe's fee in a sale or gift come from the transferor. Even if the writing service comes from another person, the document itself remains the seller's, just as if Reuven dyes Shimon's wool with Reuven's dye, the wool does not leave Shimon's ownership; at most Shimon owes the value of the dye. The principle that a craftsman acquires through improvement does not apply to the writing here, as I explained in Even HaEzer siman 120. Ramban, however, in Bava Batra 164 writes that even in a sale the Sages transferred the scribe's fee to the seller through their enactment.
