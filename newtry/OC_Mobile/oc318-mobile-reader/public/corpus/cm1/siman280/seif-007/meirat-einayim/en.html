One who died leaving a son and a tumtum, etc. The Mechaber's words from here through the end of seif 9 are taken from the beginning of chapter 5 of Hilchot Nachalot, though the Mechaber abbreviates. The Rambam begins with the general rule: whenever there are two potential heirs, one certain and one doubtful, the doubtful heir receives nothing; if both are doubtful, they divide equally. He then gives two examples. The first is the law written by the Mechaber in seif 7, illustrating a certain heir against a doubtful one. The second is seif 8, illustrating two doubtful heirs. Where there is no son, the doubt is whether the daughter is the heir or whether the tumtum or androgynos is male and therefore heir. After these two examples the Rambam writes that the laws of maintenance for daughters among sons have already been explained in Hilchot Ishut. When the estate is ample, daughters receive maintenance and sons inherit the remainder. When the estate is small, daughters receive maintenance and the sons receive nothing. He therefore continues with the case of one who dies leaving daughters together with a tumtum or androgynos, which the Mechaber states in seif 9. From this structure it is clear that the first clause, "he left a son and a tumtum, the son inherits everything," speaks specifically of an ample estate. The Rambam abbreviated there because his immediate subject was inheritance, teaching that the certain heir prevails over the doubtful heir, while he relied on the later clause for the separate maintenance law. Likewise, the rule in seif 8, that daughters and a tumtum divide equally, speaks specifically of an ample estate. In the later clause the Rambam begins with "sons, daughters, tumtum, and androgynos" but concludes by saying that the tumtum is pushed aside. "Tumtum" there is not exclusive. The same applies to an androgynos according to the Rambam, because he treats both alike. The Mishnah itself mentions only tumtum, and the Rambam added androgynos because he holds it to be a distinct creature and certainly no better than a tumtum. The Rosh and Tur disagree. Since they regard androgynos as a distinct creature, his position with a son is weaker than that of a tumtum, whether the estate is ample or small. Rama's gloss beginning "some say a daughter and tumtum, etc." belongs after seif 9, not after seif 8, because it qualifies both seifim 8 and 9. According to all this, when the Mechaber says the son inherits everything, he means that the son holds the estate and provides maintenance to the tumtum or androgynos from it. He need not deposit their maintenance with beit din or a trustee.
