(Seif 4, Shimon inherits nothing from him.) This requires study in light of Yevamot 37. A doubtful son and a yavam came to divide the yavam’s estate after the yavam had previously divided the deceased brother’s estate. The yavam’s sons said to the doubtful son, “Bring proof that you are our brother and take a share.” The doubtful son replied: “Whichever way you view it, if I am your brother, give me an equal share with you; and if I am the deceased’s son, give me half of what your father divided with me.” Rabbi Abba says that the prior judgment stands. Rashi explains that once judgment was rendered concerning the deceased’s estate, the doubtful son cannot reopen it; regarding what he seeks from the yavam’s estate, we tell him to bring proof. Rabbi Yirmiyah says that the judgment is reopened, meaning that they return half the deceased’s estate or divide the entire estate anew. We rule like Rabbi Abba, as the authorities state there. If so, here too we should say that the prior judgment stands. Why should Shimon inherit nothing after Levi, the doubtful relative, already received a portion of Reuven’s estate by virtue of Reuven’s admission that Levi was his brother? Why may Shimon not say, “The prior judgment stands regarding what you took from Reuven’s portion”? The ruling is even more difficult in light of the responsum of Terumat HaDeshen cited by the Rama in siman 268, seif 3: once judgment was rendered concerning the first event, the second event is considered separately. According to this, why does Shimon not take? Judgment had already been rendered initially that Levi takes nothing from Reuven’s portion, leaving the later matter alone. I wrote above in siman 268 what appears correct regarding this issue.
