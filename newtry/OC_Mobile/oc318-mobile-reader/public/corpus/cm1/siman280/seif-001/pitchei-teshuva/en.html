Or my brothers. Misgeret HaShulchan, Ateret Tzvi, and Maamar Kedushin cite Responsa Avodat HaGershuni 110. Brothers were known to have another brother in a distant country. A man arrived claiming to be that brother and sought a share in their father's estate. They said they did not recognize him. He produced witnesses who said they recognized him by his voice as their brother but not by his face, because he had since grown a beard. The responsum ruled that this testimony is insufficient. Although Chullin teaches that voice recognition can be relied upon in ritual law just as facial recognition can, otherwise a blind man could never be permitted to his wife, that principle applies only where there is no defect weakening the voice identification. Here the witnesses see the claimant yet do not recognize his appearance. That discrepancy undermines the reliability of their voice recognition. See also what I wrote above, siman 81:13:3.
