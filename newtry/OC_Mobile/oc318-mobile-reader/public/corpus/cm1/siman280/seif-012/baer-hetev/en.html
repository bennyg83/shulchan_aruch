Against him. Meaning, against the son. The father's heirs say that the son died first and inherited nothing, and therefore the creditor or wife cannot collect from these assets, since they were only property that the son was expected to inherit and they collect only from property actually possessed by him. The creditor or wife, however, say that the father died first and his son inherited him, so that the assets became possessed by the son and collection may be made from them. So writes the Sema, and this is also the Tur's explanation. The Shach writes that the Sema follows his own ruling at the end of siman 104. According to the opinions cited there that a creditor may collect from property merely prospective, however, the reason here must be that the heirs can say, 'We inherit directly from our grandfather.' Alternatively, the heirs here are the son's brothers and their decedent is the father's brother. This follows the Gemara in the chapter Mi Shemet and Rashbam's explanation there, where they say that Reuven died first and afterward the other died. See there. A woman's ketubah, however, cannot be collected in any event, because we rule that a ketubah is not collected from property merely prospective, as written in Even HaEzer siman 100, seif 2. Shach.
The heirs. We leave the property in its established possession, for it had belonged to the father. Moreover, they are definite heirs: even if the father died first, they are also the son's heirs. The wife and creditor, by contrast, have a right only if the father died first, and an uncertain claim cannot displace a definite one. Sema.
