In the gloss: the money is divided into thirty-six parts. I am puzzled. Above, siman 280, in the case where a house fell on a man and his married daughter, we rule that the husband does not inherit in the relevant doubt. It is strained to distinguish merely because the chazakah is uncertain whether it belongs to the husband's family or the wife's. The reason for dividing between the husband's heirs and the wife's heirs is that both sides are treated as holding the nichsei tzon barzel in a form of possession. Our case also concerns nichsei tzon barzel. If both decedents are treated as jointly holding, why should the husbands of the daughters inherit? It appears more likely that Mordechai disagrees with the earlier ruling of seif 10 and follows the alternative reasoning I wrote there. Rama's citation of both therefore requires study.
