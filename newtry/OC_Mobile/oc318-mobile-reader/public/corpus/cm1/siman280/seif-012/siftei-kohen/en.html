And it is not known, etc. The heirs of the father say that the son died first and therefore inherited nothing, so these assets cannot be collected, because if the son died during his father's lifetime they are payable only from property already in his possession and not from property merely expected to come to him. This is the Sema's language, and the Tur likewise writes this, following his position at the end of siman 104. But according to what I wrote there, that some poskim hold that a creditor may collect from property that was merely expected to come to the debtor, the reason here is instead that the heirs say, "We come by inheritance directly from the grandfather," as explained there. Alternatively, "the heirs" refers to the father's brothers, who are the deceased's paternal uncles. This is the case explained in Perek Mi Shemet by Rashbam: they say that Reuven died first and afterward Yaakov, his father, died, and we inherited all the property of our father. Reuven, our brother, has no share in it, because he died first and had no son. In that case they certainly do not inherit through Reuven, because they too are independently eligible to inherit Yaakov just as he was. Similarly, the deceased's paternal uncles, meaning Yaakov's brother Esav, Reuven's uncle, may inherit independently. In a case where a house fell upon a person and upon the relative from whom he was to inherit, such as Esav, the brother of Yaakov his father, the heirs of that relative, such as Esav's brothers, say that Reuven died first and Esav afterward, and therefore we inherit all of Esav's property. Reuven son of Yaakov does not inherit the property of his uncle Esav in place of Yaakov his father, who had already died, because Reuven died first and Esav afterward. But if neither of these explanations applies, such as where property fell to him after his death from his maternal grandfather and his paternal brothers cannot inherit except through him, then a creditor may collect from that property according to the opinion that a creditor collects from property that was merely expected to come to the debtor, as I wrote there. A wife's ketubah, however, cannot be collected from such property under any circumstances, because we rule that a ketubah is not collected from property merely expected to come to the husband, as explained in Even HaEzer, siman 100, seif 2.
