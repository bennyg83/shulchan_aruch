Shimon says, This Levi is not my brother. Rambam rules that Shimon's admission deprives him of a share in Levi's estate. Mishneh LaMelech asks why Reuven's contrary admission, that Levi is their brother, does not give Shimon a share. My brother explained that opposing admissions can be treated like two equal testimonies. Reuven's admission supports Shimon, while Shimon's own admission excludes him. The result is uncertainty. Reuven, however, is certainly an heir, while Shimon is only doubtful. A doubtful heir does not remove property from a certain heir. Therefore the estate remains with Reuven.
