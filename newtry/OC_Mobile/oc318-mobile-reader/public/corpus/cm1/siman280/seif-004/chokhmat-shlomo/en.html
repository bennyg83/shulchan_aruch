Seif 7. One who died and left a son and a tumtum, etc. Likewise, in every case of certainty and doubt, a doubtful claimant cannot dislodge one who has a certain claim. Note: The source of this ruling is the Rambam, and it requires consideration in light of what the Rambam writes in Hilchot Ma'aseh HaKorbanot 10:22: if a kohen became impure after the sprinkling, he immerses and is pure even at the time the fats are burned; nevertheless, it is doubtful whether he receives a share, and therefore, if he seized a share, it is not removed from him. This is difficult. Why do we not say there as well that the other kohanim certainly have a share in the offerings, whereas his claim is doubtful, and a doubtful claimant cannot dislodge one with a certain claim? It may be resolved according to Tosafot at the beginning of Bava Metzia. Regarding the case where one says, 'Half is mine,' and the other says, 'All of it is mine,' Tosafot ask why we do not apply this principle. They answer that in the case of a yavam, the yavam is certainly an heir, whereas here the fact that one is certainly entitled to one half does not make him certainly entitled to the other half. So too here, the impure kohen is certainly a kohen and ordinarily has a share in all offerings. Only with respect to this particular offering is there doubt whether his impurity excludes him. Thus, although the other kohanim are certainly entitled to one portion, that does not make them certain claimants to his doubtful portion. Understand this well.
