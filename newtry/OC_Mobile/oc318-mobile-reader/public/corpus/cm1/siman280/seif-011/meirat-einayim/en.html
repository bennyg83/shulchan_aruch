And it is not known which death will begin with the column if the father died, his daughter's son, and he will leave his relatives with his father's father's father's brother, and if his son dies, the property of the father's heirs who are not the son of his father's heir to his father's death:
The father's heirs and, etc., divide. These authorities do not say here that we leave the property in its original possession, namely with the father's side, as in the preceding case where a house fell upon a mother and her son. The difference is that here the house fell upon a man and his daughter's son. Even if the daughter's son died first and afterward the grandfather died, the estate does not move directly from the grandfather to his collateral heirs. Inheritance first descends to the daughter. Even if she is already dead, every inheritance first travels down through descendants. Only when the descendant has died without seed does the inheritance return upward. Thus in every scenario the property leaves the grandfather's immediate possession and passes through the daughter. The doubt is whether it then descended from her to her son, if the grandfather died first while the grandson was still alive, in which case the property may pass from the grandson to his paternal heirs, or whether it returns upward to the grandfather's relatives because the grandson died first. Since the inheritance necessarily leaves the grandfather's immediate line before the ultimate recipient is known, they divide. This differs from the mother and son case. If the son died first and the mother afterward, the inheritance never comes to the son in the grave so as to pass to his paternal relatives. In the grandfather and daughter case, even if the daughter and all her descendants died before the grandfather, the inheritance can conceptually pass through the daughter in the grave and then return to the grandfather's heirs because she has no other eligible heirs. The Rosh states this in responsum. The Beit Yosef cites it with a scribal error, and the Ir Shushan's explanation is deeply mistaken. He says that if the grandfather died first the inheritance goes to the daughter in the grave and then returns upward rather than downward to her son because a son does not inherit his mother in the grave to transmit to paternal brothers. That is plainly wrong. If the grandfather died first while the daughter's son was alive, the inheritance passes from the daughter to her living son, and only later, upon his death, to his heirs. The correct distinction is as I have written.
