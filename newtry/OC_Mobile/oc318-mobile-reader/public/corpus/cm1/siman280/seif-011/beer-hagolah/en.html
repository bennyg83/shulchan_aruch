This is law 7 there, from the baraita on 159b. The Maggid Mishneh writes that Rambam equated the case of a collapsed house with the case where persons were captured and each died, one in captivity and one in the province, and it is unknown who died first. It is obvious that they are equivalent. Yet one must wonder how Rambam ruled like this baraita after ruling regarding a collapsed house like Rabbi Akiva, that the property remains in the possession of the mother's heirs. This would seem all the more true regarding the property of the maternal grandfather. The Rashba likewise raised this difficulty. Nevertheless, they did not wish to dispute Rambam in practical halachah. The Kesef Mishneh writes that the Rosh, responsa klal 84 siman 3, resolved the difficulty: here, in any event, the inheritance passes from the father to the daughter. Although it remains within that tribe, once it has left the possession of the one who held it until now, there is doubt whether it descends or ascends, and therefore they divide it.
The Maggid Mishneh writes there that this is the stated law, although the Rashba challenged it at length. He concludes that nevertheless he did not wish to dispute Rambam in practical halachah.
