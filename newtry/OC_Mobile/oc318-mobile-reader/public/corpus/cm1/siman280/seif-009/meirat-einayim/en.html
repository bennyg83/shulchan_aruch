If the estate is abundant, etc. The law of an abundant or limited estate is explained in Even HaEzer siman 112. An abundant estate is one that, at the father's death, contains enough to support the daughters together with the sons until the daughters reach bogeret status. The sons then inherit the entire estate, take possession of the funds, and support the daughters until they mature. If at the father's death there is not enough for this, it is called a limited estate. The daughters take the assets needed for their support and are maintained from them, while if the sons are left without enough they must seek charity.
A tumtum takes three quarters. The Tur's language is: he takes half in any event, and of the other half perhaps he is male and should receive it all; that half is therefore money in doubt and is divided.
The daughter takes one quarter. This applies where that quarter suffices for her support until she becomes a bogeret. Otherwise, even if the tumtum is a son, he must still support her until maturity. Nevertheless, he need not place more than one quarter in her hand initially; when that quarter is exhausted, he supports her at his table until she matures. This is why the Mechaber says the son takes three quarters.
The tumtum receives nothing. Since the daughters certainly have the right of support while he is in doubt whether he is male, the burden of proof rests on him.
They divide equally. Since in an abundant estate the daughters have no inheritance in the estate itself, only support, they do not receive more than the androgynos, who may be a son. He likewise does not receive more than the daughter because of his doubt.
The androgynos receives nothing. He is doubtful while the daughter is certain, like the tumtum above.
The tumtum takes three quarters. Half he takes in any event, and regarding the other half perhaps he is female and should take it all; that half is therefore money in doubt and is divided.
He receives nothing in the presence of a son, etc. The Tur holds that an androgynos is an independent creature and is not included in the Sages' enactment to support daughters from an abundant estate, nor in the rule that the son must seek charity where the estate is limited.
