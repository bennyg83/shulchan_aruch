Immediately. Meaning from the point that it becomes clear to him that the matter causes damage. By contrast, when a window is blocked, we presume that the damage became clear to him immediately. Sema. See the Beurim. Mere seeing is insufficient. We require that, according to the assessment of beit din, he understood that this conduct harmed him. In the case of opening a window, the possessor must say that the owner repeatedly saw him through the window while he was using his property, to the point that beit din concludes that the owner knowingly waived his objection. See the Beurim. This avoids a difficulty from cases where a person causes damage in front of another and the injured party remains silent, yet the damager must still pay. Likewise, if one consumes another's produce in front of him, or the owner says, "Eat with me," or, "Live with me," payment may still be required:
He has established chazakah. Even according to the Rif, who holds that there is no chazakah for visual damage, that refers to a window overlooking a courtyard, where the use is fixed. From a roof overlooking a courtyard, and certainly for use of a projecting beam, chazakah can be established. Sema:
We require three years. See the Beurim. Even if he did not actually use the projecting beam throughout all three years, it can nevertheless establish chazakah. If the beam projects less than a handbreadth, even actual use does not establish chazakah:
And if he did not protest. For a projection less than a handbreadth, the owner of the projection cannot remove it whenever he wishes while the courtyard owner wishes it to remain. With a projection of a full handbreadth, however, the owner of the projection may also remove it. Shach:
