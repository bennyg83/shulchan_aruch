Since the authorities dispute whether raising the structure counts as increasing the damage, the owner may raise it. A person is forbidden to damage another, but so long as the neighbor has not established that this act damages him, his objection is not accepted. This is not comparable to the Rashba cited by the Beit Yosef in siman 154 regarding the dispute whether a kinyan is effective to waive smoke or a privy nuisance. There, if the nuisance was already established we do not compel its removal, while if one comes initially to create it we do not permit him. Although the person seeking the new act is considered the one seeking to alter the status quo and bears the burden of proof, there the damage itself is definite and he seeks a right to maintain it by virtue of the kinyan. Since the kinyan is doubtful, we preserve the existing right to object. Here, by contrast, the doubt concerns whether there is damage at all. Responsa Maharit, part 2, siman 75, challenges Mahari ben Lev, arguing that in his case the damage from a drain onto the neighbor's courtyard is definite; the claimant relies upon an existing chazakah for the drain and now wants to raise it. Since there is doubt whether the chazakah also includes this increase, perhaps he may not raise it, just as in the Rashba's case. Rabbi Akiva Eiger answers that Mahari ben Lev's reasoning is that the existing right is definite: he certainly acquired the place for the drain and the right for its water to flow there. He is therefore acting within his own established right, while the added height is only doubtfully harmful. See there, and see what I wrote below, siman 155, seif 19, seif katan 4.
