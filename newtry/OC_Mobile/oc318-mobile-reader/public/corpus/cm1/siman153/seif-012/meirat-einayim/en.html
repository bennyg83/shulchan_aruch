And he acquired from Reuven that Reuven would receive, etc. Here the language is "acquired from him" rather than "established possession against him," because a ruin does not ordinarily give rise to presumptive easement rights merely through use, as the Tur and Mechaber state in siman 154:16.
And the water from Shimon's roof, which he acquired from Reuven that Reuven's roof would receive, etc. According to the printed wording, this refers back to the preceding statement that he restored the water of his own roof to Shimon's courtyard as originally arranged, and then also restored the runoff from Shimon's roof, which Shimon had acquired a right to discharge onto Reuven's roof, so that both now flow into Shimon's courtyard. This wording is difficult, because if Reuven's roof is the receiving place, the text should say that the water is received "upon it," and because the acquired right was actually to discharge into Reuven's ruin rather than onto his roof. The better reading is therefore: "and the water of Shimon's roof, which he acquired from Reuven a right to discharge, falls upon Reuven's roof and returns to Shimon's courtyard together with the water of the roof," or language to that effect. This accords with the Tur.

But one who pours wastewater within his own property through a sewer, etc., since he pours within his own domain. The Mechaber's precise wording at both the beginning and end teaches that the permission depends upon the fact that the act of pouring itself takes place within his own property. If he poured elsewhere, even though the water later left through a sewer, it would not necessarily be permitted. This differs from the altered wording of the Ir Shushan.
