He pours wastewater on his own property through a drain. Since the pouring begins on his own property, it is permitted. In the first case, by contrast, the pouring begins in his fellow's courtyard and is therefore prohibited. Sema. See Beurim for the rationale. The primary explanation appears to be that the rule applies specifically to a drain built jointly by the owners of the courtyards, so that all of them are obligated to repair it when it breaks. Therefore, even if they originally stated that it was intended for rainwater, they may also pour laundry water into it. But if one person constructed a drain into his fellow's courtyard and acquired the right through waiver or sale, he may not pour more than the right acquired, because perhaps no more was waived or sold. If there was no permanent drain at all and he merely poured wastewater into his fellow's courtyard, he acquires no chazakah, because without a permanent structure there is no chazakah. See Beurim.
