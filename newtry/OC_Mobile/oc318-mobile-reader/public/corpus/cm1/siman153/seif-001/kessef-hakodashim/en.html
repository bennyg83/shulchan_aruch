Seif 16. (He established use at the place of the beam.) The rule in siman 153 concerning a prescriptive right to rest a beam upon another person's wall applies to a beam serving as part of a ceiling or similar permanent structure. It certainly does not follow that one acquires a right to enlarge a different use merely because a small beam was previously tolerated, such as a support for a pipe. The same is true of a conduit carrying water to a mill or to a distilling operation. Possession of the existing use does not automatically create a right to increase the burden in any degree. Regarding the two opinions in the Shulchan Aruch concerning the scope of the right acquired through a beam, one might invoke the principle that a doubtful claimant cannot displace a certain owner, as in the classic case of a doubtful heir against a certain heir. Tosafot and the commentators discuss this principle in the opening chapter of Bava Metzia. Still, the Shulchan Aruch records both opinions because there are cases where a definite claim by one party against the other's doubtful position is not resolved merely by saying that certainty defeats doubt. One could distinguish the classic inheritance case, where the doubt is objective for everyone, from a dispute in which the claimant himself asserts with certainty that he purchased a broader right. Tosafot, however, indicate that the principle can sometimes apply even against a definite claim. Another distinction is that inheritance passes all at once, whereas separate purchases of rights do not necessarily cause a partial acquisition to imply acquisition of the whole. In the present beam case, however, the wall and land indisputably belong to the wall owner. He also holds the prior ownership presumption against the person using the beam. Even if it is known that the wall owner purchased the site from that person, there is no reason to presume that the original transaction contained an unproven condition granting broad structural rights. The wall owner's position is therefore the stronger certain one.
