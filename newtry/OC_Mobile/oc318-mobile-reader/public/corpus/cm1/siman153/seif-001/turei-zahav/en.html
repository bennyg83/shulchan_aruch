(Seif 3) To build beneath the projection. The Sema's explanation in seif katan 6 is strained in my view. First, the phrase “beneath the projection” does not imply removing the projection, just as in seif 8, “to build beneath the drainpipe,” clearly does not mean that he may remove the drainpipe. Second, if removal were intended, what is meant by “and nullify its use,” which implies that the projection remains and only its use is nullified? There is also proof from the Gemara according to Kesef Mishneh's explanation of Rambam, but this is not the place to elaborate. It appears to me that the words should be understood literally. The owner of the courtyard has no right in the projection itself, even when it is less than a handbreadth wide. He may, however, build a structure beneath it, up to a point close to the projection, thereby preventing its use. By contrast, where the projection is a handbreadth wide, its owner has acquired a right in the airspace of the courtyard beneath it, meaning according to the ordinary manner in which the projection is used. No fixed distance was required here because the necessary distance follows the customary use. If the projection extends four handbreadths, however, the rule no longer follows its particular use. Instead, one must leave ten handbreadths, since that constitutes an independent domain. Therefore a fixed distance was stated there. As for the Sema's proof from the Commentary to the Mishnah, I do not see any proof. Its meaning is that the projection's owner may say: perhaps, without my knowledge, you will also remove the projection and incorporate its place into your building.
