One who seeks to project a ledge. A ledge is like a plank or the end of a beam projecting outward from an upper story. Even if it is less than a tefach and cannot readily be used as a standing surface, one can still hang objects from it, and it creates potential visual intrusion. He cannot say, 'I will hang things while facing backward and will not look into my fellow's courtyard,' because we are concerned that he may turn around and deliberately look. He could then excuse himself by saying that he turned around because he feared falling while standing at the edge of the upper story, and he would not appear as one peering like a thief. So the Gemara and Rashbam explain.
As long as he has not removed it, etc. The Tur writes this according to the opinion that even a ledge a tefach wide acquires no chazakah in less than three years, while one less than a tefach never acquires chazakah at all. That fits well. But Rama writes this on the Mechaber's ruling, which follows Rambam, and Rambam holds that even the smallest ledge acquires chazakah immediately if the courtyard owner did not protest. Once he remains silent, he can no longer protest and the owner of the ledge may use it, as the Tur writes in Rambam's name at the end of this siman and the Mechaber writes immediately below in seif 2. One must therefore say that Rama writes this law according to each view on its own terms. According to Rambam it refers to a case where the owner protested immediately and said he was not waiving the permanent placement of the ledge, but would remove it whenever he chose. After such a protest, the ledge owner may not use it.
