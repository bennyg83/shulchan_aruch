One who seeks... The Shulchan Aruch's first four seifim largely follow the Rambam, while Beer HaGolah presents the Tosafos, Rosh, and Tur approach. According to the Rambam and Shulchan Aruch, the courtyard owner may protest against extending the projection, and the roof owner may protest against building beneath it. Tosafos likewise explains that with a projection of a tefach both sides can protest, but at different stages, while with less than a tefach the roof owner can never protest. The Mishnah's chazakah concerns the airspace beneath the projection. A projection less than a tefach does not establish a right to that airspace, though the projection itself may remain. A projection four tefachim long establishes a four-by-four right and ten tefachim of usable airspace beneath it. Tosafos and the Rosh understand the protests primarily in relation to use of the projection and require three years plus a supporting claim, whereas the Rambam requires only acquiescence after awareness of the interference and no supporting claim.
