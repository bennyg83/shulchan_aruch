Under. The Sema explains that this means in the actual place occupied by the projecting beam. If the courtyard owner builds in that very place, the beam must be removed and its use is entirely canceled. Only in this respect is there a difference between a beam of one handbreadth and one of less than a handbreadth. Even with a beam of one handbreadth, however, the owner of the beam cannot prevent the courtyard owner from building beneath it up to the beam itself. See there. The Taz writes that the phrase 'under the beam' does not imply this. Rather, the words mean literally that the courtyard owner has no right in the beam itself even when it is less than a handbreadth. He may only build beneath it until close to it, thereby effectively preventing its use. With a beam of a handbreadth, by contrast, the beam owner has acquired a right in the airspace beneath the beam to the extent required for its normal use. See Responsa Rashach, part 3, siman 14.
