To establish a right to a second beam. The Mechaber follows his own view that chazakah here is immediate, because the owner's silence shows waiver. What we did not see him waive, we do not treat as waived. According to those who require three years of chazakah together with a claim that the right was purchased, however, an acquisition is not presumed to have been made only halfway. Rather, initially he needed only one beam, but the right acquired permits him to insert several beams. Sema:
When do we say this, when he admits. The intent is that he is compelled to admit it, for example where the two houses belong respectively to Reuven and Shimon and a public road separates them, so it is evident to everyone that the wall belonged to one side. Otherwise, why should he not be believed to say that the other waived rights to inserting beams, through a migo that he could have claimed joint ownership? Sema. The Taz writes that no migo applies at all here, because the entire chazakah rests on the fact that the owner remained silent and waived, and we are witnesses, as it were, that he waived no more than what he tolerated:
I am a partner in it. Meaning, according to the Sema, that they live adjacent to one another and it is unknown to whom the wall between them belongs. See the Beurim for the explanation of the Sema and the reasoning of the authorities. The Taz questions this, for if so why is insertion of beams needed at all? He therefore explains that "I am a partner in it" means, "I purchased an ownership share in it." The case is where it is known that the wall originally belonged solely to the other party, and insertion of beams is like consumption of produce that establishes chazakah and makes his claim credible:
That from the outset the wall was jointly owned. According to the Sema, this means that they live adjacent to one another, as above. According to the Taz, it means that he claims purchase and says that he expressly bought a share in the wall itself:
That originally it belonged to the other party. According to the Taz this phrase has no satisfactory meaning. He therefore explains that the case is one without an independent purchase claim. The possessor merely says that he acquired half the wall because that half was included in another purchase. The ruling is that this is not proved unless he also installed a ceiling. The principal interpretation follows the Sema, as I wrote in the Beurim:
Unless he installed a ceiling. According to the Rambam, three years are then unnecessary; according to Rabbeinu Tam, three years are required. Sema. See the Beurim. Although a claim of purchase ordinarily requires three years according to all opinions, this case resembles digging pits in another's land, which damages the other while improving one's own use and therefore creates immediate chazakah.
