He cannot establish chazakah for a second beam. It appears that here too Rambam follows his own view that chazakah arises because the owner remained silent and thereby waived his right. Therefore, he waived only what we actually see him having tolerated, not what we do not see. But according to Rabbeinu Tam, Rosh, and those with them, who require three years of chazakah and a claim of purchase, once one established chazakah for one beam he may insert many beams. The Rosh and Tur say so explicitly, explaining that there is no partial acquisition; he simply needed only one beam at first. Beit Yosef likewise writes this in seif 25.
When is this said? When he admits that the wall is not his, etc.; but if he claims, 'I am a partner in this wall.' See Rivash. The case concerns two houses on opposite sides of the public domain, one belonging to Reuven and one to Shimon, where Reuven supported a beam from his wall to Shimon's wall. It was evident to everyone that the wall he used was not his. This is also the Mechaber's intent by saying, 'when he admits that the wall is not his,' meaning that in reality he must admit it because we effectively have witnesses to that fact. It therefore follows that he is not believed to say that the other waived the insertion of beams through a migo that he could have claimed, 'I am a partner in this wall,' as the Tur and Mechaber write at the end of siman 157. When the Mechaber writes, 'and he waived to him the insertion of this beam,' Rambam is following his view that an express waiver suffices for chazakah, and therefore we do not infer a broader waiver. But if he claims actual partnership in the wall, then once he establishes chazakah through one beam he has chazakah in the whole wall. What the Mechaber later writes, 'But if he claims, I am a partner in this wall,' concerns neighbors where the wall between them is of uncertain ownership. Rama's gloss, 'but if he admits that originally it belonged to his fellow, etc.,' also concerns a case where he must admit that. Rama teaches that even if he established chazakah through many beams and can insert them wherever he wishes, this still does not prove that half the wall itself belongs to him. Some say it is proof even as to the wall, though not as to the ground beneath it. Chazakah over the ground itself is ineffective unless the other expressly transferred it to him. The Tur writes this explicitly.
Unless he made a ceiling over it. Each view applies according to its own rule. According to Rambam and those with him, three years are unnecessary. According to Rabbeinu Tam and those with him, he must have used the ceiling for three years. Likewise, Rama's conclusion, 'some say there is no distinction,' reflects the Rosh's view and therefore presupposes three years of chazakah. Rama shortened the wording because he relied on what was explained earlier.
