One who established a right to insert one beam into another's wall cannot thereby insert a second beam, because the owner waived his right only for one. When does this apply? When the user admits the wall belongs to the other person and claims only that the owner waived his right regarding this beam. If he claims, 'I am a partner in this wall,' then because he used it for one beam, he is believed and may use the entire wall after taking a heses oath that he is a partner in all of it. {Rama: Some say this applies only when he says the wall belonged jointly to them from the outset. If he admits it originally belonged to the other person, insertion of beams is not proof of ownership of the wall itself unless he built a ceiling upon it. Others say no distinction should be made: his possession establishes a right to a share of the wall's stones if it falls, but not to the ground on which the wall stands. See Rivash 210.}
