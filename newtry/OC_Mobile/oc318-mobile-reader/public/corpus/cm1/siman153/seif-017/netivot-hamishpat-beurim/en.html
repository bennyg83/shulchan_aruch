He offers a strained answer. In my view the distinction is simple. In distancing cases, the one causing the damage acts within his own property and merely harms the neighbor. Because he is in possession of his own property, he is believed that the neighbor knowingly waived, and the neighbor is the one seeking to extract a right from him, so the burden of proof rests on the claimant. Even where Mechaber mentions diverting a water channel, the case can be one in which the channel itself was made within the possessor's land, but the required distance from the neighbor's property was not maintained. Here, by contrast, the possessor seeks to acquire a right of use in the neighbor's wall. The neighbor has the chazakah of the prior owner against him. The one claiming against that prior ownership must therefore bring proof that the owner saw the use and waived. Rambam's wording supports this distinction. In chapter 11, halachah 4, of Hilchot Shekhenim, when ruling that three years are unnecessary, he writes broadly, "All the distancing requirements we stated," which can include every type of chazakah, even insertion of beams. But in halachah 7, when he rules that the possessor is believed in a dispute whether the owner waived, he lists only damages and does not again use the broad phrase "all the chazakot we stated." This indicates that Rambam gives credibility to the possessor specifically in those cases where he acts within his own property, but where he uses the neighbor's property he must prove that the owner knew and waived.
