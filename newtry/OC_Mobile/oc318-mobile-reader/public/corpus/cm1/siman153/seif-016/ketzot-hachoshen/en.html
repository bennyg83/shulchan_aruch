And regarding chazakah of a ladder and inserting beams. The early authorities disagree, as cited by the Tur. Rashbam explains that such a chazakah does not require three years. Three years are needed only for uses involving a deficiency in the land, such as opening windows, installing a drainpipe, or a gutter, which are significant uses for which people ordinarily write a document. For a minor use such as this, people do not ordinarily write a document, so the chazakah is immediate, though the user must still present a claim explaining his right. The Geonim explain that even projecting ledges, a drainpipe, a gutter, or setting up a ladder do not require three years, because the basis is waiver. Once the owner sees another using his property and remains silent, the chazakah arises immediately, even without a claim. Ramban writes likewise. I have a serious difficulty with the view that no claim is required. Granted, silence may constitute waiver, but through what act does the user acquire a continuing right of use so that the owner can no longer retract? If he presents a claim, one can understand that he says he purchased the right for money or by document and chazakah. But without a claim, with nothing more than seeing and silence, why can the owner not retract when no kinyan occurred? According to Rambam the matter is understandable, because he holds in chapter 1 of Hilchot Mechirah that land can be acquired by consuming its produce. Here too, the use itself functions as chazakah and he acquires the usage right, so the owner can no longer retract. But Raavad holds that consumption of produce is evidence only after three years and is not an act of acquisition. Rashbam at the end of Chazakat HaBatim, Ramban, Rashba, Ran, Rosh, and Tur in siman 192 likewise hold that consuming produce does not itself acquire. How then, according to these authorities, can mere use plus silence create an irrevocable right without a claim? It therefore appears to me that although consuming produce does not acquire the body of the land, if the owners agree to grant another person a specific right of use and he actually performs that use with their consent, he acquires that usage right and the owner can no longer retract. This is analogous to Bava Batra 100, where the Sages agree with Rabbi Eliezer regarding a path through vineyards that, because it is made for walking, it is acquired by walking. The Tur likewise writes in siman 192 that something whose designated use is walking is acquired by walking. It is therefore possible that even where an object is designated for some other use, if the owner grants that specific use and the grantee performs it, he acquires the usage right through that very use.
