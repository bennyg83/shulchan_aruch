And regarding presumptive possession of a ladder, etc., some disagree concerning all these easements. See siman 155:35, where Rama rules like the Rambam that three years are not required. In my view that ruling is not a universal decision for every easement. Otherwise it is difficult why Rama repeatedly records the dispute without deciding it, at the end of siman 142 regarding a window, here in siman 153 regarding a projection, gutter, and ladder, and again in siman 154 regarding a window, yet waits until siman 155:35 to state his ruling. Rather, there is a distinction. In siman 155 the cases involve required distances, such as refuse from a wall, a ladder from a dovecote, or soaking pits from vegetables. The person acts entirely within his own property and gains no use of his neighbor's property, but must distance himself because his activity causes damage. In such cases Rama rules that three years are unnecessary, because the neighbor who seeks to force removal is the one seeking to disturb the actor's established use. By contrast, a projection, gutter, ladder standing in the neighbor's courtyard, or beams inserted in the neighbor's wall involve actual use of the neighbor's property. Likewise, a window makes use of the neighbor's light and air and may also cause visual intrusion. Although the Geonim and Rambam hold that even these rights can arise immediately because they do not alter the substance of the land, Rabbeinu Tam, Ri, the Rosh, and other French authorities require three years and a claim of acquisition. Rama remained uncertain in these cases. Darkei Moshe to siman 155 itself says that we follow the Rambam there because the burden of proof lies upon the claimant, based on Nimukei Yosef. That burden of proof rationale fits the cases where the alleged tortfeasor acts within his own property and the neighbor seeks to remove him. Where he is using the neighbor's property, the neighbor is the one in possession, so the same rationale could point the other way. Since the ordinary rules of "one who extracts from another" do not fit these easements perfectly in either direction, Rama did not issue a general decision. At the end of siman 154:7, regarding a window, Rama directs the reader to siman 155:35, which suggests that he may incline there too toward not requiring three years, because the window was opened in the owner's own wall and merely enjoys light from the neighboring courtyard. Yet this cannot be a complete distinction, since Rashbam does not require three years for a ladder or inserted beams but does require it for a window. We must therefore say that visual intrusion causes the window to be treated as a more substantial impairment of the neighboring property. See siman 154:16 and my note there. Rama's silence in siman 155:43 when the Mechaber follows the Rambam likewise rests upon his reliance on the earlier framework. The Ir Shushan, who explains Rama's ruling merely by saying that a person ordinarily would not see his neighbor doing such things and remain silent, appears to understand Rama as deciding all of these cases alike. In my view that is not correct.
