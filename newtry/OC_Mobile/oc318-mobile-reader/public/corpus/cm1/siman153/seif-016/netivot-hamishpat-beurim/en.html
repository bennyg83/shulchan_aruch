And concerning the chazakah of a ladder. See Sema, seif katan 32, who discusses this at length. In my view a distinction should be made. In distancing laws, a person performs an act within his own property that causes damage to his neighbor. For those cases, waiver is enough, because it resembles one who says, "Tear my garment and be exempt." But with a projection or water spout, the claimant seeks to acquire a right of use in the neighbor's property itself. In such a case it is reasonable to require a kinyan in the land for its fruits. Therefore Rama says that three years and a supporting claim are required, meaning that he must claim that he acquired the right through a kinyan. This does not create a difficulty from opening a window, because a window has two distinct aspects. One is privacy damage, which is damage caused to the neighbor. Were there no privacy damage, one could open the window so long as the neighbor did not need to build opposite it. The second is a claimed right in the neighbor's airspace, namely that the owner of the window has acquired the courtyard's air so that the neighbor may no longer build opposite the window. In siman 154, Rama states his ruling only in connection with the words, "and he must close it," meaning that the window owner himself must close it even when the courtyard owner is not trying to build, simply because of privacy damage. Concerning that damage, Rama rules that three years and a supporting claim are unnecessary, because simple waiver is enough. But Rama does not state any such ruling regarding the separate issue whether the courtyard owner may build opposite the window. For that right in the airspace, it is entirely possible that three years and a supporting claim are required. Rashbam, cited by Sema, speaks precisely about the right to prevent building opposite the window.
