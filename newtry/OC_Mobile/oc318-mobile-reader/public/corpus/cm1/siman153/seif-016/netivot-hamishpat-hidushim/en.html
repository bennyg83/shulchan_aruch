And regarding chazakah of a ladder. In siman 155, seif katan 35, Rama rules that three years are unnecessary. This does not appear to be a general resolution, because he states it only there. Rather, Rama rules there on the basis of an unresolved legal doubt and the principle that the burden of proof rests upon the claimant. In siman 155, concerning distancing nuisances, the person acting on his own property is considered the possessor, so Rama follows the Rambam because the burden of proof rests upon the other party. But with chazakah involving a projection, gutter, ladder, or insertion of beams, the use takes place on the other's property, so the other owner is the possessor. The fact that the use has already begun does not clearly identify who is seeking to extract. Therefore Rama did not resolve the issue generally. Yet in siman 154:7, regarding opening a window, Rama refers to siman 155, implying that he intended the same resolution there. One cannot explain that because the window is opened on one's own property and benefits from the neighboring courtyard's air automatically the neighbor is somewhat the claimant, while a projection directly uses the neighbor's property. Rashba rules that establishing a ladder needs no three year chazakah, while a window does, showing that visual damage is treated as a deficiency in the land. Sema. Taz in siman 155 writes that Rama intended to rule like the Rambam everywhere, but waited until the later discussion. See Beurim.
