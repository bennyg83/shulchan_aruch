And even to build upon his roof in the form of a steep hut, etc. The Gemara says that one who has acquired the right to dripping water and concentrated runoff has also acquired the right to a steep thatched structure. Rashbam explains that this is a roof covered with willow branches so that the drops fall close together, making use of the area beneath difficult, yet the roof owner still has acquired the right. The Tur follows this explanation. The Ramah therefore derives that the owner may even raise the roof and drainpipe though the stream becomes stronger. The Rambam, however, appears to explain the Gemara differently. After ruling that one may change scattered drips across the roof into concentrated discharge through one pipe, he adds that one may construct the roof in a steep form. This indicates that he understood the steep hut as a sharply sloped roof from which the water descends quickly into the neighboring courtyard. Such rapid runoff may be less burdensome than raising the roof and strengthening the stream. Therefore the Maggid Mishneh holds that although the Rambam permits changing to a steep roof, he does not necessarily permit raising the roof beyond its former height. The Mechaber states this in the following seif, then cites an opinion permitting even that, namely the Ramah as brought by the Tur. The dispute depends on how the Gemara's expression is understood.
