Where he has no opening there, etc. In the Derishah I cited Tosafos to Bava Batra 59, s.v. 'and even the courtyard owner,' who write that although on the side where the roof is open to the courtyard one must make a parapet four amot high, so that it is not normal to extend a ledge above such a parapet and use it to look down, the case can involve a ledge projecting at the corner, at the end of the parapet on the side facing his own courtyard. On the other three sides of the roof, although a parapet is also required to prevent falling, it need not be so high. See the Derishah.
And also to build opposite it, etc. In the Perishah I explained that 'opposite it' means in the actual space of the ledge. Even if the ledge is below the window, or beside it within a tefach of the window, he may not build there, lest he stand on it and peer through his fellow's windows, as explained in siman 154:14.
There is an authority who writes that those ledges, etc. This phrase does not introduce a dispute with the preceding law. Rather, because the Mechaber began this seif by saying, 'There is an authority who writes that all this, etc.,' Rama adds another leniency concerning ledges: there is an authority who further writes that those ledges, etc. This is clear.
He may remove them entirely. Nimukei Yosef concludes that if he is a partner in the wall, he may take half the wall's thickness for his own use, as is his right. The principle of Nimukei Yosef is that since the ledges themselves cause him no present damage, he cannot protest against them, because this is a case of one benefiting while the other loses nothing. Since he cannot protest, the other acquires no permanent chazakah merely through passage of time, and he may remove the ledges when he later wishes to build there and take his portion of the wall. See below, siman 154:16, where the Mechaber writes that some say he can protest because he does not wish to have to descend into litigation later. One can say that here all agree, because everyone knows it is common practice to allow a neighbor temporarily to extend a ledge into one's courtyard until the space is needed for building.
