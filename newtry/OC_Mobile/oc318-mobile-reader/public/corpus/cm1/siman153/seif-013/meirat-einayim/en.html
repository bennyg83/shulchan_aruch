A small ladder, etc., within his fellow's courtyard, etc. Rosh explains it this way as well, and the Tur cites him. The Tur adds that Rabbeinu Yitzchak explains that in such a case even a small ladder acquires chazakah, because since he passes through the other's courtyard, the owner certainly objects. Rather, the case concerns leaning the ladder within his own courtyard against his fellow's wall. Since the ladder is small, the other does not object.
He cannot prevent him, etc. This is unlike even the smallest ledge discussed at the beginning of this siman, where the owner can protest, and if he does not protest the other acquires chazakah. There the ledge causes visual damage. Here, by contrast, since the only objection concerns placement of the ladder, it is clear that the owner does not object to his entering and leaving through the courtyard without setting up the ladder. When he enters and leaves in any event, he can use the ladder only by permission of his fellow. The owner can therefore watch his use and may revoke permission whenever he wishes. See the Derishah.
