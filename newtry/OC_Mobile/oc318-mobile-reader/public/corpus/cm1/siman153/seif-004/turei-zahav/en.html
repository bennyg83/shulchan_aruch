(Seif 8) The owner of the roof cannot object, and so forth. The Tur wonders why the Rosh did not cite the Gemara's interpretation concerning a stone gutter. It appears that this may be resolved as follows. The Rosh writes that since the Amoraim disagree and no ruling was issued, the one in possession of the right to the downspout receives the benefit of the doubt. The courtyard owner may not touch the downspout, but he may build beneath it in accordance with Rabbi Yirmeyah. His intent appears to be that because of the uncertainty neither party may interfere with the other. Therefore, concerning building beneath it, it is possible that the other Amoraim hold that one may build even beneath a stone gutter, just as Rabbi Yirmeyah at least permits building beneath a stone downspout. The Rosh consequently rules that the courtyard owner may not touch the body of the gutter in any respect, while the owner of the gutter may not prevent the courtyard owner from building on his own property. The distinction between stone and wood is relevant only according to the opinion that permits building beneath it, for according to that opinion the Gemara was compelled to distinguish them. The roof owner therefore lacks power to prevent the courtyard owner from building on his own property, since perhaps the law follows the other Amoraim. When the Rosh mentions Rabbi Yirmeyah, he does not mean that only according to Rabbi Yirmeyah may one build. Rather, regarding the downspout, we find support for leniency in Rabbi Yirmeyah's answer. Regarding the gutter itself, however, the Rosh did not rule like him, because Rabbi Yirmeyah is stringent concerning a stone gutter, whereas according to the other resolutions all gutters may be treated leniently, just as Rabbi Yirmeyah treats a downspout.
