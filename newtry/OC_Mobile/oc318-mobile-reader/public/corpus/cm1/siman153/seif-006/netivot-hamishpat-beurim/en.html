That even for this chazakah three years are required. Although siman 149 states that digging pits can establish chazakah immediately, as explained there, that is where a person improves his own use by directly damaging the other's land. Here too he benefits himself and harms his fellow, but the cases can be distinguished. Here no permanent alteration of the courtyard is necessary. The injury can be corrected simply by removing the drainage pipe, so less than three years does not establish the right. Digging pits, by contrast, creates an actual physical change in the other's land. It is possible that the view requiring a legal claim but not three years reasons that this case is sufficiently analogous to digging pits.
So too the courtyard owner established his right. At first glance this is difficult. Even if the roof owner expressly agreed and made a kinyan that the courtyard owner would always receive the roof water, it would seem that no such kinyan can operate merely on that future flow unless he transferred an interest in the land itself. The courtyard owner can still build an upper story if he wishes, as Sema writes. Why should mere consent be treated as transferring a body of property for its fruits? The reason appears to be different. The roof owner acquired a right in the courtyard so that the water from his roof could drain into it. The courtyard owner did not grant that right gratuitously, but in exchange for the roof owner's undertaking that his roof water would continue to drain there. I wrote below in siman 203 that when one party acquires movables by meshichah on condition that he provide something in return, even if the promised item is not yet in existence he becomes obligated to provide it. One party's kinyan creates the reciprocal obligation, analogous to the rule that acquisition of gold obligates delivery of silver, as Nimukei Yosef explains there. Since this is an obligation rather than an attempted transfer of a nonexistent object, it can take effect even regarding something not yet in existence.

In siman 154, seif 12, the Rama records a dispute concerning blocking a window, with some authorities requiring a claim of acquisition and not treating silence alone as waiver. It therefore appears that the Rama alludes to the same dispute here by referring to what is said regarding blocking a window. The disagreement of the poskim stated there applies here as well.
