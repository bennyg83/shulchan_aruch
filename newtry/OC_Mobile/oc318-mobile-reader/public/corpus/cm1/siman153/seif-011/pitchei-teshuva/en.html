And some permit it. Responsa Rabbi Akiva Eiger, siman 151, cites Mahari Ben Lev II, klalim 84 and 85, that where the authorities dispute whether raising a structure constitutes an additional injury, the owner may raise it. A person is forbidden to injure his fellow, but so long as the other party has not established that the proposed act is injurious, his objection is not accepted. This differs from the Rashba's ruling, cited by Beit Yosef in siman 154, regarding a doubtful acquisition of a right to smoke or a privy nuisance. There the injury itself is certain and the only doubt concerns whether a valid acquisition bars objection, so the existing right to object remains. Here, however, the doubt concerns whether there is any injury at all. Maharit II, siman 75, challenges this distinction, arguing that in Mahari Ben Lev's case the drainage injury to the neighbor's courtyard is itself certain and the issue is whether the prior prescriptive right extends to raising the drain. Rabbi Akiva Eiger answers that the original right to place the drain and discharge its water is established with certainty, so the owner is acting within a right already acquired, while the added height is only doubtfully injurious.
