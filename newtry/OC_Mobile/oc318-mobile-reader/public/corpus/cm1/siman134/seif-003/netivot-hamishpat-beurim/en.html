See Sema, seif katan 9, who writes that the owner should bring witnesses to the original delivery who can identify the object and combine their testimony with the witnesses who later saw it, after which the holder will have neither a migo of complete denial nor a migo of return. This is difficult. Even if Sema means that the holder now displays the object before beit din and the original witnesses identify it, Rama still needed the reason that his alternative claim is not a valid migo because he would be afraid to make it. Without that rationale the holder would be believed through a migo that he could simply have refrained from showing the object. He could then have claimed that he returned the item originally delivered to him, while the object later seen by witnesses was a different object. Mere identifying signs do not compel him to display it. Perhaps Sema means that the original witnesses know that the object had a uniquely identifying sign, and the later witnesses also saw that same unique sign. Their testimony can then combine to remove the object from the holder, because a uniquely identifying sign is sufficient to extract property, as Shach writes in siman 297. On this reading, Shach's question in seif 3 is also answered. Shach asks that in any event the migo is against witnesses because the witnesses identify the object. The case, however, may be one in which they do not identify the object itself, but merely state identifying signs.
