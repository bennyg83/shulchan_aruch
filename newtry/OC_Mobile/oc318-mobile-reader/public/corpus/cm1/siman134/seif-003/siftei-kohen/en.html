Although he delivered it before witnesses. See the Sema, seif katan 9, who asks why the craftsman should be believed, since the owner could bring the witnesses to delivery who recognize the article. According to the Sema's understanding that those witnesses recognize it, his question should not have been merely that their testimony could be joined, for even without that, the craftsman's migo would stand against witnesses. The simple explanation is that the witnesses to delivery also do not recognize the item, or that they are no longer available, as the Bach explains. The Sema further writes that the Mechaber copied the Tur's wording mechanically and was therefore imprecise, because in the Perisha he had explained that the words, 'although he delivered it before witnesses,' refer to the preceding clause and not to this one. I likewise found in an old parchment Tur the reading, 'although he delivered it to him before witnesses,' without the conjunction, followed by punctuation and then the words, 'If the witnesses,' etc. See Responsa Maharan Sasson, siman 109.
