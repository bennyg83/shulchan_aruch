Although the owner delivered the object before witnesses, if the witnesses who saw an object in the craftsman's possession cannot identify with certainty that it belongs to the claimant and merely think its identifying features resemble his, the craftsman is believed if he says, 'You never gave it to me.' But if he says, 'You gave it to me to repair, and afterward I purchased it from you,' he is not believed. {Rama: He has no sound migo, because he is afraid to say, 'This never occurred,' after the witnesses saw a similar object in his possession.} See note 140.
