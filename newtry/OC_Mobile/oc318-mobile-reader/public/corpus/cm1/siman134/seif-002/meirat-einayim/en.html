And it does not require him to do so, Rava said of Rashi Duffa in the midst of the houses:
If he brought it out, since it was seen, etc. This means that he brought it out before the parties came to court, so that he no longer has the migo that he could have kept it concealed. This is as I wrote in the Sema to the preceding siman, seifim 1 and 2. The Gemara's terminology is used here as well. With a craftsman, whenever there are no witnesses who saw the article in his possession, the craftsman is believed according to the law. The same would be true of an ordinary noncraftsman if he had brought it out. There, however, this applies specifically where the item had originally been delivered before witnesses, while with a craftsman the rule applies even if the original delivery was not witnessed.
