And if he said to him, he confessed. Dalai Bari was quoted as saying that he had sold him a loyal dea and wrote the column for a man in the book, Kendash:
Some say this applies specifically when it remained with him after his father's death. Tosafot and the Rosh write this, although the Tur omits it here. The Beit Yosef wonders why the Tur omitted it. In the Derishah I wrote that perhaps the Tur understood Tosafot and the Rosh to speak only of the son of a craftsman with respect to land, where the son is not physically in possession in the same manner as movable property. If he merely said, "I purchased it from the claimant," he would not be believed without a deed or three years of possession. Therefore, when he instead says, "My father admitted before me," this claim likewise requires the property to have remained with him, meaning for three years. But with movable property in the hands of the son of a craftsman, no such waiting period is needed. If he said, "I purchased it from the claimant," he would be believed immediately because he possesses it. Accordingly he is also believed to say, "The claimant admitted before me," through that migo, even without any delay. Although Tosafot and the Rosh mention utensils in their language, perhaps they did so only concerning the craftsman himself after he left his trade. Still, the Rosh's wording and the abbreviated rulings of the Rosh do not readily support this. There is also a difficulty that on this reading "it remained with him" would mean different things in the father and the son: for the craftsman father with movable property it would mean merely some period, while for the son with land it would mean three years. See the Derishah. The Mechaber cites as "some say" the view that a delay is required because the straightforward implication of the Gemara and the language of the Rif, Rambam, and Tur do not appear that way. Indeed, the Tur concludes, "If he says that he inherited it from his father, he has the same status as his father," implying that only when he says he inherited it does he share his father's disability, but if he says he purchased it from the owner, he is believed even after his father's death. Rama's gloss, "It appears to me specifically, etc.," likewise belongs only to the view introduced as "some say," not necessarily to the Tur's own view. Darkei Moshe, however, appears to use this to resolve the Beit Yosef's difficulty with the Tur, which is strained. Still, it is difficult to say that the Tur disagrees silently with his father the Rosh and with Tosafot. The first explanation therefore has merit. See the Derishah here and below siman 152.
