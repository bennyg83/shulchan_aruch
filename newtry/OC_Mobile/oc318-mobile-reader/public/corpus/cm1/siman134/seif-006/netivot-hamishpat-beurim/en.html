Only if sufficient time elapsed. According to the Rama in the gloss, even if the craftsman's son claims, "I myself purchased it," he does not have the ordinary presumption that whatever is in a person's possession belongs to him unless sufficient time elapsed, because it is possible that the article came into his possession from the craftsman. This is so even when there are no witnesses that the article was given to the craftsman for repair. The Mechaber holds that where there are no witnesses to its delivery to the craftsman, the craftsman who retired from his trade, or the craftsman's son, does have a chazakah when he claims that it was purchased. But where there are witnesses to the delivery and the object was seen in his possession, even the Mechaber agrees that there is no chazakah unless sufficient time has elapsed. The general rule is that where there are witnesses and the object was seen, it is removed from the craftsman. Why should merely retiring from his craft for one day suddenly make him believed? Tumim writes likewise.
