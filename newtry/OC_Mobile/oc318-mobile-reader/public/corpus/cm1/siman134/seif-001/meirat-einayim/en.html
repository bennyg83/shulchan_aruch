He claims, "I gave it to him to repair," etc. It appears that if the owner claims, "I lent this utensil to the craftsman," or, "I rented it to him," and it is not an article ordinarily lent or rented, he cannot remove it from the present possessor. This is so even if there are witnesses who saw it. He cannot rely upon a migo that he could have said, "I gave it to the craftsman to repair," because we do not ordinarily use a migo to extract property from one who is in possession, as the Tur and Rama write in siman 82:12. Tosafot write similarly. See the Derishah to siman 138 concerning the case where one says, "It is all mine," and the other says, "Half is mine," where I cited Tosafot and explained circumstances in which a migo may nevertheless be used to extract.
