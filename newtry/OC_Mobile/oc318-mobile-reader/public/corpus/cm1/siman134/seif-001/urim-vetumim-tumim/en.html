I gave it to you. The Sema writes that if the claimant says, I lent it to you, but the objects are not of a type ordinarily lent, he is not believed through a migo that he could have said, I gave it to you for repair, because that would be a migo used to extract property. According to what I wrote above in siman 90, however, Rabbenu Tam's reason for believing an owner who claims that ordinarily lent objects were stolen, despite the principle that a migo does not extract property, is that the object remains presumptively in the lender's ownership. The same reasoning applies here: the object remains presumptively in the original owner's ownership, so this is not a migo to extract. This may be the Rif's reason for holding that even regarding a craftsman, where there are no witnesses who saw the object, the owner is believed despite the craftsman's migo that he returned it, because the object remains presumptively the owner's and the craftsman's migo would be one to extract from that ownership. Although we do not rule like the Rif on that specific point, all may agree at least that the owner's own migo is not classified as one to extract. The Sema's ruling therefore requires study.
