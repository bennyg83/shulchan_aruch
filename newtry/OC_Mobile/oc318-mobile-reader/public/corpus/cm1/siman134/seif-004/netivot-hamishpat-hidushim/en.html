You sold it to me. This concerns a case where witnesses saw the item in the purchaser's possession. The artisan himself is therefore not believed, and for that reason the owner can recover it even from the purchaser. But if there are no witnesses who saw the item, even the artisan himself is believed. Sema. The purchaser does not take the place of witnesses who saw the item, because he is financially interested in saying that the artisan did not return it but sold it to him. If he does not say that, and instead says that he purchased it directly from the challenger, he would become obligated to swear. A witness who requires an oath is not a valid witness. However, if there are no witnesses who saw the item and no witnesses that it originally belonged to the challenger, while the purchaser from the artisan knows that it did belong to him, the purchaser functions like one witness. The artisan then cannot rely on the migo that he could have said, "It was never yours," because he becomes one obligated to swear but unable to swear, and therefore must pay. The purchaser too must return the item. Shach. See Beurim.
