Two judges. Levush explains why two judges are added rather than one: perhaps the judge who said, 'I do not know,' will later adopt the added judge's view, producing two for liability and two for acquittal. Two are therefore added, making five. Shevut Yaakov, volume 1, siman 133, discusses Reuven and Shimon who selected five judges, one of whom withdrew because he was related to Reuven. The parties agreed that the remaining four would judge, and one of those four then said, 'I do not know.' He first establishes, following Sema as cited by Baer Heitev, Bach, Agudat Ezov, and She'erit Yosef, that there is a distinction based on the number the panel originally intended to use. This reconciles Rashba with Rambam. Since this panel initially sat as four, additional judges are required. As to how many, if Levush's sole reason were to avoid an evenly divided court, one added judge would suffice here because the original panel was already even. Shevut Yaakov rejects that reason and gives another. Under his explanation, two must be added even here, despite producing an even-numbered panel, because when judges are being added the Gemara itself permits creating an even-numbered panel, and all the more so where the parties originally entrusted the case to an even-numbered court. If the parties agree on how to add judges, however, there is no question; their agreement governs.
