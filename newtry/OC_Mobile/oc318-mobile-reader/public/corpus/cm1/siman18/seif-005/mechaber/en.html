The Gemara's statement that a teacher and his student are counted as only one applies specifically when the teacher instructs the student in the law during the deliberation and the student relies upon him. Even then, if the student is like Rav Ami or Rav Asi, who required Rav's received teachings but did not require his reasoning, the teacher may instruct him during the case and the student may judge alongside him. If the teacher is not instructing him during the case, they are counted as two even though one is the other's student, provided that the student has enough independent understanding to deliberate. {Rama: Regarding matters of prohibition and permission or the laws of ritual impurity and purity, they are counted as two even if the student requires the teacher's reasoning (Nimukei Yosef). See also siman 292.}
