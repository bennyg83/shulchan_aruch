He can teach him and judge with him, etc. Meaning the student and teacher count as two. Rashi and Nimukei Yosef explain that the concern is not merely that the student learned general Torah from the teacher, for all Israel ultimately received Torah from Moshe. Rather, in a particular legal analysis one might think the student's reasoning is not independent and that had he studied under another teacher he would have adopted a different rationale. Nevertheless, in monetary law the student may present his reasoning and be counted where it is genuinely his own. In matters of prohibition, purity, and impurity, where a single scholar can issue a ruling, even other students may be counted separately when authorities disagree and the law follows the majority according to the applicable rules.
