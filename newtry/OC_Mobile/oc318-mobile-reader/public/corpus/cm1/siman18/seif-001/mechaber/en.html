How the judges deliberate a matter and follow the majority. This siman contains 6 seifim.
If one litigant brings witnesses supporting his claim, the witnesses are brought in and their testimony is received properly, as will be explained in siman 28. After the testimony is received, everyone else is sent outside and the judges deliberate. Some say it is proper for the least senior judge to begin. If they agree on one view, that is sufficient. If not, they follow the majority. If two say exempt and one says liable, he is exempt. If two say liable and one says exempt, he is liable. If one says exempt, one says liable, and one says, "I do not know," or even if two say exempt or liable and one says, "I do not know," they add two judges, making five, and deliberate again. If three acquit and two obligate, he is acquitted. If three obligate and two acquit, he is obligated. If two acquit, two obligate, and one says, "I do not know," they add additional judges. But if four say exempt or liable and one says, "I do not know," or if three say exempt, one says liable, and one says, "I do not know," whether the one now saying "I do not know" is the same judge who said so initially or another judge, they follow the majority. Rama: This appears to me to be the primary ruling, unlike what was written above in siman 13, seif 7, to the contrary. That earlier ruling follows those who disagree. It is possible to distinguish between a case where more than three judges were selected initially and one where they began with three, but this does not appear correct to me. Rather, these are two differing opinions, the first being the Rambam's view and the second the Rashba's. If a community selected four or five arbiters, their decision is not governed by majority rule, since the Torah principle of majority applies to a beit din. If the community explicitly said they should follow the majority and the arbiters divide evenly, additional arbiters are not brought in to decide, because the community selected only these arbiters. The arbiters themselves, however, may consult others and rule on the basis of their advice as they see fit.
