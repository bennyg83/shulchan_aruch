Primary. The Sema writes that he did not understand the Rama's view here. One must distinguish between a case where the judges initially sat with the intention of functioning as a panel of three, etc., and other cases. The Kesef Mishneh at the end of chapter 8 of Hilchot Sanhedrin likewise makes this distinction, and it appears clear to me. According to the Rama, however, who treats the issue as disputed and states that the primary view is to follow the majority, yet immediately thereafter writes that where a community selected judges the majority is not followed, one must say that he distinguishes between judges selected by the community, where all agree that the majority does not control, and judges selected by the litigants themselves, where there is a dispute. Thus concludes the Sema.
