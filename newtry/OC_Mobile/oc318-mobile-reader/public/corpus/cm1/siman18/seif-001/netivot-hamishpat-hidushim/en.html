They shall add judges. Majority rule applies only when all three judges sat, deliberated, and each expressed a reasoned opinion, after which two agreed to one position and the third opinion is nullified by the majority. But when one says, "I do not know," he is regarded as though he did not sit with them. We therefore say that had a third judge sat with them and expressed an opinion, the others might have agreed with him. Once judges are added, however, even if one of them says, "I do not know," since they originally convened with the intention that there be three judges and there now remain three who support one conclusion, we follow them, Sema:
The primary view. The Sema writes that the Rama did not fully apprehend the matter here, because there is necessarily a distinction between judges who originally sat with the intention of being a panel of three and other arrangements. The Kesef Mishnah likewise makes this distinction, and it is clear. According to the Rama, who treats the matter as disputed and writes that it appears primary to follow the majority, yet immediately afterward writes that where a community selected judges they do not follow the majority, one must distinguish between judges selected by the community, where all agree that the majority does not suffice, and judges selected by the litigants themselves, where there is a dispute:
