Beit din rules, etc. This applies where the absent litigant is not close enough to be summoned. If he is nearby, the court should not decide without him; it should send for him, because he may appear. Rashba and Sema. See what I wrote at the end of siman 13 in Tumim, agreeing with Bach. Rashba explains the permissibility of deciding an ordinary monetary case in absence by saying that monetary judgments can later be reopened if new evidence appears, whereas capital cases involve irreversible loss. This implies that even a monetary matter should not ordinarily be decided in absence where the practical result would be irreversible, for example where the person who would receive the property is known to dissipate assets and the opposing party would have no meaningful way to recover if the judgment later proves wrong. In such a case, if the absent party would normally invoke a claim of possession after receiving the property, the court should be cautious about deciding in his absence while the assets remain elsewhere. Rashba's reasoning makes the case resemble capital law because the loss could not be undone. The matter should therefore be left to the judge's conscientious assessment for the sake of Heaven.
