There, in the gloss: “A majority exists only in beit din” The Sema’s explanation is difficult. The rule specifically concerns communal representatives in communal affairs, who have authority to judge or compromise; communal acceptance functions like an act of acquisition. Accordingly, unless it was expressly stipulated that they follow a majority, all must agree when acting as arbitrators, just as in siman 12. Ritva’s phrase means that automatic majority rule applies to judges rendering law, not to communal compromise. The Sema may have intended this.
