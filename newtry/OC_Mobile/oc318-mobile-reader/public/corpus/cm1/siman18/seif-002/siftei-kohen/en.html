But four say, etc. Maharshal writes that the same applies if three judges acquit and two say, “I do not know”: the majority is followed. But if two acquit, one finds liable, and two say, “I do not know,” additional judges are required, because there is no majority against those who say, “I do not know.” Thus writes the Sema. The Bach disagrees; see there. See Kesef Mishneh, who cites a responsum of the Rashba. It is explicit there that the ruling follows Kesef Mishneh and Maharshal, and that the Rambam too rules like the first tanna of Rabbi Ila: three must agree on one view, and thereafter the two who do not know are disregarded, unlike Rabbi Ila. This interpretation avoids creating a major dispute among the Rambam, Maharam, and Rashba. See Responsa Maharit, beginning of siman 95.
