If one says, 'I do not know,' additional judges are added. Responsa Beit Yaakov, siman 15, discusses three judges where two favor acquittal and the third favors liability. May the dissenting judge strategically say, 'I do not know,' so additional judges will be added? He concludes that this is forbidden, citing Sanhedrin 8 and Tosafot, which show concern for reputational harm, lest people say judges were added because the original panel did not know the law. The dissenting judge cannot waive the honor of the two who favor acquittal. Shevut Yaakov, volume 1, siman 138, was asked about a scholar appointed with two unlearned judges who wished to prevent their distorted ruling by withdrawing or saying, 'I do not know,' so more judges would be added. He permits saying so when the intent is for the sake of Heaven to reach the true law. Although it is technically false because he knows the law, one may deviate from literal truth for peace, and law itself is peace. He rejects Beit Yaakov's proof and argues that where the judge believes the others are perverting the law, concern for a false judgment outweighs concern for their worldly honor, especially when he places the apparent blame on himself. His words imply permission not only when the other two are ignorant, but even if they are learned, where he is certain the law is otherwise. Urim VeTumim and Netivot HaMishpat understood him this way, and Chavot Yair, siman 147, also appears to agree, though once the judge has already stated his view, the maneuver is improper. Birkei Yosef, letters 3 and 4, disputes this broad reading. He rejects Beit Yaakov's particular proof from Sanhedrin but adduces other sources that a judge may not lie or manipulate procedure to establish his own view. He must state his genuine opinion; if the others maintain theirs, the majority rules. He then distinguishes Shevut Yaakov's case: Beit Yaakov was speaking ordinarily of three competent judges disputing a matter of reasoning, where each side has support, and the minority judge may not devise a tactic to enlarge the panel. Shevut Yaakov dealt with two ignorant judges visibly distorting the law. Even Beit Yaakov would agree there that the competent judge may use such a device to restore a true judgment.
