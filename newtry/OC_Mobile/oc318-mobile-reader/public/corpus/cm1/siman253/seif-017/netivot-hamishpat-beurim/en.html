They are given only a shekel. See Sema, seif katan 37, who suggests a case where the sons died without children, so the inheritance returns to the father and the father's brothers inherit from him; an inheritance coming from the father can then be interrupted. Shach challenges this from the Gemara and explains that the primary reason is that gift language used for the first recipient also assists the second recipient. Although gift language toward an heir can have inheritance consequences, Maharit writes that such language can operate on two levels and retain a gift aspect as well. Therefore the gift language can provide the legal mechanism needed for the later recipient.
