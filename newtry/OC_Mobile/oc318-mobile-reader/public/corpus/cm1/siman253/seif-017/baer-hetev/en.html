Rather. This is stated as a greater novelty. One might have thought that since he said, 'Do not give them,' he expressed an actual prohibition. The ruling teaches that nevertheless they are given support, because we assume that he certainly did not intend to starve his children. He said this only so that they would economize in their maintenance and not pursue luxuries. So writes the Sema. See Responsa Mahara Sasson, siman 188; Responsa Rashach, part II, siman 93; and Responsa Rama, siman 92.
Others. Although those other people are not fit to inherit him, the language of inheritance is nevertheless effective for them by virtue of the language of gift contained in the instruction, 'Give a shekel to my sons,' which is within the time of immediate speech of the inheritance language used for the others, as explained in siman 281, seif 6. This is how the Gemara in the chapter Yesh Nochalin establishes the case, contrary to the Beit Yosef. Independently, the distinction the Beit Yosef attributes to Rashi between whether the sons themselves have sons or not requires study, because the Gemara shows otherwise. If that distinction were correct, why does the Gemara conclude with a refutation rather than answer that the second set had no sons? Responsa Mahara Sasson, simanim 166 and 188, and Mabit, part II, siman 17, already address all this. Shach.
A shekel. Any additional amount required for their support should be given to them from charity. Although one who says that he should not be buried from his own assets is not obeyed, as the Mechaber writes in seif 30, that case is different because the assets are his. How can he direct that his own estate not be used for his burial merely so that his children become richer? Here, however, the money does not belong to his children. He may give his money to whomever he wishes and leave his children to seek charity. Sema.
