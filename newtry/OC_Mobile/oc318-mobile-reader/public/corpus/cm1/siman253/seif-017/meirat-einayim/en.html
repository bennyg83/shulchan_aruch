Or if he said, "Do not give them," etc. This is a novelty. One might have thought that since he said "do not give" as an express warning, we must obey. The ruling teaches that we nevertheless give them, because he surely did not intend to starve his children. He said this only so that they would be economical in their support and not pursue luxuries.
They give them only a shekel. This is in order not to cause loss to others. Above in siman 248 it was explained that inheritance cannot be interrupted, and if one gives a gift to his son and says at the time of the gift that after his son's death it should go to so-and-so, the latter receives nothing. This case differs because he never vested all his property in his sons; he gave them only a shekel each week during their lifetimes. Alternatively, the case is one where his sons died without children. See the Perishah and Derishah here and in siman 248 for more.
Only a shekel. The remainder needed for their necessary support should be supplied to them from tzedakah. Although one who directs that he not be buried from his own estate is not obeyed, as the Mechaber writes in seif 30 and the Tur at the end of this siman, there the property is his own, and he cannot order that his own estate not be used for his burial merely to enrich his children. Here, however, the money does not belong to his sons. He may give his money to whomever he wishes and leave his sons to seek support from charity.
