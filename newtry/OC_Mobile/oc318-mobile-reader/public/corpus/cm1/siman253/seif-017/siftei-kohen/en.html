Others in their place, etc. Although those others are not fit to inherit him, language of inheritance is nevertheless effective for them by virtue of the language of gift that he used when he said, 'Give a shekel to my sons.' That gift language also applies to the others because it was stated within the period of immediate speech, as explained in siman 281, seif 7. This is how the Gemara establishes the case in the chapter Yesh Nochalin, unlike the Beit Yosef. Independently, what the Beit Yosef writes in the name of Rashi, distinguishing between a case where the sons themselves have sons and one where they do not, requires study, for the Gemara proves otherwise. Responsa Rabbi Aharon Sasson, simanim 166 and 188, and Responsa Mabit, part 2, siman 17, already note all of this. See the Sema, seif katan 37, who writes alternatively that his sons died without sons, following the Beit Yosef's explanation of Rashi. A careful reading of the Gemara in the chapter Yesh Nochalin shows that this is incorrect, because otherwise the Gemara could have answered that the second recipient had no sons instead of concluding with a refutation. Examine carefully. This appears correct to me.
