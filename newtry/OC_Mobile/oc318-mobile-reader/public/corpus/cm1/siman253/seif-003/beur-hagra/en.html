Others say, etc. This follows Bava Metzia 9b, "to acquire, who, etc." Rambam explains in chapter 2 of Hilchos Mechirah that the wording implies a future act, as I wrote above siman 197:6. Likewise Tosafos in chapter 10 of Bava Batra: "so and so shall take my property; so and so shall acquire; so and so shall divide; so and so shall merit in my property, etc." Rabbi Meir says he acquired, while the Sages say he acquired only through compulsion of the heirs, according to all views, but not by force of the gift itself. Ra'avad challenges Rambam from the expressions "shall take" and "shall merit." He follows the first opinion. The phrase "to acquire" there is explained like Rashi, as stated. Ramban writes that specifically "I will give" is a mere promise and does not effect a kinyan even for a deathly ill person. See Beer HaGolah. Ra'avad further challenged from chapter 7 of Bava Kamma, "pluck a fig and thereby acquire, etc.," and from Kiddushin, "one of you shall become betrothed to me." See what I wrote there.
