(So and so shall receive such and such from my property.) This requires study, for Maharik, root 94, writes the opposite. He discusses one who used defective wording for the first beneficiary and then wrote, "And further, to so and so, such and such." The questioner assumed that the latter wording is language of gift and also repairs the defective wording used for the first. Maharik answered that this is not so, because the latter expression is itself not language of gift. Merely writing, "To so and so, such and such," without saying "from my property" and without saying "Give," is ineffective even for that beneficiary. He proves this from Gittin 65, concerning Geniva being led out in chains, where saying, "Four hundred zuz to Ravina from the wine of Nehar Panya," was ineffective because he did not say, "Give." Although "from the wine" somewhat resembles saying "from my property," it is plain there that had he said, "So and so shall receive such and such from my property," it would be effective. Maharik's own words require study: why is it not effective even without saying "from my property" as an admission, as the Beit Yosef wrote in the name of the Rashba in siman 222, seif 3, and as the Rama cites in siman 250, seif 3, regarding one who says, "These movables belong to so and so"? One may answer that the latter case is different because he says, "These movables," implying that they were before him at the time. Even there, however, there is a distinction between that wording and saying "from my property." The phrase "these movables" is effective only as an admission, not as language of gift. Consequently, if both parties admit that the beneficiary had not previously entrusted the items to him, but that the owner sought to transfer them through this language of admission, he does not acquire, as the Beit Yosef expressly wrote in the Rashba's name. By contrast, saying, "So and so shall receive such and such from my property," is language of gift, as Maharik wrote and as the Gemara says, "My property to you, and after you to so and so." The reason appears to me to be that language of admission is impossible when he says "from my property," since that expression shows that it never belonged to the beneficiary. It must therefore be language of gift. The Rama's view accordingly requires study. Apart from all this, it is difficult that the wording "from my property to so and so" is effective, as explained in this siman, seif 6. It therefore appears to me that there is a scribal error and the text should read: "language of gift, or from my property; but if he said merely, 'Such and such to so and so,' it is ineffective." I later saw that the Darkei Moshe likewise wrote what is stated here in the name of Maharik, root 94. I therefore say that, despite the Rama's sharpness, he did not read the Maharik carefully in this instance.
