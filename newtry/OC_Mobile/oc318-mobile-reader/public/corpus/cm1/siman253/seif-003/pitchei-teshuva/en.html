“I leave to so-and-so.” See Baer Heitev and Chatam Sofer, Choshen Mishpat siman 112. Shach refers the reader to Maharik, shoresh 94, where Maharik cites early authorities who disagree with the Rosh and hold that the expression “I leave” has no operative meaning. Chatam Sofer points out that Shach himself, below siman 281 seif katan 8, rules against Maharik. The wording there saying that it is “language of inheritance” is a scribal error and should read “language of a deathbed gift,” as is evident from Responsa Divrei Rivot cited by Shach. Shach's reference here to Maharik was not intended to adopt Maharik's view and invalidate wills on that basis, but only to use it where necessary as an additional supporting consideration. Radach and Maharit likewise reject the claim that “I leave” is inherently defective language. Chatam Sofer adds that in his own era, once Shulchan Aruch's rulings had become universally accepted, “I leave” had become a recognized effective formula for a deathbed disposition. It is no worse than a customary form of kinyan or a locally accepted legal expression. In present practice, even the authorities cited by Mordechai and Maharik would agree that “I leave” functions as effective language of conveyance.
