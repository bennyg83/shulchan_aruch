My property to Tuviah. See Noda BiYehuda, second edition, siman 48, concerning a will found after the death of a widow, stating that all her movable property and jewelry should go to her granddaughter Tryna. She had two granddaughters named Tryna, one of whom was married to a Torah scholar. He replied that this is precisely the law stated in Shulchan Aruch concerning one who says, "My property to Tuviah." If the matter concerned Tryna's husband, it would be clear that he would take precedence, since when one of two claimants is a Torah scholar he comes first. But because the will speaks only of Tryna, we should not innovate and say that the wife of a scholar has the same status as the scholar. Had the will concerned money, which would become nichsei melog and from whose fruits the husband would benefit, there would be room for the Torah scholar to acquire. But since the will concerns clothing and jewelry, from which the husband derives no gain, there is no such presumption, and the matter remains doubtful. If, however, the movable property mentioned in the will is not clothing but other movables that the husband may use as nichsei melog, then we may say that the jewelry follows the movable property and all belongs to the scholar's wife. Nevertheless, because it is not entirely clear that the husband's right to the fruits makes the case equivalent to a gift stated directly to him, it is proper to make a compromise. See there.
