And he left two half-palms. Meaning, in each palm another person owns the other half. It is known that harvesting from two half-palms involves more labor than harvesting from one complete palm.
That this is what he intended, etc. Even if he has, etc. The reason is that the burden of proof rests on the claimant. See the Tur, where there is a dispute: some say that if he has a whole palm, he must give the recipient a whole palm. The Beit Yosef writes that Rambam's words in chapter 11 of Hilchot Zechiyah incline toward that view. It also appears that this is the Mechaber's position, since he writes without qualification, "this is what he intended," which naturally implies that because he has no whole palm, he certainly intended these two halves. If so, Rama's gloss that this applies "even if he has whole palms" should have been stated as "some say," since the Mechaber appears not to accept it, especially because the Rosh likewise does not accept it, as the Tur concludes in his name.
