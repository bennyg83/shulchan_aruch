Halves. For example, where each of two people owns a half interest in each palm tree. It is known that harvesting two half-palms involves more labor than harvesting one whole palm. Ibid.
He intended. The reason is that the burden of proof rests on the claimant. See the Tur, which records a dispute. Some say that if the giver owns a whole palm, the recipient must be given a whole palm. The Beit Yosef writes that the Rambam's words in chapter 11 of Hilchot Zechiyah incline toward this view. It appears that this is also the Mechaber's view, since he writes without qualification that 'this is what he intended.' The plain implication is that specifically because the giver owns no whole palm, we know he intended these half interests. If so, it is somewhat difficult that the Rama states 'even if he has...' without presenting it as a dissenting opinion, since the Mechaber apparently disagrees. Ibid.
