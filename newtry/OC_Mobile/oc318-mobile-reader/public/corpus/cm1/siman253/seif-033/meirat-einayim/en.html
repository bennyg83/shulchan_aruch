"They shall belong to the sons of Naftali." Meaning, those sons already exist, as shown by the later phrase "and the sons of Naftali came down, etc." The sons of Shimon did not yet exist, since the condition says "if he will have."
They did not acquire through this gift, etc. Rashba explains that Shimon's sons did not acquire because one cannot transfer property to someone not yet in existence. Naftali's sons did not acquire because the conditions stipulated for them were not fulfilled, since Shimon now does have sons. A person may make a condition concerning something not yet in existence, as the Tur and Rama explain above in siman 209:8. The nearest relative therefore acquires by inheritance, not by the will.

If the witnesses wish, they may make another document. Meaning, we do not say that the witnesses already performed their agency and can no longer write and sign a second document, as the Tur and Mechaber write above in siman 49:6.
They may do so, but they are not obligated, etc. This law is learned from Responsa Ramban, siman 83. Rama likewise writes it above in siman 41:3, although there he does not make it depend on the witnesses' consent and instead says that the claimant comes to beit din, because there he copied Responsa Rashba, siman 978. Both responsa rest on one reason: an ordinary will document is not itself made for collection. This is difficult in light of the Tur and Mechaber in siman 255:6, who say that if one instructed because of death to give a maneh to Shimon, died, and the heirs sold the property, Shimon collects from the purchasers, and the heirs are not believed to say "we paid," so long as the will document remains in Shimon's possession. This appears to show that the document is made for collection. It is strained to say that the authorities disagree, for Rama should then have placed the rulings together and described them as a dispute. One may resolve it, albeit with some difficulty, by saying that siman 255 concerns a case where the shechiv mera acknowledged that he owed so-and-so a maneh. In that case the witnesses write a full debt instrument that supports collection, as Ramban expressly says in the cited responsum. I will quote the relevant portion in brief. Ramban writes that if the witnesses wish to prepare a separate written right for the beneficiary they may do so, because a will document is unlike ordinary documents used for collection. It is merely a memorial of the words, so the heirs cannot deny that the shechiv mera ordered anything. But if the heirs say "we paid," they are believed. Proof is that if it had the law of a debt document, the witnesses would not be permitted to write and sign the shechiv mera's words after his death when he never instructed them to write. Ramban cites the Raavad similarly. He further explains that this is why Rava needed to ask at the end of Gittin whether a shechiv mera who acknowledged a debt must say "write." Rava certainly knew the rule that three visitors may write or judge and two may write but not judge. The distinction is that in an ordinary will their writing is merely a record of what was said, whereas an acknowledgment of an existing debt, once written, is a full document collectible from heirs and encumbered property, against which the debtor cannot say "I paid." Therefore Rava asked whether the dying man must specifically instruct "write," just as a healthy person who acknowledges a debt must. Ramban repeats this principle several times: a will document is not for collection and the heirs may say it was paid, except where it records an acknowledgment of debt. Accordingly, the Tur and Mechaber in siman 255 must concern such an acknowledgment document or the like. There is some support in their precise wording, "since the will document is in his hand," calling it a document rather than merely a will. Examine this carefully. See also what I write there in siman 255 and above in siman 41:3.
