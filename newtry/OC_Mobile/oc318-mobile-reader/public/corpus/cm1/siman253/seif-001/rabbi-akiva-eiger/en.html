Note: The Derishah likewise explains that the Tur follows Tosafot in holding that a gift "from now if he dies" is regarded as an inheritance for this purpose. Shitah Mekubetzet, however, citing Rabbeinu Yonah, disagrees. Since the recipient acquires retroactively, it is not called an inheritance. Rabbeinu Yonah therefore explains the sugya differently: since we see generally that even where a kinyan is made a person's intent is not finalized until he has finished dealing with that matter, all the more so his intent regarding a will is not finalized until he has completed the entire matter. We must therefore be concerned that at that moment he may have retracted in his heart. At that time the witnesses do not yet have the status of judges, for perhaps the disposition still stands subject to retraction; they acquire the status of judges only once the will has been completed with settled and final intent. See there. According to this, it appears that with our form of deathbed gift, a judgment rendered before he has completed that matter is ineffective. But after he has completed it, they may judge even initially. Although he can still retract, the disposition is not presumed to stand for retraction.
