Two people who entered, etc. It has already been explained in the preceding simanim that the words of a shechiv mera are as though written and delivered. Thus everything he instructs is effective even before two people, or even privately where no one disputes what he said. But the two before whom he instructed do not thereby become judges empowered to resolve every doubt and establish each person's rights under the will. If he instructed before three, however, even if he did not designate them for this purpose but they entered on their own to visit him, they may act as judges. This is learned from the verse in Parashat Pinchas concerning inheritance, "It shall be for the Children of Israel as a statute of judgment." Inheritance thus requires judicial law, and judgment requires no fewer than three judges. Even when he instructs that part of his estate be given to others, the Sages said that a shechiv mera gift has the law of inheritance in that it does not take effect until after death, he may retract if he recovers, he may retract while ill and give it to another, and the estate remains liable for the support of the wife and daughters, as the Tur and Mechaber write in siman 252. Therefore the three who heard the will likewise have the status of judges.
They write but do not render judgment. If a doubt arises concerning the will, they send their written account to three judges, who decide the matter based on the written will.
When does this apply? By day, etc. This follows the ordinary law of judges. If they themselves see or hear the matter at a time fit for adjudication, namely by day, they can judge it, because hearing is no less than seeing. But if they saw or heard it at night, which is not a time to begin a judicial proceeding, they have the status of witnesses, and a witness cannot become a judge over that matter even during the day. This was thoroughly explained above at the beginning of siman 7 in the Tur and Mechaber.
