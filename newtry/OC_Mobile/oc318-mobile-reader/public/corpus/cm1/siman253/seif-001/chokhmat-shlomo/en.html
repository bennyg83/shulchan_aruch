Seif 16, gloss. Some disagree with these rulings and hold that if he said to give money to her for marriage and she died, her heirs do not acquire it. Note: In my opinion there is a clear proof for this from Nazir 24. Rava explains that even according to the Sages, when a husband transfers property to his wife he gives it only for what she needs, not for what she does not need. Thus even without an express condition, a general transfer is construed according to its purpose, and property unnecessary for that purpose reverts to the giver. The case must concern an actual completed transfer into her possession, for otherwise the Gemara's question how she could consecrate property not yet hers would remain. Nevertheless, once her husband annuls the need for which it was given, the animal returns to him. All the more so where the giver expressly states that the gift is for marriage: if she dies before the purpose is fulfilled, it returns to the giver. This is a strong proof and the contrary opinions require study.
