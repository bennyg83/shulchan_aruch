The language of Rambam, chapter 3 of Hilkhot Sanhedrin, law 6, from the statement of Rav Yehudah in chapter 8 of Bava Batra at the end of 113a.
The Kesef Mishneh writes that although testimony generally requires formal declaration, except for sanctifying the new moon, where the verse says 'such you shall see and sanctify,' and rabbinic validation of documents, the Ritva answers that what the dying person commanded in their presence itself counts as testimony because a litigant's admission is equivalent to one hundred witnesses. He further cites the Rashbam that this implies even the sick person cannot retract, such as a partial gift of a dying person accompanied by a kinyan. The Tur cites this but objects that such a gift has the law of a healthy person's gift, so the witnesses cannot simultaneously function as judges. Rather, the case concerns a dying person distributing all his property, and they cannot render judgment until after the matter is concluded in a manner in which he can no longer retract. The Bach explains that this concerns one who distributes his property with an express kinyan to each recipient from now if he dies, so that he cannot retract before death unless he recovers from his illness. This resolves the Beit Yosef's objection to the Tur.
The statement of Rav Chisda there. The Tur writes in the name of Ri that if they nevertheless rendered judgment, their judgment is valid. The Beit Yosef writes that this appears from Rambam in chapter 5 of Hilkhot Edut. The Rama likewise writes so above in siman 7.
