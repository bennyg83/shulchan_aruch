(Seif 1: If they wish, they may adjudicate.) In Bava Batra 113 the Gemara derives this from the verse, "It shall be for the children of Israel as a statute of judgment," teaching that the entire passage may become a judicial proceeding. Tosafot explain that the persons present when the testament was made become judges against their will, and none of the recipients can say, "Let us go before another beit din." This raises a difficulty with our master's rejection of the Rashbam. The Rashbam explains that the case concerns a partial gift accompanied by a kinyan, but our master objects that such a gift has the law of a healthy person's gift in every respect and instead explains that it concerns a deathly ill person dividing his estate, following Rashi. The difficulty is that a deathly ill person's gift without a kinyan is effective only rabbinically, while the Gemara is now deriving the law from a verse. Tosafot already raised this difficulty, as the Beit Yosef records. It appears to me that our master's meaning is that one cannot explain the verse as speaking only of a partial gift with a kinyan and not of a gift of the entire estate. Under Torah law, the latter too must involve a kinyan, yet it still bears the character of inheritance because death was mentioned, as Tosafot write. Under rabbinic law, it applies without a kinyan because the words of a deathly ill person are treated as written and delivered. Therefore, when our master begins the siman by speaking of one who divides his estate, he does not mention a kinyan, because he states the law as it applies to us. The Gemara itself, however, concerns a deathly ill person's gift accompanied by a kinyan, as explained. This also resolves our master's objection to the Rashbam. The Rashbam may be understood as speaking of a partial gift accompanied by a kinyan in which the donor expressly characterized it as a deathly ill person's gift. Whenever death is mentioned, it is called inheritance for purposes of this verse. It appears that the Tur objected only because the Rashbam did not expressly state that death was mentioned. The Tur's statement that the judges should not render judgment in a manner that prevents retraction is also difficult, for the case concerns a gift of the entire estate by a deathly ill person, which may be retracted. And if it takes effect from now, it is a full gift by a healthy person, as the Tur wrote above. It appears that "to render judgment" means that they should not implement the judgment in a manner preventing retraction, such as by delivering the property to one of them pursuant to the testament, for by such delivery he acquires, as stated in siman 250.
