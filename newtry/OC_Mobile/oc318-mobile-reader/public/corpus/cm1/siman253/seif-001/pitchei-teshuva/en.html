And if he commanded before them, they write. Shevut Yaakov, part II, siman 169, cites Mabit, part II, siman 148, who writes that a testament cannot be established through a single witness, even if that witness is a rabbi and judge. Mabit nevertheless suggested that the heir could be required under cherem to declare that he does not believe the witness's account to be true. Shevut Yaakov strongly rejects this. If the Torah did not give one witness legal credibility for the testament, why should the heir have to swear that he does not believe him? After extended proofs he concludes that the suggestion should not be accepted: where the deceased gave the instruction only before one witness and not in the heir's presence, the heir need not fulfill it. Responsa Kiryat Channah, siman 19, rules similarly.
