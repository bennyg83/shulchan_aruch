To visit the sick person. A dying man was asked whether his property should be sealed, and he answered, "Everything belongs to my wife." Perhaps this refers only to movable property subject to sealing, not to land. If he also said that she was exempt from an oath and that her ketubah should be collected against everything, while leaving some property outside the disposition, that can establish that he did not appoint her merely as administrator but actually intended to transfer property to her. The wording "it is hers" rather than "it shall be hers" may or may not function as an admission. It can also be read as referring to her ketubah rights. There may be an ambiguity comparable to that discussed at the end of siman 197. Language such as "acquire it" or "that she should acquire" is generally construed against the holder of the instrument where multiple readings are possible. Yet the ordinary rules of a deathbed will may counsel against forcing such narrow readings, just as the usual word "give" is given operative effect. This requires further study. A man directed that one of his three sons receive half of his house and pay his wife's ketubah. That wording does not necessarily exempt the wife from the oath ordinarily required for collection. It can mean only that the son must pay whatever beit din determines is due, while the heirs remain in possession against her. If the deceased also owed another documented debt, the statement does not prove that only the ketubah falls on that son's share. Rather, the entire ketubah is imposed upon him alone, while the other debts remain shared with his brothers. Any portion of the half house exceeding his ordinary inheritance share is a gift and is not itself automatically burdened beyond the amount properly due for the ketubah. Where similar movable property is disputed and the son seized it only after the uncertainty arose, such seizure is weak even if he makes a definite claim. If he had been lawfully in possession during his father's lifetime, his possession may be stronger, perhaps even where the brothers claim certainty. The language may itself indicate that the gift was intended to include the normal inheritance component as well. This requires further study.
