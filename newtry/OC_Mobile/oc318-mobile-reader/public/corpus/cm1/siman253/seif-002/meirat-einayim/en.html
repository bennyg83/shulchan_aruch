If he says, "So-and-so shall benefit from my property," he does not acquire. Meaning, in such a formulation we do not apply the rule that a shechiv mera's words are as though written and delivered. Even if a kinyan was made, some say it is merely a kinyan devarim, while others say it is effective. See Darkei Moshe and the Derishah.
If he said, "I leave," it is language of gift. This applies specifically to a shechiv mera, because that is the ordinary language of a dying person who says that he leaves from his estate such and such to one person and such and such to another. With a healthy person, this formulation is ineffective according to all opinions. The Mechaber first states that "I leave" works for a shechiv mera, and in the next seif says, "These expressions, etc., some say they likewise work for a healthy person," which sounds as though "I leave" is included. That is incorrect. Everyone agrees that "I leave" does not work for a healthy person. The Tur places the rule that "I leave" works for a shechiv mera after recording the dispute concerning whether the other expressions work for a healthy person, and its meaning is therefore clear.
If he used the expression "shall fall to so-and-so," etc. This is language of inheritance, because we find "fall" used for inheritance in Scripture, as in Parashat Masei, "This is the land that shall fall to you as an inheritance."
As stated in siman 252. Meaning, there it is written that unless he delivered the money to a third party for that purpose, the rule of "it is a mitzvah to fulfill the words of the deceased" does not apply.
