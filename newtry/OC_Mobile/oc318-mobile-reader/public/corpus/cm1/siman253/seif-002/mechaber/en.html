If a deathly ill person says that so-and-so shall take, hold, acquire possession of, or acquire all or part of his property, all these are expressions of gift. If he says that one fit to inherit shall inherit or take as inheritance, he acquires. If he says, "So-and-so shall benefit from my property," "stand in it," "see it," or "rely upon it," he does not acquire. "I leave to so-and-so" is language of gift. "Such-and-such shall fall to so-and-so" is language of inheritance. Rama: These rules require some operative language of gift. Merely saying, "such-and-such of my property to so-and-so," is ineffective. A direction to pay a wife's ketubah in cash is not language of gift, and the heirs need not fulfill it merely under the mitzvah of carrying out the deceased's wishes.
