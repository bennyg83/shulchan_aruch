Expressions, etc. See Responsa Avodat HaGershuni, siman 45, who wrote to the rabbi who posed the question: You treated it as fully established that the expression “he shall take” constitutes a gift even when used by a healthy person, as though this were an uncontested rule. This is not so. The view of the Beit Yosef appears to be that the matter is one of doubt in law, since he records both opinions with the formulation “some say.” See what I wrote above, siman 241, seif 4, seif katan 5. Nevertheless, it appears possible to prove that the Beit Yosef regarded the Rosh’s position, that these expressions are effective even for a healthy person, as primary. The Beit Yosef rules that the expression “I leave” is effective for a person on his deathbed because it is language of gift. It follows that he must hold that these expressions are likewise effective for a healthy person. Otherwise, why did Rav Sheshet in the chapter Mi Shemet not include the expression “I leave” together with “he shall take,” “he shall receive,” and the like? Rather, Rav Sheshet was addressing both a healthy person and a person on his deathbed. He therefore did not include “I leave,” because that expression certainly is ineffective for a healthy person. See Maharik, shoresh 94, who states this explicitly and explains at length that the two issues depend upon one another. Accordingly, you were correct to treat the expression “he shall take” as fully effective language, for this is necessarily the view of the Beit Yosef despite his presenting the two opinions as “some say.” See also the next seif katan.
