"Give two hundred zuz to the poor," etc. See what I wrote above in siman 7.
They give to each and every one. Meaning, beit din or the heirs give the two hundred zuz to both groups together. It is unreasonable to say that each group receives the full amount, since he vowed only one sum. Moreover, in the Tosefta, the source of this law, it says "they give it to both," and the singular "it" refers to the one sum he vowed, which is divided, half to one and half to the other. The Tosefta further concludes in the name of Rav Acha that one who says "give two hundred to the poor" gives it to the poor of all Israel. Obviously this does not mean that every poor person in Israel receives the full two hundred. Rather, the one amount is divided among them collectively. The same therefore applies to "give it to both." I brought further proof in the Perishah and in Yoreh Deah siman 258.
