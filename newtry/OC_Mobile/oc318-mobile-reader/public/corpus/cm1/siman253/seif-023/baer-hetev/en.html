They should give. Meaning, the beit din or the heirs should give the two hundred zuz jointly to both claimants. It is unreasonable to say that each should receive the entire amount, because the donor undertook only one sum. Moreover, the Tosefta, which is the source of this law, says, 'they should give it to both of them.' The singular 'it' shows that it refers to the one sum that was pledged, which is to be divided, half to one and half to the other. The Tosefta then cites Rav Acha that if one says, 'Give two hundred zuz to the poor,' the amount is distributed among the poor of all Israel. It is obviously impossible to mean that every poor person in Israel receives the full amount. The same interpretation therefore applies here: the one amount is given jointly to both. So writes the Sema. This also appears from the Rambam's responsum concerning a flask of oil cited by the Beit Yosef in siman 258. Where, however, a person regularly attends two synagogues in two cities and says, 'Give a Torah scroll to the synagogue,' the language that it should be given to both apparently means a Torah scroll to each one. This requires study. Shach.
