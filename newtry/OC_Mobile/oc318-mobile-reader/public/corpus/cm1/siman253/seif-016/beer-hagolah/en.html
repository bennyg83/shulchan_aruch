Samekh) The Ritva writes this in the name of his teachers and in the name of the Rashba, as cited by the Beit Yosef in seif 30.
Ayin) There, and the Rashba likewise writes this in a responsum.
Peh) The Ritva explains that in such a case the stated purpose is not a binding restriction but merely an indication of place or purpose. This resembles Bava Batra 78b, where one gives a dinar to a poor person to buy a tunic and the poor person may instead buy a cloak. The Rashba likewise writes that since no time was fixed for giving the money, even if she spent it on other matters rather than marriage, what she did remains legally effective; she merely acted contrary to the deceased's instruction. We do not rule like the view that one who departs from the householder's instructions is a thief. The Rif rules this in HaOmanin.
