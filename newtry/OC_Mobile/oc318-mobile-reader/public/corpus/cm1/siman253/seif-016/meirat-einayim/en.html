They do not give it to her until she marries, etc. But one who says, etc. At first glance the distinction appears to depend upon the word “give.” In the first case he merely says, “Two hundred zuz to so and so for her dowry,” which suggests that payment should be made only when the time for the dowry arrives. In the latter case he says, “Give two hundred zuz to so and so for her dowry,” which suggests immediate payment, and the reference to the dowry merely identifies the preferred purpose of the gift. The Beit Yosef cites Ritva to this effect. Ritva writes that if he merely designates a dowry, they wait until she marries, but if he says “give two hundred zuz to so and so for her dowry,” they must give it now, and if she dies before marriage her heirs acquire it. The purpose language is then merely an indication of his wish, comparable to one who gives a dinar to a poor person to buy a garment, where the poor person may buy another article. Rashba writes similarly and adds that if the woman spends the money for another purpose, the expenditure is legally valid although she violated the donor's wish. The Tur's language, however, does not appear to accept this distinction simply. He writes that even if he said “give,” if he gave items valued at two hundred zuz for her dowry and their value later rose or fell, the gain or loss belongs to the heirs. That would be difficult if the gift became hers immediately. The Ir Shushan attempts to distinguish between saying “for her dowry” and “as her dowry,” based on slight wording differences in the Shulchan Aruch, but I found no reliable source for such a distinction. Darkei Moshe cites Rashba that if the daughter gave to her mother what her father had designated for her and she died before marriage, then even if she had not acquired by gift, if she was the sole heir she acquires by inheritance and the transfer to the mother can stand. Rama cites a dissenting view that if the donor instructed that it be given to her to enable her marriage and she dies before marriage, her heirs do not acquire. The Beit Yosef later qualifies that, while she is alive, the money remains the heirs' property but they must give it to her immediately rather than wait until marriage, and if she dies it returns. Darkei Moshe in Even HaEzer siman 53 uses this to mediate between the views.
