They need not give, etc. See the Sema, seif katan 35, through the words, 'and it is strained to distinguish,' etc. In my view the matter is simple and this is plainly the Tur's intent. The distinction written by the Ritva is whether he said, 'Give,' or did not say, 'Give.' The Tur did not base the law on immediate delivery. This is clear. See Responsa Rabbi Eliyahu ibn Chaim, siman 28, and Responsa Masat Binyamin.
And so it appears to me to be the principal ruling, etc. Rabbi David ibn Lev, part 2, siman 67, rules likewise. Maharshal writes in the ninth chapter of Bava Kamma, siman 66, in the name of the Hagahot Maimoniyot that if others gave a man money to assist with his daughter's marriage and he then refrained from marrying her off, and certainly if she died, he must return the money to the donors, because they gave it only for the purpose of marrying her off. It appears that all authorities agree where the donors expressly said that the gift was on condition. If they did not state a condition, the matter would seem to depend on the dispute here. However, this is not correct, as appears upon examining the Darchei Moshe and Responsa Rabbi David ibn Lev, part 2, siman 67, simanim 12 and 13, and part 3, siman 58. See also Responsa Rabbi Shmuel Kohen, part 2, siman 93.
