(Seif 29, except where the other is his neighbor.) Beit Yosef writes that this implies that residence nearby is also required, and that habitual business dealings alone are insufficient. The Sema writes that even if he does not live near him, he is called his neighbor because he is associated with him in business. The words of Beit Yosef appear correct, for otherwise this would already be included in the category of one with whom he is familiar, mentioned at the beginning of the seif. Rather, here they are not so extensively accustomed to one another, but only somewhat, and residence nearby adds the personal closeness necessary to create familiarity.
