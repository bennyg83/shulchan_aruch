(Seif 8: Or to so and so, my creditor.) Although the Shulchan Aruch states this only concerning the phrase, "the sons of my wife," the same law applies here, for the reason is identical. Just as he must say, "To so and so, my wife," to identify which woman he means, so too he must say, "To so and so, my creditor," to identify which person he means. There is therefore no superfluous wording. Although, when our master presented the alternative opinion, he wrote, "Likewise, where he said, 'To so and so, my creditor,' the creditor has the upper hand, and it does not appear as interest," it seems that he stated this as a greater novelty. According to the opinion that the donor gave him a gift, the gift has no connection to the debt and there is plainly no question of interest. But even according to the opinion that the creditor has the choice, one might think that since the donor gave him the option between collecting the debt and taking the gift, the two are connected and it appears as interest. We are therefore taught that it does not appear as interest unless he said, "toward my debt." The Sema in seif katan 17 did not explain it this way. I have written what appears correct to me, and the Rama's position is sound.
