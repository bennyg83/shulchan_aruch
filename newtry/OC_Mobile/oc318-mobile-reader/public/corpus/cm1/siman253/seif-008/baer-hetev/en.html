Additional. Since he did not need to say 'as is fitting for him' but nevertheless used the extra phrase, we interpret the surplus language as intended to add something. This resembles the Tur and Mechaber's rule in siman 214 concerning one who sells a house. A pit and cistern are not included automatically, but if he says 'except the pit,' although that reservation was unnecessary, the extra phrase is interpreted as reserving also a path to the pit. So writes the Sema. See Responsa R. Moshe Alshich, siman 12; Responsa R. Aharon Sasson, siman 146; and Responsa Rashach associated with part 2, siman 23.
As his firstborn share. Meaning, he did not say 'as is fitting for him.' Sema.
As his debt. Even if the creditor seized the money, beit din removes it from him. See Responsa R. Moshe Alshich, siman 12. Shach.
An ordinary son. The father has authority to reduce the inheritance share of ordinary sons, as derived from 'on the day he causes his sons to inherit.' We therefore say he intended the son to receive only two hundred zuz as his inheritance share, because that is the implication of the phrase 'in his inheritance.' A firstborn is different, because the Torah says 'he may not make the son of the beloved the firstborn,' from which the Sages learn that the father cannot reduce the firstborn share. Likewise, he cannot unilaterally reduce what is owed to a creditor or wife. Therefore in those cases we interpret his words so the recipient has the more favorable option. Sema.
My debt. Because this wording does not appear to involve interest, since he did not say 'as payment of my debt.' Ibid.

This opinion holds that the Gemara required the surplus words 'as is fitting for him' only when the giver also said, 'Give to my firstborn son,' because mentioning 'firstborn' suggests he means the statutory firstborn share unless extra language gives the recipient the better option. But if he merely says, 'Give two hundred zuz to my son,' or 'to my wife,' there is no need to add 'as is fitting for him or her.' Ibid.
To so-and-so. See Responsa R. Moshe Alshich, siman 12, who discusses these laws at some length and will clarify them. See also Responsa Maharit, siman 112, folio 141b and c. The Sema writes that according to the Rosh, the statement is treated as a gift only when he says 'to my creditor,' not when he says 'to so-and-so, my creditor.' Tosafot in chapter Almanah Nizonet write that naming the person is even stronger than merely saying 'my creditor' with respect to concern for interest. Shach.
