There, law 6, from the Mishnah in chapter 9 of Bava Batra, 140b. The Maggid Mishneh writes in the name of the Rashba that the Mechaber explains the Mishnah as referring to a dying person, as does Rambam. The Tur likewise writes in seif 2 in the name of the Rif and Rabbi Yosef HaLevi that with a healthy person the case is difficult to formulate: even if a kinyan were made, money is not acquired by barter; moreover, it is an asmachta because he says 'if she gives birth,' and it is also merely a kinyan on words because he says 'he shall take.' Others disagree and say the case can exist even with a healthy person, where he said that real property worth one hundred should be given, together with a kinyan and the wording 'he shall take.' Some great authorities further say that even 'I will give you such-and-such' is not considered merely a kinyan on words. This is primary. The Tur likewise writes that since he obligates himself to give one hundred and encumbers his property, the obligation is valid, and there is no asmachta, but rather an ordinary conditional gift that takes effect if the condition occurs.
The Maggid Mishneh writes that the Gemara establishes the Mishnah, which says he does not take, according to Rabban Shimon ben Gamliel, who holds that sanctity does not take effect on such property. The halachah does not follow him. This is straightforward.
