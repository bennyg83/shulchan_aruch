If he said: If my wife gives birth, etc. The Bach writes that the Rambam, in chapter 8 of the Laws of Acquisition and Gift, rules that when a dangerously ill person directs that a gift be given to a fetus in its mother's womb, the fetus acquires because a person's intent is especially close to his own child. This implies that the rule is limited to a dangerously ill person, in accordance with the view of the Rif and Rabbi Yosef Halevi, as the Maggid Mishneh explains. Although the Rambam states the rule without qualification in chapter 22 of the Laws of Sale, he does so only because there he is discussing the general principle that a person may transfer to his own unborn child in a manner unavailable with respect to another fetus. Nevertheless, the special force that gives effect to the gift is the law of a dangerously ill person's instructions. The Bach therefore rejects the view that a healthy person may make such a gift merely because his intent is close to his child. The practical ruling follows the Rambam as explained by the Maggid Mishneh and the earlier authorities.
