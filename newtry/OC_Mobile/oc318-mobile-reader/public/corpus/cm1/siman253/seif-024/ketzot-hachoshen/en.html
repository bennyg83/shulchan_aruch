One who gave his property to his sons and daughter. A question arises whether a husband inherits a deathbed gift given to his wife before she actually collected it. Rama in Even HaEzer rules that a gift directed to a son for payment only after two or three years is considered expectant property, so the son's wife cannot collect her ketubah from it. Chelkat Mechokek understands this broadly. Even if no fixed delay was stated, a deathbed gift not yet collected is expectant property. Terumat HaDeshen states this explicitly in a case where a grandfather gave twenty litra to his granddaughter, who died before receiving them. He rules that the husband would not inherit them directly because they were still uncollected and therefore expectant, like a documented loan. The result changed only because she left a son who inherited the right and later died. Divrei Rivot likewise rules that a husband does not inherit his wife's uncollected deathbed gift. The same follows in reverse: a son's wife cannot collect her ketubah from an uncollected deathbed gift to him. This applies where the donor gave an amount "from my property" that still requires collection from the estate. If he gave a specific identified object, the object is treated as standing in the recipient's domain wherever it is, like property held in deposit, and is not expectant in the same sense. In that case it can be subject to the husband's inheritance or ketubah rights.
