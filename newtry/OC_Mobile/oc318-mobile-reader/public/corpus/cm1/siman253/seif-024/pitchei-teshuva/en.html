But if he said that he owes Toviyah. See Shach, seif katan 39, who argues that the Tosefta can be read according to the view that the parties divide, whereas we rule by judicial discretion. Ketonet Passim, at the end of the chapter Get Pashut, questions this answer. The Ran and Maggid Mishneh’s difficulty is that the Tosefta does not follow an assessment that the decedent loved one claimant more than the other. The Shach has not answered that point. The dispute whether to divide or apply judicial discretion concerns cases in which both claimants are equally neighbors or equally Torah scholars and there is no compelling assessment. By contrast, the preference of a neighbor or relative is based on assessment of intent and does not depend on that dispute. This therefore requires study.
