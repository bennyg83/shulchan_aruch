If a deathly ill person says, "Give two hundred zuz to A, three hundred to B, and four hundred to C," the first named does not acquire priority, provided the donor did not pause between the gifts in a manner showing reconsideration. If only nine hundred remain, the recipients divide proportionately according to their stated gifts. If a debt of four hundred fifty is produced against the estate, each bears it proportionately: A pays one hundred, B one hundred fifty, and C two hundred. If he says, "Give two hundred to A, after him three hundred to B, and after him four hundred to C," priority follows the order stated. Rama: Some say that if equal sums are given successively, the earlier recipient also has priority even without the words "after him," because otherwise the donor could have stated one aggregate gift to all. Others disagree. Therefore, a creditor collects first from the last recipient, then from the one before him, and only then from the first. Rama: The same applies when a healthy person caused them to acquire through another.
