To the first. Meaning, one might have thought that since he did not simply say, 'Give one hundred to so-and-so my son, and the remainder shall inherit everything,' this indicates that he retracted what he said initially, and intended that the second son exceed the first only by one hundred. Thus, when he said 'shall inherit,' he meant that the second son should be like an heir with respect to the remainder. Therefore it teaches that we do not say this; rather, he meant that the second son alone shall inherit the remainder. As for why he needed to say that the first should take one hundred, it may be that he had two hundred zuz belonging to his two sons in his possession, and he instructed that those be given to them, while they were not included in his own property. So writes the Tur in the name of a responsum of the Rosh. Ibid.
