The heir must be buried. They are in the place of their father and property, whose father’s name is named after them and theirs are buried:
The first has nothing more, etc. We do not say that because the donor said, “one maneh to so and so my son, and so and so shall inherit,” rather than simply saying “give one maneh to the first and the rest to the second,” he meant to retract from the earlier implication that the second should receive everything else and instead intended the second merely to share the remainder as an heir together with comparable heirs. If that were his meaning, he should have said “they shall inherit” in the plural. We therefore understand “he shall inherit” to mean that the second alone inherits the remainder. The reason he still expressly mentioned the maneh to the second can be that the donor had a separate two hundred zuz belonging to the two sons that was not part of the estate and specifically directed those funds to them. The Tur cites this from a responsum of the Rosh.
