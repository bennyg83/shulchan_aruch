A deathly ill person, etc. See Rashbam, s.v. "latfiach": "and there are, etc." So rule the Rif, Ran, and other poskim.
(Collected note) A deathly ill person, etc., and there is no, etc. Rif, unlike Rabbeinu Chananel and Rashbam, who write that Sumchus follows his own view. As stated in the gloss, Sumchus disputes only where the property stands in an alley, as explained in chapter HaShoel, or where there is some weakness in the possessor's claim in a case of doubt. See Ramban.
Others say, etc. According to Rashbam's explanation. See Tosafos there, s.v. "ein, etc.," and there 35a, s.v. "u'mah, etc." The Rosh likewise writes so.
And all this, etc. As stated there 92a: "let us see, etc." See Rashbam and Tosafos there. (Collected note) All this applies specifically to a gift, etc. As stated at the beginning of chapter 6 there and the beginning of chapter 5 of Bava Kamma: "let us see whether the value, etc." Ramban in the name of Rabbeinu Chananel and the Geonim. See there and what I wrote above siman 220:8 in the gloss. Ramban writes, however, that the Tosefta teaches it regarding a seller, for it reads: "Sumchus says, one who sells a share to his fellow, etc." It may be answered that since the Gemara changed the case and spoke of a gift of a deathly ill person, we do not rule in accordance with the Tosefta's broader formulation.
