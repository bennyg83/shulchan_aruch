If a deathly ill person says, "Give so-and-so a share in my wine pit," the recipient takes one-quarter. If he says, "Give him a share in it for barrels," he has indicated a smaller portion and takes one-eighth. If he says, "for a pot," he takes one-twelfth. If he says, "for a small drinking vessel," he takes one-sixteenth, because he revealed an intent to give only a small share. These measures are not extended by analogy to other cases. Rama: Some say that in every such expression he receives only an unspecified minimal amount. These rules concern gifts; in a sale, the price paid indicates the intended quantity.
