[Sema, letter 35] It is immediately in her possession to give to her, for otherwise the profit would be. Note: In my opinion, where one says, "Give four hundred zuz from my wine," payment must be made immediately, yet if the wine rises in value the profit belongs to the heirs, and if it declines in value the loss is borne by the heirs, because he linked the money to the wine. The same should apply here, where he said "articles worth two hundred zuz." This implies that actual articles must be given, and they must give articles until their value reaches the stated sum. It is therefore precisely like saying, "Give four hundred zuz from my wine." The Rashba's words apply only where he fixes a definite sum without linking it to particular property. Since he said "give," she immediately acquires that amount of money. See Beit Yosef, who is troubled by the duplication of this law in the Tur, because the rule here would already follow from the case in seif 27 of "four hundred zuz from my wine." See there, and see the Perishah and Bach for their resolutions. It is therefore evident that they plainly understood "articles worth two hundred zuz" to be exactly like "four hundred zuz from my wine." The Rashba must consequently agree in this case; his ruling concerns only a case where the gift is not linked to particular articles. This is clear.
