The fruit of this palm tree, he has said nothing. See Responsa Rabbi Akiva Eiger, end of siman 146. He writes that regarding the basic rule that a person on his deathbed who says “so-and-so shall take the fruit of this palm tree” is ineffective because the fruit is not yet in existence and such a gift would be unavailable to a healthy person, he is uncertain what happens where the fruit had not grown at the time of the statement but had grown by the time of death. Must the property exist at the time of the statement because the statement is treated like a healthy person’s act of acquisition, or do we say that, so long as he did not retract, the statement continues until death and is as though he gave the instruction at death? The simpler view is the first, that the fruit must have come into existence at the time of the statement. Yet Tosafot to Gittin 14b, who emphasize a case where the recipient’s heirs were not born until after the giver’s death, rather than the simpler case where they were born only after the giver’s statement, may imply that birth before death suffices even if it occurred after the statement. The matter still requires study.
