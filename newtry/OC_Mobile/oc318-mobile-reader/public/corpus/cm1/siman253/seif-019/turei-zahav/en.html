(Seif 33, to strengthen his right.) Since he first wrote, “in whichever house he wishes,” even though he later wrote, “whether in the upper houses,” and the like, the latter phrase does not limit the gift but enlarges it. Had he intended to limit it, he would not have used the word “whether,” but would have said, “in the houses in which I presently reside and make use, in the upper houses or in the lower houses.” The word “whether” is therefore an added clarification that the gift was made generously. It follows that “the upper houses” means all the upper houses, including the uppermost of the upper houses. This somewhat implies that even if he did not first say, “in whichever house he wishes,” but said only, “whether in the upper houses or in the lower houses,” the language still strengthens the beneficiary’s right, because he did not simply say, “in the upper houses or the lower houses.” This requires study.
