"To my daughter in her ketubah," etc. Through the words "according to the appraisal of the dowry," this is also the Tur's wording. Their intent is that everything a man brings in for his daughter as dowry is accepted by the groom as nichsei tzon barzel, and he writes that amount in her ketubah, obligating all his property for it, in addition to the maneh or two hundred zuz instituted by the Sages. This is what is called her dowry. Therefore the wording begins, "to my daughter in her ketubah," because the value of the dowry she brings in is written into the ketubah. In some places, for the bride's honor, jewelry and clothing she brings are appraised at one third or sometimes one half more than their actual value, as the Tur and Mechaber explain in Even HaEzer siman 66. There too it is explained that although the ketubah was written according to the inflated appraisal, when the ketubah was collected she would receive only the actual value of the dowry, with one third or one half deducted according to the local custom of overappraisal. Therefore, even if he instructed that his daughter receive four hundred zuz "in her ketubah," she receives only two hundred.
