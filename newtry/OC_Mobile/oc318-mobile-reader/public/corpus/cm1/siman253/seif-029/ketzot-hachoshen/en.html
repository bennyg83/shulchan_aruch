Under Rabbeinu Tam, shuda deDayanei depends on judicial discretion, so Shach suggests there should be no distinction between a gift and a debt or deposit. There is no difficulty. Tosafot in Ketubot, citing Rabbeinu Tam, says that even under his approach, when several creditors or ketubah claimants have instruments of equal date, all agree that they divide. The Sages protected creditors because of concern for closing the door to borrowers and protected a wife's ketubah because of marital policy. Shuda applies to purchasers or gifts, not to ordinary debts. Accordingly, even Rabbeinu Tam agrees that a debt is divided rather than subjected to shuda. A deposit, however, does not involve the concern of closing the door to borrowers and can still be subject to shuda under Rabbeinu Tam. Rama carefully mentions only that he "owes Toviah," not that he holds Toviah's deposit. This wording is exact.
