Yod) This is the language of the Rambam there, chapter 11, halachah 2, from an incident in the ninth chapter of Ketubot 85b before Rabbi Yochanan.
Kaf) The Rosh likewise writes this there.
Meaning, one who is regularly associated with him.
Lamed) Rashi explains that ordinarily a person seeks to justify his conduct and gain merit at the time of death.
Mem) According to the reason that a Torah scholar takes precedence because the deceased is presumed to favor him more closely, the rule follows that closeness of mind. But according to Rashi's explanation, that a person ordinarily seeks merit at death, in my humble opinion even if it is known that he favored his neighbor or relative more, the Torah scholar still takes precedence.
Nun) There in the Gemara, shuda dedayanei, as Rashi explains.
Samekh) The Rosh writes this in his rulings there, applying the verse, "Better is a nearby neighbor than a distant brother."
Ayin) The Maggid Mishneh writes likewise in the name of the Tosefta in the chapter Get Pashut. He explains that a person commonly borrows from or entrusts property to someone with whom he is not regularly associated.
