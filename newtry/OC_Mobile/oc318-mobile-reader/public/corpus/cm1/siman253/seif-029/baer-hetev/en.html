To him. We say that because this claimant presented himself first, it indicates that he is certain the donor intended him. We are not concerned that a dishonest person would rush forward first, because he would fear that another claimant might appear afterward and prove that the donor's intent was more closely directed to him, thereby recovering the property from the first claimant. This resembles the rule stated by the Tur and Shulchan Aruch at the beginning of siman 139 concerning two people disputing over a boat, where whoever is stronger prevails, for the reason I explained there. Equal. The Levush writes that two people came whose names were identical. It appears to me that this is not the Mechaber's intention, because such a case would be obvious and would not need to be stated. Rather, he means that both claimants came at the same time. One might have thought that because the donor used language of an obligation to pay, even if they came one after the other the second should receive a share from the first claimant's payment. The ruling teaches that this is not so: because the first claimant presented himself first, we say that the donor certainly owed the entire amount to him, as explained above. So writes the Sema. The Shach disputes this entire rule and maintains that no distinction should be made here between a gift and a debt or deposit. See there.
