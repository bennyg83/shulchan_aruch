And even, etc.
(Collected note) The judges shall do, etc. So explain Rashi and Rambam. Tosafos, the Rosh, and other poskim, however, explain that the judges may do as they wish. They bring proof from the end of the first chapter of Gittin, the chapter Mi SheHaya Nasu, and the chapter Asarah Yuchasin, and write that this is explicit in the Yerushalmi. See Tosafos in Mi SheHaya Nasu, the Rosh there, chapter 3 of Bava Batra, and Shitah Mekubetzes. Their words are compelling.
And this, etc. See siman 175:50.
And this, etc. The Tosefta in chapter Get Pashut teaches: If one says, "Make Tavi my slave a free man," and there are two slaves named Tavi, we do not interpret ordinary speech to say that he loved one and did not love the other. Rather, both become free, and the value of one is taken from both. Likewise, if one says, "I have two hundred dinar for Yosef ben Shimon," and there are two people named Yosef ben Shimon, we do not interpret ordinary speech to say that he loved one and not the other; rather, they divide equally. The latter case was explained as a loan or deposit. The same applies to the first case: since he violated the positive commandment against freeing a slave, we do not presume that he freed him gratuitously on his own, but that he was redeemed for money. Ran and Maggid Mishneh.
