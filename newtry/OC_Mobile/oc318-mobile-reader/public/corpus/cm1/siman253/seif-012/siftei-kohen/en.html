This is like one who gives, etc. It follows from this that if he expressly said, 'Give two hundred zuz to so-and-so if he marries my daughter,' this is one conditional gift, and the money is not given to him unless he marries her. This is so even though the act was stated before the condition, and we ordinarily rule that the condition is void and the act remains effective, as explained in siman 241, seif 12, and in Even HaEzer, siman 38. First, regarding a dangerously ill person we rule that his words are as though written and delivered, and all the technical laws of conditions are unnecessary. Second, this case is different because the act has not yet been performed. When he says, 'Give, etc., if he marries,' annulling the condition necessarily annuls the act. The requirement that the condition precede the act applies only where the act takes effect immediately in the meantime, as with a bill of divorce and similar cases. Ramban explains this at length in Sefer HaMilchamot, where he challenges Baal HaMaor and Tosafot. The law follows Ramban. The Bach writes likewise. This is clear.
