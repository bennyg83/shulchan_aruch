Gifts. This proves that if he expressly said, 'Give two hundred zuz to so-and-so if he marries my daughter,' it is one conditional gift and is not given unless he marries her. One should not object that the act precedes the condition, and we rule that such a condition is void while the act stands, as stated in siman 241, seif 12, and Even HaEzer siman 38. First, with a person dangerously ill we rule that his words are treated as written and delivered, so the formal laws of conditions are unnecessary. Second, this case is different because the act is not performed immediately. Where he says, 'Give...' if he marries, the condition can prevent the act from ever taking place. The requirement that the condition precede the act was stated where the act is immediately performed in the interim, such as with a get and similar cases. Ramban analyzes this carefully in Milchamot at length, disagreeing with Baal HaMaor and Tosafot. We follow the Ramban as halachah, and the Bach writes likewise. Shach.
