And it was sold and the proceeds were lost, etc. This applies specifically where the wine was sold and the money was lost. If the wine itself soured, the recipient does not lose, because the giver ordered that he receive the proceeds of the wine, meaning after it is sold.
"Give him two hundred zuz from my wine." Since wine itself is not money, he should not have said "give him money from my wine," but "from the proceeds of my wine." He deliberately said "from my wine" so that all the wine would serve as security for the two hundred zuz he ordered to be given.
Such as one barrel among the barrels, etc. A similar law is stated by the Tur and Mechaber at the end of siman 214 regarding one who says, "a house among my houses" or "an ox among my oxen I sell you," where he may show him the house that fell or the ox that died. See also the beginning of siman 241, seif 4.
Since the giver did not designate it, etc. Although he instructed the administrators to choose a particular item for him and they did so, since he did not tell them to choose it and set it aside, but to choose it and give it to him immediately, and they did not carry out his instruction, all the property remains responsible for the maneh. Rashba gives this reason in his responsum there.
