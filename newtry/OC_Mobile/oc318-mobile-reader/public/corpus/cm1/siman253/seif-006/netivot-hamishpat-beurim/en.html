If he explicitly stated that he gives it as a gift. This is very difficult. Siman 248 rules generally that gift language used toward an heir can still have the legal character of inheritance. If Rama means that he expressly said it was not by inheritance but only by gift, he should have said so explicitly. It is also difficult that the source notation cites Mahari Weil and Maharam in the name of some authorities, when the principle itself derives from Rambam in siman 248. It therefore appears that this gloss belongs at the end of seif 7. Darkei Moshe proves there that for the law of apotropos one must specifically use explicit inheritance language. Even though gift language to an heir can elsewhere be treated as inheritance, if one explicitly gives to a son as a gift it is treated as an apotropos appointment for that law. The same is therefore true where the language is simply neutral, so long as he did not expressly use inheritance language. This also explains Rama's cross reference. Siman 246 establishes that for the apotropos rule, explicit gift language to an heir is treated as gift even though in other contexts gift language toward an heir can carry inheritance consequences. Thus the same applies to neutral language here.
