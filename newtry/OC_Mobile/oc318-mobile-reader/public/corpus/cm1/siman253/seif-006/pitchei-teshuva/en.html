And some say that they are ineffective. See Noda BiYehuda, siman 25, in the responsum of the Gaon Rabbi Yosef, of blessed memory, who wrote regarding his case: Quite apart from all else, this law is itself disputed by the great authorities. Consequently, one cannot extract property from the orphans, even from assets that existed when the document was written. Moreover, it appears to me that even the Ramban and those who follow him would concede that the expression “my sons shall give” is ineffective. The Ramban, cited by the Beit Yosef, writes concerning the position of Rabbi Yosef HaLevi and the Rambam that their view is not correct, because a mere kinyan over words exists only when one says “I will give” or “I will sell to you,” or when a kinyan was made to divide. By contrast, one who says “he shall acquire” or “he shall take” indicates acquisition through this gift or through this act of meshichah. Accordingly, in our case, where he said “make a kinyan from me, and then my sons shall give,” it resembles a kinyan on the words “I will give to you.” What difference is there between saying “I will give” and saying “my sons shall give”? One might initially distinguish between “I will give,” which is merely a promise to make a gift in the future, and “my sons shall give,” which cannot mean that he is promising that his sons will give; perhaps it therefore means that his sons are to give by virtue of this present kinyan, like “he shall take” according to the Ramban. However, I saw that the Chelkat Mechokek, Even HaEzer 109:1, cites the Beit Meir that when a healthy person says “give two hundred zuz,” it is a kinyan over words. This implies that even according to the Ramban we must distinguish between “he shall take” or “he shall acquire,” which are expressions of present entitlement and imply that he acquires now, and language of giving, which does not itself express present acquisition but only a promise that a gift will be given, or mere words. Thus, in our case, even according to the Ramban he did not acquire. See there further.
