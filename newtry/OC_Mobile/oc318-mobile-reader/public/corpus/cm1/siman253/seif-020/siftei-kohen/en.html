A dangerously ill person, etc. See Responsa Rabbi Eliyahu ibn Chaim, siman 77.
Give to so-and-so, etc. It appears that this applies specifically where he said, 'Give it to so-and-so together with every lien contained in it.' We then say that it is as though he wrote those words in the document and delivered it to him. If, however, he said only, 'Give the note to so-and-so,' without adding, 'and all its liens,' the recipient does not acquire. Responsa Mabit, part 1, siman 18, writes this in the name of Mahari bar Rav. See there. The practical law requires study, because that ruling follows the Rashba's approach, which also interprets the Rambam's language in that manner. In my humble opinion, the principal view is that of the Ran and the Maggid Mishneh. The unqualified language of the Mechaber likewise appears to follow the Rambam in this way. This is clear. This also appears to me to be the practical ruling. The Sema's words somewhat imply this, as do the Tur and Mechaber above at the end of siman 66, seif 42. This is also the view of Baal HaTerumot, gate 51, part 4. See the Beit Yosef at the end of siman 66, and Responsa Rabbi Aharon Sasson, siman 165.
And the heir cannot forgive, etc. See the Sema, seif katan 44, through the words, 'and the Ir Shushan explains the reason because the Sages treated the recipient of the gift like an heir,' etc. The Sema rejects that explanation, but the Ir Shushan's words are correct according to Tosafot in the chapter HaMokher Et HaSefinah, at the beginning of page 77, and in the chapter HaZahav. The same appears in the Ran in the chapter HaKotev. See there and examine carefully. See also Tosafot at the end of the chapter HaKotev, Ketubot 85b, the Nimukei Yosef in the chapter Mi SheMet, and the other poskim.
But not with a loan, etc. See the Sema, seif katan 45, through his citation of the Darchei Moshe. He explains that when the Mordechai says the recipient did not rely upon it, this does not mean that the pledged article belonged to the recipient. See also the Bach, who explains the matter differently. Regarding a pledge, the Bach rules that if the very body of the pledge was transferred to him through the gift of a dangerously ill person, he acquires it, just as a healthy person acquires it through an act of acquisition. This is clear.
