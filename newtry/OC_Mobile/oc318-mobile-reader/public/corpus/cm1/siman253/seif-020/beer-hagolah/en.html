Vav) This is the language of the Rambam there, chapter 10, halachah 2, from Rava in the name of Rav Nachman, Bava Batra 147b to 148a.
Zayin) From the incident involving the mother of Rav Amram Chasida and a bundle of documents, Bava Batra 151a.
Chet) A statement of Rav Nachman, where Shmuel concedes, Bava Batra 147b, with the reason explained in the Gemara.
Tet) Rashbam explains this there, and the Beit Yosef cites him in seif 36.
Yod) Rashbam explains that the reason is that the recipient's mind is not settled upon acquisition. The Beit Yosef comments that it is unclear why this should matter, since the words of a deathly ill person are treated as written and delivered. He therefore understands the poskim, who state the law without distinction, not to distinguish between a debt owed by an Israelite and a debt owed by a gentile. The Bach gives a reason for distinguishing them, but concludes that where the giver transfers to his fellow the collateral securing the debt as a deathbed gift, the recipient certainly acquires the debt as well, because the words of a deathly ill person are treated as written and delivered.
