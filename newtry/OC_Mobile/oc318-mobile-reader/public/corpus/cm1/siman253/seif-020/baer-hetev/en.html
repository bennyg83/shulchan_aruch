To waive. Ordinarily, one who sells or gives a promissory note to another, even after the recipient or purchaser has acquired it by writing and delivery, may still waive the debt to the borrower, and his heirs may do so as well. In the case of a shechiv mera, however, the heir cannot waive the debt, so that the mind of the dying person not be disturbed. The Tur and Shulchan Aruch state all this in siman 66, seifim 23 and 26. The Sema rejects the explanation of the Levush that the reason is that the Sages gave the gift recipient the status of an heir, so one heir cannot waive what is held by another heir. The Shach, however, writes that the Levush's explanation is correct according to Tosafot in Bava Batra 77a and Bava Metzia, chapter HaZahav; the Ran in the chapter HaKotev; Tosafot in Ketubot 85a; the Nimukei Yosef in the chapter Mi Shemet; and other poskim.
But. The Mordechai writes that the reason is that the donor does not rely upon collection of the debt, and therefore the transfer is ineffective even as a deathbed gift. Darkei Moshe explains that the phrase 'he does not rely upon it' refers not to the recipient, as the Beit Yosef understood, but to the donor. Since the donor himself is not confident that the gentile debtor will pay, he does not intend a complete gift and the recipient therefore does not acquire. Maharam rules similarly in Responsa, siman 56, and there expresses doubt regarding a gentile's loan evidenced by a document. The Bach explains the matter differently. With respect to a pledge, however, he rules that if the shechiv mera transferred ownership of the body of the pledged item itself, the recipient acquires, just as a healthy person would acquire it through a kinyan. This is straightforward. Shach.
