If he said: If she gives birth, etc. See Responsa Maharashdam, siman 231.
The gift is void, etc. See the Sema, seif katan 62, who limits the rule to a case in which both the son and daughter are his children. The Bach also explains it differently. In my view, neither explanation is correct. The Rosh means that in the earlier case the donor did not make the birth a condition governing a separate gift. Rather, the birth itself identified the recipient. Here, however, he made the birth of one person a condition for a gift to another, and expressly made the gift dependent upon that condition. Once the condition failed, the gift was void. This is the correct distinction.
