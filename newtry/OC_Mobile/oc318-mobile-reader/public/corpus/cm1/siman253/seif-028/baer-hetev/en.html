To the son. The Tur quotes a responsum of the Rosh: If you were to divide the money according to the wording of the will, it would have to be divided into seven portions. The male child would take four portions, his brother two, and the female one, so that the male takes twice his brother and his brother takes twice the female, in accordance with the proportions stated in the will. But this cannot be correct. Why should the male lose because a female twin was born with him? The donor did not specify in his condition what should happen if a female were born. He therefore left the matter to Torah law, under which the female does not diminish the male's right and he takes the entire share. The Rosh elaborates there. The Rosh further writes that one cannot challenge this from the Mishnah concerning one who says, 'If my wife gives birth to a male,' and 'if she gives birth to a female.' Although he used alternative language, we interpret his intention as including twins as explained above. There, however, both children are his own son and daughter, and a person's mind is close to his children. This case includes another person in the condition. So explains the Sema. The Bach gives a somewhat different explanation. The Shach writes that neither explanation is correct. Rather, the Rosh means that in the Mishnah the donor did not make the birth a condition for some other legal act; the birth itself was the subject of the condition. Here, by contrast, he made the birth a condition affecting another person's gift and made that gift dependent upon the condition. Once the condition failed, the gift failed. According to this, the same rule would apply even if the gift recipient were his son or daughter, because the condition concerns another matter, and so too in all similar cases. See there carefully, for this is clear. Shach.
