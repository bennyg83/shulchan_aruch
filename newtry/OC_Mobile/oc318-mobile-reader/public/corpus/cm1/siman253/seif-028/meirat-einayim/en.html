The gift is void. The Tur, citing a responsum of the Rosh, explains that if one tried to divide the money mathematically according to the wording of the will, one might divide it into seven shares, giving the male child four, the other male two, and the female one, so that the first male receives twice the second and the second twice the female. But that cannot be correct. Why should the male lose merely because a female was born with him? The donor never specified what should happen to the female in that combined birth. Where he did not state a condition, Torah law remains controlling, and under Torah inheritance rules the female does not reduce the male's share in this setting. The Rosh discusses this at length. One cannot challenge this from the Mishnah where a donor says, “If my wife gives birth to a male, give him such amount, and if a female, such amount,” and we say that even though he used alternative formulations his intention also extended to twins. That is specifically where both possible children are his own and his mind is close to both. Here the condition also involves another person, and the same inference cannot be made.
