(Seif 13, since the giver did not designate it.) I have not understood the end of the Rama’s reasoning in this formulation. Throughout the Torah we hold that a person’s agent is like himself, and their designation should therefore be equivalent to the giver’s own designation. In the underlying ruling, Beit Yosef records the case as follows: he instructed that the guardians select from the finest portion of the business assets the sum of one hundred and give it to him, and immediately confer title upon him. The guardians selected and set aside certain promissory notes in a corner, but did not give them to him, and those notes were lost. In that case the ruling is correct, because the guardians did not fulfill the deceased’s words until they actually gave the property to him, and he had not instructed them merely to place it in a corner. It therefore appears to me that the Rama abbreviated the case here, and it should read: he instructed the guardians to select the finest property for him and give it to him.
