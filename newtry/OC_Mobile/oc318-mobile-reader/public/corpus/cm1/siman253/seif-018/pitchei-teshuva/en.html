Or a debt instrument from a non-Jew, there is room for doubt. See Responsa Knesset Yechezkel, siman 88, who writes that the Rama in siman 253, seif 20, leaves unresolved whether a loan owed by a non-Jew, even when documented or secured by collateral, can be transferred by deathbed instruction. He then attributes to the Bach an unqualified ruling that such a will is ineffective because assignment in the presence of all three parties does not apply to a non-Jew. This attribution is difficult. As shown in the preceding seif katan, the Bach rules that way only concerning an oral debt or a debt secured merely by the non-Jew’s collateral. A written debt can be transferred by a healthy person through the formula “acquire it and all its liens,” and according to the Bach it therefore certainly can be transferred by deathbed gift. Avodat HaGershuni, end of siman 11, states this explicitly. Noda BiYehuda, second edition, siman 45, likewise writes plainly that according to the Bach even a written debt against a non-Jew is acquired by deathbed gift, although he adds that one should not lightly rule against the Rama’s express doubt. This requires study.
