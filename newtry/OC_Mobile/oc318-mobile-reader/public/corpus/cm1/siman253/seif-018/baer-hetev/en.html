And with strengthening language. The Tur states this rule in the name of the Ramah, but the words 'with strengthening language' do not appear there. The Mechaber added them here and again below in seif 20, and this requires study. The requirement of strengthening language was said only about a shechiv mera who gives all his property to others and additionally makes a kinyan. As explained in Tur, siman 250, seif 29, strengthening language is then required, because otherwise the fact that he made a kinyan indicates that he intended the recipient to acquire only through the kinyan, and a kinyan does not take effect after death. A partial gift of a shechiv mera, however, has the law of a gift of a healthy person: it requires a kinyan but does not require special strengthening language. So writes the Sema. The Shach answers that the case here is where the donor expressly said that the gift should have the status of a deathbed gift. In that case a kinyan is ineffective unless it is made merely to strengthen the gift, as explained in siman 250, seif 9. He adds that the Sema's words are not understandable to him. In my opinion, however, the Sema's words are correct, because if the case involved an express designation as a deathbed gift, why would the Mechaber say that the recipient acquires only with a kinyan? Such a gift is effective even without a kinyan, except that if the donor recovers he may retract, as explained in siman 250, seif 9. Understand this carefully.
