Currency is not acquired through barter. One who says simply "a maneh" ordinarily means a coined maneh, not equivalent value. This raises a difficulty from Bava Batra, where a partial deathbed gift requires a kinyan. The Gemara compares the case "Give a maneh to so and so" with a get and infers that no kinyan had been made. But even if a sudar kinyan had been made, currency is not acquired by barter. One cannot easily say that the Gemara means any legally effective kinyan, such as incidental acquisition with land, because the ordinary phrase "a kinyan was made from him" means sudar kinyan. Moreover, according to Rashi, even a get can be acquired incidentally with land, so the comparison to a get as something not subject to kinyan becomes difficult. The point therefore requires study. See siman 202.
