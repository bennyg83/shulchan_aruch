Meaning, we do not say that perhaps he transferred to him some hidden maneh and we do not know where it is, so that nothing should be given from this property because the burden of proof rests on the claimant. The ruling teaches otherwise, and this is stated in the Gemara.
For ordinarily he is one who gives instructions because of death. This is not literally limited to the technical law of one who explicitly instructs because of death. It means only to exclude treating it as a healthy person's gift, which requires a kinyan.
Only with a kinyan and with an enhancement of his power. The Tur cites this law in the name of Ramah without the words "and with an enhancement of his power." The Mechaber added them here and again below in seif 20. This requires study. The Sages required enhancement of the recipient's power where a shechiv mera gives all his property to others and also makes a kinyan. The Tur explains in siman 250:29 that without enhancement we infer from the very fact that he used a kinyan that he intended the recipient to acquire only through the kinyan, and there is no kinyan after death. But a partial gift of a shechiv mera has the law of a healthy person's gift and requires a kinyan. Once there is a kinyan, no special enhancement should be needed. Perhaps the Mechaber wrote this because Rambam, cited by the Tur in siman 250:31, holds that even a partial shechiv mera gift is not acquired unless the recipient's power is enhanced. But that is difficult, because the Tur rejects Rambam on that point, while the Maggid Mishneh, Beit Yosef, and Kesef Mishneh all explain that Rambam said so only where the giver explicitly states that he gives because of death. In that case everyone agrees that the partial gift has the law of a full shechiv mera gift. The Tur likewise states this in siman 250:14, the Mechaber there in seif 9, and Rama in the gloss to seif 13. Here, however, the Mechaber cannot be speaking of an express deathbed gift, because he begins this seif, "This applies where he instructs because of death, etc., or where he distributes all his property," meaning the alternative case is specifically not one who expressly instructs because of death, but one who merely distributes all his property. He then says, "but where he transfers only one maneh, etc.," referring to that second alternative. This requires study.
