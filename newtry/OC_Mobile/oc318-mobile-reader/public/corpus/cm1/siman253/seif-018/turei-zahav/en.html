(Seif 31, and the first has only the one hundred.) This is difficult. Why was it necessary for him to say regarding the second, “One hundred to so-and-so”? Once he gives the first son one hundred, that son is removed from the remainder, and everything else automatically belongs to the second, since he has only two sons. One may answer that this was necessary because he was uncertain whether he had cash on hand. A creditor is paid first from cash, and he feared that one son might take all the cash that would be found. He therefore acknowledged a debt to the other as well, so that the other would also share in the cash.
