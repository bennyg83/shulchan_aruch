Neither of them, etc., even though it did not contain the minimum size for division. The words 'even though' are the Mechaber's, meaning that even where the courtyard was too small for compelled division, after they voluntarily divided it one cannot say, 'I agreed only on such-and-such condition.' This cannot be the litigant's own argument, for he would have said 'because it was not large enough,' not 'even though.'
Even if one has twice the share of the other. The Tur explains that the one with one share is just as obligated to eliminate visual damage as the one with two shares.
He need do no more. The other cannot prevent him and say, 'Build in the middle so that I can later support my beams on it,' nor can he demand a promise that when he later has funds and wishes to build in the middle, the builder will receive half his expense. The ruling teaches he need not provide such arrangements. See the Derishah and Hagahot Darkei Moshe.
Like a house and upper story, etc. Meaning, where the owner of the house is wealthy and the upper-story owner poor, or vice versa, and the structure falls. The wealthy party can rebuild everything at his own expense and use it all until the poor party pays his share. The same applies here: the wealthy partner may build the entire wall and use all of it until the poor partner pays. This is not interest.
Nevertheless, each should distance, etc. The Tur concludes: if they are eligible to receive communal charity to build a simple partition of reeds and boards, that is what should be done. I do not know why Rama omitted this.
