Before. The Tur states: "There is no presumption here that a person does not pay before the due date, because each course of the wall has its own due date." This is not limited to a completed course, for the same applies even before the course is finished. It appears that even without this rationale, after the day on which the entire obligation became due he would be believed to say that he had already paid, because he has a migo that he could say, "I paid you now, after the due date," as the Tur and Shulchan Aruch write in siman 75. The Tur gives the rationale that each course has its own due date for two practical consequences. First, even while the work is still within its overall period, and even before the present course is finished, he is believed to say, "I paid you for everything already built, including this course that you are now constructing." Second, if on the day the construction is completed he claims that he already paid for the entire wall, he is believed. Without the rule that each course has its own due date, he could not claim payment before the due date by means of a migo that he could have claimed payment now, on the day the debt matured, because that would be a migo requiring brazenness. The Mechaber abbreviated here and relied on what he wrote in siman 78. Accordingly, the case here is where, after the day the obligation matured, he says that he paid, and even if he claims that he paid before construction began he is believed by the migo that he could say that he paid now. This is the Sema's explanation. The Shach writes that this does not appear correct. Rather, the Mechaber addresses the same case as the Mishnah and Gemara, where necessarily there are circumstances in which no migo exists. That is why the Mechaber states without qualification that even if he claims to have paid before construction of the wall, he is believed in every circumstance. See what the Taz writes concerning this. See also the rules in simanim 75 and 78.
