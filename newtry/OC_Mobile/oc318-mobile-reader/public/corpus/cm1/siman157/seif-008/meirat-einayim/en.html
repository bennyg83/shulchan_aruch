Even if he claims that he paid earlier. The Tur writes that even if he claims he paid before completion of the wall, he is believed, because the presumption that a person does not pay before the due date does not apply here: each row has its own due date. 'Each row' is not literal, and the same applies before completion of a row. In the Perishah I explained that even without this reason, after the due date has passed he is believed to say, 'I paid during the term,' through a migo that he could say, 'I paid now after the term,' as the Tur and Mechaber write in siman 78. The Tur gives the reason that each row has its own term to teach two further cases. First, even while construction is still ongoing and before completion of the present row, he can say, 'I paid for everything already built, including this row.' Second, on the very day construction is completed he can say, 'I paid for the entire wall.' Without the rule that each row has its own term, he would not be believed through a migo that he could say he paid that day after the due date, because that would be a brazen migo. I explained these distinctions in the Perishah and in the Sema to siman 78. The Mechaber abbreviates here and relies on siman 78. He speaks where the claim is made after the day the term ended, and therefore even a claim that he paid before construction began can be believed through a migo. This is why he omits the reason of each row and writes 'before building the wall' rather than the Tur's 'before the wall was completed.'
