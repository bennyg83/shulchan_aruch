He swears. The Tur writes concerning Rambam's ruling that Ramah disputes it and holds that the claimant collects without an oath. Ramah reasons that here there is an even stronger presumption that payment was not made than in the case of one who sets a due date for his fellow and the debtor claims, "I paid you before the due date," where the claimant collects without an oath. The Sema suggests that because the claimant here seeks to extract money from another, the authors of the Shulchan Aruch ruled like Rambam. I do not understand this, for in the ordinary case of a debtor who says, "I paid," the claimant likewise seeks to extract money from another and nevertheless collects without an oath, as Rama writes in siman 78, seif 2, and as the Beit Yosef rules there. The Gemara in Bava Batra, pages 5 and 6, likewise implies that here the presumption of nonpayment is stronger than in the fixed due-date case of siman 78. The Mechaber and Rama therefore require study for omitting here the opinions of Ramah and Ramban. The Taz raises the same difficulty; see there. Nimukei Yosef also cites Ramban's view as his conclusion. Shach.
