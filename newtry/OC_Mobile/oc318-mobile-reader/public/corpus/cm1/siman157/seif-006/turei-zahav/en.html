In the first edition of the Tur the text reads, “before he completed construction of the wall.” This resolves why the Tur had to write that there is no presumption, and so forth. One might otherwise say that even if there were a presumption, a migo is accepted against a presumption in siman 78, since the Gemara leaves that question unresolved and the defendant is exempt because of the doubt. Here too, why compel him to pay half the expense when no one is presently in possession and we could presume that it has at least now been paid? The Tur therefore writes “before he completed construction of the wall,” meaning that his claim of payment concerns a time before completion. He then has no migo that he paid now, because even at the time of his present claim the debt is still within its term, the wall not yet having been completed. The Tur later writes that the rule is not limited to each completed course, but applies even before completion of a course. It appears that he was troubled why the Gemara had to answer that each course has its own due time. Perhaps the Mishnah teaches only the smaller novelty that immediately before the last minor portion of the wall is completed it is already deemed after the due time, like the final day of a term in siman 78. Necessarily, however, even that is obvious, for when only a small portion remains the wall is treated as complete. The Mishnah's principal novelty is therefore that each course has its own due time, from which we also know that the same applies shortly before completion of a course, just as shortly before completion of the entire wall.
