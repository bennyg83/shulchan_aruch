Rama says that if there is no local custom, the wall should be built as appears proper to the judges. Sema questions the source. Tur cites Rabbeinu Yonah that without a known custom the wall should be six handbreadths thick, and Ramban qualifies that if the wall is built with lime rather than mortar the required thickness can be reduced according to expert assessment. Sema therefore assumes Ramban still accepts a minimum comparable to the lightest wall described in the Mishnah. Rama's source is Nimukei Yosef. He writes that the Mishnah's dimensions were stated in order that the wall endure. Therefore, according to authorities who understand the measurements functionally, if lime or another construction method makes a thinner wall sufficiently stable, a thinner wall may be used according to skilled builders. The judge has only what his eyes see. Some authorities disagree and require the Mishnah's dimensions even with lime. But that dispute concerns a place where the customary materials are the Mishnah's stone types and the custom itself fixes the form. In a place with no custom at all, such as a new town, the purpose is simply to remove the visual intrusion through a wall that will endure. There is no reason to require one of the Mishnah's precise historical measurements. Rambam and the Shulchan Aruch omit the Mishnah's exact stone dimensions and instead speak generally of local custom, which supports Rama. Where there is no custom, expert judicial assessment governs from the outset.
