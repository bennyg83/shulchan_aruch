And he claims that the other sold him his share. This teaches not only that if he says, "It was always mine because I built it," he is not believed, because the presumption is that both built the partition together. Even if he says, "I purchased his share from him," he is still not believed. Although the stones are not recognizable and he therefore has a migo that he could have said, "These are not the stones from that wall," the Rambam holds that this is a migo against witnesses. Once he admits that these stones came from the partition between them, it is as though witnesses establish that they are still jointly owned. The Mechaber then cites an opinion that this is so only where the stones are recognizable, because then he has no migo. It is surprising that the Mechaber's wording implies that the Rambam discusses even unrecognizable stones, while Nimukei Yosef writes that the Rambam appears to agree with the Rif and to speak specifically where the stones are recognizable. The Maggid Mishneh likewise says this at the end of chapter 3 of Hilchot Shekhenim. See the Derishah, where I discussed this at length.
