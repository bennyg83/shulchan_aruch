In the first edition of the Tur the text here reads, “Not only if he claims, ‘I purchased them,’” and so forth. Do not mistakenly infer that because Rabbeinu Yonah had to explain that this is not a migo against a presumption, his only teaching is that the migo operates despite that presumption. Rather, this itself is his point: even where a presumption opposes the claim, as when he says, “I built it,” we do not conclusively presume that the wall belonged to both; the migo is nevertheless accepted. As explained earlier in this siman, Rabbeinu Yonah does not regard it as a migo against witnesses. His formulation means: not only if he claims, “These are not stones from this wall,” where there is no need to force the explanation that it is a migo against a presumption, because no presumption applies; but even if he says, “I built it,” where the ordinary presumption is that both built it, the migo is effective, for a migo is accepted against a mere presumption.
