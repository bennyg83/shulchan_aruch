That his fellow sold him his share. If he claims, "I built it entirely from my own funds," he is not believed, because the presumption is that both built it. Even if he claims purchase, according to the Sema he is not believed when the stones are recognizable as coming from the shared partition. Even if the stones are not recognizable and he has a migo, once he admits they came from the partition between them it is as though witnesses establish that they still belong to both. The dissenting opinion holds that when the stones are not recognizable and he has a migo, he is believed even to say that he alone built it. Shach disagrees and holds that the authorities dispute only whether a migo helps a claim that he alone built it. If he claims purchase, everyone agrees that a migo helps where available. See Beurim: here the case is where he claims that he bought the place and wall while the wall was still standing. In that case he is not believed even regarding the stones despite having a migo. Shach further writes that if the stones remained in his possession far longer than usual, to the point that partners ordinarily object, he is believed to claim purchase even without a migo. See siman 179.
