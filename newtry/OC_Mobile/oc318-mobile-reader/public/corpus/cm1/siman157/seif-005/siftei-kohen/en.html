There is an authority who says, etc. The Mechaber's formulation requires study, for plainly no one disputes that where the stones are not identifiable, the possessor may claim that he purchased them, based on a migo that he could have claimed that he returned them, as explained below in siman 179. The Tur needed to cite Rabbeinu Yonah only for the broader ruling that the possessor may even claim, 'I built it alone.' On that point Tosafot, Hagahot Ashri, and the Maggid Mishneh in the name of some commentators disagree, because such a claim is a migo against witnesses, since the presumption is that the parties built it jointly. When the Maggid Mishneh says that there is another approach, he means that the Rif and Rambam explain the claim as one of purchase or gift, whereas the other approach explains it as a claim that the possessor built it himself. They do not disagree regarding the law, contrary to the Beit Yosef. In any event, all agree that when the stones are not recognizable, he may claim that he purchased them or received them as a gift, through a migo that he could have claimed that he returned them. Examine the Maggid Mishneh carefully and you will find this proved by his statement that the view of the Halakhot applies where we see the stones in his possession and witnesses identify them. From where did he infer that the Rif's view applies only in that case? Necessarily, since the Rif explains the case as a claim of purchase or gift, the case must be one in which the stones are seen and identified. The Sema, seif katan 17, also treats a claim of purchase as a migo against witnesses, but this is entirely unpersuasive. Tosafot at the beginning of Bava Batra and Hagahot Ashri further prove that if the stones remained in his possession for a very long time, he may make this claim even if they are seen and identified. Although the Tur below in siman 179 does not state this, it appears that Tosafot and Hagahot Ashri concern a period far longer than usual, such that partners would object, whereas the Tur and Shulchan Aruch concern an ordinary extended period during which they are not particular. This appears correct. See the responsa of Rabbi Shlomo Kohen, book II, siman 171.
