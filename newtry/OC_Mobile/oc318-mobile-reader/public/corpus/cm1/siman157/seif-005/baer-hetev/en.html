His share. The Sema explains that it is not only when he claims, "It was always mine, for I alone built it," that he is not believed, since there is a presumption that both parties built it. Even if he claims, "I purchased it from him," he is not believed. Although the stones themselves are not recognizable and he has a migo that he could have claimed, "These stones did not come from that wall," the Sema holds that this is a migo against witnesses. Once he admits that they came from the partition between them, it is as though witnesses establish that they remain jointly owned. See the Taz for his explanation of this ruling.
Who says. The Shach writes that the Mechaber's words require study. It is obvious that no authority disputes that, when the stones are not recognizable, he may claim that he purchased them, supported by a migo that he could have claimed that he returned them. See there.
The wall. In that case, even if he removed the stones without witnesses present, he is not believed. This also appears from the Tur. It follows that the case must be one in which the stones are presently seen in his possession, for otherwise the statement that they are recognizable would have no application. I therefore do not understand the Bach's concluding statement that the same rule applies even when the stones are not seen in his possession. Consider this carefully. Shach.
