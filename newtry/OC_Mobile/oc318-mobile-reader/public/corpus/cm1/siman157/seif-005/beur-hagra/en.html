He claimed that he sold. As the Rif writes, in such a matter there is no chazakah without proof.
There are authorities. All cases where a person ordinarily lacks chazakah concern circumstances where witnesses establish the original ownership. The Tosefta illustrates the general rule: one who says, "This garment belonged to your father and I bought it" is believed through "the mouth that prohibited is the mouth that permitted," but where witnesses establish the father's ownership the possessor needs independent proof. See Kesubos and Bava Basra 46a.
