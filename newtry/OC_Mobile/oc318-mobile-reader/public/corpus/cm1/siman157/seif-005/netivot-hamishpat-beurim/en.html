The ruling can be understood as referring not to a claim that the other sold him his share in the stones, for that would indeed be believed, but to a claim that he sold him his share in the site and wall while it was still standing. He is not believed in that broader claim. Although with land one who is not believed regarding ownership may still be believed regarding consumed produce through a migo, here the migo stands against the evident circumstances. Why would he have had to buy with his own money what the other was already obligated to contribute to the wall? The same applies to a claim of gift, because such a gift serves no apparent purpose where the thing was already obligated to stand in that form. Taz asks that he may say, "He did not want to build in stone, so I had to build from my own funds." But such a claim requires a deed or proof, as explained in seif 7.
