If he says, "You waived it for me," he is not believed merely by ordinary possession. The same applies if he says, "You sold it to me," or, "You gave it to me as a gift." Since the party claiming the nuisance is himself also injured by the common wall arrangement and has no exclusive possession that would create a presumption, his unilateral claim does not suffice. Similarly, before the wall is built, if one party claims, "I already paid you my half so that you should build the whole wall from your funds," he is not believed. The rule allowing a plea of payment applies after the wall has been completed and one party seeks reimbursement of money. Then the underlying building obligation has already been converted into an ordinary oral debt for expenditures, and the defendant can say he paid. Before construction, however, the legal duty itself is to participate in building the partition. A person cannot escape that judicially imposed building obligation merely by saying, "I paid you so that you should perform my construction duty." The claim resembles one made against a standing judgment or other beit din obligation. Moreover, if such a plea were believed before construction, he would have a migo to say the other party waived the obligation entirely, which the law plainly rejects. Thus no such payment or waiver claim is available before the wall is actually built.
