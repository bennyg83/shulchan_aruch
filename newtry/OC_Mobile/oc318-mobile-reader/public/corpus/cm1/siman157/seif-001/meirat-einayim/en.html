That contains the minimum size required for division. The Tur and Mechaber explain the measure below in siman 171:3. Here they teach that whether the courtyard is large enough that one partner can compel division, or too small for compelled division but the partners voluntarily divide it, once divided so that each uses only his own portion, either may compel the other to build a separating wall to prevent visual damage.
Even if there is a custom, etc. The reason is that where the harm is clear and constant, as here, an inferior custom does not govern.
And you waived your right against me. The Tur states that the same applies if he says, 'You sold me the right' or 'gave it to me as a gift.' He explains that this differs from ordinary visual damage through a window, where the Rosh recognizes chazakah. Here, in a shared courtyard, the possessor suffers the same damage he causes, so the other can say, 'I did not bother to protest because I was damaging you just as you were damaging me; I waited for you to raise the matter first.' Moreover, the harm is constant, like smoke and a privy, unlike a window made for light rather than for looking, where looking into another's courtyard is itself prohibited.
