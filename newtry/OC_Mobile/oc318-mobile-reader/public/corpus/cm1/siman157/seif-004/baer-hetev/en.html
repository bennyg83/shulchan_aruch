The custom. Meaning, if the second party is not satisfied with it, he may say: I do not wish to have a flimsy fence that will fall and require me to trouble myself again to rebuild it. Tosafot and the Mordechai write at the beginning of Bava Batra and in the chapter HaPoalim that one does not follow an inferior custom that people adopted. Thus far the Sema. See Responsa Raanach, siman 29; Responsa Rashach, part 3, simanim 47 and 48; Responsa R. Moshe Alshich, siman 57; and Responsa Rashach, siman 84. Shach.
The judge. This requires study, for from where did the Rama derive this? The Tur does not write so. Rather, he states: "If there is no established custom in the city, Rabbeinu Yonah writes that it must be made using the least substantial of the walls listed in the Mishnah, namely kefisin and bricks." Only afterward, regarding the required dimensions of a wall of unhewn stone, concerning which the Mishnah states that its thickness must be six tefachim, does the Tur write in the name of the Ramban that this applies specifically when the stones are built with mortar. If the wall is built with lime, it need not be that thick, but rather according to the judge's assessment based on experts. These are unrelated matters. It appears that the Ramban agrees with Rabbeinu Yonah that here one must construct at least the least substantial wall listed in the Mishnah. Sema.
To build. See what I wrote in siman 203, seif 1, and siman 241, seif 3, regarding the difficulty in this ruling. Taz.
They divide. The Tur writes that if they made a kinyan with him by which he obligated himself to build, or if he obligated himself to give a specified sum, it is effective according to all opinions, because it is in the nature of an acknowledgment or a personal obligation. See there. Sema.
Light. See the Taz, who disagrees with the Rama here and writes that his words are astonishing. See there.
To prevent. The Beit Yosef writes in the name of the Ramah: It appears that in such a case he may prevent him and say, "If you withdraw the wall into your own property, I will have no right to lean upon it." Even if he says, "I will give you permission to lean upon it," the other may respond: "If this is by sale, I do not wish to purchase it now, and if it is a gift, I do not want it, as it is written, 'One who hates gifts will live.'" This reasoning, that he may say he does not want a gift, is stated by the Rif and Rambam regarding property that cannot be divided. The Tur cites it in siman 171, seif 13, and writes that Rabbeinu Chananel and the Rosh do not agree. See there. Sema.
