Some say that his fellow can prevent him. See Taz, who asks that the entire power to compel construction of a partition arises only from visual intrusion, as explained at the beginning of Bava Batra. If one partner eliminates that injury by some other adequate means, why should the other be able to force him to surrender space merely so that beams can later be rested on the wall? The rule can be defended somewhat. Where the courtyard is too small to require division and the partners voluntarily agreed to divide, one can certainly say, "I agreed to division only on the assumption that the customary wall would be built, so that I would have a wall on which to support beams. If you build entirely within your own portion, I cannot later construct a separate wall without losing from my portion the full width of another wall." Even where the courtyard is large enough to compel division, it is possible that the Sages required division and a partition only in a manner that does not cause one partner to lose ordinary use because of the other's chosen construction. But if each independently purchased one half of a courtyard, then the other cannot prevent his fellow from building entirely within his own portion once he adequately removes the visual intrusion.
