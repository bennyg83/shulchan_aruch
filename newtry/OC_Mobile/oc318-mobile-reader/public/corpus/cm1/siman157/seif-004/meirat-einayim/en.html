Some say that we do not follow that custom. Meaning, if the second party does not consent, he can say, 'I do not want an inferior fence that will fall and require me to trouble myself rebuilding it.' Tosafos and Mordechai write at the beginning of Bava Batra and in chapter HaSocher Et HaPoalim that we do not follow an inferior custom. Darkei Moshe cites this in siman 163 and discusses it at length, except that in tax matters we do follow even such a custom, as Rama writes there in seif 3 based on Terumat HaDeshen, siman 342.
He should repair it according to what the judge sees fit, etc. This requires study. I do not know from where Rama learned this. The Tur says only that where there is no known local custom, Rabbeinu Yonah requires the least substantial wall listed in the Mishnah, namely boards or bricks. Only afterward, concerning a stone wall where the Mishnah requires six tefachim of thickness, the Tur cites Ramban that this is only when building with mortar. If built with lime, it need not be as thick, but rather according to the judge's assessment based on experts. These are two different matters. It appears that Ramban agrees with Rabbeinu Yonah that in the absence of custom one uses the least substantial wall.
Some disagree. See the Tur, who writes that if they made a kinyan by which he bound himself to build, or obligated himself to pay a certain amount, it is effective according to all opinions because it operates as an admission or binding obligation.
If the custom is to make a light partition, etc. Beit Yosef cites Ramah that in such a case the neighbor can object and say, 'If you withdraw into your own land, I will have no right to support anything on it.' Even if the builder says, 'I will give you permission to use it,' the other can reply, 'If it is a sale, I do not want to buy now, and if a gift, I do not want gifts, as it is written, one who hates gifts will live.' Rif and Rambam use this reasoning regarding property not subject to division, while Rabbeinu Chananel and Rosh disagree, as cited below in siman 171.
Or the reverse. Meaning, the custom is a stone wall and he wishes to build only a light barrier. The Tur records a dispute: some say his fellow can prevent him, saying, 'Today or tomorrow it will fall and I will have to litigate with you repeatedly'; others say that since he is removing visual damage, he cannot be compelled to do more, and the Rosh agrees with this latter view.
