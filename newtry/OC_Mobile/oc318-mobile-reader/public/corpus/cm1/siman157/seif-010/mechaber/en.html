If a courtyard wall separating two partners fell, either may compel the other to rebuild it to a height of four cubits. If one chose to raise it above four cubits, the other is not required to share the cost of the additional height, unless the other built another wall opposite or beside the dividing wall to a corresponding height. He must then contribute to the additional height corresponding to his own wall. For example, if one built the dividing wall to a height of ten cubits and the other built another wall opposite or beside it to a height of six cubits, the latter must pay his share of the two cubits added above the basic four, because his conduct shows that he desires them. Likewise, if he cut recesses at the top of the dividing wall for placing beams, or installed upon it a large beam on which other beams rest, he must contribute to all six additional cubits that his neighbor added above the first four, even if he did not build the entire adjoining wall, because he thereby revealed that he desires the full height. Rama: The same applies along the wall's length. If he left alternating projecting stones, as builders do when they intend to extend a structure, he must pay for the entire extension made by his neighbor. The same applies if he already had a wall and built something that clearly demonstrates his desire for what his neighbor constructed. Likewise, if he derived benefit from the construction, even without making any sign that he approved it, he must pay according to the benefit he received.
