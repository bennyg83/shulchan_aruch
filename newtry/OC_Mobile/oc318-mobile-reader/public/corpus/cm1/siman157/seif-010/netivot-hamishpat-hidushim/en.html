A stone goes in and a stone goes out, and if he claims that he will not only add another line or drink a column and:
He is liable for the entire addition. This entire seif concerns a case where it is apparent that he intends to make a ceiling and rest it upon his fellow's wall. That is a clear indication that he wishes to acquire a half interest in the wall, because otherwise he may not use it. If there is no indication that he intends to rest a ceiling upon it, he need pay only according to the benefit he actually received. Thus, if he built only a wooden wall opposite it, he pays only the cost of wood. The same applies where he rests the walls of his house against the wall of his fellow's house. This is what Rama means by concluding, "and similarly if he benefited from his construction."
