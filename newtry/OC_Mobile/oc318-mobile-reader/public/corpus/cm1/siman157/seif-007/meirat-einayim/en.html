For a distinguishing facade marking is effective. The term refers to a marker made at the edge or end of the wall. In siman 158 the Tur and Mechaber explain that when neighbors divide an open valley and make a partition between them, such a marker establishes ownership. Rabbi Yosef HaLevi holds that the Sages instituted this marker specifically in a garden, where one cannot compel the other to build a wall. In a courtyard that is subject to compulsory division, one can compel the other to build the wall with him, and the Sages did not institute the marker merely for the less common case of a courtyard too small for compulsory division. Rama therefore concludes in his gloss that above four cubits, where the additional construction is not compulsory, such a marker is effective according to all opinions.
