That his fellow cannot prevent him. In my view it is evident that this is the primary opinion. If the other partner could prevent him from building on the entire wall, so that each could build only on his own half, then when one nevertheless builds on the whole wall why should his position be better than one who builds another person's wall without permission, as discussed in siman 375, seif 6, where reimbursement is assessed with the builder at the disadvantage? Even a later indication that the owner is pleased does not help according to the Rosh and Tur, as explained there in Shach, seif katan 3. Here, before the added construction, the wall was not standing for this purpose. Had it already been intended for the addition, liability would arise even before the second owner actually relied on it, as explained in seif 7. Rather, the other partner cannot prevent the construction. Since each can prevent the other from building the entire original wall solely within his own property, as stated in the gloss to seif 4, the Sages necessarily established a reciprocal right for each to build upon the entire jointly owned wall. Otherwise, whenever one needed to raise the wall and the existing half wall could not support the addition, since a standard wall has a minimum thickness of six tefachim, he would be forced to erect a new wall from the ground and lose the full wall width from his portion. Since he is permitted by rabbinic law to build on the whole shared wall, he is like one who descends with permission and is entitled to the favorable measure of reimbursement. It is also possible that because the wall is partnership property, a later indication of consent is sufficient, as explained in siman 176 concerning partners whose conduct is effective when one accepts the other's act.
