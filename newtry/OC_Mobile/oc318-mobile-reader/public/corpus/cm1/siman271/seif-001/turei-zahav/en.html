(Seif 2, but he acquires the reins.) Maggid Mishneh writes that this seif of Rambam is an explicit Mishna in Bava Metzia. I am greatly astonished, for there is no such Mishna in the first chapter, only Rabbi Chelbo’s statement on page 8 that one does not acquire a found animal by holding its reins. The acquisition of the reins themselves is not discussed. The ruling itself also requires study, for it appears that he acquires the entire rein, although its other end rests upon the donkey. The Sema writes that this is because he can pull it toward himself. This is astonishing. In Bava Metzia 9, Rabbi Abahu indeed says that he acquires because he can detach it and bring it to himself, but the Gemara rejects this as an error. The Gemara therefore concludes that the rider acquires the donkey and the portion of the reins upon the animal’s cheeks, as stated in the next seif. The Sema explains that in that case the portion of the reins upon the animal is subordinate to the animal. This is not the implication of the sugya. The Gemara does not reject the baraita’s statement that one holding the reins acquires all of them because the portion on the animal is subordinate. Rather, it rejects entirely the principle “since he can detach it.” Had that principle been accepted, he would acquire the entire rein, including the part on the animal’s cheeks, because his ability to detach it would make us view it as already removed from the donkey’s head. Rabbi Abahu indeed held this, but the Gemara rejected the whole principle. How then can we say Rambam relies on it? One cannot answer that the Gemara concerns a case where another person holds the opposite end, for the Tur writes here that even if each person lifts the end lying on the ground, neither acquires it, and Rashi likewise implies this. The Shulchan Aruch rules accordingly. It therefore appears to me that Rambam’s statement that he acquires “the reins” does not mean all the reins. He teaches only that he acquires some portion of them despite not acquiring the donkey, relying on what he later writes concerning the extent of acquisition where another person holds the donkey.
