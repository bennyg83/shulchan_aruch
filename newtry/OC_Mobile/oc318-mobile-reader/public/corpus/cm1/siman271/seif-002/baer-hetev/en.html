He leads. See above, simanim 197 and 198.
Alone. The reason he acquires the entire lead rope is that he is able to pull all of it toward himself from the animal's head. This differs from the following seif, where one person rides and another holds the rope. Since the rider acquires the animal, the rope attached to the animal's head is subordinate to the animal. Since the person holding the rope does not acquire the entire rope, he also does not acquire the section between his hand and the animal, because he lifted only one end while the other end is regarded as resting on the ground. See above, simanim 197 and 269. Thus writes the Sema. This is surprising, because it contradicts Bava Metzia 9a, which indicates that the fact that he can pull it toward himself does not cause acquisition, as I wrote in siman 269, seif 5. We must therefore say that here he acquires only the portion of the rope actually held in his hand. This is stated explicitly in the Semag, positive commandment 74, folio 154, beginning of column 3, and in Hagahot Maimoniyot, chapter 17 of Hilchot Gezelah. See there, and what I wrote in siman 195, seif 4. Shach.
