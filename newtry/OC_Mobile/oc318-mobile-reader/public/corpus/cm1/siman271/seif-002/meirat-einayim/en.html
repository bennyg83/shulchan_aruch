But not the one who leads it. The Rambam writes this in chapter 17 of Hilchot Gezeilah. The Beit Yosef writes at the beginning of siman 196 that according to the Rambam, pulling and leading acquire an animal in a sale, gift, or found property, whether a camel or donkey, except where one person pulls a camel while another leads it. There the puller acquires and the leader does not. The Rosh and Tur in siman 196 disagree. They hold that with a camel by leading and a donkey by pulling, one of those forms fails, but we do not know which. Therefore, in a sale or gift neither party acquires because the property remains in the prior owner's possession, while in a found object or ownerless property both acquire because there is no prior owner. When the Tur here writes that acquisition from ownerless property is equivalent to acquisition by sale, I explained in the Perishah that he means only where there is a definite valid acquisition, not in the doubtful cases of leading a camel or pulling a donkey, where in a sale we retain the property with its former owner but in ownerless property no such presumption exists.
