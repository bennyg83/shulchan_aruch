And he gives him his share, etc. The Tur concludes: because he wrote to him, "If I leave it fallow and do not cultivate it, I will pay you from the best." Meaning, since this clause is customarily written, it is treated as though he actually wrote it, as explained above. This is also evident from the Tur and Mechaber's conclusion that if he wrote, "If I leave it fallow I will pay you one thousand zuz," that undertaking is an asmachta and does not take effect, as though it had not been written, and he pays only according to what the field should ordinarily have produced. They do not say that he is entirely exempt because the clause is an asmachta and therefore legally nonexistent. Rather, even where the clause is treated as unwritten, he must still pay the normal amount, because the standard clause, "If I leave it fallow I will pay from the best," is treated as part of the arrangement even when not written. This is straightforward.
