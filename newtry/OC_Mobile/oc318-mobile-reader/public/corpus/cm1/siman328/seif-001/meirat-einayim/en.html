And I will set up a heap before you. Meaning, he obligated himself to plow and sow for the sake of producing a grain heap. The Sages assessed that when two se'ah remain above the expenses, so that those two se'ah remain before him as the net produce, that is already called a heap. He must continue laboring until that point, even though from those two se'ah he must still give the sharecropper's agreed portion, because he accepted that obligation as well when he wrote, "and take your share from it, etc."
Even if he did not write, etc. The Tur and Mechaber write a similar rule at the beginning of siman 42. The reason is that since this clause is customarily written, anyone who accepts the field without specification accepts it subject to the prevailing practice. It therefore has the force of a condition imposed by beit din and must be fulfilled.
