Rav Chisda disagrees with R' Yochanan only regarding a live animal. Yet in several places the Rif writes in such a situation that the halacha follows Shmuel, because the halacha does not follow R' Yochanan where both Rav and Shmuel disagree with him. Where the return is made knowingly, R' Yochanan agrees with Shmuel; where it is made without the owner's knowledge, Rav agrees with him. Rav Chisda too can be understood as agreeing with Shmuel. I later found that Ramban in Milchamos raised the same question against the Rif. Baal HaMaor and other authorities further challenge the Rif from the Gemara's attempt to connect the dispute to Tannaim: 'one who steals,' etc., Rav Zevid said, etc. In the case of a sela, R' Akiva cannot be established except according to Rav, and the Rif himself rules in the third chapter of Bava Metzia like R' Akiva. Therefore the halacha should follow Rav. Baal HaMaor accordingly rules like Rav, even regarding a live animal. Nimukei Yosef answers in Ramban's name that the rule that the owner need not be informed applies only where the thief returns the object to the place from which he stole it. Here, however, the owner's original guarding arrangement has ended, and the thief must return it to the owner's domain. This is the meaning of seif 3 concerning a purse or lamb. Proof is that R' Yochanan himself rules like R' Akiva in Bava Metzia 43b. See Baal HaMaor and Milchamos. Ramban writes there that the Tannaim in the Baraisa concerning one who robbed and secretly replaced the item necessarily disagree with Rav, because it says 'one who robbed' and not 'one who stole,' implying that the owner knew of the taking. That Baraisa is halachic according to the sugya in Bava Metzia 64a, as the Rif writes. This is the meaning of 'therefore,' etc., according to the second answer there. See Rashi there, s.v. 'he has,' etc.; the Rosh and Tur write likewise. The Rif explains the Gemara oppositely: if the owner has money in the purse, the thief is discharged because a person regularly feels or checks his money; if he has none, the thief is not discharged because the owner does not check it. The Mechaber combines both explanations. Rashba likewise writes that even the Rif agrees with Rashi where the owner did not previously know the count; the Gemara simply does not discuss that ordinary case, because ordinarily a person knows how much money he has for the very reason that he checks it.
(Likkut) 'One who steals an object,' etc., through 'because a person is accustomed,' is the Tur's wording and follows Rashi's explanation. 'And he found,' etc., likewise follows Rashi and the Tur. According to the Rif, however, 'because a person is accustomed,' etc., refers also to before the thief returned it: the owner knew the number of coins. This is the meaning of 'but if,' etc., which is Rambam's wording following the Rif. Rashba explains that the Rif too agrees with Rashi where the owner did not know the count beforehand; the Gemara simply does not discuss such a case because ordinarily a person knows the number, since people are accustomed to check their money. See Rashba. The practical dispute is therefore whether the presumption is that he knew: according to the Rif he ordinarily knows; according to Rashi and the Tur he ordinarily does not know. The Mechaber rules in this point like Rashi. Rashba, however, favors the Rif based on Bava Metzia 21b, where the Gemara says, 'Come and hear: money,' according to R' Yitzchak, etc. At first glance Rashi and the Rif also disagree where the purse contains no other money, but this is not so. The Rif explains that the robber returned the stolen money to the owner's purse. Rashi does not disagree with that case. Rashi's case is that the robber mixed the stolen money into the owner's other coins and the owner then put all the money back into his purse; there the Rif agrees that the owner is likely to check because of the money he just handled. The Mechaber therefore records both explanations regarding the case where he had none: Rashi's explanation, 'if he knows the number,' as the Tur writes, and the Rif's explanation, 'but if,' etc. Both are correct even according to Rashi.
