When does this apply, etc.; but one who steals a lamb, etc. The case of a lamb differs from the preceding case of stolen money only where the owners knew neither of the theft nor of the return. With other property that has no life, the thief is exempt immediately upon returning it. With living animals, however, return without the owner's knowledge does not suffice because the animal has become accustomed to different paths, as the Mechaber explains. This is straightforward.
If they counted the flock, etc. Since the owners knew of the theft, they know that the lamb learned to travel by different routes and will therefore guard it more carefully than the rest of the flock. The Beit Yosef cites an authority who writes that if the stolen lamb was spotted or otherwise uniquely marked and no similar animal is in the flock, then if the owners knew of the theft and the thief returned it to the flock, he is exempt even without a later count, because the returned animal is readily recognizable. See Darkei Moshe, where this is discussed at length.
