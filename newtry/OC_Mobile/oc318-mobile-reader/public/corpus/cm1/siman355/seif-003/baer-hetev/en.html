Witnesses. The Sema writes that without witnesses, since the thief returned the stolen object on his own, we have seen that he repented, and we would not say that the owners no longer want him to continue guarding it. When witnesses saw that it was stolen, however, we say that he returned it out of fear of the witnesses. This resolves the objection of the Shiltei Giborim. The Bach writes similarly. In my view, however, the mention of witnesses merely excludes a case where he denies having stolen it, or it is mentioned because of the latter clause, to teach the greater novelty that even though witnesses saw him return it he remains liable. The Rambam plainly mentioned witnesses only in order to establish liability for double payment. In truth, the same law applies when he admits stealing it: for purposes of liability until he notifies the owners, his admission has the same law as witnesses. This is also proven because the Mishnah in HaMafkid, Bava Metzia 41, was not established entirely according to Rabbi Akiva, with the first clause lacking witnesses and the latter clause having witnesses. The other authorities likewise rule without mentioning witnesses. The objection of the Shiltei Giborim is no objection from the outset. Once he stole it, his status as guardian ended and he no longer guards it for the owner. Therefore, even if he later repents, he must return it to the owners themselves. I later saw that the Ma'adanei Melech writes similarly. Shach.
