When the two houses are equal, etc. The same law applies even when they are unequal and the parties did not divide them by appraisal and monetary equalization. The Tur and Mechaber merely stated the usual case. See the Perishah.
The one whose lot was the house with two entrances paid, etc. The same applies if the house with one entrance is superior to the house with two entrances and its owner paid the one receiving the two-entrance house the excess value. The owner of the two-entrance house still receives four amot for each entrance, because otherwise the payment would have needed to be larger. In the Perishah I wrote that perhaps the Tur and Mechaber state specifically the case where the owner of the two-entrance house pays the excess as the greater novelty. One might have thought that because the extra payment was made generally, without saying it was on account of the second entrance, the additional four amot were not included. The ruling teaches otherwise. All the more so where the one-entrance owner pays the two-entrance owner, because there is then no weakness in failing to mention that the other house has two entrances. Why would he need to mention the other's advantage?
This enhancement is included, etc. Meaning, even though they made the valuation generally and did not expressly mention the four amot.
