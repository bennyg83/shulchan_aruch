But a courtyard in which each has a known share. This gloss is very difficult. If it means that each party knows the specific physical portion he uses and neither uses the other's part, then even if each house has less than four cubits before it they may divide, as explained in siman 171, seif 2. If it means merely that each has a known fractional share, such as one half, or a firstborn has two shares, then that is exactly Mechaber's basic case and Rama adds nothing. Moreover, the gloss appears to address whether the parties can compel division when there is sufficient courtyard space, while this siman generally discusses how extra courtyard rights are allocated even when they divide voluntarily. The end of the gloss, saying that if each receives four by four cubits before his house they divide, is also difficult. It implies that even where each has only one entrance they may divide with only four by four, although siman 171, seif 3, requires four cubits in addition to the entrance space. Perishah already notes that Maggid Mishneh, from whom Beit Yosef and Rama derive the rule, should read that there are eight cubits by four before each house. That solves the numerical difficulty but not the earlier ones. It therefore appears that this gloss belongs in siman 171, seif 3, where Mechaber requires four cubits in the courtyard in addition to four cubits for each entrance. Rama's gloss would then mean: this applies to an ordinary courtyard whose rights arose by first use, where one person had one entrance and another two. But where each party has a known fractional ownership share through purchase or inheritance, one need not allocate four cubits for every entrance. It is enough that each receives eight by four cubits before his house, even if one has two entrances. If the gloss is retained here, missing words must be supplied to make clear that it is speaking about the coercive division rules of siman 171.
