How four cubits are allotted for a house or courtyard, the manure of a courtyard, and billeting. This siman contains nine seifim.
When a courtyard is divided, four cubits are allotted to each doorway and the remainder is divided equally, because every doorway requires four cubits extending into the courtyard. Even if the doorway is very wide, the owner receives four cubits extending into the courtyard across its entire width. If the doorway is less than four cubits wide, its allotment is widened on the sides so that he receives an area of four by four cubits. If one house opening into the courtyard has two doorways and another has only one, the owner of the first receives four cubits for each doorway according to its width, while the owner of the single doorway receives only four cubits in front of it. This applies where two persons acquired ownerless land, such as where one built a house with two doorways and the other built a house with one doorway, and afterward they enclosed the area to form a courtyard. Rama: Some say the same applies if a father orally divided his property and gave one son a house with two doorways and another a house with one doorway. If, however, two persons jointly purchased or inherited the property, they acquired or inherited equal shares in both the houses and the courtyard, and neither receives more than the other. Even if they first divided the houses before dividing the courtyard, the person who received the house with two doorways does not thereby acquire four cubits in the courtyard for each doorway. Rather, the courtyard is divided equally.
