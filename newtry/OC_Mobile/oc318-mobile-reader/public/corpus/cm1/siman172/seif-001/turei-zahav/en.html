Seif 1: When does this apply? When two persons acquired from ownerless property. The Beit Yosef writes that at first glance Rabbi Yosef Halevi explained the dispute of Rav Huna and Rav Chisda only in one context, and later suggests that the Rambam differs. This is very surprising. It is clear that Rabbi Yochanan follows Rav Chisda, and that Rabbi Yosef Halevi speaks both of ownerless property and of inheritance. Each party acquires according to the governing law. Thus, one cannot compel his fellow to divide unless, in a case of ownerless property, each entrance receives four cubits based on what was acquired through the entrances, as Rabbeinu writes at the end of this siman. In inheritance or purchase, there must at least be four cubits for one entrance. The same applies in ownerless property where the entrances are equal. Even though his fellow will also receive an allotment, one may withhold division until each receives the proper amount. The Beit Yosef later quotes the end of Rabbi Yosef Halevi's words as saying that there must be open space of four by four before the entrance. This is a scribal error. It should read four by eight, as the Maggid Mishneh states explicitly. There is no disagreement between the Rambam and the other authorities. All agree that Rabbi Yochanan's ruling applies both to ownerless property and to inheritance or purchase. The Beit Yosef's question is also no question, for Rabbi Yochanan did not seek to define the rights attached to the entrances. He merely taught that each owner may withhold division until every entrance receives four cubits, each according to the law applicable to him.
