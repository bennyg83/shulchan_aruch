Who acquired. The Sema writes that they distinguished between the cases in order to resolve the rule that brothers who divided an inheritance have no right of way, window, ladder, or water channel against one another. They therefore explain that there the brothers inherited their father's property, whereas here they acquired ownerless property. From the Tur it appears that Rashi distinguished differently: here the father divided his property between his two sons by his own directive, as the Rama states, unlike an ordinary inheritance. The Rosh explains that the case of the brothers concerns a division made by appraisal, where one brother paid the other an equalizing payment. These explanations need not disagree, for each authority stated one applicable circumstance. The Shach writes that it appears to him that the text should read, "not by appraisal and without an equalizing payment"; examine this carefully. See also siman 173, seif 3, and the Rosh. It is nevertheless possible, albeit with difficulty, to retain the text without emendation: the case of the brothers concerns one who made an equalizing payment, yet they still do not obtain against one another window rights to the extent required for four cubits, or alternatively he has windows from another place; see there. With respect to the four cubits required for entrances and for a vineyard, however, a greater right is needed, and therefore one who made an equalizing payment does acquire it. So writes the Shach. (See also what the Taz writes on this matter.)
