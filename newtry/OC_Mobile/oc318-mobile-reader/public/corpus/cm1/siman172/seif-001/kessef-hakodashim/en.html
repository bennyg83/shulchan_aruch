Four cubits; see Sema, seif katan 2. Concerning the four cubits assigned to an entrance in siman 172, as discussed by the Tur there and in the glosses from the Perishah printed in the newer editions of the Tur, I am uncertain whether the four cubits of courtyard space, in addition to the four cubits belonging to the doorway itself, are assigned only on the side where the doorway opens, or along every side of the walls within the courtyard. The implication of the Tur, those glosses, and the Sema at the beginning of the siman is that even for the courtyard space one receives only an area of four cubits by four cubits, no more, so that together with the four cubits belonging to the doorway there are four by eight cubits, as stated in the glosses. One might have thought that at least the entire width of the wall containing the doorway receives a depth of four cubits, and that even where the wall itself is narrower than four cubits he nevertheless receives four by four cubits, just as a doorway narrower than four cubits still receives four cubits. The sources, however, do not read that way. He receives only four by eight cubits in front of the doorway, and any additional area is divided equally. The four by eight cubits must be rectangular, as stated by the Tur. This must be reconciled with the Rosh regarding mezuzah, where a true four by four cubit square is required, whereas Rambam holds that it is sufficient if the area is capable of being squared, as in Yoreh Deah 286:13 and the Shach there. Here it appears that Rambam does not dispute the Rif and Rosh that an actual rectangular area is required, although the Gemara in Sukkah 3a appears to compare the cases. This requires study. As to shop structures adjoining an entrance, what are called a ledge or stall, whether they count as part of the doorway for purposes of the four cubits, it appears that only an opening that reaches to ground level lies within the four cubits.
