It does not receive four cubits. Meaning, four cubits are not assigned to its entrance as they are to the entrance of a house. The Sages gave four cubits at a house entrance for unloading one's burden, because ordinarily a donkey cannot be brought into a house to unload there. An open portico, however, can be entered with the donkey. Even if the portico itself is less than four cubits wide, we do not add courtyard space to complete four cubits, for it is no better than a house less than four cubits wide, whose entrance does not receive four cubits, as the Tur and Mechaber write below in seif 8. But if the portico stands before the entrance of a house, four cubits must be provided for the house entrance. The width of the portico itself counts toward those four cubits because the donkey can enter there. Rama therefore concludes that whatever the portico lacks from four cubits is completed from the courtyard, and the two areas together form the four cubits due to the house entrance. The Tur writes this in the name of Ri. The first part of the Mechaber follows Rashi's explanation, as the Tur cites him. See the Perishah.
