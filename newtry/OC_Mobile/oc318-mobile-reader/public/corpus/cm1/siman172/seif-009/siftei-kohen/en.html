The manure of the courtyard, etc. See Sema, seif katan 20, where he rejects the Levush, who explains the first clause as referring to manure used to fertilize their fields, while explaining the latter clause, concerning billeting, as the expenses imposed by the king upon the residents of the courtyard. I say that the Levush understood the matter correctly. His words are precisely those of Rashi in the Gemara, who explains 'the manure in the courtyard' as division of the manure, yet explains 'billeting' as expenses for the king's men. For this reason the Levush intentionally omitted the word 'but.' It is also possible that although the Rambam and Tosafot explain billeting as expenses for the king's men, they agree that the first clause of the baraita, concerning manure in the courtyard, means division of the manure for fertilizing their fields. The word 'but' used by the Rambam and Tur is not precise enough to prove that the two clauses must be opposites. The proof is that all the well-known authorities cite no alternative to Rashi's explanation of the manure, whereas they do cite two explanations of billeting. Tosafot likewise give another explanation of billeting without offering a different explanation of the manure, which proves that they agree with Rashi on that point. Examine this carefully. The practical law concerning the expenses of removing manure requires study. See the Agudah and Chiddushei Agudah.
