The language of Rambam, chapter 15 of Hilkhot Mekhirah, law 3. The Maggid Mishneh writes that Rambam compares these cases to the one above, as written in seif 1. The Rif explains in chapter HaZahav that with respect to a defect, whenever it is discovered the buyer may return the item to its owner. Rav Hai Gaon likewise writes so.
The Maggid Mishneh writes that Rambam derived this from the law of one who betroths a woman and has relations with her, after which defects are found in her. We say there that a person does not drink from a cup unless he first examines it, and therefore he is liable for her ketubah, as stated in Ketubot, chapter 7, at the beginning of 76a. The Maggid Mishneh cites an authority who writes that if the item was one the buyer could have inspected immediately, such as something that could be tested or tasted, and he failed to do so, while the seller sold it without any express representation, the buyer cannot later rescind. The Sema also cites this.
