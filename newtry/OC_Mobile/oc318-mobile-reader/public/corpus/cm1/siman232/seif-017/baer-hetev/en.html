The seller. Meaning, the seller swears in order to exempt himself, for Shimon does not swear in order to extract money from Reuven. So states the Rosh in a responsum cited by the Tur. At first glance this implies that if the money is still in the buyer's possession and the seller seeks to extract it, the buyer should swear and thereby exempt himself. This is not conclusive, because one could say that once Shimon drew the oil into his possession he acquired it, and the purchase money became a debt in his hands. It would then resemble the exchange of a cow for a donkey discussed in simanim 223 and 224. Yet the cases are not identical, for there the doubt arose in the claimant's possession, and we apply the principle, "Here it was found and here it arose." Here, by contrast, the oil is presently murky before us, and presumably it was already murky from the outset. This is the Sema's analysis. The Shach writes that the matter is straightforward and involves no unresolved question of law, and this appears primary. See Responsa Rashdam, siman 80, on these laws, and also siman 379.
The receiver. The act was that he did not say that he had never promised to be clear unless he was sold to him, and that he was good for a few people who wanted him to be cheaper or if he claims a truth that I promised to dive, and I gave him, and that he was replaced with this alien oil if the seller had vowed to break down in his oath unless he had given him any oil.
