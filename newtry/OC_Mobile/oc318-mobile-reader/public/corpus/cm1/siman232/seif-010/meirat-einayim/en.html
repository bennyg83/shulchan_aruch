And these are called simpon. The Gemara in the first chapter of Kiddushin uses this term.
A growth in the flesh. Rambam likewise uses this wording. "Shuma" means a wart or growth in the flesh wherever it is found, even on the face. It does not mean a growth consisting only of flesh, excluding one containing bone, as we distinguish with respect to a firstborn animal, discussed in the Tur, Yoreh Deah siman 309, where a growth with bone is considered a blemish. With a slave purchased for work, as the Mechaber concludes, there is certainly no basis for such a distinction. The Tur simply writes "shuma."
But gambling is not a defect. This wording is imprecise. Stealing money, snatching and fleeing, gluttony, and drunkenness mentioned above are likewise not considered defects. The "some say" disagree only concerning kidnapping. Rama merely follows the Tur's wording: "A thief or kubiyustus is not a defect." Rashi explains kubiyustus as one who kidnaps people, and Rambam likewise. Some explain that kidnapping is significant like one proscribed by the government, while kubiyustus instead means a gambler. Rabbeinu Chananel explains this way, and the Tur concludes that this is primary. Rama therefore writes, "But gambling, etc.," as though saying that according to the alternative explanation kubiyustus does not mean kidnapping, as held by the view of the Mechaber based on Rambam, but gambling, which belongs among the conduct traits that are not considered a defect. This is clear.
