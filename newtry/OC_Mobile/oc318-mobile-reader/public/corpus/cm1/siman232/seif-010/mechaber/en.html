One who sells a male or female slave cannot be required to take the slave back for defects that do not prevent work. Such defects are called simpon. If visible, the purchaser saw them. If hidden, such as a mole, dog bite, foul breath, nasal odor, or the like, the purchaser still cannot return the slave, because the defect does not prevent labor and slaves are purchased for work, not marital relations. If the slave has a severe boil, a disease that weakens the body, epilepsy, Rama: a disorder that causes him to fall to the ground, or a state of mental stupor, it is a defect because it prevents work. Leprosy or similarly repulsive conditions are also defects, because people recoil from them and the slave cannot properly perform food and drink service. If he is an armed robber, this is a defect that destroys his entire value because the government will seize and execute him. If he is registered for royal service, this too is a defect, because the government may seize him for labor whenever it wishes. If he is merely a thief, snatcher, kidnapper, habitual runaway, glutton, drunkard, or the like, he cannot be returned, because slaves are presumed prone to such vices unless the seller specified otherwise. Rama: Some say a kidnapper is like one registered for royal punishment, but gambling is not a defect.
