(Supplement) The sale is acquired, etc. See Baer HaGolah, seif katan 1. Rashba rules this way in Kiddushin, and Ran cites him. Ran offers another explanation, that the sale is entirely void, but writes that where the deficiency can be supplied, the sale is not void and the seller must supply it. He says this is Rambam's view here. According to the first view, the objection of Rashi and Tosafot in Bava Batra 104a is resolved, although their language indicates that they disagree. Raavad likewise disagrees. Ramban later writes that only a deficiency that cannot be supplied voids the sale, such as where the field itself lacks the promised measure, but where completion is possible the error is corrected. He attributes this to Ri Migash and supports it from the case of eggs in the first chapter of Yom Tov. Ran rejects that proof because the eggs had already been spoiled and could not be returned, and in any event compares that case to fine goods found inferior rather than to a measurable deficiency. Nevertheless, Ran accepts the ruling itself. Rashba in Kiddushin maintains that the sale never becomes void and that Rava's statement that one returns means only that the overcharge is returned. He attributes this to Ri Migash and adduces support from Bava Batra 106b, although Ran rejects that proof. Rashba explains the statement in Bava Batra 90a concerning overreaching to mean that this rule makes no distinction between one-sixth, more, or less. See Baer HaGolah and Raavad's gloss to chapter 15 of the Laws of Sale. (End of supplement.)
