Shach seif katan 15, "and it is obvious." Note: The Taz above in siman 75 considered the opposite obvious, and there in Tumim, seif katan 22, he concluded that if he gave them to him in exchange for merchandise, it is a case of "I do not know whether I became obligated to you." However, if he was already obligated and gave them in payment, then if the borrower did not inspect them at all, he should have known and is liable. But if he inspected them and they were nevertheless found counterfeit, he is exempt. I have seen support for this from the Rashba in Gittin regarding those gardeners, and from his words in Gittin 75b regarding a doubt whether the payment was closer to the lender or to the borrower. See there. This is evidence that the borrower is always exempt. See what the Shach writes above in siman 126, seif katan 69. Afterward I found that the Dagul MeRevavah here also holds that the borrower is always exempt. The matter still requires study.
