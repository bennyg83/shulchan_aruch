And he erred by any amount, etc., until there is no law of overreaching, etc. Only with monetary overreaching did the Sages say that one waives an error below one sixth, for the reason written by the Rosh and cited by the Tur and Mechaber at the beginning of siman 227: seller and purchaser cannot precisely determine the exact value of an item. That reasoning does not apply to errors in measure, weight, or number.
It may be corrected forever. This means that the sale itself remains valid, while the mistaken amount is returned. Where possible, the seller completes the missing number, measure, or weight, as the Mechaber later explains regarding nuts. If, however, he sold land and said it measured twenty cubits in length but it is short by half a cubit and he cannot supplement it from adjacent land, the entire sale is void, as explained in siman 218. This differs from one who says, "I sell you a kor of land measured by rope," where the price is reduced according to the deficiency, because by saying "measured by rope" he effectively stated that the sale was by exact measure.
