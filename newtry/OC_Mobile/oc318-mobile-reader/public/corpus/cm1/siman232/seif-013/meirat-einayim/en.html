One who sells an item that had a defect, etc. Rambam in chapter 16 of Hilchot Mechirah writes, "From here you learn that one who sells an item..." This law is derived from the preceding rule concerning one who purchases a cow for slaughter, slaughters it, and discovers it was a tereifah. Although after slaughter the animal is in a worse state than before slaughter, he nevertheless returns it to the seller and is exempt. The reason is that slaughter is the ordinary intended use of the animal. It follows that if he inflicted some other defect that was not an ordinary use, he would be liable. Otherwise the ruling could have stated the greater novelty with respect to an unusual defect rather than slaughter. This is the meaning of "if he changed it, etc." See the Perishah.
