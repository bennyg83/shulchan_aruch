Reuven sold Shimon houses in another city. Before the sale was completed, non-Jews entered the houses, damaged portions, blackened the walls with smoke, and removed doors and windows. Shimon seeks to withdraw, while Reuven argues that the defect is temporary and that the cost of restoring the houses should be deducted from the price while the sale remains in force. The law follows Reuven. Rama: He sold a house, and it is still called a house. Likewise, a defect not intrinsic to the house, such as another person's right of passage or a water channel crossing it, may be removed and the sale stands. If the defect is in the structure itself, such as where the seller represented a wall as sound and it proves unstable, he cannot cure the misrepresentation merely by building a new sound wall. The same applies in analogous cases.
