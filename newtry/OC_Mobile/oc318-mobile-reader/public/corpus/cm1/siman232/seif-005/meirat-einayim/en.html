Before the completion of the sale, gentiles entered, etc., until Shimon wishes to retract. The Tur explains the facts more fully from a responsum of the Rosh. Reuven and Shimon lived in one place, while Reuven owned houses in a distant place. Shimon inspected those houses when he came to buy them and they were intact. In the interim gentiles entered and damaged them. Shimon did not know this and completed the purchase under the assumption that they remained as he had seen them. Only afterward did he learn that the damage had occurred before completion of the sale, and he claimed that the transaction was a mistaken sale.
The law is with Reuven. This applies specifically where the defect can be restored to its prior condition by money that will be deducted from the price, so that it is considered a temporary defect. The responsum says this explicitly. With a permanent defect in the substance of the house, even if slight, the sale is void, as already explained and as Rama states immediately afterward.
