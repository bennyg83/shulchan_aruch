A treifah caused by a sircha, etc. Although this defect is common, it is still a mistaken sale, as stated in Bava Batra there: not because of a majority, etc., but because not all animals have the defect. Likewise an ox found to be a habitual gorer can constitute a mistaken sale even though most oxen are not presumed guarded, as stated in Bava Kamma chapter 1 and at the end of Kesubos chapter 3.
Some disagree, etc. As stated at the beginning of Kesubos 2b: a common unavoidable occurrence is different, because since he should have stipulated, etc. The cases of the ox and slave are not proof, because there the seller knows the defect and the buyer relies upon him and therefore does not stipulate, based on the presumption that the remnant of Israel, etc. This differs from the present case.
Even a treifah, etc. This is unlike the case of improper slaughter in Bava Kamma chapter 9, 99b, because where the item cannot be eaten the sale is plainly mistaken.
