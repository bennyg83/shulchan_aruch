It comes back. I thought he had nothing and you didn’t tell me yes because you knew he had nothing but to make my opinion:
And he must specify it just as he specifies overreaching. This is the Rambam's language in chapter 15 of Hilchot Mekhirah. The Maggid Mishneh says the rule is derived a fortiori from overreaching. Overreaching can be claimed only within the time needed to show the item to a merchant, yet even there waiver is ineffective unless the defect in value is explicitly stated. Certainly, with a physical defect for which rescission is available forever, the defect must be specified. The Tur comments that this accords with the Rambam's general position that a person cannot obligate himself in an unquantified amount, whereas the Rosh holds that he can. The Beit Yosef approves the Tur's objection, though in the Derishah I argued that his text likely requires correction. In any event, it is difficult to say that the Mechaber rules here like the Rambam merely because the amount is unquantified, since elsewhere, in simanim 60 and 207, he rules against the Rambam on that issue. Rather, here the ruling rests on the a fortiori inference from overreaching. A buyer can say: I thought you were merely praising the article and that this defect was not actually present. Since the law is lenient to the buyer by allowing rescission forever for a defect, it is reasonable also to require explicit specification before waiver is effective. Even if the Tur may disagree in the law of overreaching, the Mechaber can still hold this view regarding physical defects. See the Perishah.
