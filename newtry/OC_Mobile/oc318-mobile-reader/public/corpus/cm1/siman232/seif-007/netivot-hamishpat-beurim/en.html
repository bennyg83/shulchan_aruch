Like one who expressly states the ona'ah. See Sema, seif katan 16, who questions Tur: here Tur allows an effective stipulation, while in ordinary ona'ah Tur holds such a condition does not work. The cases are not comparable. In ordinary ona'ah the object is sold for less or more than its actual value. The injured party therefore must waive a known monetary entitlement. If he does not know what he is waiving, the waiver is ineffective. Here, however, the item is sold at its fair value taking the disclosed defect into account. There is no separate ona'ah amount for the buyer to waive. Ordinarily we presume a person does not buy a defective item, but once the seller tells him that defects are present and the buyer proceeds, we see that this buyer is not particular about that kind of defect. There is therefore no problem of an uninformed monetary waiver.
