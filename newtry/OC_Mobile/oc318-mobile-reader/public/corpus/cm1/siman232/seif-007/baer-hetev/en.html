The onaah. This is the Rambam's wording. The Tur comments that this follows the Rambam's general view that a person cannot obligate himself in an unquantified amount, while the Rosh holds that a person can create such an obligation and likewise can waive it. This creates an apparent contradiction in the Mechaber, who rules here like the Rambam, but at the end of siman 207 and in siman 60, seif 2, writes that all later authorities disagree with the Rambam and explicitly rules against him. The Sema therefore explains that the ruling here is not based solely on the rule of an unquantified obligation. Rather, it follows by an a fortiori inference from onaah, as the Maggid Mishneh explains. With onaah the reason is that the buyer can say: I thought there was no onaah and assumed you merely said this to reassure me. Thus far the Sema. This is correct and is explicit in the Maggid Mishneh, upon whom the Beit Yosef relied. The Kesef Mishneh also writes that the Maggid Mishneh's words are correct. Shach.
