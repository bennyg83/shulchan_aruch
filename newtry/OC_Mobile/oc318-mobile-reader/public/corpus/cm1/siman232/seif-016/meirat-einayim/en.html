The burden of proof is upon the one who seeks to extract from another. Consequently, if the money is still in the purchaser's possession and the seller seeks to collect it, the seller bears the burden of proof. This differs from one who sells an animal for slaughter and it is found to be tereifah, where it is unknown whether the defect arose before or after the sale. There the Tur and Mechaber rule that the purchaser must prove the defect existed earlier, and if he cannot, the money is collected from him. An animal is presumed to have been sound, and since the uncertainty arose in the buyer's possession we say that the defect occurred where it was found. Cheese has no comparable presumption, because aged cheese commonly develops worms. I wrote similarly above regarding beer that soured. This differs from the implication of the Beit Yosef, who appears to equate the two cases. See the Derishah.
