One who sells, etc. The opening clause is the baraita's case where one obvious defect was present and disclosed. The concluding clause concerns the Gemara's inquiry where some defects are visible and others are not. Rashi explains that if the seller listed several defects that were not all present, yet another undisclosed defect was present, the sale is mistaken. If he singled out the actual defect, the purchaser should have examined it. Tosafot explain differently: the seller mentioned the actual defect together with another defect that was also present, and the question is whether the rule applies only when two defects were mentioned or even when every listed defect was present. The Tosefta implies that mentioning two defects suffices even if the second is absent. Rif and Rosh explain that in the first clause none of the listed defects was present but another hidden defect was, while in the second clause the seller disclosed one visible and one hidden defect and both were present, together with additional hidden defects that were not present. Since the purchaser saw that one visible defect was accurately disclosed, he was expected to investigate the hidden defect as well. Rambam adopts another reading. He distinguishes a mixture of visible and hidden defects from a list consisting entirely of one category. If the seller lists both visible and hidden defects, none of the visible defects exists, and one hidden defect does exist, the purchaser may say that he assumed the hidden defects were also merely promotional exaggeration. If visible defects were actually present, that argument fails. Rambam further rules that where the seller points out the actual defect and lists others, the sale stands even if all or some of the others are also present. Shulchan Aruch's seif 8 follows Rambam, but the wording of seif 9 appears to follow Tosafot and Rosh, and the various attempts of Bach and the commentators to reconcile the texts are highly strained.
