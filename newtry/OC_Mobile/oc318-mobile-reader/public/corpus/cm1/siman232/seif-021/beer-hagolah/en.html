So appears in Rambam, and the Tur copies it. The Kesef Mishneh writes that it is doubtful what the law is if the buyer had no opportunity to notify the seller before the item was stolen or lost. The wording implies that so long as he did not notify the seller it remains in the buyer's domain, yet reason does not necessarily support that. Alternatively, one may say that from the moment the defect is discovered it returns to the seller's domain if the buyer had no opportunity to notify him. The wording can then be read to mean that even if it was subsequently lost or stolen, it is the seller's responsibility once the buyer notified him that he wished to take it to a certain place. This requires study. In the Kesef Mishneh the author thus retreated from what he wrote in the Beit Yosef, where he exempted the buyer from theft and loss if he had no opportunity to notify the seller, and instead left the matter doubtful. The Bach, however, writes that Rambam's language should be understood literally: the buyer's duty of care does not end until he informs the seller, and until then he is liable for theft and loss as a paid bailee, because had buyers appeared he would have sold it. There is no distinction between having or lacking an opportunity to notify him. The Sema writes likewise in the name of the Levush. In my humble opinion this is correct, although I am unworthy to decide between them.
