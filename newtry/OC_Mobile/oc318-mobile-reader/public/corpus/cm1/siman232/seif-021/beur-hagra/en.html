Nevertheless, etc. The reason we do not follow the majority is that possession of the money remains with its current holder, and the burden of proof rests on the claimant, as stated at the beginning of Bava Kamma chapter 5 and Bava Batra there. The Gemara then challenges from the case of one who sells seed, etc., and says this is a tannaitic dispute. See below seif 23.
If he informed him, etc. A baraisa cited by Rif and Rosh there. The same appears in the Yerushalmi.
The same applies to matters, etc. As stated there, 93b: he said to him, “many, etc.”
A purchaser who bought an item, etc. See Beer HaGolah. This is the Rambam’s language: “From here you learn that one who purchases, etc.”
If the seller knew, etc. From the Gemara’s question, “What does he give, etc.?” The Rambam holds that the first Tanna disagrees only where the seller did not know. Although the seller must refund the price of the seed even if he did not know, as stated below in seif 18, he is exempt from consequential expenses, as explained there.
