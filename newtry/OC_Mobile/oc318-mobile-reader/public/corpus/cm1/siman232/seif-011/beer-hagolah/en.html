Gimel) This is the language of the Rambam there, chapter 15, halachah 6, from the baraita concerning a needle found in an animal, Chullin at the end of 50b.
Dalet) This is the language of the Tur, seif 11, from the same baraita. Rambam likewise rules this at the end of chapter 20 of Hilkhot Mekhirah.
Heh) Tosafot likewise write that we apply the principle "here it was found, here it is presumed to have been," in accordance with Shmuel's rule that whenever a doubt arises while the item is in a person's domain, the burden of proof rests upon him, Ketubot 76b. The Gemara explains that when the baraita says the burden of proof rests upon the claimant, it speaks in the ordinary case where the butcher already paid. Even if he had not paid, however, the burden of proof would still rest upon him, or else he must pay.
