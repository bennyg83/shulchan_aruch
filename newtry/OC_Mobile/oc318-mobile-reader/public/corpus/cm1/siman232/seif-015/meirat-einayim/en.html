He gives the same reason for requiring payment of rent for a courtyard in which the buyer lived. This is difficult. If that were the reason, the Tur and Rambam should have distinguished between a seller who had already received the purchase money and one who had not. Moreover, even if the price had already been paid, this should be no worse than rabbinic interest, which is not recovered after the fact. It resembles one who lends money secured by a field and says, "If you do not repay within three years, the field is mine," without saying "from now." The sale is ineffective, yet produce consumed during the three years is not recovered because it is only rabbinic interest. Rather, the correct reason is simple. Since the sale was void from the outset, the produce or rental value belongs to the original owner, and retaining it would be like retaining stolen property. Therefore the buyer must return the produce or rent even if he had not yet paid the purchase price. This is comparable to the rule that if, after three years, the lender continues consuming produce because he mistakenly believes the field has become absolutely his, the produce consumed after the three years is recovered. He consumed it under a mistaken claim of ownership. The rules elsewhere that prohibit eating produce because of interest concern primarily the prospective prohibition and do not necessarily require restitution after the produce was already taken. The law here is different because the possession itself was mistaken from the beginning. Nor can one object from the Rambam's rule regarding one who gives away all his property and the gift is later annulled, where produce already eaten is not returned. There, the donor genuinely wished the gift to exist until the event that ended it, such as the return of a son thought dead or the donor's return from danger. It therefore resembles an express gift for a limited period. Here, had the seller known the sale would be rescinded because of the defect, he would never have transferred the property in the first place. The same is true where the lender consumes produce after the supposed foreclosure date. Thus the produce must be returned as property held without right, not because of interest. This also explains why the Rambam, Tur, Maggid Mishneh, Mechaber, and Rama do not mention interest as the reason.
