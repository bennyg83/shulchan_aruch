And it was lost or stolen. The same applies while it remains in the buyer's possession. If the item had fallen in value by the time of theft, he pays only its value at the time of theft. The reason for liability is that he is a paid custodian, as explained above, and a custodian pays according to the value at the time of the loss. All the more so, where he had time to notify the seller and failed, his liability is not because the entire item is treated as absolutely his. If that were so he would be liable even for ones. Rather, the phrase that "it remains in the buyer's responsibility" means only that he bears the responsibility of a paid custodian. Likewise, if his liability arises from failure to notify where timely notice would have allowed rescue, his liability resembles that of a custodian who negligently failed to precede the loss with shepherds and sticks. Such a custodian pays according to the value at the time of the negligence or loss, not some earlier higher value. The seller, of course, returns the purchase money received.
