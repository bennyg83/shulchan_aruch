A case. One sold grain that he had purchased from a particular fortress and said to the buyer: because it comes from that fortress, pay me a specified additional profit per measure above what another purchaser offers, since you know that the measure used there is especially large. When the grain arrived, the measure turned out smaller even than an ordinary measure. The buyer demanded that the seller complete the quantity according to the large measure that both had expected from that place. The seller answered that he sold only whatever would actually come from that fortress, and that if their assumption was mistaken the buyer could rescind. This resembles selling "this sack" with the statement that it contains a specified quantity, where if it is deficient the sale is void and the seller need not complete it, as the Sema explains at the beginning of siman 232, because the seller sold only what would be brought from that place. It is also somewhat like a sale on trust. Even if one ordinarily says that a sale on trust is subject to rescission for an error in measure, weight, or number, where the parties expressly structured the transaction as a trust arrangement even regarding the measure, there is room to say that neither rescission nor supplementation can be demanded. If the buyer is in possession, however, he can perhaps say kim li that an express disclosure of intent is effective even for movable property, as discussed above in siman 230. Similarly, if a seller sells sacks of grain and tells the buyer to measure one sack and thereby estimate all the others because they are alike, and some are later found to contain less, then if the buyer can claim that they were not all from the same source as represented, he certainly may demand completion of the deficiency, because he waived individual measuring only in reliance on the seller's statement that they were all equal, and an error in measure is recoverable in any amount. Even if witnesses establish that all came from one seller and one place and sacks from that source are ordinarily equal, it may still not be treated as a sale made entirely on trust. The buyer can say: I did not rely absolutely on your statement. I relied only because the matter would immediately become verifiable and assumed that your seller had not departed from his usual measure. Now that he did, my claim is against you, and you may pursue him. This requires further study.
