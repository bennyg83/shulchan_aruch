If the excess money can be divided, etc. It appears that if the recipient knows that the giver did not count the money in groups of five, and five extra coins are found, they are a gift, and the same applies in comparable cases.
All of them are a gift. Meaning, if seven or eight extra coins are found, we do not say that five must be returned as an error arising from counting in groups of five while the remainder may be kept. Rather, just as the remainder was given as a gift, so too the five extra coins were intentionally given as a gift.
A gift. The Tur writes: it is possible that he intended to give them as a gift, or that he had stolen money from the recipient and was embarrassed to disclose it, so he intended to conceal repayment within the accounting. The same applies to anyone receiving money from another, whether as a loan or repayment. Even if he never had any prior dealings with that person, it is possible that a third person had stolen from him and told this giver, "When so-and-so comes to you, conceal repayment to him within the accounting." These are the Tur's words.
Provided, etc. Darkei Moshe writes: if the giver later comes and says, "I made a mistake in counting the money I gave you," Rabbeinu Yerucham, netiv 9, part 3, writes that the recipient must return it, while Hagahot Mordechai in chapter Eizehu Neshech writes that he need not return it.
They must not correspond to the count of fives. Meaning, if he owed forty dinarim and gave forty-four or forty-eight, we say the four extra may have resulted from counting in tens: for each group of ten he placed one dinar aside as a marker of how many tens he had counted, which was the customary method of counting. Thus after counting forty dinarim he had placed four aside, while the other coins used to complete each group of ten were mixed into the forty counted coins. This has the status of a counting error, not a gift. Likewise, if he finds forty-eight dinarim, one can say the money was counted in fives, with one dinar set aside after each five as a marker. There are eight groups of five in forty, producing the eight extra coins. The same applies in comparable cases. The Mechaber copies this from Rambam at the beginning of chapter 4 of Hilchot Malveh VeLoveh. From there it is clear that Shulchan Aruch contains a printing error in the closing phrase. It should read "the fives or the tens," not "in fives or in tens," and likewise the word should mean "like their count," not "in their count." This is clear.
