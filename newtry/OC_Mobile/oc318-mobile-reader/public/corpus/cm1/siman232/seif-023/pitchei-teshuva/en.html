Even if the numbers are equal. Netivot HaMishpat notes that the wording is difficult, for the text should seemingly teach the stronger case, even where most animals are purchased for slaughter. He therefore explains that the phrase “even if they are equal” is not exact. Since a numerical majority does not ordinarily determine monetary law, the decisive consideration is possession. Dagul MeRevavah also questions the wording of Ramah and Shulchan Aruch, because it implies that if most purchasers buy for slaughter, even a buyer presently in possession would have to pay. Yet the halachah follows Shmuel that monetary cases are not decided by majority. Perishah offers an explanation that is strained. Bach, however, cites Tosafot that the majority of buyers who purchase for plowing is not considered a particularly strong majority; if so, perhaps a majority purchasing for slaughter would constitute a stronger and legally effective majority. See Bach and Pri Tevuah, siman 20, for an extended treatment.
