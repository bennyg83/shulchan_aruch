Even if they are equal. The language is difficult. It would seem a greater novelty to teach that even where most buyers purchase for slaughter, the buyer may still say, "I bought for plowing," because we do not follow majority in monetary law to extract from the possessor. Perishah raises this and answers that where most purchase for slaughter we do extract from the buyer. He says the buyer's possession is weaker because he already drew and acquired the animal, unlike a seller who still holds the money. This is very difficult both in logic and from the Gemara. At the beginning of HaMokher Peirot, the Gemara asks from one who sold a slave and it was found to be a thief or kidnapper, where the buyer must keep the sale. The question assumes that if the majority of such slaves are defective we might follow the majority. Ramban in his novellae explains that the case implies even where the money is already in the buyer's possession, yet the transaction stands. If, according to Shmuel, we followed majority when the buyer was possessor, there would be no question. Necessarily, according to Shmuel, whose view we follow, one does not follow majority in monetary law to extract even from the buyer. Therefore the phrase "even if they are equal" is not precise, or it means that since majority has no force in monetary extraction, the legal analysis treats the two purposes as effectively equal. The true basis is possession. If the seller seized after the doubt arose, that seizure certainly does not help because he can claim only uncertainly. If the buyer seized and claims with certainty that he bought for plowing, while the majority of buyers in that market buy for plowing, it requires study whether seizure plus a definite claim and majority support can help. Perhaps not. There are many defects that count as defects for plowing but not for slaughter, such as blindness or lameness. Almost all ordinary physical defects are of that type; only treifah is a defect for slaughter as well. Since we rule that majority does not control monetary extraction and the buyer paid without specification, we can still say he purchased for slaughter. This may explain why the poskim state the rule without distinguishing seizure.
