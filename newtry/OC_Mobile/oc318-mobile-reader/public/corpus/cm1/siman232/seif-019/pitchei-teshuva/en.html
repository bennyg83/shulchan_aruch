A mistaken transaction. Baer Heitev cites Maharashdam's responsum. A gloss by a later gaon writes that in his view the law is plainly the reverse, with proof from siman 126. Dagul MeRevavah likewise challenges Maharashdam. He adds that even apart from that proof, Maharashdam's reasoning is difficult. The principle that one who says, “I do not know whether I repaid you,” is liable rests on the fact that a debtor should have kept track of whether he paid. Here, however, why should the payer have been more careful to verify whether the coins were genuine than the person receiving them? On the contrary, once he saw the creditor accept the coins as genuine, there was no reason for him to inspect them further. Dagul MeRevavah later found that Taz, end of siman 75, rules that the payer swears that he does not know whether the coins were his and is exempt.
