One who sold eggs and they were found, etc. The Tur cites this law concerning eggs, from a Responsum of the Rosh, as proof for the preceding law that the loss falls upon the broker. The Rosh is among those who disagree with the Mechaber's preceding ruling. Beit Yosef writes that there is no proof, because even Rambam, who in the earlier case places the loss on the purchaser rather than the broker, would agree where eggs are found spoiled. Eggs are not ordinarily broken open until one actually needs them for cooking, so what could the purchaser have done? This differs from the ox lacking molars, which the purchaser could have inspected. This is similar to the reason I wrote above concerning one who purchases a ring. This also explains why Shulchan Aruch rules without qualification above that with the ox the purchaser bears the loss, yet here rules without qualification that with spoiled eggs the seller bears it. Still, the proof is not conclusive, because here the Mechaber does not say that the seller himself had purchased the eggs from someone else, as the Responsum of the Rosh did. If the eggs were originally the seller's own, no one disputes that the sale is mistaken. In the Perishah I disagreed with Beit Yosef and wrote that the Tur is correct to equate the two cases. If with the ox the loss falls on the purchaser even though the broker himself should have inspected it so he could return it to his own seller rather than pass a defect to others, just as the purchaser should have examined his purchase and returned it to the broker, then eggs should be the same. Neither purchaser nor seller can inspect them before the ordinary time of breaking them open. That is simply the normal commercial process for eggs. If so, the loss should fall on the purchaser. Since we rule regarding eggs that the loss falls on the seller, the same should apply to the ox. Examine carefully.
It is a mistaken sale. Likewise, if he stipulated that the seller give him eggs that were laid and the seller gives eggs found inside a slaughtered hen, or if he says he wants eggs produced by a rooster and hen and the seller gives him eggs produced without a male, it is a mistaken sale. See Ran, chapter HaIsh Mekadesh, and Darkei Moshe, seif 16.
