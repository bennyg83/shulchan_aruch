An error. Responsa Rashdam, part 3, siman 80, rules regarding one who owed another money for the purchase of an object or another obligation and paid him, but after several days seeks to return some of the coins because he discovered that some were bad. The recipient says, "Why did you not inspect them at the time of payment?" If the claimant asserts with certainty that the bad coins were received from him, while the other asserts with certainty that he gave good coins, the latter swears and is exempt. But if he says, "I do not know whether these coins were mine," he must pay, and he may impose only a general cherem on the claimant, as in the rule of one who says, "I do not know whether I repaid you." If the claimant himself is uncertain and the other states with certainty that he gave good coins, the latter is exempt subject only to a general cherem. See there. This is straightforward. Shach.
