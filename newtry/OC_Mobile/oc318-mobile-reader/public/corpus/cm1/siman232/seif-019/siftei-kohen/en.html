It is a mistaken transaction. Responsa Maharashdam, part 3, siman 80, rules concerning one who owed another money for the purchase of an object or for some other matter, paid him, and after several days sought to return some of the coins because he found that some were defective. The recipient claimed, 'Why did you not inspect them at the time of payment?' If the recipient asserts with certainty that the defective coins came from the payer, and the payer asserts with certainty that he gave him good coins, the payer takes an oath and is exempt. If the payer says that he does not know whether those coins were his, he must pay, and he may impose only an unspecified ban upon the recipient, under the rule governing one who says, 'I do not know whether I repaid you.' If the recipient's claim is uncertain and the payer claims with certainty that he gave him good coins, the payer is exempt upon an unspecified ban. See there. This is straightforward.
