Peh) This is the language of the Rambam there, halachah 9, learned from the incident immediately following.
Tzadi) From the incident involving the guardian of orphans in the third chapter of Bava Metzia 42b.
Kuf) This is the very incident mentioned above. The Maggid Mishneh writes that Rambam's explanation of the episode requires study. The Kesef Mishneh and Beit Yosef explain that the Gemara's words, "therefore he swears that he did not know and the cattle keeper pays," imply straightforwardly that the broker swears and is exempt, in accordance with Rambam.
Reish) According to Rashi, Tosafot, and the Rosh there, the broker first pays the orphans and then swears in order to collect from the cattle keeper. The Gemara concludes that the cattle keeper pays him the reduced value of meat, meaning two thirds of the ordinary value. Rashi explains that the court imposed a compromise between them. Tosafot explain that this is the strict law, because had the keeper informed him, the broker would have slaughtered the animal immediately and sold the meat cheaply, rather than waiting for market day. The Rosh likewise rules this way. He also cites another explanation in the name of the Raavad: "cheap meat" does not mean two thirds, as it often does elsewhere, but rather the difference between the value of an ox for slaughter and an ox for plowing. The broker had sold it to the orphans at the price of a plow ox, but they reimburse him only at the slaughter value. It requires study why the Tur, Mechaber, and Rama omitted the law that the cattle keeper must pay and that he pays only the reduced value.
