A mistaken sale. See Baer Heitev, who cites Responsa Rashdam, and the gloss of the eminent Rabbi Eizik, who writes that in his view the rule is obviously the opposite and brings proof from siman 126. Dagul MeRevavah likewise disagrees with Rashdam and writes that the matter is not difficult according to what Shach writes in siman 156, seif katan 69, in the name of Rashba. See there carefully. Independently, Rashdam's ruling does not appear correct to him, because the main reason one who says, "I do not know whether I repaid you," is liable is that he should have kept track whether he paid, as Sema explains in siman 75, seif katan 11. Here, why should the payer have been more obligated than the recipient to inspect whether the coins were valid? On the contrary, once he saw the recipient accept them as valid, he had even less reason to investigate. He then found that Taz at the end of siman 75 rules that the recipient swears that he does not know whether the coins are his and is exempt.
