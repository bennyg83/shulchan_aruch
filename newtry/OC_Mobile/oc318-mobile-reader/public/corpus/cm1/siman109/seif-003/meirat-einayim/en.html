And if beit din announced it, etc. The Mechaber follows the Rambam at the end of chapter 12 of Hilchot Malveh VeLoveh. His ruling is based on the Mishnah and Gemara in chapter Almanah Nizonet. The Mishnah states that if the judges' appraisal was deficient by one sixth or exceeded by one sixth, the sale is void, but if they made an announcement for inspection, then even if they sold property worth one hundred for two hundred or the reverse, the sale stands. The Gemara asks regarding the first clause: If no announcement was made, then they erred in an explicit Mishnah, and even a sale at exact value should be void. It answers that the first clause concerns property that does not require announcement, or a time when announcement is unnecessary. The Rosh and Tur explain that in those cases no announcement was made at all, but if they nevertheless announced it, even though announcement was not required, the sale stands even if they erred by double. The Rif and Rambam explain that the first clause is parallel to the latter clause and also concerns a case where the required announcement was made, except that for property not requiring announcement they were not accustomed to issue a written inspection notice, so it is treated as though there had been no announcement. This explains the Mechaber's conclusion that in these cases, even though they announced them, the sale may still be void, while Rama's gloss, "some say," states the Rosh's view. The Rambam and Mechaber's opening language, however, "if they did not investigate the appraisal," is not stated explicitly in the Gemara or the Rif. The Maggid Mishneh explains that the entire passage is one clause together with what follows, "and likewise if they sold land at a time when..." See there. The Kesef Mishneh, however, writes that the Rambam's language proves there are two separate clauses, and that "they did not investigate the appraisal" means that they announced it but did not carefully conduct the appraisal and announcement according to law. He similarly explains the Rambam's definition of a written inspection notice in the Mishnah there. On this basis the words of the Mechaber and Rama are clear.

If the error is less than one sixth, their sale stands. This is because such a discrepancy is deemed waived, as with an ordinary private sale, since people do not normally insist upon such precision. At exactly one sixth, although in an ordinary transaction the sale stands and the overcharge is returned, regarding consecrated property and orphans the Sages instituted that the sale be void. This applies only when beit din sees that voiding the sale benefits the orphans. Otherwise, their position is no worse than that of an ordinary seller, as the Mechaber writes immediately afterward.
At a time when announcement is unnecessary, etc. This is explained below as specifically where property is sold for the orphans' food, for the governmental tax, or for the burial of the deceased. Not only may they sell without announcement when the money is immediately needed for one of these purposes, but even if they sell in order to repay someone who lent money for one of these needs, they sell without announcement. Otherwise people would refrain from extending such charitable loans if they had to wait through the period of announcement before being repaid. See the Perishah.

Likewise, when beit din sold items that do not require announcement, etc. The Tur in seif 5 abbreviates and combines these three cases: items that do not require announcement, a time when announcement is not made, and a place where announcement is never made. In all of them, if the judges underpriced or overpriced by one sixth, their sale is void. The Mechaber follows the Rambam's formulation in chapter 12 of Hilchot Malveh VeLoveh. See there.
Slaves and documents, etc. The Gemara explains: Why are slaves not announced? Lest they hear that they are to be sold and flee. Movable property and documents are not announced lest they be stolen. Rashi explains that when people gather around them in order to purchase them, they may be stolen.
