If beit din sold property without a public announcement, it is as though they erred in an explicit Mishnah. The sale is reversed, and they sell it again after an announcement. {Rama: Some say that if they sold land at a time when buyers were unavailable, such as during a plague or war, their act is effective, because beit din must sell only when collection is due. Others disagree. When beit din sells, the heirs bear responsibility for the warranty.} If beit din made a proper announcement, investigated carefully, and appraised precisely, their sale remains valid even if they erred and sold property worth one hundred for two hundred or property worth two hundred for one hundred. If they did not carefully investigate the appraisal and did not write a letter of inspection documenting the precise appraisal and announcement, and they erred upward or downward by one-sixth, their sale is void. If the error was less than one-sixth, the sale remains valid. Similarly, if they sold land at a time when no announcement was required, or in a place where the custom is never to announce, and they erred upward or downward by one-sixth, the sale is void even though they announced it. {Rama: Some say that once they announced it, the sale remains valid even when no announcement was required.} If beit din wishes to preserve the sale and reimburse the price discrepancy, it may do so; an ordinary person's power should not be stronger than theirs. If the discrepancy is less than one-sixth, the sale remains valid and the difference is forgiven, as in an ordinary sale, even though no announcement was made because none was required at that time. When is no announcement required? When property is sold to repay a person who lent money for burial, to provide support for a wife or daughters, or to pay the king's levy. Similarly, if beit din sold items that do not require announcement and erred by one-sixth, the sale is void; if the error was less than one-sixth, it remains valid. The following require no announcement: slaves, documents, and movable property. They are appraised by beit din and sold immediately. If the market is near the city, the item is taken to the market.
