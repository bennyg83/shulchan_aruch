In siman 109, Beit Yosef questions the Tashbetz, who writes that when property will increase in value automatically without any action, one need not apply strictly the rule that appraisal follows only its present value. Beit Yosef argues from the fact that beit din does not wait for the market day. Perhaps the Tashbetz meant his ruling only for the reason he gives at the beginning of his discussion, namely that an immediate appraisal would resemble a sale made without proper public announcement or at night. There may therefore be no conclusive proof against him. There may also be some support from the rule in Yoreh Deah that villages follow the market price of the nearby large city. Even though merchandise must be transported from the village to the city, that transport may not be treated as a significant change in the property itself. By contrast, where the increased value depends upon an actual act of improvement, the appraisal should ordinarily reflect the present unimproved state. Perhaps the Tashbetz intended his leniency only in appraisal of orphan property, where deficient public announcement presents a special concern, while in ordinary valuation he would agree with Beit Yosef. This requires further study.
