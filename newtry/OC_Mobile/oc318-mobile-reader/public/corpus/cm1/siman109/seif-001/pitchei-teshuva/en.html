We heed the heir. See Be'er HaGolah citing the Shach, who states that he has already concluded below, siman 115, end of seif 1, contrary to the Rosh and in accordance with Ramban, Raavad, and Baal HaTerumot. Shevut Yaakov, volume 3, siman 155, agrees with the Shach and calls his ruling correct. Panim Meirot, volume 2, siman 40, analyzes the issue at length and concludes that it is a doubt of law. Thus, as against an ordinary creditor, the heirs are certainly considered in possession, and even as between a widow and heirs the orphans are the possessors. Noda BiYehudah, siman 9, discusses a widow whose husband left only one-half of a house, worth less than her ketubah. The heirs wanted beit din to appraise the half-house through experts, pay her its appraised value, and retain their father's inheritance. The widow answered that to her the half-house was worth the full amount of her ketubah: either they should pay her the ketubah without deduction or she would take the half-house itself. Noda BiYehudah suggests that even Ramban and those who follow him may agree that, in a ketubah case, she cannot say "it is worth more to me." He links this dispute to the dispute in Shulchan Aruch siman 115, seifim 3 and 4, whether a creditor collects automatic appreciation from heirs and recipients of gifts. A creditor's statement "it is worth more to me" is comparable to automatic appreciation, such as an increase in value after the payment date. Ramban, Raavad, and Baal HaTerumot allow collection of such appreciation, while the Rosh and Razah do not. On this basis Noda BiYehudah suggests that the Rosh and Tur's ruling here, which denies the creditor the ability to say "it is worth more to me," applies specifically against heirs, while against the debtor himself they may agree that he can say so because he collects appreciation from the debtor. This also reconciles the Tur's language in siman 72 without requiring the Shach's distinction between movable and immovable property. In the basic ketubah of one or two hundred, however, all agree that a widow does not collect appreciation, as stated in Even HaEzer siman 100, seif 2, including appreciation caused merely by a rise in market value. Therefore even Ramban's school should agree that she cannot insist that the property is worth more to her, and the heirs may pay its appraised value. Noda BiYehudah concludes that this was written analytically; as a matter of practical law, the heirs can in any event say that they rely on the Rosh and Razah. See Pitchei Teshuva, Even HaEzer siman 100, seif katan 2, Ketzot HaChoshen here and in siman 115, and Netivot HaMishpat siman 114, seif katan 2.
