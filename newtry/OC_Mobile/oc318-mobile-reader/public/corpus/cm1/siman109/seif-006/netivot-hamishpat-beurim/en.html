They sold through an agent. The property necessarily had to be appraised by three experts, for even a widow who sells without appraisal has, according to many authorities, made a void sale. Moreover, it would otherwise be an obvious error similar to beit din selling without public announcement. The case must therefore involve proper appraisal, and nevertheless the sale is void. The reason is that the acquisition must be consummated directly by beit din, not through their agent. Certainly, if an owner tells an agent, Sell at whatever price three experts appraise, or tells him a specific price at which to sell, and an overcharge occurs, the law is that the owner himself was overcharged, not the agent. The owner cannot tell the agent, I sent you to improve matters, not to harm them, because the agent did not deviate at all. Our case would therefore seem comparable. It is strained to say that the authorities discuss only a case in which beit din sent the agent to sell in another place, not the place of appraisal, and the authorities should have specified such a circumstance. Rather, there is another reason: only where the act of acquisition itself is directly effected by beit din do their acts have special validity. When the acquisition is effected through their agent, it does not.
