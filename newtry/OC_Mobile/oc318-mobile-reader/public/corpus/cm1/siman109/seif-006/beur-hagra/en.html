And if it is not... The reason concerning an ordinary agent is the principal's claim, "I sent you to improve my position, not impair it," as stated in Kesubos and Kiddushin. By contrast, with beit din there is no distinction, because just as the orphans rely on the judges, the purchaser relies on them not to act unjustly or show improper favoritism. Beit din thus serves both sides. See siman 227 for a dissenting view based on Rashi, who explains that where beit din's sale is invalid for the orphans it is also invalid for the purchaser. On either rationale, the preceding case can result in cancellation.
And specifically... The Gemara says that just as a widow acts alone, so an ordinary agent acting alone is distinguished from beit din. Here, however, a single judge acting under royal authority functions as a court-appointed judicial officer, and his act is legally equivalent to that of the authority that empowered him. See Bava Kamma 113b. The same applies to his appraisal error.
