What we say, that if beit din erred and undervalued, etc. This concerns a case where beit din placed property in the hands of a creditor. Although the Mishnah, Rambam, and Mechaber use the language of a sale in the law of judicial appraisal, delivery to the creditor after appraisal and announcement is also called a sale. The Rosh and other Geonim, however, understand the Mishnah literally as also covering a sale to an outside purchaser.
In a judicial appraisal, but if beit din sold, etc. The reason is that when beit din gives land to the creditor for his debt, he must accept it at their appraised value, so if they erred by one sixth in either direction the transaction is void. But when beit din sells the land to an outsider, he is not obligated to purchase at their valuation; his purchase is voluntary like an ordinary private sale, and we rule that land has no ona'ah. Thus if the purchaser overpaid, even substantially, the sale may stand. But if beit din erred by selling too cheaply, their sale is void at a one-sixth error. Rambam states this explicitly in chapter 13 of Hilchot Mechirah. Raavad and Rosh hold that where the purchaser erred by more than one sixth the sale is also void, because he relied on beit din's valuation and did not anticipate being overcharged. This is the basis of Rama's dissenting view.
