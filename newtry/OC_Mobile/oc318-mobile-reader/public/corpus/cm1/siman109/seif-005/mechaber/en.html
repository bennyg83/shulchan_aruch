The rule that a sale is void when beit din erred upward or downward by one-sixth applies when they transferred property to a creditor at beit din's appraised value. But if beit din sold land or slaves belonging to orphans that were worth one hundred for two hundred, the purchaser cannot retract. An ordinary person's power should not be stronger than that of the orphans. The same rule applies when a guardian sold land or slaves: the purchaser cannot retract because of the price discrepancy, just as in an ordinary sale. {Rama: Some say that whenever the price was lower or higher by one-sixth, the sale is void.}
