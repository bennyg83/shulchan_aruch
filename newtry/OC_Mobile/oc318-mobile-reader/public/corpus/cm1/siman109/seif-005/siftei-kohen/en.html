This refers to where they placed the creditor in possession. This is the principal explanation. The words, 'But if the beit din sold,' mean even where they sold in order to pay a creditor. This is contrary to what appears from the Bach, who holds that even a sale made to satisfy a creditor has the same status as placing the creditor directly in possession. That is incorrect. According to this, the Bach's question upon the Tur, who cites Rabbenu Yonah as disagreeing with the Rambam, is also resolved. The Rosh writes in Rabbenu Yonah's name that the purchaser relies upon the beit din, meaning even where the sale was made to pay a creditor. Examine this carefully. See also the Maggid Mishneh, chapter 13 of the Laws of Sale, law 11, where the Mechaber's understanding of the Rambam is explicit, contrary to the Bach.
