Give me what your father consumed. Meaning: if his father already received the full amount due him under the arrangement, and the landowner does not wish to leave the field entirely in the sons' hands, but asks them to return whatever their father consumed in excess of the value of his labor, the son's position is stronger. He may say, "Leave the field with me and I will finish the work, since my father already received his agreed share." The second clause is different. There the father had not yet received anything for his share, and the son says, "Leave the field with me, I will finish the work, and then give me my father's entire share." Since the landowner still holds the compensation, the law favors him and he may say, "I have no dealings with you. I will pay only for what your father actually did." This is how the Rosh and the students of Rashba explain these passages. From this you learn that the Tur's and Mechaber's phrase, "rather, they appraise what he did," applies only to the second case. Likewise, the statement in the second clause that the son cannot say, "Give me what my father did," is not literal, for on that point the son is indeed entitled to payment for what the father actually performed. Rather, it means he cannot demand: "Place in my hands the field upon which my father began your work, I will finish it, and then give me the full share due my father."
