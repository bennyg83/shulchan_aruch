Alef) This is the language of the Tur. Rambam likewise rules at the beginning of chapter 17 of Hilkhot Mekhirah, based on the Mishnah in the fifth chapter of Bava Batra 83b concerning the four categories of sellers. The Maggid Mishneh writes that this case involves a complete change in the object itself, even though there is no monetary ona'ah in the transaction.
Bet) There, seif 2, and Rambam at the beginning of chapter 17, based on the Mishnah there.
Gimel) The Maggid Mishneh writes in the name of commentators that even if the price difference between the two qualities is less than one sixth, the deceived seller may rescind because the change in quality resembles a change of object to some degree. The party who was wronged may therefore cancel the sale, but the other party may not, because the object was not entirely different.
Dalet) There at the beginning of 84a, from Rav Chisda's statement.
Heh) The Maggid Mishneh explains the Mishnah: where the buyer deceived the seller by saying, "Your wheat is inferior," and it was found to be among the best quality, the seller who was wronged may retract, but the buyer may not.
Vav) There, seif 3, and in Rambam and the Mishnah there.
Zayin) This is Rambam's language there.
Chet) Rambam brings proof from the beginning of Beitzah 7a concerning eggs of a particular type. The Bach challenges the ruling: how can a person be compelled to accept something that does not suit him, especially where he expressly stipulated that it be a castrated ram? This resembles superior wheat that was found inferior, where the buyer may rescind. He brings additional proof that where a person's intent is to purchase a more significant item, it is unreasonable to say that the seller can satisfy him by supplying a larger quantity of inferior goods or money instead. The Bach concludes that this is the primary practical halachah. See siman 292, seif 17 and the discussion there.
