He sold him meat, etc. As stated in Beitzah chapter 1, 7a: according to the view that this item is intended for eating, etc.; what practical difference is there, etc.
(Collected note) He sold him meat, etc. From Beitzah chapter 1 concerning eggs of a particular type, where the Gemara says, “according to the view that it is for eating, etc.” The reasoning is sound according to Ramban, who compares this to an item sold by count and therefore requires completion. Raah and Ran at the beginning of Kiddushin chapter 2 reject the proof, explaining that in that case the buyer had already placed them for hatching and thereby spoiled them, so return was impossible. Otherwise it would resemble superior produce that turned out inferior. Bach writes likewise. See what was written above in siman 232, seif 1. (End of note.)
Unless it is known, etc. Although for most people the two are equal for eating, the buyer’s particular purpose controls when it is known, as in Bava Batra chapter 2: “for me, because I am delicate, etc.”
He sold him silver, etc. This requires study. Why should this be better than a case where superior goods were sold and found to be inferior? Avi'asaf explains the Mishnah concerning superior goods, etc., under the law of ona'ah and according to Rabbi Natan, who gives the disadvantaged party the choice, as the Mishnah in Bava Metzia chapter 4 follows his view. This is also suggested by the Gemara’s statement, “Rav Chisda said: one who sold, etc.; and our tanna taught likewise, etc.” Rashbam, however, writes that the Mishnah applies even according to our accepted law. Certainly this is so according to the Rambam’s explanation, under which the rule applies even when there was no monetary overcharge.
