For all of it is a type of silver. This requires study. Why should it differ from the case where one sold superior produce and inferior produce was found? Mordechai writes that if one sold impure silver as though it were refined, the parties cannot retract, but if he sold gold and it was found to be silver, or the reverse, both may retract. Perhaps his meaning is not that both may retract, but that the buyer may retract, and the text may perhaps be emended to read that the seller cannot retract. The language of the Beit Yosef supports this. Raavan likewise writes that if one sold silver as impure and it was found refined, the seller may retract, whereas if it was sold as refined and was found impure, the buyer may retract, like the Mishnah's rule of superior produce found inferior. I later found in Mordechai, at the end of Perek HaMafkid, a responsum of Maharam that where one sold a gold ring and lead was found inside it, this is a mistaken transaction, like wheat found to be barley or wine found to be vinegar. The Rama rules this briefly in siman 232, seif 18. Responsa Rashdam likewise writes that the view of Aviassaf is a lone opinion and challenges it from the rule of superior and inferior produce.
