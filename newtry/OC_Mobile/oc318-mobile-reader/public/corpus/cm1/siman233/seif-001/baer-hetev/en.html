Silver. This requires study. Why is this different from a sale described as superior goods that were found to be inferior? The Mordechai writes: If one sold impure silver as refined silver, he cannot retract; but if he sold gold and it was found to be silver, or the reverse, both parties may retract. One may explain that his meaning is that both parties cannot retract, but only the buyer. Indeed, it is close to certain that the text should read, "the seller cannot retract." This is also the version in Raavan, siman 105: If one sold silver as impure and it was found refined, the seller may retract; if he sold refined silver and it was found impure, the buyer may retract, as we learned regarding superior goods found to be inferior. I later found in the Mordechai at the end of the chapter HaMafkid a responsum of Maharam concerning one who sold a gold ring that was found to contain lead, ruling that it is a mistaken purchase like reddish wheat found to be white, or wine found to be vinegar. The Rama writes this briefly in siman 232, seif 18. Responsa Rashdam, end of siman 385, likewise writes that the view of Aviassaf is a lone opinion and challenges it from the rule of superior goods found to be inferior. Shach. In my humble opinion, however, one can distinguish between a ring or other utensil that was purchased and the present case, which concerns a bar of silver that can be melted down and refined, with only the expense and labor of refining borne by the seller. This is also indicated by Rashdam, who concludes that Aviassaf's statement must refer specifically to impure silver, because a larger amount includes the smaller. For example, ten drachmas of impure silver may contain eight of refined silver. Thus the quantity of refined silver that was sold is actually present, provided there is no ona'ah in the price. This is unlike other substances, such as paper and similar materials. His words explicitly support the distinction stated here.
