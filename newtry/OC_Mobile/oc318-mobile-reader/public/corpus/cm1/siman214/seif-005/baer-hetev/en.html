The responsum there explains more fully that even though he expressly wrote to Shimon that he transfers the depth and height of the house that he sold to Reuven, Shimon nevertheless acquires nothing. This is unlike one who sells a palm tree for its fruit, because there it is impossible to reserve the fruit unless one reserves the underlying substance for it. The airspace of a house, by contrast, remains reserved automatically. Thus the seller has no share in the body of the house, and how can he transfer its airspace through the substance belonging to another? From this we learn that even if the airspace remains with the seller and he has the right to build there upon columns, he still cannot sell that right to another, because he is transferring only airspace, which is not independently subject to acquisition.
