Some say that when one sells a courtyard, the purchaser acquires all its airspace even if the deed did not state "height," because there is no defined place above it. Others disagree. Rama: As explained immediately below, the first view appears to me the proper ruling. If one sold Reuven a house and did not write "depth and height," so that the airspace above was not included, and later sold all his houses to Shimon, Shimon does not acquire the airspace above the house sold to Reuven even if the deed to Shimon says, "from the depth of the earth to the height of the sky." Once the house itself had already been sold to Reuven, the seller retained only intangible airspace, which cannot itself be transferred, as explained in siman 212. If, however, there is a roof on the house sold to Reuven and that roof was included in the later sale to Shimon, since the roof is tangible, the airspace above it is likewise included. Similarly, if the house stands in the seller's courtyard and he sells the courtyard together with everything in it, the airspace is sold with the courtyard and Shimon, as purchaser of the courtyard, acquires it. Beit Yosef in the name of a responsum of Rashba.
