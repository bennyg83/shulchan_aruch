He sold all permanently attached items. Rashbam there, s.v. “he sold, etc.,” and as explained below.
And the fixed mortar. Following the anonymous Mishnah. Although the first Tanna of the baraisa disagrees, the halachah follows an anonymous Mishnah unless the Gemara explicitly identifies it as an isolated opinion whose ruling is rejected.
And the oven. According to the reading in the Mishnah order, Behag, Rav Hai Gaon, Rif, and other poskim, unlike the baraisa.
(Collected note) And the oven, etc. This follows the Mishnah. It may be that the Mishnah does not disagree with the baraisa, but follows Rabbi Eliezer regarding the fixed mortar. Rabbi Eliezer likewise disagrees concerning oven, stove, and mill, saying that every attached item is included. This explains why the ruling regarding the mill distinguishes whether it is fixed. (End of note.)
(Collected note) And the frames, etc. Rashbam explains likewise, unlike Tosafos, who applies the passage to a guardhouse. (End of note.)
(Collected note) Attached with mortar. The Gemara there. See the gloss and Beer HaGolah seif katan 100. (End of note.)
But not, etc. The baraisa lists these examples, and the same principle applies to the others.
(Collected note) But not the roof, etc. See Rashbam, s.v. “he did not sell, etc.” The same applies to the roof and tunnels. (End of note.)
Some say, etc. This is a textual error and should read the reverse.
(Collected note) Some say that if, etc. This requires study. According to the dissenting view in seif 2, writing “and I reserved nothing” even includes a roof and gallery. According to the first view there, the Rosh holds that anything not included in the ordinary wording is not added by that clause, so even items physically inside would remain excluded. (End of note.)
