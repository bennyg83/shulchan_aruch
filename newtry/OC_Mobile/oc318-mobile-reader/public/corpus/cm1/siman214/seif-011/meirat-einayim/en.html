A bolt. Meaning, the bar fixed in the wall.
The itztrobul. This is the circular structure surrounding the millstone, and it too is fixed in place.
The doorframes. These are like strips or projections surrounding the doorposts of the house, against which the door closes.
That are attached with mortar. The Gemara raises an unresolved question concerning frames attached by pegs: do they become subordinate to the doorway and therefore sell with the house, or not? It states there that if they are attached with mortar there is no question; clearly they are considered attached. For this reason all the poskim rule that an item attached with mortar is certainly sold with the house, while one attached by pegs is not, because the monetary doubt is resolved in favor of the possessor: the burden of proof rests upon the claimant, and the seller is the defendant. Rama writes in the gloss, "Some say that attachment by pegs is not considered attachment." I do not know who disagrees with the Mechaber on this. It is difficult to say that Rama is responding to the Gemara's subsequent question, where Rav Zeira asks concerning window frames. Rashbam explains that this question is presented according to the assumption that doorframes attached by pegs are considered attached and sold with the house: what is the law of window frames attached by pegs, since those are installed merely for decoration? According to Rashbam, the later question presupposes that peg attachment of doorframes counts as attachment, and perhaps Rama therefore writes that some say peg attachment is not attachment, meaning some do not follow Rashbam's explanation but instead understand, like the Maggid Mishneh, that the question concerning window frames refers to ones attached with mortar. According to that interpretation the Gemara asks: even if doorframes attached with mortar are included, what about window frames used only for decoration? Yet this reading of Rama is strained. Since the Mechaber himself writes specifically "attached with mortar," he plainly holds that peg attachment is insufficient. Rama's statement, "Some say peg attachment is not attachment," then teaches nothing. He should instead have written the opposite, that some say even peg attachment counts. Moreover, it is difficult to treat the later "if you say" question as actually resolving the first question after the Gemara had already closed it with teiku. The Gemara would not ordinarily leave the first question formally unresolved if the later discussion had resolved it. Therefore I suggest deleting the words "some say." Rama may merely have intended to explain the Mechaber's phrase "attached with mortar," lest one mistakenly think that attachment with pegs is even stronger and therefore certainly included. Rama clarifies that this is not so: only mortar attachment qualifies, while pegs do not. It is possible that the textual error arose because this gloss belongs to seif 11 and an original marginal notation "11" was mistaken by a copyist for the abbreviation "some say," who then expanded it. This is clear. Alternatively, as I have noted elsewhere, Rama sometimes uses the phrase "some say" even where there is no true dispute, in which case there is no difficulty. Nor should one object that the Mechaber rules simply that window frames are not sold even when attached with mortar, whereas according to Rashbam they would be sold. One can say the Mechaber follows the Maggid Mishneh's explanation. See the rulings of Rif and Rosh, who do not cite this question concerning the frames, and see the Derishah for the reason.
Nor the supports beneath the legs of the bed. Meaning, their practice was to place a piece of wood under the legs of a bed so that the legs would not rot in the ground. This too is an unresolved question in the Gemara and therefore is resolved leniently for the defendant. Even if these supports are attached to the ground with mortar or pegs, since they relate only to the bed and not to the house, they are not sold with the house. This is unlike the Levush, who writes that the case concerns supports not attached to the ground. That is not precise. According to the Maggid Mishneh's explanation, the Gemara's phrase "where they do not move" means they are attached to the ground, and the question remains unresolved, proving that even attached supports are not sold. Even according to Rashbam, who explains that they do not move together with the bed, so the Gemara does not explicitly say they are attached to the ground, it appears he would agree that even if they are attached they are not included, because they have no relationship to the house. This is also implied by the Tur, who wonders why Rambam needed to mention them and does not resolve that Rambam meant attached supports. Examine this carefully.
As though he wrote, "and I have retained nothing," etc. See seif 2. There Rama writes that some disagree and hold that if the seller wrote, "I have retained nothing," he acquires the cistern, reservoir, and the other items. According to the first opinion there, which is the Tur's view, even writing "I have retained nothing" does not transfer them. All the more so here, merely writing "and everything inside it" is insufficient, as the Mechaber rules here. See what I wrote at the end of siman 215, seif 1.
