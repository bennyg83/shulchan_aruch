Even if, etc. From the Gemara’s statement that it was necessary because most people, etc., according to the Rif and Rambam’s reading, reflected in the phrase “although there is a minority.” The Gemara does not say “some call it this and some call it that,” because even in that situation broad boundaries certainly indicate expanded intent. Rather, the novelty is that even where most use the narrow term, the broad clause “and I reserved nothing” can include more. This implies that where not even a minority uses the broader name, the clause does not help. The same applies to pits and cisterns, which are not called part of a house, as explained above in seif 2.
If everyone, etc. As stated there regarding the large complex, according to Tosafos in the name of Rivam. The Shulchan Aruch follows Ri’s explanation. See Tosafos there.
(Collected note) If everyone, etc. If everyone also calls an ordinary house “house,” then where he marked the exterior boundaries the entire complex is sold. In that case the broad “and I reserved nothing” clause is unnecessary, according to Ri. (End of note.)
One who gave, etc. Although another passage treats a half share differently because the whole is called “land,” here half the complex is not called “house,” as the Gemara itself frames the case.
(Collected note) One who gave, etc. See what was written there. This is the meaning of following ordinary human speech, and see below siman 218. (End of note.)
