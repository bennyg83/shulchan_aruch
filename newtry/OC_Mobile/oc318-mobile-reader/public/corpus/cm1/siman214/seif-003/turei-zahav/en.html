(Seif 6: he rebuilds, etc.) This is Ramban's position cited by our master. At first glance it appears that the rule results from the redundant phrase, "the upper story is mine," as implied at the beginning of the discussion. But this is difficult, because at the end Ramban writes that the seller has the right to rebuild if it falls, even though there is no redundant phrase. It appears to me that the rule is true even without redundancy. Ramban's language in our master's text says, "Certainly the purchaser," etc., indicating that this follows from logic. The Gemara's need for the redundant language, "on condition," in the case of collapse is understood because Ramban follows Rabbeinu Tam's interpretation, cited by him, that "upper story" means the top row of masonry. The statement, "if it falls, he rebuilds," then means that if the house itself collapses, the purchaser must rebuild it because of that retained upper row so that it can continue to exist. The Beit Yosef explicitly writes that Ramban follows Rabbeinu Tam on this point.
