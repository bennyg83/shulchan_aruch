The inner room. People commonly built an inner chamber behind the house in which to store money and utensils. It was not used for ordinary residence like the main house or side structure. Therefore some say that even if it is less than four cubits and lies within the external boundaries, it is not included in the sale of the house. A side structure differs, for everyone agrees that if it is less than four cubits and lies within the boundaries, it is sold with the house, as stated in seif 1. The Rosh and Tur, however, hold that the inner room and side structure have the same law. They are the "some say" cited by Rama in seifim 1 and 2.
And not the roof. Even if it is uncovered and has no parapet, according to Nimukei Yosef.
The cistern and the reservoir. Both are excavations in the ground in which water used by the residents of the house and courtyard is collected. A cistern is dug in hard ground that does not absorb its water. A reservoir is dug in softer ground that will not hold water unless a masonry wall is built around the interior of the excavation. This is from the Gemara and Rashbam. A cistern and reservoir are not sold with the house even if they are less than four cubits wide. It appears that for this reason Rama carefully writes at the end of the next seif, "but anything less than four cubits wide, etc., the roof is acquired." He does not mention the cistern and reservoir. This is unlike the Levush, who writes, "he acquires the roof and all these," treating them alike. That is not precise.
Even though he marked for him the outer boundaries and also wrote, "I have retained nothing," etc. It appears that this entire gloss is one unit and belongs where the second gloss appears. The Mechaber did not write "even though he marked the outer boundaries," relying on his statement at the beginning of the seif concerning the inner room. Rather, he wrote, "even though he sold him the depth and height," which also refers to the roof mentioned before it. Regarding depth, the novelty is that one might have thought the cistern and reservoir dug into the ground are included in "depth." Regarding height, one might have thought the roof is included. Rama wished to add that even if the seller also wrote, "I have retained nothing in this sale," which relates to marking the boundaries, and therefore he also repeated at the beginning of the gloss, "even though he marked the boundaries and wrote, 'I have retained nothing,'" etc. This is clear.
And he also wrote, "I have retained nothing," etc. See the Derishah to siman 215, where I wrote that if he marks the boundaries and writes, "Acquire everything within them," everyone agrees that everything within the boundaries is sold. This language is stronger than merely writing, "I have retained nothing in this sale." See there.
He must purchase a right of passage, etc. Because a seller is presumed to sell generously. In the Perishah I wrote that this applies specifically in a case like this, where the cistern and reservoir remain with the seller automatically without any express reservation, and the reservation can continue to exist even if the seller must buy a right of passage from the purchaser. But if one sells his palm tree and reserves its fruit for himself, since he revealed his intention to retain the fruit and the reservation cannot exist unless he also retains the place from which the fruit grows, we say that he generously retained for himself everything needed for that reservation, as the Tur and Mechaber write in siman 209. See there.
He need not purchase a right of passage. Since there was no need for him expressly to reserve the cistern and reservoir, we say that the reason he did expressly reserve them was to leave them in his possession as before, including the existing route of entry and exit.
Likewise, one who sells a cistern and reservoir. The word "likewise" teaches that here too we say he sold generously and included a right of passage. This is not comparable to one who sells another the future fruit of his palm tree, where we do not say he sold generously together with the place from which the fruit grows, as the Tur and Mechaber write above in siman 209. There, what he purported to sell is not yet in existence and no acquisition attaches to it, so the principle of generous sale is inapplicable.
