Seif 12. One who tells another, "I am selling you one house from among my houses," etc. Note: See my responsum to Tarnopol, fourth edition, siman 148, regarding one who stipulated that he would provide the best, or at least an intermediate-quality item rather than the worst, and whether he may nevertheless provide an inferior one. I was asked from Bolkomin concerning a man from Tarnopol who, before Purim, purchased from a resident of our community several bundles of thread, to be collected after Pesach. He paid thirty silver reinsh in advance. After Pesach he said that he had heard stones were mixed among the merchandise and refused to take it. The seller replied that he would give him merchandise without stones and would sort out the agreed quantity so that it contained none. The next day the purchaser came to take the goods and asked the seller to sort them. The seller said he had no time because sorting would take a while and he needed to travel that night to a fair elsewhere. The purchaser likewise said he would travel and return in two days. On Monday night a fire broke out in our community and the merchandise was burned. They came to litigation over the thirty silver reinsh already paid. His Honor did not explain the mode of acquisition. If there was only payment of money, with no other kinyan and no established merchant act of acquisition known as handslag, the loss plainly falls upon the seller, as stated in Choshen Mishpat siman 214, seif 2. But if there was a valid kinyan or a recognized merchant custom of acquisition, the loss falls upon the purchaser. This is not comparable to the cases His Honor cited. Where the purchaser has the right to choose which specific items he will take, the seller bears the risk until selection. But where selection lies with the seller, and had the seller presented items free of stones the purchaser would have been compelled to accept them, the risk falls upon the purchaser, as stated in siman 214. The seller need only make a formal rabbinic oath that the agreed quantity without stones was present; he then need not return the money. This is the brief answer, as time does not permit elaboration.
