Specifically a room that is four cubits wide. See Responsa Shevut Yaakov III, siman 180, concerning one who rented a room to another and within it there was an additional small room measuring less than four cubits. The question was whether the small room was included in the lease. He initially considers distinguishing between a sale and a lease, but Rambam, Hilchot Sechirut 6, as cited by Beit Yosef at the beginning of siman 313, indicates that the interpretive rules of leasing and selling are generally the same in such matters, as is also implied by the Tur in siman 316. He therefore suggests a different distinction. The word "house" naturally includes the various functional areas of a house, even if there are several. The ordinary expression "room," however, denotes a particular defined room and includes only what is specifically within that designation. Since the lessee bears the burden where the wording is doubtful, he must bring proof that the smaller inner room was included, as explained expressly in siman 312.
