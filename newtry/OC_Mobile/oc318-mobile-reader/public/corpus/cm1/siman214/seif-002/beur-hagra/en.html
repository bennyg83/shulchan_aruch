And it has a width of four cubits. The Yerushalmi there requires four by four with a height of ten. Rav Hai Gaon reads the Bavli similarly: “but not the roof, etc.; Mar Zutra said, provided it has four cubits,” referring to a gallery or roof measuring four cubits.

When does this apply, etc. As stated on 64b: a person does not wish another to take money, etc. Rabbi Akiva follows the buyer’s presumed intent. Although the Gemara ultimately broadens the principle beyond this precise case, it still follows that the discussion assumes there is no alternative route.
