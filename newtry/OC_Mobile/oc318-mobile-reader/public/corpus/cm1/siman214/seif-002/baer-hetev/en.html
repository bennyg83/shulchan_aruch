The house. See Responsa Maharashdam, siman 276; Responsa Mabit, part 1, simanim 46 and 153, and part 2, siman 74; Responsa Raanach, siman 88; and Responsa R. Levi ibn Chaviv, siman 126.
The roof. Even if it is not roofed or enclosed. Nimukei Yosef. Sema.
The boundaries. If he specified boundaries and wrote, 'Acquire everything within them,' all agree that everything within them is sold, because this language is stronger than writing, 'and I have retained nothing.' Ibid.
A path. A seller is presumed to sell generously. This applies specifically in a case like this, where the pit and cistern remain the seller's automatically without an express reservation, and his reservation can be preserved by purchasing a path from the buyer. But where one sells a palm tree and reserves its fruit for himself, since he expressly revealed an intent to retain something and the fruit cannot exist without the place from which it grows on the palm, we say that he generously retained for himself everything necessary for that reservation, as stated in siman 209. Ibid.
Except. Since he did not need expressly to reserve the pit and cistern, we say that he added the condition so that they would remain his together with a path by which he could enter and leave. Ibid.
Likewise. Meaning, here too we say that he sold generously together with the path. This is unlike one who sells the future fruit of his palm in siman 209, where we do not say that he generously sold the place from which the fruit emerges. There, what he purported to sell cannot itself be acquired because it does not yet exist, so the principle of generous sale does not apply. Ibid.
