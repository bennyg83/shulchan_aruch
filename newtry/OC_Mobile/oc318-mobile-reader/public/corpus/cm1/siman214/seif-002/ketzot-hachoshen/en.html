One who sells a house. Rosh was asked about Reuven whose house collapsed, leaving stones and beams on the premises. He did not rebuild it. Before his death he gave half the house to his two daughters, half to his wife, and separately gave his remaining movables to his wife. The daughters claimed the fallen stones and beams as part of the house; the wife claimed them as movables. Rosh ruled for the daughters. Since the beams and stones remained on the property and Reuven had not tried to sell them, we presume he intended to use them to rebuild if he had the means. They therefore remained functionally attached to the house. Rosh compares this to the rule that one who sells a field includes stones needed for the field and vineyard stakes needed for the vineyard. These fallen materials were designated to rebuild the body of the house itself. Since the case involved a gift, the conclusion is even stronger because a donor gives generously. But the comparison shows that the underlying rule applies even in a sale where the materials remain designated for the structure.
