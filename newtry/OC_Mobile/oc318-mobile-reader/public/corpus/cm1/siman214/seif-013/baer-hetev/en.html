To build it. This gloss belongs at the beginning of the seif, after the Mechaber's words, 'one who sells a place to another.' Rama then adds, 'or one who undertakes,' meaning that one should not think that only regarding a bridal house did the Mechaber write 'one who undertakes,' but not in the first clause. Rama teaches that the two are the same. Rambam's reason for using this language specifically regarding a bridal house appears to be that it is common to build a special room for one's son, where the language of sale is not appropriate. Sema's explanation is strained. Taz.
Its width. Ran writes that all of this applies only when one undertakes, or sells, to build one of these types of houses for him from the outset. But if he says, 'I sell you a house,' he may give him a house measuring four cubits by four cubits. Beit Yosef and Darkei Moshe cite this. I do not know why they omitted it here in Shulchan Aruch. Sema.
