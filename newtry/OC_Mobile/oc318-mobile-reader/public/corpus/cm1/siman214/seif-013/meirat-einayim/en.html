One who sells a place to his fellow, etc. And likewise one who undertakes to build, etc. The Mechaber begins with one who sells a place for an unspecified house and then says, “and likewise one who accepts upon himself to make a wedding house,” following the Mishnah's wording as it appears in the Rif and Maggid Mishneh. The Tur's arrangement is clearer: one who sells a place to his fellow to build an ordinary house, a cattle shed, a wedding house for his son, or a widow's house for his daughter, or one who undertakes to build one of these, makes it four by six, etc. Thus the dimensions apply whether the seller merely sells land upon which the buyer will build, or undertakes himself to build one of these structures on his own land and at his own expense. Rama's addition, “or he undertook to make it,” appears to explain the Mechaber's phrase “one who accepts from his fellow” as one who receives payment from his fellow to build it, and Rama adds the further case where he gratuitously undertakes to build it. All this applies only where he sells or undertakes to provide one of these structures from the outset. If he merely says, “I sell you a house,” he may give him a house four by four. So writes the Ran, cited by the Beit Yosef and Darkei Moshe. I do not know why they omitted it from Shulchan Aruch.
