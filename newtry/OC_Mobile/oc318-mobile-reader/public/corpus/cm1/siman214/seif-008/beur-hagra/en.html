(Collected note) If he wishes, etc., and if, etc. The Rif explains that if he wishes to build an upper story upon it, he may, and if it falls he may rebuild it. The halachah follows Rabbi Zeira, who even permits extending projections from it. Certainly he may build above it. The Gemara later proceeds according to Rabbi Zeira, and all the more so according to Rav Pappa, unlike Rashbam, who understands Rabbi Zeira as disagreeing with Rav Pappa. The Rif also treats rebuilding after collapse as a distinct question. These are the Rambam’s words here. (End of note.)
If the projection fell, etc. According to Rashbam as above, and Rabbeinu Meir rules similarly. This conflicts with the ruling in seif 6, and Ramban writes that he does not agree with Rabbeinu Meir.
If he wishes, etc., as it was previously. Meaning that if an upper story had previously been built over the projection and the projection fell, he may rebuild both the projection and the upper story. This applies only where it existed previously, for otherwise he may not add new construction, as shown by the rule that an upper owner may not change, etc. The statement that the vertical airspace is his means only above pillars, as explained. The Gemara’s phrase that the condition helps “if it falls, etc.” refers, on this interpretation, to the structure above the projection, not to the projection itself. This follows the Geonic reading: if he wishes to build on top, he may, with no separate mention of an upper story.
