The walls. Meaning, in the courtyard he may not build higher than the courtyard walls. See the beginning of the next siman, where dissenting views are recorded. Ibid.
The walls. Because additional construction burdens them and may cause them to deteriorate sooner. There is another practical consequence for the buyer if the seller built above them and later sold that structure to another person and it fell, as explained in seif 6. Ibid.
From outside. Meaning, in his own courtyard adjacent to the house he sold, he may dig down even beneath the sold house. Ibid.
So that. Meaning, this 'some say' opinion holds that even if the person digging thinks no damage will occur, he may nevertheless not dig because of the remote concern that damage might result. If he refrains from digging, the buyer will certainly be protected from that harm. This is the meaning of 'so that he not cause damage.' The words, 'If so, what benefit...' mean that according to this opinion it is difficult to understand what the seller gains by not having written to the buyer 'depth and height,' thereby reserving them for himself. The Tur discusses this there.
And carry out. Meaning, the opening must be higher, because if he needs to enter there with a bundle only on rare occasions, one can say that he can constrict himself and enter. Ibid.
The depth. Nimukei Yosef writes in the name of the Ramah that with respect to a field or vineyard, even the depth is transferred automatically, because the purchaser needs the earth down to the depths for the roots of seeds and trees. Ibid.
Everything. This excludes the opinion that both 'depth' and 'height' must be expressly written, and only from the redundancy of both expressions do we infer that all these rights were transferred. Nevertheless, the Sages instituted that sometimes one should write 'depth and height,' for example where he sells only the airspace or only the right to dig beneath the property and does not wish to transfer structures that are built there and are not included in an ordinary sale of a house. Ibid.
That he will lose. This implies specifically where otherwise his house as a whole would suffer loss and there is no alternative. But if he can build a latrine elsewhere, even at additional expense, we do not take that expense into account. It is comparable to the path that a seller must purchase for access to a pit and cistern, as stated in seif 2. Taz. See Responsa Raanach, siman 40, folio 71.
