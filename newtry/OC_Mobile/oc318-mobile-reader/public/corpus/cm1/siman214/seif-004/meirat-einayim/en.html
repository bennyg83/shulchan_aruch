He acquired only up to the tops of the courtyard walls. Meaning, in the courtyard he may not build higher than the walls of the courtyard. See the beginning of the following seif, where dissenting opinions are cited.
The depth and height are reserved. Meaning, reserved for the seller.
But he cannot build upon the walls. The reason is that he would burden them and they would deteriorate more quickly from the added weight. There is another practical consequence for the purchaser if the seller did build upon them, later sold that structure to someone else, and it then fell, as the Tur and Mechaber explain below in seif 6.
To excavate from outside. Meaning, in his own courtyard adjoining the house he sold, extending the excavation under the sold house.
So that he not cause damage. Meaning, this opinion holds that even if the person excavating believes he will cause no damage, he still may not dig because of the remote concern that damage may result. If he does not excavate, the purchaser is certainly protected from that danger. This is the meaning of "so that he not cause damage."
If so, what benefit is there, etc. Meaning, according to this opinion it is difficult: what does the seller gain from reserving the depth? In other words, what practical benefit comes from not writing to the purchaser that the depth and height are included, thereby reserving them for himself? The Tur phrases it this way.
And bring in and take out, etc. Meaning, in that case the space must be higher, because if he carries a bundle there only occasionally one can say he compresses himself and enters even through a lower opening. See the Perishah.
But he did not acquire the depth, etc. Beit Yosef writes in the name of Ramah that with a field or vineyard the purchaser acquires the depth even without specification, because he needs the soil's depth for the nourishment of the seeds and trees down toward the groundwater.
Even though he did not write "depth and height." This excludes the opinion that both expressions must be written and that only through the superfluous language do we infer transfer of all these additional rights. Nevertheless, the Sages established a practice of writing "depth and height" in some cases, such as where he sells only the airspace or the right to excavate below, and does not wish to transfer existing structures that are not included in the ordinary meaning of "house," as explained above.
