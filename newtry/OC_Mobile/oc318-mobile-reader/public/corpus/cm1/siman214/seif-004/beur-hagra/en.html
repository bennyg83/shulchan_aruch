If not, etc. This entire discussion is from Maggid Mishneh there.
He has no, etc. The Gemara there.
If he comes, etc. Rashbam explains this, s.v. “this, etc.,” though Rashbam understands it as purchasing the roof. Tosafos, Rabbeinu Meir, and other commentators disagree, as reflected in the gloss. Rabbeinu Meir and the commentators maintain that actual construction on top is impossible, since even altering an existing structure is restricted at the end of Bava Metzia, certainly constructing anew. Tosafos says likewise.
Likewise if he comes, etc. Rashbam explains this too, and on his view the depth belongs to the seller.
Some say he may not, etc. Rabbeinu Meir writes this, based on the rule that the lower owner may not change, etc. Certainly he may not excavate beneath. The clause therefore gives only the limited benefit stated.
(Collected note) Some say he may not, etc. See there, 60a: “One may not make, etc.; the Sages say sometimes, etc.” (End of note.)
There is an opinion, etc. Yerushalmi, Bava Batra chapter 3, halachah 4: a ruin is ordinarily intended to be rebuilt, whereas gardens are not ordinarily intended for building. See there.
For they are, etc. The Yerushalmi there explains that gardens are different because they were given for digging. Rabbi Nasa adds concerning ruins, etc.
And when he wrote for him, etc., and if, etc. The Gemara and Yerushalmi there ask why depth and height are written. The answer is that if he wishes to lower, he may lower, and if he wishes to raise, he may raise, meaning excavate below and build above. See Ramban and Rashbam. Rashbam needed to explain only excavation explicitly, because according to his view the vertical airspace is already included.
Although, etc. This appears from the Gemara’s wording “even though, etc.” The Gemara does not require both expressions, indicating that their presence or absence does not change the basic result.
(Collected note) If he wrote “from the ground, etc.” Rosh there and other poskim. See what was written there. (End of note.)
Reuven, etc. This applies specifically where the loss affects the house as a whole. Otherwise it is analogous to an ordinary right of way.
