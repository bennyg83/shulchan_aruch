(Seif 13, in the gloss: or he accepted upon himself to build it.) This gloss belongs at the beginning of the seif, immediately after the Mechaber says, "one who sells a place to his fellow." The Rama means to add, "or one who accepted upon himself to build it." The point is that one should not think that only regarding a bridal chamber does the Mechaber say, "one who accepts from his fellow to build," while in the opening clause a sale is specifically required. The ruling teaches that the two are equivalent. Rambam likely speaks of a bridal chamber because it is common for a person to build a separate room for his son, where the language of sale is not natural. The Sema's explanation is strained.
