He did not sell the room. See Responsa Beit Ephraim, Choshen Mishpat, siman 32, concerning a deathbed instruction to give a house "with all its rooms" to a named person. Two rooms in the building were separately rented to flour merchants, and the question was whether they were included. Beit Ephraim explains that a gift is interpreted more generously than a sale. The Mishna teaches that distinctions excluding subsidiary areas apply to a sale, whereas one who gives a gift gives all of it. Thus, even without specifying the outer boundaries, a donor is presumed to give generously. The express addition "with all the rooms" is therefore surplus language that comes to include even rooms whose use is not identical with that of the main house. This is especially so because the word "rooms" itself can naturally denote spaces used differently from the principal dwelling, and the word "all" broadens the inclusion further. He notes, however, that if the spaces rented to flour merchants functioned as shops, they might be classified as stores rather than rooms, and stores situated in a courtyard are not necessarily included unless their principal use is inward toward the house. From the facts, though, it appeared that the flour merchants did not conduct retail sales there but used the spaces merely to store flour, making them more naturally "rooms." In matters of this kind one follows local terminology. Rambam, Hilchot Mechirah 26, rules that all these technical presumptions apply only where there is no established usage. If local speech uses "house" to include the surrounding structures and upper areas, local usage governs. In contemporary usage, Beit Ephraim observes, "house" commonly includes the buildings and rooms standing on the same parcel within its boundaries. One must therefore determine the exact language used by the testator, especially if he spoke in the vernacular and the witnesses translated it into Hebrew. The witnesses may testify to the language he actually used, provided their testimony does not contradict the written deed in a substantial way. See there further and below, siman 215:6, seif katan 1.
