The Rashba’s responsum, cited in the Beit Yosef before chiddush 2, states: “When one sells or gives, so long as he did not specify his sale or gift, we do not include in his transfer more than he transferred. But where the document relates an admission concerning what he already transferred, he need only relate and admit it in general terms. If he writes and admits, recounting what occurred together with what he now wishes to add, and says, ‘I sold a house to so-and-so, whose boundaries are such and such,’ and now further strengthens the recipient’s right in a certain respect, he has admitted everything within those boundaries. This is not the moment of transfer, such that we would say, ‘Since he should have said, I did not retain anything,’ and so forth.” The Sema writes: “From all this it appears that the rule applies only to an admission made as a mere narrative, but if he intended,” and so forth. “The Rama also agrees, but he placed a stumbling block before those who study his words.” It appears to me that the Rama’s words are clear. What the Sema called a great stumbling block is in truth plainly correct according to the Rama. The Sema’s principal proof is from the Rashba’s words, “recounting what occurred.” Why did he not infer from the beginning of the responsum, where the Rashba writes, “but where the document relates an admission”? The phrase “he need only relate and admit” likewise shows that an admission is nevertheless required, and not a mere recital of events. There is a further difficulty: the Rashba’s proofs clearly indicate this. One proof is the case in Gittin, page 40, where one said, “I made my slave a free person.” Rabbi Yochanan states that all such declarations must be in a document, and Rashi explains that he wrote this wording in a document and delivered it to the slave. Thus, he delivered it as proof. The same applies in the case under discussion, which the Rashba derives from there. The transferor now admits that he already transferred the property, and through this admission the recipient presently acquires the house. It therefore appears to me that this is the essential distinction: whether he is transferring the property now, or admitting that he already transferred it and now gives him proof of that admission. In the latter case he need not state all the details; it is enough to mention the matter generally, as it was done. This is explicit in siman 60 in the Tur’s words in the name of Raavad: “One who says, ‘I gave such-and-such to so-and-so,’ has made an admission, not a transfer. Once a person admits that he gave it, we do not investigate further.” Our case is precisely the same. When the Rashba writes, “together with what he now adds,” he merely describes the ordinary case, since it is unusual to write an admission in the past tense at the time of an acquisition unless he now wishes to add some further matter. The Rama’s words are straightforward and contain no stumbling block.
