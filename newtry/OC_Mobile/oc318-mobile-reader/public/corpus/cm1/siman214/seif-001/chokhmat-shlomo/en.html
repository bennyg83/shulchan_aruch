Seif 1. I am greatly troubled why the Tur and Mechaber nowhere record the rule stated in Pesachim, chapter Makom Shenahagu, where the Gemara asks what practical difference follows and answers that it matters for buying and selling. Why do the authorities not codify that if one sells a mountain or a valley, the description may imply that it contains date palms, according to the details of that passage? This requires study. Regarding one who sells wax to another, must he provide clean wax, or may he provide the residue of honey? Tosafot at the beginning of the second chapter of Shabbat, s.v. "what practical difference," indicates that he need not provide fully refined wax, and honey residue is sufficient. In truth, however, we rule that written instruments are interpreted like vows, and vows follow ordinary human usage. In common speech, "wax" certainly refers only to clean wax. A practical difference remains where the parties speak a vernacular language but the contract was formulated in the sacred language. In vows, when one uses the sacred language we follow Scriptural usage, and the same should apply to documents. If in Scriptural usage the term includes unrefined wax, then in such a document he would not need to provide refined wax. The practical ruling still requires study.
