Into it. Meaning, it has no other entrance and one enters it only from the house. Even so, we do not say that it is subordinate to the house so as to be included automatically in the sale. Sema.
The outer boundaries. Meaning, where the seller wrote the boundaries of the house on all four sides, as is customary, and an annex was built, for example, along the southern wall of the house. If he did not write that the southern boundary of the house is the annex, but instead wrote as the southern boundary the house or courtyard lying farther south beyond it, we do not say that he also sold the annex standing within those boundaries. Rather, we say he needed to identify a recognizable boundary, whereas the annex itself was not sufficiently distinctive. Ibid.
Into it. Meaning, the upper story has no other entrance, and one ascends from the house by a ladder through an opening in the ceiling. In that case we say that the upper story too is included in the sale of the house because it stands directly above it. This is unlike an annex built on the side when it is four amot wide. Ibid.
