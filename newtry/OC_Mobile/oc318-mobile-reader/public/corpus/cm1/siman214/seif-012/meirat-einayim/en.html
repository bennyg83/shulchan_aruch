But he does not give him an upper story. An upper story is inferior to a ground floor because of the ascent and descent. Although an upper story can also be called a house, as the ruling concludes, the holder of the document has the weaker hand. Yet because the seller specifically said, “a house in my house,” rather than simply, “I sell you a house,” the language implies that he intended the portion most naturally called a house, namely a ground floor, not an upper story. This differs from the Ran, Beit Yosef, and Darkei Moshe, who understand the Rambam as allowing the seller even in this wording to give an upper story. They apparently inferred this from the Rambam's phrase, “he gives him the inferior one,” without explaining that it must be the inferior ground floor and not an upper story. Even if the Rambam does not expressly disagree, the Ran certainly does. Perhaps Rama did not wish to prolong the discussion here, since the Rosh and Tur state the rule simply as written.
