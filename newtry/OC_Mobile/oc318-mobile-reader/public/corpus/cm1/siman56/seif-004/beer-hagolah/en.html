o) The reason explained there is that the third party is not more credible than the litigant himself. If the document were in the lender's possession, since he could not validate it, it would have no effect, as explained above at the beginning of siman 46. The Bach writes that certainly it is no worse than one witness and therefore requires a Torah oath, like a third party whose entrusted item is no longer in his possession. Here too he has nothing in his possession and there is only uncertainty. The debtor cannot claim jesting or that the admission was made to avoid appearing wealthy, because both parties admit that they appointed him as third party, etc. The Bach writes that he saw Maharshal's handwriting expressing doubt about the matter, but in his view there is no doubt: it is obvious that a Torah oath is required, and this is the practical ruling, unlike the Sema.
