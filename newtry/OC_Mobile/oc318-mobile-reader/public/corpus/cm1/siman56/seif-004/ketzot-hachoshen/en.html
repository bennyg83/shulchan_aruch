Returning it would accomplish nothing when the instrument itself cannot be established. The Shach cites Nimukei Yosef as disagreeing where the shalish says the document was paid. Since there was a time when the shalish could have validated or returned the document effectively, his credibility once arose and does not disappear merely because he cannot validate it now. The Shach distinguishes: if the borrower claims the document is forged, then according to his own words the shalish could never have validated it and therefore was never entrusted through a valid migo. But if the borrower admits he wrote it and merely claims payment, he thereby admits that the shalish once had a valid position from which he could have validated or returned it, and therefore the shalish remains believed. This still requires study. The credibility of a shalish is not literally the force of two witnesses, because if actual witnesses contradict him, the witnesses prevail. It is closer to an agreed credibility clause. If the instrument itself is not validated and the only basis for saying the shalish was trusted comes from the borrower's own admission, why should the borrower not be believed through a migo that he could have said the document was forged and that the shalish was never trusted at all? With an ordinary contractual credibility clause contained in an unvalidated document, the borrower is believed through a migo of forgery to say the debt was paid, despite the credibility clause. The same should apply to the shalish. The Nimukei Yosef can be sustained only according to the authorities who do not allow a migo of forgery to overcome a credibility clause, as Baal HaTerumot holds and as reflected in one opinion in siman 46. According to our practical law, however, which permits a payment claim against an unvalidated document even where it contains a general credibility clause because of the migo of forgery, the borrower should likewise be believed against the shalish's special credibility. Therefore the Shach's distinction cannot be universally accepted.
