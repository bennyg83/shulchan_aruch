Paid, and so forth. Shach cites Nimukei Yosef, who says that if the creditor admits that the receipt was written and it is in a third party's possession, there is no need to authenticate it at all. Shach wrote above that this is also Tosafot's view. I already explained there that this is incorrect. Tosafot disagree, and Nimukei Yosef is essentially alone in this formulation. Even Rashi, whom Nimukei Yosef understands as supporting him, appears to have retracted. In Bava Metzia, Rashi explains the phrase "there are no witnesses on it" to mean that there are no witnesses authenticating the receipt. This means the receipt itself is a normal written receipt but lacks validation; Rashi does not say that authentication is wholly unnecessary merely because it is in the third party's hand. The apparent inconsistency in Rashi can be resolved. Rashi rules like the second version of R. Nachman, which requires specifically a migo. In Gittin, Tosafot criticize Rashi for this, but that is his position. The second version need not have been conclusively refuted. It can interpret the baraita's phrase "there are witnesses on it" as meaning ordinary witnesses, not witnesses of authentication. Then the third party has a migo concerning an unsigned or incompletely evidenced receipt. In Sanhedrin, where the Gemara originally challenges from the baraita, the discussion initially assumes that a receipt found in the creditor's hand is itself valid. At that stage there is no question of authenticating signatures. Therefore, "there are no witnesses on it" necessarily means no actual witnesses signed the receipt, and the challenge works. After the Gemara concludes that a receipt found in the creditor's possession is generally invalid, the first clause must be reread. "There are witnesses on it" then means either that the witnesses themselves are present and confirm the receipt, or that the signatures have been authenticated. Correspondingly, the later clause concerning the third party can also mean the receipt lacks present authentication but the third party has a migo under the second version. The contradiction disappears. Thus Rashi can consistently rule like the second version of R. Nachman and still explain Bava Metzia as requiring authentication in the ordinary case. Practically, very few authorities support Nimukei Yosef's view that an unvalidated receipt in a third party's hands automatically needs no authentication at all. Since Tosafot do not actually say this and Rashi can be explained otherwise, the halachah should not follow Nimukei Yosef's lone position.
