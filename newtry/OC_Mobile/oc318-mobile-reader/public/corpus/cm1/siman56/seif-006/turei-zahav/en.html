(Ibid.) The claimant shall declare before witnesses how the document came into his possession. Here beit din is unnecessary, unlike seif 6, which requires declaration before beit din. Above, the escrow holder wishes to record testimony for use in a later dispute, and testimony must be received by beit din; telling witnesses would later create testimony from hearsay. Here he does not seek to testify about the merits. He fears only that handing the document to the claimant will make the claimant appear possessed and cause the defendant’s asmachta defense to be ignored. He therefore makes the delivery expressly conditional before witnesses. Those witnesses can later testify directly that the transfer did not establish an unconditional right. Alternatively, above the document remains deposited and therefore requires beit din custody; here it is immediately delivered to the creditor, so witnesses suffice. It nevertheless requires study how a written declaration by witnesses helps in light of the rule against testimony from writing.
