If a person becomes custodian through an agent, as where the borrower comes with someone holding collateral or a document and says, 'The lender appointed me his agent to bring this to you for deposit under specified conditions,' and the custodian accepts it, but the lender later denies ever appointing the agent for that purpose, the custodian follows the agent's instructions. {Rama: If the lender does not deny the agency but the intended custodian refused the deposit, leaving it with the agent, the agent is not a custodian but an ordinary agent and returns it to the person who gave it to him. A custodian must always act according to the conditions of the deposit despite a party's later claim. For example, Reuven and Shimon agreed that Shimon would pay his debt in installments, deposited the document with Levi, and stipulated that if Shimon missed one installment, Levi would return the document so Reuven could collect all at once. If Shimon misses an installment, Levi returns the document even if he knows Shimon will have defenses, because Levi is a custodian, not a judge. Before releasing it, he should state before witnesses how it came to him and under what condition; they should write, sign, and give the account to him, and he should deliver it to Shimon, who may then raise any defense. Therefore, once a custodian returns a document, we do not suspect that he acted unlawfully or misunderstood custodianship unless a concrete defect arose (Responsa of the Rashba, siman 901; Rosh, klal 105, siman 6).}
