The borrower. The Sema writes that the Mechaber specifically says the borrower came together with the other person, because the borrower knows his own rights and can state with certainty that the item was deposited with the third party on a particular condition. If the borrower does not know the circumstances, he cannot make such a definite claim. The Taz writes that the Sema's explanation is unclear. He explains instead that the Mechaber is not addressing the special credibility of a third party, since Rama already wrote in seif 2 that there is no distinction whether the lender alone delivered the item, etc. Rather, the meaning is that the borrower agrees to the basic mission and amount of money with the person whom the lender says he appointed as messenger. The Mechaber needed to state this because otherwise the messenger himself would not be believed. See the Shach, who emends the Sema's text so that "third party" should read "messenger."
The messenger. The Sema cites the Tur: the third party is believed to say that the messenger gave him these instructions, and the messenger was believed at the moment the document or pledge left his hand through a migo that he could have said, "It was deposited with me on such-and-such a condition."
And he shall return. It appears that this applies where the messenger no longer has a migo that he returned it or that the transaction never occurred. If such a migo is available, he may deliver the item to the opposing party according to the condition stated before him. A true third party, by contrast, would be believed even without any present migo. Shach.
His document. In seif 3 the Mechaber writes that the third party should deliver the entrusted item only before beit din, while here he does not say so. The Sema suggests that here the arrangement was conditional: if the borrower failed to pay at one of the specified times, the entire debt would become due at once. Because that condition may involve asmachta and the borrower's legal defense is known, the law requires the stronger safeguard of placing the right directly into the creditor's possession in the specified manner. In an ordinary third-party arrangement, where we do not know whether any dispute exists, delivery before beit din is enough. The Shach finds this wording difficult, since throughout halacha delivery before beit din is stronger than delivery before ordinary witnesses. He therefore explains the difference differently. In seif 3 it is clear that under the law the third party is required to deliver the item, and his only concern is that one party may later deny the facts, leaving him with the force of only one witness. Therefore he should deliver it before beit din. Here, by contrast, the third party himself is uncertain whether the arrangement may be an unenforceable asmachta. He cannot safely present it to beit din and ask them to order delivery, because beit din advances defenses for an absent party and may refuse to direct delivery lest the borrower later claim asmachta. Therefore the third party should simply perform what he believes is his duty, delivering it before witnesses. They should give him a signed writing recording the terms of the arrangement. If the borrower later claims asmachta, he can raise that claim; if not, the creditor prevails with the document. See the Taz for another distinction.
Therefore. The Sema questions Rama's word "therefore," because the conclusion does not necessarily follow from what precedes. The Shach answers that there is no difficulty. Rama means that since in a doubtful case delivery should be before witnesses who then write and sign a record, yet here no such writing is found in the recipient's possession, nevertheless we need not suspect wrongdoing. We assume the matter was clear to the third party and that he returned it lawfully.
