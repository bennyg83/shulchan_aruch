His document. In seif 3 the Shulchan Aruch states that he should give it only before beit din. The difference is that there the case is an ordinary deposit with a third party, and we do not know whether any dispute exists. It therefore suffices for him to state the matter before beit din. Here, however, an asmachta may be involved and the party's claim and right are already known, so a stronger safeguard is required, namely that witnesses write down what occurred and deliver the writing to him, Sema. The Shach objects that beit din is always stronger evidence than witnesses. He therefore explains that there the legal entitlement to the property is known and the only fear is later denial, so appearance before beit din suffices. Here there is concern that the arrangement may be an asmachta, and if he comes before beit din they might not order delivery at all. Therefore, the only available procedure is to act before witnesses. The Tumim asks why he should not simply go to beit din and allow them to rule according to Torah law. See Beurim, seif katan 20, where I distinguished as follows. Here the third party states the circumstances and condition under which he himself is obligated to release the property. With respect to what he himself must do, he has the status of a litigant, and witnesses suffice. There, he reports matters that were explained before him concerning what the defendant must do after the third party releases the property.
