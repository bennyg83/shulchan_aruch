Presumed unpaid. See Shach, seif katan 25, through his statement that if they stipulated that repayment must be made specifically in the presence of the third party, then no oath is required, whether the borrower says, 'Swear to me,' or the lender seeks to collect from heirs, because the third party is believed that payment was not made. Dagul Merevavah writes that this is not because of the special credibility of a third party, since there is no migo. The third party's power is merely to return the document to the lender, and the lender himself would otherwise have to swear. Rather, the third party serves as a supporting witness who exempts the lender from an oath. Consequently, if he is a relative, this does not help. Urim VeTumim and Netivot HaMishpat likewise write this. Alternatively, the Shach may refer to a case where the borrower admits that such a condition was made when the document was entrusted. End of quote.
