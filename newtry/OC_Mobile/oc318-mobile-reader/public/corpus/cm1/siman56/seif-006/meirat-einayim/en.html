The borrower came together with another person. In Derishah I wrote that the case specifically says the borrower came with him because the borrower knows his own rights and can claim with certainty that the document was entrusted upon such-and-such condition. If the borrower himself knows nothing of the matter, the accompanying person cannot make such a definite claim. This differs from what Rama wrote in Darkei Moshe, seif 2. See my reconciliation there.
He appoints him as third-party custodian, etc. The Tur writes: the third party is believed as to what the agent told him, and the agent himself was believed at the time the document or pledge left his hand by a migo that, had he wished, he could have said, 'It was entrusted to me on such-and-such condition.'
The agent does not have the law of a third-party custodian. Rashba writes: if the agent told another person to accept this document with the instruction that if Reuven violated a condition it should be given to the opposing party, but that second person refused to receive it and the document remained in the agent's hand, then even if Reuven later violates the condition the agent should not hand the document to the other party. He was never appointed third-party custodian for that purpose. He must return it to the person who gave it to him.
How so? Reuven, etc. In seif 3 the Mechaber wrote that a third-party custodian should deliver the entrusted matter only before beit din. The Tur puts that rule and the present one consecutively. In Perishah I explained that in an ordinary third-party arrangement, where we do not know of specific claims or disputes between the parties, delivery before beit din suffices. Here, however, the arrangement provides that if the debtor misses one installment he must pay the entire debt at once. This has the character of an asmachta and the claim and defense are known in advance. Therefore a stronger procedural safeguard is required, namely that the custodian preserve proof of his authority and the condition before handing over the document.
Before he removes it from his hand, he should say, etc. The Tur adds: before giving the document he should make a copy of it, authenticate the copy before witnesses, and explain before witnesses how the matter occurred. Rama included this within his phrase that the witnesses should write, etc.
