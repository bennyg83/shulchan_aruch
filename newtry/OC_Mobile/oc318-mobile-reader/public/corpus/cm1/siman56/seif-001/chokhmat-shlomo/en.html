Seif 1. A third party, while the deposited matter remains in his possession, is believed concerning the terms of the deposit. I was asked about a third party who held a promissory note in escrow. A separate writing was found in his possession stating that the note had been deposited with him subject to certain conditions. He now claims the opposite of that writing and says that when he wrote it he no longer remembered the terms of the deposit, mistakenly thought the terms were such and such, and now recalls that in truth he does not remember under what arrangement it was deposited. In my opinion he is not believed. Certainly, if he has no migo, for example because the writing was seen in his possession before the dispute arose, he cannot claim that he wrote it when he did not remember the matter. A person cannot establish himself as wicked. If he truly did not remember, how could he record the terms as fact? Even if he had a migo, such as the ability to conceal the writing entirely and deny that he ever wrote it, once he admits and produces the writing but claims he wrote it while not remembering, he is not believed through that migo. The rule that a person cannot render himself wicked applies even where he has a migo, as is evident from the second chapter of Bava Metzia concerning witnesses who say they signed under financial coercion. If his present account were true, he acted improperly by creating a written record of testimony while not remembering the matter. He could die, the writing could later come before his heirs, and they might rely upon it as a valid record. Since his claim necessarily casts himself as one who acted wickedly, he is not believed to undermine the escrow writing by saying it was written when he lacked recollection. We judge on the basis of the writing. This is clear and correct in my opinion. There, seif 1: a third party while the deposited matter remains in his possession. See Shach, seif katan 3, citing Rashba. Compare Even HaEzer 173, seif 10, concerning a person who first imposed one condition and later another where the conditions appear contradictory.
