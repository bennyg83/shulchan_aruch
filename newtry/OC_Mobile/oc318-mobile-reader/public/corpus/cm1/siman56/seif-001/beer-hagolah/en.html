a) Baraita, Gittin 64a, and a Tosefta cited by the Rif and Rosh at the beginning of Bava Metzia.
b) Tur. So too writes Sefer HaTerumot, gate 52, explaining that the parties trusted the third-party custodian. We learned, "My father is trustworthy to me," and one cannot retract after accepting him, as explained above in siman 22.
c) There. So too writes Baal HaTerumot, gate 52, in the name of Rav Hai, and likewise the Mordechai in several places and Hagahot Maimoniyot. They write that even when the entrusted item is no longer in his possession, he remains believed concerning what he already did while it was in his possession.
d) Tur, seif 5, in the name of Baal HaIttur under the letter shin, and Baal HaTerumot cites him in gate 52.
e) So it appears there.
f) There, in the name of Baal HaTerumot, gate 52, from what the Gemara says where the husband claims the item was merely deposited: monetary matters are different, meaning money is subject to waiver, and Rav Huna therefore agrees that the third party is believed, there in Gittin. He writes that the Ramban answered him this way, that he received this tradition from the early authorities, and that the Rivash likewise rules so.
g) There, and so writes Baal HaTerumot there in the name of the Ramban. Likewise, if an erasure or scraping is found on the back of the document, he is believed to say that the condition of the third-party arrangement written there was erased. Responsa Rosh, rule 105, siman 4. Sema in the name of Darkei Moshe.
h) There, from the statement, "they are his agents and they are his witnesses," etc., Kiddushin 43a.
i) Because he is liable to take an oath.
j) The wording of the Rambam, chapter 16 of Hilkhot Malveh, from the incident of that woman, Sanhedrin 31a.
k) The Maggid Mishneh writes that perhaps the Rambam learned this from the Mishnah at the end of the first chapter of Bava Metzia, 20a, "and if there is a memorandum with them," etc., according to Rashi's explanation there. This is a general rule for all similar cases: since the third party is more credible than either litigant, he acts according to what was written. The Gemara's interpretation concerning a document found among torn documents applies only where the holder is the lender, but with a third party everything follows his statement and his writing. So too writes the Tur in seif 4 and Baal HaTerumot there in the name of the Ramban.
l) Statement of Rav Yirmeyah bar Abba in the name of Rav there.
