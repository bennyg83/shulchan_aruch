A third party custodian, etc. The Maharsha understood Tosafot to say that even according to Rava a custodian is believed only through a migo, because he could have destroyed the instrument. This is not correct. In Sanhedrin the instrument had already been seen in the custodian’s possession before the dispute, so the possibility that she could once have destroyed it is not a present migo. Rava nevertheless believed her because the parties had originally entrusted the matter to her. The baraita concerning a receipt in a custodian’s hands proves the same point. Even where the receipt cannot presently be authenticated, the custodian is believed because when it was entrusted to him it could have been authenticated or delivered to the borrower. The Nimukei Yosef explains that the custodian is believed without any current migo, provided the object was not worthless from the outset. The Rosh, Tosafot, Rashba, Tur, Mechaber, Rama, and Ir Shushan all support this rule. It applies only where both parties admit that he was appointed custodian. A receipt in the custodian’s possession may therefore be stronger than one in the borrower’s possession, because the custodian’s original authority sustains his statement even after authentication has become impossible. However, if a party claims that the writing is an outright forgery that could never have been authenticated, the custodian is not believed, since the parties cannot be said to have entrusted him with a mere shard. This is the meaning of the Rambam and Mechaber’s statement that he could once have burned or torn the instrument. His credibility rests on the fact that at some earlier time he had the practical power to carry out the disposition he now describes.
