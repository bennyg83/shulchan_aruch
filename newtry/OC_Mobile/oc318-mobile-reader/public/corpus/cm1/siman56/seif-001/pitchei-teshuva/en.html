Responsa Beit Ephraim, Choshen Mishpat, siman 15, discusses a question posed by a rabbi concerning a third party whose account is contradicted by the owner. The owner says that he merely deposited the item with him, while the third party says that he was appointed as escrow to deliver it to another person. The third party has a migo that he could have denied receiving it, claimed that he purchased it, or claimed that he returned it. However, he would have had to take a hesset oath on those alternative claims. The third party says: My account is true. It was given to me to deliver to so and so. When beit din rules that I must deliver it, I will do so, but I refuse to swear against the owner and will not obey that aspect of the judgment. The question is whether, because he has not sworn, his statement is ineffective, or whether, since one who refuses a hesset oath does not have his property seized, and had he claimed that he purchased the item no property would have been seized, his present statement that it belongs to so and so should likewise be effective even though he refuses to swear. Or perhaps hesset is different, because where a defendant refuses to swear we do not seize property due to his prior monetary possession, whereas when he says that the item belongs to another person there is no such monetary possession, since in either case it will leave his control, as the Shach uses this reasoning in siman 91. Beit Ephraim responds that it is clear that we disregard the third party's refusal to swear. His credibility does not arise from the oath at all, but from the fact that he could have delivered the item to the other person and could have answered the claimant with a complete denial or another claim upon which he would be believed. What basis is there to delay the other person's right merely because the third party refuses to obey the court's demand that he swear to the claimant? Consider a case where a person says, 'This utensil belongs to so and so,' in circumstances where he is believed, and then dies or travels overseas. Obviously we do not delay delivery of the item merely because the claimant protests and says that the third party lied. Likewise, in simanim 47, 62, 99, and 222, where we say that a person is believed because he could have delivered the property to so and so, we do not care whether he is willing to swear. Only where he has no migo and seeks to function merely as one witness is his testimony ineffective even if he is willing to swear, because this is a witness who requires an oath, as the Shach writes in siman 56 and Hagahot Ashri at the beginning of Bava Metzia, cited by the Shach in siman 222. Beit Ephraim discusses this at length and concludes that it is clear in practical law that the third party's refusal to swear does not impair the beneficiary's right. See there at length.
