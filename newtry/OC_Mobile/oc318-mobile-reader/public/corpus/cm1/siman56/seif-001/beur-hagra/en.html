When. Tosefta, first chapter of Bava Metzia.
Like two witnesses. There it states, "But if," etc., "like any person," meaning like one witness, as written in seif 3. This shows that while the item is in his possession, the third party custodian has the force of two witnesses, and all the Geonim rule accordingly.
Even. Gittin, because the parties trusted him. We learned similarly, "You are trusted by me," etc., in Baal HaDin. So too in the chapter Zeh Borer concerning that woman, etc.
And he need not swear. Otherwise he would not be believed, as stated in Kiddushin 43b, "and now that they instituted," etc. In Gittin it states, "Monetary matters are different," etc., implying credibility without an oath, and even concerning a prohibition he is believed there. In that setting an oath would not help. The reason is that a person does not sin where he gains nothing. In the chapter Zeh Borer it states that Rav Nachman believed him, etc., implying without an oath. See Hagahot Maimoniyot at the end of Hilchot Mishpatim, siman 44, where this is discussed at length.
And even if he wrote. Gittin there, Tosafot s.v. "third party custodian": even if the husband says, etc.; see there.
(Collected note) And even if they wrote the circumstances, etc. As Tosafot write in Gittin 64a, s.v. "third party custodian," etc. This is also compelled by the Gemara, which answers there, "Whose view is this? It is Rabbi Elazar," etc. Accordingly, the third party custodian is not believed and two witnesses are required concerning receipt. If so, the dispute between Rav Huna and Rav Chisda would not follow Rabbi Elazar. This cannot be, because Rav Huna himself rules on 86b like Rabbi Elazar regarding gittin, and they cannot be understood as disputing against the halachah. Moreover, they necessarily disagree concerning an agent for receipt, as Tosafot write on 63b, s.v. "and a third party custodian." We must therefore explain as Tosafot do on 64a, s.v. "Rabbi Elazar," etc., that the Mishnah concerns a document without signed witnesses, whereas their dispute concerns a document with signed witnesses, which is presumed to have been delivered before witnesses. Even so, the third party custodian is believed, even according to Rav Huna, unless the stated counterconsideration applies; the same is true when they are not in one city.
(Collected note) Although the time has passed, etc. This follows Tosafot's explanation in Gittin there that he is an agent for receipt.
(Collected note) And even if they contradicted him, etc. The Shach writes that this apparently means even where the custodian has no migo, as with every third party custodian. He disputes that view, and his words are correct. How can the rationale "they trusted him" apply when one says, "You stole it from me," or, "You found it"? See Beit Shmuel, siman 141, seif katan 14. The essential ruling follows the Shach, that the Rosh disagrees, and this is also the view of many poskim. Certainly, where he has a migo he is believed. Tosafot there, s.v. "third party custodian," also imply this. Accordingly, where one claims that an item was given only as a loan, the custodian is not believed if he lacks a migo, because it is an item ordinarily lent or rented. This implies that with an item not ordinarily lent or rented, he is believed even in such a case. Here the rationale of "they trusted him" is unnecessary. What the Shach writes, however, that the husband's statement "for safekeeping" means subject to a particular condition under which the get is to be given to her, is incorrect. If so, why does the Gemara challenge from the Mishnah concerning the woman, etc.? Perhaps he meant that he gave it for complete safekeeping. Tosafot there at the end of the cited passage state this explicitly. Rather, the reason is that when he entrusted it for safekeeping he thereby trusted the custodian, whereas in a loan he did not contemplate that the borrower would ordinarily lend it on his behalf; all the more so where the custodian found or stole it or the like, he is not believed without a migo, as above. See the Maharshal there.
And even if both contradict him. Gittin there. The Mishnah states, "The woman," etc., and the Gemara asks, "Why? Let him be believed," etc. According to Rashi, the challenge applies both to what she says and to what he received.
(Collected note) And even if both contradict him. As stated in the Tosefta: this one says thus, etc.
(Collected note) In a place where there is no, etc. In the first case he is believed even without a migo, as Tosafot write there, because where he has a migo even Rav Huna agrees, as stated there, "and Rav Huna concedes," etc. At the end of the chapter Zeh Borer the conclusion is that even where the item had already been established such that he has no present migo, he is believed because the parties trusted him. This refers to a case where he had a migo originally, so that the rationale of trust applies properly: even though he has no migo now, they initially trusted him. The Ran writes likewise. See the Shach, seif katan 1 and seif katan 11.
And they may require him to swear. Kiddushin there: "they themselves swear," etc. They are like all those who swear in response to a claim of uncertainty in the chapter Kol HaNishba'in, from which the law of all agents was derived. See the Shach, who explains that this resembles the case of a shopkeeper on his ledger and the partnership oath in the chapter HaNishba'in, and the like.
(Collected note) A document that was, etc. "Although," etc. This follows Rashi's explanation there concerning the case where the document had been established in beit din. The Gemara's difficulty is that he should nevertheless be believed, since if he wished he could have burned it, as Tosafot ask there. This is the meaning of "for if," etc.
(Collected note) Although the document is authenticated. This is the Rambam's wording and follows Rashi there. Tosafot, however, explain that the document had previously been seen in his possession. The Tur and Shulchan Aruch likewise write this in siman 65, seif 19.
And likewise if he died and it was found. Bava Metzia 21a: "There are no witnesses on it," etc. Tosafot in Sanhedrin there explain that this applies even where there are no witnesses at all; see there. The phrase "if he died" is necessary because the case there must involve the death of the third party custodian, since otherwise there would be no need for the receipt at all, as stated in the second chapter of Zeh Borer.
The claimant. Bava Kamma, chapter 5: "The one who is in pain," etc.
(Collected note) The claimant, etc. As stated in the Mishnah in Bava Batra 167b regarding the scribe's fee; see there.
