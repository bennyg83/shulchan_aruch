He is believed like two witnesses. Meaning, the third-party custodian is believed against both lender and borrower like two witnesses. Nevertheless, if actual witnesses contradict him, they are believed over him, as the Tur and Mechaber write nearby in seif 2. The reason for the custodian's special credibility is explained nearby in seif katan 10.
Even if he is a relative. Because it is like accepting one's father as a judge. See Perishah.
And he need not swear. Meaning, the custodian is believed without an oath. See Perishah.
Even if they wrote, etc. Meaning, the writing was lost, or another circumstance later arose concerning the deposited matter that was not recorded there. See Beit Yosef, seif 4, and Darkei Moshe, seif 9, who discuss this at length in the name of Rashba.
We do not say that they relied only upon, etc. Rather, we say that the writing was merely for remembrance.
Even though the specified time has passed. But if the lender deposited his document with someone and instructed the custodian to arrange a compromise or legal determination by a certain time, and that time passed without the custodian doing so, he returns the document to the lender. Rashba writes this in a responsum, cited in Darkei Moshe, siman 54, seif 12.
Even if one of them contradicts him, etc. The Tur writes: the custodian is believed, not only where one party claims that he was not appointed as a third-party custodian but received the item merely as an ordinary deposit, meaning to hold it without any condition and return it when requested, unlike a conditional deposit which itself constitutes third-party custodianship and creates special credibility. Even there the objection does not undo the custodian's status, since the party admits that he trusted him with the item. Rather, even if he claims, 'You stole it from me,' and the other says, 'No, you appointed me as a custodian between yourself and So-and-so,' the custodian is believed. See above in siman 48, seif 10, where the Tur writes that even if the borrower claims, 'It fell from me after I paid it and you found it,' he is not believed. The reason appears to be that we do not suspect people of theft, and falling is likewise uncommon. Here the same reason does not apply, because the party claims that he never trusted him to act as a third-party custodian.
The custodian is believed, etc. Likewise, if there is an erasure or scraping on the back of the document, he is believed to say that a condition of the custodianship had been written there and was erased. So writes Responsa Rosh, klal 105, siman 4. See there at length. See also siman 5 there concerning a claim by one party that his fellow owes him additional money from a partnership independently of the matter deposited. Darkei Moshe, seif 4.
They can make him swear even on an uncertain claim. Meaning, where the parties dispute the amount of money. Hagahot Mordechai concludes, 'as stated in chapter Kol HaNishba'in.' It appears this refers to the law there that partners, agents, and one who manages another's household may be required to swear even on an uncertain claim when suspected regarding two silver ma'ah, as the Tur and Mechaber write below at the beginning of siman 93. The same applies to this custodian when the parties have a grievance against him.
And it already left his possession in beit din, etc. The Tur's wording is: 'Even if it was already seen in beit din, after he produced the document from his possession without comment and remained silent until it was authenticated, and then said, "It is paid,"' etc.
For if he had wished, he could have burned it. Although it has now been seen in his possession in beit din, since the parties originally trusted him by placing it in his hand and regarded him as trustworthy not to tear or burn it or hand it to the opposing party, he remains believed as against them in everything he says. See Perishah. Mordechai in chapter HaChovel writes concerning a custodian who burned a document and said that the lender instructed him to burn it, while the lender denied this, that the custodian is believed. So cites Darkei Moshe.
It is merely playacting. The Tur and Mechaber write below at the end of siman 65 that even if a receipt is found in the lender's possession concerning one of his documents, this does not automatically impair the document. We say he prepared the receipt in case the borrower unexpectedly came to pay, so that it would be ready to hand over. I wrote this also above at the end of siman 49.
The claimant pays the fee, etc. The rule is that the person for whose benefit and advantage the matter is being done must pay the expense.
