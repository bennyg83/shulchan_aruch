(Seif 1, gloss: The escrow holder is believed.) The Rosh discusses a document held in escrow that contained an erasure. The escrow holder said a condition had been written there but he did not know its content, and the Rosh ruled that the erasure did not impair the document. The Sema cites this. It is difficult, because seif 4 says an escrow holder is no more credible than the litigant himself, and a comparable document in a litigant’s hand would be invalid. Perhaps when the Rosh wrote this responsum he followed the view that an erasure on the reverse does not invalidate. The gloss responds that the Rosh only suspects an erasure regarding payment claims, whereas a condition is ordinarily written within the document, not on its reverse. Thus an erasure behind the document does not by itself prove a condition. Where repayment cannot be claimed, the document may be collected even with such an erasure. The responsum also shows that an erasure on the reverse is not equivalent to loss of the document itself, for within the due date collection remains possible. The Sema, however, should not have abbreviated the responsum at this point.
