(A document that was in the hands of a third party.) The Tur writes, "even if it appears," etc. This is difficult, because it does not follow even the first version cited by the Beit Yosef, since that version requires specifically that the third party could have destroyed it. It appears to me that our master inferred from the Gemara's wording concerning the woman from whose possession a document emerged that, at the very moment they appeared before beit din, the document came out from under her hand. At that point she no longer had the ordinary migo, yet according to the first version we still apply the migo that she could previously have destroyed it. Although Tosafos in Perek HaKotev, page 84, state that we ordinarily do not apply a retroactive migo, with a third party whom the litigants trusted we do apply such a retroactive migo. This, it appears to me, is the intention of the Nimukei Yosef's statement, cited by the Beit Yosef, that a third party is believed even where there is no migo. At first glance this is surprising, because even according to the first version he at least needs the migo that he could have destroyed the document. Rather, the Nimukei Yosef means that there is no proper contemporaneous migo, only a retroactive one. Therefore Rambam writes, "had he wished, he could have burned it," referring to the earlier time, and such a migo is accepted in this context. Mordechai in Perek Chezkas Habatim writes regarding the general rejection of a retroactive migo and cites as proof the case of the woman from whose possession a document emerged. It appears to me that the Rama's following statement, "but where there is a migo he is believed," likewise means an ordinary contemporaneous migo, not a retroactive one. In that case he is believed even without the formal status of third party. But if the document had already left the third party's possession before he came to beit din, or he deposited it with beit din without qualification and only later came and said it had been paid, it appears obvious to me that he is not believed, because there is no migo at all.
