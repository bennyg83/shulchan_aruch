For the purpose of leaving the item with the recipient, the recipient's possession itself supports the shalish's earlier delivery. Once the giver admits that the item came through the shalish, we do not ordinarily fear that the recipient acquired it through a later deposit, accidental loss, or other irregular route. Possession proves that the shalish handed it over for the purpose claimed, much as ordinary possession of movables proves a sale or gift. This is true even if the recipient does not know exactly what terms the giver originally stated. For purposes of an oath, however, possession alone does not eliminate all doubt. A possessor who says, 'You sold or gave it to me,' can still have to swear against a claim of deposit or loss. The shalish cannot serve as an assisting witness if he is now financially interested, for example because if he admits loss or improper delivery he may become liable. Therefore the recipient can have to swear even though he retains the object. This explains why the shalish himself may also have to swear after an unobserved delivery. While the object remains in his hands, the presumption that a person does not sin for no benefit and his special shalish status exempt him from an ordinary oath. If he delivered in front of witnesses, his act is likewise independently established. But if the object is no longer in his hands and no one saw the delivery, there remains the possibility that it left through deposit, loss, or another event that could make him liable, and he now has a personal reason to maintain his version. The giver's definite claim, together with the presumption that an agent ordinarily performs his mission, can therefore generate an oath. Raavad's statement that the shalish is believed through a migo that he could return the item was stated in a context such as a get where no monetary interest arises. In money, a present interested migo does not substitute for neutral testimony; the shalish becomes fully trustworthy only after he actually returns the item and removes his personal interest.
