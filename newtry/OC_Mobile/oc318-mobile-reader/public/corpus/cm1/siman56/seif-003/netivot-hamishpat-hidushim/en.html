A grievance. The Sema explains that this refers to a dispute over the amount of money. Accordingly, he writes that the same rule applies where the property entrusted to a third party is still in that third party's possession. The Shach explains that the grievance means that one party claims that the third party released the entrusted property improperly. As a matter of law, the Shach concludes that when the third party says that the property should go to the person who currently holds it, he is believed, because even without the third party's present statement that person would be believed by virtue of the credibility he had at the time of the transaction. Nevertheless, the third party cannot serve as a witness to exempt him from an oath, because a witness who himself requires an oath is not treated as a valid witness for that purpose.
