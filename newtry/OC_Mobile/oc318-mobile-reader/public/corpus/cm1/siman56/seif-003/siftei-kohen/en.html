Even if he wrote it, etc. The Rashba writes that if the custodian recorded that the item was entrusted to him on this condition alone, one might say that he has already testified concerning the entrusted property and cannot later add another preexisting condition, just as witnesses cannot add a condition after their written testimony. If he merely wrote that it was delivered on a stated condition in order to prevent him from denying that condition, he may confirm it and add another condition. The Rashba further suggests that even the phrase “on this condition alone” may not prevent the custodian from explaining the condition while the entrusted item remains in his possession, because his hand is like that of the parties. In my view, if he wrote that it was entrusted on this condition alone, he is believed to add another condition only if he says that the parties stipulated it afterward. He is not believed to say that another earlier condition existed but was omitted. Even a party who had written such a statement in his own hand could not contradict it, and the custodian is no better than the parties themselves.
