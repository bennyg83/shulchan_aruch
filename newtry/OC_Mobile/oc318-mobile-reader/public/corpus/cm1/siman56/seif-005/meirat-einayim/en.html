Or the borrower died. Meaning, beit din advances for the heirs every claim their father could have made.
In the presumption that it has not been paid. It appears this means only that beit din does not itself raise for the borrower a demand that the lender swear he was not paid. The novelty is that even though the document is not in the lender's possession, the borrower is not automatically believed. But if the borrower himself demands, 'Swear to me,' the lender must swear, for a document held by a third-party custodian is no stronger than a document held by the lender himself when it contains no credibility clause, as the Tur and Mechaber write in siman 82.
One who obligated himself through a document with a kinyan, etc. Meaning, in such a case his property became encumbered from the time of the kinyan and the document may be written for the borrower even without the lender present. Therefore the reasons explained nearby are relevant.
He shall not give it to the lender, etc. The Tur writes that the document should not be delivered to the lender for two reasons. First, perhaps the borrower already borrowed with it and repaid, whereupon the document was returned to the borrower; that is why it is now found in his possession, and he merely deposited it with this custodian. Second, perhaps he never borrowed with it at all, and stipulated with the witnesses who made the kinyan that they should deliver the document to him and not to the lender. The lender therefore never acquired rights through it so that the witnesses' testimony could operate until the document left the obligor's possession. Nor should it be returned to the borrower's heirs, because perhaps he merely told the witnesses, 'Make a kinyan from me for So-and-so and write him the document,' in which case the lender acquired through the witnesses while it remained in their hands, and perhaps the witnesses mistakenly handed it to the borrower. End of the Tur's words.
