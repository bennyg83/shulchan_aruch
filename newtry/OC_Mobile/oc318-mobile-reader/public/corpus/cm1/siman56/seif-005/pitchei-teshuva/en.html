Presumed unpaid. See Shach, seif katan 25, through his statement that if they stipulated that repayment must be made specifically in the presence of the third party, then no oath is required, whether the borrower says, 'Swear to me,' or the lender seeks to collect from heirs, because the third party is believed that payment was not made. Dagul Merevavah writes: In my opinion, the third party is not believed here because of the special law of a third party, since no migo applies. What greater power does the third party have than merely to return the document to the lender, when the lender himself would still have to swear? Rather, the Shach's intent is that the third party is at minimum a supporting witness and therefore exempts the lender from an oath. If so, if the third party is a relative, his testimony is ineffective. Urim VeTumim and Netivot HaMishpat likewise write this. Alternatively, the Shach may be discussing a case where the borrower admits that he stipulated this condition at the time the document was entrusted. End of quote.
