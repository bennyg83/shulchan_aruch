One who owes. See Bava Batra 135b concerning a healthy person's gift made through a kinyan. Since the document never reached the recipient, he did not acquire it. Rabbeinu Chananel similarly explains Bava Metzia 13a, even according to Rabbi Eliezer regarding documents of immediate acquisition. See Rif's responsum and siman 65, seif 14. In Bava Batra, acquisition through another person is effective only where the donor expressly transferred it through him, not where he merely deposited it. Baal HaTerumot and Tur suggest that the debtor may have instructed the custodian to give it back to him. There is also concern for payment, so its law resembles that of a found document whose nature is unknown. See Bava Metzia chapter one and siman 250, seif 25.
(Collected note) One who owes, etc. The Sema cites Tur, but the matter requires study, for siman 250 distinguishes a gift from a loan, and siman 243 likewise validates an ordinary gift document. Bava Metzia 16b states that a found document of immediate acquisition may be returned. The Shach's distinction that the acquisition was known to be unconditional is strained. The primary reason is concern for repayment, which does not apply to a gift, as stated in siman 65, seif 14. The proof from Bava Batra 135b was rejected by the authorities, who explain that it concerns a gift of a dying person. The Rosh's wording follows his view that loan documents are treated alike whether or not accompanied by a kinyan, but his rationale still requires study. A non-acquisition document may not be written without the borrower's presence, while an acquisition document ordinarily presents no concern.
