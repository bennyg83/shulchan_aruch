If the shalish died, the lender may collect only with an oath. The same applies if the borrower says, "Swear to me," as with other documents, unless the document contains a credibility clause. If there was a condition that payment could be made only in the shalish's presence, and the shalish says payment did not occur, no oath is needed even against heirs. This assumes the shalish is fit to testify and therefore functions as a supporting witness. Otherwise the document is no stronger in the shalish's hand than in the lender's own hand, where an oath would still be required.
