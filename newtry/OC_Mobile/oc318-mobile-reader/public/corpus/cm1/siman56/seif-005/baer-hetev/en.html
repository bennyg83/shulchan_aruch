He shall give it. The Sema writes in the name of the Tur that there are two reasons for the rule. First, perhaps the borrower once borrowed with this document and repaid it, meaning that the document was then returned to him, and for that reason it is now found in his possession; he subsequently deposited it with this person. Second, perhaps no loan ever occurred, and the borrower stipulated with the witnesses that they were to give the document to him rather than to the creditor, so the creditor never acquired rights under it. Conversely, the document should not simply be returned to the borrower's heirs either, because perhaps the borrower instructed the witnesses without qualification, 'Make a kinyan from me for so-and-so and write him the document.' In that case the creditor acquired the right while the document was still in the witnesses' hands, and perhaps the witnesses mistakenly delivered it to the borrower. The Shach questions the Sema's second concern, for the Tur states at the end of siman 250 in the name of his father the Rosh that with a gift by a healthy person made through kinyan, the recipient acquires from the moment of the kinyan and the document need not reach his hand. This is the consensus of the poskim, and Beit Yosef likewise rules at the end of siman 39 that a kinyan document need not reach the recipient. One must answer that there the kinyan is known to have been made unconditionally, such as where the witnesses to the kinyan are before us. The Bach similarly resolves the matter. The Shach repeats his objections to the Sema in several places, including siman 65, seifim 2 and 5, siman 243, seif 13, and siman 250, seif 25. Since he restates them in their proper places, Baer Heitev does not repeat them here.
