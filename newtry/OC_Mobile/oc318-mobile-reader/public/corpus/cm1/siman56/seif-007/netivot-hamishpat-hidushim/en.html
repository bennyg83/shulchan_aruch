With a migo. The Shach explains this as a woman who controlled all of her husband's property and is believed as a shalish. That resolves the apparent contradiction with siman 62. See the Beurim: this applies when they say that the actual object belongs to others. But if the person says only that he owes money to others, he is not believed through a migo that he could have pledged his movables to them, unless they themselves extended merchandise on credit and are personally owed the money. If a person holds a document and says he is merely the shalish, while the lender holds a copy and denies him, then if the holder has a migo that he could have returned the original privately, the shalish is believed.
