By virtue of a migo. The Sema explains that had they wished, they could have returned the money to the person whom they now say owns it. See further siman 62 for what he writes, so that no contradiction should be inferred from there, and see Even HaEzer siman 86 concerning these laws. The Shach writes that there is no difficulty from siman 62. There the case is an ordinary wife who conducts the household's business but whose husband did not entrust her with complete authority, and therefore she does not have the law of a third party. Here, however, the case is a wife who controls all of her husband's assets and whom he has trusted with everything he owns. She therefore has the law of a third party and is believed for this reason even with a migo relating to an earlier point in time, which an ordinary person would not possess. Although the Mechaber here speaks generally of a wife who conducts business and does not expressly say that the husband entrusted her with full authority, the placement of the ruling in this siman concerning third-party arrangements rather than in siman 62 shows that he is speaking of a wife who has the law of a third party. The Shach there extensively disputes the Sema, Bach, and other responsa on this subject. He further writes that if a person holds a document and says he is a third party, while a copy of the document is in the creditor's possession and the creditor denies his claim, then if the holder has a migo that he could privately have returned the document, he is believed, unlike Responsa Mahara Sasson.
