They are believed through a migo. Responsa Radbaz distinguishes between saying, 'These items belong to so-and-so,' and saying, 'I owe so-and-so.' In the first case, one who controls the items is believed because he has authority to return them to their owner. What is in his power can function like two witnesses, even in matters of personal status. But if he says merely that the owner owes someone money, even if true he has no right to seize collateral and hand it over to the creditor without beit din. His statement therefore rests only on an ordinary migo, and a migo does not extract property where the item is legally still in the depositor's possession. If the owner explicitly authorized him to pay or to hand over collateral, then he is believed, because the act is within his authority. Likewise, where a wife or other manager says property is actually owned by another person, she can be believed where she had power over the property and therefore could have returned it. If she herself borrowed or incurred the debt and is personally obligated, she likewise has authority to repay and can be believed concerning that liability through the appropriate migo.
