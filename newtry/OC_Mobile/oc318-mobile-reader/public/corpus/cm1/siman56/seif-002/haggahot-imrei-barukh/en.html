(Shach, passage beginning "and we learned from the responsum," etc., even if he already gave it) Note: This requires study. The case in the Rosh's responsum is different, because there the borrower is effectively saying, "I do not know whether you waived the debt," which is like saying, "I do not know whether I paid you," and he must therefore pay. But if a person receives vessels from a messenger who says that someone sent them to him as a gift, and the alleged sender later denies this, the recipient's position is different. The source text appears defective at this point, but the distinction between the Rosh's case and an ordinary disputed gift remains clear.
