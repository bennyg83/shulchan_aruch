He does not acquire by chazakah. See Baer Heitev and Responsa Chatam Sofer, Choshen Mishpat, siman 134, who was asked about the legal status of a Jew who converted to idolatry with respect to forms of acquisition. He discusses the matter at length and concludes that, for this purpose, such a person remains fully Jewish, as Shiltei HaGibborim writes at the end of the second chapter of Gittin concerning the Mishnah that all are qualified to bring a get. Even HaOzer, Orach Chayim, siman 189, likewise proves that an apostate Jew may appoint an agent and may serve as an agent like any other Jew. See what I wrote in Pitchei Teshuva to Even HaEzer, siman 141, seif katan 32. However, regarding the statement there that such a person is called a member of the covenant, we do not accept that conclusion. Although the Torah states, "When you sell something to your fellow," which the Sages expound as referring to one who is with you in Torah and mitzvot, and an apostate plainly is not included in that category, that teaching can be understood as governing the practical law that one should give preference to a fellow Jew who observes Torah and mitzvot when buying from someone, as Rashi explains on the Torah. See what I wrote above, siman 156, seif 6, seif katan 6.
