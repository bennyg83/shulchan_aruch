(He does not acquire by chazakah, etc.) The Sema disagrees with the Beit Yosef regarding the Maggid Mishneh's explanation, but there is no practical difference, because even according to the Sema a Jew purchasing from a non-Jew requires a document specifically, as Rambam writes in chapter 1 of Hilchot Zechiyah. It appears to me that Rambam's words here should be understood differently. He is not discussing three years of possession. If he were, this law would not belong in Hilchot Mechirah, because the present chapter is not about evidentiary chazakah but about acts of acquisition, such as locking, fencing, breaching, and other improvements to land. Nor is there any dispute of facts here. Both parties admit the sale, but the seller wishes to retract. Rambam therefore means: just as when a Jew sells land to a non-Jew, the non-Jew does not acquire through chazakah until he performs an act of improvement to the land sufficient to prevent the Jew from retracting, so too a Jew who comes by virtue of a non-Jew and says that the first Jew sold it to the non-Jew, even where the first Jew admits that sale but wishes to retract, may retract despite the second Jew's having taken possession and improved the land by locking or fencing. The second Jew must instead produce the document showing that the first Jew sold it to the non-Jew. This, in my view, is the intention of Rambam and the Maggid Mishneh.
