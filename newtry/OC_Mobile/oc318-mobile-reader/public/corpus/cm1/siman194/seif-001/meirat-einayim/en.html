A non-Jew does not acquire through chazakah, etc., and a Jew who buys, etc. Because I saw in the Kesef Mishneh and Beit Yosef explanations of Rambam and the Maggid Mishneh on this matter that are not sufficiently clear, and there are practical halachic consequences, I considered it appropriate to copy and explain the matter, from which, with the help of Heaven, we can arrive at the truth. At the end of chapter 1 of Hilchot Zechiyah, Rambam writes: "If a non-Jew sells movable property to a Jew, or acquires movable property from a Jew, he acquires through meshichah and transfers ownership through meshichah or through payment." The Maggid Mishneh and Kesef Mishneh explain there that the phrase "or through payment" also refers to a non-Jew who purchases from a Jew, so that a non-Jew acquires a Jew's movable property through payment as well. They bring proof that Rambam holds that whether a non-Jew buys from a Jew or a Jew buys from a non-Jew, the acquisition may be through either meshichah or payment alone, without a deed. Ramban and other Geonim disagree and hold that a non-Jew does not acquire movable property from a Jew through payment, only through meshichah. See there, and see what I wrote in the Derishah and in the Sema to Yoreh Deah siman 320. Rambam continues: "Land, however, a non-Jew does not acquire from a Jew except through a deed, and he does not transfer it to a Jew except through a deed, because his mind relies only on the deed. Therefore, if a Jew purchased a field from a non-Jew and paid the money, and before he took possession another Jew came and took possession, the latter acquires and must reimburse the first purchaser's money," etc. These are Rambam's words relevant to our matter. The Maggid Mishneh writes there: "But land he does not acquire, etc., and does not transfer, etc. This is explained in chapter Chazakat HaBatim, that a non-Jew does not acquire except through a deed, and the same applies that he does not acquire." It appears to me that a scribal error fell into his words, and the text should read: "a non-Jew does not acquire, and the same applies that he does not transfer." For the law that a non-Jew does not acquire except through a deed is explained there at the end of page 35 in Rav Yehudah's statement in the name of Rav: "A Jew who comes through a non-Jew is like the non-Jew. Just as a non-Jew has no chazakah except through a deed, so a Jew who comes through a non-Jew has no chazakah except through a deed." The Maggid Mishneh cites this statement in chapter 1 of Hilchot Mechirah on Rambam's ruling that a non-Jew does not acquire through chazakah but acquires through a deed together with payment, and that a Jew who comes through the non-Jew is like the non-Jew and does not acquire except through a deed. The Maggid Mishneh explains that Rambam did not interpret this Gemara as discussing a three-year possessory chazakah in the face of a protest, but as discussing even a case with no protest: when the non-Jew comes to buy land, he does not acquire it from a Jew except through a deed. According to what I have written, that the correct text in the Maggid Mishneh is that chapter Chazakat HaBatim establishes that a non-Jew "does not acquire," the matter is clear, because the Gemara's phrase "just as a non-Jew has no chazakah" is understood by Rambam as referring even to an act of acquisition. From the fact that the Maggid Mishneh then says "and the same applies that he does not transfer," rather than deriving that too from the end of the Gemara's statement, "so a Jew who comes through a non-Jew has no chazakah except through a deed," it is evident that he understands Rambam not to interpret the latter part as discussing how a Jew acquires from a non-Jew. Rather, it concerns a protest against a Jew in possession. Another Jew says to the possessor, "What are you doing on this land? I have witnesses that it is mine." The possessor answers, "I purchased it from such-and-such non-Jew, who told me that he had bought it from you." The Gemara teaches that we do not leave the land with the possessor unless he can produce the deed that the protesting Jew gave the non-Jew when the latter allegedly purchased it. Since a non-Jew does not establish a possessory right to a Jew's field without a deed, because we presume that a non-Jew may be coercive and do not believe his unsupported claim of purchase, a Jew who derives his title from him must likewise produce the deed. The reason the Maggid Mishneh does not interpret the end of the statement as dealing with an act of acquisition, as he interprets the beginning, is apparently because the wording is not "a Jew who buys from a non-Jew," but "a Jew who comes through a non-Jew." That phrase implies not the acquisition itself but a challenge to his title, as I explained. This also explains the Maggid Mishneh's conclusion in chapter 1 of Hilchot Mechirah: "The reason a non-Jew does not acquire through chazakah is that if he could acquire through chazakah, he might take possession by force. But to write a deed for him, people will not go so far." He gives a reason why a non-Jew does not acquire from a Jew, but gives no reason why a Jew does not acquire from a non-Jew, because in his understanding Rambam there was not discussing acquisition by a Jew from a non-Jew at all, but possessory evidence against a challenge. This is the central matter Rambam intended to teach in that chapter, which concerns a person whose possession is challenged and who responds that he purchased the property and has proof of three years' possession. Rambam mentions there that a non-Jew does not acquire from a Jew except through a deed only in order to explain that for this reason a Jew who comes through him does not establish title through three years of possession but must produce the deed given by the Jew to the non-Jew. The principal laws governing how one person acquires from another are written by Rambam in chapter 1 of Hilchot Zechiyah U'Matanah, where he likewise states the laws governing how a non-Jew acquires from a Jew and a Jew from a non-Jew. In the latter place, the reason Rambam gives at the end, "because his mind relies only on the deed," in its plain meaning applies only to the immediately preceding clause, namely that the non-Jew does not transfer land to the Jew except through a deed. The reason is that the Jewish purchaser does not rely upon the transaction merely because he paid the non-Jew, since he fears the non-Jew may retract, whereas when he has a deed in hand his mind is settled. That reason does not suffice to explain why the non-Jew does not acquire from the Jew except through a deed. Therefore the Maggid Mishneh had to provide another reason in chapter 1 of Hilchot Mechirah, namely that if the non-Jew could acquire by mere chazakah, because of his coercive power he might take possession against the Jew's will. This appears entirely clear to me in Rambam according to the words of the Maggid Mishneh. It is unlike the explanation of the Kesef Mishneh in the two chapters cited above. His explanation implies that Rambam understands the end of the Gemara's statement, "a Jew who comes through a non-Jew has no chazakah except through a deed," as concerning acquisition by a Jew from a non-Jew, and it also appears that he understood the Maggid Mishneh in this way. See the end of chapter 1 of Hilchot Zechiyah, in the passage beginning "But land he does not acquire," and the following passage beginning "Therefore, if a Jew purchased." This interpretation caused the Kesef Mishneh to ask there on the Maggid Mishneh: Rambam says that if the first Jew did not take possession and another Jew subsequently did, the latter acquires without a deed. But Rambam writes in chapter 1 of Hilchot Mechirah that "a Jew who comes through a non-Jew is like the non-Jew," meaning, according to the Kesef Mishneh, that a Jew buying from a non-Jew does not acquire through chazakah. Rambam also writes in the present chapter that a non-Jew does not acquire or transfer except through a deed. The Kesef Mishneh therefore offers a forced resolution. According to what I have written, however, there is no difficulty. Rambam's statement in chapter 1 of Hilchot Mechirah concerning a Jew who comes through a non-Jew does not concern an act of acquisition. And what Rambam writes in chapter 1 of Hilchot Zechiyah, that a non-Jew transfers only through a deed, is because a Jewish purchaser who has merely paid does not rely on the sale until he has a deed. But if he first took possession, we see that he did rely upon his chazakah, for otherwise why did he perform it. This also removes the Kesef Mishneh's objection to the Maggid Mishneh's reason for why a non-Jew acquires from a Jew only by deed while giving no reason there why a Jew does not acquire from a non-Jew. According to what I have written, that chapter was not discussing the latter issue at all. There is likewise no need for the strained explanation the Kesef Mishneh gives there for why, in the statement that a Jew who comes through a non-Jew acquires only "through a deed," Rambam does not also say that payment is required, since a deed without money cannot acquire and the Jew is no better than one buying from another Jew. According to my explanation there is no difficulty, because the discussion is not the original acquisition but a challenge to the possessor. Once the possessor produces the deed that the protesting Jew gave the non-Jew, we leave the land in his possession, because we presume that the non-Jew certainly paid the purchase money as well. Nor is there need for the Kesef Mishneh's strained explanation that the reason a non-Jew does not acquire from a Jew through chazakah is that the Jewish seller does not rely upon the non-Jew to stand by the sale until a deed is written for him. That reason is difficult, for even after the Jewish seller writes a sale deed to the non-Jew purchaser, the deed is in the non-Jew's possession, so why is the Jew reassured and not afraid the non-Jew will suppress it and say he never bought the field? Moreover, according to that reason, why would the Jew not rely upon the sale once the non-Jew paid the purchase money? According to my explanation everything is coherent, and it is clear in my opinion. We must still explain how Rambam knew to interpret the first part of the Gemara's statement, "a non-Jew has no chazakah except through a deed," as referring to an act of acquisition rather than, like the second part, to three years' possessory chazakah in the face of a challenge. In my opinion, the reason is that otherwise the Gemara's premise would be difficult. If it refers to an ordinary coercive non-Jew, how can it say he has no chazakah, implying that we remove the land from him even after three years, when in practice his coercive power would prevent us from removing it? If it refers to a non-Jew who submits to Jewish adjudication, why would three years' possession not establish his claim? The Jewish owner should have protested within the three years, and failure to protest should indicate that the non-Jew truly purchased the land. Therefore Rambam explains the first clause as speaking about acquisition: a non-Jew who comes to acquire land from a Jew does not acquire through chazakah. This is obvious to the Gemara for the reason given by the Maggid Mishneh. Since ordinary non-Jews may act coercively, if a non-Jew were allowed to acquire through an act of chazakah, he might at times take possession without the Jew's consent. We need not object that even if we deny legal acquisition through chazakah he may still seize the field by force, because we are not concerned that an ordinary non-Jew will simply come and rob land that was never before in his possession. The concern of coercion is stronger where he already has some hold over the land. Examine this carefully. We can now explain the details of the Mechaber's wording. What he writes, "A non-Jew does not acquire, etc., until 'he acquires only through a deed,'" is taken from Rambam in chapter 1 of Hilchot Mechirah. The Mechaber follows his own interpretation that the Gemara's phrase "a Jew who comes through a non-Jew" concerns acquisition. Therefore he writes, "and a Jew who buys from a non-Jew is like a non-Jew," paralleling that Gemara's language. I have already explained that the Maggid Mishneh does not understand it this way.
What he writes, "he does not acquire through chazakah." He mentions chazakah as the greater novelty, and all the more so payment alone does not acquire, because chazakah is a stronger acquisition than payment. As explained in simanim 190, 191, and 192, even in a place where money alone does not acquire, and a deed alone does not acquire, chazakah alone does acquire. For this reason Rama adds, "nor through payment." This is also evident from the conclusion, "only through a deed together with payment," which shows that payment alone does not acquire.
What he writes, "together with payment." See the Kesef Mishneh, who offers two explanations and regards as primary the view that even if payment preceded the writing of the deed, the acquisition is effective. See there.
What he writes, "and he acquires only through a deed," means together with payment, because he is no better than a Jew purchasing from another Jew, as explained above.
What he writes, "nevertheless, rental from a non-Jew is acquired through payment alone." Beit Yosef writes this at the end of siman 192, and Rama cites it in Darkei Moshe here according to Rashbam. Rashbam holds that a Jew buying from a non-Jew does not acquire without a deed because even a Jew buying from another Jew does not acquire without a deed. It follows that rental, for which people do not ordinarily write a deed, is acquired through payment without a deed. Rama holds that the same applies even according to the other explanations, which say that even in a place where a Jew buying from another Jew acquires without a deed, a Jew buying from a non-Jew does not acquire because he does not rely upon the transaction. Rental is different: since it is only for a fixed period, the Jewish renter does rely upon it once the non-Jew has received the rent.
