A non Jew does not acquire through chazakah. This concerns land. Movables are discussed at the end of this siman. This is the Rambam's language. The Sema writes that specifically a non Jew purchasing from a Jew does not acquire land through chazakah, because if such acquisition were recognized, the non Jew could exploit his power and retain the land against the Jew's will. A Jew purchasing from a non Jew, however, does acquire through chazakah. When the Rambam says that a Jew purchasing from a non Jew is like a non Jew, he is not discussing the act of acquisition at all, but the three year chazakah rule: the Jew cannot claim purchase without a document. The Taz concludes that the principal interpretation follows the Beit Yosef and Mechaber, that even a Jew purchasing from a non Jew does not acquire through chazakah alone, but requires a document together with payment:
Together with payment. This applies even if the money was paid before the document was written. Sema:
And he acquires only with a document. The Mishneh LaMelech writes that without a document even the non Jewish seller may retract under strict law. See the Beurim. As between the Jew and non Jew, however, there is a complete acquisition and neither can retract. The issue is only that the Jew has not yet acquired the land under Jewish law, so in the interim it is treated as ownerless:
However, rental. According to the rationale given by the authorities that without a document the property becomes ownerless because even a Jew purchasing from another Jew does not acquire with money alone where documents are customary, rental is clearly different, because a Jew renting from another Jew does acquire through money alone, and therefore rental from a non Jew likewise works without a document. Even according to the rationale that the Jewish purchaser does not rely on the transaction without a document because the non Jew may act coercively, a time limited rental gives the renter sufficient confidence. Sema. The Taz disagrees and holds that according to the lack of confidence rationale, even rental is not acquired without a document. See the Beurim. The principal ruling in rental follows the Rama. The reason is that the concept of property becoming ownerless does not apply to rental. So long as the tenant or those deriving rights from him have not entered, the landlord can still rent it to others, and therefore there is no period of ownerlessness, unlike a sale:
Likewise if he stipulated. The Bach, Shach, and Taz disagree and hold that a stipulation does not help. See the Beurim. Their view is primary. Even if he stipulated, the Jewish purchaser still does not fully rely on the transaction because the non Jew may act coercively:
