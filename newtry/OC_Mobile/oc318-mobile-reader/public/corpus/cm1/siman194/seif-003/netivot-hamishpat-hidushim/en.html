But with movables he acquires through payment. The Sema holds that a Jew buying movables from a gentile acquires through money without meshichah, while whether a gentile buying from a Jew acquires through money is disputed. The Shach objects that Yoreh Deah siman 320 shows that both directions are disputed between Rashi and Rabbeinu Tam. He therefore explains Rama as teaching only that with movables no deed is needed as with land, while according to many authorities meshichah is still required. A practical difference arises if the first buyer paid but before pulling the item another person seized it. According to the Shach, whether the second must reimburse the first depends upon the rationale in seif katan 10. If the first buyer's money merely gave him a collateral right, an ordinary creditor cannot seize movable property already transferred to another. But if reimbursement is required because the second benefited from the first payment, the second must reimburse even with movables. See Beurim: Ramban's responsum states that even according to Rabbeinu Tam, who holds that a Jew does not acquire a gentile's movables through money, if current gentile commercial practice treats money as transferring ownership, that custom is no worse than situmta and is effective. Rama's ruling is therefore correct. Regarding the Shach's practical case where another seized before the first buyer performed meshichah, the second must return the object to the first purchaser. Since the first did not acquire through money alone according to Rabbeinu Tam, the item never became ownerless. The second holds it as theft. The first purchaser at least has an acquisition according to Rashi, who says money acquires movables from a gentile, and all the more so may tell the gentile to recover the stolen item from the second holder.
