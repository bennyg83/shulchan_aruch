But with movable property, etc. See the Sema, seif katan 16, through his discussion of whether a non-Jew acquires from a Jew. His words appear to interpret this case as one in which money alone was given without meshichah, and to distinguish between a Jew purchasing from a non-Jew and a non-Jew purchasing from a Jew. This is certainly incorrect, as I wrote in seif katan 1. Nor can the present ruling concern payment alone, for the Rosh rules at the beginning of the second chapter of Bekhorot that meshichah effects acquisition with a non-Jew, not payment. Although in the first chapter of Bekhorot the Rosh writes that it is proper also to take Rashi's view into account, the matter certainly remains doubtful. The Tur and Rama themselves rule in Yoreh Deah, siman 320, that one should perform meshichah together with payment because perhaps payment does not acquire. How, then, could they rule here categorically that payment acquires? The practical difference is that if another person took possession of the movable property before meshichah, he would acquire it. Rather, the intent here is to contrast the opening ruling that land requires a document and money does not suffice. The present clause teaches that movable property does not require a document, and that the monetary mode of acquisition applicable to movable property suffices according to its law. Thus, according to the view that meshichah is required, payment without meshichah does not suffice. Examine this carefully.
