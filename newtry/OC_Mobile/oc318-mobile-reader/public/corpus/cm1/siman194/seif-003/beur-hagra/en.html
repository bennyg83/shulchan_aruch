And all this, etc. Tur writes this, and Rambam likewise rules in Hilkhot Zekhiyah that when a non-Jew sells movable property to a Jew, the non-Jew transfers it through drawing or through payment. Maggid Mishneh explains that drawing acquires according to the conclusion of Avodah Zarah 72a. Rambam understands the Gemara to mean that drawing also acquires, while money remains effective as well, and he does not follow the discussion in Bekhorot 13a that derives only one mode from the word 'your fellow.' This is also implied by the Avodah Zarah discussion concerning idolatrous wine, where advance payment suffices even according to the view that drawing acquires. Rambam likewise records Reish Lakish's statement in Hilkhot Bekhorot while ruling that drawing acquires from a non-Jew. In truth, the accepted law follows Rabbi Yochanan that drawing is rabbinic and Torah law recognizes money, as appears from many passages and rulings. Although Tosafot in Bekhorot reject this inference, the plain Gemara and the major authorities support it. Shulchan Aruch likewise rules in Yoreh Deah that drawing acquires from a non-Jew, while elsewhere indicating that money may also acquire. The authorities who disagree with Rambam concerning money nevertheless agree that drawing acquires; Rashi's contrary view was rejected. The requirement to use both methods in Yoreh Deah is merely a stringency designed to satisfy Rashi. Thus, according to Rambam and Tur, either method suffices. Rambam's statement in Hilkhot Meilah likewise follows his view that money suffices even in dealings with a non-Jew. Where he writes that purchase from a Jew does not create misuse of consecrated property without drawing, this follows the Avodah Zarah implication that once the Sages displaced monetary acquisition, it is ineffective even for a Torah-law consequence. Rambam's statement that a non-Jew does not acquire land from a Jew, or transfer it, except through a deed because the parties rely only upon the deed, shows that money is legally capable of acquiring, but reliance is lacking. This explains why Rambam in Hilkhot Mekhirah says only that a non-Jew does not acquire through possession, not that he does not acquire through money. Possession is legally unavailable, whereas money fails because the parties do not rely upon it. Accordingly, Rama's rulings concerning rental and an express condition are correct even according to Rambam. Other authorities hold that possession too fails only because of lack of reliance. In summary, the authorities generally reject Rashbam and agree with Rambam that land is not acquired or transferred between Jew and non-Jew through money or possession. According to Rambam, possession is legally ineffective, while money fails because of lack of reliance; according to the other authorities, both failures arise from lack of reliance. Regarding movable property, all agree that drawing acquires except Rashi, whose position was rejected. Rambam also recognizes money, while the other authorities do not. Shulchan Aruch generally follows Rambam.
