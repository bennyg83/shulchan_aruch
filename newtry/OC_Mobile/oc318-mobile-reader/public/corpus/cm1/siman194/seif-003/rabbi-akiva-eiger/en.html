[Rama] He is called a moser. Note: This wording appears in Tosafos and the Rosh. I have not merited to understand it. A moser is liable under the law of indirect damage, and according to many authorities such liability is rabbinic, whereas here he is an actual robber, because he holds land that another Jew acquired. He is therefore stealing land through the agency of a gentile. This requires great study. It is even more difficult for me that Tosafos write that the first Jew is permitted to demand his money from the gentile even though, as a result, the gentile may take the land back from the present possessor, because the first Jew is lawfully demanding payment. This indicates that the first Jew cannot demand his money from the second Jew, only from the gentile. Tosafos then write that if the possessor is willing to pay the first Jew his money, but the first Jew nevertheless causes the gentile to dispossess him, the first Jew appears to be an actual moser. Yet even where the first Jew does not appear and merely demands his money from the gentile, and the gentile independently takes the land from the second Jew and transfers it to the first, the first Jew may not retain the land and must return it to the second. The land belongs to the second Jew. This is like a gentile who stole land and then gave that land to a Jew in payment of a debt, where it is obvious that the Jew must return the land to the person from whom it was stolen. This requires great study.
