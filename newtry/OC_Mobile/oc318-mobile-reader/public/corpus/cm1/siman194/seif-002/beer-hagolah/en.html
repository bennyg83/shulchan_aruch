However, if the first purchaser took possession after paying, even before the deed reached him, and afterward a second person took possession, the land remains with the first purchaser. His possession, supported by payment of money, is no weaker than the second person's possession without payment. Rabbeinu Shimshon and Rabbeinu Chananel likewise write so.
Maggid Mishneh in the name of Raavad and Rashba. Tur writes in the name of his father the Rosh that if the first purchaser is prudent, immediately after paying he should take possession with the intent to acquire ownerless property. Sema expresses surprise that the Mechaber and Rama omitted this.
There in Rambam. Maggid Mishneh writes that Rav Hai Gaon states this in Shaar 14 of Sefer HaMekach VeHaMimkar, and Ramban likewise writes that since the first purchaser's money removed the gentile's ownership, that money effectively enriched the second purchaser who later acquires.
