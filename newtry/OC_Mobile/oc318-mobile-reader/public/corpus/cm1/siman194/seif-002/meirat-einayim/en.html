In the manner one takes possession of the property of a convert, as in siman 192 and siman 275. Meaning, this property too is ownerless like the property of a convert. There it is explained that certain acts can acquire a field from a seller but do not acquire ownerless property until the acquirer does something that benefits the land itself and clearly appears to have been done for the purpose of taking possession. For this reason the Mechaber carefully writes, "in the manner one takes possession of the property of a convert." This is clear.
The latter acquires. The reason is as the Mechaber concludes, because from the moment the non-Jew received, etc.
Even if the first purchaser took possession, etc. Meaning, even if he took possession after paying the non-Jew, he still did not rely upon that chazakah to acquire until the non-Jew writes him a deed. The second person, however, did not pay money and therefore presumably intends to acquire through his act of possession. Since he paid nothing, he loses nothing if the non-Jew later removes the property from him, so there is no reason his mind should not rely upon the chazakah. The Maggid Mishneh writes similarly. See the Perishah. The Tur writes in the name of the Rosh that if the first purchaser is clever, after paying the money he should take possession as ownerless property before another person precedes him. I wrote in the Perishah that in my opinion even Rashba, who says that money plus ordinary chazakah is ineffective, would agree in this case because the first purchaser took possession specifically as ownerless property. It is surprising that the Mechaber and Rama omitted this.
But if he took possession, etc. He acquires according to everyone. I already wrote that the Maggid Mishneh says this at the end of chapter 1 of Hilchot Zechiyah.
And he reimburses the first purchaser for the money. This is the view of Rambam and those who follow him. Their reason is that although the first purchaser did not acquire the land, the property is considered held for his money like collateral until he is repaid. The Rosh and Tur, however, hold that the second purchaser need not reimburse him, because the property did not initially come into the first purchaser's hand as collateral. See the Perishah.
And responsibility remains upon the non-Jew. Meaning, even after the second person has reimbursed the first purchaser, the second does not assume responsibility toward him. Accordingly, if the non-Jew later comes and removes the field from the second possessor, the first purchaser need not return the money he received. This is how Beit Yosef and Darkei Moshe explain the view of the Maggid Mishneh and Nimukei Yosef there; see there.
And the second person is called wicked. Beit Yosef writes that these matters depend on one another. Since the possessor must reimburse the first purchaser's money, the situation is not like a found object or pure ownerless property; it resembles a poor person pursuing a cake that another takes, in which case the latter is called wicked. If he did not have to reimburse the money, it would be like a found object, to which the law of one pursuing a cake does not apply, as written in siman 237. See the Perishah.
He is called a moser. The reason is that since by law the possessor acquired the property, it is forbidden to remove it from him and nullify his right through a non-Jewish authority.
Since the second gives him his money. Tosafos and Hagahot Ashri write this; see there. All the more so if he already gave the money, for then the second would lose it, as explained. The Rosh, however, holds that the possessor need not reimburse the first purchaser, and nevertheless the first purchaser may not complain to the non-Jew. If he does complain and as a result the non-Jew removes the field from the possessor, he is called a moser. The Tur cites this in the Rosh's name; see there.
A non-Jew who had collateral or a deposit, etc. Until the words, "and gave it to Shimon by deed." With a deposit, the plain meaning is that he gave it to Shimon as a gift. With collateral, one must explain that he wrote a deed of gift concerning the excess value, meaning that Shimon is to redeem the collateral from Reuven and whatever the collateral is worth above the redemption amount belongs to Shimon. This is the meaning of the statement that once the non-Jew gives it to Shimon he has removed himself, etc., namely, he gave Shimon power to stand in his place. Here Rama does not state that the non-Jew died, and Darkei Moshe likewise does not say so. At the end of siman 72, seif 40, he writes this law in a case where the non-Jew died, and Darkei Moshe there says the same in the name of Responsa Rashba. It appears that this detail was omitted here because according to the reason given here, that the non-Jew has removed himself, the same rule applies even if he is alive. The earlier source mentioned death only because that is the usual circumstance. If the non-Jew is still alive, he would generally demand that Reuven release the collateral. Certainly, if the non-Jew asks Shimon whether he received the collateral or deposit from Reuven, it is permitted according to everyone for Shimon to tell him that Reuven did not give it to him, since it came to Reuven without payment like a found object. See what I wrote there, and see above siman 86 in the first and last glosses of that siman.
