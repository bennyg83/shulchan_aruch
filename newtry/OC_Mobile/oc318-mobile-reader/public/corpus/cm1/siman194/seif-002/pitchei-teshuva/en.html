See Baer Heitev and Responsa Knesset Yechezkel, siman 93, who writes that in his view even Beit Yosef and Rama, who hold that an express stipulation can be effective, agree that the purchaser does not acquire until the deed has been written. Only then is his intent settled, and the acquisition takes effect retroactively from the time the money was given. Thus, even if another Jew took possession in the interim, the first purchaser acquires. In the case before him, however, the issue arose because a Jew purchasing houses was not permitted to register them in his own name and therefore registered them in the name of a gentile. Since no deed was ever written to be delivered to the Jewish purchaser, even Beit Yosef and Rama would agree that the first purchaser did not acquire, and the property is not regarded as Jewish owned land. Moreover, the plain meaning of Beit Yosef and Rama, who say that a stipulation with the gentile is effective if he says, "If I wish, the money shall acquire," is that such a rule applies only where there are witnesses that he actually made this stipulation with the gentile.
