Likewise if he stipulated. Rama rules elsewhere that movables can be acquired by money where the parties expressly stipulated that money should effect acquisition. The Shach there disagrees and argues that a condition cannot create a mode of acquisition where halachah does not recognize one. Here, however, even the Shach has reason to agree. The reason money does not acquire from a gentile in this context is not that money is intrinsically incapable of acquisition, but that the parties do not rely conclusively on it. Once they explicitly stipulate that money should acquire, their intent becomes settled, similar to the cases where parties customarily write a deed and nevertheless stipulate that money alone should complete the sale. Taz and Bach disagree even here and say the condition does not help. According to what I wrote in siman 198, however, an express condition can make money effective even for movables where the ordinary rabbinic rule requires meshichah. All the more here, where the obstacle is primarily lack of settled intent, the condition should work.
