One who obligates himself to support, etc. Yerushalmi there: one who undertook to support his daughter in law, etc., the law is with her. This is also evident from the Mishnah: the first husband may not say, etc., and likewise both of them may not say, etc.; if she married, etc.
And if he stipulated with him, etc. Ketubot 103a: if she says, "I do not wish to move from my father's house," they may, etc.; this supports Rav Huna, and "let them give her," etc. The same applies here. All of this is because he wrote to her, "You shall sit in my house and be supported," etc., as stated there on 53a.
(Collected note) One who obligates, etc.; and if, etc. See Hagahot Ashri, chapter 12 of Ketubot, seif 6, s.v. "and the Gemara asks," etc., and the Rosh there, seif 1: "It is reasonable that they give her," etc.; this is unlike a widow, etc.
Nevertheless, if there is no. There: "And if she claimed," etc.
And if she became ill. The Yerushalmi there says that if she became ill it is as though she married, meaning that the husband is obligated for her medical treatment, while this obligor gives her only the value of her food. See the Rosh there.
And some say that one who undertakes. Gittin 75b: "Do we require," etc.; Rava said, there is no difficulty, etc. See Rashi there, s.v. "how much," etc. The Rosh and Rambam rule like Rava. The same applies to one who undertakes to support, etc. Even according to Rav Ashi one can distinguish that his ruling applies specifically to a get, as stated there in Rav Chisda's answer that the Mishnah follows Rabban Shimon ben Gamliel and the baraita the Rabbis. See Tosafot there, s.v. "the Mishnah," etc.: "since he distressed her," etc., because his purpose was not to provide her benefit, and therefore Rav Ashi said, etc., without qualification, since we rule like the Rabbis. In other cases, however, his intent is to provide benefit, as stated there on 74b, where Rabbah said to him, "I," etc.; even Rav Ashi would agree. Ran. There is a further proof from Nedarim 28a: once he says, "They shall be prohibited," they become prohibited, etc., where he says "today," etc.; see the Ran there.
(Collected note) Some say, etc. Nedarim 19a.
And some say that if. The holder of a document has the weaker hand. A proof is from Menachot 104b: one who vows "a minchah" or "types of menachot" brings two, and likewise throughout the chapter Get Pashut and elsewhere we say that the holder of the document has the weaker hand. This is unlike Rava's case in Gittin above, because there his intention is to cause her distress, so his wording is construed stringently, as the Gemara there states regarding the Mishnah and the Rabbis, etc.; see there on 74b.
(Collected note) And some say that if, etc. The Rama likewise rules without qualification at the end of siman 163 and at the beginning of siman 212. At the end of siman 163 he writes "one year" where collection for the entire year is made at one time. So too here, where the obligation was fixed in years, unlike at the beginning of siman 212. The first view appears primary, as I wrote from the passages in Gittin and Nedarim. The ruling at the end of siman 163 is from the responsum of the Rosh cited here. As for the beginning of siman 212, according to one explanation the phrase "he shall reside" is ineffective because perhaps he meant only one hour. Rashi and the other poskim, however, explain it for another reason, and the Rambam explains that the subject of the obligation lacks tangible substance, as stated there in the Shulchan Aruch.
One who undertakes to support. Ketubot 103a: "She may use the dwelling in the manner she used it," etc. The reason is that she rises with him to his social standard. This applies even after his death because he wrote to her, "and you," etc., as stated there: "for thus he wrote to her," etc. This implies according to the honor due her during his lifetime, as in 48a concerning one who went overseas, etc., "according to his honor," etc.; the novelty there is that she rises with him, etc.
But clothing is not, etc. There, 68a: "No, both this and that," etc.; "this," etc.
Reuven who undertook. Yerushalmi to Ketubot there: if she died, she has already died.
And even if he fixed. This is because a husband does not inherit a debt owed to his wife, as stated in Bava Batra 125b: Rav Pappa said, the halachah is that the husband does not, etc., and the firstborn does not, etc. See Tosafot there, s.v. "Rav Pappa said." The same principle applies that he does not, etc., and Tosafot there prove it from Bava Kamma 42b; see there.
