And some say that if he undertook generally to support him, or to give him one hundred gold coins per year, he is discharged after one year. See the Rosh's responsum concerning such a case. He explains that the burden of proof rests on the one seeking extraction. For the same reason, if a community generally exempts an individual from tax and later retracts, arguing that no time was fixed for the exemption, the law favors the community. Since the individual was originally obligated to pay tax with them and claims they exempted him, they may say they exempted him only for a limited period. Rama writes at the end of siman 163 that they may say the exemption was only for one year. The Rosh brings several proofs, including that even regarding an obligation to Heaven, one who vows a gift may discharge himself with the smallest gift ordinarily brought of that type. From Rama's presentation of two consecutive 'some say' clauses it follows that the first view, that an undertaking to support another generally means for the person's entire life, is Rashba's view, while the second, that one year suffices, is the Rosh's. This is also clear from Beit Yosef and Darkei Moshe here and in Even HaEzer 114. It is unlike Ir Shushan, who says the views do not disagree and that the second rule applies only because the undertaking said 'or give one hundred gold coins per year.' The Rosh's examples of tax exemption and a vow were also unstated as to duration, yet he says an unspecified undertaking is interpreted minimally. He also cites the dispute of Rava and Rav Ashi, whether an unspecified term means forever or can mean even one day, with authorities on both sides. The Hagahot Mordechai rule, cited by Rama below in siman 212:1, that one who says, 'So-and-so may live in my house' without specifying time can satisfy it with even one hour, follows the same principle: when no duration is fixed, the obligor can say he intended only the minimum period, and the burden of proof rests on the claimant. Here Rama's requirement of at least one year follows from the fact that the person revealed an intention to support for a year by saying alternatively, 'or give him one hundred gold coins per year.' We thus learn that the first view and the Rosh disagree very broadly: one requires lifetime support from a general undertaking, while the Rosh may allow even one hour absent some indication of a longer term. As to Rama's ruling at the end of siman 163 that a tax exemption is for one year, that cannot be universal. The Rosh merely says the community can claim it was for 'some period.' If an unspecified period necessarily meant at least one year, why does Rama rule regarding residence that one hour suffices? Rather, Rama in siman 163 speaks of a type of tax assessed once yearly, or a place where the entire year's tax is collected in one payment. Then the smallest meaningful unit is one year. This is the correct reconciliation of Rama's rulings here, in siman 163, and at the beginning of siman 212. It is unfortunate that he did not explain this distinction explicitly in at least one of those places. See siman 212, where I give another reconciliation, though this one appears stronger.
