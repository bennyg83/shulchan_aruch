(Netivot HaMishpat, seif katan 11) For this reason Baal HaTerumot wrote in gate 47. Note: This does not appear there in Baal HaTerumot. His principal intention is to prove that the money is not a deposit but a loan, since it was given to be spent, and therefore it has the status of purchase money where a robber later purchases the property from the original owners. See there. He concludes that if the robber says to the purchaser, "come and take your money," the purchaser must accept it. This seems at first glance to contradict his earlier words. In any event, Baal HaTerumot's intention is not that there was a sale or acquisition of the land itself by virtue of an acquisition from the robber.
