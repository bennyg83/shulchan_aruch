Mishneh LaMelech, chapter 11 of Hilchos Mechirah, writes that Peri Megadim, part II, siman 30, cites Responsa Bnei Shmuel as explaining that the Rambam's view applies only when one obligates himself gratuitously in an unquantified amount. If he gives something in exchange for the obligation, then even though the amount is unquantified, the Rambam agrees that the obligation takes effect, because in consideration of the money he fully resolves to obligate himself. The Rambam spoke only of a gratuitous undertaking. This resolves the Ramban's question from the sugya in HaNizakin. Mishneh LaMelech questions this distinction because the Ritva, at the beginning of the chapter HaNoseh, proves that the Rambam does not distinguish in this way. See there. See also Tumim and Ketzos HaChoshen. Responsa Beit Shmuel Acharon, siman 66, writes that the primary law follows the Shach, that the party in possession cannot say, 'I rely on the Rambam,' in this matter. Although the work Get Pashut, in its concluding rules, writes that one may rely on the Rambam, since it does not cite or consider the Shach's words, we follow the Shach. Moreover, in the actual case addressed there, even the Rambam would agree, in accordance with Mishneh LaMelech's citation from Peri Megadim, that his ruling concerns only a gratuitous gift. Although Mishneh LaMelech rejected that distinction, Urim VeTumim writes that all the sages of Prague agreed with it. Therefore, in that case, where the person paid a fee for the agency, he can obligate himself even according to the Rambam. End of quote. Responsa Beit Ephraim, Choshen Mishpat, siman 75, writes that all batei din in Israel ruled that a person who undertakes an unquantified obligation becomes liable. See there.
