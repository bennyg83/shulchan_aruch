See Beurim, that he must pay an amount corresponding to the proper value of the beneficiary's sustenance. If one obligated himself to provide sustenance for his daughter or daughter in law and she gives birth, he must provide throughout the period of childbirth the foods appropriate for her according to local custom, Responsa Shevut Yaakov:
The household. Meaning, when many people eat together the total expense is lower than if each ate separately. The beneficiary therefore receives only his proportional share of what his food would have cost within the household. The assessment is according to the quality of food appropriate for the obligor. The beneficiary also cannot force the obligor to pay cash. The obligor may provide actual food. Even if the separation occurs because the obligor himself gives the beneficiary all of his meals separately, he may still provide the food itself rather than money. See Beurim, seif katan 6:

Healthy. Since the woman does not receive support by virtue of the ordinary conditions of the ketubah, she has no separate right to medical expenses even where those expenses are not fixed. As long as he needs. Perhaps the meaning is that if the vow was made when the beneficiary was in need, the intent was only for as long as he remains in need. If the vow was made when he did not need support, the intent was perpetual, Sema:
One year from his vow. In siman 212, regarding one who says that a particular person may dwell in a house, the obligation is satisfied by one hour. The difference here is that the obligor revealed an intention that the arrangement relate to a year when he said, alternatively, that he would give one hundred gold coins for the year. He fixed a limit. If he fixed the obligation generally for a certain period, the obligation terminates upon the beneficiary's death and nothing is owed to the heirs. If he stipulated the reverse, payment is owed to the heirs, although he may be exempt from payment to the beneficiary's husband, Shach.
