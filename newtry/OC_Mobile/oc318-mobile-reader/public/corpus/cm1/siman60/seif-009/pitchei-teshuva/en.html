And even if he fixed a sum. See Baer Heitev, who explains that where a sum was fixed the obligor must pay it to her heirs. Netivot HaMishpat reconciles the Rama by saying that the Rama speaks where both a sum and a maintenance period were fixed, for example, 'I obligate myself in two hundred zuz for five years of maintenance.' If the recipient dies within that period, the obligor is certainly exempt even toward the heirs, as Chelkat Mechokek writes in Even HaEzer. Responsa VeShav HaKohen, siman 66, makes the same distinction. The Rama's opening case, one who undertakes to support Shimon and his wife, plainly refers to a fixed period, and therefore his later statement, 'even if he fixed a sum,' should be read as fixing a sum for maintenance during that period. VeShav HaKohen nevertheless remains uncertain about a case where no time was fixed but the obligor said that he would give a certain sum for her maintenance and she died. Perhaps the Rashba's decisive factor was that the obligation was assumed 'from now.' If he did not say 'from now,' then even without setting a payment date one could argue that the obligation was not to hand over the entire sum immediately but to provide it incrementally as maintenance became necessary. If she died, the remaining obligation might therefore cease. See siman 253, seif 15. VeShav HaKohen also discusses a case where someone obligated himself to give a fixed annual amount to certain orphans and one of them died during the period. He analyzes whether the deceased orphan's share remains payable to the heirs or whether the obligation was personal to the support purpose. See there for the details.
