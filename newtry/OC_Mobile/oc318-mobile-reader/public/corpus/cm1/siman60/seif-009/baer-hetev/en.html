He does not. Maharshal writes that the person must explain his refusal. Otherwise he may be compelled under the rule against acting with the trait of Sodom. See below, siman 184, seif 2, and Responsa Maharashach, vol. 2, siman 174. Shach.
To the witnesses. The Sema writes that the same applies if he said this directly to the borrower, even without fully explaining himself. The text speaks in terms of telling the witnesses because ordinarily, when speaking before witnesses, a person does not explain that he is using another person's name merely to avoid appearing wealthy. Otherwise, why would he have initially arranged that the document be written in Shimon's name? When speaking privately to the borrower, who knows the true ownership of the money, he would ordinarily explain that a new document should later be written in his own name. Thus the Sema.
The community. The Sema writes that the Mechaber was overly brief where greater explanation was needed. The Rosh's responsum shows that his ruling concerned a community whose custom was to write documents concerning communal revenues in the name of an individual, so that communal affairs would not be managed like a pot belonging to many partners, or for another known reason. Without such a custom, the individual would be believed to say the money was his and that he wrote at the end that it belonged to the community only to avoid appearing wealthy. This differs from the Ir Shushan, who reasoned independently that an individual does not ordinarily attribute his own money to the community merely to avoid appearing wealthy. The Shach agrees with the Ir Shushan and writes that the Rosh mentioned the communal custom only as additional support. See there.
