We do not compel. He must give a reason, because otherwise beit din can compel him to avoid conduct characteristic of Sodom. If he says, "Since you wrote it in my name, I acquired it," that claim has no substance where the lender instructed that the document be delivered to himself. This concerns a loan document. With a sale document, if when paying the purchase money he said he was buying for his fellow, the fellow acquires. If he bought without specification and only afterward instructed that the deed be written in his fellow's name, the law parallels a loan document: if he instructed that the deed be given to the fellow, the fellow acquires, but if he instructed that it be given to himself, the fellow does not acquire. Reuven is believed to say that a document held by him and written in Shimon's name actually belongs to him only where there are witnesses that Reuven supplied the loan money. If the borrower admits it, Reuven is not believed once the document was established in beit din.
To the witnesses. The same applies if he told the borrower. In the latter case, where he says, "Write me another document," compelling him means placing him under ban until he obeys beit din and instructs the witnesses to write. The new document is dated from the second date. The witnesses themselves may not write without authorization from beit din. In a sale, as explained in siman 184, he need only notify them that he bought for himself and need not expressly condition that another deed be written.
The community. The Sema limits this to a place where the custom was to record communal income in an individual's name. Otherwise the individual is believed to say that he wrote it in the community's name merely so as not to appear wealthy. The Shach disagrees: even where there is no such custom, he cannot say that, because an individual does not ordinarily write property in the community's name for that purpose.
