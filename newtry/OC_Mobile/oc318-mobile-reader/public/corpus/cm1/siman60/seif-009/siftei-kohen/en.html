A document that was written in Reuven's name, etc. The Sema, seif katan 28, writes that the congregation's practice was to have such documents written in the name of an individual, and therefore the law favors the congregation. He says this is evident from the responsum of the Rosh and challenges the Levush. In my view, the Levush is correct. The Sema did not quote the responsum of the Rosh precisely. One who studies it will see that the Rosh's principal reason is that the document concludes, 'These funds are proceeds of the congregation's lease.' The Rosh's statement that this was the congregation's custom may have been added merely for greater clarity. This is plainly also the Mechaber's understanding, for he omitted that circumstance both in the Beit Yosef and here in the Shulchan Aruch. The law itself also supports this. Since the Rosh rules that the individual is not believed because he has no written transfer and delivery, once the document itself states that the funds are the congregation's lease proceeds, it is proven that the document belongs to the congregation. How can the individual claim it as his own without written transfer and delivery? As for the question raised by the Levush and Sema, why he cannot say that he wrote this merely so as not to appear wealthy, I have written that the case differs from that below in siman 65, seif 22. Here the declaration appears in the document itself before the witnesses' signatures, which certainly means it was written when the document was originally executed. A person does not go to such lengths merely to avoid appearing wealthy. Below in siman 65, by contrast, the case concerns a statement he later wrote in his own handwriting after the document had already been signed. This appears correct to me.
And at its end, before the witnesses' signatures, it is written: 'And these,' etc. This is the correct reading. So writes the Sema, and so it appears explicitly in the responsum of the Rosh. Even if these words were not included in the Mechaber's text, this would still be its meaning. This is simple.
