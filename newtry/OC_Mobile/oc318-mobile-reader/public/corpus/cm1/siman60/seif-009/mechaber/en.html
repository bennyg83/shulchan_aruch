If Reuven lent Levi one hundred zuz and at the time instructed that the witnesses write the document in Shimon's name but deliver it to Reuven, and when payment became due Levi told Reuven, 'You are not my opposing party,' Reuven prevails. If Reuven asks Shimon to execute a deed transferring the document written in Shimon's name, Shimon is not compelled; even if he agreed to do so, he may retract. If Reuven stipulated with the borrower at the time of the loan, 'I lend you on condition that you make a document for me in Shimon's name and, whenever I wish, restore it to my name,' the borrower is compelled to do so. Even if Reuven merely said to the witnesses in the borrower's presence, 'Write me another document for this loan,' the borrower must write another in Reuven's name. If a document was written in Reuven's name but stated at its end, before the witnesses' signatures, 'This money comes from the community's lease revenues,' and Reuven claims it is his because the community gave him the document for money it owed him, while the community denies this, the community prevails.
