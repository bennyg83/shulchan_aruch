Reuven lent... Bava Kamma 102b, where Rav Sheshet says, "This is what it means..." It follows that acquisition is effective, etc. The law does not follow the scholars of Eretz Yisrael, and the purchaser acquires in every case. The same applies here to the lender.
And if he sued... There, where Rav Sheshet says that we do not compel, etc.
Even if he admitted... There: "Rather, say the latter clause, 'on condition...'" The Ra'avad explains that the purchaser said to the Exilarch, "on condition," and the latter admitted it, yet the Gemara still asks that he can say to him, etc. See Beer HaGolah, for according to Rashi it is difficult why one would initially think to compel the Exilarch.
But if... even... There, 103a.
A document that was... Bava Batra 173a, in the baraisa concerning one of the brothers, etc. Even according to Rava there, the same applies here, since the document had been deposited in his possession.
(Collected note) Before the witnesses signed. So it appears there, because the body of the document was written in their names and witnesses signed upon it. This is clear not only according to the Rama in siman 81, seif 15, but even according to the Shulchan Aruch there, who distinguishes only where he said, "You are my witnesses," and the other remained silent; where he answered yes it is different, and all the more so here. But after the witnesses signed, even a handwritten statement is ineffective, as explained there in seifim 17 and 18 and in siman 65, seif 22. See the Shach. (End of note.)
