One who comes to collect his debt by means of a document, etc. He may collect from encumbered property. Bava Batra, Mishnah 175a: "One who lends," etc.
And he did not manage. Gittin, Mishnah 48b: "One does not collect," etc.
But land, etc. Bava Batra 44b: "Let us be concerned that perhaps he later acquired," etc. The response implies that without an express clause, after acquired property is not included.
And if he wrote in the document. This is the conclusion there on 157b: "If you say that after acquired property becomes encumbered," etc.
But movable property, etc., even. There, 44b: "Movables for a creditor," etc.
(Collected note) Temporarily. Meaning, even if he transferred them only temporarily. This is compelled by the statement in Bava Batra there concerning one who designated his slave as an apotiki, etc. It must mean only temporarily, for otherwise the sale is wholly ineffective according to the Rambam, as written in siman 117, seif 1. If so, the same applies when he designated his ox as an apotiki, etc., even in such a case.
And if it is written, etc.; and if he wrote, etc. There.
And nowadays, etc. Even according to the custom, etc. Responsum of the Rosh. This accords with Bava Metzia 31a to 31b and what the Rosh writes there in the name of the Yerushalmi. The concept of the market protection enactment appears in Bava Kamma 115a; see there. Regarding what was written concerning the sale of a promissory note, etc., that it is uncommon, the Gemara there discusses a sale, etc., of something worth one hundred for two hundred. Rabbi Shimon says that the market enactment was not made for such a case. The reason is that it is uncommon. Although the halachah there was decided against him, that is because the other view holds that this too is common, as stated in Bava Metzia 108a concerning one who bought for two hundred, etc.; see there.
(Collected note) And nowadays, etc. See the Shach, who disagrees.
And likewise if he gave. This follows from Ketubot 79a concerning a document of evasion, etc. See the Rosh there, seif 3, who writes that Rabbi Shimon says, etc., and "it does not appear to me," etc.; see there.
