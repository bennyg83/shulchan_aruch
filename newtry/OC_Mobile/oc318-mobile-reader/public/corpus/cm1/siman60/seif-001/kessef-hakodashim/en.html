Seif 3, gloss. All of the support expenses. Where a person wrote that he would support a married couple at his table and a quarrel arose between them because of the provider, he must pay the full cost of food and lodging, not merely the reduced amount represented by the rule of "the blessing of the house." If he made similar commitments to two couples and the couples themselves quarrel, logic suggests that the second couple should yield to the first. Although movable-property creditors ordinarily have no priority over one another, here the central benefit is the physical place to live, which is comparable to real property. Perhaps one could say that an unavoidable circumstance exempts the provider, or that he could not reasonably have anticipated the need to stipulate for such a quarrel. Where neither side should have anticipated the circumstance, the burden of proof rests on the claimant. It may therefore be that the commitment to support them specifically at his table applies only while peace exists, whereas after a quarrel he need pay the second couple only the amount of the household benefit. If the two couples later reconcile but the first nevertheless excludes the second, perhaps the second likewise receives only that reduced amount. If both commitments were written on the same day, there may be room to apply a principle resembling "buy or sell," requiring one party to resolve the indivisible use right. The matter requires further study.
