The market. Even if the property later comes into the creditor's possession after the sale, the purchaser already acquired it, Shach. If a woman borrowed and then married, although we rule that the husband has the status of a purchaser, the market protection enactment does not apply to him, Shach. If movable property was transferred only through maamad shloshtan, the Alshich, cited by the Shach at the end of siman 126, holds that the market protection enactment does not apply. The Shach disagrees. The Tumim agrees that it does not apply, because maamad shloshtan is itself only a rabbinic enactment and the Sages do not ordinarily create a second enactment upon an enactment. A deathbed gift was given the force of inheritance by the Sages, and therefore even an oral creditor may collect from it. If a later creditor collected before an earlier creditor whose lien attached through agav of land, the earlier creditor may recover from him. With documents. The Rashba in a responsum disagrees and holds that debt documents are not encumbered through agav at all. Another practical difference concerns one who first sells a debt document through agav of land or another effective kinyan and then sells it again to a second purchaser.
