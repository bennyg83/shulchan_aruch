(Netivot HaMishpat, seif katan 14) For here the coins are not present. Note: This is the Shach's language: "unlike here, where the coins are not present." At first glance it is very surprising. How can the Shach simply assume that the coins are not present? He should have written that the case is one where the coins are not present. It therefore appears that the Shach means as follows. Where a person conveys one hundred gold coins and the document states that all his property is encumbered for collection, even if those very coins were present and initially acquired by the recipient, the recipient thereafter left them with the donor to be spent. The donor therefore undertook to repay when the holder of the document demanded payment, and encumbered his property for collection. Thus even if one hundred gold coins are now found in the donor's possession, we do not adjudicate that those very coins must be given to the recipient. The original coins were converted into a debt, which the donor may satisfy even with equivalent property. Since the litigation is not over these specific coins, we do not apply the principle "here they were found, here they were." This differs from simanim 112 and 250, where the litigation concerns the very property presently found, and if we apply "here found, here existing," the property itself belongs to the recipient. Even if this was not the Shach's intent, it appears to me to be a sound distinction. There is some support from Responsa Mahari Weil, siman 112, in the laws of chazakah. Independently, if the recipient left the hundred coins with the donor to spend and replace, then certainly "here found, here existing" no longer applies, because even if the original coins were present at the time of the conveyance, the donor was permitted to spend them and repay other coins.
