Warranty. The Shach writes that although the Beit Yosef cites Ramban that one who accepted warranty must maintain the property in the recipient's possession, one must say that Ramban refers to a case where the person explicitly obligated himself to do so. Thus, even if the acquisition itself is ineffective, the personal obligation still takes effect. Shach.
Pledge. The Mordechai writes that many Geonim disagree and hold that the pledge should not be returned until the other party gives the promised gift. Darkei Moshe cites this. The Shach expresses surprise that Rama rules here without noting any dissent. See below, siman 207, seif 11, and siman 333, seif 1.
Liable. This liability permits collection only from unencumbered property. Even if he writes a document, it does not permit collection from purchasers where the obligated item was not yet in existence at the time of writing, even if the obligation is fixed, such as "this house" or "this amount that I will inherit from my father." Since the property did not yet exist when the document was written, no public notice of a lien arose. This follows Rashi and Tosafot. Rambam and those who follow him appear to hold that a fixed obligation created by document can be collected from encumbered property even where the subject did not yet exist. See Responsa Mahara Sasson, siman 23. Shach.
That they made a kinyan. The Shach writes that this requirement is needed only according to the opinion that a person cannot voluntarily create a new obligation unless a kinyan is made, and that saying "You are my witnesses" is insufficient. This creates difficulty for the Mechaber, who at the beginning of siman 40 rules like Rambam that saying "You are my witnesses" is sufficient, while here he requires a kinyan. Perhaps the Mechaber holds that with something not yet in existence or not presently in one's possession, even "You are my witnesses" is ineffective according to all opinions, but the Shach says he does not find this distinction convincing and leaves the matter requiring study. The Sema writes that even according to those who hold that a kinyan to "give" something in the future is only a kinyan on words and does not acquire, as Rama writes in siman 245, seif 1, here the person obligated himself personally to give it, and that obligation is effective according to all opinions.
The claimant. Rama writes similarly in siman 112, seif 3. There the Mechaber records a dispute. At the end of siman 250 the Mechaber rules without qualification that the heirs must prove the deceased did not own the property at the time of death, which appears inconsistent with what he writes here and in siman 112. The Sema reconciles the sources by saying that here the defendant claims with certainty that the relevant money did not exist at the time of the transfer, so the burden of proof rests on the claimant. At the end of siman 250, however, the heirs merely say perhaps the deceased acquired it later, so we follow the claimant's certain assertion and apply the presumption, "Here it is found, here it was." Although siman 112 also concerns heirs, who generally cannot know their father's affairs with certainty, there the property is land. The presumption "Here it is found, here it was" does not apply in the same way to land, which remains physically in its place even as ownership changes. With movables presently found in the owner's house, however, we say that since they are found here now, they were here at the time of the gift. In the present case, even though it too concerns movables, the defendant's certain claim shifts the burden back to the claimant. The Shach offers a different answer: in siman 250 the movables themselves remain identifiable and present, so we say that since they are now found here they were here previously. Here, however, the particular coins no longer exist. As for siman 112, the Shach concludes that because those words are attributed to the Geonim and we rule there against the Geonim, we likewise should not follow the rule at the end of siman 250. The Bach also writes that the poskim contradict one another and that the burden always rests on the recipient to prove the property existed. This is primary. The Taz writes that in simanim 250 and 281 he demonstrated that there are differing opinions and discusses the practical ruling there. See Responsa Maharashach, part 2, siman 9; Rashdam, siman 309; Responsa Rama, siman 92; and Responsa Maharam of Lublin, siman 108.
