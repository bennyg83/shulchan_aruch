This applies where he transferred it with language of sale or gift. In Derishah I inferred from the phrase 'with language of' that everything depends on the formulation. Therefore, if he writes, 'I hereby obligate myself to give you,' etc., even though this is a gift and concerns an item not presently in his possession, the obligation attaches to his own person, which presently exists. I brought further proof there. Even according to those who hold that if a kinyan is made upon a promise 'to give' or 'I will give,' nothing is acquired because it is merely a kinyan on words, as Rama writes below in siman 245, seif 1, here everyone agrees because he obligated himself personally to give. This is also implied by Rama's gloss nearby concerning a document that says, 'because I gave,' where he writes: 'but he did not encumber himself by becoming obligated.' This implies that had he personally undertaken the obligation, he would be liable even with the wording 'I gave,' although according to that view 'I gave' is like 'I will give.' See Derishah and understand carefully.
Even if he gave him collateral, it is nothing, etc. The Tur explains that whenever the underlying acquisition does not take effect except through the collateral, the collateral itself accomplishes nothing. In such cases we say, 'there is no maneh here, and there is no collateral here,' unless he says, 'Acquire from the body of this collateral such-and-such amount.' So write the authorities below in siman 207 and in Even HaEzer at the end of siman 50.
The claimant must bring proof. Rama likewise writes this in siman 112, seif 3. Yet at the end of siman 250 the Mechaber rules without qualification that the heirs must prove that the decedent did not possess the item at the time of death, which is the opposite of what he writes here and in siman 112. Rama says nothing there except, 'See above siman 60, seif 6,' thereby alluding to this contradiction. It appears to me that the cases can be reconciled. Here the defendant asserts with certainty that the item was not in the transferor's possession at the time of the transfer, and therefore the party seeking to extract must bring proof. At the end of siman 250, however, the heirs merely say, 'Perhaps he acquired it later,' as explained there. We therefore follow the claimant's definite assertion and say, 'It was found here, so it was here,' meaning it was already present at the time of the gift. Although siman 112 also deals with heirs, and heirs ordinarily cannot assert details of their father's affairs with certainty, and the unqualified wording therefore includes a doubtful claim, similar to the preceding clause where the purchaser says perhaps, as the Tur explicitly writes, one can distinguish. Siman 112 concerns land. With land we cannot apply the presumption, 'found here, therefore it was here,' because land always remains in its physical location even though ownership changes. The rule at the end of siman 250 applies specifically to movable property found in its owner's house, concerning which we say that since it is found there now, it was there at the time of the gift as well. Here too the case concerns movable property, but because the defendant asserts with certainty that it was not there at the time of the transfer, the burden of proof rests on the claimant. Understand this well.
