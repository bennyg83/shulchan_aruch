Or accepted responsibility. The Gidulei Terumah, Shaar 43, part 4, end of folio 194, asks that the responsum of the Ramban cited by the Baal HaTerumos there in Shaar 43, which the Beit Yosef himself cites immediately below, clearly disagrees and holds that if he accepted responsibility upon himself he must place the property in the recipient's possession. It appears to me, however, that the Ramban speaks where he obligated himself to do so and accepted responsibility to place it in the recipient's possession. Therefore, even though the act of acquisition is ineffective, the personal obligation nevertheless takes effect upon him, requiring him to place it in the recipient's possession. This is compelled by the words of the Baal HaTerumos there. Otherwise, how could he immediately thereafter cite the Rambam, whose words would contradict what he had first written, namely that it is reasonable that this does not help? The Baal HaTerumos likewise rules without qualification in Shaar 64, part 2, and writes that the Rif ruled so in a responsum, without citing the Ramban there at all. Rather, it must be as I have written. This appears correct to me.
And even if he gave him collateral, it is nothing. So too is written in Mordechai at the beginning of the first chapter of Kiddushin. There, however, he writes that many Geonim disagree and hold that the donor may not recover the collateral until he gives the gift. Darkei Moshe cites this, and it indeed appears there. I am surprised that the Rama here simply adopted the Tur's ruling without noting that there are dissenting authorities. See below, siman 207, seif 11, and siman 333, seif 1, and what I write there.

I obligate myself, etc. Meaning, he personally obligated himself. Even according to those who say below, siman 245, seif 1, in the gloss, that if a kinyan was made from him to 'give' or with the wording 'I will give,' the recipient does not acquire because it is merely a kinyan on words, they agree here because he obligated his own person. So writes the Sema, seif katan 18.
He is obligated. This is specifically to collect from unencumbered property. To collect from encumbered property, even if he wrote a document concerning the obligation, it is ineffective even when the object is fixed and definite, such as where he writes, 'This house,' or, 'This sum that I will inherit from my father, I obligate myself to give you.' Since the property was not in existence at the time of writing, no public notice arises from that writing. This is clear from Rashi and Tosafos in Perek HaNizakin at the end of 50a, according to the authorities who rule in accordance with the view that such obligations are not deemed written, whose words I cited above in seif katan 12. The Rambam and those who follow him, whom I cited there, however, imply that with a fixed amount he may collect from encumbered property when the obligor obligated himself in a document even though the property was not yet in existence. See there carefully.
Provided that a kinyan was made from him. It appears that the Mechaber derived this from Baal HaTerumos, Shaar 64, as the Sema also notes. In my humble opinion, however, Baal HaTerumos follows his own view there, at the end of that shaar, cited by the Beit Yosef above at the end of seif 1, that a person cannot create an obligation that he did not previously owe except through a kinyan, and that saying 'You are my witnesses' does not suffice. According to the Rambam, however, who rules that one who says 'You are my witnesses' becomes obligated, the same should apply here. Since he said, 'Be witnesses for me that I obligate myself,' he should be bound even without a kinyan. This is difficult for the Mechaber, who at the beginning of siman 40 ruled like the Rambam, yet here writes that a kinyan must be made from him. Perhaps the Mechaber holds that with respect to something not in existence or not in his possession, saying 'You are my witnesses' is ineffective according to all views. It does not seem reasonable to me to distinguish in this manner, and the Mechaber's position requires study.

The burden is upon the claimant to bring proof. The Sema, seif katan 20, asks from the ruling below at the end of siman 250 and gives a strained resolution. It appears to me that there the movable property is intact, so we say that since it is now found here, we presume that it was here previously as well. Here, however, the coins are no longer intact. Nevertheless, the ruling at the end of siman 250 is difficult in light of the ruling below in siman 112, seif 3. The Sema's distinction between movable property and land is unpersuasive, and moreover what source does the Mechaber have for such a distinction? The principal ruling below derives from a responsum of the Rashba in the name of the Geonim, and these are the same words of the Geonim cited by the Tur below in siman 112. Accordingly, since we rule below in siman 112 not in accordance with those Geonim, the same follows regarding the ruling at the end of siman 250. The Bach likewise writes at the end of this siman that the authorities contradict one another. In all cases the burden is therefore upon the recipient to bring proof. This is the primary ruling.
According to the view of the Rif and some of the great authorities. The Beit Yosef writes that the Rambam in chapter 22 of Hilchos Mechirah also agrees with the Rif. He further writes that since the Rif and Rambam agree, that view is adopted, and for this reason the Shulchan Aruch here cites only the Rif's view. In truth, however, there is no proof from the Rambam. The Rambam there states only that the property must be in his possession, but he may nevertheless agree that the wording 'I gave' is wording that effectuates a valid transfer. Likewise, what the Beit Yosef writes, that the Rashba in a responsum agreed with the Rif that if he did not possess it at that time he accomplished nothing, is puzzling. Plainly the Raavad does not dispute the Rif on that point. If it is known that he did not possess the property at that time, he accomplished nothing. Their dispute concerns only the wording 'I gave.' The Rama in Responsa, siman 92, also challenges the Beit Yosef's words on this point. See there further regarding these laws. See also Responsa Maharam of Lublin, siman 108.
