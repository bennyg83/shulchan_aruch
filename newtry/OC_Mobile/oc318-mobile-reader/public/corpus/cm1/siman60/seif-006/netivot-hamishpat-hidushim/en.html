Warranty. This means ordinary warranty language written in sale documents. It is ineffective here because if there is no valid sale, there is no corresponding warranty. But if he expressly undertook personal liability and placed it upon himself, he is liable because the personal obligation takes effect. If one sells a stolen field that he actually robbed and possessed and accepts warranty, the sale is effective between robber and purchaser until the true owner comes and takes it. Since there is a sale between them, warranty applies to improvements and produce. If he never robbed or possessed it but merely pointed out land that was not his and purported to sell it, the law is like selling something not yet in existence. Regarding the purchase money, whether it has the status of an oral or written loan depends on whether the purchaser knew the seller lacked title. Even if the purchaser knew, the seller is responsible for accidents affecting the money.

The claimant. In siman 250 the Mechaber rules that the heirs must bring proof, because there the giver asserts certainty while the heirs are uncertain. In siman 112 the dispute concerns land, where the presumption of "found here, presumed to have been here" does not apply in the same way. The Shach distinguishes that siman 250 concerns existing movables, while here the money is no longer physically present. The Beurim explains further: with household articles that are currently found, the presumption that they were always there can operate where the donor gave everything. Here only one hundred gold coins were given, so that presumption does not show that these particular coins were included, and a migo can remain. Even if he accepted accident liability, the presumption does not overcome the possessor's monetary presumption requiring payment from his own assets. In siman 112, where merchandise and produce are involved, the relevant presumption is different, namely that all property was already established in the donee's possession. Therefore the authorities dispute it there. If the document says "I gave," we ordinarily assume he gave it in an effective manner. But if the recipient admits that no effective acquisition had yet occurred, the wording "I gave" does not help, and he cannot use a migo to say the property had previously entered his possession because that would be a migo to extract money. If the document contains both "I gave" and "I am giving," we follow the later expression.
