(Mechaber, seif 9) A document in which it was written, etc. Note: The Rosh's responsum shows that Reuven first claimed that because the document was written in his name the money belonged to him. He further claimed that the community gave him the document so that he could collect money the community owed him. The Rosh answered each claim separately. As to the first claim, that the money was his, he was not believed because the document itself said that it concerned a lease of the community, and the community's custom was to write its leases in the name of an individual. As to the second claim, that the community transferred the document to him in satisfaction of money owed him, this was ineffective because assignment required writing and delivery. The Shulchan Aruch here addresses only the second claim, namely, that the document originally belonged to the community but was given to him so he could collect it for his own debt. The Mechaber therefore had no need to mention the Rosh's first reason. The claimant himself admits that the document belonged to the community and says only that they transferred it to him. It is therefore extremely difficult to understand the Sema and Shach, who ask why he is not believed to say that the money is his and that the document was written in the community's name merely to avoid imposing an oath. They overlooked the Shulchan Aruch's wording, which clearly indicates that he himself admits that the document belonged to the community and only claims that they gave it to him for his debt. There is no trace in the Shulchan Aruch of the first claim in the Rosh's responsum.
