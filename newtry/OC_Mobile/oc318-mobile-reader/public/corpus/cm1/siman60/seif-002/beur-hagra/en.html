One who obligates himself, etc. This law is derived from the Yerushalmi cited by Tosafot and the Rosh at the beginning of the chapter HaNoseh: "By what does he become obligated to support his wife's daughter? Is it not," etc. In order not to make the Yerushalmi disagree with our Gemara, which says that Rabbi Yochanan and Reish Lakish disagree on this issue, the Rambam establishes the Yerushalmi as referring to an obligation involving something not quantitatively fixed, like the Mishnah. Rabbi Yochanan agrees in such a case. The Yerushalmi answers there that the Mishnah concerns stipulation documents. The Rambam likewise writes in Hilchot Mechirah, chapter 11, halachah 17: "Why is one who stipulates with his wife," etc.
Five years. This is the Mishnah there, against which they raised the difficulty above. The reason is that one's own support is not a quantitatively fixed obligation, as stated in Ketubot 59a, "something fixed," etc., unlike Rashi and Tosafot in Gittin 51a. Ramban; see Baal HaTerumot.
(Collected note) Or that he did not, etc. Meaning, as the Tur and Baal HaTerumot write in the Ramban's name, and as the Beit Yosef also cites: if he says, "I will give you such and such an amount of wheat each year," the value may rise. See Darkei Moshe, seif katan 3. Otherwise it is quantitatively fixed, as the Beit Yosef explicitly writes in the supplemental material, item 1, in the Rashba's name. The practical difference concerns collection from encumbered property, as stated at the beginning of the chapter HaNizakin, because such obligations are not fixed. See Even HaEzer, siman 114, seif 4.
Although they performed a kinyan from him, for in the Gemara in Ketubot there and in Gittin there, the Mishnah of HaNoseh is established as a case where a kinyan was performed, yet the Yerushalmi nevertheless establishes it as involving stipulation documents.
They disagreed with him based on the discussion there, because the amounts are not fixed, which shows that an ordinary creditor obligation does take effect. Likewise, in the Gemara in the chapter HaNoseh, the Mishnah there, "one who marries," etc., is challenged against Reish Lakish, and the answer is that it concerns a stipulation document. This implies that according to Rabbi Yochanan there is no need to establish it specifically as a stipulation document.
