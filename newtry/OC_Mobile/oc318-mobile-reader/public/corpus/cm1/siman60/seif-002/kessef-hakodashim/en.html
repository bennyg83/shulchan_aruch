See Shach, seif katan 17. The Shach writes in siman 60, end of seif 3, that where a fixed sum was undertaken the obligation passes to the woman's heirs, and Urim VeTumim and Baer Heitev in Even HaEzer 114 write similarly. Darkei Moshe, however, when quoting Rashba in siman 207, appears to indicate that this is so specifically when both the amount and the due date were fixed before the woman's death. Since she could already have demanded payment, the Yerushalmi's expression "she died, she has already died" is inapplicable, because the obligation was complete before her death. Yet the question in Responsa Rashba 970 expressly speaks of a woman who died before the due date stated in the document, which explicitly described the obligation as support, and Rashba nevertheless writes that the debt exists, except that the husband does not inherit it because it was only prospective property. I was puzzled because the responsum's language resembles the passage copied by Darkei Moshe. I initially wished to say that the wording in the responsum is transposed and actually means that the due date had arrived before her death. Rabbi Shmuel Katz suggested that the wording is correct and does not conflict with the question. Because the woman could have borrowed money for her support in reliance on the fixed sum that the obligor undertook "from now," it is considered as though the payment time had effectively arrived earlier. Rashba's wording, which emphasizes an obligation created from now, supports this reading to some degree and may also explain the Shach's unqualified ruling. Nevertheless, the reasoning still requires study. Even an unfixed support obligation can be converted into a monetary claim through the household-benefit rule, so she could likewise have borrowed on that basis. Moreover, the holder of a document is at a disadvantage against the possessor when the text is doubtful. What basis is there to impose liability merely because she could have borrowed and consumed funds before the due date, and how do we know that she actually did so? The matter requires careful study. By contrast, where one obligates himself to pay a fixed sum at the time of his wife's daughter's marriage, it appears that all agree he is exempt if she dies before the marriage, although the later authorities do not state this explicitly.
