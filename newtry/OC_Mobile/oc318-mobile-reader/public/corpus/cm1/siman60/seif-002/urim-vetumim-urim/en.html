Or he did not fix a limit. Meaning both that he fixed neither the number of years nor the amount of support. Rama teaches that even then most poskim disagree with Rambam and hold that a kinyan is effective, Sema. If he says, "Each year I will give you one hundred," Rashba holds that this is a fixed obligation even though the number of years is not fixed. The same applies if he says, "I will give you all the produce of this field," or, "a tithe of whatever is produced each year." See Tumim, where I clarified that this is specifically when the obligation is to transfer the actual produce. If he undertakes to pay its monetary value or to purchase equivalent produce from his own resources, it is considered unfixed because future prices are unknown. See there.

And this is our ruling. Most authorities likewise conclude that one cannot claim certainty in accordance with Rambam. In particular, where a father writes the customary half male inheritance document for a daughter and omits the customary fixed redemption sum, but writes that he absolutely obligates himself to give his daughter, from whatever property remains in his power one hour before his death, the value of one half of the share a male heir would receive, this is a valid complete debt. If the document states that he received consideration from his daughter, and it was made at the time of betrothal and marriage as part of the obligations undertaken at marriage, it is a fully valid document without concern. Although the amount is unfixed, most poskim disagree with Rambam, and moreover Rambam himself agrees that obligations undertaken as part of marriage arrangements can take effect even when unfixed. This is especially so with a written document and kinyan, and particularly where receipt of consideration is stated. As I explained in Tumim at length, in such a case even Rambam appears to agree that the obligation is binding and that his limitation concerns only gifts. We ruled accordingly, with the agreement of the sages of the generation in Prague, regarding the half male inheritance document held by the late distinguished R' David Weisel against the notable R' Leib Pinti, of blessed memory.

Regarding collection from encumbered property, Shach writes that this remains a doubtful question of law, because one might follow Rambam, who rules like Rabbeinu Chananel that an unfixed obligation cannot be collected from encumbered property. See Tumim, where I concluded that if a kinyan was made, collection from encumbered property is certain. A document alone may not suffice. But marriage settlement documents made at the time of marriage are effective according to everyone by writing alone even for collection from encumbered property, despite the amount being unfixed.
