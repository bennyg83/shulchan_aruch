t) The Rama wrote this to teach a greater novelty according to those who disagree, as the Sema also writes. The Rashba writes in a responsum that even if one obligated himself in a fixed amount, such as one maneh each year, although the number of years is not fixed, the amount is considered fixed according to the Rambam. The Ramban writes that if he fixed a quantity, such as a certain number of measures of wheat each year for five years, it is considered an unquantified obligation because the wheat may become very expensive. From siman 66.
y) Rambam, chapter 11 of Hilkhot Mekhirah, who writes that his teachers ruled this way. The Raavad writes, "I do not know from where they ruled this." The Maggid Mishneh likewise writes that he knows no proof for this law. The Beit Yosef and Kesef Mishneh write that it appears the Rambam learned it from the Gemara's effort to find a case of daughters who already existed at the time of the kinyan. How so? For example, after he divorced his wife, he reconciled with her to support the daughter she had borne him before the divorce, Ketubot 102b. Since the Gemara did not establish the case where she had already given birth and he stipulated to support her and made a kinyan, this indicates that a kinyan is ineffective outside the time of kiddushin for an unquantified obligation.
k) The Raavad there in his glosses, and the Maggid Mishneh in the name of the Ramban and Rashba. So too writes the Tur in their names, and the Ritva and Baal HaTerumot, gate 64.
