And they disagreed with him. See Shach, seif katan 12, who answers the difficulty raised by Tosafot and the Rashba. In his view, Ulla's earlier statement applies only to maintenance obligations, which are treated as written because they arise from a condition of beit din. Here, however, the reason is that an ordinary kinyan is presumed destined to be written and is therefore treated as though written. See Korban Netanel, Ketubot, chapter HaNosei, note 15, who rejects the Shach's answer and writes that the correct conclusion is that actual writing cannot be said to create collection from encumbered property in this manner.
