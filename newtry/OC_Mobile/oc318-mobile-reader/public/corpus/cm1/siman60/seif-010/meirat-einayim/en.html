And now Shimon claims, etc. Here too the Mechaber shortened the source. The responsum says that afterward Levi gave Reuven a written authorization to collect the twenty gold coins from Shimon. Reuven came and demanded payment from Shimon, and Shimon raised his defense. The answer was that even though Reuven had written the document in Levi's name, he had not necessarily transferred the money to Levi. This implies specifically where the document never came into Levi's possession. If the document had actually been delivered to Levi, we could say Reuven granted him the money by writing the document in his name and delivering it. After the borrower changes the document to the purchaser's or recipient's name, the original creditor cannot even expressly forgive the debt, as explained in siman 66:23. This is clear, and Ir Shushan also writes so. The Tur at the end of this siman cites Rambam that if a person says, 'The document in your hand contains a right belonging to me,' etc. The Mechaber omitted that law here, relying on what he wrote at the end of siman 16 and because he understood the Tur's citation of Rambam here as contradicting the Rosh's view cited there. Beit Yosef and Darkei Moshe say so. In Sema, Perishah, and Derishah I explained that there is no contradiction.
