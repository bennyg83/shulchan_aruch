Sema's discussion supports Taz in Yoreh Deah that although a mortgage with deduction of fruits on account of an earlier debt can involve prohibited interest, if a fresh kinyan is made for the mortgage arrangement there is room for validity. The present case likewise concerns a mortgage under which the creditor consumes fruits. Sema also writes that if the debtor purported to transfer the body of the field itself, the deed would not acquire without money. This seems difficult because Sema himself elsewhere says that where the consideration is an already existing debt, the field can sometimes be acquired by deed alone. The answer is that in a true sale for an earlier debt, the whole amount of the field's value must be represented by the debt so that no purchase price remains outstanding. If the debt is worth less than the field, there is still a deficiency in the purchase price and deed alone does not complete the sale. A mortgage is different. Even if the field is worth far more than the debt, the entire field can be placed under the mortgage arrangement and treated as sold to the creditor for purposes of consuming its fruits. The relevant consideration is complete for the mortgage lien itself even though it would not suffice to purchase the full body of the field. This is Sema's intent.
