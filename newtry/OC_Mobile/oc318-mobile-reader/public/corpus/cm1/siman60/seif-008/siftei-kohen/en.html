Although their contention that the incident of Issur the convert applies only to a person on his deathbed and not to a healthy person is incorrect, and all the authorities disagree with them on that point, nevertheless their ruling here may be correct. Once the document was written in Levi's name, the money represented by it became Levi's property. Even though Levi acted as Reuven's agent, money is given to be spent, and therefore Shimon became Levi's borrower, while Levi became Reuven's borrower. The document belongs to Levi, and Levi's admission is ineffective unless he transfers it to Reuven by writing and delivery. Moreover, even if Levi states that he acted as an agent in a manner whereby the money never left Reuven's ownership, such as the case discussed below in siman 66, seif 5, seif katan 17, who says that Levi is believed once the document has already been written in his own name? We find nowhere in the authorities that a debt document is transferred other than by writing and delivery, nor do they mention that the holder may simply admit that the money belonged to another. One may answer that the authorities always discuss a case in which it is known that the money originally belonged to the named holder of the document. Even so, they should not have stated the rule without qualification in every place. This requires study. See the responsa of Maharashdam, simanim 64 and 192, and the responsa of Maharam ibn Sasson, siman 112.
