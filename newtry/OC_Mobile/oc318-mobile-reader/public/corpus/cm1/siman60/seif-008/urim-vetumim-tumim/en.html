If he does not prejudice others, etc. But if he does prejudice others, he is not believed, and he has no power to give the document by means of a written assignment, because the Sages did not institute market protection for documents. Therefore, even if the document were in Reuven's possession by written assignment, Levi's earlier creditors could still come and collect by means of their documents. The Taz asks what loss Levi's creditors suffer, since the document is in Reuven's possession and cannot be removed from him. Why would the borrower pay them? He can say, "Return my document to me," which cannot be done without Reuven's consent. Ultimately, Levi's creditors will therefore not collect. See below, siman 66, where the Beit Yosef cites Baal HaTerumot, chiddush 10. There is no difficulty. First, Levi's creditors are still prejudiced because they may persuade the borrower to pay them in accordance with a judgment of beit din, without regard to the fact that the document remains in Reuven's possession. Thus there is at least a possible liability toward Levi's creditors. More fundamentally, in this case, where Levi owes others, everyone agrees that the document is removed from Reuven against his will. Below in siman 66, Reuven claims, "I purchased it." If his claim were true, he would be entitled to the document, but because he has no proof he is not believed. Regarding the document actually in his possession, however, he is believed to say that he once had a written assignment and lost it, and therefore it is not removed from him. Here, by contrast, the document was written in Levi's name. Even if we say that Levi sold it to Reuven, it is nevertheless removed from Reuven because the other creditor is earlier, and the case concerns one where Levi had subjected movable property together with land to those earlier creditors. Reuven therefore has no valid claim against them. The document is forcibly removed from him and given to the borrower so that, according to Reuven's own claim that the document belongs to him by purchase, the borrower may pay Levi's earlier creditors. Thus there is no difficulty, and in a case of this kind the document is indeed removed.
