If he admitted, etc. The Shach disagrees and says that since the deed is written in Reuven's name, Reuven cannot simply say it is not his and thereby transfer it to another without written assignment and delivery. His argument is not compelling. The rule stated by Baal HaTerumot is more correct.
If he does not owe others as a D.C. model, and if he does not have a loyalty bill, he is not loyal to him from his body, and he would have lost his Bible, and if he had not said that he had a broken or cursed man, he would have been hired, and this is his intention for the benefit of the patrons who belonged to him and called his guardianship
Where it harms others. If the admission prejudices other creditors, he is not believed. Accordingly, beit din compels the borrower to pay those creditors. Even if Reuven physically holds the original debt instrument, beit din removes it from him and delivers it to the borrower in the manner necessary to satisfy Levi's creditors. See Tumim.
And if he comes, etc. This differs from one who sells a debt instrument and can afterward waive the underlying debt. In an ordinary sale of a debt, some residual personal lien remains with the seller, which is why waiver can operate. Here, however, once he admits that the debt truly belonged to another person from the outset, no underlying lien ever remained with him. He never lent his own money. Therefore he lacks the power to waive the debt.
