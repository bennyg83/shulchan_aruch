See Responsa Beit Ephraim, Choshen Mishpat, siman 75, concerning a document stating that Shimon undertook to give Reuven maintenance or a fixed annual sum, and the question whether the choice belongs to the obligor or the beneficiary. He relates a practical ruling of his uncle, the Gaon Rabbi Sender, concerning a physician who made a written arrangement with a young assistant, whose compensation was either half the fees for shaving services or a fixed sum. At the end of the year they disputed who held the choice. The judges initially wanted to apply the rule that the burden of proof rests on the party seeking to extract, because they did not know of a direct source. Rabbi Sender pointed out that the rule is explained in Yoreh Deah, siman 177, seif 26, in the name of Ramah, concerning an iska arrangement in which the investor and recipient agree on an equal division of profit and loss in a case where the recipient is a distinguished person and the arrangement must be structured permissibly. If the document says that the investor 'will give' the recipient half of the profit and loss arrangement, the matter is understood to remain in the investor's control, and he may give the recipient whichever permissible form he prefers. If the document says that the recipient 'will take' half of the profit and loss arrangement, the choice belongs to the recipient. From this we learn that in the present case, where the document says the obligor undertook 'to give him maintenance or its monetary value,' the choice belongs to the obligor. He may either provide maintenance or pay the specified amount. It appears that in Ramah's case, if neither expression, 'to give' or 'to take,' appears, but the document merely states that the arrangement is half profit and half loss, the burden of proof rests on the party seeking to extract. In our case, however, the principle is simple: where a person undertakes an obligation toward another, the manner of performance remains with the obligor unless the wording of the document clearly shows that the choice was given to the beneficiary. This is the substance of the ruling. See there.
