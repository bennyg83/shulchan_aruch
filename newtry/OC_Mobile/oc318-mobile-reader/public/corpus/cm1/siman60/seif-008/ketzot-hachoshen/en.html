Reuven produced a document. The Shach questions this ruling because once the document was written in Levi's name, it appears to belong to Levi. Since loan money is given to be spent, Shimon becomes Levi's borrower and Levi becomes Reuven's borrower. The document therefore cannot pass to Reuven merely through Levi's admission without ordinary writing and delivery. In my view a debt instrument can indeed be acquired through formal admission. If Levi formally admits that the document always belonged to Reuven and that he acted only as Reuven's agent, Reuven acquires the instrument by the law of acquisition through admission, even if both know that historically the money did not belong to Reuven. Formal admission is itself a valid mode of acquisition. The limitation I wrote in siman 123, that an oral debt cannot be transferred by admission, applies because an oral debt is not otherwise transferable and is outside the transferor's control. A written debt can be transferred through writing and delivery, so it is a transferable asset and can likewise be transferred through formal admission. This does not, however, transfer the debtor's personal obligation in a manner that prevents waiver, because the personal lien cannot be independently transferred by any mode of acquisition. Therefore the original creditor can still waive unless the opposing party himself admits that Reuven truly owned the debt from the beginning. But for acquiring the instrument and its collectible property lien, formal admission can substitute for writing and delivery.
