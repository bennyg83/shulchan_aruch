The wording of Rambam, chapter 7 of Hilkhot Sekhirut, law 7. The Maggid Mishneh derives it from the incident of the man who deceived the woman in Bava Metzia 101b.
The Gemara explains that where he deceived the owner, even if the courtyard normally stands for rent, she can say, 'I am willing to rent to anyone else, but not to you, because you appear to me like a crouching lion.' Rif, Rosh, and Tur likewise write so.
The Rambam is there
The Sema and Bach write that Rambam also agrees that after the produce has been taken out to market, the custodian must notify the owner immediately. If he fails to notify him and the produce is then lost through an unavoidable accident, he is liable under the law of causative damage. Rambam's position is only that it is an act of piety to notify the owner even before taking the produce to market, so that he will not have to cast it onto the market, for perhaps some accident will occur between bringing it to market and giving notice, in which case he would bear liability before Heaven.
