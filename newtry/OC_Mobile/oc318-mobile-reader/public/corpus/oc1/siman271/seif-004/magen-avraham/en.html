It is forbidden to taste. It appears that rinsing the mouth is permitted, since he does not intend enjoyment of tasting. Once it is dark, even if he did not accept Shabbos, and if he accepted Shabbos even while it is still day, it is forbidden to taste. If he wants to accept Shabbos while it is still day, recite kiddush and eat, and pray Maariv at night, he may do so. Even if he has only one cup, he should recite kiddush over it and recite Birkas HaMazon without a cup, and he may not eat first, unlike Havdalah.
He must interrupt. Although regarding lulav in siman 652 he does not interrupt when he began permissibly, here it is different, because kiddush was instituted before the meal and in the place of the meal.
That he spreads a cloth. This is to cover the bread, and afterward they reveal it, so that the meal should come in honor of Shabbos.
Without the blessing over wine. The same applies that he need not bless over wine drunk during the meal, because after drinking the cup of kiddush there was no diversion of mind from later drinking.
He recites the blessing of HaMotzi. Since kiddush was an interruption, he must bless again. This differs from prayer at the end of siman 178, for once Shabbos became sanctified he became forbidden even to taste, as the Rosh explains. According to the other view, kiddush is not an interruption; if he recites kiddush over bread, he would have had to say HaMotzi before kiddush, and at that time there had not yet been an interruption, so all agree that he is exempt.
