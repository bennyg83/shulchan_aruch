It is forbidden to taste. This is only a D'Rabbanan prohibition. The reason is that the obligation of kiddush takes effect immediately when Shabbos enters, even if he did not explicitly accept Shabbos, for once it becomes doubtful darkness Shabbos automatically takes effect upon him. If he accepted Shabbos even while it is still broad daylight, the law is the same. If he wants to accept Shabbos while it is still day, recite kiddush and eat, and then pray Maariv at night, he may do so, provided it is half an hour before the time of Maariv, as above in siman 235, seif 2.
Before he recites kiddush. Even if he has only one cup, he should recite kiddush over it and say Birkas HaMazon without a cup, even according to the view above in siman 182, seif 2, that Birkas HaMazon requires a cup. He should not eat before reciting kiddush.
Even water. It is permitted to rinse his mouth with water, since he does not intend the enjoyment of tasting.
He must interrupt. Although he began permissibly, here it is different, because kiddush belongs to the meal, and initially it was instituted that he recite kiddush before the meal and in the place of the meal.
That he spreads a cloth. To cover the bread until after kiddush, and afterward he removes it, so that it appears that the meal is now coming in honor of Shabbos. Even if he has not yet sat down to eat, the law is also so, that a cloth must be spread at the time of kiddush, as below in seif 9. The novelty here is that even though he is in the middle of eating, spreading a cloth suffices.
Without the blessing over wine. Since he already blessed during the meal at the beginning of his drinking, he need not now bless Borei peri hagefen before kiddush. The same applies that he need not bless over wine during the meal, for there is no diversion of mind between drinking the kiddush cup and drinking the wine during the meal.
And afterward he recites HaMotzi. This means that when he comes afterward to finish his meal, he recites HaMotzi anew, for he does not fulfill with the HaMotzi he recited at the beginning of the meal, since the kiddush in between, which he had to say because he became forbidden to eat until he recited kiddush, is considered an interruption. Therefore he must bless HaMotzi a second time, though Birkas HaMazon is said only once at the end of the meal. This is unlike when he has no wine and recites kiddush over bread, where the time for his HaMotzi blessing is before kiddush and there was not yet an interruption requiring a second blessing; therefore according to all views he need not recite HaMotzi again.
He does not recite HaMotzi. For this view holds that kiddush is not considered an interruption. Since in doubt regarding blessings we are lenient, one should follow this latter opinion.
