Magen Avraham, seif katan 2. See what I wrote at the beginning of siman 193. In Dagul Merevavah he wrote that a man who already prayed Maariv cannot discharge through kiddush a woman who did not pray Maariv, for he is no longer obligated in the matter according to the Torah. Although regarding blessings over mitzvos one who has fulfilled can discharge another, the reason is that all are guarantors for one another, as Tosafot write in Rosh Hashanah 29. But the Rosh wrote in the third chapter of Berachos that a woman is not included in mutual responsibility, and therefore she discharges only one whose obligation is D'Rabbanan. In this there is room to doubt, for one may say women are not guarantors for others, but men are guarantors even for women. See there. In my humble opinion, there is no distinction between men and women regarding mutual responsibility, and we find nowhere that women do not have the law that if one fulfilled, she can discharge another. Regarding Yannai the king and the queen who ate bread, it implies that if Shimon ben Shetach had eaten a kezayis he would have acted properly. If women are obligated in Birkas HaMazon according to the Torah, how could he discharge Yannai's wife, since she would be obligated according to the Torah and Shimon ben Shetach only D'Rabbanan, unless there is mutual responsibility? Unless we say women are not guarantors for others, but men are guarantors even for women; if so, the doubt of the above Gaon is at least resolved. But it appears that the Rosh means that mutual responsibility applies only to one obligated in this mitzvah, who is responsible for others. If women are obligated in Birkas HaMazon according to the Torah, then if they already fulfilled, they truly can discharge men, for since they belong to this mitzvah they are also included in mutual responsibility. But in a mitzvah in which they are not obligated, meaning if women are obligated in Birkas HaMazon only D'Rabbanan, they do not have the law of mutual responsibility in that mitzvah. In responsa I wrote a clear proof from what is explained in the sugya of Megillah, that if a deaf person who cannot make himself hear has not fulfilled, he cannot discharge others, for he is not obligated in the matter. So we rule below in siman 585, seif 2. There the Rosh's question is difficult, for one who has fulfilled can discharge another, and the Rosh's answer does not apply, since there it speaks of men who are included in mutual responsibility. It must be that the Rosh did not intend to distinguish between women and men regarding mutual responsibility, but rather that one who is not subject to obligation in this mitzvah is not included in mutual responsibility for others. Therefore regarding shofar, since he does not hear and is not obligated in the mitzvah of shofar, he is not included in mutual responsibility for others. Examine this well.
