In the Tur he brought one opinion that one must bless over the wine, and the Beit Yosef wondered that this is not found at all. Nearby it will be explained in our words that this is the view of the Raavad. Regarding HaMotzi, however, he brings here the dispute cited by the Tur. The reason of the first opinion, that one must bless HaMotzi while over wine he need not, was written by the Rosh: certainly the prohibition of eating causes kiddush to be an interruption requiring HaMotzi, but Borei peri hagefen, which precedes kiddush, is because of the prohibition of drinking and is not an interruption. The meaning appears to be that although drinking is forbidden like eating, two factors are needed: a prohibition to eat and an act of interruption, namely making kiddush. Regarding wine, there is only the prohibition and no act yet, for when he must bless Borei peri hagefen because of kiddush, he has not yet said kiddush. Therefore the cup over which he recites kiddush does not require a wine blessing. This is not so with the blessing over bread, whose time is after kiddush. The Rosh holds that here kiddush is considered completion and withdrawal from the eating he already ate, since the prohibition to eat rests upon him until he performs kiddush. This is unlike what the Rosh wrote in siman 178, that prayer is not an interruption in eating because it is not completion and withdrawal like Birkas HaMazon. Here there is certainly a prohibition when the time of kiddush arrives, and he is forced to perform a new act, saying kiddush. Prayer is different: although he performs a new act, there is no prohibition of eating at that very moment when he prays, for it certainly does not speak of one who waits until near the end of the day when he has no possibility to eat a little. Therefore it is not completion and withdrawal. The second opinion holds that kiddush too is not an interruption requiring a new HaMotzi, for here he is not forbidden to eat except because of another preventing obligation placed upon him, namely to perform kiddush; once the impediment is removed, he returns to his first matter. This is like one who became mute during eating, Heaven forbid, and was immediately healed; he certainly need not recite HaMotzi again. Therefore it is not completion and withdrawal from the first eating. This appears to me a correct reason for these opinions. Since in doubt regarding blessings we are lenient, he certainly should not bless HaMotzi either. See what I wrote in siman 299 with Heaven's help. It is obvious that he need not pray Maariv if he spreads a cloth and recites kiddush, since he began eating in complete permissibility, as is the law of every Maariv prayer.
