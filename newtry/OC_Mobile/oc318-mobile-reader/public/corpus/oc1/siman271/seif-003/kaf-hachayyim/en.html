(13) [Se'if 3] If he cannot afford wine, etc. If one lacks wine for Kiddush during twilight on Friday night and a nearby neighbor wishes to give him wine, with a karmelis separating them, two people may transfer it; one lifting and the other placing. This is for the mitzvah of Kiddush over wine, at least rabbinically; according to major authorities the Sages did not prohibit divided transfer in a karmelis. It is also twilight, and Taz, siman 349, says the combination of twilight and karmelis (two rabbinic factors) is treated leniently [Os Hi LeOlam, maareches heh 71; Pischei HaDevir].
(14) There: And to prepare the meal needs, etc. This applies when he has bread. If he has none, he should buy bread and recite Kiddush over it, for Kiddush must be in the meal-place and he must eat bread, as siman 188 states [Magen Avraham and later authorities]. A kezayis suffices and the remainder should buy wine [Eshel Avraham]. This appears limited to one satisfied by a kezayis; if not, he is not obligated to distress himself when Kiddush can be recited over bread.
(15) If he has enough for nighttime Kiddush but not both daytime Kiddush and daytime honor, perhaps daytime honor takes precedence, since Ran says the Sages instituted daytime Kiddush because daytime honor exceeds nighttime honor. Nevertheless, perhaps bread suffices and the remainder should purchase wine [Magen Avraham; Eliyah Rabbah]. One loaf may suffice, allowing the remainder for daytime Kiddush, since lechem mishneh is not so absolute an obligation, as Magen Avraham, siman 254, states [Tosefes Shabbos].
(16) There: What was taught, that daytime honor takes precedence, etc. Maharshal, Yam Shel Shlomo, Gittin 4:51, protests that people neglect this and instead add delicacies Friday night. He personally refrained from fish Friday night, since it was important to him, so no number of nighttime delicacies equaled a morning meal containing fish. Machazik Berachah and Shaarei Teshuvah cite him. Shaarei Teshuvah notes that Maharshal similarly avoided beloved fish on Rosh Hashanah; one who favors another food should reserve it for the morning meal. According to the Arizal, fish must be eaten Friday night; one should instead honor the morning meal with another favorite food or increase a dish absent at night.
(17) There: But if he has only one cup, etc. Although he can recite Kiddush over bread, the primary mitzvah is over wine, so daytime honor is deferred. Rosh, Arvei Pesachim 17, writes similarly regarding a lamp and Kiddush [Erech HaShulchan].
