(7) [Se'if 2] Women are obligated, etc. If a man prayed and now owes only rabbinic Kiddush, having fulfilled Torah law in prayer, while a woman did not pray and remains Torah-obligated, he may nevertheless discharge her. For Kiddush, one who already fulfilled his obligation may discharge another, as above at the end of siman 167 and below, siman 273:4. Rabbi Akiva Eiger, responsum 7, rules so and challenges Dagul MeRevavah's doubt; Mishbetzos Zahav agrees. See above, siman 193, paragraph 2, regarding women hearing a language they do not understand; we recorded that custom follows Rashi that they fulfill the obligation.
(8) There: They may discharge men, etc. Bach cites Maharshal that women do not discharge men and agrees; Knesses HaGedolah says this is the custom. Because the matter is disputed, men should not rely on a woman's Kiddush except under pressure, when he cannot recite it and cannot find a man to do so; he may then rely on the permitting authorities.
(9) There: They may discharge men, etc. Plainly a minor cannot discharge a woman. Even at thirteen we fear he has not produced two hairs; for Torah matters the presumption of two hairs is not relied upon until his beard fills, as in Choshen Mishpat 35. The woman should recite Kiddush herself [Magen Avraham], or if unable, repeat it word for word with the minor [Shulchan Aruch HaRav]. If she prayed Maariv, however, an adult may discharge her because her Torah obligation has passed [Eliyah Rabbah; Dagul MeRevavah]. Dagul MeRevavah therefore says even a child under thirteen may discharge a woman who prayed, since one rabbinic obligation can discharge another; the same applies to a man who prayed if the child has not yet prayed. Rabbi Akiva Eiger 7 agrees. Even if the child also prayed and is obligated through two rabbinic layers while the adult has one, Rabbi Akiva Eiger permits him to discharge those who prayed. Above, siman 186, paragraph 8, we cited a dispute and concluded this works only after the fact. When possible, an adult should not initially fulfill Kiddush through a minor even if the adult prayed and the minor did not, because some hold Kiddush over wine is Torah law [Mishbetzos Zahav; Eshel Avraham]. We said in paragraph 3 only that after the fact prayer's mention of Shabbos fulfills Torah Kiddush. Although mitzvos require intention and one did not intend Kiddush during prayer, his intention was for Shabbos prayer, which itself requires mentioning Shabbos, and this necessarily supplies Kiddush intention [Machatzis HaShekel].
(10) Does mentally contemplating Kiddush suffice, or must Shabbos specifically be mentioned verbally because it says 'remember'? Megillah 18a says regarding Amalek: perhaps 'remember' means in the heart; since Scripture also says 'do not forget,' which addresses the heart, 'remember' must mean verbally. Shabbos says only 'remember,' so perhaps contemplation suffices. Eshel Avraham, paragraph 2, alludes to this [Keren LeShlomo].
(11) Maharash's Derashos says two householders should not recite Kiddush simultaneously, because two voices are not heard; their families would not fulfill the obligation. This differs from Megillah, siman 690:2, and Hallel, siman 488, where listeners concentrate because of publicizing the miracle. The matter requires consideration. Accordingly, members of the meal should not recite aloud along with the head of the household [Eliyah Rabbah; Mishbetzos Zahav].
(12) If two people recite Kiddush together, and their practice is to pause while one says Borei Pri HaGefen and the other answers amen, then the second says Borei Pri HaGefen, the first may not answer amen. That would interrupt between his blessing and tasting, and they may not arrange matters this way [Panim Meiros II 5; Shaarei Teshuvah]. If he mistakenly answered, he does not repeat the blessing, as above in siman 167, paragraph 61.
