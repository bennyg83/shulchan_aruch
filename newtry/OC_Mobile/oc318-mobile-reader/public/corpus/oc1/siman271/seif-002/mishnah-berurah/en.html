(3) For zachor is juxtaposed to shamor - "Remember the Shabbat day to sanctify it," stated in the first dibrot, and "Guard the Shabbat day to sanctify it," stated in the later dibrot, were both said in one utterance. Zachor refers to the positive mitzvah of Kiddush, as above, and shamor refers to guarding from labor. Just as regarding the prohibition of labor women are certainly warned, for in negative commandments there is no distinction between time-bound and not time-bound, so too in the positive commandment of zachor women are obligated. It is obvious that a minor cannot discharge a woman, for a rabbinic obligation cannot come and discharge a Torah obligation. Even if he is thirteen years old, we are concerned lest he has not brought two hairs, for in Torah matters we do not rely on the presumption that since he reached the age he reached the signs of hairs, until his beard is filled, as written in Choshen Mishpat siman 35. Therefore the woman should make Kiddush for herself. If she does not know how to make Kiddush herself, she should say it with him word by word from beginning to end and not intend to fulfill through his Kiddush. In this manner, since she is saying the Kiddush herself, it is proper that bread or wine also be placed before her at the time of Kiddush, and she should not rely on the fact that the boy is holding the cup or bread in his hand, since she is not fulfilling through his Kiddush. This advice works even if he is a very young minor. All this is when the woman did not pray Ma'ariv; but if she prayed, according to the above Magen Avraham she has already fulfilled the Torah obligation of Kiddush with this, and one may rely on a thirteen-year-old boy to discharge her afterward with Kiddush, meaning that he intends to discharge her. Eliyah Rabbah wrote that two men who want to discharge their households should not make Kiddush simultaneously, for two voices are not heard.
(4) And they discharge men - So agreed Taz, Magen Avraham, Gra, and other Acharonim. Nevertheless, initially one should be stringent that a woman not discharge men who are not members of her household, for the matter is undignified, as Eliyah Rabbah and Derekh HaChayim write.
(5) Since they are obligated, etc. - Therefore she can discharge others even if she has already fulfilled Kiddush, as with a man below in siman 273 seif 4, for regarding Kiddush men and women are equal. Know that when one person discharges another in Kiddush, he must intend to discharge him and the listener must intend to fulfill; therefore it is proper for the head of the household to remind the members of his household to intend to fulfill. See below in siman 273, Mishna Berurah seif katan 30, what we will write there.
(6) Like them - But regarding age there is a distinction: for a male, his Torah obligation begins when he is thirteen, and for a female when she becomes twelve, for then she becomes obligated in all the mitzvot.
