For remember is compared to observe. The phrase Remember the Shabbos day to sanctify it stated in the first Tablets, and Observe the Shabbos day to sanctify it stated in the latter Tablets, were said in one utterance. Remember refers to the positive commandment of kiddush, as above, and Observe refers to guarding from labor. Just as women are certainly warned regarding the prohibition of labor, for in negative commandments there is no distinction between time-bound and non-time-bound commandments, so too with the positive commandment of Remember women are commanded. It is obvious that a minor cannot discharge a woman, for one who is obligated D'Rabbanan cannot discharge one obligated according to the Torah. Even if he is thirteen years old, we are concerned that perhaps he did not bring two hairs, for in Torah matters we do not rely on the presumption that since he reached the years, he reached signs, until his beard fills out, as written in Choshen Mishpat, siman 35. Therefore the woman should recite kiddush for herself. If she does not know how, she should say it with him word by word from beginning to end and should not intend to fulfill through his kiddush. Since in this manner she is saying kiddush herself, it is proper that bread or wine also be placed before her at the time of kiddush, and she should not rely on the boy holding the cup or bread, since she is not fulfilling through his kiddush. This counsel helps even if he is a very small child. All this is when the woman did not pray Maariv, but if she prayed, according to the above Magen Avraham she already fulfilled the Torah obligation of kiddush through it, and one may rely on a thirteen-year-old boy to discharge her afterward in kiddush, meaning that he should intend to discharge her. Eliyah Rabbah wrote that if two people wish to discharge their households, they should not recite kiddush at the same time, for two voices are not heard distinctly.
And they discharge men. So agreed the Taz, Magen Avraham, Gra, and other authorities. Nevertheless, initially one should be stringent that a woman should not discharge men who are not members of her household, for it is undignified.
Since they are obligated, etc. Therefore she can discharge another even if she has already fulfilled kiddush, just like a man, as below in siman 273, seif 4, for regarding kiddush men and women are equal. Know that when one person discharges another in kiddush, the reciter must intend to discharge him and the listener must intend to fulfill. Therefore it is proper for the head of the household to remind his household members to intend to fulfill; see below in siman 273, Mishnah Berurah, seif katan 30, where we will write about this.
Like them. However, regarding age there is a distinction: for a male, his obligation according to the Torah begins when he is thirteen, and for a female, when she becomes twelve, for then she becomes obligated in all mitzvos.
