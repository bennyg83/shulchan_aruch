He pours from it, etc. The amount of a cheekful.
From the second cup. The Magen Avraham agreed with the view of Tosafot that with a cup of obligation, the tasting must specifically be from a cup containing a reviis of wine. Therefore the proper practice is as follows: first pour from the first cup into another cup, being careful that a reviis of wine remains in the first cup, and taste a cheekful from the first cup. Afterward pour the wine from the other cup into this cup, for by this its defect is repaired, as above in siman 182, seif 6, and one may recite kiddush over it if it now contains a reviis of wine. Although Eliyah Rabbah rejected his words and held that the Shulchan Aruch's counsel is preferable, see Tosafos Shabbos, who resolved it.
He recites kiddush over what remains. This language implies that he recites kiddush over this very cup. Although above in seif 10 he wrote that the cup must be full, that is only for the mitzvah in its preferred form and does not prevent fulfillment if he has no other cup. It is obvious that if he has another cup smaller than this one, he should pour the wine into it so that it will be full.
Exactly a reviis. This is not specific; the same applies even if he has more than this, but after tasting a cheekful, a reviis will not remain. The wording was used as a novelty, that even in this case he has a remedy by mixing in water. This speaks where the wine was strong, so that after mixing it one can still recite kiddush over it. See below, siman 272, seif 5.
This is specifically, etc. This refers back to what was written above, that when he has one cup he recites kiddush over it at night, and it teaches that this is specifically, etc.
It is better, etc. The Shulchan Aruch is speaking where the cup is of exact measure and already mixed; otherwise, he could recite kiddush at night and mix it to complete it also for havdalah, as above.
That he leave it for havdalah. Some say nighttime kiddush takes precedence over havdalah. Even according to the Shulchan Aruch, if he has beer for havdalah, it is better to recite kiddush over wine, for according to all views one may recite havdalah over beer, as written in siman 296, seif 2, while many poskim forbid kiddush over beer, and Rabbeinu Tam is stringent even over bread. Therefore even in the morning it is better to recite kiddush over wine if he has beer for havdalah. However, regarding the practice to recite kiddush in the synagogue and also havdalah there, certainly havdalah, which discharges the many from their obligation, takes precedence over kiddush, which is only a custom, as written in siman 269.
For nighttime kiddush takes precedence. As above in this siman, seif 3; see there in Mishnah Berurah, seif katan 8. The Magen Avraham brought in the name of Kenesses HaGedolah that it is good to recite kiddush over a large cup so that some of that cup remains for the daytime kiddush and for havdalah, meaning that he repairs it each time so that it is not defective, and in the morning and for havdalah he takes a smaller cup than the first so that it will be full. But Eliyah Rabbah questioned this practice, and Maamar Mordechai also rejected it and wrote that it is better to act according to our custom.
