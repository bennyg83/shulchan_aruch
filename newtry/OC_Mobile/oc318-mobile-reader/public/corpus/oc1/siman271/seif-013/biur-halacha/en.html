He must drink, etc., a cheekful. Even after the fact this amount is indispensable, as stated in the Gemara. The Mechaber's use of language implying an initial requirement appears intended to teach that even initially this amount suffices, and one need not drink the entire cup, unlike the law of the four cups in siman 472, seif 9. See the later authorities there in the name of Tosafos. Bach explains the reason there. This is also evident from Pesachim 107a, where the baraisa states, "If he tasted any amount of wine," while Rabbi Yose son of Rabbi Yehudah says, "a cheekful." The entire discussion clearly concerns the initial requirement.
And it is the majority, etc. See Mishnah Berurah. The Mechaber rules likewise above in siman 190, following Tosafos and other authorities. In siman 612, seif 9, however, the Mechaber writes that the amount is less than a reviis, following Ran, cited there in Beit Yosef, who holds that the Gemara in the chapter Yom HaKippurim proves only that a cheekful is less than a reviis. Tosafos Shabbos asks why the Mechaber does not cite Ran's opinion here even as a minority view. Perhaps in siman 612, which concerns liability for a sin offering, the Mechaber states the amount for which all opinions agree there is liability. As to prohibition, even any amount is prohibited by Torah law. Here, however, where the drinking requirement for Kiddush is D'Rabbanan, he relies upon Tosafos and the other authorities that for an average person the majority of a reviis suffices, since that is his cheekful. Their wording implies that for a small person, according to his size, even if he is thirteen years old, the required amount may be less than the majority of a reviis. Derech HaChaim writes likewise. The reasoning parallels Yom Kippur, where a small person is certainly liable for less than the majority of a reviis if it fills his own cheek and thereby settles his mind. In my opinion, however, this requires study. The Gemara's unqualified statement that one must drink most of the cup implies that less than this does not suffice under any circumstances. The Gemara's mention of a cheekful may teach that for a large person the amount can exceed the majority of a reviis, as Magen Avraham writes, but does not necessarily reduce the minimum for a small person. Yom Kippur is not a proof, because there liability depends upon settling the person's mind. Kiddush may instead have been enacted specifically to require drinking most of the cup over which Kiddush was recited. This is especially possible according to Ran, who holds that a cheekful for an average person is less than a reviis. According to him, even a very small person's cheekful might never be less than most of a reviis. The Gemara can be somewhat distinguished, since the requirement to drink most of the cup may be indispensable specifically for the four cups and not for ordinary Kiddush. The matter requires study. For an actual minor reciting Kiddush because of chinuch, one may certainly rely upon the lenient view that his own cheekful suffices even when it is less than the majority of a reviis. Know also that when the authorities state that for an adult a cheekful is measured according to his own mouth, as regarding Yom Kippur, Yoma 80 implies according to Beis Hillel, whose view is accepted, that the amount is measured generously and not tightly. In our case, this distinction has no practical effect where the amount exceeds a reviis, because, as we wrote in Mishnah Berurah, one reviis suffices for every person. It matters only where the amount is less than a reviis.
Of a reviis. See Mishnah Berurah. The great authority Tzelach, in the chapter Arvei Pesachim, Pesachim 109, writes that he carefully measured the amount of flour requiring challah according to Rambam, chapter 6 of the laws of Bikkurim, which follows Rav Chisda's calculation based upon forty seah for a mikveh. He then measured the challah amount by displacing forty three eggs from water, as stated in Tur and Shulchan Aruch, Orach Chaim, siman 456. The first vessel held twice the second amount. He therefore concluded that eggs have become half their former size. Accordingly, all our present measurements are mistaken and must be doubled, whether a reviis, which we estimate as one and a half eggs, the challah amount of forty three eggs, or a kezayis of matzah and maror, which we estimate as half an egg. He extensively proves that finger measurements have not diminished from those recorded by Rambam, and therefore only the eggs have become smaller. In my opinion, although his difficulty is very great and serious, his conclusion also faces a serious difficulty. Yoma 80 proves that an average person's two cheeks together hold more than a reviis. Tosafos and Ran write likewise. If the present reviis is actually double, namely the volume of three of our eggs, then an average person's two cheeks must hold more than three eggs. Tzelach himself proved that people's finger dimensions have not become smaller, and the same should obviously apply to their other limbs. Yet I personally tested the two cheeks of several average people and found that at most they held the volume of only two average eggs with their shells. This indicates that our eggs did not become half their former size, but only somewhat smaller. Alternatively, perhaps Ran's view cited in Beit Yosef, siman 612, is correct, that one cheekful is less than a reviis and its precise amount is unknown. It could therefore be somewhat more than the majority of a reviis, approximately the volume of one egg, so that both cheeks together would hold approximately two eggs. The measurements therefore require great study, because practical examination shows a contradiction between the two standards. Rav Chisda's measure of a reviis in Pesachim 109, based upon forty seah for a mikveh, is accepted by the authorities. According to Tzelach's measurement, and also Chasam Sofer in responsum 127, Orach Chaim, it equals the volume of three of our average eggs with their shells. Yet the Gemara in Yoma indicates that it can hold at most only two. In practice, regarding Torah obligations such as eating a kezayis of matzah on the first night of Pesach, one should certainly be stringent in accordance with their view. The same applies initially to nighttime Kiddush, whose essence is a Torah obligation. Chasam Sofer likewise rules stringently regarding a reviis. Regarding daytime Kiddush and other cups of blessing, however, one may rely upon the common custom reflected in Magen Avraham, Peri Megadim, and the other authorities. In any event, initially the cup should hold the volume of approximately two eggs with their shells, as explained above. Know further that even according to Tzelach, who requires the cup to hold approximately three of our eggs, one may certainly be lenient regarding how much must actually be drunk. One need drink only his own cheekful, even if that amounts to only about one third of the cup. Derech HaChaim rules that each person is measured by his own cheekful. Although we questioned this above, we do not impose both stringencies together. Therefore, a person with limited wine may drink only his own cheekful and leave the remainder until the next day. In the morning he may recite Kiddush over a smaller cup, as explained above.
