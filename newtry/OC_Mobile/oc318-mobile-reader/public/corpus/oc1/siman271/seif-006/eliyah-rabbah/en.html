And some say, etc. The Olas Tamid asks why prayer is not an interruption in siman 178, whereas kiddush here has two opinions. This is not difficult, for the Rosh distinguishes explicitly: once Shabbos was sanctified, eating became forbidden to him, so eating after kiddush is considered a new meal. Prayer does not forbid the meal, so later eating completes the original meal. The later authorities agree with the 'some say,' since doubts regarding blessings are ruled leniently.
He finished, etc. This means that he washed mayim acharonim or said 'let us bless,' as in siman 179. Before that, even if he finished eating, everyone agrees that he spreads a cloth and recites kiddush.
And he blesses HaMotzi, etc. Even the opinion in seif 4 agrees here, since he already washed and this is an interruption. The Bach and later authorities write that he recites Borei pri hagefen at kiddush even if he drank wine previously.
Levush, and they said, etc. What we say in seif 4, that even without declaration it is forbidden to taste, refers to after dark. Here it can be explained as referring to while it is still day.
