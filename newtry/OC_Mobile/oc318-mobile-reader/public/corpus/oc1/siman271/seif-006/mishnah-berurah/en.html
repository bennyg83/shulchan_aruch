If he finished his meal. This also speaks where he washed mayim acharonim, and before beginning Birkas HaMazon he saw that the day became sanctified upon him. In this case the first opinion holds that he first blesses Birkas HaMazon over the first cup, for since he washed his hands he must bless immediately. But if he did not wash his hands, the first opinion also agrees that he spreads a cloth and recites kiddush first, and he does not bless Borei peri hagefen at kiddush if he drank wine earlier during the meal, as above in seif 4. Even if he said, Let us bless, he also recites kiddush first, since he has not yet washed his hands, though in this case he must bless Borei peri hagefen at kiddush, since he said, Let us bless, as above in siman 179, seif 1.
Over a second cup. But he does not say both over one cup, for one does not bundle mitzvos together.
Of Shabbos. For we follow the present time, since at the moment he blesses the day has already become sanctified. Nevertheless it is obvious that according to all views, if he did not mention Shabbos, he does not go back.
After the beginning of the meal. For it was during weekday. All this is specifically when he ate nothing after nightfall; but if he ate even a little after nightfall, according to all views he must mention Shabbos.
If he should taste. For some hold that he blesses Borei peri hagefen and tastes immediately, as with every cup of Birkas HaMazon, and this is not considered tasting before kiddush because the cup belongs to the meal that preceded the obligation of kiddush. According to them he need not again bless Borei peri hagefen over the second cup of kiddush, only the blessing Asher kideshanu. Others hold that he does not taste from it, because it is forbidden to taste before kiddush, and therefore he cannot bless Borei peri hagefen over it. Rather, he first blesses Borei peri hagefen over the kiddush cup, recites kiddush and drinks it, and afterward drinks this cup of Birkas HaMazon without a blessing.
To eat a little. Some say that since he ate at the beginning, it is considered kiddush in the place of a meal and he need not eat again. Others say it is not considered the place of a meal, since that meal was weekday, and he must now eat at least a little on Shabbos.
And some say that even, etc. They hold that he necessarily must eat immediately after kiddush so that it should be considered in the place of a meal, for the meal he ate initially does not help for this, as above. Since he must in any case eat, why should Birkas HaMazon intervene? His law is like one who recites kiddush in the middle of his meal, mentioned above in seif 4, who spreads a cloth and recites kiddush.
And he recites kiddush and blesses HaMotzi. This is unlike above in seif 4, where some say he need not bless HaMotzi, for here it is different, since he washed mayim acharonim, as above, and he has completely withdrawn from the first eating according to all views, as above in siman 179, seif 1. For this reason he must also bless Borei peri hagefen over the kiddush cup and does not fulfill with the Borei peri hagefen he blessed over wine he drank earlier during the meal. If he did not wash his hands, his law is like above in seif 4.
And he eats a little. Meaning at least a kezayis, so that it should be considered the place of a meal.
To remove himself, etc. Meaning that if he acts according to the first opinion, he will fall into doubts: whether to mention Shabbos, whether to taste from the cup of Birkas HaMazon, and whether he must eat after kiddush. Although now he does not fulfill according to the first opinion, which holds that he must recite Birkas HaMazon immediately, this is preferable, for if he acts according to the first opinion there is a concern of an unnecessary blessing according to the second view, since he would need to recite Birkas HaMazon twice.
