He finished his meal. It appears from the Rif, Rosh, and Rabbeinu Yerucham that specifically when he began to wash mayim acharonim, the Rif holds that he must bless immediately. If he has not yet washed, all agree that he spreads a cloth and recites kiddush. Possibly even if they said 'let us bless,' they recite kiddush and Borei pri hagefen over the cup.
And he must mention Shabbos. Although at the end of siman 188 the ruling follows the beginning of the meal, here perhaps it is doubtful, and out of doubt one should mention Shabbos in every case, since there is no concern if he mentions it unnecessarily, as at the end of siman 108.
And some say that he does not mention. Although the Tosefta in Berachos states explicitly that he mentions it, there he also ate after dark; here, however, he finished his meal while it was still day. If he ate after dark, all agree that he must mention Shabbos.
If he will taste. It appears that the one who tastes recites Borei pri hagefen over it, and he does not recite Borei pri hagefen over the kiddush cup. According to the view that he should not taste, he does not bless over the Birkas HaMazon cup and does not drink from it until after kiddush.
To eat a little. For some say that since he ate at the beginning, this counts as kiddush in the place of the meal.
And he recites HaMotzi. This is when he washed his hands, and then even Borei pri hagefen must be repeated. If he did not wash, see seif 4.
