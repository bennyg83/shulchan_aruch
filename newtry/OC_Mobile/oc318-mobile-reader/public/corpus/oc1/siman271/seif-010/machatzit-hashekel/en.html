Seif katan 10, and they said, etc. The Raavad raised this difficulty in chapter 4 of Hilchos Berachos.
And the Kesef Mishneh answered, etc. Just as Shabbos establishes produce for ma'aser, so it establishes the prohibition for kiddush. Even food whose preparation was completed may sometimes be eaten casually, but one of the things that establishes it as fixed is Shabbos. The Kesef Mishneh explains that when they said 'come and let us recite kiddush,' even at doubtful dark this establishes the prohibition to eat or drink before kiddush.
And it requires study from the beginning of siman 299, etc. The Magen Avraham follows his own view there, that even doubtful dark forbids starting to eat before Havdalah. He analyzes the dispute between the Rif and the Rosh about whether one must interrupt a meal for Havdalah after dark, and concludes that regarding beginning a meal there is no dispute: even at doubtful dark one may not begin. Accordingly, for kiddush too, even at doubtful dark one must interrupt, so the Raavad's question returns. The Magen Avraham therefore explains that here the declaration 'come and let us recite kiddush' is needed to forbid them even while it is certainly still day.
'They must return' was printed in error; it begins a new seif katan.
Nevertheless it appears to me, etc. They did not divert their minds from the wine, because when they said 'come and let us recite kiddush' their intent was to drink from the kiddush cup. Although the Beit Yosef seems somewhat to require a new blessing, he presents it only as a possible explanation of the Tur and Rabbeinu Yerucham according to the Rambam.
The Tur speaks about an opinion that requires Borei pri hagefen on the kiddush cup. The Rambam says that if they wish to resume voluntary drinking they must bless again, and the Beit Yosef understands that perhaps even the kiddush cup requires a blessing. Still, since he says this only as a possibility, perhaps the Rambam agrees that the kiddush cup itself does not require a new blessing.
And the Olas Shabbos wrote that through declaration, etc. If the Rambam required a blessing even on the kiddush cup, that would answer the Raavad's question, for the declaration teaches that even the kiddush cup needs Borei pri hagefen. But according to the Magen Avraham, who holds that the kiddush cup does not require a new blessing, the declaration teaches mainly that later voluntary drinking needs a new blessing, as though they had said 'let us bless.'
