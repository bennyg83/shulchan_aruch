And they said, come and let us recite kiddush. The Raavad asks that even without this declaration they may not drink, as in seif 4. The Kesef Mishneh answers that there it is already dark, while here it is not yet dark; once they said 'come and let us recite kiddush,' they became forbidden to drink. This requires study from siman 299, which implies that even at doubtful dark one must interrupt. Therefore here must refer to while it is certainly still day, and they must bless again because it is like saying 'let us bless.' A careful person should avoid entering these cases, because there are many differing opinions.
Before he ate. Meaning that he had not yet eaten the slice of HaMotzi. Nevertheless he must bring lechem mishneh before him, and this is not an interruption, as in siman 167.
He should eat first. Meaning, the slice of HaMotzi, and afterward he must interrupt and recite Havdalah. See the beginning of siman 299. Maharam cites Ritzba that he should recite Havdalah first and then repeat HaMotzi and eat, since it is forbidden to eat before Havdalah, but this is not comparable to feeding one's animals, which is considered a meal need.
