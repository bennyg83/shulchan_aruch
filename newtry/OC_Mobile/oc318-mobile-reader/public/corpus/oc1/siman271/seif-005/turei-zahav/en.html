Two who were drinking, etc. This is the language of the Rambam in chapter 4 of Berachos, and he concluded that if they said, Come and let us recite havdalah, they need not bless again. The Raavad wrote on this: confused words are here. The interruption of kiddush, even if they did not say, Come and let us recite kiddush, since the day became sanctified, obligates them to interrupt. This is unlike havdalah, where although it became dark he does not interrupt. Nevertheless, if he said, Come and let us recite havdalah, he has removed himself from drinking and may not drink without a blessing. The Kesef Mishneh explained their dispute: the Gemara says that Shabbos establishes for kiddush but not for havdalah. The Raavad explains this as without saying, Come and let us recite kiddush, and automatically when we say havdalah does not establish, this too means without saying, Come and let us recite havdalah; but with such a statement it is diversion of mind, like saying, Let us bless. The Rambam holds that when we say Shabbos establishes for kiddush, this is when they said, Come, etc.; if so, when we say it does not establish for havdalah, it is even when saying, Come and let us recite havdalah. We see explicitly according to the Raavad that by kiddush, even without their statement, he interrupts and blesses Borei peri hagefen, just as by havdalah when they say, Come, etc., for in this they are equal. The Beit Yosef therefore wondered on the Tur: why did he write that some say he recites kiddush and blesses over the wine, claiming this is not found? Here the Raavad wrote it explicitly. There is a difficulty, for the Rambam already ruled at the end of chapter 29 of Shabbos that once the day became sanctified it is forbidden to drink, and he mentioned nothing of this statement. The Kesef Mishneh answered that there it is definite darkness, and here doubtful darkness. This is difficult: if so, definite darkness without a statement should have the same law as doubtful darkness with a statement, and the Rambam should have written in chapter 29 of Shabbos that one must bless Borei peri hagefen before kiddush, as he wrote here by doubtful darkness with a statement. Furthermore, the rulings of the Shulchan Aruch would contradict each other, for nearby he brought opinions regarding HaMotzi, implying that regarding Borei peri hagefen all agree he need not bless, while here the Rambam writes that he must. It seems to me that regarding the prohibition of drinking, definite darkness without a statement is the same as doubtful darkness with a statement. But regarding Borei peri hagefen they are not the same, for in darkness without a statement the prohibition itself does not create diversion of mind requiring another Borei peri hagefen; but where there is a statement, even in doubtful darkness and certainly in definite darkness, the statement creates diversion of mind requiring another blessing. This is as I wrote in the view of the Some say in the preceding seif, which is the Rosh's view regarding the distinction between Borei peri hagefen and HaMotzi: the prohibition alone is insufficient to create diversion of mind. So it appears to me.
Although they are not permitted. This implies that although they bless again, they transgress by drinking after the sanctity of the day has taken effect. Above in siman 179 the Shulchan Aruch ruled that if one said, Let us bless, it is forbidden to drink until he blesses, which implies that after the blessing there is no prohibition. He holds that there is a distinction between this statement and that one. The Rambam here took both cases and concluded about both that they are not permitted. Nevertheless, it appears to me that regarding halacha we distinguish: regarding Let us bless, there is no prohibition after a second blessing; regarding Shabbos, which establishes itself for kiddush, there is a prohibition even with a second blessing until he recites kiddush.
And he remembered before he ate. It appears obvious that even after he ate a little the same applies, that he recites kiddush and need not bless HaMotzi again. Even according to the opinion in seif 4 that he must recite HaMotzi, that is because he had already eaten while it was still day. Here, however, where he blessed HaMotzi after Shabbos entered, he certainly need not recite HaMotzi again. It mentioned here before he ate only because of the end of the case, that regarding havdalah he should not recite havdalah before eating, since one does not recite havdalah over bread, and havdalah would be an interruption between HaMotzi and eating. Rather, he should eat a little first, then recite havdalah, and finish his meal. This is unlike kiddush, which is not an interruption, even though at first he blessed HaMotzi not with kiddush in mind but merely for eating.
