Seif 4. Only kiddush, without the blessing over wine. This is from the words of the Rosh, who derived it from the Yerushalmi. But the Ramban in Milchamos, in the sugya of One may not give him fewer than four cups, holds that he must also bless Borei peri hagefen, and the Yerushalmi follows Rabbi Eliezer, for even Birkas HaMazon is not an interruption. See there. Perhaps this is the view of the Some say cited by the Beit Yosef; see the Beit Yosef, who was pressed in this. See Bach, who wrote that the reasoning of the Rosh is forced. In my humble opinion, from the Mordechai's words it appears otherwise, for he inferred that one who recites kiddush in the synagogue may taste even though he did not fulfill kiddush, from Shmuel's statement, Even the obligation of kiddush he did not fulfill, which implies that the obligation of wine he also did not fulfill. How is it possible that he drank? It must be that he may taste. Now, if we say that kiddush is an interruption, what practical difference is there to Shmuel that he did not fulfill the wine obligation because of change of place? Let it follow because immediately after tasting he becomes forbidden to drink until he recites kiddush at home, and automatically he would need to bless Borei peri hagefen, since kiddush would be an interruption. Rather, this proves like the Rosh, that since the blessing Borei peri hagefen belongs before kiddush, kiddush is not an interruption. The Rosh follows his own view, that it is forbidden for the reciter of kiddush to taste, and according to Shmuel he truly should not drink; therefore he needed to bring proof from the Yerushalmi. See Tosafot 106b, s.v. ta'am, how he can recite kiddush again at home, and see Maharsha there, who holds that according to Shmuel he drank wine, as the Mordechai inferred. Nevertheless, it seems to me that in halacha Tosafot do not hold like the Mordechai that he may taste, for if so they asked nothing, since he tasted permissibly. Rather they hold like the Rosh, that it is forbidden to taste; only Shmuel's language implies a practical difference in that he did not fulfill the wine obligation where he drank in prohibition, and they answered that since he tasted only a minimal amount, it is not called tasting. This resolves the Maharsha's question on Tosafot, that perhaps he truly should not recite kiddush at home, for since we do not instruct him to taste, one can say that if he did taste he cannot recite kiddush at home. But the Maharsha's question is really not valid, for why then did Shmuel say he did not fulfill the wine obligation, since he would not drink at home at all because he cannot recite kiddush? According to this it is also proven that Tosafot's view is like the Rosh, for otherwise the question would still remain: why say he did not fulfill the wine obligation, since kiddush would in any case be an interruption? Examine this well.
