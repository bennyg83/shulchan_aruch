And they said, come and let us recite kiddush. This speaks while it is still day, for at doubtful darkness, and certainly at definite darkness, even if they did not say, Come and let us recite kiddush, the obligation of kiddush automatically takes effect and it is forbidden to eat and drink, as written in seif 4.
Until they recite kiddush. Through saying, Come and let us recite kiddush, the obligation of the sanctity of the day takes effect upon them, and they become forbidden to drink.
And if they wished, etc. The reason is that they diverted their mind from further drinking before kiddush, and therefore this is similar to one who says before Birkas HaMazon, Let us bless, where one who drinks afterward must bless again. Nevertheless, regarding the kiddush cup, the Magen Avraham holds that he need not bless Borei peri hagefen, and he fulfills with the blessing recited at the beginning of his drinking, for they did not divert their mind from the kiddush cup when they said, Come and let us recite kiddush. It appears that he likewise need not bless over the wine he will drink after the kiddush cup, for he diverted his mind only from the drinking before kiddush. Since there are many opinions in these laws, every careful person should avoid coming to such a situation.
Although they are not permitted. This is because the sanctity of the day took effect upon them. The Taz wrote that if they did not say, Come and let us recite kiddush, but it grew dark, if they wish to drink again they need not bless again, for although it is forbidden because it is before kiddush, the prohibition alone does not create diversion of mind.
Before he ate. This means before he tasted the piece of HaMotzi. After he tasted it, he recites kiddush over wine if he has wine. But before this, he must recite kiddush over the bread, even if he has wine, for otherwise Borei peri hagefen would be an interruption between the blessing of HaMotzi and eating. However, he must bring before himself another lechem mishneh, and this is not considered an interruption, for it is a need of the meal, as above in siman 167, seif 6.
He recites kiddush over the bread. Meaning, he says the blessing Asher kideshanu, but he does not now say Vayechulu; rather, he says it during the meal.
And afterward he eats. If he remembered after he ate a little, whether he has no wine and says the blessing Asher kideshanu over bread, or whether he has wine and recites kiddush over wine, when he returns to eat the bread he need not bless HaMotzi a second time.
He should eat first. Meaning the piece of HaMotzi, so that the blessing should not be in vain, and afterward he must interrupt and recite havdalah. See what the Magen Avraham wrote at the beginning of siman 299, that according to the poskim who hold havdalah is D'Rabbanan, he need not interrupt, but rather completes his meal and afterward recites havdalah.
