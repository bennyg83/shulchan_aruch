If the cup spilled, etc. An incident came before me in the lands of Moravia: I recited kiddush over a cup that I thought was wine, but it was water, and in a second vessel that was also before me there was wine. I had already said all of kiddush. At first I considered drinking the other wine in the second vessel, because kiddush over wine is only D'Rabbanan, derived as a support from Remember it over wine, while according to the Torah verbal recitation alone suffices, as Tosafot write at the beginning of Arvei Pesachim in the name of Rabbeinu Tam. Their enactment was only to attach the wine to the Torah kiddush, and thereby one fulfills the mitzvah; but if he already fulfilled the mitzvah according to the Torah, they did not require a new kiddush merely because of the wine. There is also a clear proof from what is written in seif 15, that if the cup spilled before he tasted from it, he brings another cup and blesses Borei peri hagefen, and need not repeat kiddush; rather, he fulfilled with the first kiddush. Although the cup spilled and he did not fulfill, for even if he drank less than the measure he did not fulfill, we nevertheless do not say that afterward he must recite kiddush again. So too here, what difference is there whether it spilled and he did not drink at all, or whether it was water? In the end he did not fulfill kiddush over wine. So too the Tur in siman 269 cites Rav Natronai Gaon, who wrote that one is obligated only to hear kiddush and fulfills even without tasting. Although the Tur wondered about him there, that is because it is not in the place of a meal, but not because one cannot fulfill by hearing alone. Then I saw this law in the rulings of Mahari, siman 115, where he wrote simply that one should recite kiddush again over wine. He argued: what of it if kiddush over wine is D'Rabbanan? There are many D'Rabbanan mitzvos over which we bless. Would one say that if he blew mistakenly on Rosh Hashanah during the standing blasts he need not repeat them, since he already fulfilled according to the Torah with the seated blasts and these are only D'Rabbanan? Certainly no one would say this. I am very astonished at that great rabbi for uttering such words, for what proof did he bring from shofar, where no additional blessing is required? What concern is there if he blows again? Certainly, if he blew mistakenly and finished the entire prayer, he need not repeat the prayer, for we rule that the blessings and blasts do not prevent one another, and this is an explicit Talmudic ruling. Here, however, he would need to bless the kiddush blessing a second time, and there is concern for a blessing in vain. However, after further analysis it appears that one must distinguish the matter, as I will explain with Heaven's help. This case resembles what is written in siman 272 regarding kiddush in the place of a meal, where we rule that if he recited kiddush intending to eat here, and some cause arose requiring him to uproot from that place, he must recite kiddush again. Although kiddush in the place of a meal is only D'Rabbanan, even according to Rashbam who derives it from You shall call Shabbos a delight it is only an asmachta, nevertheless he must recite kiddush again. So too here he must recite kiddush again over wine. One should not say that there, in the law of the place of the meal, perhaps the kiddush was proper and he fulfilled, but he is forbidden to eat away from the place of kiddush. That is not so, for on the contrary the Beit Yosef ruled there that even if he does not want to eat afterward at all, he did not fulfill kiddush, and he derived it from the Gemara that Rava's lamp went out, he went to Rabbah his son, recited kiddush again, and tasted something. Thus, since he did not fulfill the D'Rabbanan kiddush, he must recite kiddush again; the same applies here. But according to this, it is very difficult why, when the cup spilled, he need not repeat kiddush over another cup, since he did not fulfill D'Rabbanan kiddush, namely kiddush over the cup. It is also difficult from one who recited kiddush and spoke before tasting: he must bless Borei peri hagefen again but not kiddush, as written in this seif from Behag, cited by the Tur at the end of this siman. It is likewise difficult from what the Tur writes in the name of Behag in this siman, that the one who recites kiddush himself specifically must taste; if someone else drank the cup, they bring the reciter another cup and he blesses Borei peri hagefen but does not recite kiddush again. Why do we not say that since he at least did not fulfill, he should recite kiddush again? It seems to me that the essence depends on kiddush requiring tasting wine at the time of kiddush. About this they said, Remember it over wine, that there must be tasting of wine there and that kiddush be said over it. If a mishap occurred in the tasting of wine, he recited kiddush over it according to law, but since he cannot taste, he can repair the defect by tasting other wine. But regarding kiddush in the place of a meal, if it turns out that he cannot eat there, there is no repair for that defect, and therefore he must recite kiddush again where he will eat. In our case, since he did not recite kiddush properly, it is as though he did not recite kiddush at all, and therefore he must recite kiddush again over wine. However, there is a distinction: if at the time of kiddush there was wine on the table or on the bench near him prepared to drink, then certainly he need not recite either Borei peri hagefen or kiddush again, but should drink what was prepared before him, as Rama wrote in siman 209 from the responsum of Maharil, for it is as though he recited kiddush over that wine, and in this we say all cups are included in the blessing. So it appears to me correctly in this matter.
