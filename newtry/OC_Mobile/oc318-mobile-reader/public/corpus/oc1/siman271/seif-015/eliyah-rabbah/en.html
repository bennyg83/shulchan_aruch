Levush, to repeat the blessing, etc. Although in seif 5 we hold that kiddush without Borei pri hagefen can be valid, before kiddush is different, because he diverted his mind from drinking before kiddush. So it appears from the Magen Avraham and the Lechem Mishneh. The Olas Tamid distinguishes that verbal declaration is worse, so even for kiddush one must recite Borei pri hagefen, and the Taz inclines this way, but this is not clear.
Levush, before he ate, etc. This means before he ate the slice of HaMotzi. The Taz explains that if he remembered after eating, he should recite kiddush and need not repeat HaMotzi. It appears to me that before eating he recites kiddush over bread so that he not eat before kiddush; after he ate, he should recite kiddush over wine. Yet according to Shiltei Gibborim, once he washed his hands kiddush became fixed over bread, and this may apply all the more after eating bread, though perhaps having eaten makes it worse. This also relates to seif 12, that initially one should wash, since kiddush must be in the place of the meal.
Levush, the slice of HaMotzi, etc. He should eat it so that the blessing on washing and the blessing of HaMotzi not be rendered wasted; an enactment should not cause their loss. This rejects the Magen Avraham's citation from Maharam that he should first recite Havdalah and then repeat HaMotzi, for avoiding a blessing in vain is preferable to this D'Rabbanan concern of eating before Havdalah.
Levush, even if, etc. It is clear that if he remembered at night after tasting, he recites kiddush at night, unlike the Beit Yosef. The same law applies on the night of Yom Tov if he did not recite kiddush, that he recites it by day.
[Levush] And additionally, if, etc. They did not also write the reason that the meal should come in honor of Shabbos because that reason does not apply to the daytime meal, as Mordechai writes in chapter Arvei Pesachim. One should cover the bread with a white cloth, according to Tzeidah LaDerech.
[Levush] In remembrance of the manna, etc. Since manna did not fall on Shabbos or Yom Tov, as Tosafot Pesachim 100 writes. A practical difference is that according to the first reason, once Borei Pri HaGefen has been said the bread may be uncovered, while according to the manna reason it remains covered until after kiddush. Perishah writes that the cloth should remain until all diners have drunk and one wishes to bless over the bread. Then the bread should be taken out from beneath the cloth and only afterward should the cloth be removed, resembling the manna being removed from beneath the dew. This requires study, because in Parashat Beshalach the verse says, "the layer of dew rose," and Rashi explains that when the sun rose, the dew above the manna lifted and they saw it. Thus the dew disappeared first. In practice I have not seen people insist on taking out the bread before removing the cloth. Perhaps Perishah holds that only on the first day, before they knew it was manna, the dew remained until sunrise, while afterward they gathered earlier before sunrise when the dew still lay over it, as implied by the doubled phrase "morning by morning." The Levush derives from the second reason that even one who makes kiddush over bread should cover it, and Taz and Olat Tamid say this is the custom. I require study, because Tzeidah LaDerech writes that when making kiddush over bread no cloth is needed even though he cites the manna reason. Moreover, when making kiddush over bread one must place his hands on it just as one holds the cup, as below in siman 483. It therefore appears that before kiddush he should cover it, but when he begins kiddush he should uncover it and place his hands on it.
