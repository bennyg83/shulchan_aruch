Levush, to repeat the blessing, etc. Although in seif 5 we hold that kiddush without Borei pri hagefen can be valid, before kiddush is different, because he diverted his mind from drinking before kiddush. So it appears from the Magen Avraham and the Lechem Mishneh. The Olas Tamid distinguishes that verbal declaration is worse, so even for kiddush one must recite Borei pri hagefen, and the Taz inclines this way, but this is not clear.
Levush, before he ate, etc. This means before he ate the slice of HaMotzi. The Taz explains that if he remembered after eating, he should recite kiddush and need not repeat HaMotzi. It appears to me that before eating he recites kiddush over bread so that he not eat before kiddush; after he ate, he should recite kiddush over wine. Yet according to Shiltei Gibborim, once he washed his hands kiddush became fixed over bread, and this may apply all the more after eating bread, though perhaps having eaten makes it worse. This also relates to seif 12, that initially one should wash, since kiddush must be in the place of the meal.
Levush, the slice of HaMotzi, etc. He should eat it so that the blessing on washing and the blessing of HaMotzi not be rendered wasted; an enactment should not cause their loss. This rejects the Magen Avraham's citation from Maharam that he should first recite Havdalah and then repeat HaMotzi, for avoiding a blessing in vain is preferable to this D'Rabbanan concern of eating before Havdalah.
Levush, even if, etc. It is clear that if he remembered at night after tasting, he recites kiddush at night, unlike the Beit Yosef. The same law applies on the night of Yom Tov if he did not recite kiddush, that he recites it by day.
