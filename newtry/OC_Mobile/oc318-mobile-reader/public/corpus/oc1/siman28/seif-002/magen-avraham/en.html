The tefillin of the head. He removes the tefillin of the head after first removing the strap from the finger. Gallei Razaya.
While standing. The same applies to the windings around the finger. The tefillin of the arm should be removed while seated, because the manner of removal should correspond to the manner of putting them on, as stated in siman 25, seif 11, in the gloss, based upon the writings of the Arizal. According to our custom, however, in which the tefillin of the arm are also put on while standing, they too must be removed while standing. Shlah writes that he saw his teacher remove the tefillin of the head with his left hand. The reason is to show that it is difficult for him to remove them, because their mitzvah applies throughout the entire day. We remove them after prayer only because we lack a sufficiently clean body. He therefore removes them with the left hand. See what I wrote in siman 123, seif 3.
In the case. Bach writes that one should make a narrow case so that the tefillin are placed one above the other. In my opinion, since the tefillin of the head possess greater sanctity, as explained in siman 42, it is forbidden to place the tefillin of the arm upon them, as stated at the end of Yoreh Deah, siman 282. It is possible that this restriction applies specifically to Torah and Neviim, because the Torah has much greater sanctity. Regarding other sacred objects, perhaps we are not particular, for it is permitted to place Kesuvim upon Neviim and Neviim upon Kesuvim even though their sanctity is unequal, as explained in siman 153. Nevertheless, it appears to me that this is why the common practice is to place the tefillin side by side. When Shulchan Aruch writes “and upon it the tefillin of the arm,” the meaning is that the tefillin of the arm should be positioned slightly higher, so that one encounters them first. The custom is to mark which pouch belongs to the tefillin of the head and which to the tefillin of the arm. Since the tefillin of the head possess greater sanctity, one may not later change their pouch and place the tefillin of the arm in it. If one stipulated this initially, however, it is permitted, as explained in siman 42. The custom is to wind the straps like wings, alluding to the wings of a dove. Mateh Moshe in the name of Sefer HaMussar. One should not place the straps upon the boxes. Gallei Razaya. Although at first glance this appears permitted, just as the mantle is wound around a Sefer Torah, Tosafos in the first chapter of Bava Basra write that when Tanach is attached in a single scroll, it is rolled from beginning to end, because it is degrading for the Torah to be wound around the Neviim and serve as their protector. The same should apply here: the straps would become protectors for the tefillin. Their words, however, require study. Megillah 27a asks how a scroll is rolled, since one sheet lies upon another, and answers that where there is no alternative it is permitted. This implies that one may place one Chumash upon another, or one Torah upon another, only where no alternative exists. According to Tosafos, the proof is difficult, because in rolling one object becomes a protector for another. Moreover, the wording “one sheet lies upon another” implies that the primary concern is that one lies upon another. Who told them this reasoning that one becomes a protector for another? Rashba, in his novellae to the first chapter of Bava Basra, writes that only where no alternative exists may one place one Chumash upon another, but where another arrangement is possible it is forbidden. This also implies here that it is forbidden to wind the straps upon the tefillin. Perhaps one may distinguish. When one object is simply placed upon another, it does not serve as a protector, and it is forbidden to place objects of unequal sanctity one upon another unless no alternative exists. In winding, however, one does serve as a protector. Where the sanctities are unequal, it is preferable that the object of lesser sanctity be outside and serve as a protector, because we are more concerned that the protector be of lesser sanctity than that it lie beneath the more sacred object. The Gemara in Megillah may then be explained: one sheet lies upon another and thereby serves as a protector, but since there is no alternative it is permitted. Nevertheless, regarding the straps, since they can be placed along the sides, it is forbidden to place them above the boxes. One should not hold the straps and wind the tefillin, as explained in siman 147, seif 5, and should not shake them out of the case, but remove them with his hand. Mateh Moshe; Sefer Chassidim.
