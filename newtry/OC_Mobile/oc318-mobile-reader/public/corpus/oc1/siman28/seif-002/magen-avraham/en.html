Shel rosh. He removes the shel rosh after removing the strap from the finger [Galia Raza].

Standing. So too the finger wrappings; and shel yad while seated — as their placement, so their removal, as written in siman 25 section 11 in the gloss (writings of the Ari). If so, for our custom that we also place the shel yad while standing, one must also remove them while standing. Shelah wrote that he saw his teacher remove the shel rosh with the left hand; the reason is to show that removing them is hard for him because their mitzvah is all day — but because we lack a pure body we remove them after prayer, and therefore remove with the left. And see what I wrote in siman 123 section 3.

In the bag. The Bach wrote to make a narrow bag so they lie one upon the other. In my humble opinion, since the shel rosh's sanctity is stricter, as written in siman 42, it is forbidden to place the shel yad upon it, as written in Yoreh De'ah siman 282. It is possible we are not strict except regarding Torah and Prophets, whose sanctity is greatest; but regarding other matters we are not strict, for one may place Ketuvim upon Nevi'im and Nevi'im upon Ketuvim even though their sanctity is not equal, as written in siman 153. Nevertheless it seems to me that the world is accustomed to place them next to each other. What is written here "and upon it" means to place them slightly toward the upper side; they are accustomed to mark which is shel rosh and which shel yad, for since the shel rosh's sanctity is stricter it is forbidden to change the pouch afterward to put the shel yad in it; however, if he stipulated initially it is permitted, as written in siman 42. They are accustomed to wrap them like wings, after the wings of a dove [Matteh Moshe in the name of Sefer HaMusar]. One should not place the straps upon the batim [Galia Raza]. Even though it seems permitted, as with a mantle wrapped around a sefer Torah — and moreover Tosafos wrote in Bava Batra chapter 1 that when binding Tanach in one volume one rolls from beginning to end, for it is degrading to the sefer Torah to wrap around Nevi'im and become a guard for Nevi'im — if so, here too the straps become guards for the tefillin. Nevertheless their words require investigation, for it is stated in Megillah 27a: "How does one bless when rolling?" — "Behold a page sits upon its fellow" — meaning when rolling a sefer Torah they place one page upon another; but since it is impossible it is permitted. Here too, since it is impossible it is permitted; perforce the explanation is that it is permitted to place Humashim upon Humashim or Torah upon Torah. According to their words, what proof does he bring from binding that one becomes a guard for the other? And from the language "behold a page sits upon its fellow" it implies the main stringency is that one lies upon the other — and who told them this idea that one becomes a guard for the other? In the chiddushim of the Rashba, Bava Batra chapter 1 folio 14, he wrote: specifically where it is impossible one places one Humash upon another; but where it is possible in another manner it is forbidden to place one upon the other — so too here it is forbidden to wrap the straps on the tefillin. One may say: regarding placement, since no guard is made, it is forbidden to place one upon the other except where impossible; but in wrapping, where a guard is made, when the sanctities are not equal it is better that the lighter sanctity be outside so it serves as guard for the other, for they are more strict about a guard than about what lies under the other. In the Gemara in Megillah it explains "behold a page sits upon its fellow" — and one becomes a guard for the other; but since it is impossible it is permitted. Here too, etc. Nevertheless it seems to me regarding straps, since one may place them on the sides, it is forbidden to place them on top. One should not grasp the straps and roll the tefillin, as written in siman 147 section 5; nor shake them from the bag but take them out by hand [Matteh Moshe section 8].
