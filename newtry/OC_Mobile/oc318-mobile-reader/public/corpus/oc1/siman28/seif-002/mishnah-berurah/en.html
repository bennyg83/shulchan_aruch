(5) Removes first — after removing the three wrappings from the finger.

(6) Standing — so too removing the finger wrappings; and for our custom that we also place the shel yad while standing, one must also remove them while standing, for as their placement so their removal. Some sages were accustomed to remove the shel rosh with the left hand, which is the weaker hand, to show that removing them is hard for him; and if he is left-handed, so that all his work is with his left, he removes with the right so as not to do the removal quickly.

(7) And upon it the shel yad — the Bach wrote, and so the Taz above in siman 25 note 10, meaning to make a long narrow bag so they lie one upon the other. Magen Avraham wrote it is better to place the tefillin side by side, as is the custom. What the Mechaber wrote "and upon it the shel yad" means to place them slightly toward the upper side so one encounters them first. In Shulchan Shlomo he wrote it is good that the shel yad be on the right side of the bag, also for this reason. Some make two pouches, one for shel yad and one for shel rosh; but even with two pouches it is better to place the shel yad pouch slightly toward the upper side so one encounters it first and does not pass over mitzvot — for the Taz's view above in siman 25 is that even through a pouch one may pass over a mitzvah; see Magen Avraham in siman 25 note 1. Those who make tefillin bags should mark which belongs to shel rosh and which to shel yad so they be careful and not take out the shel rosh first from the bag; also, since the shel rosh's sanctity is stricter, be careful thereby not to change the bag afterward to put the shel yad in it unless he stipulated initially, as below in siman 42.

(8) First — and he need not pass over the mitzvah. It is proper to be careful not to remove the shel yad until he has placed the shel rosh in the bag, so he not forget and place the shel yad in the bag first.
