B) There — that he not distract his mind from them. Here, regarding distraction of mind: according to Rabbeinu Yonah, the Rosh, and the Tur, distraction is not called hesech hadaat and is forbidden only when one stands in levity and lightheadedness, as written in siman 44 in the Tur and Beit Yosef — see there. So too the Bach there, citing the above, except that he wrote: nevertheless it is a mitzvah of the chosen to have his mind always on the tefillin and not distract his mind from them by engaging in idle talk and brooding over evil thoughts; for this reason a person must handle them every hour so he not distract his mind from them, as above in siman 28 and siman 37 — up to here. So too Magen Avraham there note 2. Shaarei Teshuvah in this siman letter 1, Razban letter 1. However, the Shagat Aryeh siman 34 wrote that all distraction of mind is forbidden and proved this from Rambam and Ramban and their camp, and wrote that such is the essence — see there. So too the Ari in Shaar HaKavanot derush 5 on tefillin, that it is forbidden to distract one's mind from the tefillin at any moment, even while praying, except when engaged in Torah or praying the Eighteen Blessings he is exempt — see there. So too in Pardes Chochmah, gate of tefillin chapter 10. Otzrot Chaim folio 34 side 2 in the matter of the Noda B'Yehuda. Nagid U'Mitzvah. Mitzvat Shemurim. And see Orach Chayyim letter 50. And in Shaar HaGilgulim introduction 22 folio 22 side 1 he brought a terrible incident: once the Ari saw with his eyes a great sage of a generation several years before him whose time had come to elevate him now to a higher partition than at first; for in proportion to the elevation by which they elevate him he must become more refined even in fine points of mitzvot like a hair's breadth. Among the matters he was punished for distracting his mind from the tefillin by saying Yehai Shemei while not touching them in vain and not born to confusion, as written after Kedushah and "and a redeemer shall come to Zion," and because he had kavanah in that prayer he distracted his mind from the tefillin and was punished for this slight lapse — and what shall one do who lacks kavanah in his prayer and in the tefillin on his head — up to here. This was brought in Pardes Chochmah there and in Otzrot Chaim there. Nagid U'Mitzvah there. Mitzvat Shemurim there. Eliyah Rabbah there. Shaarei Teshuvah letter 1. Machberet letter 2. Shatz page 40 side 3. Nefesh Shimon letter 5. Chas Lamed letter 2. And Chas Lamed wrote there regarding the fence of not distracting one's mind from the tefillin: I saw men of action who would write on every page of the siddur thus: kavanat tefillin — up to here. So too Kaf HaChayyim siman 10 letter 26, words of Menachem in siman 37 letter 2. It is obvious that it does not mean specifically writing on every siddur page, but even if one writes this on a piece of parchment or paper and places it above the siddur pages so he sees it at every moment and remembers — enough. So too Ben Ish Chai, Parashat Chayei Sarah letter 17.
