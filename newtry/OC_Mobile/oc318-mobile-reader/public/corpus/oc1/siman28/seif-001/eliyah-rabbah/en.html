[1] To touch the tefillin at every time, etc. This is derived a fortiori from the tzitz, which contains only one mention of Hashem's Name, yet the Torah says “continually,” teaching that the Kohen Gadol may not divert his attention from it. Bach writes: We have not heard what this touching means. Is it conceivable that throughout the entire time the tefillin are upon him, he may never remove his hand from them? Rather, the meaning is that whenever he remembers them he must immediately touch them, so that he not come to distraction. Rambam writes that one may not divert his attention from them even for a single moment. Piskei Mahari, Terumas HaDeshen, part 2, siman 120, writes that one should touch them so that they not shift from their proper place. Shulchan Aruch of Mahari Luria states: One may not divert his attention from tefillin even in the middle of prayer, except while occupied with words of Torah and during the eighteen blessings of the Amidah. If one's thought turns even slightly away from Torah, he should immediately return his thought to the tefillin and be vigilant in this. Sefer Mitzvos Shemurim elaborates extensively that one must not divert his attention, for there is no greater spiritual blemish than this, and cites an incident concerning it. See there, and see Magen Avraham, siman 44.
