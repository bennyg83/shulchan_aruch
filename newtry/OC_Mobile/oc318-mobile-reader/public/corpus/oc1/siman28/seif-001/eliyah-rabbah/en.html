[1] To handle the tefillin every hour, etc. Kal vachomer from the tzitz, which has only one Divine Name — the Torah said it shall be always on his forehead that he not be distracted. The Bach wrote: we have not heard what this "handling" means; shall he never remove his hand from them while they are on him? I wonder. It seems he means thus: a person is obligated to handle them every hour that he remembers them, for immediately after he remembers them he is obligated to handle them so that he not come to distraction of mind — up to here. And Rambam's wording "that he not distract his mind even for a moment" — up to here. And in Piskei Mahariv (Terumat HaDeshen part 2, siman 120) one must handle them so they not move from their place — up to here. And Mahari Luria wrote in Shulchan Aruch: one should not distract his mind from the tefillin even in the middle of prayer, only when engaged in words of Torah and in the Eighteen Blessings of standing prayer. But if his thought strays slightly from Torah he must think immediately and be careful — up to here. And in Sefer Mitzvot Shemurim he greatly expanded on not distracting the mind, for there is no greater flaw than this, and he brought a story — see there; and see Magen Avraham siman 44.
