3) There: that he not divert his mind from them. One who proofreads a sefer Torah to prepare it for the Torah reading on Shabbos while the tefillin are upon him has not acted properly, for he necessarily diverted his mind from the tefillin in order to set his mind, his eyes, and his heart on correcting the sefer Torah properly regarding missing and extra letters, joined and separated words; and sometimes he must write and correct and sew some yeriah; and at times it happens that the weekly parashah is long, or they are attached; and throughout this time he has not escaped hesech hada'as. Petach HaDevir, ot 1. And see there, where he wrote that although there is no place to rebuke him, for Ri and the Tur in siman 44 count hesech hada'as only when he stands in laughter and kalut rosh; but one occupied with his labor and his trade, and his mind is not actually upon them, this is not called hesech hada'as; and so wrote plainly Chayei Adam, klal 14, ot 15. Nevertheless, one who fears the word of Hashem should properly be careful: since we, in our time, do not wear tefillin all day as in the time of the Talmud, at least during that brief time it is proper to be careful of hesech hada'as even when it is not in laughter and kalut rosh. Moreover, Shaagat Aryeh, siman 39, forbade hesech hada'as entirely. And all the more so our master the Arizal and Mitzat Shemurim, of blessed memory, were very stringent in this and explained that it causes a great defect; see Eliyah Rabbah in this siman; certainly every baal nefesh should be stringent. And see there further, that he brought a proof from the words of the Bach, siman 38 seif 7, and the Peri Megadim there, Mishbetzos Zahav, ot 4; see there.
