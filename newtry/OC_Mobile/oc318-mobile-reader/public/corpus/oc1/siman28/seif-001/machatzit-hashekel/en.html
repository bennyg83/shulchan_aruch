(Seif katan 1) At every time, etc. Meaning, whenever he remembers them, as Bach writes. His proof is as follows. We derive the law from the tzitz, which contains only one Divine Name, yet the Torah says, “It shall be upon his forehead continually,” teaching that he may not divert his attention from it. Tefillin, which contain many Divine Names, all the more so. This derivation teaches only that he may not divert his attention from them. It does not teach that he must touch them. Moreover, is it conceivable that throughout the entire time the tefillin are upon him he may never remove his hand from either the tefillin of the arm or the tefillin of the head? Rather, it must mean that whenever he remembers them, he touches them.
So that he not forget them and divert his attention. If he does not remember them, this is nevertheless not called distraction, as Magen Avraham writes in siman 44, seif katan 2; see there.
