Se'if 1: After donning a tallis with tzitzis, he should put on tefillin, for we ascend in holiness and do not descend, etc. This might teach the following case: If a new synagogue was built and both the Aron HaKodesh and a Torah scroll are brought to its entrance, the Aron should enter first and the Torah scroll afterward, based on the principle that we ascend in holiness and do not descend, rather than bringing the Torah scroll first and then the Aron. This appears exactly comparable to our law. Yet this requires study. Parashas Naso states, 'Then the Kehatites, bearers of the sacred objects, shall journey, and they shall erect the Mishkan before their arrival.' Rashi explains 'bearers of the sacred objects' as objects of sanctity. He was troubled that if this referred to the Sanctuary structure, it was the families of Gershon and Merari who carried it; the Kehatites carried only its vessels. Rashi also explains that Gershon and Merari erected the Mishkan before Kehat arrived. Why specify here that Kehat carried sacred objects when that detail seems unrelated, and why not similarly specify what Gershon and Merari carried? The point is that since Kehat carried the holiest objects, they should seemingly have traveled first. Scripture explains why they nevertheless traveled later: Gershon and Merari erected the Mishkan before Kehat arrived. The Kehatites thus found it standing and immediately brought the vessels inside, rather than leaving the Aron outside its place while the structure was erected. Thus, although they bore the sacred objects and should have preceded everyone, it was preferable that they travel last so the Mishkan would be standing upon their arrival. This implies that without that reason, the more sacred Aron should have traveled first. Accordingly, if an Aron HaKodesh and Torah scroll are both waiting to enter, the Torah scroll should enter first and the Aron afterward, since the Aron will immediately follow and the scroll will be placed inside it. This proves that in such a case we do not apply 'we ascend in holiness and do not descend.' Why, then, does our law require the tallis first and tefillin afterward because we ascend in holiness? Regarding the Mishkan, absent the practical reason for erecting the structure before Kehat arrived, the Aron would have traveled before the Mishkan; we would not have sent the less sacred Mishkan first under the principle of ascending in holiness. There, greater sanctity takes precedence, while here the opposite appears true. This requires further study.
