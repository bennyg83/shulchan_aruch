Section 1. After one dons a tallit with tzitzit he puts on tefillin, because "we ascend in holiness and do not descend," etc. NB: From this one might learn: if they built a new synagogue and came to bring in the aron hakodesh and a sefer Torah, and both came to the doorway — one carrying the aron hakodesh and one carrying the sefer Torah — the aron should enter first and then the sefer Torah, by force of "we ascend in holiness and do not descend," and not the reverse (sefer Torah first and then the aron). This is exactly analogous to this law. But if so, there is difficulty: in Parashat Nasso it is written "and the Kehatites who carry the sacred objects shall set out, and they shall erect the Mishkan before they arrive," and Rashi explains "who carry the sacred objects" — the holy vessels. It was difficult for him: if it refers to the Mishkan itself, behold the sons of Gershon and Merari carried the Mishkan, whereas the Kohathites did not carry the structure of the Mishkan but only the Mishkan vessels. Therefore he explained the intent is "the sacred objects." He also wrote "and they shall erect the Mishkan before they arrive" — the sons of Gershon and Merari erected the Mishkan until the sons of Kohath came. It is difficult: per his explanation here that the sons of Kohath carried the sacred objects — that is not the subject here; why did he not explain what the sons of Gershon and Merari carried? But the intent is: since the sons of Kohath carried sacred objects, it was more fitting that they go first before everyone; therefore he gives the reason that they traveled now because in the meantime they were erecting the Mishkan until they arrived; when the sons of Kohath came they found the Mishkan standing and immediately put in the vessels, and the ark did not need to stand outside in the meantime. But if they had traveled first, the ark would have lain out of place before the Mishkan was erected; therefore he said "and the Kehatites shall set out," even though they bore the sacred Mishkan objects and it would have been fitting for them to go first, nevertheless since "they shall erect the Mishkan before they arrive" it is better that they travel last. This implies that otherwise, if not for this reason, the ark would travel first, for the more holy goes first. So too if both aron hakodesh and sefer Torah stand ready to enter, the sefer Torah should enter first and then the aron, since immediately after the aron enters he will place the sefer Torah in it. Per this reasoning it is proved that we do not say in such a case "we ascend in holiness and do not descend" — and if so, why here does he place the tallit first and then tefillin by force of maalin bekodesh? There it implies that otherwise the ark would travel first and we would not say maalin bekodesh to have the Mishkan travel first; rather necessarily the more holy is preferred — yet here it seems the opposite. Requires investigation.
