In Magen Avraham, se'if katan 4: According to their view, it is difficult: What proof does he bring from wrapping, where one object becomes a protective covering for the other? His intent appears to be as follows. How did the Gemara know that when there is no alternative, one sacred object may be placed upon another? Perhaps placing one upon another is forbidden when their levels of sanctity are equal, while wrapping is different: There is no degradation to the lower object because the upper object rests upon it only as a protective covering. Although their sanctity is equal, one may be wrapped around the other when there is no alternative. But how does this prove that merely placing one upon another, without the function of protection, is permitted? Magen Avraham then cites the Gemara's wording, 'But one sheet rests upon the other, etc.' This wording implies that the concern is the mere fact that one rests upon the other; the degradation of the lower sheet. The Gemara does not ask the reverse: When they are wrapped, the upper sheet protects the lower and the upper is thereby degraded. The Gemara must consider the degradation of the lower object, upon which another rests, more serious than the degradation of the upper object that serves as a covering. If so, when a bound Torah-Nevi'im-Kesuvim scroll is made, it would have been preferable to roll it from the end toward the beginning, so that a sheet of Nevi'im would not rest upon the Torah scroll and degrade the Torah. When rolled from the end toward the beginning, the Torah rests upon Nevi'im. Although the Torah thereby serves as its protective covering, this is still preferable to placing the Torah beneath Nevi'im. One might say that it is more important that the Torah not serve as a covering for something less sacred, but who established this reasoning? Perhaps the concern is only placement; serving as a covering is no different from ordinary placement. Rashba writes in his novellae that where another arrangement is possible, it is forbidden even to place one object upon another. Certainly, then, wrapping tefillin straps around the tefillin should be forbidden: The straps have a lower sanctity. What source permits placing them upon the tefillin merely because they serve as a covering? Perhaps wrapping was permitted only when no alternative exists, just as ordinary placement was. Here an alternative exists, as Magen Avraham ultimately concludes, because the straps can be placed along the sides. Magen Avraham answers, 'One can say that regarding placement, etc.' He means that although ordinary placement is permitted only when no alternative exists, wrapping objects of unequal sanctity is different. There, the claim that there is no alternative does not apply, for one could place the Torah over Nevi'im by rolling the bound scroll from its end toward its beginning. Nevertheless, because one object becomes a covering for the other, it is preferable that the less sacred object protect the more sacred one rather than the more sacred protect the less sacred. Although this results in the more sacred object lying beneath the less sacred, we are more particular that the more sacred not serve as a covering for the less sacred than we are about placement. Magen Avraham writes concerning the Gemara, 'This is its meaning: But one sheet rests upon the other, etc.; and it becomes a protective covering for the other, etc.' His intent appears to be that under the initial assumption that placement is more serious than serving as a covering, the Gemara could not be explained this way: Why would it leave aside the issue of placement and ask because the sheet serves as a covering? Now, however, Magen Avraham has taught that we are more particular about an object serving as a covering than about its placement. The Gemara's words can therefore be understood: 'But one sheet rests upon the other.' In any event, one of the sheets is degraded, whether the lower one or the upper. If you say the lower is not degraded because the upper serves as its covering, then the obvious concern is the degradation of the upper, which has become a covering for the other. The Gemara necessarily permits this when there is no alternative. Certainly, then, mere placement is permitted when no alternative exists, since there is greater reason for concern when one object serves as a covering. Magen Avraham concludes that regarding tefillin straps, however, they can be placed along the sides. That is, Tosafos's resolution concerning a bound Torah-Nevi'im-Kesuvim scroll works well because there is no alternative. Either the more sacred object rests on the less sacred; which avoids the placement prohibition but causes the more sacred to serve as a covering; or the reverse arrangement creates concern because the less sacred rests upon the more sacred. It is therefore reasonable to say that serving as a covering is the greater concern. Where one can arrange matters so that nothing rests upon the sacred object, however; even though placing the straps at the sides means they will not serve as a covering; one may not override the prohibition of placement merely to obtain a covering. This explanation resolves Magen Avraham's statement that regarding placement, since one object does not become a covering, it may be placed upon another only when there is no alternative. His wording implies that if it did become a covering, it would be permitted even when another arrangement was possible. How, then, can he conclude that tefillin straps must be placed at the sides when possible? Why should that matter, if placing them on top is permitted because they protect the tefillin compartments? It is strained to distinguish by saying that when both objects have equal sanctity, serving as a covering permits placement even when another arrangement is possible, whereas when their sanctity is unequal the less sacred may not rest on the more sacred despite serving as its covering. The opposite is more logical: If the less sacred serves as a covering, the only degradation is to that upper, less sacred object, which should not concern us; whereas with equal sanctity, serving as a covering should still be forbidden when another arrangement is possible because of the upper object's degradation. Indeed, it is clear that equal levels of sanctity may not be wrapped one around another when an alternative exists. Magen Avraham explains the Gemara's question, 'But one sheet rests upon the other,' as meaning that one becomes a covering; it is permitted only because no alternative exists. Thus, with equal sanctity, it is forbidden when an alternative exists even though one protects the other, because the upper object is degraded. Magen Avraham's wording; that regarding placement, since it does not become a covering, etc.; appears to imply that serving as a covering always permits it. According to my explanation, however, his words are readily understood. He means only that when one object does not serve as a covering, placement is forbidden whenever an alternative exists. When it does serve as a covering, there are cases where placement is permitted despite an alternative; for example, with objects of greater and lesser sanctity. One could place the more sacred on the less sacred, but it would then protect the less sacred; we therefore permit placing the less sacred upon the more sacred, so that the less sacred protects the more sacred. If it is possible to avoid placing either object upon the other entirely, even though neither will then have a protective covering, that presents no concern. This is why the tefillin straps may not be placed on top when they can be placed at the sides. Accordingly, when Magen Avraham says that with ordinary placement, since one object does not become a covering, he does not mean that becoming a covering itself permits one object to rest upon another. He means that where one serves as a covering, we sometimes override the placement concern and place one on top despite another possible arrangement, so that the more sacred object will not have to protect the less sacred. Thus, when wrapping objects of unequal sanctity, the less sacred is wrapped around the more sacred even though a sheet of Nevi'im will rest upon the Torah; this is nevertheless preferable to wrapping the more sacred around the less sacred, for it is degrading to a Torah scroll to serve as a protective covering for Nevi'im. Tefillin straps, however, cannot be permitted merely because they would serve as a covering. Magen Avraham initially understood Tosafos to mean that Nevi'im's serving as a covering for the Torah itself creates the permission and overrides the placement concern; he therefore thought the same should permit the tefillin straps. He ultimately concludes that the permission exists only because the Torah cannot be made to protect Nevi'im. Where it is possible to avoid placing anything on top at all, serving as a covering provides no permission. Since many find Magen Avraham's words here difficult to understand, I have written what appears correct to me.
