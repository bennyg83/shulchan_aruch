Magen Avraham note 4) And according to their words it is difficult: what proof does he bring that in binding one becomes a guard for the other? It seems his intent is: from where does the Gemara learn that it is permitted to place where impossible? Perhaps placement is forbidden when sanctities are equal, and binding is different because of degrading the lower one — here the upper one placed upon it is only in the capacity of a guard; and even when sanctities are equal we permit one upon the other when impossible, unlike placement, which without the status of guarding — from where do we learn it is permitted? He then wrote the language "behold a page sits," etc. — meaning this language implies we are strict about one lying upon the other because of degrading the lower, and he did not say the reverse that when wrapping the upper becomes guard for the lower and there is degradation of the upper; rather perforce the Gemara is stricter about degrading the lower that one lies upon it than about the upper becoming a guard. So too regarding binding Tanach: he should roll from end to beginning so a prophets' page not lie on the sefer Torah, degrading the sefer Torah in that Nevi'im lie upon it — unlike when rolling from end to beginning that Torah lies on Nevi'im; and even though it becomes a guard, nevertheless it is better than if Nevi'im were under. And even though one may say the lower should not be guard for lighter sanctity, nevertheless better. Who told them this idea of becoming a guard — perhaps we are strict only regarding placement and no more, and even when a guard is made it is like ordinary placement? In Rashba's chiddushim he wrote: where possible, even one upon the other is forbidden; if so, all the more so forbidden to wrap straps on tefillin, for straps are lighter sanctity. If because of guarding one would be lenient — from where? Perhaps only where impossible is it permitted, as with ordinary placement; and here it is possible, as he concludes finally, to place on the sides. Magen Avraham wrote: one may say regarding placement, etc. — meaning although in placement we permit only when impossible, in wrapping where sanctities are not equal it does not depend on impossibility, for one may place Torah on Nevi'im by rolling end to beginning; nevertheless, since a guard is made, it is better that lighter sanctity guard the stricter than the stricter guard the lighter, even though thereby the stricter lies under the lighter — they are more strict that the stricter not be guard for the lighter than that it lie under. What Magen Avraham wrote in the Gemara "behold a page sits," etc. — "and one becomes guard for the other," etc. — seems his intent: per the initial thought that placement is stricter than guard, one could not explain thus — why did the Gemara abandon placement and challenge from guard? Now that Magen Avraham taught we are stricter about guard than placement, the Gemara's words are well explained: it challenges "behold a page sits" — and if so, in any case there is degradation of one of them, lower or upper; if you say no degradation of the lower because the upper becomes guard, it is obvious from degradation of the upper; perforce when impossible it is permitted; if so, all the more so in placement where guard must be stricter, and even so permitted when impossible. He concludes regarding straps: since one may place them on the sides, etc. — meaning specifically Tosafos regarding binding Tanach fits this answer: there it is impossible except either placing the stricter on the lighter — no prohibition from placement — nevertheless there is prohibition because the stricter becomes guard; and conversely there is concern from placement. Therefore one may say guard is stricter than placement; unlike where possible not to place, so that even on the sides there is no guard — nevertheless one may not waive placement prohibition for that. Per what I wrote, Shaarei Teshuvah's words that Magen Avraham wrote regarding placement, since no guard is made it is forbidden, etc. — imply that if a guard were made in placement it would be permitted even where possible; if so, how does he conclude regarding straps that since possible on the sides, etc. — what of it? It would still be permitted on top since it becomes guard for the batim? Forced to say: specifically one upon the other with equal sanctities we permit in such a case if a guard were made even where possible; unlike unequal sanctities, forbidden to place lighter on stricter even if a guard is made, since possible — that is, rather the opposite: since a guard is made and here is only degradation of the upper, and since lighter sanctity is involved, we do not care; unlike equal sanctities. Moreover it is simple that with equal sanctities it is forbidden even where a guard is made when possible, for Magen Avraham explains the challenge "behold a page sits" — meaning a guard is made, but since impossible we permit; evidently when possible it is forbidden when equal sanctities and one becomes guard for the other because of upper degradation. If so, Magen Avraham's language "regarding placement, since no guard is made," etc. — implies that becoming a guard in every manner permits; per what I wrote, it means: specifically when no guard is made it is forbidden in every possible case; unlike when a guard is made, then sometimes permitted even where possible — e.g. stricter and lighter sanctity, where one may place stricter on lighter and nevertheless permit placement because a guard is made, for they are strict that the stricter not be guard for the lighter; unlike where possible not to place at all, so even without guard we do not care. Therefore straps are forbidden since possible, etc. Per this, what Magen Avraham wrote regarding placement since no guard is made does not mean guard causes permission to place one upon the other; only that when a guard is made we sometimes waive placement prohibition and place upon even where possible above, so as not to make a guard for what is beneath; therefore in wrapping, where a guard is made with unequal sanctities, wrap the lighter on the stricter even though a prophets' page sits on Torah. Nevertheless better than wrapping stricter on lighter, for it is degrading for sefer Torah to be guard for Nevi'im; therefore straps may not be permitted because they would become guards. Initially Magen Avraham thought that per Tosafos, Nevi'im being guards for sefer Torah causes permission and waives placement — therefore straps were permitted; finally he concludes this is only because impossible to make sefer Torah guard for Nevi'im, but where possible not to place at all there is no permission because of guard. Because many find Magen Avraham's words here difficult, I wrote this to my humble opinion.
