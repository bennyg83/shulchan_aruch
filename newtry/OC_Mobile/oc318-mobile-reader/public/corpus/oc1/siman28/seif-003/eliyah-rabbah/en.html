[3] Levush: Because it is written “and they shall be,” etc. Rashi explains that the teaching is derived from the plural word “and they shall be.” This is not conclusive, for perhaps “and they shall be” refers to the preceding matters, just as does “and you shall bind them.” Levush therefore explains that the teaching is derived from the word “totafos,” which is read in the plural. Malbushei Yom Tov and Lechem Chamudos, page 95, challenge Levush. If so, why is the phrase “and they shall be” needed? The verse could have said, “You shall bind them as a sign upon your hand and as totafos,” for the knot of the head is also required. Since it is not written that way, the knot of the head must be a halachah transmitted to Moshe at Sinai. But then the verse could have been written that way, and “you shall bind them” would refer also to the head, leaving only the form of the knot, as with the arm, to be transmitted orally. In my humble opinion, the Gemara in Menachos 36a derives that the tefillin of the arm are put on first from the sequence: “You shall bind them as a sign upon your hand,” and afterward, “and they shall be as totafos.” One might ask that the Torah necessarily had to mention one before the other. It is also difficult why the Gemara did not derive the same from parashas Bo, where it says first, “It shall be as a sign upon your hand,” and afterward, “as totafos,” while the verse “you shall bind them” appears later in Vaeschanan. It therefore appears that the Gemara's derivation is from the word “and they shall be”: after the tefillin of the arm, the tefillin of the head shall then be present. This may be compared to the exposition in Berachos that “and they shall be” means they must remain in their proper order and may not be read backward. Otherwise, why is “and they shall be” needed, since “you shall bind them” was already stated regarding the preceding passages? The derivation is not merely from the order of the verses. If it were, one might say that the order applies only initially, but if one encountered the tefillin of the head first, the rule against passing over a mitzvah should prevail. Rather, this is learned from “and they shall be.” Accordingly, this cannot be learned from parashas Bo, where this precise plural expression is absent. Although it says there “and they shall be as a sign,” that phrase, as Levush writes in seif 9, teaches that both tefillin share a single state of validity. This also answers the question why “and they shall be” is needed. Yet according to Levush, who derives from “totafos” that whenever the tefillin are between your eyes there must be two upon you, why is another verse required to teach that the tefillin of the arm are put on first? Without his interpretation, one could say that the Gemara derives the order merely from the sequence of the verse, in keeping with the rule that whatever appears first in a verse precedes for a blessing. Then, once the Gemara concludes that whenever they are between your eyes there must be two, the earlier derivation becomes unnecessary. Me'adanei Yom Tov writes similarly. According to Levush, however, the difficulty remains. One may answer that from the word “totafos” alone we would know only that both must be present at the moment of putting them on, but not throughout the entire time. A clear proof is that Levush, in siman 25, seif 6, and at the beginning of siman 26, gives as the reason for putting on the tefillin of the arm first that the Torah mentioned them first. This would be unnecessary if the principle “whenever they are between your eyes there must be two” already established both laws. We must therefore conclude that both cannot be learned from the same source. One may also say that from the principle alone we would not know that, if one encountered the tefillin of the head first, he must pass over that mitzvah and take the tefillin of the arm. It would teach only the initial order. This is implied in siman 25, at the end of seif 1. My father, the Rav, challenged my interpretation from Levush's words that the reason one blesses over the tefillin of the arm first is that the verse mentioned them first. This shows that Levush also learns from the order of the verse, not from the extra word “and they shall be.” I answered that even if the teaching comes from “and they shall be,” its meaning is that one should act according to the order established in the verse. Another answer to the main question is that “and they shall be” is needed lest one think that because whenever the tefillin are between your eyes there must be two, one who lacks tefillin of the arm may not put on the tefillin of the head alone. The word “and they shall be” teaches that they are two independent mitzvos, as explained above in siman 26. The implication in Malbushei Yom Tov and Lechem Chamudos that “you shall bind them” does not refer to the tefillin of the arm requires examination. See what I wrote in siman 27, seifim 7 and 8, and Tosafos on Menachos 36a, s.v. “from the time.” Levush's statement that the teaching is derived from the word “totafos” is also found in Sefer HaTerumos, siman 211: “The word totafos implies two.” This source also escaped Me'adanei Yom Tov. Nevertheless, Rashi's view appears correct. If the teaching came from “totafos,” the Gemara should have derived it from the verse “and it shall be when Hashem brings you,” where “totafos” is also written defectively, as Rashi explains in Sanhedrin 4b. Furthermore, this would be difficult according to the authority who maintains that the written form is determinative.
