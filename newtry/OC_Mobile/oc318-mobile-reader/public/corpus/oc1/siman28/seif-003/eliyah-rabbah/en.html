[3] [Levush] As it is written "and they shall be," etc. Rashi explained that from "and they shall be," which is plural, he derives it; but it is not necessary — perhaps "and they shall be" refers to the matters above, as with "and you shall bind them." Therefore it seems he derives from that we read totafot missing a vav in cholam, which is plural (Levush). In Malbushei Yom Tov and Lechem Chamudot folio 95 they challenged the Levush: if so, why do I need "and they shall be" — let it write only "and you shall bind them for a sign upon your hand and for totafot," for the head binding is also required. And since it is not written thus, it is halachah leMoshe miSinai; if so, let it write "and you shall again bind also upon the head" and we would not need halachah leMoshe miSinai except for the form as with the shel yad — up to his words. It seems to my humble opinion that the Gemara in Menachot 33a learns to place the shel yad first from "and you shall bind them for a sign upon your hand" and then "and they shall be for totafot." It is difficult: perhaps the verse was forced to write one before the other. It is also somewhat difficult why he did not learn from Parashat Bo, where it is written "and it shall be for a sign upon your hand" and then "and for totafot," and the verse of "and you shall bind them" is later in Parashat Vaetchanan. One cannot say he inferred because the Merciful One wrote a second time that the hand precedes and did not change — sometimes this first and sometimes that first — as with Moshe and Aharon; that is not implied in the Gemara's language. Moreover, in Parashat Bo it is also written earlier in Parashat Kadesh "and it shall be for a sign upon your hand," and then "for a remembrance between your eyes." Therefore it seems the Gemara's teaching is from the word "and it shall be" — meaning "and then it shall be for totafot" after the shel yad comes the shel rosh. It may be explained as they expounded in Berachot: "and they shall be" in their being — it shall be, and that one not read it out of order; if not, why do I need "and they shall be," for the matters were already stated with "and you shall bind them," as the Levush's difficulty — but he did not learn precedence from it. If so, the difficulty is perhaps precedence ab initio, but not if one happened to put on the shel rosh first to transgress the mitzvah; rather we learn from "and it shall be." If so, one cannot learn from Parashat Bo, where "and they shall be" is not written, even though there it is written at the head of the verse "and they shall be for a sign" — that is as the Levush wrote in section 9, that there shall be one being for both, and thereby their difficulty "why do I need and they shall be" is rejected, for we need "and they shall be" as I explained. But it is difficult: if we now learn from totafot per the Levush that whenever they are between your eyes both shall be on, why do I need a verse that one places the shel yad first? Without his interpretation I would say the Gemara's explanation "and then and they shall be for totafot" is not from superfluity but because the verse preceded it, as we say: whatever is earlier in the verse precedes for blessing; if so, the Gemara's conclusion that whenever they are between your eyes both shall be on reverts. We really do not need a derashah to place the shel yad first — the verse itself comes for that, as Maadanei Melech wrote there; but per his interpretation that he learns from "and it shall be," it is difficult. One may say the Levush holds that from the derashah of totafot we do not learn that whenever they are between your eyes both shall be on, but only at the time of placement and not always. A clear proof: the Levush wrote in siman 25 section 6 and at the beginning of siman 26 that the reason for shel yad first is that Scripture preceded it — which is wondrous, for we learn from totafot that whenever they are between your eyes both shall be on, as I explained; rather perforce we do not hear both from him. Requires investigation. One may also say that from the derashah of "whenever" alone we do not hear that if one happened to put on the shel rosh first he transgresses — only that ab initio he should take the shel yad; so it implies in siman 25 end of section 1. My master my father the rabbi challenged me on the main part of my interpretation of the Levush above, who wrote the reason to bless the shel yad first is Scripture's precedence; behold the Levush also learned from Scripture's precedence and not from superfluity of "and it shall be." I replied that even though we learn from "and it shall be," nevertheless the verse teaches that one do what is earlier in the verse, as we say. One may further answer the main difficulty: perhaps we need "and it shall be" lest we say that since whenever they are between your eyes both shall be on, if he has no shel yad he may not place the shel rosh alone; therefore it is written "and they shall be" to teach that they are two mitzvot, as above siman 26. And what seems from the words of Malbushei Yom Tov and Lechem Chamudot that I brought, that "and you shall bind them" does not refer to the shel yad — see what I wrote in siman 27 sections 7 and 8, and in Tosafos Menachot 36a s.v. mishe'at, etc.; requires investigation. What the Levush wrote, that we read totafot, etc. — I found in Sefer HaTerumot siman 211: the language of totafot implies two — up to here. This was also omitted by Maadanei Melech — see there. In any case Rashi's view seems to me because it is difficult for him: if from totafot the Gemara should learn from "and it shall be" in "when He brings you," where totafot is also written deficiently, as Rashi explained in Sanhedrin 4a — up to here. Moreover, if so, it is difficult for one who says there is a tradition for the missing letter.
