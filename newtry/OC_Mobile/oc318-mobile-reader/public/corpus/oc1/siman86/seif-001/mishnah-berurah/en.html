(1) Foul water — it is certain that those mikvaot whose water is foul in summer days — needless to say it is forbidden to bless within them; we also require distancing like for feces [Peri Megadim]. And specifically if it is foul to the point that people are accustomed to suffer from that odor. A garden into which foul bad water is channeled, and it is adjacent to the synagogue — even if the garden owner preceded, one must distance, for it is a matter of prohibition [Shaarei Teshuvah]:

(3) Like, etc. — meaning before him as far as his eyes can see, and behind him four amot from where the odor ceased. Peri Megadim wrote that its prohibition is from the Torah like feces. Ateret Tzvi wrote, brought in Peri Megadim: in wine cellars where there is a bad moldy odor and it disgusts one, it is forbidden to recite Shema. And likewise all such cases. And in Rambam chapter 4 of Laws of Prayer halachah 9 it implies it is permitted, and only the great sages were careful about this at the time of prayer — see there.
