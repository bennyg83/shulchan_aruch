(א) Foul water—that is, those mikvaos whose water becomes foul in the summer months; it goes without saying that it is forbidden to recite a berachah within them, for we require distancing as from excrement [Peri Megadim]. And specifically when it stinks to the point that people are accustomed to be distressed by that smell. A garden into which foul and bad water flows, and it is adjacent to a beit kenesset—even if the garden owner preceded [in establishing the garden], one must distance, for it is a matter of prohibition [Shaarei Teshuvah].

(ב) Or steeping water—that is foul—and all the more so the soak of launderers in which they launder small garments that contain excrement [Shnos Eliyahu].

(ג) "Like," etc.—that is, before him as far as his eyes can see, and behind him four amos from where the smell ends; and the Peri Megadim wrote that its prohibition is from the Torah, like excrement. The Eliyah Rabbah wrote, and it is cited in the Peri Megadim: In wine cellars that have a moldy bad smell and disgust one, it is forbidden to read Kerias Shema there; and the same applies to anything of this kind. And in Rambam, chapter 4 of Hilchos Tefillah, halachah 9, it implies that it is permitted, and only the great sages were careful about this at the time of prayer—see there; and it is surprising that they did not note this.
