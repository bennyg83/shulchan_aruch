Provided it was first surrounded and afterwards settled, etc. If you ask: Since it says that a city first settled and afterwards surrounded is judged like a village, how can a city surrounded by a wall from the days of Yehoshua bin Nun read on the 15th? Perhaps it was settled first and only afterwards surrounded. One can answer that the settlement of non-Jews is not considered settlement. It follows that when Yehoshua conquered it, it had a wall but was not considered settled until Jews settled there, etc. Yet there remains room to analyze cities surrounded by walls outside Eretz Yisrael. Perhaps their settlement preceded their walls, in which case they are considered as though they have no wall. One must therefore say that we follow the majority, for in most cases their walls precede their settlement, since the practice of most countries is first to surround a place with a wall and only afterwards settle it.
