(2) and was delayed and did not return, etc. Rosh and Tur's method: we follow his intent when he left his place, and both actually agree: walled-city resident who went to town and intent to delay there until day of the 14th lights — and so it was, delayed there at day's beginning — obligation of town's reading falls on him and he is unwalled of his day; but if he returned to his place that was walled before the day — what he thought at first to be in town at town's reading time does not help. And townsman who went to walled city and intent to delay there at beginning of day of the 14th — and so delayed there for Rashi — does not help him to be like walled-city resident, for he sits idle there and there is no reading and nothing applies to him; therefore he must read like his town. But for Rosh and Tur also here he departed from his town's custom in wine, for at time his town's reading begins he is not there and he intended to be in walled city — therefore even if he returns to his town on the 14th he reads on the 15th, since his obligation at its beginning departed — voided entirely; and this is called walled of his day. Beit Yosef and Kessef Mishneh wrote per this method the explanation of unwalled of his day is not comparable to explanation of walled of his day — for walled of his day means he lost his town's day which is the 14th, i.e. he intended to delay there on the 14th — called walled and his townspeople's obligation departed; and unwalled of his day means he delayed in town on the 14th which is reading time of place he went — and obligation of that day's reading fell on him — end; and I did not understand his words: what difference between them, for in both we require at time of his going from his place his intent to delay in place he goes there at beginning of day of the 14th — and so it is here, each changed from his place's custom; except reason not equal: for walled-city resident the reason obligation of town fell on him on the 14th since they read then; and for townsman the reason too since he intended to delay there at beginning in walled city — automatically his place's law departed; and Rambam's words on this seif appear to accord with this: if he did not intend to return except after reading time of the 14th — automatically obligation of town's reading falls on walled-city resident and townsman departed from his reading obligation — therefore each reads with people of place he is: walled-city resident reads on the 14th per place where he is; townsman reads on the 15th in place where he is at reading time of the 14th — even if he returned home after departure he reads on the 15th; and since Rambam did not mention this explicitly it appears he holds he must delay in walled city until the 15th and read with them; and per this it settles well more "people of place where he is" — always reads with people of place: whether townsman on the 15th in walled city or walled-city resident on the 14th in town; and that he tied delay to beginning of day of the 14th after reading time per Rambam's language is difficult: why not follow night reading — if townsman's intent all night in walled city with day's beginning and so acted — automatically departed obligation of his town's night reading; and possible Rambam truly intended night reading which is whole night; and likewise difficult on Tur who wrote but if intent to stand there part of the 14th — since no intent to return to his place at obligation time of his place — departed reading of his place;
