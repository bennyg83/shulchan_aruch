If his intent was to return to his place at the time of reading. Meaning, of the fourteenth. We derive it from what is written: "therefore the Jews of the unwalled towns who dwell in unwalled towns." Since it already says "the Jews of the unwalled towns," why does it need to write "who dwell in unwalled towns"? It teaches us that an unwalled-town resident for a day is called unwalled. Automatically, a walled-city resident for a day is also called walled.
And he was delayed and did not return, etc. The approach of the Rosh and the Tur is that we follow his intent when he went from his place, and both cases are exactly equal. A resident of a walled city who went to an unwalled town and intended to remain there until the daylight of the fourteenth, and so it happened that he remained there at the beginning of the day, has the obligation of the town's reading fall upon him, and he is an unwalled-town resident for a day. But if he returned to his place, which was a walled city, before the day, it does not help that he initially thought to be in the town at the town's reading time. A resident of an unwalled town who went to a walled city and intended to remain there at the beginning of the fourteenth day, and so it happened that he remained there, according to Rashi this does not help to say that he becomes like a resident of a walled city, for he is sitting idle there and there is no reading there, and there is nothing to take effect upon him. Therefore he must read like his town. But according to the Rosh and Tur, in this case too the custom of his town is removed from him, since at the time the reading time of his town begins he is not there and he intended to be in the walled city. Therefore, even if he returns to his town on the fourteenth, he reads on the fifteenth, because once his obligation was removed at its beginning, it is entirely nullified. This is called a walled-city resident for a day. Beis Yosef and Kesef Mishneh wrote that according to this approach, the explanation of an unwalled-town resident for a day is not comparable to the explanation of a walled-city resident for a day. For walled-city resident for a day means that he lost the day of his town, which is the fourteenth, namely that he intended to remain there on the fourteenth, and he is called walled and is removed from the obligation of the people of his place. An unwalled-town resident for a day means that he remained in the unwalled town on the fourteenth, which is the reading day of the place to which he went, and the obligation of that day's reading fell upon him; end quote. I did not understand his speech, for what difference is there between them? In both we require that when he goes from his place he intends to remain in the place to which he is going at the beginning of the fourteenth day, and that this actually occurs. Through this, each one is changed from the custom of his place. The reason is not the same: with the resident of a walled city, the reason is that the obligation of the town takes effect upon him on the fourteenth, since they read then; with the resident of an unwalled town, the reason is also that since he intended to remain there at the beginning in the walled city, he is automatically removed from the law of his place. The words of the Rambam in this section also appear to be directed this way. If he did not intend to return until after the time of reading, meaning after the reading of the fourteenth, automatically the reading obligation of the town falls upon the resident of the walled city, and the resident of the unwalled town is removed from his own reading obligation. Therefore each reads with the people of the place where he is: the resident of the walled city reads on the fourteenth according to the place where he is, and the resident of the unwalled town reads on the fifteenth in the place where he is at the time of the reading of the fourteenth. This means even if he returned to his place after the removal, he reads on the fifteenth. Since the Rambam did not mention this explicitly, that even if he returned to his place, it appears that he holds that he must remain in the walled city until the fifteenth and read with them. According to this, the phrase "the people of the place where he is" is settled even better, meaning that he always reads with the people of the place: the resident of an unwalled town on the fifteenth in the walled city, and the resident of a walled city on the fourteenth in the town. That which the delay depends on the beginning of the fourteenth day after the time of reading, according to the language of the Rambam, is difficult: why should we not follow the night reading? If the resident of an unwalled town intended to be the whole night in the walled city through the beginning of the day, and he did so, automatically he was removed from the obligation of the night reading according to the custom of his town. Perhaps the Rambam truly intended the night reading, which is the whole night. So too it is difficult regarding the Tur, who wrote: but if he intended to stand there for part of the fourteenth, since he did not intend to return to his place at the time of the obligation of his place, he was removed from the reading of his place. In the Gemara as well, Rava mentioned only intent to return on the night of the fourteenth, and did not mention part of the day. It appears that the Tur too intended to say that this is enough: if he intended to remain there the whole night alone. But the measure of the night reading is perhaps, in pressing circumstances, the whole night until sunrise, as with Shema in siman 235. The "part of the fourteenth" that the Tur used means from dawn. This requires analysis.
