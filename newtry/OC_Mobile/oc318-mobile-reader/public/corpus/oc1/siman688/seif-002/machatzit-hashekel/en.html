Seif katan 2: Presumably, etc. "And ordinary towns are surrounded, etc." That is to say, he wrote that a gentile's objects acquire their Shabbos residence in their place. If a gentile took them outside their techum and brought them into a city surrounded for dwelling, although they have only four amos, like the law of one who left the techum, nevertheless the entire city, since it is surrounded for dwelling, is considered like four amos. About this he said, ordinary towns are surrounded, etc.
And it appears to me that for this reason, etc. For what the Ran wrote, that presumably it was surrounded and then settled, according to Beis Yosef is forced by his difficulty: how will we know outside the Land which city was surrounded and then settled, so as to read in it like the law of a walled city or like a town? Granted, in Eretz Yisrael, the dwelling of gentiles in the days of Yehoshua is not called dwelling, and since afterward Jews came and settled it, and it had already been surrounded before Jews settled it, it is surrounded and then settled. But outside the Land, where it depends on gentile dwelling, how do we know that it was surrounded and then settled? To this he answered that we follow the majority, and most towns are surrounded and then settled. But Maharam follows his own view, for he holds above in siman 401 the opposite, that ordinary towns are surrounded for dwelling, meaning settled and afterward surrounded; the Ran's difficulty therefore returns to its place. Therefore he proved that what we require for megillah, that it be surrounded and then settled, is not literal; the same law applies if it was settled first but their intent from the outset at the time of settlement was to surround it. This is called surrounded and then settled, for we follow their intent. The Ran's difficulty is then settled, for most towns, at the time of settlement, intend to surround them, and this is called surrounded and then settled.
And Rama's opinion requires analysis. That is to say, he wrote that presumably it was surrounded and then settled, which are the words of the Ran. Aside from the fact that above in siman 401 he ruled like Maharam, that ordinary towns are surrounded for dwelling, which is the opposite of what he wrote here, here too he contradicts himself. For the Mechaber wrote, "or if it was settled first with the intention to surround it, etc.," and these are the words of Maharam mentioned above, which are forced by the Ran's difficulty: outside the Land, how will we know whether it was surrounded and then settled, as above? Perforce one must say that he holds that ordinary towns are not surrounded and then settled, as the Ran wrote, but on the contrary are ordinarily surrounded for dwelling. For this, one must say that intent to surround it suffices, and such is the ordinary case with towns, as above. If so, when Rama wrote that presumably it was surrounded and then settled, which are the words of the Ran to settle the above difficulty, from where does he know Maharam's law that if their intent was to surround it, it suffices? For now the Ran's difficulty is settled without this. If so, granted that Maharam's law can be applied to ordinary towns, but the reason is that ordinarily they were settled with intent to surround them according to Maharam; yet Rama's language, saying that presumably it was surrounded and then settled, which are the words of the Ran, requires analysis.
