Provided that they are not distant, etc. In the Gemara this measure is stated, and Rashi explained that it applies to adjacent, for it explains until where it is called adjacent. This implies that regarding visible but not adjacent, they did not give a measure; rather, whenever it is visible, even from afar, it is included. For otherwise, if visible too required a measure, it should not have mentioned visible, only adjacent, since everything would depend on adjacent, meaning within a mil. But from the language of the Tur and Rambam it implies that it applies also to visible. The Maggid Mishneh already wondered about the Rambam because of the difficulty we mentioned, and he needed to emend his words so that it applies only to adjacent alone, as with Rashi. Beis Yosef elaborated on this in various ways. What appears to me clearest is this: they gave a measure of a mil for the level ground between city and city, but not for the slope of the mountain on which the city sits. About this they stated a measure even regarding visible: although we do not count the slope, nevertheless the level ground must not be more than a mil. This is with a mountain, where we count the mil only until the edge of the mountain on which the city sits, because we consider the entire mountain as part of the city, since through the mountain the walled city is visible to this town, and therefore we do not count the slope. But when it sits in a valley, we do count the slope, for there is only the advantage of adjacent, and therefore it must truly be adjacent to the walled city, not more than the measure. I will prove this distinction to you from the sugya. It says: granted, visible even though not adjacent can be found on a mountain; but adjacent even though not visible, how can one find it? Rabbi Yirmeyah said: where it sits in a valley. This has astonished me from my youth: what did the one who asked think? Why was he more comfortable with a mountain? Did he not know that we can find in a valley the same as on a mountain, until Rabbi Yirmeyah came and opened this possibility for him? According to my words, with Heaven's help this is settled. The questioner found it difficult as follows: granted, with a mountain, where the advantage comes from being visible, we properly do not count the slope of the mountain that causes the visibility, as I wrote. But regarding adjacent and not visible, it is difficult: how can one find it? If it sits in a valley and there is a slope down below, you certainly must count the slope toward the measure of a mil, since that slope has no advantage. If so, you must say that the city together with the slope is within the measure; but then it is difficult, for the walled city would certainly be visible to the town if it were in this manner, that is, within the measure of the mil. To this Rabbi Yirmeyah answers: in a valley, meaning that there is a depression without a slope, but like a vertical wall from top to bottom, and below sits the city, and the walled city is not visible to it because it sits above on the side of the wall. Nevertheless, the dwelling of the city is adjacent within a mil. A proof that "valley" means this can be brought from the verse "and the streams of Arnon," where Rashi explained that they were like walls on this side and that side, and the stream was deep between them, like what we mentioned. I am also satisfied regarding another difficulty I had in the Gemara's baraisa: until how far? Rabbi Yirmeyah said: like Chamasan to Teveria, a mil. Why not just say a mil? It teaches us how much the measure of a mil is, like Chamasan to Teveria. This is very difficult: do we not know that the measure of a mil is two thousand amos? The entire tractate Shabbos is full of this. According to my view it is well settled, for Teveria sat in a valley, as Rashi explained in the chapter Kol Kisvei: may my portion be with those who bring in Shabbos in Teveria, because it sits in a valley and it becomes dark while it is still day. This teaches us that the measure of a mil regarding megillah is if the ground slopes like from Chamasan to Teveria. With this everything is settled with Heaven's help. Let it not appear forced in your eyes that the measure of a mil was included regarding visible and adjacent in one measure; nevertheless, the words appear correct, to distinguish by simple reasoning as I wrote, with the matter and its reason. So it appears to me correct, with Heaven's help.
