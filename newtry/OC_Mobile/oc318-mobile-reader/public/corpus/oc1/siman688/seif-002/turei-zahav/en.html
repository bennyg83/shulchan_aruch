Provided they are not distant etc. — gemara gives this measure; Rashi explains "near" — he explains how far is called near — implies by sight and no fixed measure for near was given except whatever appears, even from afar; otherwise if even by sight we need measure, it should mention sight alone not near alone — all depends on nearness, i.e. within a mil; but Tur and Rambam imply also sight. Maggid already wondered on Rambam from our difficulty and had to emend his words that it refers only to nearness alone like Rashi; Beit Yosef extended on this variously — it appears correct: they gave measure of a mil for level ground between city and city, not the slope on a hill the city sits on; therefore they said measure even in sight — though we don't count the slope, the mil on level ground must not exceed a mil; on a hill we don't count the mil only to the hilltop where the city sits — we count the whole hill for the city since by the hill the walled city is seen to this town; we don't count slope; but one sitting in a valley we count slope too since only nearness applies — must be literally near the walled city within measure. I prove this distinction from the sugya: fine, visible though not near — find it on hilltop; near though not visible — how find? R' Yirmiyah: sits in a valley — I long wondered why call it valley — prefer hill, didn't he know we can find in valley like hill until R' Yirmiyah opened this door — per my view the questioner is resolved: visible from elevation side is fine since we don't count hill slope causing sight as above; but near not visible is difficult — how find if sits in valley with slope below? must count slope for mil measure since no elevation advantage — city with slope is within measure; question: surely walled city visible to town in this arrangement within mil measure — R' Yirmiyah answers valley: valley without slope like vertical partition top to bottom, city below, walled city not visible because above the partition — permitted residence is at least near within mil; proof: nachal explained thus from verse "and the nachalim Arnon" — Rashi there: like partitions on both sides, deep nachal between — like what we said. Also resolves gemara difficulty in baraita: R' Yirmiyah said like distance from Hamat to Tiberias a mil — say a mil? teaches measure of mil how much — very difficult, don't we know mil is 2000 amot, whole tractate Shabbat full of this? Per my view Tiberias sat in a valley as Rashi explains "let my portion be with Shabbat-enterers in Tiberias because it sits in a valley and dark from plag" — teaches mil measure for Megillah if ground with slope like Hamat to Tiberias — all resolves well; don't think forced that mil measure included in sight and nearness together — matters appear to divide simply as I said, word matches reason, correct in my humble opinion.
