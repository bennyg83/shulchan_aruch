a) [Seif 1] Walled cities surrounded by a wall from the days of Joshua ben Nun, etc. And that they tied the matter to Joshua and not Moses — because Joshua is the main conquest of Eretz Yisrael. So too Ra'em in Tosafot and likewise Meiri. In Berachot 2b and Orach Chayyim 2b: why did they tie the matter to the days of Joshua? To give honor to Eretz Yisrael that was destroyed at that time, so they would read like Shushanites and be considered as walled cities even though they are now destroyed, since they were walled in Joshua's days they read on the 15th, and it would be remembrance for Eretz Yisrael in the miracle. Rambam chapter 1 of Laws of Megillah halacha 5: the reason they divided this mitzvah into separate days and set a special day for unwalled towns and a special day for walled cities, unlike other mitzvot — Beit Yosef in name of Ran: because Jews in unwalled towns and Jews in Shushan did not rest from enemies on one day, for Persian Jews rested on the 14th and Jews in Shushan on the 15th, and each rejoiced on his day of rest with feasting and joy; therefore when they fixed Yom Tov for generations they fixed each on the day he rested and equated all walled cities to Shushan because the greater miracle was there — see there. And that we do not read Megillah nowadays on Purim on the 11th, 12th, 13th as written at the start of tractate Megillah — because they said in the gemara: R' Yehuda said: when are these days? When years are fixed and Israel dwell on their land; but nowadays since they look at it they read only in its time. Rashi: but nowadays when all these ceased and Israel scattered and messengers of Beit Din do not reach them — all await Megillah reading and say: on the 14th of Adar we read Megillah; if they remain to Adar 15 — Purim and Nisan 15 is Pesach; if they advance reading they make Pesach at end of thirty days of reading and they would eat chametz on last days of Pesach — end of his words; Beit Yosef. And so Rambam in the cited chapter: nowadays one reads only in its time, the 14th and 15th; sons of villages and towns read on the 14th and sons of walled cities on the 15th. And so Tur.
