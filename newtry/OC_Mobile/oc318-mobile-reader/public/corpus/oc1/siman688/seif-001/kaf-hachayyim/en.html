1) [Seif 1] Cities surrounded by a wall from the days of Yehoshua bin Nun, etc. The reason the matter was made dependent on Yehoshua rather than Moshe is that Yehoshua was the principal agent in the conquest of Eretz Yisrael. So wrote the Re'em in his Tosafos, and so too wrote Meiri. Birkei Yosef, letter 2. Erech HaShulchan, letter 2. Why was the matter made dependent on the days of Yehoshua? In order to accord honor to Eretz Yisrael, which was desolate at that time, so that its residents would read like the residents of Shushan and would be considered as though they were cities surrounded by a wall. Even though they are now desolate, since they were surrounded by a wall in the days of Yehoshua they read on the 15th, and there will thereby be a remembrance of Eretz Yisrael in connection with the miracle. Rambam, Hilchos Megillah 1:5. As for the reason they divided this mitzvah among different days and established a special day for unwalled cities and a special day for walled cities, unlike other mitzvos, the Beit Yosef wrote in the name of the Ran that the Jews dwelling in unwalled cities and the Jews in Shushan did not rest from their enemies on the same day. The Jews in the unwalled cities rested on the 14th, while the Jews in Shushan rested on the 15th, and each made feasting and rejoicing on the day of his rest. Therefore, when they established a festival upon themselves for future generations, they fixed it for each group on the day on which it rested, and they equated all cities surrounded by a wall with Shushan, because the miracle occurred there, see there. As for why in the present era the Megillah is not read on Purim on the 11th, 12th, or 13th, as stated at the beginning of tractate Megillah, it is because they said there in the Gemara: R' Yehudah said, when does this apply? When the years are properly fixed and the Jewish people dwell upon their land. But in the present era, since people calculate by it, they read it only at its appointed time. Rashi explains: But in the present era, when all of these have ceased and the Jewish people are dispersed, and the emissaries of beis din do not reach them, everyone watches the date of the Megillah reading and says, 'We read the Megillah on 14 Adar; 15 days then remain in Adar, and on 15 Nissan we celebrate Pesach.' If its reading were advanced, they would celebrate Pesach at the end of 30 days from the reading and would consequently eat chametz during the latter days of Pesach. End quote. Beit Yosef cited his words. So too wrote Rambam in the aforementioned chapter, that in the present era it is read only at its appointed time, which is the 14th and the 15th: villagers and residents of unwalled cities read on the 14th, and residents of walled cities read on the 15th.
