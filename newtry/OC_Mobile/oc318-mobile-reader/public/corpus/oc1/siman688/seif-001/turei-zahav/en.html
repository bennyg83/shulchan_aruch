(1) Walled cities. Ran asked: why did they divide this mitzvah into separate days and set a special day for unwalled towns unlike other mitzvot; and he answered because they did not rest equally, as Beit Yosef wrote. Beit Yosef questioned Ran's answer from the gemara's challenge "but Shushan like whom acted," etc. — behold the essence of walled cities did not act on the 15th except to resemble Shushan; and what does Rava say, etc. And in my humble opinion there is no difficulty: Beit Yosef understood the challenge "Shushan like whom" means not like walled cities and not like unwalled towns — for if like walled cities we require from Joshua's days, and Shushan they did not know if walled from Joshua's days; and not like unwalled towns who read on the 14th while Shushan on the 15th. Therefore Beit Yosef asked his difficulties; but Ran did not explain thus — rather when the questioner asked "Shushan like whom acted" he was truly challenged also on walled cities who depend on Shushan; but he asked: and Shushan itself on what does it depend, meaning like whom did they learn to act on the 15th — from this automatically difficulty on walled cities who depend on Shushan. And Rava's answer: since the miracle of the killing was on the 14th and they rested on the 15th — therefore they fixed there on the 15th; and if so when there is distinction between Shushan and unwalled towns, it is reasonable to liken all walled cities to Shushan and unwalled towns to other towns of that era; except it was fitting to distinguish walled cities of Achashverosh at the time of the miracle, but Eretz Yisrael was apart from the obligation of reading as walled cities and they wished not to disgrace Eretz Yisrael that was destroyed in those days and to include them with Shushan where the greater miracle was, and fitting to give Eretz Yisrael every superiority possible; therefore it depends on walled from Joshua — automatically Eretz Yisrael has Shushan's superiority, and from this follows the division of walled to unwalled. And what Beit Yosef wrote in name of Rambam: why tied to Joshua's days to give honor to Eretz Yisrael, etc., and remembrance for Eretz Yisrael in this miracle — end; meaning through this they will see this matter tied to the Temple and not Achashverosh as fitting, and they will ask about it and answer they do so for honor of Eretz Yisrael — as appears to me, correct explanation of Ran's words without doubt. Beit Yosef labored in vain to answer in another way and say they made remembrance of praise of Eretz Yisrael in this miracle — wondrous, for if so difficulty also on Chanukah miracle — should fix something for the miracle for Eretz Yisrael, as Ran asked initially regarding other mitzvot:

(2) But presumably it was walled. At first glance difficult from what is written siman 401, siman 691, siman 665 — and most towns are walled for residence where they build houses first and afterward wall them; and one can say there they are not strict except that there be a wall for residence, i.e. excluding a fortress whose wall is not for residence but for war; and we say most towns at first build some houses temporarily as preparation and afterward wall and afterward settle with many buildings — and it is fine "walled and afterward settled," for the first buildings were only initial preparation; and likewise here we follow the walling that precedes settlement and do not follow beginning of buildings, since main settlement is afterward — found what is written siman 401 that they first build houses means only initial preparation that came only as sign there would be a city. And what is written here that presumably it was walled and afterward settled means main settlement and fixing place is afterward; and likewise Ran's language here in Beit Yosef is precise: he wrote that through most countries they wall first and afterward settle — behold he mentioned settlement and not building houses, for settlement is the main buildings for most — clear in my humble opinion:
