Walled cities surrounded by a wall. The Ran asked why this mitzvah was divided into different days and a special day was fixed for unwalled towns, unlike other mitzvos. He answered that they did not rest equally, as Beis Yosef wrote. Beis Yosef asked against the Ran's answer from the fact that we ask in the Gemara: but Shushan, according to whom did it act, etc.? Since the entire essence of the walled cities did not act on the fifteenth except to resemble Shushan, what is Rava saying, etc.? In my humble opinion, there is no difficulty here at all. Beis Yosef understood that when it asks, "Shushan, according to whom did it act," it means that it is neither like walled cities nor like unwalled towns: not like walled cities, because we require from the days of Yehoshua, and regarding Shushan we do not know whether it was walled from the days of Yehoshua; and not like unwalled towns, for they read on the fourteenth, while Shushan reads on the fifteenth. Therefore Beis Yosef asked his questions. But the Ran did not explain this way. Rather, when the questioner asked, "Shushan, according to whom did it act," in truth the difficulty also applied to the walled cities that depend on Shushan; but the difficulty was: upon what does Shushan itself depend? This is the meaning of "according to whom," meaning from whom did they learn to act on the fifteenth? Automatically, there is also a difficulty regarding the walled cities that depend on Shushan. Rava's answer is that since the miracle of the killing occurred there on the fourteenth and they rested on the fifteenth, therefore they established there on the fifteenth. If so, once there is a distinction between Shushan and the unwalled towns, it is reasonable to compare all walled cities to Shushan and the unwalled towns to the other towns that existed at that time. It would have been proper to distinguish based on those walled from the days of Achashverosh, when the miracle occurred, but Eretz Yisrael would then have been outside the obligation of reading as walled cities, and they did not want to degrade Eretz Yisrael, which was desolate in those days. In order to include it with Shushan, where the miracle was greater, and because it is fitting to give Eretz Yisrael every possible distinction, they therefore made it depend on being walled from Yehoshua. Automatically, Eretz Yisrael too has the distinction of Shushan, and from this came the distinction between walled and unwalled places. This is what Beis Yosef wrote in the name of the Rambam: why does the matter depend on the days of Yehoshua? In order to apportion honor to Eretz Yisrael, etc., and so that there be a remembrance of Eretz Yisrael in this miracle; end quote. Meaning, through people seeing that they made it depend on the Temple-era Land and not on Achashverosh as would have been proper, they will ask about this and answer that they do so in honor of Eretz Yisrael. In my humble opinion, this is certainly the correct explanation of the Ran's words. Beis Yosef labored for nothing to answer differently and say that they made a remembrance of the praise of Eretz Yisrael in this miracle. This is astonishing, for if so, regarding the Chanukah miracle too they should have enacted some element in the miracle for the sake of Eretz Yisrael, as the Ran asked at the beginning, what makes this different from the other mitzvos?
But presumably it was surrounded. At first glance this is difficult from what is written in siman 401, siman 391, and siman 325, that ordinary towns are surrounded for dwelling, for they build houses first and afterward surround the town. This is not difficult at all. There, we are particular only that the enclosure be for dwelling, to exclude a fortress whose enclosure is not for dwelling but for war. In that context we say that ordinary towns, at their beginning, first build some temporary houses and mere preparation, and afterward surround with a wall, and afterward settle the town with many buildings. It is therefore properly surrounded and afterward settled, for the first buildings were only a beginning. The same applies here: we follow the surrounding that comes before its settlement, and we do not follow the beginning of the buildings, since the main settlement is afterward. It emerges that what is written in siman 401, that they first build houses, means only the initial beginning, which came only as a sign that a city will be here. And what is written here, that presumably it was surrounded and afterward settled, means the main settlement and fixing of the place afterward. So too the language of the Ran here in Beis Yosef is precise, for he wrote that this is the way of most countries: they surround with a wall first and afterward settle it; end quote. He mentioned the language of settlement, not building houses, because settlement is the main body of most of the buildings. This appears clear to me.
