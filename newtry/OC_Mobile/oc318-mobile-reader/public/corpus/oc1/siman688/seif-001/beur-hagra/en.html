Seif 1: Even if they are not, etc., and even, etc. As written in the Yerushalmi, the reason they distinguished cities walled from the days of Yehoshua bin Nun was in order to give honor to Eretz Yisrael, which was desolate in those days, so that walled cities in the Diaspora would not be considered more important than them. See Ran.
And even if they are in the Diaspora. From what we say according to R. Yehoshua ben Korchah, "like Shushan," etc., and from R. Yehoshua ben Korchah we learn for the Rabbis, for they dispute only the time. There on 5b: R. Asi read in Hutzal, etc. And we say in the final chapter: Where is Bavel? etc. In Erev Pesachim, Yosef of Hutzal is Yosef the Babylonian, and at the end of Ketubot we hold that Bavel, etc., is translated as Hutzal. In the Yerushalmi, first chapter of Megillah: if it became desolate and became a gentile city, what is the law? In it they do not read; outside it they read. Meaning, if in the Diaspora they read even though there were no Jews in it, all the more so in Eretz Yisrael. See Ran. But his explanation is not precise. This is the Yerushalmi's language there: It was taught: one adjacent to a city and visible with it is like it. R. Aibu bar Gari: like this Chamtah, etc. If the city became desolate and became of idolaters, come and see: in it they do not read, but outside it they read. Meaning, if outside the city they read, for there it is not because of the wall, since we say in Arakhin and it is brought in the Yerushalmi there that whatever is inside the wall, etc.; if so, even if the wall was nullified they read, all the more so inside it. At first glance, this is proof for Tosafot's interpretation that walled cities do not require ten idle men, for that was the question, that it became of idolaters. But one may say that it means most of it became idolaters and the sanctity of the wall was nullified. See Tosafot, Ketubot 45b, s.v. al. It is possible to explain the Yerushalmi as a language of resolution: in it they do not read, but outside it they read, since it did not state the opposite, outside it first.
And even if they do not have. From what is written there: a city settled and afterward, etc.; necessarily when it does not have ten idle men, since it says "like a village." Tosafot, s.v. kerakh. The Ramban and Rashba disagree with this; see there.
Or that was settled, etc. From what is written on 5b: and not a wall-roofed place. See the above Tosafot, and their question, "and if you say below," etc.; with this their question is resolved.
But ordinarily, etc. From what is written there: was the matter of Tiberias doubtful to him? etc. But perhaps he was uncertain whether it was settled and then walled. And there: but Rabbi was in Tiberias, etc.
