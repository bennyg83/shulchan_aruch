Section 1. "Walled cities surrounded by a wall from the days of Yehoshua bin Nun, etc." Note: The source of this law is in the Gemara of Megillah, in the passage "walled cities surrounded by a wall from the days of Yehoshua, etc.; from where are these words derived, etc.," until the end of the sugya. "And according to our Tanna, is it satisfactory? Since he has 'unwalled, unwalled,' why do I need 'province and province'?" See there. When I was involved in this, the Gemara was difficult for me: why does it ask, since he has "unwalled, unwalled," why do I need "province and province"? According to what is explicit in the Gemara below, and so we rule, that a doubtful walled city reads on both the fourteenth and the fifteenth, in Sotah Rabban Gamliel raised a contradiction: it is written "every pure person may eat meat," implying that a definitely pure person may eat, but one of doubtful status may not eat; and it is written "and every impure person, etc.," implying that one who is definitely impure may not eat, but one of doubtful status may eat. It answers: here it speaks of impurity of meat, etc. If so, it implies that wherever the verse specifies the matter, it speaks only of certainty and not of doubt. Therefore one can say that if only "unwalled" were written, I would say specifically one that is certainly unwalled reads on the fourteenth, but one that is doubtfully unwalled reads on the fifteenth. Especially since we derive by verbal analogy from "the unwalled cities, very many," and there the matter was not doubtful, for in the days of Moshe and Yehoshua themselves they saw whether it was surrounded by a wall or unwalled. In the days of Yehoshua himself no doubt applied, only certainly walled or certainly unwalled. Therefore, if we derived only "unwalled, unwalled," I would have thought specifically one that is unwalled reads on the fourteenth, but one that is doubtfully unwalled reads like a walled city on the fifteenth. Therefore "province and province" is written, to teach that a province that is certainly unwalled reads on the fourteenth, and a province that is certainly walled reads on the fifteenth, but one of doubtful status reads on both days. One cannot say that a doubtful one should not read at all, for are they not Jews? Furthermore, it is written "from Hodu to Kush," as the Gemara asks above. Therefore, perforce, we expound this way: one that is certainly unwalled reads only on the fourteenth, and one that is certainly walled reads only on the fifteenth, but one of doubtful status reads on both. This is unlike the case where "province and province" was not written and only "unwalled" was written, where I would say specifically one certainly unwalled reads on the fourteenth, but one doubtfully unwalled is judged like one certainly walled and reads only on the fifteenth. This requires analysis, and analyze carefully. See Taz, seif katan 1, regarding what he used to settle the words of the Ran in explaining the Gemara's challenge, "but Shushan, according to whom did it act?" See there. This is astonishing, for in the Gemara it is explicitly stated: not like unwalled towns and not like walled cities. I later saw in Peri Megadim in the name of Chemed Moshe that he sensed this. I am even more astonished than this: if the Taz's explanation is correct, the same question should be difficult according to the one who says from the days of Achashverosh, and in the Gemara it is explicit that it asks only according to the one who says walled from the days of Yehoshua bin Nun; see there and analyze carefully. In this siman I elaborated in my novellae in two places, in the sugya of "there is no difference between the first Adar and the second" and in the sugya of when it falls on Friday, "and who is it? It is Rebbe"; see there, and you will find satisfaction with Hashem's help. There, in that section: "provided that it was surrounded and afterward settled, etc." Note: This is the opinion of Maharam of Rottenburg that the Tur brought, but the early and later poskim require analysis: why did they not bring here the opinion of Tosafos, who explicitly disagree with this, in their comment under "a walled city that was settled and afterward surrounded"? They asked: why do I need the verse "that has a wall" to exclude Shur Iger, etc.? Let it follow because in such a case it was settled and afterward surrounded, etc. If the words of Maharam are correct, what is the difficulty? In any case, this was settled with intent to surround it, and this is considered surrounded and afterward settled. Perforce, Tosafos hold that it must be actually surrounded first, and not merely settled with intent to surround it. If so, why did they not bring the opinion of Tosafos, who disagree with this? This requires analysis. While I was involved in this, I saw that Pnei Yehoshua in his novellae asked: what practical difference is there in this statement, since we require surrounded by a wall from the days of Yehoshua bin Nun? If it was surrounded after Yehoshua bin Nun, in any case it does not help; and if his intent is that it was surrounded from the days of Yehoshua bin Nun, how can one find a case where it was first settled, since the Ran holds that the dwelling of gentiles is not called dwelling? See there. In my humble opinion it appears that the Ran's view, that gentile dwelling is not considered dwelling, applies only after Israel came to the Land and acquired it; then gentile dwelling is not dwelling. But before Israel came to the Land, when the land was theirs, gentile dwelling is considered dwelling. It is an explicit verse in the Torah several times that it calls them inhabitants of the land, as written in Parashas Mishpatim and Parashas Masei. Therefore, before Israel acquired it, gentile dwelling was dwelling; but once Israel acquired it, gentile dwelling was not dwelling. Therefore the Ran speaks regarding the Second Temple era, and one can properly say that gentile dwelling is not dwelling. But Rabbi Yehoshua ben Levi speaks of the first sanctification from the days of Yehoshua bin Nun, and therefore he properly teaches that specifically surrounded and afterward settled; but if it was settled and afterward surrounded, even gentile dwelling is considered dwelling, and it is not considered surrounded by a wall from the days of Yehoshua bin Nun. This is simple and correct; analyze carefully.
