Ibid. If it were regarded as a wall. Although for the laws of houses in a walled city it is certainly not a wall, regarding Megillah he remains in doubt, because it affords protection. Alternatively, perhaps since the area remains visible, it is treated as an unwalled city. According to this, in the case of a shur igar, where no one lived there until the houses were completed, it is properly considered first surrounded and afterward settled, as Tosafos write. The same may apply according to Maharam in seif 1, who holds that settlement undertaken with the intention of later enclosing the city is effective. Although a shur igar is not called a wall regarding the laws of houses in a walled city, perhaps regarding Megillah, since the city is not exposed and is protected, its residents read on the fifteenth. Alternatively, it may nevertheless resemble a city that was settled first and enclosed afterward, because there too it is partly exposed and partly concealed, yet since we do not find that such a structure is legally regarded as a wall, they do not read on the fifteenth. See Tosafos, Megillah 3b.
