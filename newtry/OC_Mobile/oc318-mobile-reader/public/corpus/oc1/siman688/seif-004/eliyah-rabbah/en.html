4) And provided that they are not, etc. Beis Yosef wrote in the name of the Ran: perhaps when we say, until how far, up to a mil, it refers back to the entire matter, both to visible with it and to adjacent. Nevertheless, it does not seem that if it was several parsahs distant, because people can see it from the walled city, it should be judged like the walled city; end of his language. It appears to me that what he wrote, "nevertheless, it does not seem," refers to adjacent, meaning adjacent even though it is not visible to the walled city. What he wrote, "that if it was distant, etc.," means that for this reason the Gemara asks "until how far" regarding the whole matter; however, it appears that the reason is because if it was distant, etc. That is to say, it is not reasonable that if it is visible it should always be judged like the walled city, and therefore we need to know its measure. So it appears to me, excluding Beis Yosef and Rabbi Moshe Alashkar, siman 110, who did not descend to this point. They understood what the Ran wrote, "nevertheless, it does not seem," to mean that it does not seem to him to say so, and because of this they were very forced in explaining it; see there. Many difficulties also arise for me against their explanation, but my way in this book is to be brief. From Shulchan Aruch, which changed the language of the Tur and wrote first the law of visible and afterward the law of adjacent, this is only to teach that it applies specifically to adjacent. Levush, who wrote like the language of the Tur, implies that he holds like the Ran. The Maggid Mishneh asked that if so, the Gemara in Megillah, page 4, should not have mentioned visible at all; everything depends on adjacent. He answered that regarding visible, where it sits on a mountain, we do not measure the mil along the ground, but by stretching the rope from city to city through the air. Darkei Moshe answered that adjacent is called such if one can come to the walled city within a mil, but regarding visible, even if a river interrupts and one must go around a great distance, nevertheless it is considered part of the city since it is within a mil. The Taz explained that until the foot of the mountain, on level ground, it must not be more than a mil, but the mountain is not counted at all; rather, it is like part of the city, since through the mountain the walled city is visible to this town. But with adjacent, where it is in a valley, we also count the slope. I further saw in Beis Yosef that he explained the Gemara's "adjacent to it" as meaning specifically within the city's ibbur, while visible is specifically within a mil. So it also implies in the book Derech Tamim, glosses to the Rif, page 19, and the view of Rabbi Moshe Alashkar inclines this way. It appears to me that there is proof from Rokeach, siman 237: a walled city and all adjacent to it are judged like the walled city, and all visible with it up to a mil; end quote. Since he separated them into two clauses, it implies that up to a mil does not apply at all to adjacent, but to visible. So it appears to me as the halachah. With this it is resolved why the Gemara asks: adjacent even though not visible, how can one find such a case? That is to say, even if it is in a valley, nevertheless, since it is so adjacent, within the city's ibbur, presumably it is visible to it. It answers that it sits in a valley that is deep without a slope, very deep, and is not visible. I elaborated on this because I saw in Agudah this language: so they now judge regarding taxes and loans that are not under their jurisdiction; end quote. So too in Ramban, page 79, of blessed memory: the same applies to all enactments of residents of a walled city.
