A walled city that is uncertain. Maharshal wrote: this is astonishing; why do they not bless over it on both days like every calendar doubt regarding a blessing? It is forced to distinguish between a Torah festival day and megillah, which is rabbinic. Granted, where there is no doubt about the wall, one cannot ask why we do not read the megillah on the fourteenth and fifteenth because of the calendar doubt, as with shofar and lulav, for Mordechai already answered that because it is written "and it shall not pass," after we are not expert in fixing the month. But with an actual doubt, it is not relevant to say "it shall not pass," for perhaps today is its main time. Why should we be more concerned for a doubtful blessing in vain than with a calendar doubt regarding a festival? One can distinguish that there, the sanctity of the day causes him to be obligated even in the blessing because of doubt, for if you do not treat it as the sanctity of the day even regarding the blessing, you make it a weekday. Furthermore, the sanctity of the day depends on the court, and they accepted it upon themselves this way; end quote. The difficulty is not resolved by these two answers, for regarding megillah too there is sanctity of the day regarding all the laws dependent on Purim. The second answer is also difficult: regarding the court itself, why did they not accept them equally? It appears to me to answer his difficulty as follows. It is different with a festival day, where there is one side on which he will not fulfill the mitzvah of the festival at all: if the second day is sacred, it will not help that he treated yesterday as sacred, for it was a weekday. Therefore because of the doubt he must treat the second day as sacred as well, and likewise the reverse. But with megillah, we find in any case that the megillah has many times, namely the eleventh, twelfth, and thirteenth, and we derive this from the phrase "in their times," meaning that there are many times. Also, in a place that observes on the fifteenth they advance it, such as if it falls on Shabbos. In Beis Yosef in the name of the Yerushalmi, the whole month until the fifteenth is valid for megillah. If so, even on the side that you say the main obligation is on the fifteenth, what they read on the fourteenth still helps, for in any event we find advancing in this matter. Therefore there is no full concern, and the fourteenth is enough, since it is for most of the world. This is correct in my humble opinion.
