Section 4: A walled city, etc. The Geonim ruled that he should read only on the fourteenth, for we follow the majority of the world. Furthermore, a doubt in rabbinic matters is treated leniently; only so that the reading of the megillah should not certainly be nullified for him, he reads on the fourteenth and is exempt from the fifteenth. Maggid Mishneh and Ran. That is to say, since on the fourteenth one fulfills after the fact, as written below, this is the reason of the Rambam, who wrote that he should not bless, etc. Mishneh LaMelech elaborated on this for no need. The Geonim wrote that although we say that Chizkiyah read, etc., this was a pious practice. Furthermore, that applies specifically in Eretz Yisrael, where its cities were known at the time of the enactment because of the law of walled-house cities, etc. One must say that the text in the Gemara is Hotzal of Beis Binyamin. See what I wrote in section 1.
And he should not bless. So wrote the Geonim, from what is written in chapter 2 of Shabbos: a doubt in rabbinic matters, etc. The Rif in chapter 19 of Shabbos in the name of Rav Hai Gaon, the Rosh there, and the Rivash, siman 220, ruled that even on a Torah-level doubt one does not bless. They explain the sugya in Shabbos there as follows: a definite rabbinic matter, meaning something enacted by the Sages because of a definite obligation, requires blessing; but a doubtful rabbinic matter, meaning something they enacted because of doubt, such as demai, does not require blessing. Rava disagrees and says that since they enacted it, even because of doubt, it requires blessing. But other doubts, even Torah-level doubts, do not require blessing, as the Rif and Rosh wrote in chapter 19 of Shabbos regarding milah, and the Rosh in chapter Kisui HaDam regarding a koy. See Rabbeinu Yonah in chapter 3 of Berachos, the Ran in chapter 2 of Shabbos and chapter 19 there, the Rambam in chapter 6 of Hilchos Sukkah and chapter 2 of Hilchos Milah and Chanukah, the Raavad, Maggid Mishneh, and Lechem Mishneh there, and in chapter 3 of Hilchos Milah, the Raavad there. Kesef Mishneh brought a responsum of the Rambam, whose essence is as above, and his proof is from what is written in Sukkah, chapter 4: we sit, but regarding blessing, etc.; and so he wrote in chapter 6 of Hilchos Sukkah. The reason is that the blessing, even for a Torah mitzvah, is rabbinic, as written in chapter 2 of Berachos, and there in chapter 3, regarding a doubt whether he read, etc., since it is rabbinic, he need not; see the Rosh there in the name of Baal Halachos Gedolos. The Raavad, the sages of Lunel, and Rabbeinu Yonah in chapter 3 of Berachos, whose words the Rosh brought in Chullin there, hold that since Rava held specifically because of the majority, etc., it follows that in every Torah-level doubt, such as demai, one blesses; so too the second festival day, and every doubt, even not because of enactment. There, regarding Shema, it is because its root is rabbinic, unlike the blessing on a mitzvah, where since the mitzvah is Torah-level, one blesses. Sukkah is different because the two sides contradict each other, as the Rif wrote there. But in Shulchan Aruch he ruled like the Rif, Rambam, and Rosh, as written above in siman 17 and in Yoreh Deah, siman 29 and siman 265. The Rambam in chapter 3 of Hilchos Megillah and Chanukah retracted from what he wrote in the responsum, that Rava disagrees with Abaye; there he holds that they do not disagree, but Rava added. Therefore he holds that even an enactment because of a Torah-level doubt does not require blessing. But here, where the doubt is rabbinic according to all views, no blessing is needed. What the Raavad wrote, that according to Rava another doubt, even rabbinic, requires a blessing, means a matter that the Sages enacted over a doubt, as he himself wrote in chapter 6 of Sukkah and chapter 3 of Hilchos Milah.
Only on the fourteenth. For even residents of walled cities fulfill after the fact on the fourteenth, as written in the Yerushalmi, chapter 2 of Megillah, halachah 3: Can a resident of an unwalled town discharge a resident of a walled city? Let it come from this rule: anyone not obligated in the matter, etc. Can a resident of a walled city discharge a resident of an unwalled town? Let it come from this rule, etc.; everyone fulfills on the fourteenth, which is the time of its reading. So too there in chapter 1, halachah 1, 5:4. Therefore one blesses on the fourteenth even initially, as Tosafos and other poskim wrote regarding a blessing where there is uncertainty whether it is "Who creates the fruit of the ground" or "Who creates the fruit of the tree," and as written in chapter 6 of Berachos, where Rabbi Akiva jumped and blessed one blessing of three, and Rabban Gamliel said to him: why do you, etc.
