In Responsa Zichron Yosef, Orach Chaim, siman 18, he asked: since Shevat is full, it is impossible to be uncertain that Purim would be on the fifteenth. At first glance this greatly requires analysis. But it appears to me that since according to the order of the calendar, Cheshvan and Kislev are sometimes both deficient, sometimes both full, and sometimes regular, if in a year they are deficient, or one of them is deficient, there is room to be concerned that if the months were sanctified by sighting, they might have been full, and consequently Purim would be on the fifteenth. If they had been concerned for this and made two days, they would have needed to make two days also when both are full, so as not to distinguish between one year and another, as we say elsewhere: we decree regarding Nisan because of the messengers of Tishrei. Then, when both are full, we would certainly be violating "it shall not pass." Since now we are expert in the fixing of the month, we are not concerned in this matter for the custom of our fathers. This works well, with Hashem's help.
