(s.k. 3) And apart from this, etc. — meaning in Tur he wrote: and likewise adjoining villages even if not visible, etc., or visible with them even if not adjoining, etc., provided they are not more than a mil distant — end. And it implies that also for visible with it one requires it not be more than a mil distant, for this "provided they are not distant" certainly refers to what he adjoined to it. And in the gemara it implies for near and not visible one requires it was not a mil distant; and if one said this "provided" that Tur wrote refers to both visible and near — then for what was "visible" mentioned, for it adds and subtracts nothing, since whether visible or not visible a mil is required; see Rabbi Leibish to settle. Therefore here in Shulchan Aruch he reversed and put the law of visible first and afterward the law of near, even though he did not write "provided," etc. If so this "provided" refers to near even though not visible with it, but visible even if very far as the Talmud plainly stated:
