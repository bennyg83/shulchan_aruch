The elder recites the blessing, etc. This means where all are eating from their own food. Otherwise, he should give the honor to the guest, or to one of his household who is considered like a guest. If he repeatedly brings up phlegm, another should recite Birkat HaMazon, for it is improper that he interrupt many times and spit while the others wait.
So that he may bless, etc. In the derashot of Maharshal it is derived from "and you shall bless Hashem your God," the word "et" including the host, as cited by Malbushei Yom Tov. I wrote above that this teaching is in the Yerushalmi.
[Levush] Those who cause Him, as it were, to be hated, etc. This is imprecise, for the verse speaks of wisdom. Maharshal likewise explains that they cause the Torah to be hated, as do Malbushei Yom Tov, Rashi, and Rabbenu Yonah. See above, siman 167 seif 14, where all the laws of this siman were explained. In the liturgical phrase "We thank Your Name, etc., with permission of the kohanim and Leviim," some read "the kohanim, the Leviim," meaning that kohanim are themselves called Leviim. Their reason is that nowadays Leviim and Yisraelim are equal, as Rashi explains in Parashat Re'eh. Perishah, however, distinguishes that there the matter concerns a gratuitous gift, which a Yisrael is not obligated to give him. This resolves Magen Avraham's difficulty. Accordingly, where there is no kohen, a Levi has precedence over a Yisrael for Hamotzi and Birkat HaMazon, as also appears from Bach and Perishah.
[Levush] And one who recites Birkat HaMazon, etc. According to this, the rule applies specifically to a guest and to the cup of Birkat HaMazon, as Magen Avraham writes. I say, however, that nowadays the one reciting the blessing blesses all those seated, for he says "us," etc. Yet there is difficulty from the Gemara in Berachot 55: from where does the Gemara know that one who curses is dealt with more quickly? Why is "I will curse" written specifically regarding one who curses you, whereas regarding one who does not bless, the formulation "do not curse him" could equally have been written, or "do not bless him"? One may answer that otherwise, why is "I will bless those who bless you" needed? It could be derived from "he who curses you I will curse," since the measure of good is greater, etc.
