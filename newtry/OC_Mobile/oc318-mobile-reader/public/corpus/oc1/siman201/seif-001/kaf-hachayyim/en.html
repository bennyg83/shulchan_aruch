1) [Seif 1] The greatest recites the blessing, etc. 'The greatest' means the one greatest in wisdom, as stated above in siman 167, paragraph 97; see there. Shulchan Aruch HaRav in this siman, paragraph 1, writes the same. The rule that the greatest recites the blessing applies when they all eat from their own food. Otherwise, he should give the honor to a guest or to one of his household members, who is like a guest. Eliyah Rabbah, paragraph 1; Eshel Avraham, paragraph 1. If the greatest brings up phlegm, called coughing in the vernacular, another should recite the blessing, because it is improper for him to interrupt many times and spit while the others wait. Gloss to Bircas Maharam; Magen Avraham, paragraph 1; Eliyah Rabbah there; Shulchan Gavoah, paragraph 1; Beis Menuchah, paragraph 1. Likewise, one should not initially honor a person missing a finger or one afflicted with boils to lead Birkas HaMazon, as stated above in siman 181, paragraph 28; see there.
2) There: The greatest recites the blessing, etc. This provides support for the custom that a mourner leads the blessing, since he reclines at the head. The custom was expanded considerably, however, so that a mourner leads throughout all twelve months. This custom has a basis only when both individuals are equal. If the host is a mourner and has a guest, or if the guest is a mourner, in every case the host may lead if he wishes. She'eilas Yaavetz, siman 74; Birkei Yosef, paragraph 12; Shaarei Teshuvah, paragraph 1; Beis Menuchah, paragraphs 4 and 5.
3) There: The greatest recites the blessing, etc. Nowadays some are not particular about any of this, lest they come to quarrel with the host by telling him that a certain person is the greater and more fitting one. The host therefore gives the blessing to whomever he happens to wish, without concern for whether he is greater or lesser, fitting or unfit. Levush; Knesses HaGedolah, Hagahos HaTur, paragraph 1; Olas Tamid, paragraph 1; Shulchan Gavoah there. Sephardic communities, however, still observe the custom that the greatest recites the blessing, and there is objection if the host gives it to whomever he wishes. Beis Menuchah, paragraph 6. This refers to leading the Zimun, for regarding Birkas HaMazon the practice is for each person to recite it himself, as stated in siman 183, paragraph 38; see there.
4) A common saying among people is that one who honors another to lead Birkas HaMazon honors him with forty gold coins. Birkei Yosef, paragraph 4; Beis Menuchah, paragraph 11. See what Sifsei Kohen writes in Choshen Mishpat at the end of siman 382.
