One who blesses. And if the greatest one brings out saliva that they call "hostatin," one person should bless, for it is not proper that he stop many times and spit while the others wait — Maharash, Magen Avraham. And likewise l'chatchila one should not give permission to bless Birkat Hamazon to one who is missing a finger or one with a boil. Responsa of Chavat Yair siman 176.

For the host (ba'al habayit). "And you shall bless Heaven" — to include an am ha'aretz. Maharash.

Whoever he wishes. It is difficult either way: if one gives permission to bless to one member of the household, it is obvious that they too are guests for this matter, that they bless for the host. And it appears that if one relies on his table and pays him the cost of his meals, and that one need not bless for the host, he may give him permission to bless. And further it appears that if two eat in one house and one has a guest at his table, by law the guest should bless; and if that host wishes to waive, he may give permission to bless to whomever he wishes. Magen Avraham, see there.
