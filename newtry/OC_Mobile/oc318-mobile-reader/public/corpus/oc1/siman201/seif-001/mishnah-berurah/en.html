(1) The greatest blesses — this means the greatest in wisdom among all those reclining; he shall be the one who blesses Birkat Hamazon for all of them. And even though nowadays the custom is that each one blesses for himself quietly, as above siman 183, nevertheless the custom in some places is to honor him that he be the one who leads zimmun; and this applies when all those reclining are householders who ate from their own; for if there was one host, the matter depends on him to give to a guest to bless, as below, or to whomever he wishes from his household members, who also bless for the host. If the greatest one brings out saliva [that they call "hostatin"], another should bless, for it is not proper that he stop many times and spit while the others wait [Achronim]. Some places have the custom to give permission to a mourner [for his father and mother all twelve months] to bless — but specifically when all are equal, meaning that all eat from their own; but when one eats from the host's bread, it depends on the host's will — to whomever he wishes he may give; and all the more so if he wishes to bless himself.

(2) At the end — of the meal: wherever if they bring them something, they must eat, as above siman 197 seif 1, see there; and it is reasonable that if he delays at his meal, they are not obligated to wait for him until he finishes his meal so that he be the blesser, but rather another blesses and he answers [amen].

(3) If there is a guest there — meaning when he eats from the host's bread.

(4) He blesses even, etc. — and if there are several guests there, it depends on the host's judgment, and even to give to the youngest among them.

(5) And what blessing does he bless him, etc. — in Sefer Lechem Chamudot he wondered why we change the wording of the blessing for the host from what is stated in the Gemara.

(6) He may — for they did not enact giving to a guest except for his benefit, so that he bless him; and all the more so if the guest is not proper in his eyes, he may do so.

(7) To whomever he wishes — meaning that even if one relies on his table and pays him the cost of his meals, and that one need not bless for the host, he may also give him permission to bless.
