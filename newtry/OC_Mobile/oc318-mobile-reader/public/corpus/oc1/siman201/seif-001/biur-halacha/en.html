Came at the end — in the Gemara some say that if a great person came at the end he does not bless, for the essence of the meal blesses [one of those who were at the beginning of the meal — Rashi], and the Gemara concludes that the halachah is that a great person blesses even if he came at the end. And it would appear that this is only regarding a great person on account of his importance; but among ordinary people all agree that the essence of the meal blesses, and not one who came at the end of the meal — and this requires further study.

And if the host wishes to waive, etc., to whomever he wishes — see Mishna Berurah what he wrote: that he has authority in his hand to give to another even though he is not so much a guest — Magen Avraham and Shaarei Teshuvah. And it requires study: if there is a great person there, whether the host is permitted to pass the zimmun from him and give it to another, or even to himself — for in general we hold that a great person blesses; and only when there is a guest who reclines with the host did they say that he blesses, so that he bless the host; but if the guest does not bless, it appears well that the right to bless remains with the great person — why should it be in the host's power to pass it from the great person? And all the more so if the guest is the greatest of them all — certainly, it would appear, he has no permission to pass it from him. And the Shulchan Aruch, which permitted the host to give to whomever he wishes — possibly this is when there is no great person there, and all were equal; and in particular, for there are among the poskim who hold that always the right is with the great person, and a guest who blesses is only when they are equal [see Tosafot R' Yehuda and Rabbenu Yona]. And behold, regarding the host himself one can argue that even when there is a great person, the permission is in his hand to bless himself — for it is implied in the Shulchan Aruch that the guest's honor is more important than the great person's honor, so that he bless the host; and per what is explained below, that one must return for a cup of blessing, the host can say: they only enacted this for my takkanah — I prefer this. But only regarding giving to another does it seem as we said: where there is a great person there, he has no permission for this.
