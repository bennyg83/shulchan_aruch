Seif 2: A Torah scholar of Israel shall not precede a priest who is an am ha'aretz. Note well: I saw in a small book called Sharvit HaZahav on the laws of milah that he brought a responsum of Maharshal, Yoreh De'ah siman 239, that his view is that a priest who is a mohel precedes every person — and he disagrees with him. And in my humble opinion it appears that without this it is not a matter of the mitzvah of "and you shall sanctify him," for in every mitzvah this was not said except in a matter that has no agency — such as reading the Torah first; in this the Torah imposed an obligation to precede the priest. But in milah, the main obligation is upon the father; only if the father wishes, or if he is unable, does he make an agent in his place afterward. And since his agent is his agent, there is no obligation to make the priest specifically an agent — only since it is his mitzvah in his hand, he may make an agent for whomever he wishes. And we have not found anywhere in the Torah that when a person has some matter and wishes to make an agent, he is obligated to make specifically a priest an agent. So too here: since milah is an obligation upon the father, if he honors another it is only in the manner of agency, to be like him — for whomever his heart desires he may make an agent. And with this it would appear to decide: where there is no father and the court must circumcise him — in this agency does not apply — then it is fitting to honor the priest more; but where there is a father, he is permitted to honor whomever he wishes. And this fits well, in my humble opinion, at the time — and requires study.
