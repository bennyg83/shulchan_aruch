Seif 2. A Torah scholar who is an Israelite should not be given precedence over an unlearned kohen. Note: I saw in a small work entitled Sharvit HaZahav on the laws of circumcision that it cites Responsa Maharshadam, Yoreh De'ah, siman 239, who holds that a kohen who is a mohel takes precedence over every other person, and the author disputes him. In my humble opinion, apart from that objection, this matter has no connection to the mitzvah of “You shall sanctify him.” That mitzvah was stated only regarding matters that cannot be performed through agency, such as being called first to the Torah, where the Torah imposed an obligation to give precedence to the kohen. Circumcision, however, is primarily the father's obligation. If the father wishes, or is unable to perform it, he appoints another person as his agent. Since that person acts as his agent, there is no obligation to appoint specifically a kohen. The mitzvah belongs to the father, and he may appoint whomever he wishes. We do not find anywhere in the Torah that when a person has some matter to attend to and wishes to appoint an agent, he is obligated to appoint specifically a kohen. So too here, since circumcision is the father's obligation, when he honors another person to perform it, that person acts only as his agent and stands in his place. He may therefore appoint whomever he desires. On this basis, it would appear proper to distinguish as follows: where there is no father and the beis din is obligated to circumcise the child, agency does not apply, and it is then fitting to honor the kohen first. Where the father is present, however, he may honor whomever he wishes. This appears correct in my humble opinion. Examine it carefully.
