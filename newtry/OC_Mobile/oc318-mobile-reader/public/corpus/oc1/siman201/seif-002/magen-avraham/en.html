One should not give precedence. The Gemara states that one who does so is liable to death because he disgraces the Torah.
To open first. This refers to Torah reading, according to Rashi and Ran. The Rosh writes that the kohen should be the first speaker at every public gathering, speaking and expounding first. The same applies in a yeshiva, where he should speak first, as Rashi explains in Gittin. The Tur, Yoreh De'ah, siman 28, in the gloss, writes that if an Israelite was called first, he is not required to pay ten gold coins, because the verse is only an asmachta. Tosafos in Chullin 87a write similarly. This requires study, for in Gittin 59b the Gemara explicitly states that the law is D'Oraisa. Rambam likewise counts it among the mitzvos, mitzvah 32. See chapter 4 of Hilchos Klei HaMikdash and Choshen Mishpat, siman 382. Rivash, siman 94, likewise writes plainly in the name of Semag that it is D'Oraisa, as does Responsa Maharam, siman 107. The Yerushalmi, chapter 5 of Gittin, cites a dispute among the Amoraim on this matter. See Maharik. The Gemara further states that regarding taking a choice portion first, the kohen takes precedence. Tosafos and the Rosh explain that this applies specifically to maaser ani distributed within the home, or charity divided among companions reclining at a meal. It does not apply to jointly owned property, for the Sages said that one who sets his eyes upon the choice portion will never see a sign of blessing. This requires study: why are people not careful nowadays to give the kohen precedence in all these matters? One should be careful, since these obligations are D'Oraisa. Perhaps the reason is that we are not expert regarding the lineage of kohanim, as stated at the end of siman 457. Nevertheless, if the kohen wishes to waive his honor in favor of another, he may do so, as Semag writes. The Tur writes that some say a levi must be given precedence over an Israelite, since Rabbi Yehoshua ben Levi said, “In all my days, an Israelite never recited the blessing before me.” The Tur rejects this, explaining that the statement was due to Rabbi Yehoshua ben Levi's own greatness. Bach writes that this cannot be correct, for it is impossible that Rabbi Yehoshua ben Levi never ate with someone greater than himself, and yet he still recited the blessing. Bach brings further proofs; see there. The liturgical poem Nodah Leshimcha likewise says, “With the permission of the kohanim and leviim.” Some read, “the kohanim, the leviim,” meaning that kohanim are themselves called leviim. The Mishnah at the end of Horayos states that a levi precedes an Israelite, and this is also ruled in Yoreh De'ah, siman 251, seif 9. Since we find that a levi precedes regarding receiving charity and being called to the Torah, the same should apply to other matters, for a principle is not applied only halfway. This requires study: why does the Gemara in Gittin cite other verses, and likewise in Horayos? See Midrash Rabbah, Parashas Naso, and Yerushalmi Horayos. I later saw in the Yerushalmi at the end of Horayos that a levi and an Israelite are equal, and only when the leviim served on the platform did a levi take precedence. In Yoreh De'ah, however, the ruling does not follow that view, as Beit Yosef writes there. This requires study, for Rashi on Parashas Re'eh likewise writes that they are equal, and so does the Sifri there.
