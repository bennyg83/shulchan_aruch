He shall not precede. And we say in the Gemara that he is liable for death, for he disparages the Torah.

To open first — in the reading of the Torah (Rashi and Ran), and the Rosh wrote to be the head who speaks in every assembly, to speak and expound first; and the same applies in a yeshiva — he speaks first (Rashi on Gittin). And it is written in Tur Orach Chayim siman 28 in the Hagahah that if an Israelite went up first, they do not obligate him to ten [gold coins] — this is because the verse is merely an asmakhta; and likewise Tosafot on Chullin daf 87; but this requires further study, for in Gittin daf 59 we explicitly say that it is d'oraisa, and the Rambam too counts it among the counted mitzvot siman 32; and see chapter 4 of Hilchot Klei HaMikdash and Choshen Mishpat siman 382; and likewise Rivash siman 94 in the name of Semag explicitly that it is d'oraisa; and likewise in responsa of Maharam siman 107; and in Yerushalmi chapter 5 of Gittin he brings the dispute of the amoraim in this matter; and see in Maharik who said: in the Gemara "and take a beautiful coin first" — the priest precedes; and Tosafot and the Rosh explained that specifically regarding maaser ani that is divided within the house, or regarding charity among friends reclining at a meal; but regarding a partnership matter, no — for we say, one who sets his eyes on a beautiful portion never sees a sign of blessing; and this requires further study: why are we not careful nowadays to precede the priest in all those matters, and one should be careful in this since they are d'oraisa; and perhaps we are not expert in priestly lineages, as is written above siman 157; however, if the priest wishes to share honor with another, he may (Semag). The Tur wrote that one must precede a Levi before an Israelite, for R' Yehuda haLevi said, "In my days an Israelite never blessed before me"; but it does not appear that there he spoke on account of his greatness, for R' Yehuda haLevi was great; and the Bach wrote that this is not so, for it is impossible that R' Yehuda haLevi never ate in his days with one greater than himself, and nevertheless he blessed; and he brought proofs there; and likewise he established in the hymn "Nodah Leshimcha," with the permission of the priests and the Levites; and some read "the priests the Levites" — meaning that priests are called Levites. And in the mishnah at the end of Horayot they say: a Levi precedes an Israelite; and likewise in Yoreh De'ah siman 251 seif 9; and since we see that regarding taking charity and reading the Torah he precedes, therefore the same applies to other matters — there is no learning by halves; and this requires further study: why does he bring in Gittin other verses, and in Horayot likewise; and see in Midrash Rabba parshat Nasa and Yerushalmi of Horayot. Afterwards I saw in Yerushalmi end of Horayot that it is stated there: a Levi and an Israelite are equal — only at the time of the duchan a Levi preceded; but in Yoreh De'ah they ruled not like him, as the Beit Yosef wrote there; and this requires further study: for Rashi on parshat Re'eh likewise wrote so, and so in the Sifri there.
