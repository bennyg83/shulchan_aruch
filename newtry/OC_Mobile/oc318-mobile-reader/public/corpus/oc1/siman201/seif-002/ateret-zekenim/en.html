He shall not precede, etc. And this is what the Talmud states in the chapter Bnei HaIr: Rabbah said in Bach — R' Yochanan said: any Torah scholar who blesses before him — even twenty-three commoners — is liable for death, as it is said (Proverbs 8:36), "Those who love death" — do not read "from me" (mishanai) but "cause me to be hated" (mishanya'i). And Rashi explained that they cause people to hate them, for they say there is no satisfaction in the Torah when they see a Torah scholar lowered before a commoner. Or there is another explanation: it appears as though the blessing is unworthy — that he should be the blesser on account of the importance of the commoner who is fit to bless. I found in the name of Maharshach: even twenty-three commoners — one may not precede them; but a Torah scholar who is a priest — there is a mitzvah to precede him, namely when he is equal to an Israelite Torah scholar; but if the priest Torah scholar is not equal to the Israelite Torah scholar, then it is better as an act of piety to precede him — thus is implied from Tosafot in the chapter Bnei HaIr regarding the incident of R' Perida, and on this there is support for the custom in these countries that they take permission from the priests even in a place where there is a Torah scholar to give permission to an Israelite — even a commoner — to bless (Tur). And there are those who are stringent not to give permission even to a commoner priest to bless before a Torah scholar (my teacher in Bach). And some say that a Levi has no precedence at all to bless before an Israelite (Tur in the name of Maharam of Rotenburg).
