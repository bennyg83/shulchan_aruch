(Note 4) To open, etc. — they do not obligate him to ten gold coins. He wrote in Prisha there: it does not specifically mean ten, for one who goes up to the Torah blesses two blessings — one before and one after — and for each blessing he is obligated ten gold coins; but rather he means they do not obligate him to ten gold coins — meaning the obligation is for the blessing that the Sages obligated ten gold coins, and automatically one who goes up to the Torah reaches twenty gold coins.

Regarding what is called, etc. — so wrote the Tur there: in the reading they are all equally obligated, this one like that one; and even if a priest went up and an Israelite stood in his place, they do not obligate him to ten gold coins. And that which we say "and you shall sanctify him" for all matters of sanctity is an asmakhta, and we do not derive from it in Beit Din — end of his words.

And this requires further study, for in Gittin, etc. — for we learned: a priest reads first on account of ways of peace. And it challenges: if you say on account of ways of peace — is this not d'oraisa, as it is written "and you shall sanctify him," etc.; and it answers: on account of ways of peace — the priest cannot waive to another reading, lest discord arise.

And likewise he established in the hymn, etc. — and likewise in Yoreh De'ah siman 251 seif 9 regarding redeeming captives, or for sustenance, or for clothing; and if there is a Levi and an Israelite and there is not enough in the charity purse for both, the Levi precedes in all the above matters.

For there is no learning by halves. And Darkei Moshe in Yoreh De'ah challenged the Tur: for here in Orach Chayim it appears that the Tur's opinion is that a Levi has no precedence; and there in Yoreh De'ah he rules plainly like the one who holds that he precedes an Israelite in all the above; and he interpreted, saying: it appears that here (meaning in Yoreh De'ah) it speaks regarding sustaining him — certainly a Levi has precedence over everyone, for today or tomorrow the Beit HaMikdash will be built and the Levi will return to his service and to his duchan; not so in Orach Chayim, which speaks regarding honoring him for the moment — and sustenance does not depend on this; it seems reasonable to the Rema that he has no precedence, etc. — end of his words; see there what he wrote further.
