Seif katan 4: To open, etc. They do not obligate him to pay ten gold coins. The Perishah writes there that “ten” is not precise, for one called to the Torah recites two blessings, one before the reading and one afterward, and for each blessing he would owe ten gold coins. Rather, the meaning is that he is not obligated in the ten-gold-coin payment established by Chazal for a blessing. Consequently, one called to the Torah would otherwise owe twenty gold coins.
For the verse, etc. The Tur writes there: Regarding Torah reading, everyone is equally obligated, and even if an Israelite stood in place of the kohen, he is not required to pay ten gold coins. The law derived from “You shall sanctify him,” concerning every matter of sanctity, is only an asmachta, and payment is therefore not collected from him by beis din.
This requires study, for in Gittin, etc. The Mishnah states that a kohen reads first because of the ways of peace. The Gemara asks: You say it is because of the ways of peace, but is it not a Torah law, as it is written, “You shall sanctify him”? It answers that because of the ways of peace, the kohen may not waive his honor and allow another to read first, lest this lead to quarrels.
And so it was established in the liturgical poem, etc. This is likewise ruled in Yoreh De'ah, siman 251, seif 9, regarding redeeming captives, providing sustenance, or clothing. If there is a levi and an Israelite, and the charity fund does not contain enough for both, the levi takes precedence in all these matters.
For a principle is not applied only halfway. The Derishah in Yoreh De'ah asks regarding the Tur: Here in Orach Chayim, the Tur appears to hold that a levi has no precedence, while in Yoreh De'ah he rules without qualification like the opinion that a levi takes precedence over an Israelite in all the matters mentioned above. He answers: “It appears that there, meaning in Yoreh De'ah, the discussion concerns sustaining his life. In that respect a levi certainly has precedence according to all opinions, because today or tomorrow the Beis HaMikdash may be rebuilt and the levi will return to his service and his platform. This differs from Orach Chayim, which concerns honoring him temporarily, where his livelihood does not depend upon it. Rama therefore holds that he has no precedence.” See there for what else he writes.
