Not to. See Baer Heitev, and what he wrote in the name of Kenesses HaGedolah; and see there in Kenesses HaGedolah, who permits on the night of immersion, or when going out on the road, or when coming from the road; see there. And what Baer Heitev wrote, "But one who fasts, etc.," so wrote Eliyah Rabbah, which implies that one who fasts also on the tenth, nevertheless one should not refrain on the night of the eleventh.
Midday. See Baer Heitev; and Machtzik Beracha wrote that those who fear Hashem customarily are stringent the whole day, etc. (and so wrote Eliyah Rabbah), for from the Bach it implies to be stringent the whole day, and so it is proper to do for Torah scholars and perushim. And a woman who has given birth, even though she fasts after seven [days], is permitted on the night of the tenth to meat and wine, and all the more so when Tisha B'Av is postponed and she fasts, that she is permitted at night to meat and wine; see there. And those who are accustomed not to recite Shehecheyanu from the Seventeenth of Tammuz: also on the night and day of the tenth it is forbidden. And if most of the people of the city eat meat on the night of the tenth, and one person customarily did not eat, but he did not accept it as a vow, only practiced it as a mere custom, and it happened that his yom limud of mourning concluded in the week in which Tisha B'Av falls, and he has distress not to shave: it is permitted to shave on the night of the tenth, since most of the city do not have this stringency, and also he practiced it only regarding eating; and Hagahos Maimoniyos is lenient in every case regarding haircutting; see there. And what he wrote in the name of Magen Avraham, when it falls on Thursday, etc., and it is permitted to cut hair in the morning, until here; and so wrote Eliyah Rabbah at the end of siman 559, with a proof from siman 551, se'if 16, from the Tur and Bach. And so he wrote in She'elos Yaavetz, part 1, siman 106; see there.
Permitted. See Baer Heitev; and he wrote in Shiyarei Kenesses HaGedolah, who wrote in a responsum that even those who wish to fast for two days [may do so] with all the five afflictions, but to idle oneself from words of Torah, or not to put on tzitzis and tefillin, one has no permission to neglect; see there. And even one who fasts on the tenth: if Tisha B'Av falls on Shabbos and is postponed, he need not fast on the eleventh day; see Peri Megadim.
Forbidden. See Baer Heitev; and Machtzik Beracha wrote in the name of Zera Emes that to cut hair is permitted on the night of a postponed Tisha B'Av; and even if they were accustomed not to cut hair, it is a mistaken custom and needs no annulment; and even one who wishes to be stringent in this as well: nevertheless, one should not be stringent for a mohel who needs to go to another city to circumcise the next day; it is permitted for him to cut hair on the night of the tenth of a [postponed] Tisha B'Av, for he is obligated for the honor of the milah, so that when he arrives in the other city he will be free to fulfill the mitzvah of milah immediately; see there.
