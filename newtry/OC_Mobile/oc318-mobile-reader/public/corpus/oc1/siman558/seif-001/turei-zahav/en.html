A proper custom, etc. In the Tur this language is written, and he concluded with this language: only to restore the soul, so that it be close to affliction; end of his words. And my father-in-law and teacher, of blessed memory, explained that this phrase "only to restore," etc., does not refer to meat so as to say that a small amount of meat is permitted to restore the soul; rather, this is what he is saying: one should eat even other foods only enough to restore the soul, but meat not at all. Also from the language of the Shulchan Aruch, which did not copy the conclusion of the matter, it implies that he explained it thus. But if so, he should nevertheless have written this matter, to teach that even with other foods one should reduce. And it appears that the author of the Shulchan Aruch did not explain it as to be stringent with other foods to reduce them, for even abundance in them is not joy, for there is no joy without meat; rather, this is what the Tur is saying: one should not eat meat, which brings joy, but rather eat other foods, which are only to restore the soul. And see what I wrote in siman 559, se'if 10. And this custom has already spread throughout all these lands thus, namely for about forty years; and before that they would eat meat on the night of Tisha B'Av, and so it is in the words of R' Shlomo Luria in a responsum to conduct oneself thus. And he further wrote that on the tenth it is forbidden to bathe or to cut hair or to launder, unlike what is written in the minhagim; and according to what the Rama wrote that some are stringent until midday, it is all the more obvious that after midday everything is permitted; see what I wrote at the end of siman 559.
