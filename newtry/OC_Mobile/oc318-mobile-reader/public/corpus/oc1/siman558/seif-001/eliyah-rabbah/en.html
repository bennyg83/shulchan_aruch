[Levush] A person should not indulge, etc. Even with other foods that are not meat and wine (Bach); and the view of the Taz is to be lenient. Magen Avraham wrote that at a seudas mitzvah all the invited guests are permitted to eat meat, for this night is light, since by the law of the Gemara it is permitted to cut hair and to launder; however, the meal that they make on the night before the wedding is not called a seudas mitzvah, and one should not eat meat; and so I heard from the elder rabbis; and all the more so there should be no musical instruments there until after midday; until here. But in Eliyahu Zuta I wrote that the Gaon, my grandfather, of blessed memory, permitted the chasan and kallah and their father and mother to eat meat, but not the other people of the meal. And in Ashkenaz, where they send sevalonos (bridal gifts) at that time, it appears to me that all the people of the meal are permitted to eat meat, for so he wrote in the Responsa Maharam Mintz, siman 109, that it is a seudas mitzvah; and so wrote Magen Avraham in siman 640, s.k. 13, and siman 444, even if he has not yet betrothed; and not like what Magen Avraham wrote here that it is not a mitzvah, since they do not recite "shehasimcha bimono," which would imply even when they send sevalonos, for there are many seudos mitzvah at which they do not recite "shehasimcha bimono." Afterwards the Kitzur Shlah was printed, and he wrote that in most of Ashkenaz the invited guests eat meat and drink wine and have musical instruments, and it is impossible to protest against them; and one who has fear of Heaven should not be present at that meal, for a seudas sevalonos is not called a mitzvah; until here. And in my humble opinion, their custom has a foundation and support, as I explained; and even for one who fears Heaven it is permitted and a mitzvah.
