(s.k. 1) A warm pot — and if, etc., on the fire — one could say the law of shehiyah, and see siman 253. And in Sefer Teshuvah the Rabbeynu wrote the reason that the warm pot does not add heat; on the contrary, its heat diminishes and goes — end of his words, the Rabbeynu, brought in Taz; and see what Taz wrote and what was written against him in Sefer Teshuvah; and Sefer Teshuvah wrote as follows: and therefore it must be that it speaks of one who does it in the manner of insulation — end of his words; meaning that he covers it with garments, for otherwise why would the Rabbeynu need a reason — for this is not insulation at all, only that it stands on a warm pot, and what connection does this have to insulation; and Sefer Teshuvah concluded as follows: and in any case you must know that what Magen Avraham wrote here — that if, etc., on the fire one could say the law of shehiyah — is a printing error, and it must read the law of insulation, for this is called adding heat from the fire beneath it, as written siman 257 seif 8, etc., end of his words, Sefer Teshuvah; and in my humble opinion it is strained to emend Magen Avraham as well — see siman 253, where the law of shehiyah is explained and not the law of insulation; also according to Sefer Teshuvah, that the Rabbeynu needed a reason because it speaks of one who did it in the manner of insulation — whence to the Rabbeynu that Tur speaks of this matter that required a reason; also Tur's wording does not imply that it speaks of the manner of insulation; and according to Tur the Rabbeynu wrote his words; therefore it appears to me that Magen Avraham was explaining the Rabbeynu's words — that therefore he needed a reason, for he follows his view in his long book siman 253, and likewise in Shulchan Aruch siman 253 end of seif 1: if the pot stands on coals even though it is uncovered above the sides, it is called insulation — therefore he needed his reason here to distinguish between standing on the fire or on a warm pot; but for us, as Rama wrote there in siman 253, that even standing on coals in such a manner is not called insulation but the law of shehiyah — therefore Magen Avraham did not copy the Rabbeynu's reason for us; if so, for us even standing on the fire there is no concern of insulation, only of shehiyah, and the whole law speaks of where it is not in the manner of insulation — and see Magen Avraham's words.
