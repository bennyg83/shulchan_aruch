For this is not considered insulating in a substance that adds heat. The Beit Yosef wrote that the reason is that the hot pot does not add heat. On the contrary, its heat continually diminishes. End quote. It appears to me that this applies only when one wishes to insulate the item for many hours, so that the pot may cool before that period ends. If, however, he wishes to insulate it for a short time and remove it while the pot is still hot, it is forbidden, since in any event the pot is then a substance that adds heat. Therefore, those who, during the winter, take a vessel containing a beverage on Shabbos and insert it into a vessel filled with hot water so that its chill will dissipate, and then drink the beverage, do not act properly. This is literally the manner of hatmanah, since the entire vessel is insulated within the hot water, and hatmanah is forbidden even for a short time. The principal basis for permitting the case here is that the upper vessel is not actually insulated within the lower pot, but merely rests upon it. Concerning this the Tur and Shulchan Aruch wrote that it is not considered insulating, meaning that the manner of hatmanah is absent here, as I have explained. See what I wrote in siman 318, se'if 13.
