As the Chiddushei HaRan writes at the beginning of the chapter Bameh Tomnin regarding a basket in which one insulated, whenever the basket is in contact with olive waste, which adds heat, partial hatmanah is considered hatmanah. The Beit Yosef therefore needed the reason that the lower pot is cooling, meaning when there is no fire beneath it. On Shabbos it is forbidden if the upper item will become heated to yad soledes bo by a kli rishon, for one would then be cooking on Shabbos. If, however, it is impossible for the upper item to reach yad soledes bo heat, it is permitted even on Shabbos to place a cold item there, since the lower pot does not add heat, as in siman 257, se'if 6; see there. See also siman 318, se'ifim 6, 7, 8, 13, and 15. The Taz introduced a novel ruling here. A vessel containing hot water, even a kli sheni whose contents are at yad soledes bo heat, and all the more so a stove whose coals were swept out or covered with ash, does not constitute insulation in a substance that adds heat when one insulates something there for an extended period, such as an hour or two, because it gradually cools. An explicit indication of one's intent regarding the insulation is effective, and when it is evident that he does not intend to add heat, it is permitted even if the item is entirely insulated within it. This is not so when he insulates it for a short time and wants it to become warm there. That is certainly considered insulation in something that adds heat, even in a kli sheni, because for the moment it certainly adds heat, as sensory experience demonstrates. Therefore, those who entirely immerse a cold vessel in hot water contained in a kli sheni do not act properly. According to our practice, when the entire vessel is immersed, this is hatmanah, and for the short time involved the water is considered to add heat. It is therefore forbidden even in a kli sheni, as I wrote in siman 318, letter 19. In a kli rishon, there is in any event also a prohibition of cooking. According to this, the Tur's statement that this is not considered insulating in a substance that adds heat means that whenever part of the item remains exposed it is not considered hatmanah according to our practice, as in siman 253, se'if 1, regarding an item that touches the coals. This is why the Rama wrote nothing here. Even if the lower pot stands on the fire, since the upper pot remains exposed, the case is not one of hatmanah but only of shehiyah, as the Magen Avraham wrote. See Eliyah Rabbah here. According to what I have written, the matter is well explained. With God's help, this will be clarified in siman 259 among the laws of hatmanah; this is not the place to elaborate.
