A cold item. Even if it is raw and will now cook through the heat of the pot beneath it, it is nevertheless permitted, since he places it there before nightfall.
For this is not considered insulating, etc. This means that even if he covered both the vessel and the pot with garments, and the cold item certainly gains heat from the pot, it is nevertheless permitted. The pot is not considered comparable to olive waste and similar materials that add heat. Those materials add heat of themselves, whereas the pot possesses no heat of its own. On the contrary, its heat continually diminishes. It is therefore permitted. This applies specifically when the cold item is placed there before nightfall. It is forbidden, however, to place it on Shabbos upon the pot beneath the covering of garments so that it will warm, even if the heat is not great enough for it to reach yad soledes bo. On Shabbos the Sages permitted insulating a cold item only so that its chill would dissipate, but here, where heat is generated through the hatmanah, it is forbidden. If the pot is not covered with garments, there is no prohibition against placing the cold item upon it unless it can reach yad soledes bo heat, as stated below in siman 318, se'if 6. The later authorities wrote that on Shabbos it is forbidden to take a vessel containing a cold beverage and insert it into a vessel filled with hot water so that it will warm inside, even if it cannot reach yad soledes bo heat. This is literally the manner of hatmanah, since the entire vessel is insulated within the hot water. If the water is hot enough that the beverage can reach yad soledes bo heat, then even when the vessel is not entirely immersed, but remains exposed at the top, and even when his intention is only to remove the chill, it is nevertheless forbidden, lest he forget it there until it reaches yad soledes bo heat and thereby become liable for cooking, as stated below in siman 318, se'if 14.
