What Rama wrote in the gloss: that one's body is repugnant from the orifices — that is when one cannot stand until a parsa; nevertheless there is no such case of "you shall not make yourselves detestable" prohibited in words of Torah. See Levush, Pri Megadim 63 on seif 2, see there. And what Rama wrote in seif 2 — that is because the Mechaber wrote that both in prayer and in Kerias Shema, if he awoke in the middle of prayer he is not permitted to interrupt; but in Kerias Shema if he wishes he reads as usual and if he wishes he stops (see Levush). But before Kerias Shema he cannot stand until a parsa — it is forbidden even for Kerias Shema and even for words of Torah, as written in seif 1, except that he does not return and read Kerias Shema bedi'eaved. And Rama emended that if there is [reason] on account of "you shall not make yourselves detestable" he interrupts, etc.; Magen Avraham 2. But in Kerias Shema certainly even Magen Avraham agrees that if he desires so much that there is "you shall not make yourselves detestable," he should interrupt in the middle of Kerias Shema — and this is obvious; Magen Avraham.

Need — Ateret Zekenim. And similarly Perisha and Nekudas HaKesef questioned, and Berachos 15a textual reading: Rif and Rambam hold for Kerias Shema, but for prayer one returns, etc.; and even if time passes it is forbidden to pray without water. And Tosafos there 15b to him: for prayer too if time passes one does not wait; and similarly if time has not passed one waits. But afterward one does not return entirely even slightly; and before him too, if he is under duress or weak-hearted he may pray with cleaning what cleans. And how did the Mechaber write here like the Rif and Tosafos? And one could say he adopts a stringency — for perhaps this is a greater stringency: that one is not permitted to pray without water even if time passes. And in Ralbag the Mechaber wrote Tosafos entirely: if he has ready water he takes — implying to take even for prayer; and if time passes it is obvious he need not return; and even without time passing he need not trouble after it, only he is obligated to return to what is available to him, etc. And behold one can distinguish: on the road — such as when time has not passed he is obligated before him four mil and after him one mil; but in his home they did not trouble him at all. Or one can distinguish between hands that are filthy where one returns after them, and ordinary hands where one need not return after them. Or one may say below regarding Minchah that the main beginning of the Mechaber is nine and a half [hours] (Magen Avraham and Taz there) — the law cannot be conceived before him four mil, which is the measure of an hour and a fifth, and all the time of Minchah for R' Yehuda until half of Minchah is this measure (less than three mil) — therefore he did not write this there. Also for the Mechaber, by kal vachomer it implies prayer is from the Torah; if so it is not proper to delay on account of passing time for rabbinic water; and even though the first view is only words of Torah, nevertheless the Mechaber ruled when time passes one need not wait. And in our innovations we wrote that perhaps there are three disputes: Rif, Ra'avad, and Tosafos — see Peri Chadash here — and one need not elaborate. (In Taz one must say this is the ruling of the Rif).
