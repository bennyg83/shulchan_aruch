(Seif katan 1) And he must, etc. This is evident from the Gemara, page 23: The Sages taught that if one was standing in prayer and urine began flowing down his knees, he must interrupt until the flow ceases. The Gemara asks to what point he returns when he resumes. Rav Hisda and Rav Hamnuna disagree: one says that he returns to the place where he stopped, and the other says that he returns to the beginning of the prayer. The Gemara concludes that if he paused long enough to complete the entire prayer, all agree that he returns to the beginning. They disagree only where he did not pause that long. One Sage holds that the person was disqualified. Rashi explains that when he began praying he already needed to relieve himself and was therefore disqualified from prayer; consequently, what he prayed was not prayer and he must return to the beginning. The other Sage holds that the person was fit and his prayer was valid. Rashi explains that although he could not refrain from urinating until he finished, what he prayed before the flow began was a valid prayer. We rule like the opinion that he returns to the place where he stopped, as stated above in siman 78. Thus, even if he sensed before prayer that he would need to urinate, which is why the other opinion requires him to return to the beginning, we nevertheless rule that he returns only to the place where he stopped, and what he prayed initially counts. Regarding the Rif's version of the Gemara in this form, see Beit Yosef.
And this is evident from siman 80; this is the correct reading. There it states that if one knows with certainty that he cannot restrain himself from passing gas until he finishes Krias Shema and the Amidah, it is better that he allow the time for Krias Shema and prayer to pass rather than pray. If it appears to him that he can restrain himself during Krias Shema, he should put on, etc. Thus, if he can restrain himself until he reads Krias Shema, that suffices, and he may read Krias Shema while wearing tefillin. The same applies here: if he can restrain himself for the time required to walk a parsah, which is the time in which he can finish the Amidah, it suffices. Bach writes that the time required to walk a parsah is close to one hour, and the Sages state in Berachos that the measure for prayer is one hour. Therefore, if waiting will cause the prayer time to pass, he may pray in this manner. Magen Avraham compares defecation to urination and to the law of passing gas, especially where tefillin are on his head.
