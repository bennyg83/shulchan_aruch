(Comment 1) And one must [return] — as our master taught in the Gemara on page 23: the Tanna taught, If one was standing in prayer and urine was dripping onto his garments, he interrupts until the urine stops. And they said: When he returns, to which place must he return? Rav Huna and Rav Chisda — one said to the place where he stopped, and one said to the beginning of the prayer. And they conclude: If he waited long enough to finish the entire [prayer], all agree he returns to the beginning; they disagree only when he did not wait — one master holds he is a person set aside (Rashi: when he began to pray and needed to relieve himself, he is set aside from praying; therefore what he prayed is not prayer and he returns to the beginning, etc.), and one master holds he is a fit person and his prayer counts (Rashi: even though he cannot wait to urinate until he finishes his prayer, what he prayed before urinating is valid prayer, etc.). And we rule like the one who said he returns to the place where he stopped. Above in siman 78 it is clear that even if he felt before prayer that he needs to urinate — so why would the other view think he returns to the beginning? Nevertheless we rule he does not return except to the place he stopped, and what he prayed initially counts for him. On the Rif who has this text in the Gemara, see Beit Yosef.

And similarly in siman 80, as it should be. And this is what he wrote there: one for whom it is clear that he cannot restrain himself from passing gas until he finishes Keriat Shema and prayer — it is better that the time for Keriat Shema and prayer pass and that he not pray, etc. And if it appears to him that he can restrain himself at the time of Keriat Shema, he should leave off, etc. — from this it is clear that if he can restrain himself until he reads Keriat Shema, that alone suffices, and it is permitted to recite Keriat Shema with tefillin. The same applies here: if he can restrain himself for the measure of a parasang, which is the measure in which he can finish the prayer — and as the Bach wrote, the measure of a parasang is close to the measure of an hour, and the measure of prayer is stated in Berachos as the measure of an hour — then since if he waits the time for prayer will pass, it is permitted to pray in this manner. And he compares Magen Avraham's ruling on defecation to urination, and especially when tefillin are on his head.
