He needed to relieve himself, etc. — And a prayer leader who needs to relieve himself before the repetition of the prayer leader: if he can restrain himself for the distance of a parsa, since after the fact his prayer is valid prayer, and there is in this [a consideration of] human dignity (kevod habriyot), it is simple that he may rely on this on the view of the Rif, who is lenient lechatchila regarding the measure of a parsa; and it seems that even those who are strict agree here because of human dignity. But if he cannot restrain himself for the measure of a parsa, for according to all opinions his prayer is an abomination — what is the law? And one cannot bring proof to be lenient from what Magen Avraham agreed to, that if an urge arose for him in the congregation in the middle of prayer to the point that there is concern for [violating] "you shall not make yourselves detestable" (bal teshaktzu) — nevertheless he may finish the prayer because human dignity overrides the prohibition of bal teshaktzu. If so, here too in our case, for seemingly the prohibition of one who needs to relieve himself for prayer is also only a rabbinic prohibition; nevertheless it is not comparable, for it is different there, for the prohibition that the Sages forbade on one who needs to relieve himself to pray and said his prayer is an abomination — there is no [such factor], since he checked himself at the outset and began in a permitted state; and you know that even Rama, who is strict, agrees there that after the fact, if he did not interrupt, his prayer is prayer because of this reason, as the Gra wrote. Not so in our case, where he needs to relieve himself before he begins to pray, for the Sages decreed on this and said his prayer is an abomination; if so, all his blessings are void and there is in them [violation of] "you shall not take [the Name in vain]" (lo tisa). Perhaps one may not be lenient because of human dignity; and perhaps the Sages removed their prohibition from this, and thereby the blessing is not void — requires further study. And he must return and pray — See Mishna Berurah regarding minors: the first view (yesh em le-achrona kama) is Magen Avraham and Chayei Adam, who hold that he need not return and recite blessings after the fact; the second view (yesh em le-achrona batra) is Eliyah Rabbah and the Biur Halacha, who agree that he must return and recite blessings; and we have no power to decide between them, for although Eliyah Rabbah brought in the name of responsa of Rashba and responsa of Rama and Maggid Mishneh who hold like him — see in Magen Giborim and in Sefer Maamar Mordechai, who brought many views of the Rishonim who hold like Magen Avraham; and according to them, even if he cannot finish his prayer — for in the middle his feet began dripping water onto his knees — nevertheless afterward, when the dripping of water stops and he must return to his prayer, he need only return to where he stopped, even if he knew from the outset that he needed to relieve himself; see there:

The measure of a parsa — See Mishna Berurah. And that which he did not state explicitly the measure of an hour and a fifth: Chiddushei R. Akiva Eiger wrote in the name of Ginat Veradim that this comes to teach that even if by sitting in one place he can restrain himself a little more, nevertheless we estimate as though he is walking and cannot restrain himself and walk up to a parsa. And the reason for the measure of a parsa — see in Bach, Gra, and Pri Megadim, and in Chayei Adam I saw a new reason: the Sages estimated that if he cannot restrain himself for this measure, certainly the food in his intestines has already turned to excrement, and it is written "and all my inward parts [shall say]: Holy" (Tehillim 86:12); therefore he must return and pray. And it is not clear according to his words what to do with the responsum of Rashba, who holds that even for minors after the fact his prayer is an abomination. And further, if so — if he estimated for himself that he can hold back for the measure of a parsa and transgressed and prayed, and after prayer immediately went and relieved himself — would the prayer be void because of this?
