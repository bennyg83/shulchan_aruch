(1) He shall not pray — for it is written, "Prepare to meet your God, O Israel" (Amos 4:12), and it is written, "Guard your foot when you go to the house of God" (Ecclesiastes 5:1). And our Sages expounded: The Holy One, blessed be He, said: Guard your orifices at the hour that you stand in prayer before Him.

(2) And one must [return and pray] — some say specifically for adults; but for minors, bedi'eaved one need not return and pray again. And there are those who disagree.

(3) A parsa's walk — this is four mil, which is the measure of an hour and a fifth.

(4) He can restrain himself — that is, from the time he began prayer he had strength to wait this amount. And it is implied that even if afterward, immediately after prayer, he went and relieved himself and it was within this measure, he has fulfilled bedi'eaved.

(5) But lechatchilah — that is, even if he can restrain himself more than a parsa, since he feels somewhat that he needs his orifices — and even if by cleaning himself he will not be able to pray with the congregation — nevertheless it is better to pray alone with a clean body. However, if he sees that by checking himself the time for prayer will entirely pass, Magen Avraham and the other later authorities agree that he may pray if he estimates that he can restrain himself for the measure of an hour and a fifth.

(6) Even for words of Torah — and all the more so for Kerias Shema and other blessings. And bedi'eaved it is implied from the words of Peri Megadim that he need not return and recite; and so too Ramban in Milchamos.

(7) That his body is detestable — even if he is not so desirous that there would be in it [violation of] "you shall not make yourselves detestable," since he cannot restrain himself until a parsa; and within a parsa it is permitted even for Kerias Shema. And Shulchan Aruch HaRav above in siman 6, se'if kaf-daled is stringent about this. And if he teaches Torah to many or preaches, and in the middle he needs his orifices — even for adults he is permitted to restrain himself, for "you shall not make yourselves detestable" is only a rabbinic prohibition, and it is set aside because of human dignity.
