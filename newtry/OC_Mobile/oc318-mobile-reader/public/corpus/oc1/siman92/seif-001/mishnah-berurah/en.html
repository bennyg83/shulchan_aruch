Should not pray. For it is written, “Prepare to meet your God, O Israel,” and it is written, “Guard your feet when you go to the House of God.” Chazal said: The Holy One, blessed is He, said: Guard your openings at the time you stand in prayer before Me.
And he must. Some say this is specifically for defecation, but for urination, after the fact he need not repeat the prayer. Others disagree.
The walking of a parsah. This is four mil, which is the measure of an hour and a fifth.
Can restrain himself. Meaning, from the beginning of the prayer he had the strength to wait this amount of time. It implies that even if immediately after the prayer he went and relieved himself, and it was within this measure, he fulfilled his obligation after the fact.
But initially. Meaning, even if he can restrain himself for more than a parsah, since he feels somewhat that he needs to relieve himself. Even if by cleaning himself he will not be able to pray with the congregation, it is nevertheless better to pray alone with a clean body. However, if he sees that by checking himself the time for prayer will pass entirely, the Magen Avraham and the other later authorities agree that he may pray if he estimates that he can restrain himself for the measure of a parsah.
Even in words of Torah. All the more so for Krias Shema and the other blessings. After the fact, it implies from the words of the Peri Megadim that he need not go back and read, and so wrote the Ramban in Milchamos.
That his body is repulsive. Even if he does not have such a strong urge that there is a prohibition of “do not make yourselves repulsive,” since he cannot restrain himself for a parsah. But for a parsah it is permitted, even for Krias Shema. The Shaarei Teshuvah above, in siman 6, seif katan 4, is stringent in this. If he teaches Torah publicly or gives a derashah, and in the middle he needs to relieve himself, even for defecation he may hold himself back, for “do not make yourselves repulsive” is only a D'Rabbanan prohibition, and it is pushed aside for human dignity.
