1) [Se'if 1] If he needed to relieve himself, he should not pray, etc. If he guards himself and waits to pray until he first relieves himself, not only is he rewarded for taking care not to stand in prayer before God with a body full of excrement and urinary filth, but he is additionally rewarded for adorning himself to stand before Him with a clean and dignified body, without any filth. Just as one adorns himself with dignified garments before a king, he adorns himself with a clean body (which is also the soul's garment) when standing in prayer before God. This too is included in 'Prepare to meet your God, O Israel,' interpreted as: adorn yourself before God. Bach.
2) There: If he needed to relieve himself, he should not pray, etc. Sefer Chasidim 821 states: 'Prepare to meet your God, O Israel' means that one should not come to need the privy while the congregation is going to the synagogue, so that he may pray with them. If circumstances left him no opportunity to relieve himself beforehand, it is improper to go to the synagogue while needing to do so, for it says, 'Bless Hashem, my soul, and all my innards, His holy Name.' Eliyah Rabbah 1 cites this.
3) There: He must repeat the prayer. Pri Chadash 1 rules likewise and writes that this is the view of all the poskim, rejecting Tosafos in chapter Mi SheMeiso, who describe it as a distortion that cannot be corrected. See Maamar Mordechai 2; Levush 1; Soles Belulah in Mekor Chaim 1; Beis Oved 34; Shulchan Aruch HaRav 1; Chayei Adam 3:5. Chayei Adam specifies that only the Shemoneh Esrei must be repeated; for other blessings he has fulfilled his obligation after the fact. Chesed LaAlafim 1, Kitzur Shulchan Aruch 12:3, and Ben Ish Chai, Mishpatim 2, agree. See our words above in siman 76, paragraph 37.
4) There: He must repeat the prayer. This applies specifically to defecation; for urination he need not repeat it. Magen Avraham 1; Soles Belulah 2; Birkei Yosef Katan; Halachos Ketanos II:79; Yad Aharon in Hagahos Tur; Beis Oved 1; Eshel Avraham 1; Machatzis HaShekel 1. Eliyah Rabbah 1-2, however, writes that the Gemara's and poskim's general phrase 'needed to relieve himself' includes both needs. He finds this implied by se'if 3 and explicit in Responsa Rashba 131, Responsa Rema 98, and Maadanei Melech 24. Mahari Ayash agrees in Lechem Yehudah, cited by Nehar Shalom 1, and Machazik Berachah 1 likewise writes explicitly that it covers both, unlike siman 406. Maamar Mordechai 1 nevertheless defends Magen Avraham and challenges Rashba, Rema, and Maadanei Melech. Machazik Berachah's objection is answered by Divrei Mordechai I, page 50a, cited by Divrei Menachem in Hagahos Tur 1 and Yafeh LaLev III:2. Erekh HaShulchan 1 cites Rashba, Rema, and Maadanei Melech, but concludes that prayer need not be repeated for urination, as also implied by Behag. The later authorities rule so: Shulchan Aruch HaRav and Chayei Adam there; see Nishmas Adam 1 and Ben Ish Chai there.
5) There: This applies when he cannot restrain himself for the time needed to walk a parsah, etc. I found it written that the Sages set a parsah because the first chapter of Berachos says the early pious men would remain in prayer for one hour, approximately the time an average person walks a parsah; ten parsaos in a twelve-hour day. Rabbeinu Yonah proves that it means an actual hour, one of the day's twelve, since the Gemara asks: there are three prayers daily, totaling nine hours, etc. Bach; Knesses HaGedolah, Hagahos Tur 1. Olas Tamid 3. Prishah 2 notes that walking a parsah takes more than an hour on that calculation, but a person does not walk continuously all day; additionally, the brief pauses before and after prayer, described at the beginning of the next siman, combine with prayer to equal walking a parsah. Ateres Zekenim 1 and Shesilei Zeitim 3 adopt his second answer. Gan HaMelech 48 explains that the walking measure is stringent: while sitting, one can restrain himself longer than while walking. Erekh HaShulchan 2 and Ben Ish Chai, Mishpatim 2, cite this. See Beis Oved 35.
6) There: Initially, he should not pray until he examines himself, etc. Bach writes that even if the prayer time will pass while he relieves himself, he may not pray first, even if he can restrain himself for a parsah. Magen Avraham 1 says one may perhaps rely in this case on Rif, who initially permits prayer when he can restrain himself for a parsah. Yad Aharon in Hagahos Tur and Beis Oved 1 cite this, and Eliyah Rabbah 3, Nehar Shalom 1, Kisei Eliyahu 1, Beis Oved 36, Shulchan Aruch HaRav 1, and Kitzur Shulchan Aruch 12:3 agree.
6) The same applies to a shaliach tzibbur who needs to relieve himself before the repetition: he may proceed because of human dignity. Likewise, if called to the Torah, he may ascend. Ammudei HaShulchan on Kitzur Shulchan Aruch there, in the name of Mishnah Berurah.
6) Eliyah Rabbah writes there that if he will miss communal prayer, he should first relieve himself. Chayei Adam 3:4, Chesed LaAlafim 2, and Ammudei HaShulchan agree. Chida, however, writes in Kesher Gudal 7:33 that even if the time for communal prayer will pass, one who can restrain himself for a parsah may pray. See Neveh Shalom there.
6) There, gloss: Anyone who needs to relieve himself is forbidden even in words of Torah, etc. Accordingly, the reference to prayer is not exclusive; the same applies to Krias Shema and its blessings. Olas Tamid 4. At times a person restrains himself out of human dignity while standing among a congregation; so as not to trouble the public to stand for him, or not to pass before the heads of the holy people, and the like. Praiseworthy is one who orders his ways and weighs the loss of one mitzvah against another. He should always examine himself early so he need not forfeit communal prayer or trouble the public. Carefulness leads to diligence, and diligence to cleanliness. Chesed LaAlafim 3. See Machazik Berachah, siman 3, paragraph 16.
7) There, gloss: As long as his body is repulsive because of its openings. This means he cannot restrain himself for a parsah. Mishbetzos Zahav, beginning of the siman. Regarding bal teshaktzu, see what we wrote above on siman 3:17.
