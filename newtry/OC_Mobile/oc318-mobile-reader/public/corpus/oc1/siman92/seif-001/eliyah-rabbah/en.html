[1] One must relieve himself, etc. — For this too is included in the verse "Prepare to meet your God" (Amos 4:12). And further it is written "Guard your foot when you go to the house of God" (Kohelet 5:6) — if it deals with guarding his feet that they not strike a stone when he goes to the house of God wherever he goes, rather it says guard his orifices (Levush). And in Sefer Chassidim, siman 874, he learned from this that one should relieve himself before the congregation goes to synagogue so that he need not delay afterward. He also wrote to see that feces not stick to his shoes when going to synagogue, as it says "Guard your foot," etc.; therefore it is written "Remove your shoe from upon your foot" (Shemot 3:5). And what the Levush wrote "wherever he goes" etc. is difficult — perhaps it comes to teach us that even though we hold in siman 90, seif 12, that there is a mitzvah to run when going to synagogue, one must nevertheless guard — lest you say "agents of a mitzvah are not harmed" — it teaches us where damage is common it is different, as stated in Pesachim 8a.
