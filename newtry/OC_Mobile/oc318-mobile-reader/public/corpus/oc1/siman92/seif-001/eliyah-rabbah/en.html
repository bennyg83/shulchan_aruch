One who needs to relieve himself, etc. This too is included in the verse, 'Prepare to meet your G-d.' It is also written, 'Guard your feet when you go to the House of G-d.' If this meant that he should guard his feet so that he not strike them on a stone when going to the House of G-d, that would apply wherever he goes. Rather, it means that he should guard his bodily needs, as the Levush explains. In Sefer Chasidim, siman 904, he learned from this that one should relieve himself before the congregation goes to the synagogue, so that he will not need to delay afterward. He also wrote that one should be careful that excrement not adhere to his shoes when he goes to the synagogue, as it says, 'Guard your feet, etc.' Therefore it is written, 'Remove your shoes from upon your feet.' What the Levush wrote, that it would apply wherever he goes, etc., is difficult, for perhaps the verse teaches that although we rule in siman 90, seif 12, that it is a mitzvah to run when going to the synagogue, he still must be careful. One should not say that agents of a mitzvah are not harmed; the verse teaches that where danger is common, the matter is different, as stated in Pesachim 8a. Possibly this is what he means: why specifically the House of G-d, the same should apply to any other mitzvah matter. But this is forced.
The measure of walking, etc. This is the measure of an hour, as the Bach writes. See above, siman 58, seif katan 5, and this requires study. Know that when the Gemara and poskim refer to one who needs to relieve himself, all bodily needs are implied, whether major or minor, as is implied in seif 3, and as is explicit in the responsum of the Rashba, siman 131. So too wrote the responsa of the Rama, siman 98, and Maadanei Melech, page 24. It is therefore astonishing that the Magen Avraham wrote simply that with minor needs he need not repeat the prayer, for the major needs mentioned above escaped him. Also, the proof he brought from the Gemara in Berachos 23 is not precise, for there the person did not need to relieve himself before prayer, but during prayer. However, there is some difficulty from below, at the end of siman 406 in the laws of techumin, where some say that only major needs are called nekavim.
But initially, etc. The Olas Tamid wrote in the name of the Bach that if checking before prayer will cause the time for prayer to pass, it is permitted even initially, since he can restrain himself for the measure of a parsah. This is not precise. Only according to the Rif's text, which reads in the Gemara that it is permitted initially, and since the Bach rules like him, did the Bach write that the case is where the time for prayer will pass. But according to the text of the Rosh and Rabbeinu Yonah, that it is forbidden, and this is how we rule, the Bach forbids it initially even if the time for prayer will pass. This is clear. Nevertheless, as a matter of practical halacha, the Magen Avraham wrote that perhaps one may rely on the Rif in this matter, and it appears to me that I saw in the Agudah that he ruled like the Rif. It appears to me, however, that even if prayer with the congregation will pass, he should relieve himself first. See above, siman 58, seif katan 7, that prayer in its proper time is preferable to prayer with the congregation.
