The time it takes to walk, etc. Maadanei Melech, page 24, explained that this applies even if they are absorbed into the ground. This is forced. But in Kol Bo, page 8, I saw the following wording: One who urinates into a vessel, or on an elevated place with something lower beneath it, need not distance himself because of the water, etc. It is permitted to urinate in the place where one prayed. Nevertheless, it is an attribute of piety not to urinate within four cubits of the place of prayer, Megillah 27, R. Y. 15. Sefer Chasidim, siman 828, wrote that one should not urinate or spit in the place where he covered the blood of a wild animal or bird with a blessing while the blood remains covered.
Because of the droplets, etc. Malbushei Yom Tov wrote: This is obvious, and did it need to be stated? Also, why four cubits? Is it worse than flowing water in siman 78, where he waits until it ceases? Rashi explained that the concern is that his garments may become soiled by droplets from his organ. It appears to me that this teaches that after he urinates in streams, there are droplets for the time it takes to walk four cubits. And what was said above, 'until they cease,' is also explained to mean that they cease completely, meaning that he waits from the beginning of his urination until the end.
