But Magen Avraham note 2 challenged Moreinu our Teacher, may he rest in Eden — for behold in Rashba siman 131 it is written explicitly that even when he can restrain himself until he finishes — and you have no shorter time than this — and he wrote that one may rely on Rashba; and in the congregation even Terumat HaDeshen agrees it is permitted, as written siman 150, that human dignity is great and pushes aside "do not turn aside"; and even individually one may rely on Rashba that it is permitted — for Semag wrote that it is forbidden to interrupt his prayer unless on account of danger to life; kal vachomer on account of a rabbinic prohibition, "they said and they said"; see there. And similarly Alef HaRokeach note 4 — that since bal teshaktzu is only a rabbinic prohibition, we do not require him to interrupt prayer, for that too is a prohibition; see there. And similarly the later authorities — that if he was aroused in the middle of his prayer, whether for large or small, he should not interrupt if he can restrain himself until he finishes the eighteen [blessings] — Chayei Adam general rule 25 note 2; Raz note 2; Beit Yosef note 37; Shiyurei Torah note 4; Ben Ish Chai, Parashat Mishpatim note 3:

[8] And if he cannot restrain himself — he interrupts for large [needs], but for small ones he does not interrupt; and even if water is dripping on his knees, he waits until the water finishes and returns to his prayer, and need not distance himself as written in siman 78 — Raz there.
