But initially, etc. The Olas Tamid wrote in the name of the Bach that if checking before prayer will cause the time for prayer to pass, it is permitted even initially, since he can restrain himself for the measure of a parsah. This is not precise. Only according to the Rif's text, which reads in the Gemara that it is permitted initially, and since the Bach rules like him, did the Bach write that the case is where the time for prayer will pass. But according to the text of the Rosh and Rabbeinu Yonah, that it is forbidden, and this is how we rule, the Bach forbids it initially even if the time for prayer will pass. This is clear. Nevertheless, as a matter of practical halacha, the Magen Avraham wrote that perhaps one may rely on the Rif in this matter, and it appears to me that I saw in the Agudah that he ruled like the Rif. It appears to me, however, that even if prayer with the congregation will pass, he should relieve himself first. See above, siman 58, seif katan 7, that prayer in its proper time is preferable to prayer with the congregation.
