[6] And if he fears, etc. In Nachalas Tzvi he wondered about Levush and Shulchan Aruch [who differ] from Rif and Rambam, who ruled that one must return four mil — he holds likely that even when there is no time and the time of prayer passes, one returns after water — the words of Shulchan Aruch and Levush contradict [this], up to here. And I wrote in Eliyah Zuta that he omitted the words of Lechem Chamudos there, who resolves this with good reason not to rule like them when there is no time. Again I saw in Taz that he wondered — for to Rif there is a stringency, that it is forbidden to pray until he has water; and to my impoverished mind this is a greater stringency — that he not pass the time, for it is possible with something that cleans; therefore he relied on this on Tosafos and Rosh. And further it seems to me that Shulchan Aruch holds that when the beginning of the time of prayer will pass, he must return; but when he fears that the time of prayer will pass entirely, Rif and Rambam agree that he cleans his hands with stones — and here Shulchan Aruch deals with when the time of prayer will pass entirely; and so it seems in Bach below siman 233 there, [regarding] the opinion of Rav Amram, even though it does not seem so in Beit Yosef. And behold Bach disagrees with Shulchan Aruch and wrote: since one need not return after water for Keriat Shema, for all agree one need not return for morning prayer either, since one must adjoin redemption to prayer — up to here. And it seems he goes after his reasoning below in siman 233, where he wrote that the primary [view] is like Rashi, that one need not return for prayer — but as a stringency we are concerned for Rif and Rambam; and anything that is not a mitzvah by law, one does not interrupt with a new song — for only for the need of a mitzvah, such as to answer Amen Yehei Shemei Rabbah and Kedusha, one interrupts, as above siman 66; examine this. However, according to Beit Yaakov, the primary view is like Rif and Rambam — one may interrupt with a new song. Also it is possible that he holds that even for Keriat Shema one must return after water at the beginning of its time; only at the end of its time one need not return — which is not so regarding prayer, where even at the end of its time one must return; and he ruled like Shulchan Aruch as a stringency, that also for Keriat Shema one must return if there is time, and at the end of its time also for prayer one need not return; examine this. Also it seems to me like the view of Tur — according to what Beit Yosef explains, that also for Keriat Shema one must return — for he explained "one who turns back for water to pray" — whether for Keriat Shema or for prayer; if so, certainly they are equal, not like Bach. Bach wrote in siman 233 that specifically on the road one need not return, but in his house he must return even for morning prayer if there is time — up to here; and it seems the same law applies to Keriat Shema.
