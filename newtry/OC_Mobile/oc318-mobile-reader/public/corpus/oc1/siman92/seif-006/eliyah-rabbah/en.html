And if he fears, etc. In Nachalas Tzvi he wondered about the Levush and Shulchan Aruch, for the Rif and Rambam, who rule that one must go back four mil, hold that even if there is no time and the time for prayer will pass, he must go back after water; the words of the Shulchan Aruch and Levush therefore contradict each other. I wrote in Eliyahu Zuta that the words of Lechem Chamudos there escaped him, for he resolves this well, namely, not to rule like them when there is no time. Afterward I saw in the Taz that he wondered that, on the contrary, according to the Rif there is a stringency that it is forbidden to pray until he has water. In my humble opinion, it is a greater stringency that he not let the time pass, since cleaning with something that cleans is possible; therefore the Shulchan Aruch relied in this matter on Tosafot and the Rosh. It also appears to me that the Shulchan Aruch holds that when only the beginning of the time for prayer will pass, he must return, but when he fears that the entire time for prayer will pass, even the Rif and Rambam agree that he should clean his hands with a pebble. Here the Shulchan Aruch is speaking where the entire time for prayer will pass. So too is implied in the Bach below, siman 233, regarding the opinion of Rav Amram, although it does not seem so from the Beit Yosef. Now the Bach disagrees with the Shulchan Aruch and wrote that since one need not go back after water for Krias Shema according to everyone, one should not go back for Shacharis prayer either, since one must join redemption to prayer. It appears that he follows his own reasoning below in siman 233, where he wrote that the primary view follows Rashi, that one need not go back for prayer, only that stringently we are concerned for the Rif and Rambam. Whenever something is not a mitzvah by law, one may not interrupt at Shirah Chadashah, for only for the need of a mitzvah, such as answering Amen Yehei Shemei Rabbah and Kedushah, may one interrupt, as above in siman 66. Consider this carefully. However, according to the Beis Yaakov, that the primary view follows the Rif and Rambam, perhaps one may interrupt at Shirah Chadashah. It is also possible that he holds that even according to the Rif and Rambam one may not interrupt there, because of joining redemption to prayer.
