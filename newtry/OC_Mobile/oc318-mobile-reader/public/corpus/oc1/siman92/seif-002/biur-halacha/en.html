If in the middle of his prayer an urge arose, etc. — See in Chiddushei R. Akiva Eiger, who remained with a difficulty on the proof of Rashba that it is not proof, and the pivot of his difficulty goes according to what is decided for him, that if he prayed facing dripping urine after the fact his prayer is prayer. However, according to what we brought above in siman 79, many views disagree on this — it works out simply. However, if it is difficult for me regarding the Shulchan Aruch, it is difficult for me why they brought Rashba for the halachah, for behold the main proof is from what is decided for him that he may not move from his place in the middle of his prayer even if dripping urine happened upon him in the middle of his prayer, as is brought in Magen Avraham and Biur HaGra; if so, according to what the Shulchan Aruch ruled in siman 90, seif 27, in this — not like Rashba, and as the Acharonim wrote that this ruling is not like Rashba, and Beit Yosef retracted from what he agreed with him in siman 78 — if so, Rashba's proof for this ruling goes away automatically; and why did the Shulchan Aruch cite him in this siman for the halachah? And perhaps Beit Yosef finds Rashba's reason persuasive, that since he already began in a permitted state without checking:

He shall restrain himself until he finishes — See Mishna Berurah, that from the law it is permitted only Kriat Shema and its blessings and no more. And it is simple that this is like what we learned in Megillah 23: one may not spread [the hands] over Shema, pass before the ark, lift the hands [for Birkat Kohanim], read from the Torah, or read haftarah from the Prophets with fewer than ten; and see in the Ran, who wrote in the name of the Yerushalmi that this comes to inform us that each of these is a separate matter on its own; therefore if one of them began with ten and some of them left — for we hold that they finish — he is permitted only to finish that matter and not to begin another matter. From this one may deduce also in our case, for Rashba holds that since he already began that matter properly, having checked himself at the outset, he is again permitted to finish the matter, for in any case it is forbidden for him to begin another matter from all those mentioned in the Mishnah; and all such cases. And thereby it is simple that in Kriat Shema, which Rashba permitted to read in his usual manner since he began in a permitted state — it is only until Shemoneh Esreh, and not Shemoneh Esreh itself, which is a separate matter; and likewise in the Mishnah:

He reads in his usual manner — It is simple that the same applies if he began to hear the Torah reading, and before that he did not need to relieve himself, and in the middle a slight urge arose in a manner that does not involve bal teshaktzu: he may hear the entire Torah reading and respond "Blessed is the the Omnipresent, the blessed One, forever and ever" [until the haftarah, which is called a separate matter, as above in siman 55 in Mishna Berurah], since he began in a permitted state. And the same applies in the middle of Hallel, Megillah, and Birkat Hamazon — and every such case: he may finish the matter in his usual manner, since he does not feel such an urge that there would be concern for bal teshaktzu. And it seems that the same applies to words of Torah: if he is learning for himself and before the study he did not need to relieve himself, and in the middle of the subject matter a slight urge arose — even though he estimates for himself that he cannot restrain himself until the measure of a parsa — nevertheless he may finish the subject matter. And one should not reject [this] by saying that there, Kriat Shema with its blessings is all one matter and one may say he may finish the matter since they already began; and likewise for Torah reading and all the above — not so for Torah study, where "finish" does not apply, and one cannot say that here it is preferable because of having begun, for we do find in words of Torah too that there is advantage because he already began — see Shabbos 11a in the Gemara and Magen Avraham siman 106, seif kaf-vav. In any case it seems to me that if by interrupting now he will cause afterward bitul Torah, he should certainly finish the subject matter; and especially if it is only a slight arousal and he can restrain himself for a parsa — he need not be strict about this:

But if not so — See Mishna Berurah in what he wrote in the name of Magen Avraham regarding prayer. And regarding Kriat Shema too Magen Avraham agrees that if he feels such an urge that there is concern for bal teshaktzu, he is obligated to interrupt and check himself, since he can interrupt in the middle [Peri Megadim]; and likewise for all the aforementioned matters:

It is better to interrupt — And if he interrupted and relieved himself and waited thereby long enough to finish the entire prayer, according to all opinions he returns to the beginning; and likewise for Kriat Shema according to what Rama ruled above in siman 65. But when he did not wait [long enough], it is not clear to me well, for seemingly he should afterward return to the beginning, for even one who holds (Berachos 23a) regarding water dripping onto his knees that he returns to where he stopped — the reason is because the man is fit; and the Rosh and Rashba explained in a responsum that even at the time of dripping he is fit by Torah law — thus implying that if not so, since one hour was deferred he does not return again. And it seems that in our case, regarding relieving oneself, which is then forbidden by Torah law, thereby according to all opinions he is deferred; and it is not comparable to one walking in filthy alleyways, who requires waiting to finish entirely, as stated in siman 85, for it is different there, where the man is fit but the place is not fit, as the Rosh wrote there. However, in truth one may reject that the view "the man is deferred" does not help except combined with [the fact] that he should not have stood to pray from the outset since he needed to relieve himself — and thus it is proven in the Rosh there. Not so in our case, which deals with one who checked himself at the outset [but all this may be rejected for the Rosh, not for Rashba, for according to him that baraita of water dripping also applies where he did not need to relieve himself from the outset but only in the middle this matter arose for him, and nevertheless the Gemara says only because the man is fit by Torah law]. And one may say further according to what seems from the words of R. Yehuda Ria that if we suppose he waited in order to finish entirely, he does not return to the beginning — there is no Amora who would hold that when he did not wait to finish entirely he returns to the beginning because of coercion; and similarly it seems from the words of the Rosh; if so, the same applies in our case according to what we rule in halachah. One may also say that all this we need to force ourselves according to their explanation; however according to Rashi's explanation, who explained "the man is fit" — that is, from the outset when he stood to pray — if so, perhaps Rashi would hold that even if in the middle he interrupted for some matter forbidden by Torah law to say sacred words, nevertheless he is called "the man is fit"; and similarly it seems from the words of Chayei Adam, who wrote that he should not interrupt with speech while relieving himself in the middle — implying that he holds he does not return to the beginning. And it seems this is derived from the words of the Poskim, who wrote everywhere anonymously that if he waited to finish entirely because of coercion — whether the man was not fit or the place was not fit — he returns to the beginning; and they did not write specifically if the man was not fit in a rabbinic matter like that case of water dripping onto his knees — implying that he holds even in a Torah matter he does not return to the beginning:
