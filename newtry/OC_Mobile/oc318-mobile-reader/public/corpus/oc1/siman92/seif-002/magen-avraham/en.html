In the middle. The Rashba writes as follows: Since he had no need at all to relieve himself when he began praying, and he had checked himself beforehand, if the urge arose in the middle he should not interrupt, even if urine is running down his knees, as stated in the Gemara. They did not require him to move away, and certainly not when he can restrain himself until he finishes. The same applies if he needs to relieve himself of feces. So long as he can restrain himself until he finishes, he does not interrupt. If, because of extreme pressure, an involuntary occurrence happened in the middle and he passed gas, he waits, and so forth. This is the end of the Rashba's words in responsum 131. The Darchei Moshe writes: This requires study in light of the Terumas HaDeshen that I cited in siman 103, which implies that one may not restrain himself, whether for feces or urine, and must interrupt. Perhaps here the case is one in which the urge is not so strong as to involve the prohibition of making oneself repulsive, but only strong enough that he cannot restrain himself for the distance of a parsah. This is the end of the Darchei Moshe's words. This requires study, for the Rashba explicitly writes that it is enough that he can restrain himself until he finishes, and there is no shorter period than that. He also writes that because of extreme pressure he passed gas. In my view, one may rely on the Rashba. In public, even the Terumas HaDeshen agrees that it is permitted, as stated in siman 103, because human dignity is so great that it overrides the prohibition of 'do not deviate.' Even when praying alone one may rely on the Rashba, for the Semag writes that one may not interrupt his prayer except because of a concern for danger to life. All the more so may one not interrupt because of a Rabbinic prohibition, for the Sages who imposed the prohibition also determined its limits. It is also possible that the Terumas HaDeshen prohibited interruption only in the case of passing gas, where the Sages permitted him to move away four cubits, as stated in siman 103, because that is not considered an interruption in prayer, as stated in siman 104. But to relieve oneself he agrees with the Rashba.
