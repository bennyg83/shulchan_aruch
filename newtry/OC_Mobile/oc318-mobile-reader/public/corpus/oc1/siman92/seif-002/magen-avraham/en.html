In the middle. Thus wrote the Rashba: since he did not need to relieve himself at all when he began to pray, and his need was only approaching beforehand, and in the middle he was awakened to it — he does not interrupt; and even if urine is dripping, etc., as is stated in the Gemara. And they did not say that one must distance himself — all the more so when he can restrain himself until he finishes. The same applies when he needs to defecate: as long as he can restrain himself until he finishes, he does not interrupt. And if an incident occurred to him in the middle from great pressure and he sneezed, he waits, etc. — end of siman 131. And Darkei Moshe wrote, and there is a difficulty with Terumat HaDeshen, for I wrote in siman 103 that it appears forbidden to restrain oneself — whether for defecation or urination — and one must interrupt. And it is possible that here we are dealing with one who does not feel the urge so strongly that there would be bal teshaktzu, but rather he feels that he cannot restrain himself for a parasang, etc., end of Darkei Moshe. And there is a difficulty: behold, in the Rashba it is written that when he can restrain himself until he finishes, and you have no shorter time than this. And furthermore he wrote that from great pressure he sneezed — and there is a difficulty. And it seems to me that one may rely on the Rashba; and in a congregation even Terumat HaDeshen agrees it is permitted, as he wrote in siman 103, for the honor of people overrides a negative commandment of you shall not turn aside. And even when praying alone one may rely on the Rashba that it is permitted, for Semag wrote that it is forbidden to interrupt one's prayer except on account of concern for danger to life — how much more so on account of a rabbinic prohibition, for they said and they said. And it is possible that even Terumat HaDeshen forbade only sneezing, which the Sages permitted, etc., as written in siman 103, that one may distance four cubits, for this is not called an interruption in prayer, as written in siman 104; but regarding attending to one's needs he agrees with the Rashba.
