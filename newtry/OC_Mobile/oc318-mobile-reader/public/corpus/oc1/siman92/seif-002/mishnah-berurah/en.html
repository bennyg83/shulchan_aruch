(8) In the middle — that is, that before prayer he checked himself or had no need for his orifices at all; therefore even though afterward he was aroused, even for adults he is not permitted to interrupt in the middle. And it is obvious that after he finished Shemoneh Esreh it is forbidden for him to restrain himself further and to say Kedushah, for that is a different matter — as stated above in siman 55, se'if gimel in the gloss; see there. And if he can restrain himself up to a parsa, see above in se'if kaf-zayin.

(9) At the time of Kerias Shema — that is, although in Kerias Shema and its blessings he may go from this place to another to relieve himself there or to urinate, as concluded at the end of the section — nevertheless, if he wishes he may recite in his usual way and need not interrupt, since he checked himself beforehand. See in Biur Halachah where we explained that it is not permitted except until Shemoneh Esreh; but Shemoneh Esreh, which is a different matter, he is forbidden to say since he needs his orifices — therefore he should interrupt at least at the blessing "Emes veYatziv" and go out to his orifices so that afterward he may adjoin redemption to prayer; and it is better if he can go out between the sections. And likewise if desire was aroused in him before he began the blessings of Kerias Shema — it appears he is forbidden to say the blessings of Kerias Shema again before he checks himself. And if so, according to what is explained above at the end of siman 54 that Barchu belongs to the blessing of Kerias Shema — and after Barchu is considered as the middle of the chapter of Yotzer Or — if desire was aroused in him at the end of Pesukei deZimra, he is forbidden to say Barchu, for that is a different matter — unless he can restrain himself up to a parsa; one may rely to be lenient, as above in se'if kaf-zayin.

(10) Who is not desirous — that is, he is not pressed to expel from his orifices but only from a general arousal [Eliyah Rabbah in siman 3].

(11) But if not — that is, even though he can restrain himself until he finishes — nevertheless it is better that he interrupt, both for adults and minors, because of the prohibition of "you shall not make yourselves detestable" — and this refers to the beginning; also regarding prayer. See in Magen Avraham who elaborates on this law and concludes that with the congregation, even Terumat HaDeshen agrees to Rashba that one may not interrupt in the middle of prayer because of human dignity; and even when alone one may rely on Rashba not to interrupt, and so too in Chayei Adam that it is forbidden to interrupt. And in Derech HaChayim he wrote that if there is in it [violation of] "you shall not make yourselves detestable" he may interrupt; in any case, even according to him, he has no obligation to interrupt since he can restrain himself until he finishes prayer. And if he cannot stand until he finishes Shemoneh Esreh, he is permitted to go out and relieve himself in the middle, only that he should not interrupt with speech [Chayei Adam]. And it is implied from this that afterward he need not return to the beginning, since before prayer he did not need his orifices — provided he does not delay through going out enough to finish the entire prayer.

(12) And if he wished — this refers to Kerias Shema stated above, from which this derives — that it depends on his will; but in prayer he is not permitted to move from his place until he finishes [Peri Megadim].
