In the middle. Meaning, before prayer he checked himself, or did not need to relieve himself at all; therefore, even if afterward the need arose, even for defecation, he may not interrupt in the middle. It is obvious that after he finishes the Shemoneh Esrei, it is forbidden for him to continue restraining himself in order to say Kedushah, for that is a different matter, similar to what is stated above in siman 55, seif 3 in the gloss, see there. If he can restrain himself for a parsah, see above, seif katan 7.
At the time of Krias Shema. Meaning, although during Krias Shema and its blessings he can go from this place to another place to relieve himself or urinate, as the seif concludes, nevertheless if he wishes he may read in his usual manner and need not interrupt, since beforehand he checked himself. See Biur Halacha, where we explained that this is permitted only until the Shemoneh Esrei. But Shemoneh Esrei, which is a different matter, he may not say, since he needs to relieve himself.
That he has no urge. Meaning, he is not pressed for elimination, but only has a mere stirring (Eliyah Rabbah in siman 3).
But otherwise. Meaning, even though he can restrain himself until he finishes, nevertheless it is better that he interrupt, whether for defecation or urination, because of the prohibition of “do not make yourselves repulsive.” This refers to the first clause and also to prayer. See the Magen Avraham, who discusses this law at length and concludes that in public even the Terumas HaDeshen agrees to the Rashba that one should not interrupt in the middle of prayer because of human dignity. Even privately, one may also rely on the Rashba not to interrupt. So too the Chayei Adam wrote that one should not interrupt.
And if he wishes. This refers to the Krias Shema mentioned immediately before it, which depends on his will. But during prayer he may not move from his place until he finishes (Peri Megadim).
