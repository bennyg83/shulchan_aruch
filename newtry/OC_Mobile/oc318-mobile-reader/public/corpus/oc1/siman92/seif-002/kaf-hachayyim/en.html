8) [Se'if 2] Gloss: Specifically when the urge is not so strong, etc. Meaning that he has an urge and cannot restrain himself for a parsah. Darkei Moshe 1. Magen Avraham 2 challenges Rema: Rashba 131 says it suffices if he can restrain himself until finishing, the shortest possible period. Magen Avraham permits reliance on Rashba, especially in public, where Terumas HaDeshen also permits it, as in siman 150, since human dignity overrides the rabbinic prohibition of 'do not deviate.' Even privately one may rely on Rashba: Semag says one may not interrupt prayer except for possible danger to life; all the more so not for a rabbinic prohibition, which the Sages themselves defined. Eliyah Rabbah 4 likewise says bal teshaktzu is rabbinic and does not require interrupting prayer, which itself is forbidden. The later authorities therefore rule that if the need arose during prayer, whether for defecation or urination, he should not interrupt if he can restrain himself until completing Shemoneh Esrei. Chayei Adam 25:2; Shulchan Aruch HaRav 2; Beis Oved 37; Shesilei Zeitim 4; Ben Ish Chai, Mishpatim 3.
8) If he cannot restrain himself, he should interrupt for defecation but not for urination. Even if urine is flowing onto his knees, he waits until it stops and then resumes prayer, without needing to distance himself, as stated in siman 78. Shulchan Aruch HaRav there. If he has much urine, so that it will reach the ground, and people are present before whom he is embarrassed, it appears to me that he should go elsewhere to urinate because of human dignity, then return and complete his prayer. He should not speak, and should recite Asher Yatzar only after completing the prayer.
9) There: If he wishes to move away and urinate, he may. For defecation, however, he must restrain himself if able and should not interrupt even Krias Shema and its blessings, or Pesukei D'Zimrah from Baruch She'amar onward, provided the urge is not so strong that restraining himself violates bal teshaktzu, even though he cannot last a parsah. If bal teshaktzu applies, he interrupts for either urination or defecation during Krias Shema and its blessings. Shulchan Aruch HaRav there. Mishbetzos Zahav at the beginning of the siman likewise writes that Magen Avraham certainly agrees that one interrupts Krias Shema for an urge involving bal teshaktzu.
