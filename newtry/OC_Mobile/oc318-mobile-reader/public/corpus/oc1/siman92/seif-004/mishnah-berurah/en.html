(13) To wash — the reason for this see above at the beginning of siman 4 in Mishna Berurah. And also for Minchah and Maariv prayer netilas yadayim is required, as below in siman 233, se'if 2. And the Rambam's opinion is that where one needs to wash his hands and did not wash, and also did not clean them with something that cleans — even bedi'eaved his prayer is not prayer, and the law is unresolved [Peri Megadim]. And see in Biur Halachah where we explained that he does not return and pray again.

(14) In water — and drying is not indispensable here as it is indispensable for netilas yadayim for eating [Bach].

(15) It is required — and specifically when he knows they are dirty; but with unstated hands one may be lenient — rely on siman 65; see there.

(16) To return, etc. — and if he will need thereby to go afterward alone, or will not be able thereby to reach his desired place still within the day — he need not return at all.

(17) One who walks on the road — and one riding, his law in all this is like one walking on foot. Also there is no distinction between a bad road and a good one. All this is specifically when he knows with certainty that he will find water there; but in doubt, no.

(18) Behind him — and all the more so when he sits in his house, he is certainly obligated to return ab initio for water if he knows his hands are dirty.

(19) Up to a mil — and many poskim hold that a mil itself, its law is like more than a mil.

(20) The time for prayer — entirely. And if he is in a place where there are ten who pray, and if he returns for water the time of the congregation's prayer will pass — Bach wrote that even though he has leeway, he need not return, and should clean with something that cleans.

(21) He should clean his hands — that is, he should not return then for water lest the time pass; and all the more so regarding Kerias Shema — it is certainly forbidden for him to wait for water if there is concern lest the time pass, but rather he should clean and recite. And there are many poskim who hold that for Kerias Shema, immediately when the beginning of its time arrives, if he has no water he cleans and recites and does not wait for water.

(22) Siman 233 — where the time of Minchah is explained.
