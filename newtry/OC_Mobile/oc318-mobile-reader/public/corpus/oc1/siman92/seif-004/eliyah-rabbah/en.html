It is better, etc. This implies that if he wishes he need not interrupt. At first glance, he is obligated to interrupt so that he not violate Bal Teshaktzu. Perhaps because Bal Teshaktzu is only a D'Rabbanan prohibition, we do not obligate him to interrupt prayer, for that too is a prohibition. Similarly, Terumas HaDeshen, siman 16, wrote that where there is disgrace and degradation, we are not concerned because of Bal Teshaktzu. It appears more correct that he wrote, 'it is better to interrupt,' in order to teach that if he transgressed and prayed, his prayer is valid, since the need arose in the middle of prayer; but initially he is obligated to interrupt. However, I examined the responsum of the Rashba itself, siman 131, and it is explicit there that even if his urge is so strong that Bal Teshaktzu applies, he should restrain himself. This is astonishing regarding the Rama, who explained the Rashba as speaking where he does not have such a strong urge. Therefore it appears to me that the first approach is primary. Possibly because the Rama was uncertain in his interpretation of the Rashba, he wrote the language, 'it is better, etc.' Afterward the Magen Avraham was printed, and I found that he disagreed with the Rama, maintaining that it is always forbidden to interrupt based on the above responsum of the Rashba. But what he wrote, that Terumas HaDeshen also holds this way, does not appear so from Terumas HaDeshen, siman 16, and this requires study.
