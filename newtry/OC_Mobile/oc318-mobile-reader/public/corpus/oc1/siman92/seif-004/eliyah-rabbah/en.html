[4] Better to interrupt, etc. — It implies that if he wants he does not interrupt; and ostensibly he is obligated to interrupt so as not to transgress bal teshaktzu. Perhaps because bal teshaktzu is only a rabbinic prohibition, we do not require him to interrupt prayer — for that too is a prohibition. And in this vein, Terumat HaDeshen wrote in siman 16 that here, where there is disgrace and contempt, we are not concerned for bal teshaktzu. And it is more plausible that for this reason he wrote "better to interrupt" — to teach that if he transgressed and prayed, his prayer is prayer, since he was aroused in the middle of prayer; but ab initio he is obligated to interrupt. But I examined the body of the responsum of Rashba siman 131, and it seems there explicitly that even if he desires so much that there is a concern of bal teshaktzu, he restrains himself — and this is puzzling regarding Rama, who explained Rashba's words as "he does not desire so much." Therefore it seems to me that the first [view] is primary, and perhaps because Rama softened his explanation of Rashba, he wrote the wording "better," etc. Again, the book of Magen Avraham was printed and I found that he disagreed with Rama — that it is always forbidden to interrupt from the responsum of Rashba mentioned above — except that what he wrote, that Terumat HaDeshen also holds thus, does not seem so in Terumat HaDeshen siman 16, and it requires study:
