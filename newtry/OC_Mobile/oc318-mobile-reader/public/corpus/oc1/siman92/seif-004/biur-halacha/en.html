He must wash [his hands], etc. — See in Mishna Berurah what he wrote in the name of Rambam regarding after the fact. And regarding stam yadayim (hands without knowing if they are dirty) it is simple that one need not repeat prayer because of this. And although Rambam's view seems to imply that the impediment applies even to stam yadayim, as Peri Megadim wrote — nevertheless we have no practical difference in this, for Beit Yosef already wrote that therefore we do not recite a blessing on stam yadayim, because of the view of the Ran and the first view brought in the Tur, who hold that stam yadayim are fit for prayer, and doubtful blessings we rule leniently; if so, all the more so regarding repeating prayer. And apart from this, it is difficult to reconcile well Rambam's final view here, for in all four [other] matters alone he explained explicitly in Rambam that even after the fact one returns and prays; in this he explained nothing — implying that the impediment he mentions is that his prayer was not proper because his hands were soiled, but he is not obligated because of this to return and pray. Therefore it seems to me that even according to Rambam, if his hands were soiled from beads of sweat by scratching his head or touching a filthy place — although lechatchila it is forbidden to read and pray and even to bless, as above at the end of siman 4 — nevertheless after the fact he does not return. And I found some support for our words from Rambam, Hilchot Kriat Shema chapter 3, halachah 11: "If he was traveling, etc., or his hands were soiled from ritual impurity," etc., see there; and this was brought above in Magen Avraham siman 76, seif kaf-gimel. And behold, Kriat Shema and prayer share one law, that it is forbidden with soiled hands, as implied in Rambam, Hilchot Berachot chapter 6, halachah 2, and in Hilchot Kriat Shema chapter 3 he certainly deals with [the case] without netilah, and nevertheless Kriat Shema is not voided because of this. And although lechatchila it is certainly forbidden to read because of lack of netilat yadayim, Rambam did not descend to this here because he deals here only with the law of Kriat Shema, and we have no practical difference regarding after the fact. Not so if we would say that even after the fact it impedes — that law would not apply at all. And although Rambam concludes there "and several Geonim ruled that it is forbidden," etc., "and so one should act" — this too is only lechatchila, as is proven from several places there, s.v. "and several Geonim," etc. And I saw in Peri Megadim above, siman 76, seif kaf-gimel, that he wanted to innovate that specifically opposite [the soiling] one may read but not on himself; if so, according to this perhaps even after the fact he returns and reads because of lack of netilah — but it does not seem so in Rambam, who is the source of this law; see there, as it seems to me:

But if he must go back behind him, etc. — And one sitting in his house: it is simple that his law is like regarding netilat yadayim, for Chayei Adam ruled that his law is like [water] behind him, and it suffices up to a mil; see there, for one statement in the Gemara was stated on this matter in both directions, and several laws we derive from netilat yadayim for our matter — see in Dvrei Chamudos in the chapter "One who was reading." And it is likewise proven in Pri Megadim that only according to Tosafos need he not go back for water in his home if he does not have [water ahead]; but per the Rif and Rambam, whom the Shulchan Aruch ruled like, even at home he is obligated to go back for water, and his law is like [water] behind him. And the words of Peri Megadim in Mishnat Zekeinim, letter bet, who wrote in one manner that the Mechaber, who is lenient in siman 233 seif 2, is because [he is] in his home — and as Pri Megadim [wrote] — is not clear, for if so he should not have troubled to go back behind him up to a mil when walking on the road, for one depends on the other, as is explained in Pri Megadim. Also in letter gimel in Mishnat Zekeinim it seems he retracted from what he wrote, that if in the middle of Kriat Shema or psukei dezimra he touched a filthy place, he must go after water available in the city to wash his hands; and it is simple that this implies that even if water is not available before him, since his hands are soiled and he knows there is water in the city, he must go after it. But on the contrary it is difficult for me: if so he would need to go back for water up to a mil — Pri Megadim did not write his leniency of "in his home" only according to Tosafos, not according to what the Mechaber ruled like the Rif and Rambam, for whom he should have gone back up to a mil. And perhaps Peri Megadim made a middle path between these views and ruled that for water available in the city he must go back.
