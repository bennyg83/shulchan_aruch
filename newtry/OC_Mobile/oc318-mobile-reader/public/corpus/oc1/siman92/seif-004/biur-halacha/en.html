He must wash, etc. See Mishnah Berurah regarding what I wrote in the name of Rambam concerning the law after the fact. With hands of unspecified status, it is clear that he need not repeat the prayer. Although Rambam appears to hold that the requirement is indispensable even for hands of unspecified status, as Peri Megadim writes, this makes no practical difference for us. Beit Yosef already explained that we do not recite a blessing when washing hands of unspecified status because Ran and the alternative opinion cited by Tur maintain that such hands are fit for prayer, and in cases of doubt concerning blessings we are lenient. Certainly, then, one should not repeat the prayer. Apart from this, it is difficult to determine Rambam's precise intent. Regarding each of the other four prerequisites, Rambam explicitly explains that even after the fact one must repeat the prayer, whereas here he says nothing. This indicates that when he calls it indispensable, he means that the prayer was not conducted properly because the hands were soiled, but not that one must repeat it. It therefore appears to me that even according to Rambam, if one's hands were soiled with traces of perspiration because he scratched his head or touched a covered part of the body, although initially he may not recite Krias Shema, pray, or even recite a blessing, as explained above at the end of siman 4, after the fact he does not repeat. I found some support for this in Rambam, chapter 3 of Hilchos Krias Shema, halachah 11: “There was excrement, etc., or his hands were soiled from a privy, etc.” See there. This ruling is cited above by Magen Avraham in siman 76, seif katan 3. Krias Shema and prayer have the same law regarding soiled hands, as appears from Rambam, chapter 6 of Hilchos Berachos, halachah 2, and chapter 3 of Hilchos Krias Shema. That passage certainly concerns a case without washing, yet Krias Shema is not invalidated. Although initially one may certainly not read because he has not washed his hands, Rambam does not address that point there, because he is discussing only the law of Krias Shema and its after-the-fact validity. If the lack of washing invalidated the reading even after the fact, the ruling would have no application. Rambam concludes there that several Geonim ruled that it is forbidden and that this is the proper practice, but this too refers only to the initial requirement, as Kesef Mishneh proves there. I saw that Peri Megadim above, siman 76, seif katan 3, wished to innovate that only another person facing him may read, but the person himself may not. According to that, it is possible that even after the fact he must repeat because he lacked washing. This does not appear from Rambam, who is the source of the ruling. See there. This is what appears correct to me.
But if he must go back, etc. If one is sitting at home, it is clear that his law is the same as with washing the hands, where Chayei Adam rules that it is treated like water behind him and he need seek it only up to one mil. See there. The Gemara states one teaching concerning both matters, and many laws for our subject are learned from washing the hands; see Divrei Chamudos in the chapter Hayah Korei. This is also evident from Perishah. Only according to Tosafos need one not seek water at home if he has none. According to Rif and Rambam, whose view the Shulchan Aruch follows, one at home must also seek water, with the same limit as water behind him. The words of Peri Megadim in Mishbetzos Zahav, letter 2, suggesting in one approach that the Mechaber's leniency in siman 233, seif 2, is because the person is at home, following Perishah, are not convincing. If so, one traveling would likewise not have to turn back up to one mil, for the two laws are interdependent, as Perishah explains. In letter 3 of Mishbetzos Zahav, Peri Megadim also appears to retract this, writing that if one touches a covered part of the body in the middle of Krias Shema or Pesukei DeZimrah, he must go to water available in the city and wash his hands. This is presented as obvious. It implies that even if water is not immediately before him, since his hands are soiled and he knows that water exists in the city, he must seek it. Yet the opposite question then arises: should he not have to go back for water up to one mil? Perishah's leniency for one at home was written only according to Tosafos, whereas according to the Mechaber's ruling like Rif and Rambam, he should have to go back up to one mil. Perhaps Peri Megadim adopted a compromise between these opinions, ruling that one must seek water available in the city. The common practice is not to follow Peri Megadim's stringency by interrupting Pesukei DeZimrah to seek water available somewhere in the city, since in most cases this would cause him to lose prayer with the congregation. Bach and Eliyah Rabbah have already been lenient in this matter, as I wrote in Mishnah Berurah, seif katan 20.
