5) There: This applies when he cannot restrain himself for the time needed to walk a parsah, etc. I found it written that the Sages set a parsah because the first chapter of Berachos says the early pious men would remain in prayer for one hour, approximately the time an average person walks a parsah; ten parsaos in a twelve-hour day. Rabbeinu Yonah proves that it means an actual hour, one of the day's twelve, since the Gemara asks: there are three prayers daily, totaling nine hours, etc. Bach; Knesses HaGedolah, Hagahos Tur 1. Olas Tamid 3. Prishah 2 notes that walking a parsah takes more than an hour on that calculation, but a person does not walk continuously all day; additionally, the brief pauses before and after prayer, described at the beginning of the next siman, combine with prayer to equal walking a parsah. Ateres Zekenim 1 and Shesilei Zeitim 3 adopt his second answer. Gan HaMelech 48 explains that the walking measure is stringent: while sitting, one can restrain himself longer than while walking. Erekh HaShulchan 2 and Ben Ish Chai, Mishpatim 2, cite this. See Beis Oved 35.
