[5] There: and these words [apply] when he cannot restrain himself for the measure of walking a parasang, etc. — As we wrote that the Sages gave the measure of a parasang per the statement "we do not stand" — that they are equal in prayer [for the duration of] one hour, namely the measure of walking a parasang approximately by a person's pace between the intermediate ten parasangs per day, which is twelve hours. And Rabbi Yosei Lechem proved that he means an actual hour — one of the twelve hours of the day — from that which is said in the Gemara: "Since three prayers per day, behold nine hours," etc. Bach. As cited in Knesset HaGedolah in Hagahot Tosephta, letter 1; Avodat HaTumah, note 3. And Perishah wrote, note 2, that although one must walk a parasang more than an hour by the calculation of twelve hours per day [for] a person's pace between ten parasangs — nevertheless he is not walking the entire day; and furthermore we require some waiting beforehand and waiting a bit afterward, as written at the beginning of the next siman; and with all this, it is the measure of walking a parasang — up to here. And similarly Eshel Avraham, note 1, per the second reason that Perishah wrote; Shiyurei Torah, note 3. And that which they specified the measure of walking — Rabbi Gan HaMelech wrote siman 48 — in order to be strict regarding one who needs to relieve himself: when he sits in his place he can restrain himself somewhat, but when he walks he cannot restrain himself as much as when sitting; see there.
