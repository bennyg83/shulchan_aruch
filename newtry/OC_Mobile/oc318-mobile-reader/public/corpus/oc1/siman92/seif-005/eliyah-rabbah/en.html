More than a mil, etc. This implies that for exactly a mil he must return. Lechem Chamudos and Nachalas Tzvi, page 14, wondered at him, for he abandoned the text of the Rif and the Rosh, according to which for exactly a mil he does not return, and ruled based on what appears from Rambam, chapter 4 of Hilchos Tefillah. Furthermore, even Rambam himself may be explained like the Rif, as the Kesef Mishneh himself wrote. If so, there is no one who ruled that for exactly a mil he must return. Therefore they ruled halachically against the Shulchan Aruch; see there. But I found in Kol Bo, page 7, and in Seidah LaDerech, page 43, that they ruled that for exactly a mil he must return. So too is implied in Sefer Chasidim, siman 18, and this is primary. So too the Lechem Mishneh explained Rambam's words, though his words require great study, for he reads there like the Rif's text. Know that the Kol Bo is speaking where he can still arrive at his desired destination, etc., as above in siman 90, seif 17. Beis Yaakov responsa, siman 35, wrote that a rider's law in this respect is like one walking on foot, and there is no distinction between a bad road and a good one. See siman 249, seif katan 3.
