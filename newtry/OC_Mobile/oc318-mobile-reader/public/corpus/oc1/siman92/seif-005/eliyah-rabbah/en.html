And if water is not available, etc. This implies that even if there is dirt on his hands, if water is not available he cleans them, etc. So too appears to be the view of Lechem Chamudos, page 14, that even where there is dirt he need not go back four mil, since he already washed in the morning and we rely on the poskim who hold that one need not go back four mil. But the Olas Tamid did not see his words, and understood the Shulchan Aruch as speaking specifically where there is no dirt. He also challenged the Shulchan Aruch, saying that we did not find any posek who made such a distinction. In truth, as a matter of practical halacha, one should not be lenient where there is dirt, for the Shulchan Aruch implies that dirt is more severe than mere distraction, in accordance with the view cited as 'some say' in the Tur. If so, only where there was distraction did the Shulchan Aruch rely on the poskim who say that one need not go back four mil. Now in Magen Avraham, siman 103, it is implied that even with mere distraction he must go back when there is time, and he is not intending otherwise.
