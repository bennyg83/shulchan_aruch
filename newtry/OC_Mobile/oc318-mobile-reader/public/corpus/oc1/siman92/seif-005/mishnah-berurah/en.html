(23) And he diverted his attention — that is, he prolonged greatly between netilas yadayim and prayer until his attention was diverted; and all the more so diversion of attention applies between morning netilas yadayim and Minchah prayer.

(24) They require netilas yadayim — for since his attention was diverted from guarding them, the hands are busy, and perhaps they touched a filthy place.

(25) For prayer — but for Torah, blessings, and Kerias Shema, unstated hands are fit; but in siman 4 Peri Megadim wrote that for Kerias Shema unstated hands are invalid.

(26) He need not [return], etc. — nevertheless he must clean them with something that cleans, lest a little feces or sweat adhere through touching covered places, and it will be wiped through cleaning. Specifically with unstated hands the author is lenient; but if he touched a filthy place, and all the more so when he relieved himself — even for Minchah prayer he must return for water.
