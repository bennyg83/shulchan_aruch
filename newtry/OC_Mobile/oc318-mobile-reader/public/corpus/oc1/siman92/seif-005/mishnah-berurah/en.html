And he diverted his attention. Meaning, he waited a long time between the washing and the prayer, until he diverted his attention. All the more so, diversion of attention applies between the morning washing and the Minchah prayer.
Require washing. Since he diverted his attention from guarding them, hands are active, and perhaps they touched a filthy place.
For prayer. But for Torah, blessings, and Krias Shema, ordinary hands are valid. However, in siman 4 the Peri Megadim wrote that for Krias Shema ordinary hands are invalid.
He need not, etc. Nevertheless he must clean them with something that cleans, for perhaps a little excrement or sweat is stuck to them because he touched covered places, and it will be removed through the cleaning. The Mechaber is lenient specifically regarding ordinary hands, but if he touched a filthy place, and certainly if he relieved himself, even for Minchah he must go back after water.
