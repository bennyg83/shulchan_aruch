And if he diverted his attention — And if he soiled his hands with mud: according to Avi HaEzri, brought in the Tur, it seems he must wash his hands with water if he has [water]. But per the Rosh, whom we rule like, that even if he touched a filthy place, cleaning suffices if he has no water — perhaps the Gemara does not deal with soiling with mud, for there cleaning always suffices. And Peri Megadim above in siman 4 is strict when he has water; and it seems to me one should examine the view of the Rosh:

And if he does not have water, etc. — See in Mishna Berurah, seif kaf-kaf-vav; and this flows from Magen Avraham and Eliyah Rabbah and Shulchan Aruch haRav. And according to this, the Mechaber's reason is because regarding stam yadayim he rules like the view of Tosafos, that even if time will not pass, cleaning suffices if he has no water; and this is also the Mechaber's reason in siman 233 seif 2, see there; and thereby the difficulty of Taz there is removed, as many Acharonim [wrote]. And from the words of the Gra in his Biur it seems that when he has no water, the Mechaber relies on the view of the Ran, that stam yadayim are fit for prayer; and according to this, even cleaning is not required; and this is also the Mechaber's reason in siman 233 seif 2 regarding Minchah prayer, see there in his Biur. And I did not wish to conceal this, because many Acharonim explain like Magen Avraham and Eliyah Rabbah. In any case, according to all opinions in the view of the Shulchan Aruch, when relieving oneself or touching a filthy place, cleaning does not suffice, even for Minchah, and even when he has no readily available water — rather he must go back for water, as the Shulchan Aruch ruled in seif 4, and not like the view of Dvrei Chamudos, who is lenient also with cleaning; and likewise Magen Giborim wrote that one should be strict in this:
