And he diverted his attention. If his hands became soiled with mud, according to the Avi HaEzri cited by Tur, it appears that he must wash them with water if water is available. According to the Rosh, however, who rules that even if one touched a covered part of the body, cleaning by other means suffices when no water is available, it is possible that the Gemara was not discussing hands actually soiled with mud, since in that case cleaning always suffices. Peri Megadim above in siman 4 is stringent when water is available. In my humble opinion, the matter requires study according to the Rosh.
If he has no water, etc. See Mishnah Berurah, seif katan 26. This ruling derives from Magen Avraham, Eliyah Rabbah, and other Acharonim. According to this explanation, the Mechaber's reason is that for hands of unspecified status he follows Tosafos, who maintain that even when the time for prayer will not pass, cleaning by other means suffices if he has no water. This is also the reason for the Mechaber's ruling in siman 233, seif 2. See there. This resolves the Taz's difficulty there, as many Acharonim write. Biur HaGra implies that when one has no water, the Mechaber relies upon Ran, who holds that hands of unspecified status are fit for prayer. According to that, even cleaning would not be required. This would also explain the Mechaber's ruling in siman 233, seif 2, regarding Minchah; see Biur HaGra there. I did not wish to state this categorically because many Acharonim explain the ruling like Magen Avraham and Eliyah Rabbah. In any event, all agree that according to the Shulchan Aruch, if one relieved himself or touched a covered part of the body, cleaning alone does not suffice, even for Minchah and even if water is not immediately available. Rather, he must seek water, in accordance with the Shulchan Aruch in seif 4, and unlike Divrei Chamudos, who is lenient even in this case with cleaning alone. Magen Gibborim likewise writes that it is proper to be stringent.
