[1] And that which we say, etc. After the fact, Beit Yosef and Bach write in the name of Rashba that one fulfills through hearing even with respect to the after-blessing; see there. Nowadays, when people are lax about this, Taz writes that one should recite the blessing aloud so that others may fulfill their obligation through him. Derishah writes that “bread” is not meant literally, but the same law applies to kinds of porridge made from the five species of grain, as Rosh writes above in siman 178 regarding foods that require a blessing in their place. This requires study as a matter of practical halachah, because Levush's wording, that zimmun applies only to bread because of its importance, does not imply this. Importance of this kind applies only to bread, as stated above in siman 178, seif 3. Moreover, some poskim above hold that only actual bread requires a blessing in its place. What Beit Yosef and Levush write simply, that wine is like fruit, also requires study, for I found that Semak rules that with respect to the after-blessing wine is like bread. I later saw that Responsa Raavan, siman 196, Rabbeinu Yonah, and Semak agree with Beit Yosef. Malbushei Yom Tov writes that the authors of Shulchan Aruch also omitted the law that if wine and fragrant oil were brought before a person, he blesses first over the wine, because we already stated in siman 177, seif 2, that nowadays the law of items brought after the meal does not apply. Yet above, in simanim 174 and 177, it is explained that Levush holds that this custom applies even nowadays, and therefore the Tur records the law. It is also difficult, for even outside a meal this law applies when both items arrive together. In my humble opinion, since Maadanei Melech, page 54, and Derishah explain the Tur's reason to be that wine requires a blessing of its own and is therefore more important, perhaps the matter is already included in what Shulchan Aruch and Levush write in siman 211, seif 3, that wine takes precedence even over olives. According to this, however, it is difficult why the Tur also records the law in siman 211. Their reason is also difficult, for it would imply that other beverages do not take precedence over a blessing on fragrance, which does not appear from Beit Yosef at the end of this siman. Olas Tamid, cited below in siman 216, seif katan 6, also indicates that every blessing over eating or drinking precedes a blessing over fragrance. I likewise found this explicit in Chiddushei Rashba, page 33, and Kol Bo, page 21, who explain that bodily benefit that enters the body is more significant than fragrance. Another reason may be that both body and soul benefit from eating and drinking, unlike fragrance. Perhaps Derishah and Maadanei Melech stated that wine is especially important only in comparison with oil, which serves both for fragrance and anointing, as stated by the Gemara at the end of the chapter Keitzad Mevarchin. For that reason, the special importance of wine is needed. This requires study.
[2] And some say that according to our practice, etc. Rama writes: “Some say that for all items other than bread and wine, reclining is ineffective, and the same applies nowadays to sitting. Therefore, the current custom with fruit is that each person blesses for himself.” This shows that even according to this opinion there is a distinction. It is the view of Raavad, cited by Beit Yosef in siman 174, who rules in accordance with the latter version of Rabbeinu Yonah; see there. Olas Tamid appears to have understood that Rama derived this from Rashba in this siman, and therefore asks that Rashba himself does not accept this reasoning. On the contrary, Rama should have ruled like Tosafos, Mordechai, and Semag, who hold that reclining is ineffective even for wine, and the same would apply nowadays to sitting. This is imprecise. Nevertheless, it still requires explanation why Rama did not rule like Tosafos, Mordechai, and Semak, especially since Beit Yosef writes above in siman 174 that Raavad is a lone opinion against all the poskim. It appears to me that Rama records Raavad because he saw that the custom with fruit is for each person to bless for himself, while with wine the custom is otherwise. He therefore supported the custom with Raavad's view, which explains his conclusion, “Therefore, the current custom,” etc. Malbushei Yom Tov asks why Levush did not rule like Rama, and Lechem Chamudos, page 53, writes that Rabbi Mordechai Yaffe erred here. In my humble opinion, Levush was correct. He found it difficult to base our custom on the lone view of Raavad. Rather, he holds like Tosafos, Mordechai, and Semag, that by law reclining is effective for all these items. I found this explicit in Semag, page 111, and Bach's statement in siman 174 that Semag agrees with Raavad that reclining is ineffective by law is therefore most surprising. As for the statement of Semag and those who agree with him that we are not accustomed to establish a fixed gathering over wine, Levush understands that this applied specifically in their times, as is also implied by Bach in siman 174. Nowadays, since people do establish a fixed gathering over wine, its law is like bread, and therefore the custom is that one person blesses for everyone.
Nowadays the practice regarding fruits, etc. Olat Tamid writes that the same should be practiced with other beverages, such as liquor and the like, so that one person does not discharge another with his blessing. The world does not practice this, and he calls their practice mistaken. According to what I explained above, however, by strict law one person's blessing can discharge others for all foods; the matter depends only on whether there is an established manner of joining together. Perhaps nowadays people do establish themselves together over other beverages as they do over wine. Moreover, it appears that after the fact, if one intends to discharge himself by listening, he fulfills his obligation even with fruits, as with the after-blessing discussed above. See above, siman 167 seif 13, where Taz and Magen Avraham write that nowadays beer and mead are like wine, with one person blessing for all. It is obvious that when fruits are brought during a meal, one may bless for all, because once reclining or joining is effective for bread, it is effective for the other foods as well.
