Even without reclining. The Gemara states that at large meals the custom was for the guests to gather and sit on benches while drinking until everyone had arrived. They would then go up and sit in the place of the meal. In the first place, each person would bless for himself, because since they intended to move, this was not considered a fixed gathering at all and was even inferior to ordinary sitting. Tosafos write: Why is incense different, where reclining is unnecessary? They answer that with incense everyone benefits simultaneously, whereas with eating they benefit one after another. This implies that for incense even sitting is unnecessary. The same is implied by siman 298, seif 14, for there they were not sitting in the sense of a fixed gathering for eating or a similar purpose, but were occupied with learning, and nevertheless one person blesses for everyone. Rabbeinu Yonah there also implies this.

They must separate. If the items came at the end of the meal, one person blesses the after-blessing for everyone, since their eating of bread created a zimmun for them. Beit Yosef in the name of Rashba. See siman 177, seif 2.
For there is no zimmun. If one person recited the blessing and the others heard it, they fulfilled their obligation. Beit Yosef in the name of Rashba. See what I wrote in siman 167, seif 13.
Reclining is ineffective. If the items came during the meal, one person blesses for everyone, because since reclining is effective for bread, it is likewise effective for the other items. Gemara.
With fruit. The same applies to all other items except bread and wine. Bach and Darchei Moshe. It would appear that our beer has the status of wine in this regard, because people commonly establish a fixed gathering over it and drink it outside a meal. The same applies to mead, and this is also implied by Darchei Moshe. Rama therefore writes precisely that the current custom applies “with fruit.” Bach writes that even with wine it is preferable for each person to bless for himself, since some hold that nowadays there is no fixed gathering even over wine. Agudah, Chullin, siman 108, writes: “The established custom in Ashkenaz is that with wine one person blesses for everyone, because people commonly establish a fixed gathering over it. However, if two or three people enter someone's house and he gives them wine to drink casually, each blesses for himself.” One should not depart from the custom. See what I wrote in siman 208, seif 16.
