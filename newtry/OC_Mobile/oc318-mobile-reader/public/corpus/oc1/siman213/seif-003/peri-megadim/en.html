Rosh Hashanah and many other places explain that according to the opinion that mitzvos require intention, both the one acting and the one fulfilling through him require intention.
I have. I have some reflections here, which I will set out with Hashem's help. In siman 60 the ruling is stated without qualification in accordance with the first opinion, that mitzvos require intention. Magen Avraham writes that this applies specifically to a D'Oraisa obligation, not a D'Rabbanan one. Yet all blessings, except Bircas HaMazon, are D'Rabbanan according to the Mechaber in siman 209, seif 3. One might reject this by saying that whenever the mitzvah itself is D'Oraisa, its blessing also requires intention. See siman 589, where one opinion states that speech requires intention. To distinguish by saying that when a person performs a D'Rabbanan act himself he does not require intention, but one who hears it from another requires intention even for a D'Rabbanan obligation, appears difficult. In Rosh Hashanah 29b the Gemara infers from one who blows shofar merely as music that mitzvos do not require intention, and challenges this from one who passes behind a synagogue and fulfills only if he intended. What is the challenge, if acting and hearing are different?
Also. Rambam, Hilchos Berachos, chapter 1, halachah 11, rules that one who hears from a person obligated only D'Rabbanan cannot fulfill a D'Oraisa obligation through him; see there. This can refer only to Bircas HaMazon, because according to Rambam all other blessings, even me'ein shalosh, are D'Rabbanan. What difference, then, does it make that the underlying mitzvah is D'Oraisa? See Kesef Mishneh there. Even when answering amen, the person reciting the blessing must be obligated on the Torah level. The passage there implies that answering amen is more effective than merely hearing, as though the listener had actually recited the blessing. Nevertheless, according to our ruling he must answer amen in any event, as in siman 266, seif 2. Sometimes answering amen is specifically required, as in siman 219 and in the Tur: after another person recited HaGomel in a context where it was not a blessing in vain, the obligated person fulfills only if he answered amen. Regarding blessings over mitzvos, although one who has already fulfilled may discharge another, it would appear that the obligated person should likewise need to answer amen, as with HaGomel. One may distinguish that there the one reciting is not obligated, whereas here he is considered obligated by virtue of mutual responsibility, which is precisely why one who has already fulfilled may discharge another.
