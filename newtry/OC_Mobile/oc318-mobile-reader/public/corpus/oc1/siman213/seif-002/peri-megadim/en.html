If. If fruit was brought during the meal, since reclining and a fixed gathering are effective for bread, one person likewise blesses for everyone over the fruit. See Eliyah Rabbah, letter 3, and Beit Yosef in the name of Rashba concerning the incident of Rabban Gamliel and the elders. See Derishah, who writes that even regarding the after-blessing, one person blesses for everyone over fruit brought after the meal. See siman 177, Magen Avraham, letter 7.
Further. Derishah writes that “bread” is not meant literally; the same applies to kinds of porridge, and one person blesses for everyone. See Eliyah Rabbah, letter 1. Siman 178, seif 5, implies that the halachah follows Magen Avraham, letter 2, that this applies to grain as well; see there. Especially nowadays, when not everyone knows the wording of the blessing me'ein shalosh by heart, it is proper for one person to bless and the others to fulfill their obligation through him. After the fact, even with fruit they fulfill their obligation, as Taz, letter 1, writes in the name of Rashba, and as Magen Avraham, letter 4, also writes. See what I wrote in siman 178, seif 4. Know that regarding kinds of porridge, some hold that the blessing me'ein shalosh is D'Oraisa; see siman 209, Taz, letter 3. According to this, one who ate only a small amount cannot discharge one who ate to satiety. The verse “You shall eat and be satisfied” applies to the seven species according to the opinion that me'ein shalosh is D'Oraisa; see siman 186. All the same considerations discussed there regarding the opinion that women's obligation in Bircas HaMazon is D'Rabbanan apply likewise to me'ein shalosh; see Magen Avraham there, letter 1. Accordingly, the one who ate the larger amount should bless, as should an adult male over thirteen years and one day. If one person knows how to bless and the other does not, the knowledgeable person should have him repeat word for word. If that is impossible, one person certainly discharges everyone, even with respect to the after-blessing over fruit, as Rashba writes and as cited by Taz and Magen Avraham.
