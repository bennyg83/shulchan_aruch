(א) That he not begin any melacha, etc. — and even half an hour before the time is forbidden lest he come to delay; and likewise forbidden to enter the bath and all matters mentioned above in siman 232. One who forgot to check at night is forbidden by day in all matters until he checks [Acharonim].

(ב) And he not eat — but a mere taste is permitted, meaning bread like an egg's volume and no more, or fruit even in quantity, as we wrote at the end of siman 232 — see there in Mishna Berurah; and see in Biur Halacha that much fruit is not permitted except in the half hour before bedika, but once bedika time arrives it is also not proper to delay much even through fruit.

(ג) He should not study — also in this some forbid even in the half hour before lest he come to delay much, unless he asked someone to remind him when the time comes; and some permit regarding study beforehand and only when tzeit hakochavim time arrives it is forbidden. And even for those who forbid, it is only for one studying at home; but those who teach a halacha lesson in the beit midrash after prayer are permitted, for he will certainly be compelled to come home; nevertheless even this is permitted only for study without pilpul, but with pilpul we are concerned in every case lest he come to delay.

(ד) Until he searches — and regarding Maariv prayer: those who pray Maariv at its time with the congregation pray beforehand since it is burdensome to gather them afterward, and also there is not so much concern lest he forget to check, for prayer is a fixed matter and "lest he delay" does not apply to it; but one who prays at home gives to another to check while he prays, whereby he fulfills both in their time; and if there is no other he prays first. However if he is always accustomed to pray alone he checks first, since he is accustomed thus we are not so concerned lest he forget; and Kaf HaChayyim wrote he should always pray first, for frequent overrides infrequent.

(ה) And if he began to study — and likewise other melachot.

(ו) While it was still day — in time of permission, therefore he need not stop; for even in Shema which is d'oraisa we hold in siman 232 that if he began permissibly he does not stop — see there; and if he began at time of prohibition, all agree he stops, for bedikat chametz is d'oraisa as long as he did not nullify, and regarding d'oraisa matters one stops as written siman 235.

(ז) That he must stop — even though he began permissibly. Their reason: the essence of the mitzva l'chatchila the Sages fixed at the beginning of the night, therefore he must stop so the mitzva be fulfilled properly.

(ח) And so it appears to me as essential — and likewise for other melachot. And nevertheless it appears one need not stop until tzeit hakochavim time arrives.
