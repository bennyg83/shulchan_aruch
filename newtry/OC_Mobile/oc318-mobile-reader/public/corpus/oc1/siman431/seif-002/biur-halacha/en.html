And he not eat — see Mishna Berurah that fruit is permitted even in quantity, and this is evident from Magen Avraham who compared it to siman 232; and it would seem that even when the time of bedika arrives, i.e. tzeit hakochavim, it is also permitted to eat fruit even in quantity, for the prohibition is only a fixed meal that may be prolonged and one may forget bedika — and to me this requires study, for this is not comparable to other matters like Shema and prayer whose mitzva is not specifically at the beginning of the time, and therefore they decreed only on a fixed meal that may be prolonged and one may forget entirely; which is not so here, for the Sages fixed the time l'chatchila immediately at the beginning of the night as many poskim wrote — if so delay itself even without eating is forbidden when the time arrives; and therefore it appears that in the half hour adjacent to tzeit hakochavim one may eat fruit as much as he wishes, but once the time arrives one should not be lenient with much delay even through fruit; and so it appears in Be'er Heitev that only in the half hour do we be lenient for this.
