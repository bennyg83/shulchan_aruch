(א) That he not begin. And half an hour before the time is forbidden lest he come to delay; and one who did not check at night is forbidden by day until he checks.

(ב) And he not eat. But a mere taste is permitted — meaning fruit or bread like an egg's volume as written siman 232 — see there.

(ג) He should not study. And one who teaches a halacha lesson in the synagogue is permitted, for specifically one who studies at home is forbidden since he is not compelled to rise from his place — but this one will certainly go home as Knesset HaGedolah. And Magen Avraham wrote and it appears to me specifically one who states a halacha without pilpul; but it is forbidden to engage in pilpul even if not at home, for in this there is certainly room to decree lest he delay the lesson. Bach wrote: one who did not pray Maariv with the congregation prays at home before bedika, for there is concern lest he be preoccupied with his search and forget to pray; but one who always prays Maariv alone at night should check first since he is accustomed and will not forget. And Magen Avraham wrote: since the time of bedika is before tzeit hakochavim he should check first and then pray after tzeit; and if he did not check until after tzeit he should give to another to check while he prays. And if there is no other he should act as Bach wrote and in s.k. 1 above. And those accustomed to pray Maariv on the 10th should pray first in the synagogue since it is burdensome to gather them afterward — Magen Avraham.

(ד) That he must. And if he said to his friend to remind him of bedika he is permitted as above siman 275 s.k. 3 and 16.
