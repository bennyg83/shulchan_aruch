(א) And even if he has, etc. In the Gemara after R' Nachman bar Yitzchak that I mentioned, Abaye said: therefore a Torah scholar should not open at that time in the evening, etc. — and requires study what "therefore" connects to R' Nachman bar Yitzchak's reasons and not on the body of the mishnah "light on the 14th we search," etc.; and per my words it is settled: since it must be adjacent to his coming and he must wait, therefore that hour must be completely free — for if he wishes to eat it is obvious it is forbidden, for there is no fixed eating and the way is to prolong from eating to eating and he will distance from time of coming more; but even if he has a fixed time to study such as one chapter and he could finish it at that hour when he is free as we remembered, nevertheless forbidden since there is concern study will come to him and he will delay somewhat and be far from his coming and transgress the enactment of the Sages who fixed adjacent to his coming; and with this is resolved what Maharach wrote on evening Shema that they forbade study only that he not say "I will eat a little and drink a little," etc., and we say there if accustomed to review he reviews and recites Shema and prays — what is here that they forbade also study, and he elaborated; and per my words there is no difference: there they were not strict that it be adjacent to his coming, for certainly there is no prohibition even if he delays somewhat, only they were strict on complete nullification of the mitzva as they say there on prohibition of eating and drinking that sleep seizes him and he sleeps all night — therefore they did not forbid study where there is no concern sleep seizes him, for a person's way is to eat and afterward sleep; unlike here where there is concern of distancing from time of his coming which is the essence of the mitzva as I wrote — therefore even for study there is this concern, for in any case he will delay somewhat and distance from time of coming home. And Mahariv in laws of Pesach wrote: and it appears to me the same applies to Maariv — one who did not pray with the congregation is forbidden in melacha and study before he prays, as stated that he should not say "I will eat a little," etc.; and he challenged from the Gemara we remembered where explicitly it states one accustomed to review reviews — and it appears to resolve: the Gemara refers to one who has a fixed lesson to study at that hour before Shema and stops every day and recites Shema — therefore permitted even if sometimes he delays somewhat more, such as an hour or two — what of it, since he has a fixed lesson when to study and when to stop; although regarding bedika likewise forbidden as I wrote — but Mahariv refers to one without a fixed lesson for that hour, only that now by chance he wishes to study, and there is no measure to his study — this is forbidden lest he delay very much; and although Mahariv used language "and the same applies to Maariv prayer" — he did not mean all matters, only prohibition regarding study without fixed measure; but with fixed measure they are not comparable, for bedika is forbidden and Maariv prayer permitted — and so appears to me for halacha.

(ב) And if he began, etc. This is Rabbeinu Yonah's view in the Tur, and he derived from Abaye's language who said "a person should not open" — implying opening specifically is the concern. And it appears Rabbeinu Yonah did not permit this except where he has a fixed lesson, since there is a measure — only concern of distancing from his coming they did not decree as long as he began permissibly before time of a person's coming home; but without fixed measure he agrees with the Tur he must stop, as written regarding Maariv prayer nearby; but for eating it is obvious he must stop even per this view, for the reason we remembered that there is no fixed eating.

(ג) And some say he must stop. This is the Tur; and it appears the language "should not open" teaches per the majority: not only if he sat to study before the time when then there is no concern of nullifying the mitzva — obviously forbidden lest fulfillment of the mitzva not enter his heart while engaged in Torah; but even if he already fixed in his heart obligation of bedika and opens to study on condition of fixed lesson — nevertheless forbidden lest he distance much from coming home which is the essence of the time where there is change and feeling not to transgress the mitzva as above — correct to resolve the two views. And it is written in Maharil that a mere taste is permitted at that hour and they forbade only a meal.
