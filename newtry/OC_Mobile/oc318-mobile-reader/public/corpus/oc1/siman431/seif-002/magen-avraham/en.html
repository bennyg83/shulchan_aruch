(א) That he not begin. At the end of the 13th day [Rambam]; and it appears to me half an hour before night is forbidden as written siman 232 and 235 and siman 489 s.k. 4; and one who did not check at night is forbidden by day until he checks.

(ב) And he not eat. But a mere taste is permitted (Bach, Maharil) — taste means fruit or bread like an egg as written siman 232.

(ג) He should not study. And those people who state a halacha in the synagogue after prayer are permitted the lesson — for specifically one who studies at home is forbidden since he is not compelled to rise from his place, but this one will certainly go home (Ra'avad siman 79 thus). And it appears to me specifically one who states halacha without pilpul; but forbidden to engage in pilpul even if not at home, for certainly there is room to decree lest he delay the lesson and come to be prevented from the mitzva and forget afterward to check. And forbidden to enter the bath and all matters mentioned in siman 232. Bach wrote: one who did not pray Maariv with the congregation — I found in Hagahot he should check first and then pray, and so in machzorim; however it appears to me he should pray first, for there is concern lest he be preoccupied with his search and forget to pray; unlike prayer which is fixed and arranged — not preoccupying; but one who always prays Maariv alone at night should check first, since he is accustomed he will not forget — and so Mahariv: one who prayed in the synagogue and once did not pray in the synagogue is forbidden to study until he prays Maariv; but one who always prays Maariv at home is permitted to study before he prays — end of Bach's words; and he did not examine Mahariv well at the end of his words where he wrote explicitly that even one accustomed to pray at home is forbidden to study until he prays — and so in Shulchan Aruch siman 89 s.k. 6. And that which is stated in the Gemara: he enters the synagogue and reads and reviews and afterward recites Shema and prays — refers before Shema time as Terumat HaDeshen and Shelah wrote and as I wrote — and there is no place here; and here it appears to me bedika time is before tzeit as Rashi wrote, and then he checks and prays after tzeit; and if he did not check until after tzeit he gives to another to check while he prays as written siman 70; and if there is no other he acts as Bach wrote. And those accustomed to pray Maariv on the 10th pray first in the synagogue since it is burdensome to gather them afterward as Tur wrote siman 235 and as I wrote regarding stating the lesson.

(ד) And if he began to study. It implies he speaks of permissibility — all stop.

(ה) While it was still day. It implies if he began at night in prohibition all stop, for bedikat chametz is d'oraisa as long as he did not nullify; and we hold regarding d'oraisa matters one stops as written siman 235 — see Ra'avad part 1 siman 48.

(ו) And some say he must stop. And even though if he began permissibly it says in siman 235 he does not stop — there it is frequent, unlike here which is once a year — more concern lest he forget (Bach); and difficult, for lulav is also once a year and nevertheless need not stop if he began permissibly as written siman 651; and furthermore in Sukkah it challenges from what is taught regarding lulav that one stops and regarding Mincha it teaches one does not stop — what is the challenge, for lulav is not so frequent? And Rama wrote Chanilish that here since it is to separate from prohibition it is stricter; and to me what is written "if he began while it was still day" refers to half an hour near evening when he began in prohibition and therefore must stop; and Rabbeinu Yonah holds he need not stop, for he holds it is not d'oraisa, for behold the time of biur chametz has not yet arrived.
