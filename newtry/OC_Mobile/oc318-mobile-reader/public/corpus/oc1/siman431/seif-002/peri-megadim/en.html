And see siman 492 Taz note 4 and Magen Avraham here note 8 — one may say the mishnah teaches light of the 14th means the time of the 14th as the Gemara's difficulty, and I would say the night of the 14th; and every time and section in the year, even if he began in the permitted period, the ruling is not so now that it is rabbinic — the time should not open like siman 434; rather the difficulty for one authority is even in the permitted period. In our novellae we wrote on this. Taz wrote that during bein ha-shemashot it is forbidden to begin even a fixed chapter only, lest it continue somewhat and pass the start immediately at twilight — unlike Shema where there is no prohibition in the usual change of section and the like, but no fixed [study] even Shema is forbidden; with this he answered Maharil what Bach challenged. It implies during bein ha-shemashot it is forbidden — before bein ha-shemashot one chapter is permitted; Magen Avraham note 3: during bein ha-shemashot forbidden — see there. One who examines Rashi Pesachim 4a s.v. ba'orata: on the thirteenth we reach the fourteenth night — the night of departure of the thirteenth which is entry of the fourteenth, the night of bedikat chametz; in the Gemara "we reach the fourteenth" with dalet — his intent: "orata" in Chazal's language is bein ha-shemashot, forbidden in bein ha-shemashot of the 13th and 14th because bein ha-shemashot applies here and there — per one explanation that is not so, rather orata is actual night, as if he said we reach without dalet that it was the night of bedikat chametz, and during bein ha-shemashot we do not check — and so taught the school of Shmuel: the night of the 14th we check chametz (Magen Avraham 1:5); but in Rambam chapter 2 Laws of chametz uMatzah 3: and one does not fix a derashah on it at the end of day 13 — nevertheless in Machatzit HaShekel and Bach on this. In Shema if accustomed to read he changes per Rabbenu Yonah in Berachot that they required him to read — one may say Shema before this is forbidden at twilight l'chatchila; per Rosh one may say; in our Berachot novellae we wrote from this — see siman 235 note 3 and Magen Avraham 9 and Peri Megadim there; Eliyah Rabbah and see Magen Avraham 3:4 explained further; one who examines this.
