At the beginning of the night of the fourteenth. Rosh writes in a responsum, klal 14, siman 3: “After drawing the water, immediately at the beginning of the night he should inspect for chametz. He should not eat or engage in anything else until he has inspected.” Maharil writes similarly. This implies that the inspection must be performed at actual night, not during twilight, but after the stars emerge, even though some daylight remains. My brother-in-law, the author of Eliyah Zuta, likewise writes this, unlike Bach, whom the other later authorities followed. His position is correct, well reasoned, and compelled by the language of the poskim. The words of Raavad cited by Beit Yosef may also be explained this way. It is explicit in Raavad’s Tamim Deim, in his strictures at the beginning of Pesachim against Baal HaMaor. Ran also holds this view.
Where chametz is ordinarily brought. This is not literal. Even where chametz is not ordinarily brought, if the place is used we are concerned that chametz may have been brought there inadvertently. Tur writes likewise, as explained below in siman 433.
