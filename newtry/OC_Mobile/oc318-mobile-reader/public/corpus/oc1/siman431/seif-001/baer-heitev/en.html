At the beginning. Meaning close to the entrance of the night — Rabbeinu Yeruham, Ran, Magen Avraham, and Kaf HaChayyim ruled that bedika must be at night itself after tzeit hakochavim and not in bein hashmashot. (And Ra'avad inferred from the word "light" that there is still a bit of day, for because of elegant language the tanna did not switch from light to darkness; and Rashi holds specifically bein hashmashot — and from this Kaf HaChayyim inferred that when one did not recite Shema before bedika and two obligations come together he should recite first because it is more frequent — see there.)
