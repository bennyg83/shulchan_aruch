One who carries a load, etc. In the gemara it is stated thus: a tanna taught — one who was carrying a load on his head: if his tefillin are squeezed, it is forbidden; and if not, it is permitted. With what load did they speak? With one of four kav. A tanna taught — Rabbi Chiya: one who takes out manure on his head while tefillin are on his head — he may not move them to the sides nor bind them on his arm, because he treats them with a degrading custom; but he may bind them on his arm in the place of tefillin. For Rabbi Shila they said: even their handkerchief is forbidden to place on the head when there are tefillin on it; and it is implied that Rabbi Shila disagrees with the baraita that requires specifically four kav. And therefore the Rambam and Shulchan Aruch ruled that in every case it is forbidden; except that the Rif, Rosh, and Tur ruled this baraita, and also Rabbi Shila — and this is puzzling. And therefore Beit Yosef explained in the name of Mahari Abohav that the first clause deals with bedieved, and Rabbi Shila deals with one who now wishes ab initio to place a load — and this is difficult; for if so, the Tur should have written "but ab initio he may not place even a handkerchief," etc. And Beit Yosef himself explained that the first clause deals when the load is already on his head and he wishes to put on tefillin afterward; and Rabbi Shila deals when he already put on tefillin and afterward wishes to carry a load — and on this it is difficult, for it says at the outset "and tefillin on his head," implying they are already on his head, and it should have said "and he wishes to put on tefillin." And in the name of Mahariatz he explained that the first clause deals when the load is not on his head at all but behind him, only the rope from which the load hangs is on his head and the rope interposes on the tefillin; and Rabbi Shila deals when the load is on his head — and this is very strained. And Rama in his gloss here implies that he explains the first clause deals with something customary to place on the head, and in this the measure is four kav; and Rabbi Shila deals with what is not customary to place on the head, and in this it is forbidden even in a small amount — and this too is strained, for the tanna taught "carrying a load," and something customary on the head such as a hat or turban is not called a load. And it seems to me correct that therefore the tanna taught at the outset two matters: first, if the tefillin are squeezed; and second, the measure of four kav — for certainly regarding squeezing alone the matter depends, that whenever the tefillin are squeezed it is forbidden, even if it is less than four kav; only that if it is more than four kav we do not follow squeezing, for then they are certainly squeezed. And this "squeezed" that is explained as "pressed" — plainly it does not mean the tefillin moved completely from their place, for in that case it is obviously forbidden; rather it means the tefillin remain in their place under pressure because of the load placed on them, which shortens their space; and therefore Rabbi Shila on this [rule] that even their handkerchief is considered a load and disqualifies if it squeezes and presses the tefillin; but something customary to place there does not forbid even though it squeezes and presses the tefillin, for in any case they remain in their place, only not with ease — so it seems to me, correctly.
