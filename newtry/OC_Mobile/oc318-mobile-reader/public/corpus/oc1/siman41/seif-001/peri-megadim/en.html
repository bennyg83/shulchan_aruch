One who carries. See Taz and Bava Metzia 105b, and see Beit Yosef. Bach agrees with Taz that whenever the burden presses the tefillin, it is forbidden even if it weighs less than four kav. Ordinarily, however, a burden of four kav causes pressure and a lighter burden does not. Tur's wording, that four kav are forbidden if they press the tefillin but permitted if they do not, refers to a burden placed beside the tefillin rather than directly upon them. Bach nevertheless explains Tur differently: Tur states without qualification that even a kerchief is forbidden, and does not limit the prohibition to a case where it presses the tefillin. The Mechaber rules like Rambam, chapter 4 of Hilchos Tefillin, halachah 23, who understands Rabbi Sheila to disagree and forbids even a kerchief. Rabbi Sheila's expression means a very small amount, without any fixed measure. Rama disagrees and holds that both teachings are accepted as halachah. A burden normally carried upon the head requires the measure of four kav, while an item not normally carried upon the head is forbidden even in the smallest amount. Darkei Moshe, cited by Magen Avraham, explains this well. Rama did not write “some say,” because the Mechaber did not explicitly state a contrary view. It is difficult why Rambam and the Mechaber omitted Rabbi Chiya's ruling that one who carries manure upon his head is forbidden even when it does not rest upon the tefillin at all but only at the side, because of disgrace. Tur cites it. Know that the phrase “a burden upon his head” is not exclusive. A burden of four kav upon the arm is likewise forbidden because it presses the arm tefillin. For less than four kav, however, according to the Mechaber, whose concern is disgrace, covered arm tefillin are permitted, as explained. If one carries manure upon his head while wearing only arm tefillin, it is certainly permitted. See Bach. If he carries manure upon his arm, however, it is possible that this disgraces the tefillin even when they are covered by his clothing. See Bach. Eliyah Rabbah cites Sefer Chasidim, siman 476, that one should not place a burden between his upper arms, because this creates an interposition between the tefillin and his heart. His clothing is not considered an interposition because it is normally worn there. See what I wrote elsewhere concerning this point in Magen Avraham. This is not the place to elaborate.
