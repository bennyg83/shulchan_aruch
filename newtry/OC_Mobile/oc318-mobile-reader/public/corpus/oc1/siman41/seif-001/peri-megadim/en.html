One who carries — in Taz, in the gemara Bava Metzia 105, see there; and see Beit Yosef; and Bach agreed with Taz in this, that whatever squeezes, even in less than four kav, is forbidden; but in an unspecified case, four kav constitutes squeezing and less does not; and the Tur wrote four kav if they squeeze, and if not — meaning one may not place it on the tefillin except from the side. And nevertheless Bach explained the words of the Tur in one answer, that in an unspecified case he wrote a handkerchief is forbidden and did not explain specifically if they squeeze; and the Mechaber ruled like Rambam, of blessed memory, chapter 4 of Laws of Tefillin, who holds Rabbi Shila disagrees and even a handkerchief is forbidden (and he holds a quarter of a quarter, meaning a small amount with no measure at all); and the Rashba in his gloss disagreed and said both are the law, for when the load is discussed in a place where their custom is to carry on the head, we require four kav; and something not customary, even more so is forbidden — Darkei Moshe brought this, and Eliyah Rabbah. And what he did not write "and some say" is because it is not explicit in the Mechaber that he disagrees; but it is difficult to me why the Rambam and Mechaber omitted this of Rabbi Chiya, who takes out manure on his head even when nothing at all is placed on the place of tefillin but only to the sides — it is forbidden due to degradation and humiliation, and the Tur brought it. And know that a load on his head — the law of four kav applies; even arm tefillin are forbidden if squeezed, except in less than four kav per the Mechaber, whose reason is degradation; in this, covered arm tefillin are permitted, as stated; and to take out manure on his head when he has only arm tefillin — obviously permitted, see Bach there; but to take out manure on the arm is possible [to forbid] even when covered by his garments, for there is degradation to the tefillin, see Bach. Eliyah Rabbah wrote in the name of Sefer Chassidim siman 976 that one should not place a load between his forearms, for it separates between tefillin and heart; but his garments are not considered separation in this — as Magen Avraham wrote in this in one answer; and here we need not elaborate.
