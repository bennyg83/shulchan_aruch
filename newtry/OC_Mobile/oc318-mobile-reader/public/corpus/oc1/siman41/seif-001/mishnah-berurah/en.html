(1) One who carries a burden. This refers to the entire day, not during Krias Shema and prayer. See below, siman 97, seif 5.
(2) Upon his head. If he carries a burden upon his arm in the place of the tefillin, which are presumably covered and therefore not disgraced, he need not remove them unless the burden weighs four kav. At that weight the tefillin are presumably pressed by it. Peri Megadim discusses this further.
(3) He removes the head tefillin. If he can shift the burden to the sides so that it does not rest upon the tefillin, Peri Megadim implies that it is permitted. Nevertheless, if the burden weighs four kav, it appears proper to be stringent even in that arrangement. See Beit Yosef. It is forbidden to carry manure upon one's head, even a small amount and even at the side, because in every arrangement this disgraces the tefillin upon his head. If he removes the head tefillin, it is permitted even though he continues wearing the arm tefillin. If he wishes to carry manure upon his arm, it is possible that this likewise disgraces the tefillin he is wearing even when they are covered by his clothing. Peri Megadim.
(4) It is permitted. Although the hat rests upon the tefillin, this does not disgrace them because it is worn as clothing. If the hat is large, however, he should ensure that its weight does not move the tefillin from their proper place. Chayei Adam.
(5) Four kav. This equals twenty-five pounds of the measure used for weighing silver in Prague. Lechem Chamudos.
(6) Are pressed. This does not mean that the tefillin move entirely from their place and are no longer positioned according to law. That would obviously be forbidden even with a light burden. Rather, it means that they remain in their proper place but are compressed by the burden resting upon them, which reduces the space available to them. Bach is lenient regarding this compression when caused by a turban or hat. Peri Megadim.
