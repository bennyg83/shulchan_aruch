(1) One who carries a load — this deals with all day, not during the hours of Keriat Shema and prayer. And see below siman 97 seif 5.

(2) On his head — and if he carries a load on his arm in the place of tefillin, since presumably they are covered and there is no degradation, he need not remove them, only when the load is four kav, for then presumably the tefillin are squeezed thereby [Peri Megadim, see there further].

(3) Removes head tefillin — and if he can move the load to the sides, not in the place of the tefillin, it is implied from Peri Megadim that it is permitted; nevertheless, if there is a load of four kav in it, it seems one should be stringent even in such a case — see Beit Yosef. And to take out manure on his head, even a little and even to the sides, is forbidden, for in every case there is degradation to the head tefillin on him; unless when he removes, then the head tefillin from upon him are permitted even though he carries the load on his arm. And if he wishes to take out manure on his arm, it is possible that even if the tefillin are covered by his garments, there is degradation to the tefillin on him [Peri Megadim].

(4) Permitted — even though it rests on the tefillin, there is no degradation to the tefillin, since it is the manner of dress; but if the hat is large, he should beware lest the weight of the hat displace the tefillin from their proper place [Machatzit HaShekel].

(5) Four kav — they are twenty-five litras with which silver is weighed in Prague [Levush].

(6) Pressed — this does not mean that the tefillin move completely from their place and are not placed as required, for in that case it is obviously forbidden, even if the load is light; rather, it means they remain in their place under pressure because of the load placed on them, which shortens their space; and the Bach is lenient in this matter regarding a turban and hat [Peri Megadim].
