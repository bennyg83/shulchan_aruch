One asks, etc. In the Tur the wording is "one asks and expounds," which matches our Gemara in Pesachim 6 and Bekhorot 58. In the Rif the reading is only "one asks," and so is the reading in Avodah Zarah and Sanhedrin 12. At first glance there is no practical difference, for the intent is one matter: to discuss the laws of Pesach before Pesach. So too Rashi writes in Rosh Hashanah 7 and Bava Kamma 113, that they would publicly expound the laws of Pesach thirty days before Pesach. However, the Ran in the first chapters of Pesachim and Megillah says that "one asks" means that during this period, if two students come and one asks about the laws of Pesach, one attends to him first because he asks on topic; but there is no obligation to study thirty days before Pesach, only on Pesach itself. This is what we learned, that Moshe ordained for Israel to expound the laws of Pesach on Pesach, etc. Therefore the wording here is precise, "one asks" alone. These are the words of the Rashba at the end of chapter 4 of Megillah. But many Rishonim disagree: Rashi in several places, Tosafot in Megillah 4, the Rokeach, the Itur, and Or Zarua all state explicitly that there is an obligation to expound the laws of Pesach thirty days beforehand. It is also very difficult from Sanhedrin 12, where it clearly implies that "one asks" means expounding, for the Gemara says that from Purim we expound the laws of Pesach, as taught: one asks in the laws of Pesach thirty days before Pesach. The substance also proves this, for if it only meant giving precedence to a student who asks on topic, there would be no publicity and no reason people would come to treat chametz lightly. The Bach, Peri Chadash, and Be'er Heitev already challenged the Ran from these sugyot.
I later found that the She'iltot also writes simply: thirty days before Pesach we expound matters of Pesach, as taught, one asks in the laws of Pesach thirty days before Pesach. Behag is even more explicit: thirty days before Pesach a Jew must learn the laws of Pesach and teach the members of his household, as taught, etc. Thus the Ran and Rashba are isolated against all these Rishonim. From Rabban Shimon ben Gamliel in the sugya itself it is also evident that it does not refer only to answering a questioner, but to studying and remembering the laws; and from Rabban Shimon ben Gamliel we infer the first Tanna as well, since they disagree only about the time, not the essence. Mateh Yehudah also noted this. What is stated at the end of Megillah, that Moshe ordained that Israel expound the laws of Pesach on Pesach and Shavuot on Shavuot, has already been answered by the commentators; see Beit Yosef, Bach, and others. The Gra appears to explain that it does not mean only the festival day, but the festival and the prior period as explained elsewhere; the Bach writes similarly, although many later authorities disagree with his claim that after studying thirty days before, one need not study on the festival itself. Chok Yaakov offered a new answer from the Yerushalmi and Tosefta: at home one asks about Pesach on Pesach, but in the study hall, where many gather to learn, one asks thirty days before. Peri Moshe explains similarly. Eliyah Rabbah rejects this, since our Bavli in several places cites the baraita simply, "one asks thirty days before," without distinguishing a study hall; this implies that the Bavli does not accept the Yerushalmi's distinction. The Behag cited above also supports him. In any event, in batei midrash and study groups there is certainly an obligation to study thirty days before, even according to the Yerushalmi, as Peri Moshe writes.
