(א) They ask about the laws of Pesach, etc. — for Moshe stood at the first Pesach and warned about all the laws of the second Pesach; and the same for other festivals — they deliver derashot beforehand thirty days in their laws; and see Biur HaGra whose view is that for Shavuot it suffices from the first of Sivan; and some say the obligation of thirty days is only for Pesach because it has many laws such as grinding wheat, baking matzot, koshering vessels, and biur chametz — if these are not done according to law before Pesach there is no remedy on Pesach, unlike other festivals where a few days before suffice; in any case on the festival itself all agree one must ask and deliver derashot on each festival in its laws, as stated at the end of Megillah 32 — Moshe enacted for Israel that they ask and deliver derashot on the matter of the day — laws of Pesach on Pesach, laws of Shavuot on Shavuot, and laws of the festival on the festival:

(ג) And the custom, etc. — it is an ancient custom from the time of the Gemara; and this matter is brought in Yerushalmi chapter 1 of Bava Batra; and the townspeople may compel each other regarding this matter:
(ד) Wheat to distribute — and in our countries the custom is to distribute flour to them, whereby their enjoyment is closer; and the measure of giving is according to what they need for all the days of Pesach; and it is obvious that if he is a great poor person who has nothing with which to bake matzah they must give him also the cost of baking, for that is included in "his lack that he lacks":
(ה) Twelve months — and if we see he entered the city with intent to settle there he is obligated immediately — see Sha'arei Teshuvah. And this law of twelve months applies also to a poor person, for then they are obligated to give him like other poor of the city; and if his intent is to settle immediately they must give him; Acharonim wrote in the name of Semag that nowadays they practice all charity distributions — whether for householders or for the poor — within thirty days. And even a poor person who does not reside here forty days — although they are not obligated to give him flour for all days of Pesach at once — nevertheless they must give him matzah on Pesach as they must give him bread all year — food for two meals each weekday he stays in the city, and on Shabbat food for three meals as written in Yoreh Deah siman 256 see there:
