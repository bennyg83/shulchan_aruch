Seif 1. “One asks and expounds concerning the laws of Pesach,” etc. See Magen Avraham, who writes that the same applies to the other festivals. See what I wrote in my novellae to Orach Chayim, laws of Sukkah, siman 636, in my Orach Chayim work from the year 5612, page 127, where I brought a proof supporting Beit Yosef that this applies specifically to Pesach and not to the other festivals. See there, with Hashem’s help, and examine carefully.
Ibid., in the gloss: The custom is to purchase wheat, etc. Note: With Hashem’s help, it appears to me that there is a sound reason for this custom. We instituted the declaration in Ha Lachma Anya, “Whoever is hungry, let him come and eat.” If a poor person actually came and was not given food, the speaker would be uttering falsehood before Hashem. We therefore instituted giving the poor money for wheat. If the poor person later comes, one may tell him: I said only, “Whoever is hungry, let him come and eat,” but you are not now among those left hungry, because I already gave you money for wheat. If you spent the money on something else, you caused your own condition. Thus, one does not speak falsely before Hashem. This appears to me a sound explanation. Examine it carefully.
