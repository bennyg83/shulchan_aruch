(1) They ask — Taz. Said the layman who writes: I have here reflections on matters and I am setting them forth with Heaven's help. Here in explanation they said in the first chapter of Avodah Zarah 5b that expounding on the festival offering is obligatory because of the sacrifice (Avodah Zarah — even though visiting the sick on Yom Tov is rabbinic, nevertheless all Israel who have leisure must expound thirty days, and Moshe warned accordingly — he thought perhaps many are impure, and one may say in extenuating circumstances, and Avodah Zarah in the name of Rashi on visiting the sick on Yom Tov and it should be chosen, etc. — slight difficulty in Avodah Zarah); and Tosafot Avodah Zarah s.v. vehatnan wrote: although even now they expound — meaning as Megillah 4a s.v. mai: even on Yom Tov they expound thirty days so as not to distinguish Pesach from other festivals, and nevertheless they expound the topic of the day — all the more Purim; thus even nowadays we are obligated — it was after the destruction — and if so there is no difficulty at all, for Moshe enacted that they expound even on the festival itself, even though they expounded thirty days prior; the obligation is also on the festival as proven in Sukkah — see Magen Avraham there; one who examines Ran on Megillah and first chapter of Pesachim may say their intent is that "they ask and expound" means the questioner asks why we were commanded on that festival, what miracle and reason — and this is "they ask and expound," meaning etc.; but "they ask and expound" on that day certainly means expounding Pesach laws is obligatory, and "they ask" means to be a questioner as in Pesachim first chapter — Ran: if not that they asked as questioners, etc. — difficulty: they asked in their time why it was diminished; one must say the explanation: if not that the questioner thirty days prior is called a questioner, Moshe would not answer — from this we learn a questioner in such a case is so called. Beit Yosef challenged thus from himself and concluded Ran explained "they ask." In Shulchan Aruch the author omitted "and expound" — see there that so in one version of Beit Yosef; and so too Atzeret and Sukkot ask in this sense; Taz explained the practical difference, therefore the author wrote thus. In Machatzit HaShekel siman 86 note 13 before festivals the customary expounding is questioning by owners — he did not distinguish the three pilgrim festivals, only "by way of," etc.; in Levush it is written that on Pesach wheat, etc., requires thirty days, and since they expound on these they expound on all — this "they ask," etc. — meaning "they ask and expound": since one must expound laws practiced before Pesach, then the questioner then — even a Pesach matter — is a questioner; Atzeret and Sukkot do not need thirty days and thus are questioners not in that sense — how then does Machatzit HaShekel siman 86 note 13 imply one time for all, i.e. thirty days? One may say somewhat.

(ב) And what Taz wrote therefore it is difficult — certainly the reason is he places obligation on him since they ask as stated in the Gemara first chapter of Pesachim 6a; possibly Tur is not accustomed to explain reasons so much; therefore he said it comes to settle the language they ask and deliver from itself what is needed that day — namely a question in context even though for Pesach itself; practical difference for money; and deliver with him on the visitation day — see Bach in the name of Rashba; and now each forty regarding one traveling in a caravan; and Megillah Moshe enacted Israel ask and deliver on the matter of the day; and so Purim — to ask and explain the miracle and reason for which, etc., as Bach wrote; and so appears from Ran; unlike they ask and deliver thirty days in Pesach laws — deliver what is needed now as stated; and ask what will be in the future — question in context:
