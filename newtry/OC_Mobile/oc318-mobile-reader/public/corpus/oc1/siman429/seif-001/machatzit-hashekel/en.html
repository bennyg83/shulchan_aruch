(א) s.k. 1 on Pesach laws. And the same for other festivals — to exclude Rabbeynu Yitzchak who holds specifically Pesach requires thirty days because Pesach has many laws, unlike other festivals. Further as Tosafot wrote Pesach requires thirty days because of sacrifice; although for inspecting blemishes four days suffice, nevertheless time is needed to search and find a fitting animal. And wondrous; and even nowadays when there is no sacrifice the original enactment remains — and this applies specifically to Pesach festival when each needs a Pesach sacrifice, unlike other festivals. On this Magen Avraham disagrees that other festivals also require thirty days for derashah; that they specified Pesach explicitly is because from it we derive as stated in Pesachim 6b — behold Moshe stood at the first Pesach and warned about second Pesach for those impure on first Pesach; they asked why they are deprived of offering the festival sacrifice; it would suffice to answer they are impure and forbidden to make first Pesach; he added they should make second Pesach; between first and second Pesach are thirty days — thus thirty days before one must deliver; from Pesach we derive for other festivals; and so Rashba in Sukkah 4 on what we learned there — Beit Shammai disqualify an old sukkah and Beit Hillel permit; which old sukkah? any made thirty days before Sukkot; Rashi's reason for Beit Shammai: it must be made for its sake; if made generically it is invalid; but if made within thirty of the festival, since they ask festival laws generically it is made for its sake; from this Maharsha wrote it proves not like Rabbeynu Yitzchak — from this it proves also on Sukkot they deliver thirty days before the festival; a fortiori on Shavuot as stated:

(ב) And so in Bekhorot chapter 9 57b we learned three threshing floors for animal tithe — at half-Pesach, half-Shavuot, and half-Sukkot; Rashi: by Torah law one may eat and sell before tithing the remainder; if he tithed he did a mitzva; the tenth is holy and offered when unblemished on the altar — only blood and limbs to the altar, the rest eaten by owners like Pesach, nothing to the kohen even breast and thigh; rabbinically when one of the three threshing periods arrives it is forbidden to eat until he tithes; they gave the reason so animals be available for festival pilgrims; since one enjoys doing a mitzva with his money in a matter of only slight loss he enjoys tithing his animal even though not obligated since he loses only the portions burned. Possibly when many Israelites went on pilgrimage to Jerusalem their animals were not yet tithed; without tithe he would not wish to sell — therefore the Sages enacted that in those three periods one must tithe. Tosafot wrote: although before the threshing floor one may eat without tithe, some wait until the threshing floor and tithe everything together; thereby animals are available for pilgrims. "Half" means half — fifteen days before Pesach, fifteen before Shavuot, fifteen before Sukkot; since they ask and deliver thirty days before the festival, fifteen days before the festival is called "half" — half of thirty.
