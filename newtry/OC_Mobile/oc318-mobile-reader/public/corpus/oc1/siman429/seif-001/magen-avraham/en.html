Regarding the laws of Pesach. The same applies to other festivals. Their laws should be taught thirty days beforehand. Bach and Maharshal, Sukkah 9a, and Bechoros, chapter 9. Nowadays the custom is to teach publicly on Shabbos HaGadol and Shabbos Shuvah. The principal purpose is to instruct the people of Hashem in the ways of Hashem and teach them the deeds they must perform. Maharil would also teach the laws of Sukkos on Shabbos Shuvah. The first chapter of Megillah implies that on the festival itself one is obligated to expound upon the subject of the day, such as the Exodus on Pesach. See there concerning Purim that falls on Shabbos. Twelve months. The same law applies to a poor person who has lived here for twelve months. The community is obligated to provide for him. Beit Yosef, Yoreh Deah, siman 256. Semak writes that nowadays the period is thirty days. Olas Shabbos did not examine the source. It appears to me that even a poor person who has not lived here for thirty days must nevertheless be given matzah to eat, although he is not given wheat.
