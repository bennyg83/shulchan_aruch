(א) Throughout Nissan. Because twelve princes offered twelve days and each day of the offering was his festival; afterward Pesach and Isru Chag — thus most of the month came out in holiness; therefore they make all of it holy. And see siman 580 and 420 and 573 and 496 — not like Eshel Avraham:

(ב) In public. It implies he holds an individual fast is permitted as Bach wrote; therefore Rama wrote one does not fast at all:

(ג) Tziduk HaDin and not Kaddish (Hagahot Maimoniyot). Bach and Shelah wrote that after Isru Chag one fasts on the day one's father died; and the same on Rosh Chodesh; and it appears to me one should not be strict, for these fasts are mere stringencies:

(ד) Dream fast. And it is obvious one need not sit fast-for-fast; although Shakh in Yoreh Deah siman 215 in the name of Maharam Metz wrote that days when Tachanun is not said are like days forbidden by their words — nevertheless for this it appears they originally accepted to fast a dream fast; and the Rabbeynu in Yoreh Deah is lenient on individual fast as stated; and if he fasted on Shabbat within Nissan a dream fast it appears he sits fast-for-fast in Nissan, for Bach in siman 568 wrote that several Geonim wrote in the name of Semak that one who fasts on the first day of Yom Tov sits a second fast for his fast and they disagreed; Rama ruled in siman 288 not like them — see in Semak per our textual reading and its explanation; if so a fortiori one fasts in Nissan; and so in Rosh HaLevi siman 4; the same between Yom Kippur and Sukkot or on days of restriction; nevertheless it appears if he is close to the tenth of the month he fasts on the tenth which is the fast of the righteous as written siman 580; the same if it was Sunday Rosh Chodesh Nissan he fasts — see siman 573:

(ה) Mizmor LeTodah. For thanksgiving bread was chametz and even on erev Pesach it is forbidden to offer lest they become notar:

(ו) Isru Chag. In Beit Yosef here it implies one may fast; and so siman 494 that specifically after Shavuot it is forbidden because it is a day of slaughter; and so Ta'anit 18a not like Bach; however in Tosafot Rosh HaShanah 19b it implies the custom is not to fast.
