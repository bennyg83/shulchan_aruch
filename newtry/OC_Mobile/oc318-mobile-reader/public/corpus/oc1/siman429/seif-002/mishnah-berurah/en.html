(א) Throughout the month of Nissan — because twelve princes offered on twelve days and all the days of the offering were his festival; and also the days of Pesach itself; and Isru Chag — thus most of the month came out in holiness; therefore they make all of it holy:
(ב) Tzidkatkha — also Yehi Ratzon after Torah reading is not said in Nissan; also souls are not mentioned; also Tziduk HaDin and the Kaddish after it are not said. The rule: Tziduk HaDin, Yehi Ratzon, Rachum VeChanun are equivalent; and likewise on Motza'ei Shabbat and Motza'ei Yom Tov after midnight they are not said; but on erev Rosh Chodesh and erev Chanukah after midnight they say Tziduk HaDin. And it is good to read in Nissan each day its nasi; on the 13th the portion Beha'alotcha until "so he made the menorah" [Acharonim]:
(ג) To mention in public — meaning they do not mention the fast in public; nevertheless an individual accustomed to fast on a parent's yahrzeit may fast per the Mechaber's view; our custom is as Rama that one does not fast at all [Acharonim]:
(ד) Even the day, etc. — meaning throughout the month of Nissan; and likewise erev Rosh Chodesh Iyar one does not fast — even those accustomed to fast every Rosh Chodesh; but bride and groom on their wedding day practice fasting even on Rosh Chodesh Nissan for a reason explained siman 573 [Acharonim]:
(ה) But a dream fast they fast — and one need not sit fast-for-fast in the month of Iyar to atone for having fasted in Nissan on his dream as one must do for one who fasted on Shabbat and Yom Tov and Rosh Chodesh and Chol HaMoed who must sit fast-for-fast as explained siman 418. And if he fasted on Shabbat within Nissan a dream fast, he may fast the next day Sunday for what he fasted on Shabbat, since the prohibition of fasting is only custom. And even if Rosh Chodesh Nissan fell on Sunday and he fasted on the Shabbat before, he may sit fast-for-fast on Rosh Chodesh Nissan for what he fasted on Shabbat [Acharonim]:
(ו) Mizmor LeTodah — for thanksgiving bread was chametz; and even on erev Pesach it is forbidden to offer it lest it become notar:
(ז) On erev Pesach — and they rise early to synagogue on erev Pesach so he finishes his meal before the fourth hour [Maharil]:
(ח) Isru Chag — the custom not to fast on any Isru Chag. To impose a cherem in synagogue for some great need is permitted in Nissan even if forbidden in Tishrei [Acharonim]:
