And the firstborn fast, etc. The Tur cites from Maseches Soferim that students fast on Monday, Thursday, and Monday because of desecration of Hashem's Name for three days. It appears to me that these three days refer to the period after Pesach, etc. Maseches Soferim itself states: They interrupt for Purim in order to observe the fast days of Mordechai and Esther, and after Purim they fast on Monday, Thursday, and Monday. Why do they not fast during Nisan? Because on the first of Nisan the Mishkan was erected, etc. The students fast during it on Monday, Thursday, and Monday because of desecration of Hashem's Name and because of the honor of the Sanctuary that was burned. End quote. The Beit Yosef writes that this refers to the Monday and Thursday fasts observed throughout the year, which are likewise not observed in Nisan. This is difficult. Why does the text mention Monday, Thursday, and Monday? It should have mentioned only Monday and Thursday. I also find it difficult why these fasts are connected to the days of Purim, since it says, after Purim they fast on Monday, Thursday, and Monday. It appears to me that every fast established by the Sages had to resemble an earlier fast. This follows the Mordechai's statement that the Monday, Thursday, and Monday fasts after Pesach and Sukkos resemble the fasts of the early rains, etc. Likewise here, in the days of Mordechai and Esther they transgressed the festival and fasted on Pesach, as we expound from the verse, 'And Mordechai passed.' We have already established that one who fasts on a day when fasting is forbidden must undertake a fast for having fasted, as we find regarding a Torah scholar who fasted on Shabbos. Presumably, therefore, after Purim in Mordechai's time they fasted for having transgressed during Pesach of the preceding year. This practice remained established for later generations. Although its original reason does not apply to us, the fasts were not abolished. The text therefore properly asks why these fasts are not observed in Nisan. Since they arose because of Pesach, as explained, they should have been observed after Pesach during Nisan. It answers as it does. Accordingly, the authority cited by the Tur properly added the words, for three days, after quoting Maseches Soferim, so that we should not mistakenly interpret it as referring to the Monday and Thursday fasts of the entire year, as the Beit Yosef in fact did. Rather, it refers only to three days, for Maseches Soferim specifically mentions Monday, Thursday, and Monday. Had it referred to the entire year, it should have mentioned only Monday and Thursday. The Tur concludes that it refers to after Pesach, etc. This parallels the practice of the earlier generations, who fasted those days after Pesach because of Pesach. Likewise, we fast because of Pesach, lest people have sinned during those days through eating, drinking, and the rejoicing of the festival. This appears correct to me.
Even one day, etc. My father-in-law, of blessed memory, writes that one should fast on a parent's yahrzeit during Nisan, since it is no less significant than the students' fast. The custom has already spread, however, that on every day when Tachanun is not recited one does not fast. Regarding a bridegroom's fast, see siman 573.
