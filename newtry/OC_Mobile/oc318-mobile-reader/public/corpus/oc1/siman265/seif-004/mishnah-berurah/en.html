(12) "One may place a vessel." Even on Shabbos. (13) "To catch the sparks." Meaning the flame or sparks dripping from the lamp, so that whatever is beneath it will not catch fire. (14) "Actual sparks." This means sparks that go out immediately. It is therefore permitted to move the vessel even after the sparks have fallen into it, and there is no issue of rendering the vessel unusable even for a short time. The ash of the sparks themselves, however, may not be moved because it is nolad. [Magen Avraham; see Eliyah Rabbah] (15) "Even before Shabbos." Although regarding other melachot we rule that it is permitted to begin the process before Shabbos even though the melacha is completed on Shabbos, as stated at the beginning of siman 252, and we do not decree lest one begin it on Shabbos, here the matter is different because extinguishing is directly caused by the arrangement. [Hagahot at the end of chapter Kirah and other authorities] (16) "Because he brings," etc. Where there is danger of fire, it is permitted to place a vessel full of water to receive the sparks. Likewise, if he sees that a lamp is about to fall upon a table, in a pressing situation there is room to place water in a manner that prevents danger, subject to the distinctions stated by the authorities. (17) "And he should not do so on Shabbos," etc. Pouring water into the receptacle in which a wax or tallow candle stands, so that when the flame reaches the level of the water it will be extinguished, is forbidden because it directly prepares the later extinguishing. Where needed, one should place the water there before lighting the candle. It is permitted, however, to insert the candle into sand or a similar substance in such a way that when the flame reaches that place the fuel will no longer burn and the flame will go out by itself. That is considered indirect extinguishing. [Later authorities]
