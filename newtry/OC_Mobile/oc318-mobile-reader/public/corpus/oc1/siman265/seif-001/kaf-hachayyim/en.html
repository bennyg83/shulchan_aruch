1) [Seif 1] One may not place a vessel, etc. The reason for the prohibition is that once he set the oil aside for the lamp, if he uses any of it he is liable for extinguishing. When he attached the vessel, however, there is no concern that he will use its oil, because he refrains from doing so on account of the Shabbos prohibition. Rashi; Beis Yosef.
2) There: And he will become liable for extinguishing. This follows Rambam, who rules like Rabbi Yehudah that one is liable for labor not needed for its intrinsic purpose. Nevertheless, it is forbidden even according to Rabbi Shimon. Magen Avraham, paragraph 1. See below, siman 278; siman 316, seif 8; siman 334, seif 27; and our words there, with the help of Heaven.
3) There: And he will become liable for extinguishing. If a burning lamp contains small pieces of tallow and one removes one piece, he is not liable if he takes the piece farthest from the wick. Nevertheless, this is certainly forbidden ab initio both on Shabbos and on Yom Tov. It is likewise forbidden to cut a piece from a candle while it is burning. Bach in the name of Or Zarua; Knesses HaGedolah, Hagahos HaTur, paragraph 3; Olas Shabbos, paragraph 1. See below, siman 514, seif 3.
4) There: If he attached it with plaster, etc. This means that it is permitted even if the homeowner attached it with plaster or clay so firmly that it resembles a potter's vessel. Beis Yosef; Eliyah Rabbah, paragraph 2.
