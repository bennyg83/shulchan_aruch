[3] [Levush:] And likewise one may not, etc. Bach explains that the ruling is presented in the form of ‘not only this, but even that’: even in the case of a bowl, where the wick connects the two vessels, we nevertheless do not say that he will refrain from taking the oil; rather, it is prohibited. According to this, the clause concerning a bowl was necessary according to the Rabbis. This is the version of Rambam in his Commentary to the Mishnah. In our Gemara, however, the reverse is stated: that the clause was necessary according to Rabbi Yehudah. This concludes his words. This is puzzling, for Rambam’s Commentary to the Mishnah explains the matter in accordance with our Gemara on Shabbos 29b (that the clause was necessary according to Rabbi Yehudah) since Rabbi Yehudah prohibits the bowl because the wick joins it to the lamp; see there. This is also implied by Tosafos Yom Tov. Tosafos Yom Tov wrote that it is possible that the Gemara’s version is uncertain; I likewise found this in Kol Bo, siman 36; see there. Tosafos Yom Tov further asks why the Mishnah did not teach only the cases of a bowl and an earthenware vessel and omit the egg. He answers that one might have thought the egg is prohibited because the wick is not inside it; see there. This is difficult: if so, why does the Gemara say that this case is necessary because the vessels are separate? That consideration itself would necessarily remain relevant, and it alone would suffice. Lechem Mishneh, in the chapter Bameh Madlikin, answers that one might otherwise have thought that Rabbi Yehudah permits specifically an earthenware bowl because it is repulsive. This concludes his words. According to this, however, now that the first clause was taught, how do we know that Rabbi Yehudah permits a bowl that is not made of earthenware?
