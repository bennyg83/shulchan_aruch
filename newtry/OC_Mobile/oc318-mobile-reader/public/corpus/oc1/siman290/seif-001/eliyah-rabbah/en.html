[1] [Levush:] And he has a quorum, etc. Regarding the ruling above in siman 284, seif 3, that one fulfills his obligation by hearing the blessing over the Torah, one must say that reciting the blessing oneself is preferable. Rama in siman 46 concludes that one fulfills the obligation by listening only when he has no fruit, and even then he must intend to fulfill the obligation and listen carefully. Shelah writes in the name of Emek Berachah that when fruit is brought during the meal on Shabbos, it is proper to delay eating it until after Bircas HaMazon so that one gains an additional concluding blessing. On a weekday, however, one should not do so, because it would create an unnecessary blessing. Magen Avraham at the end of siman 215 objects that Yoma 70 states that on Yom Kippur another Sefer Torah was not brought because that would cause an unnecessary blessing, even though one lacks the usual total of one hundred blessings on Yom Kippur. In my humble opinion, the Kohen Gadol in any event recited many blessings over the offerings and immersions. See the Mishnah in the chapter Ba Lo Kohen Gadol. See also siman 100.
