[1] [Levush] And he has the required number, etc. Regarding that which was stated above in siman 284, seif 3, that one fulfills the blessing over Torah through hearing it from another, it must be said that reciting it oneself is preferable. Rama in siman 46 concludes that one fulfills through hearing only if he has no fruit, and even then he must intend to fulfill and listen. Shelah, citing Emek Berachah, writes that when fruit is brought during the Shabbos meal, it is proper to delay eating it until after Birkat HaMazon so that he will gain an additional final blessing. On weekdays one should not do this because it causes an unnecessary blessing. Magen Avraham at the end of siman 215 objects from Yoma 70a, where a second Torah scroll was not brought on Yom Kippur because that would cause an unnecessary blessing, even though on Yom Kippur one lacks the usual total of one hundred blessings. In my humble opinion, the Kohen Gadol in any event recited many blessings over the offerings and immersions. See the Mishnah in the chapter Ba Lo Kohen Gadol.
[2] With sleep, etc. Provided that he does not indulge in excessive sleep, lest it cause him to neglect Torah study. When the Sages said that Torah scholars should delight themselves on Shabbos, they did not mean that they should spend the entire day in pleasures, only that they should devote more time to delight than on other days. On the contrary, a person is obligated to develop a new Torah insight every Shabbos. The Zohar states that on Motzaei Shabbos, when the additional soul returns to its place, the Holy One, blessed is He, asks it what Torah insight the person developed (Shelah 34). One should not say on Shabbos, "Let us sleep so that we can do our work on Motzaei Shabbos," even if he intends to write Torah, but should say simply, "Today is Shabbos" (Sefer Chasidim 266; see siman 307).
