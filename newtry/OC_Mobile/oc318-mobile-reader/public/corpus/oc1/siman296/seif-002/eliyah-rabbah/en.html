[6] [Levush] Beer is more beloved, etc. For one who does not prefer beer to wine, Havdalah should be recited over wine (Taz). I already wrote that Magen Avraham limits this to beer that is chamar medinah, whereas in Ashkenaz it is prohibited otherwise.
[7] And incidentally to Kiddush, etc. This is the primary ruling, contrary to the alternative view in Shulchan Aruch and the custom recorded by Levush (Piskei Tosafot). It is surprising, because Levush himself writes that the custom is to use bread. Perishah writes that in his humble opinion a printing error entered the later edition, for the latter position is that of Rif, Rosh, and Tur, and Rama normally rules like them. In my humble opinion Levush is correct, for I found this in Agudah siman 417 in the name of Shibbolei HaLeket and many great authorities, as well as in Tanya and Shiltei Gibborim, p. 43. Moreover, some authorities permit Havdalah over bread throughout the year when wine is unavailable. Even Rosh wrote only that another method is preferable. The commentators did not raise this issue with the wording of their Shulchan Aruch, which also follows the later printing. Nachalat Tzvi, Olat Tamid, and Rabbi Moshe Rivkash likewise appear to understand it this way.
[8] [Levush] Or beer, etc. Meaning even if beer is available, but it is not chamar medinah. This is also implied by Pesachim 107a.
