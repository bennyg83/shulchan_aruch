(7) Over bread - This is not comparable to Kiddush, for Kiddush is different, since it is in the place of a meal and is part of what Kiddush requires, unlike Havdalah.
(8) One may make Havdalah over beer - If he has no wine; but if he has wine, it has priority over all beverages, as Or Zarua writes. Chazal also greatly praised the mitzvah and said: one who makes Havdalah over wine, or hears others making Havdalah, the Holy One, blessed be He, calls him holy and makes him a treasured people, as it says, "You shall be to Me a treasure from all peoples," and it says, "I separated you from the peoples," as Tur writes.
(9) Chamar medinah - See above in siman 272 seif 9 in Mishna Berurah and Biur Halachah there, what is chamar medinah. What he wrote, that the same applies to other beverages, means beverages people are accustomed to drink; but beverages that people are accustomed to on Pesach, such as licorice water, are not chamar medinah, since they are not accustomed to drink it all year, and the same applies to anything similar. Even mead is chamar medinah only in countries where people are accustomed to drink it, but where they drink it only occasionally it is not chamar medinah and is worse than beer. It is not proper to make Havdalah over spirits, for Magen Avraham wrote that the one making Havdalah usually drinks the entire Havdalah cup and does not give members of the household to drink from it; with spirits it is difficult to fulfill this. But when he has no other beverage he may make Havdalah over it, provided he is careful to drink at least a cheekful, which is most of a revi'it; otherwise he has not fulfilled. Sha'arei Teshuvah wrote in the name of Birkei Yosef that one should not make Havdalah over milk or oil, since people are not accustomed to drink them as beverages.
(10) Except for water - Even if most of the country's drinking is water, because it is not significant. Regarding kvass and borscht, it is proven from Taz in siman 182 and Eliyah Rabbah there that even in pressing circumstances one does not bless, for although the masses drink them, nevertheless they are not significant and are like water. Even the view of Magen Avraham there, who copied Rashal's lenient view in pressing circumstances, applies specifically to the cup of Birkat Hamazon, where there is no concern of a blessing in vain, as is proven in Rashal's responsum, unlike Havdalah. Chayei Adam wrote that in pressing circumstances one may make Havdalah over kvass, and I do not know his reason. Perhaps he speaks where it was made into a significant beverage like beer; and even this is specifically if the main drink of the masses is this beverage, for otherwise it is not chamar medinah.
(11) And it is better, etc. - Magen Avraham inclines that in a place where beer is chamar medinah, one could say it is preferable to a blemished cup of wine. See Biur HaGra, who also wrote that Rama's law is specifically when the beer is not chamar medinah.
(12) And they were accustomed to make Havdalah - Specifically in a place where beer is chamar medinah; but in Germany, where it is not chamar medinah, the fact that it is beloved does not help, and it is forbidden to make Havdalah over it.
(13) For it is beloved to him - Since he did not drink it throughout Pesach.
(14) One also makes Havdalah - Meaning, although generally one does not make Havdalah over bread, as above, here, since Kiddush is primary, one says the whole yaknehaz over bread.
(15) The first reasoning is primary - His reason is that many Rishonim hold one does not make Kiddush over beer, as above in siman 272 seif 9; therefore it is better to make the whole yaknehaz over bread. Nevertheless, it appears that in this case he should be careful initially to seek wine, even if bread is more beloved to him than wine, for several great Rishonim hold that one does not say Havdalah over bread even on Motzaei Shabbat that is Yom Tov; only if he cannot find wine is bread preferable to beer.
