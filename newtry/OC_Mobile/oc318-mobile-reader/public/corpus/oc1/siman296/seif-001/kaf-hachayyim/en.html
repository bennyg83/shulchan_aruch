1) [Seif 1] The order of Havdalah, etc. Rambam writes in chapter 29 that Havdalah is a Torah obligation like Kiddush. Some say it is rabbinic. Maggid Mishneh there; Magen Avraham at the beginning of this siman. If one recited Havdalah in the prayer, all opinions agree that Havdalah over a cup is rabbinic. See Berachos 33a, above in siman 271, paragraphs 3 and 5, and note this well. See further below, paragraph 54.
2) Rabbi Tzadok says: Anyone who neither recites Havdalah over wine after Shabbos nor hears it recited by others never sees a sign of blessing. One who recites Havdalah over wine or hears it recited by others is called holy by the Holy One, blessed is He, and made His treasure, as it is stated, 'You shall be My treasure from among all the peoples,' and, 'I distinguished you from the peoples.' Rabbi Yochanan said: Three inherit the World to Come, one who lives in Eretz Yisrael, one who raises his sons to Torah study, and one who recites Havdalah over wine after Shabbos. This applies when he saves wine from Kiddush for Havdalah. Therefore, if one has only one cup and it does not contain enough for both Kiddush and Havdalah, he should reserve it for Havdalah and recite Kiddush over bread, because when the Sages instituted Havdalah, they instituted it over wine. Tur. The Shulchan Aruch rules this way below in seif 4; see there.
2) It is also stated in chapter 2 of Shevuos: Rabbi Chiya bar Abba said in the name of Rabbi Yochanan that anyone who recites Havdalah over wine after Shabbos will have sons. This is derived from the verse, 'To distinguish between the holy and the mundane,' alongside the verse, 'To distinguish between the impure and the tahor,' which is followed by, 'When a woman conceives.' Rabbi Yehoshua ben Levi says he will have sons fit to render halachic decisions, as it is written, 'To distinguish and to instruct.' Maggid Mishneh at the beginning of chapter 29 cites this statement as support for Rambam's view that Havdalah, like Kiddush, is a Torah obligation; see there. Mateh Yehudah at the beginning of this siman cites it.
3) There: The order of Havdalah is wine, spices, etc. Mateh Yehudah, paragraph 1, explains this order in the name of Tashbetz in Maamar Chametz. Wine comes first because the sense of taste is coarser than the other senses, perceiving an object only through physical contact. The sense of smell is more refined, because it smells from a distance. Sight is more refined still, because its perception reaches the heavens through seeing the stars. Havdalah embodies wisdom that distinguishes between the holy and the mundane, and was therefore established in Chonen HaDaas. Intellect is more refined than the other senses, because it apprehends angels. The apprehension of angels progresses from the simple to the profound. The senses are arranged similarly: the tongue that tastes is lowest; above it is the nose that smells; above it are the eyes that see; and above all is the mind that understands. See there. Thus, the order of Havdalah proceeds from below upward: first the blessing over wine in the mouth, then the blessing over fragrance in the nose, then the blessing over the lamp perceived by the eyes, and finally the blessing of Havdalah in the intellectual mind.
3) Our Sages instituted four distinctions: between holy and mundane, between light and darkness, between the Jewish people and the nations, and between the seventh day and the six days of labor. These correspond to the four husks alluded to in the verse, 'Behold, a stormy wind came from the north, a great cloud, and a flashing fire, with a brightness surrounding it.' Midrash Talpiyos, branch of Havdalah, folio 118d; see there. This means that the four distinctions are recited in order to subdue those husks. See Yafeh LaLev, paragraph 1.
4) There: The cup must not be blemished. Although the Gemara mentions only a cup of blessing, the cups of Kiddush and Havdalah are included among cups of blessing. Beis Yosef. See our words on siman 182, seif 3, where all the laws of a blemished cup are explained, and siman 183, seif 3, where all the laws of a whole cup are explained; see there.
5) There: The cup must not be blemished. The same applies to the requirement that it be whole, meaning that the cup must not be broken, as Tur writes. The same also applies to the other requirements for a cup of Birkas HaMazon. Maran Beis Yosef writes regarding the Kiddush cup above in siman 271: Rambam further writes there that it is subject to all the laws of a cup of Birkas HaMazon. It requires rinsing inside and out, must be full, held in the right hand without assistance from the left, and raised at least one tefach above the ground. These are his words. The Shulchan Aruch rules this way there in siman 271, seif 10; see there. This is obvious. Mateh Yehudah, paragraph 2.
6) There: The cup must not be blemished. A blemished cup may nevertheless be repaired by adding a little wine or water. Similarly, in pressing circumstances when one has no other cup, he may recite Havdalah over a blemished cup, as stated at the end of siman 182 and in our words there, with the help of Heaven.
7) Shaar HaKavanos writes that under no circumstances should water be added to the Havdalah cup. Pri Etz Chaim, gate 18, chapter 24, writes the same. It appears that this applies when another option is available. If it is impossible, such as when one has less than a revi'is and must add water to complete the revi'is, he should not omit Havdalah for this reason.
8) There, gloss: During Havdalah they should direct their eyes, etc. This refers to those answering amen. Tur, siman 299; Magen Avraham, paragraph 1. Levush writes: He should direct his eyes toward the cup and lamp, and all the listeners should likewise direct their eyes toward them, since the blessings concern these objects. These are his words. This implies that the person reciting Havdalah must also direct his eyes toward the cup and lamp.
