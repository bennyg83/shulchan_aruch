Havdalah. Rambam, chapter 29, writes that Havdalah is a Torah obligation like Kiddush. Some say it is Rabbinic. See Maggid Mishneh there and the end of this siman.
With the cup. Meaning those who answer amen. Tur.
Defective. Taz writes that this explanation is unintelligible. Magen Avraham explains that there is a defect and degradation to the cup of blessing when wine is poured from it before one drinks. Therefore, they pour before completing the blessing. Mateh Moshe writes that one should not pour during the blessing. Rather, when filling the cup, he should fill it to overflowing so that some spills upon the ground. Shelah, Hagahos Yayin Nesach, and Taz write similarly. Maharshal and Be’er Sheva write that the pouring should be abolished entirely because it degrades the beverage, as stated in siman 171. According to our practice, we must say that there is no concern when only a small amount is spilled. Magen Avraham.
From the cup. Meaning after drinking, as explained above, because otherwise it would constitute an interruption.
A mitzvah. Women are accustomed not to drink from the Havdalah cup. See Shelah for the reason. Shibbolei HaLeket writes that the person reciting Havdalah ordinarily drinks the entire cup and does not give any to the members of his household. He must sit while drinking it. This is from Mateh Moshe.
