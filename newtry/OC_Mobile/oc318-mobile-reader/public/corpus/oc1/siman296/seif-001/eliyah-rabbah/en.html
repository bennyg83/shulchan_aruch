[1] He should direct his eyes, etc. And likewise everyone, etc. However, Tur at the beginning of siman 299 implies that this refers to those listening.
[2] The custom is to spill, etc. Be'er Sheva, p. 120, objects from the rule that water is channeled before a bridegroom only in vessels because otherwise there is degradation, even when done as a sign of blessing. He concludes by praising the Sephardim who do not spill wine. Taz writes similarly and explains that the statement that every house in which wine is not spilled, etc., means that a person should not become angry if wine was accidentally spilled by a member of his household, because anger is a sign of poverty. It does not mean that the Sages instructed people deliberately to spill wine. It is possible, however, to distinguish that spilling a small amount of wine is not degrading, as Magen Avraham writes. Moreover, Mateh Moshe gives other reasons, including that one must give the assembly of Korach their share, analogous to mayim acharonim.
[3] [Levush] After he began, etc. Rama's wording is "before he finishes Borei Pri HaGafen, etc." It appears to me that both are required: after he begins "Baruch Atah Hashem Elokeinu Melech HaOlam," he should finish "Borei Pri HaGafen." Piskei Tosafot writes likewise, unlike Shelah, who says to fill the cup to its very brim so that it will spill, and unlike Mateh Moshe. Taz's objection can be answered based on what I wrote in siman 182.
[4] [Levush] He should drink, etc. Women have the custom not to drink from the Havdalah cup; see Shelah for the reason. Shibbolei HaLeket writes that the person reciting Havdalah customarily drinks the entire cup and does not give any to his household (Magen Avraham). Tur in siman 299 likewise cites Rav Saadiah that the household need not drink, etc. Bach explains that women and children were not given because Havdalah is rabbinic, but adult sons are obligated to drink. This does not appear from Shibbolei HaLeket, who says that the reciter customarily drinks it all, as Beit Yosef there also implies. Mateh Moshe, siman 509, however, writes that he drinks a cheekful and gives the household to taste, implying that he does not follow Rav Saadiah and Shibbolei HaLeket. Tur there cites Rav Amram similarly.
[5] [Levush] Nevertheless it is preferable, etc. Magen Avraham objects that beer may perhaps be preferable to blemished wine, for one was permitted to eat before Havdalah rather than recite Havdalah over a deficient cup, as stated in seif 3. On Motzaei Pesach too Havdalah is recited over beer because it is beloved. He therefore says Rama refers to a place where beer is not chamar medinah. This does not appear correct, because Rama and Levush are discussing chamar medinah mentioned immediately beforehand, and Levush's later exception for Motzaei Pesach makes this even clearer, for there too he himself later discusses chamar medinah. There is also proof from Mordechai, chapter Arvei Pesachim, who writes that it is preferable to recite Kiddush over bread rather than beer even when beer is chamar medinah, because it nevertheless does not have the status of wine. Furthermore, seif 3 speaks only when there is less than a revi'it, but with a blemished cup it appears that one may not eat and should instead recite Havdalah over the blemished cup, as Beit Yosef explicitly writes in the name of Rosh according to our ruling in siman 182 that after the fact one may bless over a blemished cup. Rabbenu Yerucham, end of Netiv 62, writes likewise. Abudraham somewhat implies otherwise, but his words are very difficult in light of Rosh in the chapter Elu Devarim and require study. It is also surprising that this contradicts his ruling in the laws of Birkat HaMazon that a cup is required only as the preferred manner of the mitzvah.
