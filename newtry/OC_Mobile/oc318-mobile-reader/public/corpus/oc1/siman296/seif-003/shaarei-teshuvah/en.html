(In Shulchan Aruch) seif 7 — Even if they heard, etc., and Be'er Heitev wrote in siman 235 in the name of Mahari Weil: one who heard the shaliach tzibur in borei peri hagafen and intended to fulfill with this blessing and the other blessings did not want to fulfill with the shaliach tzibur's blessing and says them himself — l'chatchila one should not do so, for he must hold the cup in his hand; and b'dieved he fulfilled since he intended to fulfill havdalah on the wine before him, which is the cup in the shaliach tzibur's hand; and Be'er Heitev wrote proof that l'chatchila the cup must be held in his hand, from responsa of Lechem Tzvi regarding a nazir guest and as written in responsa above — end of his words; Mahari Weil brought from Magen Avraham siman 219 regarding shehecheyanu on Yom Kippur and wrote from Beit Meir siman 183 who wrote it is proper that each bless for himself — there is no proof, for there the reason is as Rosh wrote that the blessing is long, etc., and therefore he wrote that whoever can fulfill with the shaliach tzibur's blessing and says himself has a blessing that does not require [the shaliach tzibur] even without the reason that there is no cup in his hand; and in Be'er Heitev he disagreed and brought from responsa of Maharshadam and Mabit and wrote: since in Shulchan Aruch above siman 6 he ruled not like their words and such is the custom — if so, here too one may bless all blessings and intend not to fulfill; and all the more per what Magen Avraham wrote siman 219 and likewise in Hagahot Yesh Nochalin regarding kiddush that each says quietly from the start of the blessing "Who has sanctified us" until the end and finishes before the one making kiddush answers amen, as siman 59 — end of his words; and apparently this contradicts what Mahari Weil and Be'er Heitev imply, that his intent is to do so l'chatchila even though there is no cup in his hand only in the makor's hand, and he intended in what he blessed in borei peri hagafen — and it appears there it is b'dieved, for it is burdensome that each household member make kiddush on his own cup independently. Therefore also in havdalah, even though the host's way is to make havdalah and discharge all household members, it is fine when household members say the havdalah blessing relying on the cup in the host's hand who makes havdalah — and Mahari Weil admits to this; and specifically regarding a shaliach tzibur who is accustomed to intend to discharge listeners, which is not so in havdalah at home where they are negligent and it is not his intent to discharge others — therefore better each says havdalah blessing independently and relies on the cup in the host's hand; or all rely on the host to discharge them in all blessings, for presumably the host intends to discharge all household members and those standing there to hear havdalah blessing like the shaliach tzibur in synagogue. And some practice that if the host makes havdalah intending not to fulfill with the havdalah blessing and blesses on spices and candle while the host makes havdalah, and afterward takes another cup and blesses borei peri hagafen and says havdalah blessing on it — and to me it appears that although the havdalah blessing is done properly l'chatchila, nevertheless he changes the order and the coin the Sages established in the havdalah order to say yotzer hame'orot as above; therefore one who wants to return for a cup to do the mitzvah properly should wait also with spices and candle to arrange them on a cup — and see below siman 298 one who has no cup, etc., implies when he has one he should not bless on the candle when he sees it, and likewise on spices — only do in order.
