3) There: The order of Havdalah is wine, spices, etc. Mateh Yehudah, paragraph 1, explains this order in the name of Tashbetz in Maamar Chametz. Wine comes first because the sense of taste is coarser than the other senses, perceiving an object only through physical contact. The sense of smell is more refined, because it smells from a distance. Sight is more refined still, because its perception reaches the heavens through seeing the stars. Havdalah embodies wisdom that distinguishes between the holy and the mundane, and was therefore established in Chonen HaDaas. Intellect is more refined than the other senses, because it apprehends angels. The apprehension of angels progresses from the simple to the profound. The senses are arranged similarly: the tongue that tastes is lowest; above it is the nose that smells; above it are the eyes that see; and above all is the mind that understands. See there. Thus, the order of Havdalah proceeds from below upward: first the blessing over wine in the mouth, then the blessing over fragrance in the nose, then the blessing over the lamp perceived by the eyes, and finally the blessing of Havdalah in the intellectual mind.
3) Our Sages instituted four distinctions: between holy and mundane, between light and darkness, between the Jewish people and the nations, and between the seventh day and the six days of labor. These correspond to the four husks alluded to in the verse, 'Behold, a stormy wind came from the north, a great cloud, and a flashing fire, with a brightness surrounding it.' Midrash Talpiyos, branch of Havdalah, folio 118d; see there. This means that the four distinctions are recited in order to subdue those husks. See Yafeh LaLev, paragraph 1.
