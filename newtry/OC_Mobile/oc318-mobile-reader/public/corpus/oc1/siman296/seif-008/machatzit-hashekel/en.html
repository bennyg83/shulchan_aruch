(ס"ק ח) He should not eat even, etc. — and see above section 474; meaning the novelty of the Rif — even though one must eat a Yom Tov meal at night, nevertheless in this case the Yom Tov meal is postponed. And Magen Avraham hinted to see there — he wrote if one did not eat on Shabbat night he may complete it on Shabbat day, and likewise Rama; and presumably the same on Yom Tov; see Magen Avraham explicitly in section 491 — but he mainly hinted to section 474 for our case where the Rif's novelty is not clear since in 474 it is explained one does not discharge Shabbat meal except with bread, and certainly on Yom Tov; if he has bread for the night meal, per Rama here seif 2 the first reason is primary that on Yom Tov falling on Motza'ei Shabbat since they make Kiddush on bread they also make Havdalah on it — why then say he should not eat at night when he can make Havdalah on bread and eat the night meal; if he has no bread, what novelty in what we teach that even on Yom Tov he must postpone the meal until tomorrow — he cannot fulfill Yom Tov meal without bread anyway; now that he has no wine he is forbidden other foods too but no novelty that Motza'ei Shabbat is more than weekday. The Rif follows his view like the second opinion in seif 2 that even Yom Tov on Motza'ei Shabbat one does not make Havdalah on bread — see Magen Avraham above siman 254 s.k. 23.
