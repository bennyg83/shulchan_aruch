Women are obligated. Although Havdalah is a time-bound mitzvah, men and women are equal regarding the laws of Shabbos, since “remember” is juxtaposed to “observe,” as explained at the beginning of siman 271. Havdalah is also included in “remember” according to the view that it is a Torah obligation. Even according to the view that it is rabbinic, it was instituted in the pattern of Kiddush. The dissenting opinion maintains that because Havdalah is recited after Shabbos, it is not inherently part of the laws of Shabbos and is therefore treated as an ordinary time-bound mitzvah from which women are exempt.
They should not recite Havdalah for themselves. The Bach writes that even according to the view that women are exempt, they may voluntarily accept the obligation and recite Havdalah for themselves, just as they recite blessings over shofar and lulav. Magen Avraham concludes that this is the primary ruling. Although women customarily do not drink from the Havdalah cup, when no one else is available to discharge her obligation she should recite Havdalah herself and drink, so that the mitzvah not be neglected.
From the men. If the men have already recited Havdalah or fulfilled their obligation in the synagogue, they should not recite it solely for women unless adult or minor males are also present to listen, because according to the dissenting opinion this would be a blessing in vain. Some poskim permit one who has already recited Havdalah to repeat it for women, but there is no need to enter a doubtful situation when a woman can recite it herself. See Biur Halachah regarding whether a woman reciting Havdalah for herself is obligated in the blessing over the flame. See references 100, 133, 589.
