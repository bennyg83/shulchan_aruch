Borei Nefashos. See Baer Heitev and Birkei Yosef, who writes that the great authority and wonder of the generation, author of Batei Kehunah, wrote in the margin of Yad Aharon that he saw people wait until the beverage cooled, etc. He wrote as follows: This is not the normal manner of drinking it, they do not prepare it in this way, and this is not its usual mode of enjoyment or proper preparation. Such individuals disregard the practice of the world, like the case of kutach concerning which the Gemara says, “If he burns it,” etc. See Menachos 70a, Tosafos to Bechoros 23b, Rambam, chapter 14 of Hilchos Maachalos Assuros, and chapter 8, halachah 16, together with the words of the Maggid Mishneh, that consumption must be in its normal manner. See also what my teacher Peri Chadash wrote in Yoreh Deah, siman 103. The case cited by the Rosh in the chapter Kol Shaah concerns only a Rabbinic prohibition and was treated stringently. See Mishneh LaMelech, chapter 8 of Hilchos Maachalos Assuros, and my teacher Peri Chadash in responsum 10. From all this it is evident that those meticulous individuals accomplished nothing. At least regarding the blessing, their practice is disregarded in relation to normal human practice. End of his words. In my humble opinion, this requires study. How can the principle that an individual practice is disregarded apply to the after-blessing of Borei Nefashos? Even one who drinks water because he is thirsty recites Borei Nefashos afterward. If he means that because this is not the normal manner of enjoyment, the person’s practice is disregarded and he should not recite Shehakol beforehand, that too is clearly incorrect. One certainly recites a blessing over any degree of benefit, unless the item actually harms him. Otherwise, a blessing is required. In our countries people are not so particular to drink it extremely hot, and the principle that one’s practice is disregarded does not apply. Therefore, at the end of the drinking, when it has cooled somewhat, he can drink a reviis without interruption within the time required to drink a reviis, and properly recite an after-blessing. This is especially so because many poskim hold that even for drinking the relevant interval is the time required to eat a pras. It may also be argued that since this is its normal manner of drinking, the intervals do not count as an interruption. See below, siman 210, Taz, regarding whiskey. The works cited by Baer Heitev and other authorities discuss this at length. It appears that if one is uncertain whether he interrupted for the duration of drinking a reviis, then although in cases of doubt concerning blessings we rule leniently, he should nevertheless correct the situation by drinking a reviis or eating something whose after-blessing is Borei Nefashos, thereby discharging the coffee as well. See Responsa Shemesh Tzedakah, siman 2, containing responsa by the author and his son. They conclude that one should recite the blessing. See there at length.
