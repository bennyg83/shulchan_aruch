Cheese. If one blessed on cheese borei peri haAdamah, one has not fulfilled one's obligation. HaLeket vol. 1 siman 238.

And spoiled. That is, a little — for if it spoiled completely, one does not bless on it at all. Beit Yosef.

And regarding broth. Of meat; whereas broth of fruit — see above siman 202.

Herbs and mushrooms. They grow from the air.

And regarding kora (palm shoots). For people do not plant the tree with intent to eat the kora when it is soft, only that it should harden and become a tree. And it appears to me that regarding shoots of legumes called in Arabic "shotin" there is a distinction: those that are sown in a garden — one should bless borei peri haAdamah even on the shoots, for thus they were sown to eat them alive; but those sown in fields to leave until they harden and cook — on those one should not bless borei peri haAdamah but shehakol until after their cooking. Taz — see there; and in responsa Pnei Meiros siman 65 he disagrees and wrote one should not distinguish, and on all one blesses borei peri haAdamah.

Barley. And on coffee and tamar also shehakol — see above siman 202 small seif 19.

Wild grasses. Specifically regarding grasses he said thus, for they are not significant in themselves nor on account of planting, for they grow without sowing. But good fruits such as jagodas from Poland — one should bless on them as written in siman 203 seif 1.

Anise. And if one cooked it, one blesses on its liquid borei peri haAdamah. Magen Avraham seif 14 — see there.

Cumin. Cumin in foreign tongue.
