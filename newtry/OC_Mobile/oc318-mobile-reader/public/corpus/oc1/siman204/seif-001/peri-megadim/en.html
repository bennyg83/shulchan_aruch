Bach concludes regarding Borei Pri HaGafen that if the taste is that of vinegar but the smell is that of wine and no surface film has formed, or even if a film has formed but the taste remains that of wine, one recites Borei Pri HaGafen. If a film has formed and the taste is that of vinegar, one recites SheHakol. Regarding Kiddush, however, if the taste is that of vinegar, or if the disqualifying film alone has formed, one does not recite Kiddush over it. The fact that Kiddush may be recited over wine containing small white specks after it is strained does not contradict this, because the white film described here is more substantial than those specks; alternatively, the term refers to the beginning of souring. See there. For practical halacha one should rule like Taz in siman 272, seif katan 3, and Magen Avraham, seif katan 2, agrees. This is why the Mechaber there did not mention the taste explicitly: it is self-evident that one does not recite Borei Pri HaGafen when it has the taste of vinegar, even if the film has not formed. The Rama's statement can be understood as applying even in the morning, and it does not acquire the status of chamar medinah. See there, seif 9. A white film disqualifies wine for Kiddush and is not comparable to the small white specks. See Magen Avraham, siman 272, letter 4, who agrees with Bach on this point.
