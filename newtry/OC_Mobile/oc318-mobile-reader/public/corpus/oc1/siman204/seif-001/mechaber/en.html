The laws of the blessings for other foods. It contains thirteen se'ifim.
Upon something that does not grow from the ground — such as the meat of a domesticated animal, a wild animal, or a bird, fish, eggs, milk, cheese, bread that has become moldy, a cooked dish whose form has changed and become spoiled, and novlos, which are dates that the heat cooked and scorched and dried out — and upon the govai [a type of locust], upon salt, upon brine, upon soup, upon truffles and mushrooms, upon kora {which is the soft [shoot] added to a tree each year, called palmito [Tur]}, upon vine-shoots, upon sweet almonds that one eats while they are still soft in their shells, upon chaziz, which is unripe grain, upon a raw gourd, upon barley flour, upon date-beer and barley-beer, upon barley-water that is cooked for a sick person, upon field-grasses that are not sown, upon dill, which is called anito {i.e., anis}, and upon cumin and coriander {which are made for flavoring and not for eating}, and upon vinegar mixed with water such that it is fit to drink — one recites "Shehakol."
