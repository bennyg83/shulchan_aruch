And moldy bread — see in Mishna Berurah, and know that it is explained in Rambam that all of these whose berachah is shehakol have borei nefashot as their final berachah, and it is proven there that this also applies to moldy bread. And it would seem that since it is still fit for eating somewhat, how can one exempt oneself from Birkat HaMazon? Granted, regarding HaMotzi, which is d'rabbanan, since they are not significant they said one should bless only shehakol; but regarding Birkat HaMazon, which is d'oraisa, how is one's obligation removed? For at least under duress it is fit to eat. And in the gemara 36b they compare berachah to Yom Kippur, and regarding Yom Kippur it appears in Yoma that eating under duress is called eating, as they say there [chapter 82] that when one ate grape tendrils he is liable — and it is reasonable that grape tendrils are not complete eating but only under duress. And likewise it is proven explicitly in siman 202 seif 2 that for berachah it is called eating and one blesses on it its appropriate berachah. In our matter, because it is not significant they lowered its level, but it is not reasonable that because of this one is entirely exempt. And perhaps one could say that specifically when one did not eat the amount of satiation — one's obligation of Birkat HaMazon is only d'rabbanan in general; but when one ate the amount of satiation — one's Birkat HaMazon is d'oraisa, and one is obligated to bless Birkat HaMazon since under duress it is fit for eating. And even though initially one blessed shehakol — we do not care about that, and similar to what Shulchan Aruch ruled regarding pat haba b'kisnin in siman 168 seif 6 — see there. And it requires study for practical application.
