(א) And moldy bread — and it spoiled somewhat through this; for if it spoiled completely and is not fit for human eating, one does not bless on it at all, as below regarding a cooked dish.
(ב) And spoiled — it also speaks when it spoiled somewhat and not completely; for if not, even shehakol would not apply to bless on it [Beit Yosef].
(ג) That were cooked and burned, etc. — meaning through burning by heat they were cooked and dried and changed for the worse; and see what we wrote above in siman 202 seif 9 in Mishna Berurah and Biur Halacha.
(ד) And on locusts (gobai) — it is a species of kosher locust; and to exclude R' Yehudah who said in the mishnah: anything that is a cursed species [that come on account of a curse] — one does not bless on it.
(ה) And on salt — since one has at least some benefit from it when one puts it into one's mouth:
(ו) And on broth (merak) — of meat; whereas broth of fruits and vegetables depends on a dispute — see in siman 202 seif 10 and seif 11:
(ז) Chamaim — those found under the ground and created from the fat of the earth; and mushrooms grow on trees:
(ח) That it is soft — of a palm tree: when its branches grow each year, the addition in this year is soft and fit for eating, and in the second year it hardens and becomes like wood:
(ט) The addition, etc. — for although it grows from the ground, nevertheless we do not bless on it even borei peri haAdamah but shehakol, for people do not plant the tree with intent to eat the korah (palm heart) when it is soft, for it diminishes the branches of the tree — only that it will harden and become wood and grow fruit. Taz wrote that wet legumes in garden [plots] that are sown with intent to eat them raw — one blesses borei peri haAdamah even on the shoots (sharvitin) alone, for with that intent they are sown; but field [legumes] whose custom is to leave them until they dry and eat them cooked — one blesses on the legumes when they are raw, even when they are wet, shehakol; and if one cooked them, borei peri haAdamah. And Peri Megadim is very doubtful of his words, and likewise is the view of many latter authorities that one should not distinguish between legumes and legumes — and one always blesses on them when they are wet borei peri haAdamah, and such is the custom of the world [Shelah]. However, regarding the shoots there are opinions among the latter authorities: when one eats them alone without the legumes — whether one blesses on them borei peri haAdamah or shehakol; therefore l'chatchila it is good to be careful to eat them with the legumes and bless on the legumes alone borei peri haAdamah and not bless again on the shoots; and if it happened that one ate the shoots alone — bless shehakol:
(י) Grape tendrils (lulvei gefanim) — for this reason too, as with korah:
(כ) Sweet [almonds] — and bitter almonds; see above in siman 202 seif 5:
(ל) When they are soft in their shells — and even though we hold above in siman 202 seif 2 regarding all other trees that once they produce any fruit it is immediately considered fruit — that case is different, for one benefits from the body of the fruit for which they plant it; but here with sweet almonds in their youth one does not benefit from the fruit but only from the outer shell, and people do not plant them with intent for the shell but with intent for their pits when they ripen; and it is comparable to korah mentioned above:
(מ) Spoiled grain (shachat) — grain that did not reach a third [of growth] and is fit for eating; and because the fruit is not finished, one descends one level from borei peri haAdamah to shehakol; and the same applies to all fruit of the ground that is not finished and is fit for eating — shehakol [Magen Avraham and Sha'arei Teshuvah]:
(נ) Live [meat] — which is better cooked than raw; therefore when one eats it raw its blessing changes to shehakol, as below in siman 205 seif 1:
(ס) Barley flour (kemacha de'se'orim) — even what is made from ears that were dried, for they are somewhat good for eating; and some say that "barley" — the same applies to flour of all five species of grain, as explained below in siman 208 seif 5. And the reason he specified barley is that one might have thought that since it is hard on the worms in the intestines one should not bless on it at all — it teaches us that since one has benefit from it, one must bless [Gemara]:
(ע) And beer of barley — since it is clear and stands for drinking, it is not considered in the category of a dish of the five species of grain to bless borei minei mezonot, for one does not bless borei minei mezonot except on food, as written in siman 208 seif 6. And it is implied in Tosafot daf 38 s.v. hai that even if they contain an olive-sized amount within the time to eat a half-loaf — nevertheless we do not bless on them except shehakol [Mishna Berurah]:
(פ) And on barley water, etc. — also for the above reason, for it is made only for drinking:
(צ) Wild herbs, etc. — and even if they are good to eat, like a grass called [shtzav in German] that are sour leaves — and even after they are cooked, since it is food for kings, one blesses shehakol, for it is not considered so much as fruit; but on salad greens (shalaten) and the like that are sown — one blesses borei peri haAdamah [Chayei Adam]:
(ק) That are not sown — specifically regarding grasses that are not important on their own account nor on account of sowing, for they grow without sowing; but fruits that are good for food, even if they grow in forests on their own, like peaches and the like — one should bless on them borei peri haAdamah:
(ר) And on cumin — it teaches us. And it is called in foreign tongue "Elianderi":
(ש) That are made for taste, etc. — and it speaks whether moist or dry; and likewise if one compounded them in honey — also shehakol [Eliyahu Rabbah and Peri Megadim]:
(ת) And not for eating — however, if one boiled the anise, one blesses on its liquid borei peri haAdamah, for therefore it stands to impart taste to something cooked in it:
(23) Until it is fit to drink — for although at its beginning it was wine, once it changed and became vinegar it lost its importance.
