Unripe dates, etc. The Tur defines them as a type of dates that do not ripen on the tree. Perishah explains that their ripening is not completed on the tree, but at the time of the blessing they are already fit to eat because they are placed in straw or dried in the sun, and the heat ripens them, as Rashi explains regarding dates ripened by heat. This is also brought by Beit Yosef at the beginning of siman 202. When Beit Yosef criticizes Rabbenu Yerucham's explanation that these are a species of fruit whose ripening was incomplete and therefore receive Shehakol, his intent is that Rabbenu Yerucham appears to say they remain unripe even when one wishes to eat and bless over them, which is incorrect. Perishah thus understands the Tur and Shulchan Aruch as giving the same explanation. Bach, however, writes there that these are two different explanations, as I wrote in siman 202 seif katan 13. The words of Lechem Mishneh cited above also contradict Perishah, and the matter requires study.
Truffles are those found beneath the ground and formed from the richness of the earth. Mushrooms are another type that sometimes grow on trees, as Rabbenu Yonah writes. Taz writes that pods of legumes called pea pods that are sown in a garden receive Borei Pri HaAdamah. Those sown in a field are planted so that they will harden, and therefore even those pulled while still moist receive Shehakol.
[Levush] See above, siman 202, etc. This means that there it was stated that after the fact one has discharged his obligation if he recited Borei Pri HaEtz over water in which fruits were soaked or cooked. One must say that there the fruit's own flavor is perceptible in the drink, whereas in beer the flavor of the barley or dates is not perceptible at all. So writes Bach.
[Levush] For they are not sown, etc. Olat Tamid requires study: the berries that grow on brambles in siman 203 seifim 3 and 4 are likewise not sown, yet one does not recite Borei Pri HaAdamah over them. See above, siman 203 seif katan 2.
And over cumin, etc. Bach asks why this differs from siman 202 seif 16 regarding pepper, ginger, zedoary, and the like, which are also intended for flavoring, and if eaten by themselves receive no blessing. He answers that perhaps the meaning is that anise, coriander, and the like, even if fit to eat by themselves while fresh, receive only Shehakol because they are grown for flavoring, not eating. Therefore, when dried and no longer fit to eat by themselves, if cooked in honey they still receive only Shehakol. They differ from spices, which while fresh are grown for eating. It appears from Bach that when anise, coriander, and the like are eaten by themselves no blessing is recited at all. Perhaps when fresh he agrees that one recites Shehakol. Olat Tamid's quotation of Bach does not imply this. If they are preserved in sugar, one should likewise rule like Bach and recite Shehakol. Magen Avraham implies that even when eaten by themselves one recites Shehakol because they are pleasant to the palate. It appears to me that Bach's view is primary.
