1) [Seif 1] Over something that does not grow from the earth, etc. One who eats raw egg yolk to clear his voice should recite a blessing, since it nourishes him, even though he does not enjoy its taste. If, however, one mistakenly recited a blessing over something thinking it was sweet and discovered it was bitter, eating it does not prevent the blessing from having been in vain, since he derives no enjoyment. Machazik Berachah, paragraph 1; Shaarei Teshuvah, paragraph 17. Regarding the concluding blessing over egg yolk, I have already written in my responsum Be'er Mayim Chaim that it has the law of food and its measure is a kezayis. Regarding egg white, there is uncertainty whether its measure is a kezayis like food or a reviis like a beverage. We therefore wrote there that one should eat less than a kezayis so as not to become obligated in a concluding blessing, or drink a reviis at once and recite the concluding blessing. If one ate a kezayis of yolk together with white, he should eat or drink something whose concluding blessing is Borei Nefashos and intend to cover them; see there.
2) There: Milk and cheese. If one recited Borei Pri HaAdamah over cheese, Halachos Ketanos, volume 1, siman 238, writes that he did not fulfill his obligation. See, however, Eruvin 27b, which considers everything produce of the earth except water, salt, and fish. The Rambam in chapter 7 of Hilchos Maaser Sheni, law 5, writes that honey, eggs, and milk are likewise included among produce of the earth because they derive from things that grow from the earth; see there. Regarding truffles and mushrooms, Berachos 40b likewise states that they grow from the earth, and the Shulchan Aruch rules accordingly in Yoreh Deah, siman 217, seif 23 and elsewhere. It therefore appears that since they are considered produce of the earth, after the fact, if one recited Borei Pri HaAdamah, he fulfilled his obligation. Likewise, if one recited Borei Minei Mezonos over any food, he fulfilled his obligation, because in the Torah's language everything except water and salt is called nourishment. Chayei Adam, rule 58, paragraph 3, and Nishmas Adam there; Ben Ish Chai, parashas Balak, paragraph 13.
3) There: It spoiled. This applies specifically if it spoiled somewhat. If it spoiled completely, since it is unfit to eat, no blessing is recited over it. Beis Yosef; Taz, paragraph 2; Magen Avraham, paragraph 1. The same applies to moldy bread that remains somewhat edible. Eshel Avraham, paragraph 1; Chayei Adam, rule 49, paragraph 3.
4) There: And fallen dates, etc. Bach rules that the same applies if they fell from the tree before completing their ripening. Magen Avraham, paragraph 2. See above, paragraphs 65 and 66.
5) There: And over the govai. This is a kosher species of grasshopper. Rashi on Berachos 40b; Derishah, paragraph 5.
6) There: And over salt and salt water. This refers to salt that is somewhat fit to eat and is not excessively bitter or astringent. Likewise, salt water refers to a case in which much water was added, making it fit to drink. Otherwise, no blessing at all is recited.
7) There: And over broth. This refers to meat broth, for there is a dispute concerning fruit broth, as stated above in siman 202, seif 10. Lechem Chamudos, chapter Keitzad Mevarchin, paragraph 52; Magen Avraham, paragraph 3. One who drinks broth with lemon water for medicinal purposes recites Shehakol. If he mixed into the broth an item that he ordinarily enjoys eating, for medicinal purposes, then even if that item is the minority and most of the mixture is broth, that item is primary. He recites its appropriate blessing, which covers the subordinate broth. Birkei Yosef in Shiyurei Berachah, paragraph 1; Shaarei Teshuvah, paragraph 17.
8) There: And over truffles and mushrooms. Truffles are those created beneath the ground from the earth's richness. Mushrooms are another type that sometimes grows on trees. Rabbeinu Yonah; Derishah, paragraph 8; Eliyah Rabbah, paragraph 2; Eshel Avraham, paragraph 4. In Bavel, the practice is not to eat mushrooms. I heard that the reason is that they contain worms. Therefore, in a place where people eat them, they must be examined lest they contain worms.
9) There: And over the palm shoot. This is the growth added to the palm's branches. During the first year it is soft and fit to eat, but afterward it hardens like wood. Tur. Beis Yosef explains that since the palm is planted principally for dates, the palm shoot is not considered fruit. Therefore, one recites only Shehakol over it.
10) It appears that a distinction applies to legume pods called green peas. Those sown in gardens with the intention of eating them raw with their pods require Borei Pri HaAdamah even over the pods, since they were sown for this purpose. Those sown in fields to remain until they harden and are then cooked do not require Borei Pri HaAdamah even if uprooted while moist; one recites Shehakol until they are cooked. Taz, paragraph 4; Eliyah Rabbah, paragraph 2; Shulchan Gavoah, paragraph 1. Responsa Panim Meiros, siman 85, however, writes that no distinction should be made and that Borei Pri HaAdamah is recited over all of them; see there. Yad Aharon, Hagahos HaTur, cites this. Even HaOzer writes the same, but says that Shehakol should be recited over the pods because they are not the principal fruit. If one recited Borei Pri HaAdamah over the legumes, however, he need not recite Shehakol again over the pods; see there. See Shaarei Teshuvah, paragraph 5, who writes that although Yaavetz justifies the words of Taz, universal practice follows Panim Meiros and Even HaOzer, making no distinction. Regarding pods called sugar peas, which many people chew together with the peas, it appears that all agree; see there.
11) There: And over sweet almonds, etc. The reason is that when they are soft people eat their outer peel, but they are not planted with the intention of eating them while soft. They are planted to eat their kernels after they ripen. Therefore, one recites Shehakol over them. Beis Yosef in the name of Rashba; Taz, paragraph 5; Magen Avraham, paragraph 10. See above, siman 202, seif 5, and our words there, paragraph 49.
12) There: And over unripe grain, etc. This is grain that has not reached one-third of its growth. Since the fruit is incomplete, one recites Shehakol, descending one level. If so, the same applies to any ground produce whose fruit has not completed its growth: one recites Shehakol. Magen Avraham, paragraph 7. Tree fruit, however, is significant, and once fruit emerges one recites Borei Pri HaEtz. Eshel Avraham, paragraph 7. This applies when it is neither bitter nor astringent and can be eaten without difficulty, as stated above in siman 202, seif 2, and in our words there, paragraph 34; see there.
13) There: And over unripe grain, etc. This applies specifically to unripe grain growing wild. Over that grown in a garden, one recites Borei Pri HaAdamah. Tosafos on Eruvin 28b, beginning with the words, 'And this'; Ateres Zekeinim, paragraph 3.
