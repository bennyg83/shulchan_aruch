There, seif 9 — one ate a food or drank a drink of prohibition because of danger, etc. NB: behold, among the rishonim two views are found on this — see on this in the Taz and Magen Avraham. And in my humble opinion, one can bring proof from the Talmud that one is obligated to bless on it initially and finally — from what we recite in tractate Shevuot 26b: Ravina asked Rava — one swore on bread and is in danger, what is the law? And he challenges there: one in danger — let him permit it for him, sir — see there in Rashi's explanation. And it is difficult: why does he challenge? Let us say the question is regarding berachah — for regarding other prohibitions, since prohibitions are deferred before a sick person and are not permitted, it is forbidden to bless on them initially and finally. But regarding an oath — behold Rashi wrote in the end of chapter 8 of Berakhot that "deferred" applies only where permission is not stated explicitly in its body; but where permission is stated explicitly regarding it, it is not considered deferred but only permitted, because it is not stated in it "this is a general rule" — see there. If so, here too — since it is written in the oath "man" except for one who is forced — therefore the prohibition of the oath is not stated at all regarding one who is forced; therefore it is not deferred but only permitted; and therefore he asks: if the forced circumstance was renewed afterward — for example that he is in danger — shall we say that this too is in the category of forced that the Torah wrote "except for one who is forced," and automatically it is a real permission and requires berachah? Or perhaps the Torah did not say "except for one who is forced" except if at the time of the oath his heart was forced — they forced him; but if the forced circumstance was not renewed except afterward, then it was not permitted and it is only like other prohibitions that are deferred because of danger, and automatically it does not require berachah — and what does he challenge, let him permit it for him, sir? And perforce all forbidden food in a place of danger requires berachah, and therefore there is no practical difference regarding berachah, and therefore he challenges properly, let him permit it for him, sir. And behold, this cannot be permitted — for why does he challenge? Perhaps the question is whether it is permitted for him to eat thus if he needs permission, since he can ask about it; and therefore he asks whether it was truly permitted and we do not need to return for permission and he does not need to ask, or it is only deferred and automatically one needs to ask. And likewise there is difficulty even according to Rashi's explanation — for the question stands whether he needs a korban, that it is intentional shevuah or not; and what does he challenge? One could say that even though there is danger, nevertheless it is intentional shevuah, for it was in his power to ask about it. But this is the resolution: perforce the danger itself was not an opening to ask about it — for it was born; and if he has another opening or regrets — in such a case, where it is possible through asking, there is no doubt that he certainly needs to ask, for even if we say prohibitions were permitted before a sick person, that is when there is danger; but where there is no danger they were certainly not permitted. And if so, since it is possible through permission it is considered as though there is no danger here; and from this they feed the lighter first, and all the more where there is real permission — certainly in no manner may one feed them prohibition, since because permission is possible it is as though there is no danger. If so, here too — since it is possible through asking, again it is considered as though there is no danger at all, and certainly one needs to ask, and there is no general doubt in this. But our difficulty stands well — let us say there is a practical difference regarding berachah; and perforce it must be that on all prohibitions one requires berachah — and study well. However, per the Shach in Yoreh De'ah siman 238 that a sick person in a place of danger does not need permission at all — this proof is rejected — see there. However, the Shach concludes there that one should permit for him; and it appears thus, for behold all prohibitions are deferred before a sick person and are not permitted, and therefore one certainly should permit for him — and this is clear; study well.
