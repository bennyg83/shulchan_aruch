(9) They refrain, etc. It deals with a case where it does not become complete vinegar until it harms in drinking — only a taste of vinegar is in it — and they refrain from it; for if not so, one does not bless on it at all, even shehakol — and this is simple. And from this it will be explained that it is not like Sefer Or Chadash, who wrote — and these are his words: wine that soured, and likewise all drinks, until people refrain from them, etc., in siman 204 — end of his words. For it is not so — regarding other drinks on which one blesses shehakol, its berachah remains until it harms in drinking; and it is possible that it deals with drinks on which one blesses borei peri haGefen, as above in siman 202 — for then one blesses only shehakol. Furthermore, Or Chadash wrote — and these are his words: vinegar that one throws on the ground and it does not boil — it does not have the status of vinegar, and one blesses on it — Bach siman 205 — and it implies thus explicitly in Yoreh De'ah siman 123, end of his words. And it is astounding, for it is not found thus in Bach there; also from Yoreh De'ah there it is proven to the contrary — that the reason there is because we are not expert whether it is vinegar, therefore they are stringent regarding yayin nesech until it boils; but regarding berachah, if we are not expert we follow the lenient approach not to bless. And furthermore, it is explained there that three days before it boils it is certainly complete vinegar — and how can one bless, perhaps it will boil within three days? And this is what Levush and the poskim here wrote — the measure is when it harms, it is complete vinegar — and study well; also Magen Avraham was not precise on this — see there.
