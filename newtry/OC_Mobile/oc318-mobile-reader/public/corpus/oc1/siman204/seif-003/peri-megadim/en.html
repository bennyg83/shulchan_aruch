And withered fruit. Taz — at first glance his words are wondrous; and in our chiddushim on Berakhot 42 we challenged this; and in Olas Tamid Raavad note 13 he aroused on this that certainly withered fruit in Berakhot is bushlei kemara, and withered dates of the first chapter of Demai — there they argue: some say tamarei dezikai, and conclude in the difficulty for one view bushlei kemara. And what the Taz wrote on Lekach Yashar also tamarei dezikai shehakol is difficult — Raavad 42b: other trees when brought out are fruit and borei peri haEtz; and therefore conclude in Berakhot that all agree on bushlei kemara that the sun burned them and they spoiled and changed to inferiority and are like better alive than cooked — shehakol; and as Magen Avraham wrote in Raavad note 21 — see there. And on the Tur Lekach Moshe explained that "they do not finish on the tree" means forever; and this is bushlei kemara that afterward one places them in straw baskets or in the sun and they finish through the sun — like grape tips, shehakol Berakhot 39b; and this is Beit Yosef on Rabbeinu Yerucham that "its cooking did not finish" implies that if they were standing on the tree they would finish; and if so borei peri haEtz on other trees when brought out; and this is that there is no relevance to "their cooking is not yet finished." And in Perishah I saw he explains Beit Yosef's attack on Rashi, who explains withered fruit whose cooking is not yet finished — shehakol; and this is that even if they finished now through the sun after they fell, nevertheless shehakol. And per Beit Yosef's view in Tur Berakhot 4 and Shulchan Aruch likewise "cooked and the heat burned them" means afterward they finish through sun heat; and from the language "the heat burned them" it implies taste changed to spoilage and is not fit to cook on the tree, but the sun spoiled them; and so in Perishah, as Magen Avraham wrote in Raavad note 21. And until here he challenged thus that Beit Yosef should challenge the Tur thus; and we already wrote on this. And so as not to attribute to our master the Taz zt"l, Heaven forbid, words written without attention — we wrote in our chiddushim on Berakhot 42: one can say that initially he thought tamarei dezikai that fell before their cooking finished — for if after cooking finished, whence the legs to say shehakol? — and he rejects from Demai "curse of middle ages": middle ages is not a curse but that the wind detached them, etc.; but after he concludes in Demai date withered fruit they argue — and this is tamarei dezikai that never cook forever: if the wind detached them, and the owner — how do we know this in a buyer's circumstance? It is not recognizable; from ma'aser, dates that never cook are recognizable and called tamarei dezikai, for they are not gathered — only left on the tree and the wind detaches them, for they are unimportant; if their cooking is not yet finished, such withered fruit certainly are not tithed — possibly see Maaserot 1:2 Rashba in the name of Yerushalmi — see there; and per this view, withered fruit in Berakhot can also discuss thus, like grape tips Berakhot 39b shehakol; and the kind "curse" is fine — this kind never cooks forever; and he says for one view that Demai tamarei zikai is — a fortiori Berakhot includes dates, both zikai and bushlei kemara, etc.; and the content of our intent is that it seemed tamarei zikai are those the wind detached before their cooking finished — difficult borei peri haEtz, etc.; unlike what concludes in Demai, where he necessarily states that they never cook forever, as the Tur wrote; and this is what the Taz wrote initially "they did not cook on the tree," and afterward wrote this type cannot cook on the tree. And still requires study. And the conclusion per this view is that the Taz challenged Beit Yosef: why did he not write withered fruit that never cook forever, and that this is Rashi's explanation? And one can say: true, he relied on grape tips, and in Rashi it implies not yet finished; and therefore Yoreh Deah Rezah note 5 extended; and in our chiddushim we extended on this.
