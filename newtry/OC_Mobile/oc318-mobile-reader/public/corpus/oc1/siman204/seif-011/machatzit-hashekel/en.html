(siman 11) For taste, etc. — and see Bach who was strained and explains that here it speaks when they are sauced; but if they are truly dry one does not bless at all, like pepper and ginger; rather since if one ate them moist one blesses shehakol since they are made for taste — if so, likewise when sauced one blesses shehakol as if they were moist — and so wrote Magen Avraham. And I do not know how these two laws are hinted at — when dry not to bless at all like pepper and ginger. And Magen Avraham holds they are not comparable: specifically pepper and ginger and the like that are not fit to eat in their dry state — therefore one does not bless on them; whereas anise and coriander even dry are fit for eating, therefore even when dry one blesses on them; but since they are made for taste when dry, therefore one blesses shehakol — and in this Shulchan Aruch speaks. And also when they are moist or sauced — Bach holds one blesses shehakol since made for taste; on this too Magen Avraham disagrees and holds one blesses borei peri haAdamah — for also peppers are made for taste and nevertheless moist or sauced one blesses borei peri haAdamah, and even borei peri haEtz would be fit to bless were it not for taste, as Rashba wrote cited in Magen Avraham siman 202 siman 35.
