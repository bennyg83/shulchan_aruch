(7) [Levush] And its taste is vinegar, etc. Bach explained: specifically when it is also visible to the eye that it soured — and this is when a film forms, which they call in Ashkenaz "kainik," upon which a white film grows; but without a visible white film, even if its taste is vinegar, one does not bless shehakol. And the Taz at the beginning of this siman objected to him that he was not precise, for kainik is valid even for kiddush, as appears in siman 272 regarding wine in the mouth of the barrels, etc., end of his words. And one may wonder on him that he did not see in Bach siman 272, note 6, who resolves this himself — that there its smell and taste are wine, and in the mouth of the barrels alone there is flour in it, which is not so here, where the wine itself is bad and its taste is vinegar, and because of its badness a white film forms.
