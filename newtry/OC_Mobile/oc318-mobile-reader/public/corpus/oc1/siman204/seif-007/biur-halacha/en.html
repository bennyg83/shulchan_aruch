One who drinks water for his thirst, etc. — they say in the gemara: to exclude what? To exclude where his mother choked him — and Ra'ah explained in his novellae on what the gemara said "to exclude what" — that people do not drink water except for their thirst, meaning that stam people do not drink water except for thirst because water has no pleasure from them without this, as he himself explains there. But if one knows about oneself that one did not drink for thirst — that one has no pleasure at all from the drinking and drinks because of some reason, as we will illustrate below — he too admits that one does not bless, for it is comparable to one who drinks because his mother choked him. And likewise the language of Rambam in chapter 8 of Hilchot Berakhot, halachah 1, teaches thus: one who drinks water not to quench his thirst does not require a berachah — neither before nor after; and he omitted what the gemara said "his mother choked him" because he holds it is not precise but only the usual manner of speech is used — for otherwise why would he drink? — and the same applies to all similar cases. And I found in Az Yashir in Adrat Eliyahu who wrote thus explicitly, and these are his words: "for his thirst" means specifically for his thirst, but not for thirst one does not bless at all; and what they said "his mother choked him" is not precise but rather all who do not drink for thirst — end of his words. And likewise in Sefer Tosefet Shabbat in his collection. And therefore, if one drank water only in order to combine for Birkat HaMazon [for Magen Avraham who ruled in siman 197 that it helps for combination] or if one has a doubt in berachah whether one is obligated in a final berachah and wants to drink a revi'it of water in order to become obligated in borei nefashot — if one knows about oneself that one has no pleasure at all from the water, one is not obligated to bless on them, and automatically also for combination it is not considered combination at all. And see what we wrote in Mishna Berurah that if one moistened one's mouth one enjoys the drinking — in general it is for thirst. And know further that it appears from the words of the poskim [see in the siddur of Rav Amram and in the novellae of Rashba, Berakhot 41, and other rishonim] that if one drinks water to moisten the food in one's intestines, this is called drinking for thirst; and even though we rule in siman 174 that during the meal one does not bless on water that one drinks — there is a practical difference from this regarding where one drinks after Birkat HaMazon in order to moisten.

His mother choked him, etc. — see in Mishna Berurah, and it appears that the same applies if one drinks water in order to lower the food to its place (for sometimes it happens that the food stands in the middle and it seems to one as though it presses against his heart) — this too is included in this category, since one does not drink for pleasure.
