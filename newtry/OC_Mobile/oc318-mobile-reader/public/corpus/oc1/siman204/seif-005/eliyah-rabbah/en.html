(5) And on cumin, etc. Bach challenged: what is different from siman 202 seif 16 regarding pepper, ginger, and citron — which are also made for taste, and if one eats them in their raw state one does not bless on them at all? And he resolves: perhaps this is what it means — anise, alexanders, and all these — even if they were fit to be eaten in their raw state while moist, their berachah is only shehakol, for they are made for taste and not for eating; therefore now, when dry and not fit for eating in their raw state, if one cooked them in honey one does not bless except shehakol, and it is not comparable to spices that are made for eating while moist, end of his words. And it appears to be his view that one does not bless at all on anise, alexanders, and all these when they are in their raw state; however, it is possible that when they are moist he concedes that one blesses shehakol — and the account of Olat Tamid does not imply thus in his copying of Bach's words; and if they were spiced, too, one may rule like Bach and bless shehakol; however, from Magen Avraham it implies that even in their raw state one blesses shehakol, for they sweeten the palate — and it appears to me that Bach is primary.
