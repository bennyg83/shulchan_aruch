(10) There — and regarding shoots of legumes that are called in Arabic "shotin" — there is a distinction: those that are sown in gardens in order to eat them alive in their shoots — one blesses borei peri haAdamah even on the shoots, since they were sown for this intent; but those that are sown in fields to leave until they harden and they cook them — one does not bless on them borei peri haAdamah even when uprooting them while moist, but only shehakol until after they are cooked. Taz, note 4; Eliyah Rabbah, note 2; Orach Mishpat, note 1. However, in responsum Panim Me'irot siman 85 he wrote that one should not distinguish, and on all of them one blesses borei peri haAdamah — see there; and Elya Rabbah brought this in Hagahot Tur. And likewise in Eshel Avraham; but on the shoots he wrote to bless shehakol because they are not the main fruit — only if one blessed borei peri haAdamah on the legumes, one need not return and bless shehakol on the shoots — see there; and see Shaarei Teshuvah, note 5, who wrote that although the Aleph Yod settles the words of the Taz, nevertheless the people follow Peri Megadim and Eshel Avraham who do not distinguish. And those shoots that are called in Arabic "tzukar" — many chew the shoots together with the legumes — it appears that all agree — see there.
