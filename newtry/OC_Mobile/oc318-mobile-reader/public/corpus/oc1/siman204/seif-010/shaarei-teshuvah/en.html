The primary ruling appears to follow Magen Avraham, especially since Rabbeinu Yonah, cited by Beit Yosef, holds that one recites a blessing over vinegar. Regarding the law in Yoreh Deah, one may say that this is not the normal manner of libation, and the stringency there is only because we are not expert in determining it. See there, and see what I wrote in Yad Ephraim on this matter. Regarding the gloss to seif 5, that the wine must not be only one part in six parts water, see the words of my grandfather in Bircas Shmuel on Bava Basra. He writes: I heard someone question the custom of placing so much water upon raisins that the raisins are only one part in six of the water. If so, the wine should be nullified in the water, as stated in siman 204. Yet Menachos 55a states regarding dried figs that since one can cook them and restore them to their former volume, they are measured according to their size before cooking. The same should apply to raisins. We see that after cooking or steeping in water they expand to twice their former size or more, and they should therefore be measured accordingly. One must only ensure that there is not so much water that, even after the raisins have expanded, they are still only one part in six of the water, for then the blessing would certainly be in vain. End of his words. See also what I wrote in Yad Ephraim. According to this, his statement that after expanding they should not be only one part in six is not precise. Even at a smaller ratio one should not recite the blessing, because the expansion depends primarily upon the moisture absorbed by the raisins. See there. See also Responsa Chacham Tzvi, siman 140, regarding dough kneaded with raisin wine and whether it is considered fruit juice. He writes that since raisin wine is produced through human action, it does not have the power to change the essential character of the water. See there.
