10) It appears that a distinction applies to legume pods called green peas. Those sown in gardens with the intention of eating them raw with their pods require Borei Pri HaAdamah even over the pods, since they were sown for this purpose. Those sown in fields to remain until they harden and are then cooked do not require Borei Pri HaAdamah even if uprooted while moist; one recites Shehakol until they are cooked. Taz, paragraph 4; Eliyah Rabbah, paragraph 2; Shulchan Gavoah, paragraph 1. Responsa Panim Meiros, siman 85, however, writes that no distinction should be made and that Borei Pri HaAdamah is recited over all of them; see there. Yad Aharon, Hagahos HaTur, cites this. Even HaOzer writes the same, but says that Shehakol should be recited over the pods because they are not the principal fruit. If one recited Borei Pri HaAdamah over the legumes, however, he need not recite Shehakol again over the pods; see there. See Shaarei Teshuvah, paragraph 5, who writes that although Yaavetz justifies the words of Taz, universal practice follows Panim Meiros and Even HaOzer, making no distinction. Regarding pods called sugar peas, which many people chew together with the peas, it appears that all agree; see there.
