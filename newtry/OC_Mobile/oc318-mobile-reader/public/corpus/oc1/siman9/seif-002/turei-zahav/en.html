“They exempt in all kinds of garments.” Meaning: from the Torah the mitzvah of tzitzis requires two white threads and two tekheles threads — and tekheles is wool — nevertheless [wool] exempts [when tied to] linen, for so it is written explicitly regarding tekheles; by the same token two white wool threads exempt on linen. Since the Shulchan Aruch ruled like the Tanna of R. Yishmael that other materials are only rabbinic, obviously wool exempts on them — and with this, best is a linen or wool garment, for with those one makes tzitzis even from wool and linen together, as the Merciful One permitted kilayim in tzitzis, as it says “gedilim,” etc., after “you shall not wear shaatnez”; not so other species that have no biblical obligation — therefore no kilayim permission there except specifically wool or linen. Now that we have no tekheles, it follows that even on wool or linen garments we cannot perform tzitzis kilayim is not permitted for us — hence forbidden for us wool tzitzis on a linen garment or the reverse. For those who hold (previous clause) that all garment kinds are biblically obligated — even with other species wool and linen strings are permitted by Torah like Rava we cited — in our time [still] forbidden for the reason we stated [no tekheles].

“Not to make linen tzitzis.” The Tur wrote: Semak wrote — however it is proper to be careful not to make linen tzitzis on silk — end quote. Beit Yosef explained the reason: he holds like R. Tam that a linen tallis is exempt from tying tzitzis on it, as stated shortly — therefore Semak wrote “on silk,” meaning one of the other species. One could ask: our Tur concludes afterward permitted linen tzitzis on a linen tallis. It appears to me Semak’s words per Tur [mean]: Geonim and R. Tam forbade linen for tzitzis altogether — nothing exempts but wool specifically — for the reason Beit Yosef cited in the name of Rif [against] those who forbid — but Rif rejected them by explaining the sugya, namely Rava’s words “min kanaf” — how to interpret the Talmud, beginning of Yevamos; Rif’s view that linen also exempts — as Tur also wrote afterward; Semak concludes: granted one may rule like Rif that linen exempts — that is on a linen garment where tzitzis can only be of linen — [but] if one has only a linen tallis he annuls tzitzis — as Beit Yosef cited Rosh responsa about that country’s linen tallis — therefore we may rely on Rif who permits; but on silk, since wool is possible, why act against those who forbid with linen tzitzis that is not tzitzis at all — therefore proper to be careful — and Semak’s wording “there are two explanations — however proper to be careful” hints at the two views we mentioned — he concludes this matter. The Tur’s version of Semak did not have the word “even” as in our texts — therefore he explained: on linen there is no prohibition — the caution is not to make linen tzitzis as above per Tur — correctly — therefore he cited Semak before the dispute of Geonim and Rif for this purpose — if he cited at end one might explain Semak as “even on silk and certainly linen” — truly other versions yield conclusion to be careful even on linen tallis — therefore he cited first to teach: specifically on silk be careful — but on linen valid like Rosh’s conclusion — as above.
