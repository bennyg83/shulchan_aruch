(6) Of wool, etc. Bach writes that some authorities prohibit even woolen tzitzis on garments of other materials because the threads do not match the garment, as explained in Beit Yosef. Therefore he would recite a blessing only upon a wool tallis with wool tzitzis. The common custom, however, is to place wool tzitzis on garments of other materials. Eliyah Rabbah permits this, and Maaseh Rav also implies this practice.
(7) In the present time. When techeiles was available, four threads were placed, two white and two techeiles, techeiles being wool dyed with the blood of the chilazon. Since the mitzvah was then performed completely, the Torah permitted the two white threads to be wool or linen, whether the garment was wool or linen. According to Rama's view in seif 1 that garments of all materials are obligated according to the Torah, wool and linen threads together were likewise permitted in those garments. Today, without techeiles, the mitzvah is not performed in its complete form, and the Torah did not permit kilayim.
(8) Even for other materials. The reason is that a type of wool resembles silk. One might therefore place linen tzitzis on a wool garment, thinking it is silk, and create kilayim. According to this reason, other materials that do not resemble silk could be permitted. Other reasons are cited by Beit Yosef, Bach, and Taz, and according to them one should be stringent for all materials. In pressing circumstances one may rely upon the lenient authorities, as Eliyah Rabbah writes. Maaseh Rav indicates that Gra was also lenient.
