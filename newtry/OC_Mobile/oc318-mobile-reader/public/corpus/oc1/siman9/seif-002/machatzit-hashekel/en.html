(Seif katan 2) At all. The reason, etc. Beit Yosef cites Mahari Abuhav's explanation of Semak's warning not to make linen tzitzis. There is concern that one may place linen tzitzis on a wool garment because silk may appear to be a type of wool. Beit Yosef understood this to mean that silk resembles wool, and objected that a silk tallis does not resemble a wool one. Magen Avraham therefore explains the reverse: there is a type of wool that resembles silk. The statement that silk appears to be wool means that a silk garment resembles a known variety of sea wool. Tosefes Shabbos asks why silk tzitzis should then exempt a silk garment, since one might mistake a wool garment for silk and use silk tzitzis upon it. He answers that when both materials are before us together, their distinction is apparent, comparable to the law in Choshen Mishpat at the end of siman 45. This is well said. Chavos Yair, responsa 28 and 30, adduces further proofs. In my humble opinion, however, this does not fully resolve the matter. It should then also be forbidden to sew a silk garment with linen thread, lest one mistake the sea wool for silk and sew it with linen, thereby creating shaatnez. One must therefore say that this is too remote a concern. The matter presently requires further study, as I cannot examine it fully now.
