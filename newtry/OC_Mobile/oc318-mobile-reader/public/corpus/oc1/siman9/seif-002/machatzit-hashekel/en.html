(Seif katan 2) The general reason, etc. Rabbi Yehudah brought in the name of Mahari Weil, who gave a reason for what Semak wrote — not to make linen tzitzis: lest one place linen tzitzis on a wool tallis, because silk resembles wool — so far. Rabbi Yehudah understood Beit Yosef to mean silk resembles wool and they will be confused. On this Rabbi Yehudah challenged: behold a silk tallis does not resemble wool — so far Rabbi Yehudah. Therefore Magen Avraham wrote the intent is the reverse — there is a kind of wool resembling silk; what he wrote "silk resembles wool" means silk [garments] look like the well-known wool from the seashore. In Tosefet Shabbat, in his glosses on the first folio of his book, he wrote: if so silk will not exempt silk, lest one think a wool garment is silk and make silk tzitzis; one may say when both are together before us there is certainly recognition between them — and such a case is in Choshen Mishpat, siman 45, so far — and he spoke well. See responsum Chavot Yair, siman 28; and siman Lamed-Dalet brought further proofs for this view. Even in my humble opinion this view does not suffice here, for it would still be difficult — then it should be forbidden to sew a silk garment with linen threads, lest they err with wool garments from the seashore that are [called] silk and sew them too with linen threads and it become shaatnez; therefore one must say it is a distant concern that they will err that it is silk — and I have no time now to examine this now.
