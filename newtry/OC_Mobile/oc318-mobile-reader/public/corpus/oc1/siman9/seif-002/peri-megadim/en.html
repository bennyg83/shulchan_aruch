Whether garments of other materials are obligated according to the Torah or D'Rabbanan has several practical consequences. First, it is preferable to make a garment that fulfills a Torah obligation. See seif 6, where even according to Rama this is preferable. Second, a doubt concerning such garments is treated leniently if their obligation is D'Rabbanan. Nevertheless, as I wrote in Yoreh Deah 11 and in Orach Chayim 467 and 489, one should not intentionally create a D'Rabbanan doubt, though this may help where he has no other garment or tzitzis. Third, where one owns many garments, perhaps he need not inspect one whose obligation is only D'Rabbanan, since the rule requiring clarification where it is easy may not apply. This requires qualification; see siman 8. Fourth, if one sits in public and a tzitzis breaks on a weekday, human dignity may override a D'Rabbanan obligation; see siman 13. Fifth, if one has silk and wool tallisos and first recites the blessing over the silk one, he must explicitly intend to exempt the wool one, rather than relying upon a general intention to wear both; see siman 8:12. Sixth, if one wears kilayim in tzitzis today, according to the view that the Torah still permits it and only the Sages prohibit it, he would not be disqualified as a witness according to the Torah, whereas transgressing a D'Rabbanan garment obligation may disqualify only D'Rabbanan. Seventh, one who wears a tallis without tzitzis may be compelled until he fulfills the mitzvah. These distinctions also affect disciplinary lashes, witness disqualification, and a tumtum. The question how such a person recites the blessing requires study.
