And some say On the Taz and see later authorities. In the Gemara, Menachos 39b — Rechava, Rav Yehudah, and Rava; and there is also another version: Chana of the school of R. Yishmael, who departs from this tanna. And the practical difference is whether other garments are biblical or rabbinic. (1) It is more fitting to make a garment from which one fulfills the obligation biblically; nevertheless in seif 6 it is fitting even for the other view — see there. (2) A rabbinic doubt is lenient: if there is some doubt regarding other garments whether they are obligated — nevertheless, as he wrote in Yoreh De'ah 11, Magen Avraham 367, letter 2 at the end, and Turei Zahav 489 in Magen Avraham and Taz 6 — one should not make a rabbinic doubt lechatchilah; however, when he has no other garment and no tzitzis at all [it is different]. (3) If he has many garments he need not check this one, for in rabbinic matters perhaps we do not say: wherever one can clarify easily, one clarifies — nevertheless this requires study; see above in siman 8 in Magen Avraham and Taz 8–9. (4) One sitting in congregation and one [string] snapped — if rabbinic, on a weekday one may say human dignity overrides rabbinic law; see siman 13 in Magen Avraham, letter 8. (5) If he has two tallisos, silk and wool, and blessed on the silk — he must intend explicitly to exempt the wool, not silently, even if he intends to wear both — see above in siman 8, seif 12, in Taz and Magen Avraham; proof from “mustard and olive” in Eliyah Rabbah 1 in Beit Yosef — see there. (6) If he wore kilayim at this time in tzitzis biblically — some say that now it is permitted only rabbinically; if so it does not disqualify biblically for testimony, and rabbinic garments disqualify biblically — I wrote this in the introduction. (7) If he wears a tallis without tzitzis that they prepare for him until his soul departs — if he made tzitzis and was not lashed, they lash him afterward with makos mardus; if rabbinic, they lash in Shevuos — there is a practical difference for disqualifying testimony rabbinically; see the introduction on this, and a tumtum has a practical difference too; see above — and it requires study how he blesses.
