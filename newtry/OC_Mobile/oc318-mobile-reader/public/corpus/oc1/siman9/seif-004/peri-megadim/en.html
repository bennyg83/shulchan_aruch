That one not On the Taz. I must copy the text of Semak in our possession — it is the text that everyone has, which Beit Yosef brought (DK year 1506, with God's help), and thus: in siman 31 (page 13a): “Kilayim in tzitzis is entirely forbidden for us, for we have no tekheles; and a linen tallis is exempt even in its kind. Wool and linen exempt both in their kind and not in their kind; other species exempt in their kind, and not in their kind they do not exempt.” In this there are two hundred years of commentators' explanations, and it is difficult in Menachos. Nevertheless it is proper not to make any tzitzis of linen, even on a silk tallis — end quote. There are three explanations in this. Beit Yosef explained that Semak rules like Ra'ah (Menachos 39a) that linen tzitzis are forbidden on a linen tallis — is this a night garment? Is this wool tzitzis? — and like Beis Shammai — see there in Menachos; on this Semak wrote, “nevertheless it is proper to be careful even on silk (and other species), for one does not attach linen at all to linen,” and although it is a decree upon a decree it is close, and even now a decree has not moved from its place. And now explain in Semak: kilayim for us is forbidden, etc.; linen tallis exempt even in its kind — not to mention wool, for kilayim is forbidden even in its kind; linen is forbidden — a decree has not moved from its place. And one could ask the word “even” — rather the difficulty is from the word “in its kind” — “in its kind” he should have said (see Derishah). Also difficult: what is this that he wrote “wool and linen exempt in their kind,” for linen is forbidden in its kind for linen and cannot be conceived at all — one must force “in their kind” as a scribal error in the language, and “wool” applies; and “not in their kind” — silk and the like — linen exempts; nevertheless it is proper to annul linen tzitzis even from silk, etc. And one could ask: (2) “even” — he challenged the Tur in two things: he should have brought the Geonim first and afterward cited Semak; and moreover a strong difficulty — it implies the Tur agrees with Semak, whereas the foundation fell — the halachah is not like Rabbeinu Tam and the Geonim; how much more so linen in silk is permitted. Also the word “in this” has two explanations without exposition. The second explanation — the Bach's explanation in my commentaries we expanded: the text does not read two “even”s, (1) (2), and explain: kilayim forbidden for us; linen tallis exempt in its kind — meaning its kind exempts it; linen for linen is fine; and tzitzis exempt not in their kind — in this there are two explanations: some say wool alone or linen alone; some say wool and linen together specifically exempt not in their kind (Hagahos Maimuni, chapter 3 of tzitzis, letter 6). If so, it is proper to be careful not to make linen for silk, lest together — and kilayim is now forbidden; and the same for wool — except precisely: behold linen for linen is permitted, to reject Rabbeinu Tam and the Geonim; and the Tur's words are precise, that he rules like Semak that linen in silk and other species — lest like Hagahos Maimuni, etc.; and for linen — meaning in its kind — and although the Geonim and Rabbeinu Tam forbade, we follow Semak in this, and the Rosh agreed. The third explanation is the Taz's (and similarly the Prishah) — the text does not read (2) “even,” and he explains like the Bach: exempt in its kind when one attaches linen to linen. However in what he wrote there are two explanations: they are the Geonim whom the Rif brought, beginning of Yevamos; and in Hilchos Ketanos Beit Yosef brought that some sages hold that for us tzitzis does not exempt linen at all, for linen tzitzis have no place biblically except tekheles wool, and white is also wool (see further in Beit Yosef and Bach, view of Razah and Baal HaItur); and Rava specifically said tzitzis exempt not in their kind, etc. And one could ask: Pesachim — for a linen tallis it does not help, for the garment biblically and linen have no place at all. Also there is Rabbeinu Tam's view apart from this, who says: although linen has a place, nevertheless the halachah is like Beis Shammai that a linen sheet is entirely exempt — a decree. Thus on a linen tallis there are two concerns: (1) the Geonim's view, some sages in the Rif, and Rabbeinu Tam's view, etc.; and on silk and the like there is only the concern of some sages, lest for us, not like Rava, linen does not exempt (possibly also for the view that other species are rabbinic, or Baal HaItur and Razah — understand this see later authorities). Therefore Semak wrote, “nevertheless on silk and the like,” since it is possible with silk or wool, one should be careful not to make linen — unlike on linen, although there are two concerns of Rabbeinu Tam and the Geonim, nevertheless since a linen tallis has no remedy with wool, and other species they did not permit linen tzitzis. The Tur ruled exactly like Semak, and his words “even” mean: on a linen tallis there is more concern — Rabbeinu Tam, Geonim, some sages — than in another matter, unlike silk. And that the Tur ordered thus and not the reverse — for “nevertheless it is proper to be careful on silk” is because of linen tallis, for Semak forbids on silk, and how much more on linen tallis (as the reading “even”) — therefore he placed Semak first, and the halachah is like Semak.
