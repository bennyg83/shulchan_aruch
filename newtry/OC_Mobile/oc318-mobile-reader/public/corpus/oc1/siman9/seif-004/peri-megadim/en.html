I must copy the text of Semak in our editions: “Kilayim in tzitzis is entirely forbidden for us because we have no techeiles, and a linen tallis is exempt even with its own material. Wool and linen exempt both their own and other materials; other materials exempt their own but not other materials. There are two explanations of this, and it requires study in Menachos. Nevertheless, it is proper not to make any linen tzitzis, even for a silk tallis.” There are three explanations. Beit Yosef understands Semak as ruling like Rabbeinu Tam that linen tzitzis are prohibited even in linen garments because of the decrees concerning night garments and wool tzitzis. “Even for silk” then extends that decree to other materials. This reading is difficult in several ways, including Semak's statement that wool and linen exempt their own kind and the Tur's apparent agreement with Semak despite permitting linen in linen. Bach's explanation deletes one occurrence of “even.” Semak means that a linen tallis is exempted by its own material, but there are two views whether wool or linen separately exempts other materials, or only wool and linen together. It is therefore proper not to use linen for silk, lest the latter view be correct; linen in linen remains permitted. This fits Tur, who rejects Rabbeinu Tam's prohibition of linen in linen. Taz, apparently following Perishah, offers a third explanation. The two views are those of the Geonim and Rabbeinu Tam. Some Geonim maintain that without techeiles linen tzitzis have no Torah function at all, while Rabbeinu Tam independently rules that a linen sheet is entirely exempt by decree. A linen tallis thus involves two concerns, whereas a silk garment involves only the Geonic concern that linen does not exempt other materials. Semak therefore warns against linen in silk where wool or silk can be used, but allows linen in linen because otherwise the linen tallis would have no remedy. Tur follows Semak: despite the additional concerns regarding linen garments, they cannot be remedied otherwise, whereas linen should be avoided for silk. This explains Tur's order and wording.
