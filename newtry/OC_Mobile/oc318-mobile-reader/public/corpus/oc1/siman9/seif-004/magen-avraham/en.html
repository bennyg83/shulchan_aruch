Of its own material. A tallis whose warp is wool and woof is silk, or the reverse, is exempt according to the Torah and obligated D'Rabbanan even if most is wool. This is unlike fibers blended together. Radbaz, responsum 199. He concludes that according to Maharam of Rothenburg such a tallis has no remedy. For this reason the custom in Ashkenaz is to prohibit entirely a garment blended from cotton and linen. Nevertheless, according to those who permit making a silk tallis, one should place wool threads upon it, not silk. One should not rely upon this alone, but should wear beneath it a tallis katan made entirely of wool, thereby fulfilling the mitzvah, and with the upper garment he will beautify himself; see responsum 244 and Hagahos Maimoniyos.
There is room for doubt. The threads are neither entirely the material of the corner nor entirely within “You shall make twisted cords for yourself from them.” Terumas HaDeshen writes this in accordance with his view that other materials are obligated according to the Torah, following Rava. According to Rabbi Nachman, however, other materials are obligated only D'Rabbanan. Why should it be worse if some of the tzitzis are wool, which exempts according to the Torah? See Rosh in the name of Baal HaIttur, who holds that according to the Torah wool is required, yet in silk garments no such requirement exists because any threads exempt them. Beit Yosef therefore requires study: in seif 1 he rules that other materials are obligated only D'Rabbanan, yet here he writes that the matter is doubtful.
