“Of its kind.” A tallis whose warp is wool and woof silk, or the reverse, is exempt by Torah law and obligated rabbinically even if the majority is wool; this is not comparable to mixed threads [Radbaz 199]. In the end of his words, according to Maharam of Rothenburg there is no remedy for this tallis. For this reason Ashkenazim treat a garment mixed with wool, linen, and cotton as strictly forbidden; still, those who are lenient to make a silk tallis should tie wool strings, not silk, and should not rely on that alone but make a small tallis that is entirely wool underneath in order to fulfill the mitzvah with it, and with that he may be lenient above—see siman 244 and Hagahos Maimoniyos, Dalet Beis, 1—see there.

“There is room to question it.” For “the corner shall be of the same kind” and “you shall make yourself twisted cords” are not entirely as Taz writes, and he follows his view that other materials are [obligated] by Torah law like Rava; but according to Rosh that other kinds are rabbinic, why should it be inferior when one ties some Torah-grade tzitzis? See Rosh citing Baal HaItur that by Torah law wool is required, yet for travelers no requirement at all—they discharge with anything—see there. If so, Rabbeinu Yerucham requires study: in his conclusion he ruled they are rabbinic, yet here he wrote there is room to question—requires study.
