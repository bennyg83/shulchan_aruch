[4] Levush: white tzitzis, etc. This is not because white threads are required, but because they are permitted. Levush's statement that two white wool threads together with two techeiles threads are valid in a linen garment means that since wool is required for techeiles, white wool is also valid; it is therefore all the more permitted. This follows the first view below. Levush further writes that in our time, when we lack techeiles, a partial positive commandment is not strong enough to override the prohibition of shaatnez. I do not know why he did not invoke Reish Lakish's rule in Menachos 40a: whenever a positive and negative commandment conflict, if both can be fulfilled, one must do so; only when that is impossible does the positive commandment override the negative. Today, without techeiles, both can be fulfilled, so the positive commandment does not override the negative. Levush himself gives this reason in the name of the second view. One need not object that this reasoning should also prohibit it when techeiles exists. Since techeiles must then be placed, its accompanying white wool is different, as the Gemara implies. Malbushei Yom Tov writes that he does not know who the “some say” are, and that the Gemara resolved the question that even linen tzitzis in wool are permitted. I later found Beit Yosef, siman 11, citing Rambam, who rules otherwise because the question was not conclusively resolved; this is astonishing regarding Malbushei Yom Tov. He further writes that the later “some say” is the rule of Reish Lakish, and that there is no dispute: according to the Torah the law follows the first view, but in practice one should not do so because of Reish Lakish. This interpretation is correct and is also implied by Beit Yosef, Tur, Semag, and Semak. The rule follows the first view at the Torah level, but because we can fulfill both commandments without shaatnez, linen tzitzis should not be used in wool garments and wool tzitzis should not be used in linen garments in our time.
