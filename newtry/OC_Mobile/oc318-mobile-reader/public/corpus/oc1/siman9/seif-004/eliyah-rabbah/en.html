[4] [Levush:] white tzitzis, etc. Not that they needed this, but he says they are permitted in this way. What he wrote — "and so it implies: two white wool threads with two tekheles threads on a linen garment," etc. — means that since wool is required for tekheles, white is also valid; therefore it is all the more so, and as below for the view of "some say" that this is permitted specifically. What he wrote that nowadays, when we have no tekheles, one should not rely on a partial positive commandment to override shaatnez, etc. — I do not know why he did not give the reason of Resh Lakish in the chapter on tekhelet, folio 40, who said: wherever you find a negative and a positive commandment, if you can fulfill both it is better; if not, the positive comes and overrides the negative. Here too, nowadays when we have no tekheles we can fulfill both, and therefore a positive commandment does not override a negative commandment. This reasoning he wrote himself in the name of "some say." One should not challenge: if so, in the time of tekhelet one should also have permitted the first view. One may say he holds that since tekhelet must be cast because of its exceptional nature, so it implies in the Gemara. What he wrote "and some say even," etc. — Malbush Yom Tov wrote: I do not know who these "some say" are; on the contrary, in the Gemara it was resolved as a question whether even linen on wool is permitted — so far. Now I found explicitly in Beit Yosef, siman 11, folio 14b, in the name of Rambam, who ruled and explained the reason because we do not hold plainly like the question mentioned above — see there; this is astonishing regarding the Gaon Malbush Yom Tov. "And some say also," etc. — I wrote in Eliyahu Zuta Malbush Yom Tov's wording: this is his language — namely Resh Lakish; it seems to me there is no dispute, but the essential law from the Torah is like the first view, and as Rabbah said in the Gemara — only that we say one should not do so because of Resh Lakish, that one can fulfill both. He should not have written "some say," but "this is Torah law, but the Sages, of blessed memory, said that even in this," etc. — so far his wording. In my humble opinion he intended the first explanation Beit Yosef gave there, like Rambam, who explains there that he speaks from the Torah. Malbush Yom Tov omitted all of Beit Yosef's words there.
