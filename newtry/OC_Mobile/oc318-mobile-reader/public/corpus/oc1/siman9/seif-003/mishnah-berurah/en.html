(9) Of other, etc. This law applies whether garments of other materials are obligated according to the Torah or only D'Rabbanan.
(10) Only their own kind. If the warp is one material and the woof another, the garment has no remedy except wool tzitzis, which exempt every garment, or linen tzitzis in pressing circumstances, as explained above.
(11) Such as silk. This applies only if the silk was spun and twisted for the sake of tzitzis; otherwise it is invalid.
(12) For a silk garment. Even if wool, linen, or another material was sewn beneath it, we follow the upper layer because it is the principal garment, as explained below in siman 10, Magen Avraham, seif katan 5.
(13) They do not exempt. If a tallis has warp of its own material and woof of wool, or the reverse, it should specifically have wool tzitzis, which exempt every material, not tzitzis of another material, even if most of the garment is that other material, because warp or woof alone is not considered a garment. Even if the wool threads are not individually distinguishable, such as where a small quantity was mixed throughout both warp and woof, it still appears preferable to use wool tzitzis, because some authorities maintain that threads are significant and are not nullified. It is preferable to make the entire tallis of wool without any admixture, thereby beautifying the mitzvah. A tallis blended from cotton and linen has no remedy, except that in pressing circumstances one may use linen tzitzis, as explained at the end of seif katan 8.
