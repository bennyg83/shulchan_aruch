(9) “Of other, etc.” — this law applies whether one holds other kinds are from the Torah or rabbinic.

(10) “Only in their kind” — and if the warp was of one kind and the woof of another kind, it is obvious that this tallis has no remedy except with wool tzitzis, which exempts all garments, or with linen tzitzis in a time of need, as stated above.

(11) “Such as silk” — and all this is specifically if the silk was spun and twisted for the sake of tzitzis; otherwise not.

(12) “For a silk garment” — and even if underneath it was sewn with wool or linen or another kind, we follow the outer layer which is the essence of the garment, as stated below in siman 10 in Magen Avraham sk 5.

(13) “They do not exempt” — and a tallis whose warp is of its kind and the woof is wool, or vice versa — one should place wool tzitzis on it specifically, for it exempts all kinds and not from other kinds, even if most of it was of other kinds, for warp or woof alone is not considered a garment, as above in note 1. And even if the wool threads are not recognizable by themselves, such as when he mixed a little wool thread in the warp and woof and its place is not recognizable — nevertheless it appears better to make tzitzis from wool threads, for some say threads are significant and are not nullified. And it is better that he make the tallis entirely of wool without any mixture of other kinds at all, and with this he may be lenient above. And a tallis that is mixed with cotton, wool, and linen — this tallis has no remedy unless in a time of need he makes linen tzitzis on it, as above at the end of note 8.
