(Seif katan 3) They do not exempt, and Bach, etc. — wool tzitzis, because some say, etc. — printing error; it should be "and some say on silk," etc. — this is another, more lenient view than Bach. There are three disputes in the matter, as will be explained; Bach's view is to be stringent, even per Baal HaEitur. He wrote this is the reason of "some say" in seif 6 not to make a linen tallis — "some say" also hold like Baal HaEitur: in the chapter HaTekhelet, Rabbi and Sages dispute — Rabbi holds tekhelet prevents white and white prevents tekhelet, for he expounds "corner, the same species as corner" — white threads, for most of their tallitot were white; then it is written "tekheles thread" — tekheles threads; on both the verse says "you shall see it" in singular, implying both are tekheles and white threads — one mitzvah; therefore they hinder one another. Sages hold they are two mitzvot and do not hinder one another; "you shall see it" in singular refers to each separately — white alone and tekheles alone. If it were Sages, they would have expounded "corner" as Rabbi expounded further — it would be more reasonable to expound "you shall see it" like Rabbi that they are one mitzvah; rather they expound "corner, the same species as corner," and it applies also after it — "tekheles thread" — meaning if one has no tekhelet he takes threads that are the corner's kind, i.e., the garment's kind, and also the kind of "tekheles thread," i.e., wool, for tekhelet is wool; nothing is missing except tekhelet dye — but in any case there must be wool, which is the tekhelet kind; therefore one does not make a linen tallis, and likewise other kinds, for one cannot fix tzitzis: if tzitzis are wool (on linen it is forbidden as kilayim) they are not the garment's kind; if tzitzis are the garment's kind they are not the "tekheles thread" kind — for two kinds are required: garment kind and tekheles-thread kind. Therefore one can exempt only a wool tallis with wool tzitzis — garment kind and tekhelet-thread kind. This is Bach's reason not to bless except on wool tallis and wool tzitzis, to satisfy Baal HaEitur. He also wrote: do not challenge that we establish Rav Nachman, who holds other kinds are obligated only rabbinically, from the baraita "wool and linen exempt all kinds" — one might think linen and wool together, which are kilayim, nevertheless the Torah permitted them in tzitzis — how can other kinds, rabbinic obligation, be permitted through a rabbinic mitzvah against kilayim prohibition? We answer: the baraita means wool or linen, where there is no kilayim, exempt all kinds — thus wool and linen exempt all garments. How did Baal HaEitur write specifically wool garment and wool tzitzis? One must say he holds like Maharam of Rothenburg, whom Hagahot Maimoni brought — he holds like the simple sense of the Gemara that only wool and linen together exempt all kinds, but wool alone or linen alone do not; and the Gemara's answer that wool alone or linen alone exempts all kinds is specifically for Rav Nachman, who holds other kinds are rabbinic — therefore they exempt in every case, for they said to obligate them in tzitzis, and they said wool alone or linen alone also exempts. But he ruled like Rava that other kinds are from the Torah — therefore he exempts only wool and linen together, not each alone. In this Bach's view is explained: even a silk tallis with silk tzitzis — do not bless. Then Magen Avraham wrote: some say on silk specifically silk exempts, not wool — Maharam of Rothenburg's view above, who does not hold like Baal HaEitur requiring corner-kind and tekhelet-kind, but holds wool and linen exempt only together, not each alone in other kinds; therefore a silk tallis has no remedy with wool tzitzis, but for him one may fix it with silk tzitzis since it is corner-kind though not tekhelet-kind. Then he wrote in the name of Terumat HaDeshen that the world is accustomed to make wool for silk and did not heed Baal HaEitur or Maharam, but hold the plain Gemara and as answered for Rav Nachman — and likewise for Rava wool alone and linen alone exempt all kinds. He concludes there in Terumat HaDeshen: one who wants silk tzitzis should be careful not to take threads as they are spun and woven from the store, for we require spinning and weaving by a Jew lishmah; rather take silk floss from the store and have a Jew spin and weave (lishmah), because I saw people not careful in this — I wrote to be careful, end of his words. See Rabbi Shlomo Kluger that some are stringent that carding also requires lishmah; but Rama wrote there the custom is to be lenient on carding. See siman 8, seif 13, seif katan 16 in the name of Agudah how to conduct the blessing on a silk tallis.
