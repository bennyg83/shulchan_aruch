(Seif katan 3) They do not exempt, and Bach, etc. The text should read, “and some say that with silk, etc.” This is a distinct, more lenient opinion than Bach. There are three views. Bach is stringent in order to satisfy Baal HaIttur. He explains the “some say” in seif 6, who prohibit making a linen tallis, as following Baal HaIttur. Rabbi and the Sages dispute in Menachos whether techeiles and white threads prevent one another. Rabbi understands “the corner” as threads matching the corner, usually white, followed by “a thread of techeiles,” and the singular phrase “you shall see it” combines them into one mitzvah, so they prevent one another. The Sages consider them two mitzvos and apply “it” separately to each. Baal HaIttur understands that the Sages require, when techeiles is unavailable, threads both of the material of the corner and of the material of techeiles, namely wool. Therefore only a wool garment with wool tzitzis can be fully remedied in our time. Linen and other garments cannot satisfy both requirements. This is Bach's reason for reciting a blessing only over a wool tallis with wool tzitzis. Bach further explains that the Gemara's answer to Rabbi Nachman's view, that wool or linen separately exempts all materials, applies only because other materials are obligated D'Rabbanan. Baal HaIttur rules like Rava that other materials are obligated according to the Torah, and therefore follows Maharam of Rothenburg's view that only wool and linen together exempt other materials. This explains Bach's position that even a silk tallis with silk tzitzis should not receive a blessing. The more lenient opinion cited by Magen Avraham is Maharam of Rothenburg: wool and linen exempt other materials only together, but each separately exempts only its own kind. Thus a silk tallis cannot be remedied with wool tzitzis, but can be remedied with silk tzitzis because they match the corner, even though they are not the material of techeiles. Terumas HaDeshen then reports the common practice to place wool tzitzis on silk garments, disregarding both Baal HaIttur and Maharam and following the plain conclusion of the Gemara that wool alone or linen alone exempts all materials. He adds that one who uses silk tzitzis must not purchase already spun and twisted silk thread from a shop, because spinning and twisting must be performed by a Jew for the sake of tzitzis. Rather, he should purchase raw silk and have a Jew spin and twist it for the mitzvah. See the beginning of siman 11 regarding the stricter view that even combing must be for the sake of the mitzvah, though Rama records the custom to be lenient. See siman 8:13, seif katan 16, in the name of Agudah, regarding the blessing over a silk tallis.
