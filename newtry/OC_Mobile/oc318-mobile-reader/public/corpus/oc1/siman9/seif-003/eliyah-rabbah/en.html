[3] Only in its kind, etc. Levush wrote the reason: it is written "the corner" — from this it implies "the same species as the corner," etc. — and Rashi did not explain it so in the chapter "With what may one light," folio 27; rather the entire word "corner" is superfluous, for it is written "on the corners of their garments," and it should have written "and they shall place on the tzitzis a tekheles thread." See Tosafot Menachot, folio 39, in the passage beginning "even," etc. Olat Tamid wrote — this is his wording — and in Knesset HaGedolah he wrote in the name of Radbaz: a tallis of wool with the warp of silk, or the reverse, is exempt from the Torah and obligated rabbinically even if the majority is wool; he concludes in the name of Maharam of Rothenburg that such a tallis has no remedy — therefore in practice it requires investigation — so far his wording. I examined Radbaz's responsum itself, siman 199, and saw that his reason is that Hagahot Maimoni wrote in the name of Maharam that wool and linen do not exempt other kinds, but only wool and linen together; wool alone does not exempt. Therefore this tallis has no remedy: tzitzis of wool alone do not exempt, and of silk also do not exempt because the warp is wool. Per this, Olat Tamid was not precise when he left it as "requires investigation," for in this siman he himself wrote that we do not hold like Hagahot Maimoni, but wool alone also exempts all kinds; if so, this tallis has a complete remedy with tzitzis of wool. Shulchan Aruch and Levush also ruled plainly that wool exempts all kinds — thus they do not hold like Maharam, and Beit Yosef wrote so explicitly. I am also greatly puzzled at Hagahot Maimoni for writing so in the name of Maharam of Rothenburg, and Agur and Terumat HaDeshen wrote so in his name and Beit Yosef brought it — yet in Maharam of Rothenburg's responsum, siman 844, he wrote explicitly: linen tzitzis on a tallis of shira'in do not help, for this is not "corner, the same species as corner," and the Torah said "a tekheles thread," meaning wool; but tzitzis the Torah did not specify. That which it says "you shall make for yourself twisted cords" — that is when both are together, two tekheles threads; but where one cannot cast both together, as in our case, one should make tzitzis only of wool, as it is written "tekheles thread," or of its kind, as it is written "corner, the same species as corner" — so far his wording. Behold, he holds explicitly that wool exempts all kinds because of "tekheles thread"; and that which he holds they exempt only together refers to linen, for linen exempts only in its kind — linen tzitzis for a linen garment, or even for another kind such as silk if the tzitzis are wool and linen together; but for us linen does not help except for linen, whereas wool exempts all kinds — and this is astonishing regarding these great ones. In Maharam's responsum my eyes were opened regarding Tur's words where he wrote in the name of Semak that one should be careful not to make linen tzitzis on silk — perforce he holds to be concerned for Maharam's view above that linen does not exempt other kinds; wool certainly exempts because of "tekheles thread." Therefore Tur also ruled that linen exempts on linen, from "corner, the same species as corner." Beit Yosef and Bach greatly expanded with many forced interpretations on Tur's words and left me room. The practical ruling on the tallis mentioned above: make tzitzis specifically of wool; silk does not exempt even if most of it is silk, for warp without woof or the reverse is not a garment; however, if one mixed wool and silk and made a garment from them, we follow the majority — if mostly wool it is all wool, if mostly silk it is all silk. A tallis mixed with linen and silk or other kinds has no remedy at all.
