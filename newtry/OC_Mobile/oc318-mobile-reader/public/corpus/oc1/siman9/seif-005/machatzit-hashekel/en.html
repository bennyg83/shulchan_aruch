(Seif katan 5) There is room for doubt, because neither “the corner,” etc. These are the two teachings derived by Rava, as explained above.
The corner, meaning the material of the corner. The verse also says, “You shall not wear shaatnez, wool and linen together; you shall make twisted cords for yourself,” meaning from wool and linen.
According to Rava. One must therefore fulfill one of the two verses completely: either all the threads must match the material of the corner, or all must be wool or linen under “You shall make twisted cords for yourself from them.”
But according to Rabbi Nachman, etc. Why should it be worse? According to him, the Torah requires specifically wool or linen tzitzis, while other materials are obligated only D'Rabbanan and the Sages allowed threads of other materials. If some of the threads are wool, which exempt according to the Torah, this should be preferable. See Rosh in the name of Baal HaIttur, cited above in seif katan 3.
Even so, for silk garments, etc. Meaning, for other materials the Gemara concludes according to Rabbi Nachman that either wool or linen exempts them, since the Torah imposes no tzitzis obligation upon them and the Sages who obligated them permitted any adequate material.
Even if it is not wool, as stated above in seif katan 3. This supports Magen Avraham's reasoning.
