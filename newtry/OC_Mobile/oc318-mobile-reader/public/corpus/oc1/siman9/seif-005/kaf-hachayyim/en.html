5) There, "But garments of other species," etc. {Rama: And some say that all of them are obligated by Torah law, etc.} In the Gemara, Perek HaTekhelet, 39b, there is a dispute whether garments of other materials are obligated in tzitzit by Torah law or only rabbinically. Rav Nachman holds that garments of other species are not obligated by Torah law, while Rava holds that garments of other species are likewise Torah-obligated. Rif and Rambam rule like Rav Nachman, and Maran rules likewise, following his usual method. Tosafot, Rosh, Sefer Mitzvot Gadol, and Mordechai rule like Rava, and this is Rama's position. There is also a dispute in Rava's view. Some hold that even according to Rava, wool or linen tzitzit exempt garments of other materials, and even wool and linen together, despite being kilayim, exempt them. Maharam, as cited by Hagahot Maimoniyot, Terumat HaDeshen, and Agur, however, holds that according to Rava, for garments of other materials that are Torah-obligated, only wool and linen together exempt them, which applied when tekhelet was available. Nowadays, wool or linen alone do not exempt garments of other materials; each material is exempted specifically by its own kind, such as silk tzitzit for silk and cotton tzitzit for cotton, because we derive from "the corner" that the tzitzit should be of the material of the corner. See Beit Yosef, Bach, and Machatzit HaShekel, seif katan 3. Therefore every God-fearing person should be stringent for himself and should place only cotton tzitzit on a cotton tallis, and likewise for other materials, in order to avoid the dispute. Shulchan Aruch HaRav, letter 4; Chesed LaAlafim, letter 2; Kitzur Shulchan Aruch, siman 9, letter 12; Ben Ish Chai, Parashat Noach, letter 1. See seif 6 and our comments there.
