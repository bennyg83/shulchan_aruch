Sheep — See Mishna Berurah in what he wrote "or rams"; so wrote all the later authorities. It is a printing error in Beer Heitev in what he wrote that ram's wool is exempt. And what he further wrote in Mishna Berurah that wool from a ewe that nursed a goat is not considered wool at all and exempts only its own kind—this Beit Yosef wrote; see Bechoros page 17 in Divrei Chamudos there. However, one who examines the Rambam's words seems from them that what he said "invalid for tzitzis" applies also to techeles; and Da'as Chachamim's proof from what he wrote—if it is dyed techeles does it reduce?—can be rejected simply: meaning it lost the quality of techeles and then it is only like white wool threads; and indeed the Gemara's wording there implies wool in any case did not lose its species. And what he further brought from what they said "whoever puts techeles..." is astounding—for because of this must we say wherever the word techeles is mentioned the intent is ordinary tzitzis? But we can actually reconcile Beit Yosef's words: therefore this wool does not exempt other species; for on this too we can say the Gemara's analogy—just as flax that was not changed, so wool that was not changed. But according to this it should be able to exempt a garment of wool from a ewe that nursed a ewe, since this too is called wool on it—and it needs study. But regarding reishis hagez it seems certainly wool from a ewe that nursed a goat is exempt, per what we conclude in Chullin page 137 that we require it be fit to stand in service, and we conclude in Bechoros that it is not fit to make techeles from it; if so, not fit to make the belt from it either. But then why does the Gemara challenge only on reishis hagez and not have the position of the tanna of R' Yishmael etc.?—for this question applies only to a goat that nursed a ewe, not more; it would have been better to challenge that it is not fit to make techeles from it and the challenge would fall also on a ewe that nursed a goat, and one could extend on this—but this is not the place. Simply the Gemara wanted to challenge even on what he did not yet know from the statements below. In Peri Megadim I saw in our topic a wondrous thing—that he wrote plainly even wool from a ewe that nursed a ewe but whose father was a ram is still called changed wool and not considered wool, even for one who holds we are not concerned for the father's seed; I do not know his source for this. According to his words there would also be a great leniency regarding kilayim; in my humble opinion it seems clearly the opposite, and perhaps there is some printing error in his words and one should not rely on this at all for kilayim, especially since the Gra's view in Yoreh De'ah siman 298 is that even wool from a ewe that nursed a goat is also considered wool but one is not lashed for it; see there. If wool was mixed with other species see Mishna Berurah; see Peri Megadim who was aroused regarding Magen Avraham's words—for from his words plainly we always follow the majority when two species were mixed, meaning if he mixed two species that are not similar in smell at all, such as wool with silk, and similarly camel wool with flax, etc.—this matter is disputed whether we follow majority; but one who examines Tur Shulchan Aruch Yoreh De'ah siman 299 will see plainly the law is with Magen Avraham, whose language there is: one who had wool and flax mixed, he brings another species and mixes it with them and nullifies one of them; he brought Beit Yosef that this law flows from the Yerushalmi that states: wool and flax that were mixed are forbidden—how does he do it? He brings a litra and another 102 grams and nullifies them—behold according to their explanation in the Yerushalmi the litra and more is to nullify either the wool or the flax, whichever is smaller in its measure; if so it clearly implies even if the wool was two litra and linen one litra and camel wool only a litra and more it is permitted. Per the above Peri Megadim he could not nullify the 102g in any way since they are not equal in smell unless camel wool increases specifically over wool. Later I found in Baal HaIttur Yoreh De'ah siman 299 in Chiddushei Ra'avan that he also brought in the name of Tashbatz like these words.

Only rabbinically — If he took a wool tallis without the required measure and sewed to it a piece of other species to complete its measure—it is exempt from the Torah per the Mechaber, for something recognizable does not apply nullification by majority; but if it has the measure he is obligated from the Torah. If it has Torah measure but the majority of the tallis is other species—apparently even the Mechaber obligates from the Torah, for something recognizable does not apply nullification by majority; but from Magen Avraham siman 10 sk 5 it seems we follow the main garment—so too in Peri Megadim. Woven talleisim of wool and other species with mixed threads of wool and other species in warp and weft—he concludes in Sefer Artzos HaChaim that here we follow majority: if the majority of threads is wool he is obligated from the Torah even per Mechaber, and if not, rabbinically [nevertheless it seems better to make wool tzitzis on it]; so implies Sefer Eliyah Rabbah that only regarding warp and weft we do not follow majority, for warp or weft alone is not considered a garment unlike here. If it is mixed of flax and other species it remains uncertain in Artzos HaChaim and Eliyah Rabbah which tzitzis to make on it.
