Only. See Taz. Menachos 39b and Rashi there discuss the derivations from kilayim, garment afflictions, impurity of creeping creatures, a corpse, seminal emission, and tzitzis. It is true that kilayim does not require a general analogy, because Shabbos 27b concludes that wool and linen are written explicitly. Tosafos explain that kilayim may nevertheless be superfluous for another exposition, so that kilayim and garment afflictions should not form two verses teaching the same rule. Otherwise Rav Pappa's statement would not have been called an error. Rashi on Shabbos 26b explains a general and particular formulation regarding garment afflictions, whereas kilayim is not such a formulation, since “garment” appears regarding wearing in Vayikra and “You shall not wear shaatnez, wool and linen together” appears in Devarim. General and particular rules are not formed from separate passages. I discussed this at length in my novellae to Shabbos. Rashi therefore says “and it specified in one of them,” meaning garment afflictions, not kilayim. I do not know why Taz mentioned kilayim, since Shabbos concludes that Rav Pappa's statement was erroneous. I had thought to explain that Rav Pappa follows his own view in Bechoros 17a. Rav Nachman bar Yitzchak explains that the wool of a ewe born from a goat does not contract garment afflictions, as Rambam rules. Rav Pappa explains instead that it is invalid for techeiles, based on the comparison to unchanged linen. Rabbi Acha bar Yaakov explains that one is not lashed for kilayim involving it. Perhaps Rav Pappa holds that the extra mention of wool and linen permits the comparison to unchanged linen, and garment afflictions can then be derived from kilayim without creating two independent verses. Yet Rav Ashi concludes in Bechoros that a vine trained over a fig tree is invalid, implying that the comparison applies without an extra verse. Since Ravina and Rav Ashi arranged the Gemara, Shabbos concludes anonymously that Rav Pappa's statement was erroneous. Taz therefore should not have cited kilayim.
