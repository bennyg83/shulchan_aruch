One is not obligated in tzitzis from the Torah except on a garment of linen or of sheep's wool; even wool from a ewe that nursed a goat is not called wool—it is partly fluff—and it does not exempt a garment from tzitzis except with its own kind (Beit Yosef in the name of the Rambam).

Some say that all of them are obligated from the Torah, and so is the halachah. Some say that nowadays one should not make any tzitzis except wool on wool; but besides all this it is a blessing in vain—even silk on silk, and all the more linen on linen, and certainly wool or linen on silk. A God-fearing person fulfills all opinions and blesses only on tzitzis of wool on wool, as stated later in seif 6. The meticulous, when wearing a wool tallit katan with tzitzis of wool underneath, wear a silk tallit on top and bless on both together—and this is proper (Bach).
