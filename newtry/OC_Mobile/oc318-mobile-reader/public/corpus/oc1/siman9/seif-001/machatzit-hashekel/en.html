(Seif katan 1) Wool, etc. Rams. See Yoreh Deah 298. The reason only sheep's wool is called wool is that the Torah requires a material without a qualifying name. Ordinary “wool” means sheep's wool. Camel wool or rabbit wool has a qualifying name, as stated in the Yerushalmi cited by Beit Yosef. Ram's wool has the same law as ewe's wool, because it too has no qualifying name and is ordinarily called wool. This is stated in that Yerushalmi and in Yoreh Deah 298 regarding kilayim, where wool without a qualifying name includes both ewe's and ram's wool.
It appears to me that wool, etc. Which he blended, etc. Meaning, while the materials were still raw wool, before being spun into threads, the minority is nullified by the majority. This is the Mishnah's case and is ruled in Yoreh Deah 299:1. Once the materials have been spun into threads, however, they are not nullified, as stated there and below in seif katan 4. Magen Avraham's statement that when they were blended the majority governs, and that an exact half is treated stringently, is also stated there in Yoreh Deah.
A ewe, etc. Rambam. This is stated in Bechoros 17a. Wool is compared to linen: just as linen has not changed, for every flax plant grows from another flax plant tracing back to the six days of Creation, so too the wool must not have changed since Creation.
A ewe from a ewe. Unlike a ewe born from a goat, whose changed wool is invalid for tzitzis. Since it is not the wool specified by the verse, it has the law of other materials and exempts only its own kind, namely a garment made from the wool of a ewe born from a goat. Know that the dispute between Beit Yosef and Rama whether other materials are obligated according to the Torah is the dispute of Rabbi Nachman and Rava. Rabbi Nachman follows the academy of Rabbi Yishmael, which derives ordinary references to “garment” from the laws of garment afflictions, where the Torah specifies wool and linen. Thus only wool and linen garments are obligated according to the Torah, while other materials are obligated D'Rabbanan. Rava derives from the juxtaposition, “You shall not wear shaatnez, wool and linen together; you shall make twisted cords for yourself from them,” that tzitzis may be made from wool and linen. He also derives from the superfluous word “the corner” that the threads must be of the material of the corner. These verses are reconciled by teaching that wool and linen exempt garments of every material, whereas other materials exempt only their own kind. The phrase “the corner” therefore concerns other materials. It necessarily follows that garments of other materials are also obligated according to the Torah, because the verse itself discusses their tzitzis.
