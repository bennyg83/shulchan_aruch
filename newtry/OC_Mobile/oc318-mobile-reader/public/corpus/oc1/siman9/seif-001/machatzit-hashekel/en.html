(Seif katan 1) Wool, etc. Rams — see Yoreh De'ah, siman 298. Meaning: for this reason only sheep's wool is called [wool], for we require that it have no qualifying name; plain "wool" is sheep's wool, unlike camel's wool or rabbit's wool, which have qualifying names, as in the Yerushalmi Rabbi Yehudah brought. If so, ram's wool also has the law of sheep's wool, for ram's wool also has no qualifying name and is also called plain "wool" — so too in the aforementioned Yerushalmi; and in Yoreh De'ah, siman 298, for kilayim we also require specifically wool with no qualifying name; he wrote there that both sheep's wool and ram's wool are called plain "wool."

And it seems to me regarding wool, etc., that were mixed, etc. — meaning: while they are still wool, before threads are spun from them, the minority is nullified in the majority, as in the Mishnah there, and so is ruled in Yoreh De'ah, siman 299, seif 1; unlike when threads were already spun from them, which are not nullified, as stated there — and likewise below, seif katan 4, so far. What Magen Avraham wrote on "when mixed" that we follow the majority, and if half-and-half we follow the stringent view — so too in that siman in Yoreh De'ah.

Ewe, etc. Rambam — and likewise in the Gemara, Bekhorot, folio 17: we compare wool to linen — just as linen that was not changed, any linen is superior to other linen retroactively until the six days of Creation, so too wool that was not changed since the six days of Creation.

Ewe from ewe. Unlike ewe from goat, whose wool changed and is invalid for tzitzis; since it is not the wool kind of the verse, it has the law of other kinds and exempts only in its kind — i.e., a garment made from wool of a ewe daughter of a goat. Know that the dispute of Rabbi Yehudah and Rama whether other kinds are obligated from the Torah is the dispute of Rav Nachman and Rava. Rav Nachman holds like the tanna of the house of Rabbi Yehudah, who holds that where tzitzis is written plainly "on the corners of their garments," we learn from garment plagues, where it is written "in a wool garment or in a linen garment" — here too "their garments" means specifically wool and linen are obligated from the Torah (and likewise wherever garments are written plainly it means specifically wool and linen garments); but other kinds are exempt from the Torah, yet rabbinically obligated. Rava derives from "you shall not wear shaatnez, wool and linen together — make twisted cords for yourself," and since the verse joins them: we expound "make for yourself" from them — from wool and linen; thus we require tzitzis specifically of wool and linen. Further we expound from "corner, thread" — the word "corner" is superfluous — to teach tzitzis must be specifically of the corner's kind, i.e., the garment's kind; if so the verses contradict one another. Therefore we expound wool and linen exempt all garment kinds, and other kinds exempt only in their kind; if so, "corner" comes to teach we require the same species as the corner regarding other kinds. Therefore we must say other kinds are also obligated from the Torah, for we establish the "corner" verse on other kinds.
