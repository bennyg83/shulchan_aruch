1) [Seif 1] "One is not obligated in tzitzit by Torah law," etc. Meaning a garment of sheep's wool. The same law applies to ram's wool. Beit Yosef; Magen Avraham, seif katan 1; Eliyah Rabbah, seif katan 1; Pri Megadim, Eshel Avraham, letter 1; Machatzit HaShekel, letter 1; Shulchan Gavoah, letter 1; Mor Uketziah; Kitzur Shulchan Aruch, siman 9, letter 12. What Baer Heitev writes in seif katan 1 contains a scribal error. I later saw that Maamar Mordechai, letter 5, writes likewise. 2) There, "Or of sheep's wool." Goat hair is called hair, not wool. Camel hair, although it is called camel wool, is not called "wool" without qualification; the same applies to rabbit hair. Beit Yosef; Olat Shabbos; Olat Tamid, letter 2; Machatzit HaShekel there. 3) There, "Or of sheep's wool." If a ewe is the offspring of a goat, its wool is invalid for tzitzit. Rambam, chapter 2. If so, it is not considered wool at all and does not exempt a garment except one of its own type. Beit Yosef; Olat Tamid, letter 2; Magen Avraham, seif katan 1; Pri Megadim, Eshel Avraham, letter 1. The same applies to a ewe that is the offspring of a ewe that itself is the offspring of a goat; Olat Tamid writes that its status is the same as the direct offspring of a goat. Pri Megadim writes similarly regarding a ewe whose father is a male goat and mother a ewe. Whether these types exempt one another, Pri Megadim leaves requiring study. 4) There, "Or of sheep's wool," etc. If sheep's wool is mixed with other fibers, we follow the majority. Magen Avraham writes that this is also implied by the last chapter of Kilayim. Machatzit HaShekel explains that Magen Avraham means while they are still raw fibers, before threads have been spun from them; at that stage the minority is nullified in the majority, as stated in the Mishnah there and as we rule in Yoreh De'ah, siman 299, seif 1. Once threads have already been spun, however, they are not nullified, as stated there, and so too below in seif katan 4. Shulchan Aruch HaRav, letter 6, writes likewise. If the mixture is half and half, Machatzit HaShekel writes that we rule stringently, as stated there in Yoreh De'ah. Meaning, he should use wool tzitzit, which according to the ruling in seif 2 exempts garments of every species. Shulchan Aruch HaRav writes likewise. According to those who hold that wool tzitzit do not exempt garments of other species, as will be explained below with God's help, such a garment would have no solution. Shulchan Aruch HaRav writes this as well. All this follows Magen Avraham, who holds that even when the materials are of different species we follow the majority in determining the garment's identity. Pri Megadim, however, writes that there is a dispute concerning this, and some hold that nullification by majority applies only where the fibers are of the same species. See there. 5) There, "But garments of other species," etc. {Rama: And some say that all of them are obligated by Torah law, etc.} In the Gemara, Perek HaTekhelet, 39b, there is a dispute whether garments of other materials are obligated in tzitzit by Torah law or only rabbinically. Rav Nachman holds that garments of other species are not Torah-obligated, while Rava holds that they are Torah-obligated as well. Rif and Rambam rule like Rav Nachman, and Maran rules likewise, as is his general method. Tosafot, Rosh, Sefer Mitzvot Gadol, and Mordechai rule like Rava, and this is Rama's position. There is a further dispute in Rava's view. Some hold that even according to Rava, wool or linen tzitzit exempt garments of other materials, and even wool and linen together, despite being kilayim, exempt them. Maharam, however, as cited by Hagahot Maimoniyot, Terumat HaDeshen, and Agur, holds that according to Rava, for other materials that are Torah-obligated, only wool and linen together can exempt them, which applied when tekhelet was available. Nowadays, wool or linen alone do not exempt garments of other materials; each material is exempted only by tzitzit of its own kind, such as silk tzitzit on silk and cotton tzitzit on cotton. This follows the derivation "the corner" means material matching the corner. See Beit Yosef, Bach, and Machatzit HaShekel, seif katan 3. Therefore every God-fearing person should be stringent for himself and should place cotton tzitzit on a cotton tallis, and similarly with other materials, in order to avoid the dispute. Shulchan Aruch HaRav, letter 4; Chesed LaAlafim, letter 2; Kitzur Shulchan Aruch, siman 9, letter 12; Ben Ish Chai, Parashat Noach, letter 1. See also seif 6 and what we write there.
