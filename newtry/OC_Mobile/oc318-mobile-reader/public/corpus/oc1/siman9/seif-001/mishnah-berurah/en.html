(1) “Rather, etc.” — as taught by the school of R. Yishmael: we derive from Negaim, for the verse specified therein wool and linen — wherever the term “garment” is written, the intent is wool and linen.

(2) “Sheep” — or male rams; but all other kinds, such as camel’s wool, goat’s down, hemp (shira’im), rabbit’s wool, and likewise cotton (kanavos) and everything of that sort — are not included in wool and linen, and are not obligated in tzitzis except rabbinically. And the wool of a ewe that is the daughter of a goat — Beis Yosef wrote that its wool is also not considered wool and is only like other kinds; it follows that with all of these, if one made tzitzis from any of them, it exempts only in its own kind. Wool of sheep or linen that was mixed with other kinds — we follow the majority: if the majority is wool and linen, it is obligated from the Torah, and if not, rabbinically. And this is specifically when they were mixed and spun and a garment was made from them; but if the warp is wool or linen and the woof is of other kinds, or the reverse — according to this view it is not obligated except rabbinically, even if most of it is wool and linen, for warp or woof alone is not considered a garment. And regarding which tzitzis to make on it, see below in note 13, and see in Biur Halacha.

(3) “Rather rabbinically” — even though one must bless on a mitzvah that is rabbinic as well, nevertheless there is a difference regarding several matters. 1. It is more fitting to make a garment with which one fulfills [the obligation] from the Torah. 2. If his tzitzis strings got mixed — some for their sake and some not for their sake — and he has no other tzitzis, or if he has a doubt about the garment whether it is obligated in tzitzis: if it is rabbinic, a doubt in rabbinic law is lenient. 3. If he is sitting in the congregation on a weekday and one of his tzitzis strings broke — if it is rabbinic, one could say human dignity overrides rabbinic [law] and he is not obligated to remove his tallis immediately; see siman 13. And there are many more differences — see in Peri Megadim.

(4) “And some say all of them, etc.” — they rule like Rava. Rava posed a contradiction: it is written regarding tzitzis “the corner” — implying the corner’s kind, meaning even if it is not of wool and linen, nevertheless the tzitzis should be of the garment’s kind. And it is written “you shall not wear sha’atnez, wool and linen together; twisted cords you shall make for yourself from them” — how is this? Wool and linen exempt both in their kind and not in their kind; other kinds — in their kind they exempt, not in their kind they do not exempt. And Rama decides like this view, for it is plausible to the majority of the latter authorities like Rava, to expound “the corner’s kind,” and it follows that all kinds of garments are obligated from the Torah in tzitzis, and it must be from the garment’s kind.

(ה) “And such is the halacha” — and one who wants may be strict with himself to heed the first view and make a wool tallis so he will be obligated in tzitzis from the Torah according to all. And it is proper that he make both the tallit gadol and the tallit katan of wool.
