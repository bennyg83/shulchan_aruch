(1) Only, etc. As taught in the academy of Rabbi Yishmael: we derive from the laws of garment afflictions, where the Torah specifies wool and linen, that wherever the Torah uses the word garment without qualification it means wool or linen.
(2) Sheep. Or male rams. Other materials, such as camel wool, goat hair, silk, rabbit wool, hemp, and similar fibers, are not included in wool and linen and are obligated in tzitzis only D'Rabbanan. Beit Yosef writes that the wool of a ewe born from a goat is likewise not considered wool, but only another material. Consequently, tzitzis made from any of these materials exempt only a garment of the same material. If sheep's wool or linen was blended with other fibers, the majority determines the law: if most is wool or linen, the obligation is according to the Torah; otherwise it is D'Rabbanan. This applies when the raw fibers were blended, spun, and woven into a garment. If the warp is wool or linen and the woof is another material, or the reverse, according to this view the obligation is only D'Rabbanan even if most of the garment is wool or linen, because warp or woof alone is not considered a garment. Regarding which material should be used for its tzitzis, see seif katan 13 and Biur Halachah.
(3) Only D'Rabbanan. Although one also recites a blessing over a D'Rabbanan mitzvah, there are several practical differences. First, it is preferable to wear a garment through which one fulfills a Torah obligation. Second, if tzitzis spun for the mitzvah became mixed with threads not spun for the mitzvah, and no others are available, or if there is doubt whether the garment is obligated, a D'Rabbanan doubt is treated leniently. Third, if one is sitting in public and one tzitzis thread breaks on a weekday, where the obligation is D'Rabbanan it may be said that human dignity overrides the D'Rabbanan prohibition and he need not immediately remove the tallis. See siman 13. Peri Megadim lists many other differences.
(4) Some say that all of them, etc. These authorities rule like Rava. Rava notes that “upon the tzitzis of the corner” implies that even where the garment is not wool or linen, the threads must match the garment's material. Yet the adjacent verses say, “You shall not wear shaatnez, wool and linen together; you shall make twisted cords for yourself from them.” The reconciliation is that wool and linen exempt both their own and other materials, while other materials exempt only their own kind. Rama rules like this view, followed by most later authorities. It follows that garments of every material are obligated according to the Torah and should have threads matching the garment.
(5) This is the halachah. A God-fearing person should nevertheless be stringent for the first view and use a wool tallis, thereby fulfilling the Torah obligation according to all opinions. It is proper that both the tallis gadol and tallis katan be wool.
