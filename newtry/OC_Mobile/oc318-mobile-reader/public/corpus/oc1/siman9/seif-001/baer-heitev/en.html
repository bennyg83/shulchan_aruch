Sheep's wool. Camel wool, goat hair, silk, ram's wool, and the wool of a ewe born from a goat are exempt according to the Torah and obligated only D'Rabbanan. Each of these materials exempts only a garment of its own kind. If sheep's wool was blended with other materials, the majority determines its status. Magen Avraham. A tallis whose warp is wool and whose woof is silk, or the reverse, is exempt according to the Torah and obligated D'Rabbanan even if most of it is wool. If the fibers themselves were blended, the majority determines the law. Knesses HaGedolah in the name of Radbaz. He concludes there in the name of Maharam of Rothenburg that such a tallis has no remedy; see there, and see Halachos Ketanos, part 1, siman 168, and part 2, siman 106. A God-fearing person should wear a tallis made entirely of wool. According to those who permit a silk tallis, the tzitzis threads should be spun from wool and not silk. One should not rely upon this alone, but should wear beneath it a tallis katan made entirely of wool, thereby fulfilling the mitzvah, and with the upper garment he will beautify himself. Magen Avraham, seif katan 4. He likewise writes in siman 8, seif katan 15, that the custom is not to recite a blessing over a silk tallis. Rather, one recites the blessing over a woolen tallis, removes it, and then wears the silk one; see there.
