Sheep's wool. But camel wool, goat fluff, silk, ram's wool, and wool from a ewe that nursed a goat are exempt from the Torah and only rabbinically obligated; each exempts only its own kind. Sheep's wool and other kinds that are mixed together—we follow the majority (Magen Avraham). A tallis whose warp is wool and weft silk, or the reverse, is exempt from the Torah and obligated rabbinically even if the majority is wool. If they are mixed together we follow the majority, as Knesset HaGedolah in the name of Radbaz; he concludes, in the name of Maharam of Rothenburg, that this tallis has no remedy; see above. See Hagahos Maimoniyos vol. 1 siman 168 and vol. 2 siman 106. A God-fearing person should wear a tallis entirely of wool. According to those who are lenient to make a silk tallis, he should spin threads of wool and not of silk, and not rely on this alone; rather he should make a tallit katan entirely of wool underneath to fulfill his obligation, and with this be modest on top (Magen Avraham note 4). So too in siman 8 note 15: they are accustomed not to bless on a silk tallis but to bless on the wool one, remove it, and wear the silk one; see there.
