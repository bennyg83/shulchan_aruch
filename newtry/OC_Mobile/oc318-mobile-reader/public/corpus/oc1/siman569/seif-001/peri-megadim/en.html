He must. See the Taz in Yoreh De'ah, siman 290: if he stipulated explicitly, he does not need a double condition; see there at length; and see the Levush here. And behold, even if the individual did not begin to fast at all, but only accepted upon himself several fasts or charity for the sake of some illness or distress: whether he was answered before he begins to fast, or [the sick] died, etc., he must fulfill his vow; see there. And regarding what the Taz wrote distinguishing between an individual and a congregation: that for a congregation the heart of the beis din stipulates, but for an individual unspoken intentions of the heart are not [effective] words; see Beis Yosef on this there; there is also another reason, that we do not impose a burden upon the congregation; and one needs a reason for what difference there is that for an individual unspoken intentions of the heart are not words, while for the many they are [effective]. And the Taz holds that what the Mechaber wrote, "a congregation that was answered," is a novelty: that even so, before chatzos they should not complete; and all the more so if [the sick] died and they were not answered, that it is obvious that before chatzos they should not complete; and see Magen Avraham, and with God's help it will be further elucidated there. And where many vowed charity, one may also distinguish between an individual and a congregation when [the beneficiary] died and the like: that the heart of the beis din stipulates and they are exempt, for their intent was if he lives; while an individual must give charity so long as he did not specify. And according to the reason of the Rosh, of blessed memory, namely that a congregation says Hallel with a satisfied soul, as in siman 575 according to some opinions: if so, regarding a vow of charity this does not apply, and they must give even if the sick person died and the like; and all the more so if he lives, even according to the reason of the Maggid Mishneh, they must give charity.
