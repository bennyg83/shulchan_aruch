{1} Designated for eating - But an animal designated for raising offspring or for milking: it is forbidden to milk even into foods, as above; and even milk that dripped from its udders by itself is forbidden because of nolad.
{2} To eat the milk - That is to say, that same day; for the need of tomorrow it is in every respect forbidden.
{3} If into a pot, etc. - That is, even if his intent is afterward to put the milk into food, nevertheless since now there is no food in the pot, the milk has the status of a liquid, and automatically it has the status of nolad: for originally, when it was in the udders of the animal, the milk had the status of food like the whole animal that was designated for eating, and now it is a liquid.
{4} It is permitted - For in this case the milk does not have the status of nolad, for it has the status of food since it comes into food, and originally too it was food since the animal is designated for eating. And know that there are among the Rishonim who are lenient even with an animal designated for milking or for raising offspring; and it appears that where joy of Yom Tov would be prevented, when he has nothing to eat except dairy foods, one may rely on this to be lenient [Chayei Adam]; and it is better, if he can, to have the milking into foods done by means of a non-Jew.
{5} Where the milk comes to improve it - That there is in the pot so much food as the milk needs for improving it, or at least that most of the milk is needed for the food; and through this even the minority of milk remaining that is not needed for the dish is permitted.
{6} Or if there are crumbs in it, etc. - That is also [only] where at least most of the milk is absorbed in the crumbs.
{7} All his flock, etc. - He stated an obvious matter; and the main point is that the milk is not rendered permitted by a little bread that he puts at the bottom of the vessel so long as most of the milk is not absorbed in it; and one should protest against the common people who transgress regarding this [Acharonim].
{8} And a non-Jew who milks an animal, etc. - That is, [an animal] designated for eating, or when he milks his own animal, regarding which there is no muktzeh issue [as above in siman 310]; and even [an animal] designated for milking is like one designated for eating.
{9} And a Jew sees him - For if he does not see him, it is forever forbidden for a Jew to drink them, as is stated in Yoreh De'ah siman 115, se'if 1.
{10} One should be lenient - That is, even if he milked into an empty pot, for it is not called nolad, because the Jew himself would have been permitted to milk it into a pot that has food in it, and therefore it is included in what is prepared [Magen Avraham and other Acharonim]. And in Beis Meir he wrote that even according to this view one should be lenient only when milking into foods; and he came to innovate for this view only that when a non-Jew milks his own animal it is permitted even if [the animal is] designated for milking, for there is no muktzeh regarding what belongs to a non-Jew, as above.
{11} But they customarily treat it as forbidden - That is, into an empty pot it is forbidden even for a non-Jew who milks; but if he milks into foods from his own animal, even if it is designated for milking, it is permitted, as above in s.k. 8; and likewise a Jew is permitted to milk from a non-Jew's animal, even if designated for milking, into foods, for there is no muktzeh regarding what belongs to a non-Jew, as above.
{12} It is permitted on the second day of Yom Tov - That is to say, even if it was milked into an empty pot and from an animal designated for milking, [for] one of those days is certainly a weekday. However, if it was milked on Shabbos, even into foods and from an animal designated for eating, it is forbidden on the first day of Yom Tov that falls after it on Sunday, because of hachanah; but on Monday it is permitted. But on Yom Tov of Rosh Hashanah the two days are considered like one day, and wherever it is forbidden on the first day it is forbidden on the second. And a non-Jew who speaks casually, saying that that milk was milked on the first day of Yom Tov of the Diaspora, is believed, and it is permitted on the second day. And the law of squeezing unripe grapes is as on Shabbos, for we hold that it is forbidden to squeeze them even into foods, as above in siman 320, se'if 5; and see there in the Biur Halacha, s.v. "to eat," that we decided there that even in order to eat immediately it is forbidden.
