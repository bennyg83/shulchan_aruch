That it stands for eating — see Mishna Berurah what he wrote that where Yom Tov joy is prevented one may rely to be lenient even from an animal standing to be milked; see in Shaarei Teshuvah what he wrote on this. Further, Rambam's view is that milking for food even on Shabbat is only d'rabbanan because there is no usual unloading thus; aside from this the essence of milking prohibition on Yom Tov even not into food if for that day's need — many poskim hold no d'oraisa prohibition because it is ochel nefesh, and all the more so Rambam that threshing itself on Yom Tov is also not from Torah; even per Ramban and Rashba who hold it is from Torah, nevertheless for milking on Yom Tov Ramban in his novellae holds it is d'rabbanan — it is among matters whose melacha is for its day that Torah permitted in ochel nefesh, for if he milks from erev Yom Tov it spoils like baking and cooking — his words are clear in the sugya Shabbos 95a, explained there for R' Eliezer intentional on Yom Tov receives lashes; Ramban explains it does not refer to milking, beating, and cheesemaking, only to combing, spreading, and rolling honey cakes alone; Rivash brought in his name and so in name of Tosafot Rosh in name of Rabeinu Chananel who also hold d'rabbanan (only for Rivash himself it appears not clear to him — see there); so explained in responsum to Rav Hai Gaon and in Tosafot Shabbos there s.v. rolling at the end, and R' Yitzchak challenged, etc. — explained he also holds all are d'rabbanan only still requires explanation, for the sugya is explicit in Yevamos 114 that one may let a child suckle milk on Yom Tov because on Yom Tov for a lav they did not decree — behold explicit that to milk in the usual way is a lav prohibition; yet I found in Ramban in Milchamot chapter Kegiz that he raised this against Geonim's words and wrote they explain lav prohibition is on all general Yom Tov melachot forbidden, not on this melacha itself; but Rashi explained there literally and it appears he holds milking in the usual way is d'oraisa due to unloading; so in Avodat HaKodesh for Rashba (Gra brought siman 495) explained d'oraisa (not as in his novellae); nevertheless since the essence of prohibition for many poskim is d'rabbanan we cannot protest those lenient in milking for food even from a milking animal where Yom Tov joy is prevented; nevertheless if possible through a gentile it is certainly better.
