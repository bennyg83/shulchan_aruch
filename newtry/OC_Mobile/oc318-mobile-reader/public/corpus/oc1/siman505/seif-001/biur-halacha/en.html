That is designated for eating - See the Mishnah Berurah, what is written: that where joy of Yom Tov would be prevented, one may rely to be lenient even from an animal designated for milking; and see in Shaar HaTziyun what is written about this. And furthermore, according to the view of the Rambam, one who milks into foods, even on Shabbos, it is only rabbinic, because that is not the usual manner of mefarek. And besides all this, the very law of the prohibition of milking on Yom Tov, even not into foods, if it is for the need of that day: according to several poskim there is no Torah prohibition in it, because it is ochel nefesh. And it goes without saying according to the Rambam, that threshing itself on Yom Tov too is not from the Torah; and even according to the view of the Ramban and the Rashba who hold that it is from the Torah, nevertheless regarding one who milks for Yom Tov the Ramban holds in his novellae that it is rabbinic, for it is among things whose labor is for that day, which the Torah permitted for ochel nefesh, for if he milks from before Yom Tov it will spoil, as with baking and cooking. And his words are clarified in the sugya of Shabbos, folio 95, where it is clarified that according to R' Eliezer, one who intentionally [does melachah] on Yom Tov is lashed; and the Ramban explains that this does not refer to milking, making cheese curds, and making cheese, but only to sweeping, sprinkling [water], and taking honey from combs alone. And so the Rivash brought in his name, and likewise in the name of the Tosafos of the Rosh in the name of Rabbeinu Chananel, who also hold that it is rabbinic (and only according to the Rivash himself it appears that this matter is not clear to him; see there). And so it is clarified in a responsum of Rav Hai Gaon, and likewise in Tosafos on Shabbos there, s.v. "one who takes [honey]," at the end: "And it is a wonder to R' Yitzchak," etc.; it is clarified that they too hold that all of them are rabbinic. But still clarification is needed, for there is an explicit sugya in Yevamos 114 that one who groans [in pain] is permitted to suck milk on Yom Tov, because Yom Tov is a lav-prohibition and the Rabbis did not decree; so it is clarified that to milk in the usual manner is a lav-prohibition. But I found in the Ramban in the Milchamos in chapter "A barrel" that he raised this against the words of the Geonim, and wrote that they will explain that "a lav-prohibition" means regarding the general melachos of Yom Tov that are forbidden, and not regarding this melachah itself. But Rashi explained there according to its plain sense, and it implies that he holds that milking in the usual manner is D'Oraisa because of mefarek; and likewise in Avodas HaKodesh of the Rashba (and the Gra brought it in siman 495) it is clarified that it is D'Oraisa (and not as he wrote in his novellae). And nevertheless, since the main prohibition according to several poskim is rabbinic, we do not have [grounds] to protest against those who are lenient when milking into foods even from a milking animal, where joy of Yom Tov would be prevented; and nevertheless, if this is possible by means of a non-Jew, it is certainly better.
