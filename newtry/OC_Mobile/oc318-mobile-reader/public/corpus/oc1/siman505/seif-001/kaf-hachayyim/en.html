1) [Se'if 1] An animal designated for eating, etc. An animal designated for its milk or for breeding is muktzeh. It is forbidden to milk it, and its milk is forbidden on Yom Tov because of muktzeh. Levush; Magen Avraham, subsection 1. Nevertheless, see Kol Bo, siman 58, which writes: 'Some say that even when an animal is designated for its milk, its milk is not muktzeh if it is not intended for making cheese and drying the cheeses for commerce. This resembles a hen designated to produce eggs and hatch chicks. This is also the view of Razah.' These are his words. Beis Yosef cites him. This is also the view of Taz, subsection 1, and some later authorities. Knesses HaGedolah, Hagahos Beis Yosef, subsection 4, however, cites several halachic authorities who agree with the ruling of Shulchan Aruch that permission applies only to a goat designated for eating. He writes that all these great authorities are not set aside in favor of Razah, Ramban, and the alternative opinion cited by Kol Bo. See there. Pri Chadash likewise writes that the ruling of Shulchan Aruch is clear and agreed upon by all the commentators except Rabbeinu Chananel. See there. Maamar Mordechai, subsection 1, similarly writes that several early authorities permit only an animal designated for eating, in accordance with Shulchan Aruch, unlike Taz, who relied upon the alternative opinion cited by Kol Bo. See there. Derech HaChaim, in the laws of moving muktzeh, subsection 51, also rules accordingly. He writes that although Taz permits it, the halachah does not appear to follow him in this matter. See there. Shulchan Aruch HaRav, subsection 3; Chayei Adam, rule 81, subsection 4; Mikraei Kodesh, siman 15, subsection 1; Mishnah Berurah, subsection 1, rule likewise.
