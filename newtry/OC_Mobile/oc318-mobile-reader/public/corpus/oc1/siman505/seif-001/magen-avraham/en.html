Designated for eating. But an animal designated for raising offspring or for its milk is muktzeh and is forbidden in every respect; and according to the one who permits muktzeh, everything is permitted, for if he wished he could slaughter it and eat everything (so it is implied in the Ran and the Hagahos Ashri).
If it has no food in it, it is forbidden. For it is nolad: originally food, and now a liquid; and if there is food in it, it is a liquid that comes into food and is like food. And this requires further study, for in siman 320, se'if 7, he wrote that according to Rabbeinu Chananel it is forbidden; so why did he state it here without qualification? And one must say that regarding Yom Tov his view is to be lenient; see what is written there, se'if 5.
One should be lenient about it. For it is prepared, since even a Jew is permitted to milk it in order to eat them (Rashba). And he goes according to his reason, for he holds like the Ramban (Beis Yosef). And this requires further study: what has this to do with the Ramban? For necessarily the Rashba is dealing with [an animal] designated for eating; for if it is not designated for eating, even milking into foods is forbidden according to those who forbid muktzeh. Rather he is dealing with [an animal] designated for eating; if so, regarding a non-Jew's animal, muktzeh does not apply to it, as the Levush wrote (see siman 498, se'if 3). And therefore the Rashba wrote that even though the non-Jew milked it into a pot that has no food in it, it is permitted, for it is not called nolad, since the Jew himself would have been permitted to milk it into a pot that has food in it. And the Ramban permitted only [an animal] designated for milking; and so the Ran wrote explicitly in his name at the beginning of chapter "A barrel" and at the beginning of the first chapter of Beitzah. And this requires further study regarding the Darkei Moshe, who wrote: And it is possible that when [the animal] is designated for eating, even the Hagahos Ashri concedes that it is permitted; end quote. And this is not so; and one must say that the Rama retracted, and therefore he wrote in the Shulchan Aruch that they customarily treat it as forbidden. And what the Levush wrote, that there is no muktzeh and nolad regarding what belongs to a non-Jew, is incorrect, for nolad is forbidden regarding what belongs to a non-Jew, as with an egg and milk, as he wrote in siman 513 and 517. The Maharil wrote that he ruled a heter for what was milked on the first day of Yom Tov or on the preceding Shabbos, to eat it on the second day, on Sunday which is the second day of Yom Tov; and he said that it is stated in the Hagahos Ashri from the Or Zarua that one should not compare it to an egg; and the reason is that he did not entirely divert his mind from it as with an egg, since there is a heter to milk by telling a non-Jew, because of tza'ar baalei chayim; end quote. It implies that on that same day it is forbidden to eat it; so he wrote in the Darkei Moshe in his name. But in the Yam shel Shlomo he challenged what he wrote, that one should not compare it to an egg, for even regarding an egg, what was laid on this [day] is permitted on that [day]; and therefore he wrote that [Maharil] holds that even on the very day it was milked it is permitted. And furthermore, Behalachos Gedolos permits squeezing for the need of ochel nefesh; and although we do not rule like him, nevertheless if it was done by itself or by means of a non-Jew, it is permitted to eat it that same day; end quote. And so he wrote in a responsum, siman 79; and so wrote the Shelah and Mateh Moshe in his name. And what is written in the Yam shel Shlomo in the indices that it is forbidden on Yom Tov is a scribal error, and one must say it is forbidden on Shabbos. And this requires further study, for his main foundation is upon the Maharil, and in the Maharil the implication is, on the contrary, that it is forbidden that same day. And furthermore, according to his words, the statements of the Hagahos Ashri from the Or Zarua would contradict each other, for in our editions they wrote in the name of the Or Zarua that it is forbidden. And it appears to me that the words of the Maharil are as their plain sense: because we hold that if a non-Jew caught fish or gathered fruit on Shabbos, there are those who forbid even on the second day, Sunday, as a decree lest one tell the non-Jew, as is written in siman 515; and therefore he wrote that regarding milk it is permitted, and he said the reason that is stated in the Hagahos Ashri from the Or Zarua, that one should not compare it to an egg, meaning that milk is not as severe as an egg, etc., since there is a heter, etc. And if so, it is permitted on the second day, Sunday, for it is not applicable to decree lest one tell a non-Jew to milk, since it is permitted to tell a non-Jew to milk; except that the milk is forbidden that same day because of liquids that flowed out, as is written in siman 305, se'if 20; and also on [Yom Tov] day it is forbidden by the law that Shabbos does not prepare for Yom Tov; but on the second day it is permitted. So it appears to me clearly in explaining the words of the Maharil; and if so, there is no heter to eat it that same day. And Moreinu HaRav Maharam of Lublin in a responsum, siman 17: As a matter of law, milk that was milked on Shabbos is permitted on the second day, Sunday (for the reason I wrote); except that I am accustomed to be stringent so as not to distinguish between the first day of Yom Tov and the second; end quote. And this is an excessive stringency, for the Rama ruled that even what was milked on the first day is permitted on the second; therefore it appears to me that one should not budge from the ruling of the Rama. And therefore, with an animal designated for eating, and one milks into foods, even a Jew is permitted to milk his animal; and if it is not designated for eating, and one milks into foods, it depends on the dispute written in siman 495. And with a non-Jew's animal, even if it is not designated for eating, it is permitted to milk into foods; but to milk into an empty pot is forbidden in every respect. And if a non-Jew was milking into an empty pot, it is forbidden that same day; and if it was milked on Shabbos, even into foods and from an animal designated for eating, as is written in siman 305, it is forbidden on the first day of Yom Tov that follows it, but on the second day it is permitted. And a non-Jew speaking casually is believed in this matter, as is written in siman 513, se'if 6. One who is distressed by hunger is permitted to suck from the animal with his mouth on Yom Tov, for it is mefarek in an unusual manner, and in a place of distress they did not decree (Tur; Rif); see siman 328, se'if 33. The Darkei Moshe wrote: And the law of squeezing unripe grapes is [as] in siman 320; and so Beis Yosef wrote in the name of the Semag and the Semak, that it is forbidden to squeeze them on Yom Tov even into foods, for it is sorting food from waste; so wrote the Hagahos Maimoniyos. And even though it is permitted to sort on Yom Tov, as is written in siman 510, nevertheless to squeeze food from waste is forbidden. And what is written in the Yam shel Shlomo, that Behalachos Gedolos permitted squeezing for the need of ochel nefesh, requires further study, for at the beginning of Beitzah we forbid liquids that flowed out on Yom Tov as a decree lest one squeeze; see there. And it is forced to say that we decree lest one squeeze when not for need.
