Designated for eating. For if it is designated for its milk, it is muktzeh because of its mother; and even if it is designated for eating, if [one milks] into a pot that has no food in it, it is forbidden, for originally it was food by extension of its mother, and now it is a liquid, and this is nolad. This is the view of the Rif. But in siman 320 the Shulchan Aruch also brings the words of Rabbeinu Chananel that it is forbidden even when [the animal is] designated for eating, and one must say that here he retracted. And as for this [case of an animal] designated for its milk, which is forbidden: Beis Yosef wrote in the name of the Kol Bo, and this is his language: There are those who say that even if it is designated for its milk, its milk is not muktzeh if it is not designated for cheese-making and for drying cheeses for commerce, comparable to a hen designated for producing eggs and for raising chicks; and so is the view of the Rosh; end quote. And the Maharshal at the beginning of the first chapter brought this and wrote: And this is nothing but words of prophecy, for from where do we know to be lenient, for it is implied in the Gemara that it depends solely on [whether the animal is] designated for its milk; end quote. And I do not know why he called it words of prophecy, for regarding a hen they did not say simply "designated for eggs," but specifically for producing eggs; so too here regarding a goat for its milk. Rather, it appears to me that the matter does not depend on selling the cheeses, but rather: if they are particular not to eat the milk as it is, but leave it to collect a lot to make cheeses, that is what they forbade because of muktzeh. And since it is a rabbinic matter, the implication is to be lenient.
And for example, where the milk comes to improve it. It implies that even the rest of the milk in the pot that is not needed for improving [the food is permitted]; for this is the language of the Rivash: This is when [the milk] comes into food to improve it, for then it appears that he does not want it as a liquid; and so are the words of Rashi. But one who milks all his flock for liquids is not permitted on account of one slice that they put in the vessel; end quote. It implies that if there is no excess [liquid], liquids are not permitted beyond what is absorbed in the food in the pot. And this is the language of the Semak that the Hagahos Maimoniyos brings: If there is cooked food in the dish, it is fine to milk into it on Yom Tov; but if there are crumbs in it, it is certainly permitted, for the milk enters into it and becomes food; yet nevertheless the spirit of the Sages is not pleased with him. But regarding a piece of bread with which the milk does not mix, this Rabbeinu Yechiel forbids; yet if a non-Jew does so and a Jew sees him, it is permitted for the Jew to eat it, but to drink it is forbidden; end quote. And this is not like the words of the Rivash, who explicitly permits with crumbs; and the Shulchan Aruch ruled like the Rivash. And it is contrary to the law that many of the common people customarily put a little bread in a vessel and milk a great deal of milk upon it; rather, a great deal of the milk must be absorbed in the food, and one may eat specifically that food on that very day, and then the milk remaining there is permitted.
One should be lenient about it. Responsa of the Rashba; and he holds as the Ran wrote in the name of the Rambam in chapter "A barrel," that there is no muktzeh by reason of an issur that comes by itself, such as this case, for he did not push it aside by hand and did not transgress regarding it even unwittingly; once the issur has departed, the muktzeh has departed from it. Therefore this milk that was milked is permitted, even though the goat was then muktzeh; nevertheless, this milk that was milked from it into the pot, the muktzeh has departed from it; end quote. And if so, he disagrees with what the Shulchan Aruch wrote, that specifically with a goat designated for eating is the milk permitted; and if so, the Rama should have written here, "And there are those who say that the milk is permitted." And in the Derashos of the Maharil he also wrote that specifically on Shabbos before Yom Tov, when it was milked then, the milk is forbidden on the first day of Yom Tov [and not] on the second day of Yom Tov, that is, because of hachanah, for Shabbos does not prepare for Yom Tov; implying that what was milked on Yom Tov by means of a non-Jew is permitted that same day. But in the first chapter of Beitzah the Ran brought that the main [view] is not as the Ramban wrote, but specifically with a goat designated for eating is it permitted, and that is as the Shulchan Aruch wrote like the Rif; and milk milked on Yom Tov without food is forbidden that same day, and all the more so if it was milked on Shabbos before Yom Tov, that it is forbidden because of hachanah. And therefore the Rama wrote: But they customarily treat it as forbidden, and one should not change [the custom]. And the Maharshal at the beginning of the first chapter was lenient regarding what was milked that same day by means of a non-Jew, and forbade only what was milked on Shabbos before Yom Tov, as the Maharil wrote. And since the Rama wrote that such is their custom of prohibition, to him you shall hearken. And on the second day of Yom Tov it is obvious that it is permitted according to everyone, as the Maharil wrote. And in Responsa Maharam of Lublin, siman 17, he also wrote that as a matter of law it is permitted on the second day of Yom Tov, except that he concluded that he is stringent to forbid even on the second day, so as not to distinguish between the first and the second; and this is a stringency without reason. And it appears that he did not see the words of the Maharil above who permits explicitly, since he did not cite him; and the Maharshal too permitted at least on the second day of Yom Tov according to all opinions; and this is like an egg in kutach, for even an egg laid on Shabbos is permitted on the second day of Yom Tov that follows it.
On the second day of Yom Tov of the Diaspora. All the more so if it was milked on Shabbos before Yom Tov, that it is permitted on the second day of Yom Tov of the Diaspora, for even an egg is permitted in this case, as is written in siman 513.
