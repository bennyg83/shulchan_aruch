(1) First among the middle blessings — another reason for the order of the blessings is written in Sefer Seder Hayom: Ata Chonein is first, because the request for wisdom and knowledge in a person — without them good is better than the evil inclination into which he has fallen; therefore one must have proper intention in it, for this is the main request that a person must ask of the Creator, may He be blessed — that He give him intellect and proper knowledge to despise evil and choose good. Hashivenu comes after knowledge, for through knowledge one gives his heart over his sin; and the intention of this blessing is to ask of the the Master, may He be blessed, that He subdue and humble the pride of his inclination. Selach Lanu comes after repentance — one must bring to mind the sins or transgressions themselves. Refaenu is healing after redemption, for whenever a person is in distress he does not attend to his illness; and the main intention is to ask of our Heaven that He heal us so that we be healthy and strong to engage in Torah properly and observe all the mitzvos. The blessing for livelihood comes after healing, for when a person is ill he does not ask for food; but after he is healed he asks for his livelihood to restore and strengthen his body and soul; and one must have intention that the the Master, may He be blessed, arrange our livelihood with ease and not with distress, in permitted ways and not forbidden. And the fact that one makes Havdalah in this blessing — because it is wisdom that a person distinguishes between one thing and another; therefore they fixed it in the blessing of wisdom; and there is a hint regarding binah: the acronym — in the heavens, wine, candle, Havdalah. It is written in the Tur: that which they enacted to say "Our Father" in the blessing Hashivenu and in Selach Lanu, unlike the other blessings — the reason is that we mention before Him that the father is obligated to teach together with his son; and in Selach Lanu — because it is written, "And he will return to the the Master and He will have mercy on him"; therefore we mention the father's mercy upon the son. See in Eliyah Rabbah, who cites opinions that some say one must say "chonenanu me'itekha," etc., and not "vechonenanu," because it is the beginning of a request; and there are those who say one must say "vechonenanu" — see there.
