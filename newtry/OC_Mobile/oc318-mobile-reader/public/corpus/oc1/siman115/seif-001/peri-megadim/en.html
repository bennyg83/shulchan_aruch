It is written in the Tur, etc., that repentance that the Holy One, blessed be He, accepts is from kindness and not from strict judgment; therefore they mention the father's mercy (see in Sefer HaIkarim). One can say the Mechaber omitted the reason of the gemara Megillah 17b from the verse "and they sanctify," etc., "and those who err in spirit shall have binah" (Isaiah 29:24); and he adopted the Tur's second reason that is not mentioned in the commentary — only from a gemara and the piyyut of R. Eleazar ben Kalir; Be'er HaGolah and Nekudat HaKesef wrote Megillah, but it is not [there]. In the piyyut of additional Musaf of Rosh Hashanah: "foot — five hundred fifteen — straight to the throne," meaning 15 distances: seven firmaments and eight heavens (likewise Beit Yosef, and see Orach Chayyim 1) — repentance reaches there, and the Holy One, blessed be He, sees and accepts — straight, straight, straight — see there. One can say da'at, binah, and haskal are three levels, one above the other — see Levush siman 294; and it appears there that da'at is level one, for "You grant man da'at." The explanation: if so they are the same in this, and in our request "grant me da'at for Torah." However, in Rashi (Exodus 31:3) "and I will fill with wisdom, understanding, and knowledge" — each and every one — see there; and likewise it appears from Ibn Ezra and in Margaliyot Tovah; and therefore the verse "You grant man da'at" — the levels of man: man is the soul (as Rama wrote regarding "basar adam"). "Chonenanu me'itekha da'at" — each and every one — not through an angel, as it is written "he teaches" — they suspected him regarding another man's wife; this is Gabriel. And in Sefer HaMagid we explained the verse "stiff-necked," and the like, according to Ibn Ezra: three chambers in the brain near the bone — sechel in the middle, binah behind, da'at of memory; and whoever sends forth his flesh is thin, more swift; and this is what is meant by "stiff-necked" and "they have no da'at" — for memory is a great foundation for placing the sinner's sins before his eyes and the good that the Holy One, blessed be He, did for him, and they forget this — understand. That Ata Chonein begins on Shabbat, not like all middle blessings which are requests — understand this in the piyyut. And "Who teaches man binah" diminishes somewhat — understand.
