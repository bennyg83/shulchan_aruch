Because it is permitted, etc. There is another reason for the order of the blessings. The Seder HaYom explains: Atah Chonen comes first because without knowledge and wisdom a person is worse than a stillborn child. Therefore one must have proper concentration in this blessing, for the primary request a person must make of the Creator, blessed be His Name, is that He grant him sound intellect and understanding, so that he knows to reject evil and choose good. Hashiveinu follows knowledge, because through knowledge a person takes his sin to heart; the intent of this blessing is to ask Hashem, blessed be His Name, to subdue and humble the pride of his evil inclination. Selach Lanu follows repentance, and he should bring to mind the sins or transgressions themselves. Refaeinu, healing, follows redemption, because while a person is in distress he does not properly attend to his illness; the principal intent is to ask our God to heal us so that we may be healthy and strong to engage properly in Torah and keep all the mitzvot. The blessing for livelihood follows healing, because while a person is ill he does not seek food, but after he is healed he seeks his livelihood in order to restore the health of body and soul. One should intend that Hashem, blessed be He, provide our livelihood peacefully and not with distress, permissibly and not through prohibition. Havdalah is inserted in this blessing because discerning distinctions is itself wisdom; an allusion is that the initials of the words for spices, wine, candle, and Havdalah form the word binah. I wrote in responsa that our text, knowledge, understanding, and intellect, is correct. So I found in the Rambam at the end of Sefer Ahavah and in Abudarham, and so the Levush writes in siman 294, that these three levels are progressively higher. This is unlike those who reverse their order. Some say one should say 'Grant us from You' and not 'And grant us from You,' because it begins a request; Abudarham implies this. Yet the Rambam there has 'And grant us,' as do all the siddurim, and this is also implied by the Beit Yosef in siman 294. The Tur writes that Hashiveinu contains fifteen words, corresponding to seven atmospheres, seven heavens, and above them the Throne of Glory, to which repentance reaches, totaling fifteen. The blessing begins with the letter corresponding to five and ends with it, alluding to the Ten Days of Repentance. Knesset HaGedolah, however, writes that it corresponds to eight atmospheres and seven heavens. He must mean that the atmosphere from the uppermost heaven to the Throne of Glory is also counted.
