[1] Because man's superiority, etc. Another reason for the order of the blessings. In Seder Hayom: Ata Chonein is first, because apart from knowledge and wisdom in a person, good is better than the evil inclination into which he has fallen; therefore one must have proper intention in it, for this is the main request that a person must ask of the Creator, may His name be blessed — that He give him intellect and proper knowledge, knowing to despise evil and choose good. Hashivenu comes after knowledge, for through knowledge one gives his heart over his sin; and the intention of this blessing is to ask of the the Master, may His name be blessed, that He subdue and humble the pride of his inclination. Selach Lanu comes after repentance — one must bring to mind the sins or transgressions themselves. Refaenu is healing after redemption, for whenever a person is in distress he does not attend to his illness; and the main intention is to ask of our Heaven that He heal us so that we be healthy and strong to engage in Torah properly and observe all the mitzvos. The blessing for livelihood comes after healing, for when a person is ill he does not ask for food; but after he is healed he asks for his livelihood to restore and strengthen his body and soul; and one must have intention that Heaven, may He be blessed, arrange our livelihood with ease and not with distress, in permitted ways and not forbidden. And the fact that one makes Havdalah in this blessing — because knowing the distinction is wisdom to him; and there is a hint regarding binah: the acronym — in the heavens, wine, candle, Havdalah. I wrote in responsa that our formulas are correct: da'at, binah, and haskal; and I likewise found in Rambam at the end of Sefer Ahavah, and in Abudraham; and likewise Levush wrote in siman 294 that these three levels are one above the other, and not like those who reverse them. There are those who say "chonenanu me'itekha" and not "vechonenanu," because this is the beginning of a request; and so it appears in Abudraham. However, in Rambam there it states "vechonenanu," and so it is in all siddurim; and so it appears in Beit Yosef siman 294. The Tur wrote: Hashivenu contains 15 words, corresponding to the seven heavens and seven firmaments and above them the throne of glory — for repentance reaches until there; thus there are 15. And the blessing begins with a heh and ends with a heh, corresponding to the Ten Days of Repentance.
