(א) Above ten handbreadths — for all above ten is not a place of walking; primary walking place is on the ground or at least within ten, which is also considered walking on the ground; the Sages were uncertain whether techum prohibition applies here.

(ב) He jumped, etc. — the same regarding walking in air by jumping, as below.

(ג) And each does not have, etc. — for he is like one walking in air; but if they had four by four convenient for walking, he is considered walking on the ground; plainly techum prohibition applies there. Even if only one pillar of four handbreadths, part within his techum and part outside — forbidden to go from one head to the other (Rashi).

(ד) Four by four — but if on one side less than four by four, even if very long, not convenient for walking — also included in the doubt.

(ה) In d'rabbanan — such as outside techum of two thousand cubits until twelve mil, which all agree is d'rabbanan.

(ו) In this way — meaning via pillars.

(ז) Per those who say, etc. — meaning Poskim who rule like Yerushalmi that beyond twelve mil is d'oraisa, learned from verse "let no man go out of his place on the seventh day" — and this place is beyond twelve mil corresponding to Israel's camp. From Mechaber's words "and whatever is d'rabbanan," etc., it implies he leans to rule stringently on land beyond twelve mil even above ten, which is d'oraisa doubt; Beur HaGra leans to Poskim that it is always d'rabbanan.

(ח) And since on seas and rivers, etc. — meaning even beyond twelve mil they are not d'oraisa, because they do not resemble, etc.; for those who say d'oraisa we learn from desert — walking in desert was on dry land not water; therefore we are lenient regarding above ten.

(ט) One who came in a ship on Shabbat — not meaning he boarded on Shabbat — for then he was at entry of day on land and acquired residence there and forbidden to descend to port; but meaning he traveled with the ship on Shabbat and reached port.

(י) Always, etc. — if even once below ten during Shabbat he acquired residence there; if from that place to port is more than two thousand — again forbidden to descend to port, for he has only four cubits — his sitting place on ship considered all as four cubits. When sailing Shabbat on ship standing above ten intending to descend Shabbat elsewhere beyond two thousand — must be careful: as soon as he acquired residence on ship at twilight while above ten, not to go to dry land; if he went to dry land one hour he acquired residence there and forbidden again to descend to port outside two thousand from his residence on land — as siman 346 — see there.

(כ) He descends, etc. — if ship far from port he descends via ramp. To pass via ferry on the water (called pram) if tied to ship and port — all permit descent; if not tied — some permit, some forbid, lest it appear like a raft since he did not rest on it from daytime; nevertheless in Shulchan Aruch Rabbi Zalman implies: if he did not leave ship to land from entry of day until now — permitted.

(ל) From place he reached — meaning if he reached a place below ten far from port, they measure two thousand cubits from there.

(מ) If it is above ten, etc. — this refers to the measure of twelve mil for seas and rivers; and the same applies regarding the measure of two thousand on dry land.

(נ) We follow leniency — and we rely that from when Shabbat entered until he reached the port it was above ten, because a d'rabbanan doubt is treated leniently.

(ס) And if he already went out — meaning he reached one place below ten, acquired residence there, and went more than two thousand from there — by law he has only four cubits, as one who went outside the techum; for even under duress he has only four cubits. Nevertheless, if under duress he entered a walled city, the whole city is considered as four cubits for him, as the law of one whom non-Jews took out and placed inside a walled city in siman 405. And if he did not go beyond two thousand but only his measure ended in the middle of the city, he may walk only until the place where his measure ended. One who comes on Yom Tov from outside the techum from a place he reached below ten — nevertheless on the following Shabbat he is permitted to walk two thousand from where he stands, for Shabbat and Yom Tov are two separate sanctities, and there is no prohibition of hachanah since he did nothing (Acharonim).

(ע) Because of rain, etc. — and all the more so if non-Jews pushed him into the city, the whole city is like four cubits for him; nevertheless he may not tell a non-Jew to push him.

(פ) Or if he needs, etc. — all this is when he did not find a modest place to relieve himself, and likewise a place to shelter from sun or rain, until within the city; otherwise it is forbidden for him to enter, as below in siman 406; this is what the Rama wrote — see below, etc.
