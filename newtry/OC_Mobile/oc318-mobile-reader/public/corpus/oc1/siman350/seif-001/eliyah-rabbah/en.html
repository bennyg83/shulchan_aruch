[1] Provided that he does not carry them, etc. If he did carry them, the Raavad and Magen Avraham, chapter 15, hold he is liable, unlike the Rambam. The Bach above asks why the qualification 'provided that' is necessary, apparently not having seen the Rambam. The Rambam implies that even carrying above ten handbreadths can incur liability.
[2] That he needs, etc. The same applies to similar foods and garments because they are objects needed by him in his present location, as stated in Avodat HaKodesh, page 14. See the Rosh at the end of Eruvin regarding his question, since the case was explained as above ten. See also the Yerushalmi there. This requires study, and I have abbreviated.
[3] And some explain, etc. It is proper to be stringent according to the first view, as stated by the Maggid Mishneh there. This is also the primary ruling for one who examines the early authorities.
