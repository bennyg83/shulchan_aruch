(3) "In any language" — specifically when he understands that language. The same applies to prayer and Birkat HaMazon, and to Kiddush, Birkat HaMitzvot, Birkat HaP'ri, and Hallel (later authorities; see Biur Halachah). All this is by law; but for the mitzvah in the optimal manner it is specifically in the Holy Tongue — so wrote Bach in siman 193, and see in the books of later authorities that in our days, even by law, one should be careful not to recite it in another language but only in the Holy Tongue, for there are many words that we do not know how to translate them well — such as the word "veshinantam," which has several explanations: one meaning study, and one meaning sharpness, as the Sages said that words of Torah should be sharp in your mouth, that if a person asks you something, do not stammer and say to him. And likewise many words in Keriat Shema whose explanation we do not know well in another language — such as the word "et" and the word "totafot," and the like. But when we read Shema in the Holy Tongue, and likewise in prayer and Birkat HaMazon, Kiddush, and other berakhot — even if he does not understand the language, he fulfills, for it is not common that a Jewish son does not know the explanation of the first verse in Keriat Shema, whose intent is for delay, as written in siman 63, se'if 4.
