(ג) [Seif 2] One may read it in any language, etc., and there is no distinction between an individual and the congregation [Beit Yosef, Bach, Prisha, Shakh in Hagahot Beit Yosef letter alef, Olat Tamid letter bet, Ma'amar Mordechai letter alef]. And specifically when he understands the language [Magen Avraham comment 1, Peri Chadash letter alef, Baer Heitev letter alef seif 2 letter alef, Turei Zahav letter bet]. And so too Rambam in chapter 2 of Laws of Keriat Shema halacha 10. And so from the words of Levush. And the same applies to prayer and Birkat Hamazon. But Kiddush, Birkat Hapeirot, Birkat Hamitzvot, and Hallel; one fulfills even if he does not understand the language [Magen Avraham there, Baer Heitev there, Sha'ar Hatziyun there, Turei Zahav there]. And see below siman 101 seif 4 whether specifically in a congregation one may pray in any language or even as an individual; see there. And see Pri Megadim letter alef comment 1 who wrote that there is room to doubt regarding the holy tongue if one does not understand; perhaps he fulfills as with Megillah in a foreign tongue in Hebrew script; and other languages specifically require hearing with understanding. Or "hearing" means lekhatchilah one must make heard to his ears from the Torah, not rabbinically (see there. And see Shakh in Hagahot Tur letter alef what he wrote on this) see there.
(ד) There; one may read it in any language, etc. This is from the perspective of law; but according to what is written siman 61 letters alef and bet in the name of the Zohar and the Ari that there are intentions corresponding to the sections and the number of words and also other intentions related to initial and final letters as written in Sha'ar Hakavanot in the discourse on the eight [words] of Shema; this will not be if he does not read it in the holy tongue; see there and examine.
