(comment 2) Not, etc. — such as in a house, etc. And this is Rabbeinu Yonah's language in siman 85: I wonder at R' Yitzchak who wrote, etc., that one obligated in a blessing who cannot say it because his hands are not clean, or who stands in a place that is not clean — such as a sick person whose bed is not clean and who cannot utter with his lips the prayer — he may think the blessing or prayer he is obligated in, even though he does not fulfill his obligation, etc., end quote. And it is possible he does not speak of actually filthy hands or an actually filthy place, but when the hands or place are not clean — but there is no actual filth in them. And if he were not coerced he would need to clean his hands and the place so he could utter with his lips. And now that he is coerced, etc. Nevertheless he may think it since there is no complete filth there, end quote Rabbeinu Yonah. And behold Magen Avraham explained that where you find it in the middle room of the bathhouse; and regarding hands one must say they are presumptively clean hands and not filthy; nevertheless for prayer (but not for a blessing as above siman 4 seif 26) one must clean with something that cleans as below siman 92 seif 4. And in coercion when he cannot clean them — speech is forbidden; nevertheless thought is permitted — see Taz.
