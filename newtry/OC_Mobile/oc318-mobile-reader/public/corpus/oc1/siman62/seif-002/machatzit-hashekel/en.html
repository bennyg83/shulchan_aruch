(note 2) "That is not," etc. — such as in a house, etc. And this is the language of Rabbeinu in siman 85: I wonder at R. Yehuda who wrote, etc., that one who is obligated in a berakhah and cannot say it because his hands are not clean, or he stands in a place that is not clean — such as a sick person whose bed is not clean and who cannot articulate with his lips the prayer — he should contemplate the berakhah or prayer he is obligated in; and even though he does not fulfill his obligation, etc. — end quote. And perhaps he did not mean literally filthy hands or an actually filthy place, but rather when the hands or place are not clean — yet there is no actual filth in them. And if he were not under duress, he would need to clean his hands and the place so that he could articulate with his lips. And now that he is under duress, etc. — nevertheless he may contemplate it since there is no complete filth there — end quote of Rabbeinu. And behold, Magen Avraham answered that where you find this — in the middle house of a bathhouse. And regarding hands it must be that they are ordinary hands and are not filthy; and nevertheless for prayer (but not for a berakhah, as above siman 4, se'if 26) one must clean with something that cleans, as below siman 92, se'if 4. And under duress, when it is impossible to clean them, speech is forbidden — nevertheless thought is permitted; see Taz.
