He may recite it in any language. See Mishnah Berurah regarding its statement that the same applies to prayer, Bircas HaMazon, Kiddush, and so forth. Although Magen Avraham wrote in the name of Tosafos that for Kiddush, blessings over mitzvos, and Hallel one fulfills the obligation even without understanding the language, the later authorities, including Korban HaEdah on Yerushalmi Sotah chapter seven, Magen Gibborim, and the glosses of Rabbi Elazar Landau, have ruled otherwise and demonstrated from several proofs that one does not fulfill the obligation. Regarding what Mishnah Berurah wrote, that in Lashon HaKodesh one fulfills the obligation even without understanding the language, see Peri Megadim, who is uncertain about this. It is surprising that he overlooked the explicit words of the Levush in siman 193, who writes that one who recites the blessing fulfills his obligation even if he does not understand the language. In truth, in siman 193 Peri Megadim expressed no uncertainty at all and cited the Levush as practical halachah. This is also ruled in practice in the glosses of Rabbi Elazar Landau to Sotah chapter seven; see there. I further wrote in Mishnah Berurah that such a case is uncommon because, in my humble opinion, even aside from this, he certainly does not fulfill his obligation in Krias Shema. Even assuming that the phrase "in any language that you understand" does not refer to Lashon HaKodesh, since its status as a language is not lost even when he does not understand it at all, he is nevertheless no better than one who recited without intent, who does not fulfill his obligation. I therefore wrote that such a case is uncommon. Know further that it appears obvious to me that matters which may be recited in any language may be recited only in a language spoken by the people of that country. If the people of the country cannot speak that language, and only he and a few individuals know it, it is not considered a language at all for that country, which does not recognize it. Lashon HaKodesh is inherently a language, whereas every other language exists only by social convention. Since the people of that country do not recognize this language, it is not called a language there. A proof is Kiddushin 6a: One who says "charufasi" in Judea effects kiddushin, because in Judea an betrothed woman is called a charufah, but elsewhere she is not betrothed, even though both parties intended kiddushin. Although kiddushin may be effected in any language, since this is not a term of kiddushin in that place, it is as though he gave the money without saying anything, and she is not betrothed. The Rosh and Shulchan Aruch likewise write that he must use a term of kiddushin employed in that place, apart from the Rambam's stringent view regarding "charufasi." I found a similar statement in the Ritva on the Rif at the beginning of Nedarim, that "in any language" means the language spoken in that place. Accordingly, understand correctly that when we wrote in Mishnah Berurah that he must also understand the language, it means that the fact that the people of the country speak it is not enough. Tosafos, who are lenient regarding Kiddush, blessings over mitzvos, and so forth, speak only in such a case. However, I examined siman 690, seif 9, which at first glance does not accord with our view. Perhaps Megillah is treated leniently because of publicizing the miracle. Yet the Ran there, in his discussion of this matter, does not imply such a distinction. This requires practical study.
