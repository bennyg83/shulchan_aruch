One may read it in any language — see Mishnah Berurah in what he wrote, and the same applies to prayer, Birkat Hamazon, Kiddush, etc. And behold, although Magen Avraham wrote in the name of Tosafot that in Kiddush, Birkat Hamitzvot, and Hallel one fulfills even if he does not understand the language — the Achronim already ruled [Knesset Hagedolah on Yerushalmi chapter 7 of Sotah, and in Sefer Magen Giborim, and in Hagahot R' Elazar Landau] and proved with several proofs that he does not fulfill. And what Mishnah Berurah wrote regarding the holy tongue that even if he does not understand the language he fulfills — see Pri Megadim who doubts this; and it is astonishing that he overlooked the words of Levush in siman 193 who wrote explicitly there that the one who blesses fulfills even if he does not understand the language — and in truth there in siman 193 he did not doubt this at all and cited the words of Levush as the law. And so it is written as practical law in Hagahot R' Elazar Landau on chapter 7 of Sotah — see there. And what I further wrote in Mishnah Berurah that this is not common, etc. — because in my humble opinion it appears that without doubt he certainly does not fulfill in Keriat Shema; for even if we say that what we say "in any language that you hear" does not refer to the holy tongue — for in it even if one does not hear it at all, the language is not nullified from him through this — nevertheless who is better than if he read and did not intend, for he does not fulfill; therefore I wrote this is not common, etc. And know further that it appears to me plainly that those things said "in any language" are specifically if the people of that country speak so; but if the people of the country cannot speak that language and only he and a few individuals know that language — this is not considered a language at all for this country that is not accustomed to that language. For the holy tongue is a language by essence; not so other languages — they are only by agreement of the country; and since the people of this country do not recognize that language, it is not called a language at all. And proof for my words from Kiddushin 6a: one who says "cherufati" in Judea — she is betrothed, for in Judea they call a betrothed woman "cherufah"; but in other places she is not betrothed even though both intended betrothal — and betrothal is in any language; nevertheless since this language is not the language of betrothal in that place, it is as if he gave money and said nothing — she is not betrothed. And so wrote Rosh and Shulchan Aruch that we require he say the language of betrothal of that place [only know Rambam is stringent regarding "cherufati"]. And similarly I found written in Ritva on Alfasi of Nedarim chapter 1: "in any language" means the language spoken in that place. And accordingly you will know correctly that what we wrote in Mishnah Berurah that one must also understand that language — means it does not help that the people of the country speak that language; and Tosafot who are lenient in Kiddush and Birkat Hamitzvot, etc. is specifically in this manner. (However I examined siman 490 seif 9 where it does not seem like our words; perhaps in Megillah they were lenient because of publicizing the miracle; however in Ran there in this matter it does not seem like our distinction — requires further study for practice.)
