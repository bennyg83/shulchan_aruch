'One may read it in any language' — see Mishnah Berurah in what he wrote; and the same is true for prayer and Birkat HaMazon, Kiddush, etc. And behold, even though Magen Avraham wrote in the name of Tosafot that for Kiddush, Birkat HaMitzvot, and Hallel one fulfills even if one does not understand the language, the later authorities already ruled [Knesset Yechezkel on the Yerushalmi, chapter 7 of Sotah; Magen Gibborim; Hagahot R' Elazar Landau] and compelled with many proofs that one does not fulfill. And what Mishnah Berurah wrote that in the Holy Tongue, even if one does not understand the language, one fulfills — see Pri Megadim who is uncertain about this; and it is astounding that he ignored the words of Levush in siman 193, who wrote there explicitly that the one who blesses fulfills even if he does not understand the language. And in truth, there in siman 193 he was not uncertain about this at all, and he cited the words of Levush as the halakhah. And likewise it is written as practical halakhah in Hagahot R' Elazar Landau, chapter 7 of Sotah — see there. And what I wrote further in Mishnah Berurah that this is not common, etc. — because in my opinion it appears that in any case one certainly does not fulfill in Keriat Shema; for even if we say that what we state 'in any language that you hear' does not refer to the Holy Tongue — that even if one does not hear it at all, the language is not nullified for him thereby — nevertheless, who is better than 'if he read and did not intend, he did not fulfill'? Therefore I wrote that this is not common, etc. And know further that it appears to me plainly that those matters that are said 'in any language' apply specifically when the people of that country speak so; but if the people of the country cannot speak that language, and only he and a few other individuals know that language — this is not considered a language at all for this country that does not conduct itself in that language. For granted, the Holy Tongue is a language by essence; but another language is not except by agreement of the country; and since the people of this country do not recognize that language, it is not called a language at all. And a proof for my words is from Kidushin 6a: one who says 'charufati' in Judea — she is betrothed, for thus in Judea they call a betrothed woman 'charufah'; but in other places she is not betrothed, even though both intended the name of betrothal; and kiddushin is in any language — nevertheless, since this language is not a language of betrothal in that place, it is as if he gave money and said nothing, and she is not betrothed. And so RaSh and Shulchan Aruch wrote that we require that he speak the language of kiddushin that is in that place [except know the opinion of Rambam who is strict regarding 'charufati']. And similarly I found written in Ritva on the Rif of Nedarim, chapter 1: 'in any language' means the language that they speak in that place. And therefore you will know correctly that what we wrote in Mishnah Berurah that one must also understand that language means that it does not help that the people of the country speak that language; and the Tosafot who are lenient in Kiddush and Birkat HaMitzvot, etc., is specifically in this manner. (However, I looked in siman 490, se'if 9, that apparently it does not sound like our words; and perhaps in Megillah they were lenient because of publicizing the miracle; however in Ran there on this matter it does not sound like our distinction — uncertain in practice.)
