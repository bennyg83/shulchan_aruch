In any language that you hear, etc. In the chapter 'Hayah korei,' the rabbis taught: Keriat Shema as it is written, for it is written 'and they shall be' in their being — the words of Rabbi [Yehudah haNasi]; and the Sages say: in any language, for it is written 'Shema' — in any language that you hear; and it is known that the halakhah follows the Sages. However, I wonder how Rabbina said in the chapter 'Mi shemeto' on the Mishnah that a baal keri contemplates Keriat Shema in his heart — this implies that thought is like speech; behold, this is not like the one who says that if one fulfills through thought, it is obvious that certainly Keriat Shema in any language — through thought the language is not relevant, as Rashba wrote in his novellae, Berakhot folio 14. And it appears to my humble opinion that Rabbina also did not say except regarding a baal keri, who is considered under constraint; but not under constraint — for everyone, he did not fulfill. And according to this, for us who hold like Rav Chisda, who argues on Rabbina, as Beit Yosef wrote — even under constraint he does not fulfill; and so I found that he ruled explicitly in Sefer Tamin De'im, siman 200. And if so, wonder on Shulchan Aruch and Levush, se'ifim 4 and 5, who ruled that one fulfills under constraint. And for practical law it appears to me that if it is impossible to articulate with his lips, he should contemplate; but when the constraint passes and the time of Keriat Shema still remains, he must return and read it; and see below siman 101; and see what I wrote at the end of this siman. Kenesset HaGedolah wrote: one who hears Keriat Shema and did not read fulfills his obligation — Maharam, part 1, siman 41; and in siman 42 he wrote in the name of R' Moshe Alashkar that he did not fulfill — end quote. And I looked into the responsum itself and saw that R' Moshe Alashkar attacked the Maharam; and his reason is that the mitzvah of Keriat Shema is the reading and not the hearing, for it is written 'and you shall speak of them'; and he brought many poskim who ruled so, and his words are correct; and so it appears from Sefer Tamin De'im there. However, specifically if he is expert he does not fulfill; or even when they are not expert — in an individual? But in ten, R' Moshe Alashkar wrote there itself in the name of Abudraham that the prayer leader exempts one who is not expert; and so wrote Magen Avraham, siman 59. And also in this I wonder: behold, Abudraham wrote folio 31 — this is his language: one may ask — why does the prayer leader say the berakhot of Keriat Shema in a loud voice in order to exempt one who does not know, and Keriat Shema he says quietly? And one may say that from the berakhot of Keriat Shema the prayer leader can exempt one who does not know because the matter depends on hearing, like reading; but from Keriat Shema he cannot exempt him because it is said 'and you shall speak of them' — end quote. Behold that even one who is not expert — the prayer leader does not exempt him; and if so, according to R' Moshe Alashkar the words of Abudraham contradict; and it requires study. And in any case, for practical law the words of R' Moshe Alashkar appear correct, that the prayer leader exempts one who is not expert; for it appears that since he is among ten and answers amen, it is as if he speaks himself — since he is not expert, and we read regarding him 'and you shall speak of them.' And a proof for this from the words of R' Yona that Beit Yosef brought in siman 69 — see there. And study is still required on what Abudraham wrote there that there is no fool in the world who does not know to say the verse Shema Yisrael, and through that he fulfills his obligation.
