What the author wrote in se'if 1 — "fulfilled" — Berakhot 15a-b; and one should examine: he was not precise whether he did not emphasize and moved between adhering words, or as R. A. wrote in Sefer HaChinuch, parashat Va'etchanan — but one who reads to pronounce precisely did not fulfill, as Tosafot wrote there, 13a, that letters missing that are in the power of reading are lacking; and one may say that he fulfilled, for by halakhah, mitzvot require intention — this comes from a Mishnah according to its plain meaning: "if he intended," etc., or an anonymous Mishnah and afterward a dispute — the halakhah is not like the anonymous one; and even one who reads to pronounce precisely for mezuzot and the like fulfills. And we expanded on this in our novellae; and see Rambam's commentary on the Mishnah and chapter 2 of Keriat Shema, halakhah 1, and so is written there. In Tur and Beit Yosef in the name of Mahari — they cool Gehinnom; nevertheless, sometimes truly measure-for-measure, and sometimes the opposite: he warms himself and they cool for him, per the time and need; and everything is included in "nevertheless."

And even initially — Taz. And behold, there is a remedy to clean with a pebble and dust, per R' A., letter dalet, Yeshu'ot Ya'akov; but in Keriat Shema his hands are also filthy and there is no water — he cleans with a pebble and dust and speaks with his mouth. And behold, there is some examination here, for here the author and Rambam wrote: if due to constraint he fulfills through thought; and likewise in siman 66 regarding prayer — see there. And it appears that the author's view is: without constraint, even bedieved he does not fulfill through thought — from Mishnah 15:1, "if he did not make heard, he fulfilled" — behold, thought — no; constraint — yes, from baal keri, chapter 2, like Rabbina there; and in the Yerushalmi they brought Beit Yosef here; and Magen Avraham, siman 185, halakhah 1 in Magen David — under constraint he fulfills, Rama, see there. And therefore, when the constraint passes, he need not return to recite and pray, for thought is like speech, and the Sages were strict without constraint that he does not fulfill, and under constraint they established on words of Torah. And behold, the author heard that one may [recite] in any language — if so, it is no worse than thought, as R' A. wrote, Berakhot 15; and also one need not make it heard to his ears — nevertheless, even initially it is only rabbinically when necessary that he hear Shema, that he intend and understand the first verse of what he says, as written in siman 63, se'if 4, and as written, Berakhot 13b and Taz 1: "be attentive and hear." But Rambam has a different approach here: in Keriat Shema, chapter 2, halakhah 1, "if he did not make heard to his ears, he fulfilled" implies that through thought he does not fulfill; and in chapter 1 of Berakhot, halakhah 6, all berakhot one fulfills through thought even without constraint — end quote there; and he distinguishes between Keriat Shema, which one hears in any language — thought, no — and other mitzvot. And even in Magen David, words of Torah one fulfills in his heart — if so, there is no proof to say that in Keriat Shema we require intention of the heart for the first verse, only intention to fulfill, which is nevertheless and Rambam, chapter 1 of Keriat Shema — there is no decisive ruling whether he holds only the first verse of words of Torah; and Peri Chadash in siman 67, and Eliyahu Rabbah there, will be explained. But Peri Chadash here and Chad wrote that when the constraint passes he must return to recite and pray — see there; and the wording of Shulchan Aruch implies as he wrote that he fulfilled and need not, as long as he was under constraint, like the Mishnah of baal keri and Rabbina on this — understand; and in our novellae we expanded, and here there is no place to expand. And in Levush here, se'if 5, he concludes that thought is like speech; and in words of Torah it is forbidden to contemplate in a filthy place — "and you shall make them holy" — and per the one who says thought is like speech, "and you shall make them holy" is not needed. Understand in Beit Yosef here. And see Eliyahu Rabbah there further in Magen Avraham on this.
