[1] And he was not precise in them, etc. Sefer HaChinuch, in parashas Vaeschanan, writes that this does not mean that he omitted words or letters, for if he did not recite all of Krias Shema he certainly did not fulfill his obligation. Rather, "he was not precise, yet fulfilled his obligation" means that he did not leave space between adjoining words, did not sharply pronounce the letter zayin of "tizkeru," or did not lengthen the dalet of "echad" as one should ideally do. This passage escaped the notice of Responsa Beit Yaakov, siman 76. The Gemara states that whoever is precise in reciting them is rewarded by having Gehinnom cooled for him. I heard the following mnemonic: the shin of Shema and the alef of echad form eish, "fire." The remaining letters, mem-ayin of Shema and chet-dalet of echad, form the initials of Chananyah, Mishael, Azaryah, and Daniel. This alludes that one who recites Krias Shema with intent is saved from fire like Chananyah, Mishael, Azaryah, and Daniel. So writes Magen Avraham.
