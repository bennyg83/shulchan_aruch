That he is not clean, etc. Thus Beit Yosef explained the words of R' Yona who permits thought; but I found in Perishah, siman 85, who disagreed with Beit Yosef, that wherever one is not clean means a filthy place, and it is forbidden to contemplate; and what R' Yona wrote that one may contemplate — his intent is not on the text of Keriat Shema and its berakhot, but rather that he contemplate that he is obligated to read Keriat Shema and its berakhot, and he should grieve that he cannot do it; then the Holy One, blessed be He, accepts the thought as if he were praying in a clean place — and this is permitted even in a completely filthy place; and so I saw in Shelah, folio 66, who explained the words of R' Yona thus. And Magen Avraham wrote on this that the wording of R' Yona does not imply so — end quote; and perhaps if he had seen that Perishah also explained thus he would have conceded. And likewise one who is ill or under another constraint — it implies in Perishah there that he should contemplate in this manner and not contemplate the text of the berakhah; and in this one may question: since he is not in a filthy place, why may he not contemplate the text of the berakhah? And this supports what I wrote above that thought is not like speech even under constraint. And behold, from the words of Rama one learns that Taz — one who is thirsty to drink at night in his bed, that he cannot wash his hands and bless — should contemplate the berakhah in his heart and drink — end quote. And one may distinguish between Keriat Shema, which is obligatory, and a berakhah on benefit; also it appears to me that in this it suffices if he rubs against the wall or with a pebble on anything clean and can bless, as above siman 4 and siman 92, se'if 7. Beit Yosef wrote, and this is his language: Kol Bo wrote that 'one may read in any language' applies in a congregation, but not individually, as R. Yehoshua said: a person should not ask his needs in Aramaic — end quote. And I do not know what question of needs there is in Keriat Shema; therefore it does not appear to distinguish; and so is the opinion of all the poskim, that they did not distinguish between individual and congregation — end quote of Beit Yosef. And so that Kol Bo not be considered mistaken, I looked and found a novel matter that Kol Bo wrote on the words of R. Yehoshua: every person of understanding must contemplate and not err to say that R. Yehoshua's intent is that the asker should ask his needs of an angel so that his prayer be joined — and not to offer and make heard before Hashem, blessed be He, as they said 'do not cry out to Michael and Gabriel but to Me cry out and I will answer you.' Rather, the explanation of the statement comes to awaken a person to intend in his prayer; and because Aramaic is foreign and is not fluent in a person's mouth, and the nature of the soul is to become mixed in it and intention is not found to pray in that language — they said that the ministering angels do not recognize it; meaning, the good forces from which good qualities are drawn are called ministering angels; and this means: if he has a defending angel; and the evil forces are called angels of destruction; and he warned about this because the forces of the soul are not practiced in that language and one cannot intend in his prayer because of the foreignness of the language — end quote. And if so, it is settled that since the reason is because of intention, it is reasonable that all the more so and a fortiori accepting the yoke of the kingdom of heaven requires more intention, and one should not read in Aramaic. But all the other poskim understood the statement according to its simple meaning — that the ministering angels literally do not recognize, as below siman 101; therefore they did not distinguish in Keriat Shema. And that Kol Bo permits in a congregation — one must say his reason is that then they practice with each other and intend more; also their merits are numerous — so it appears to me.
