[4] That he is not clean, etc. Beit Yosef thus explains Rabbeinu Yonah's permission to contemplate. I found, however, that Perishah in siman 85 disagrees with Beit Yosef. He explains that "he is not clean" means that he is in a filthy place, where even contemplation is forbidden. Rabbeinu Yonah's statement that he may contemplate does not mean that he should mentally recite the text of Krias Shema and its blessings. Rather, he should think that he is obligated to recite Krias Shema and its blessings and feel distress that he cannot do so. The Holy One, blessed is He, then accepts this thought as though he had prayed in a clean place. This is permitted even in a thoroughly filthy place. I also saw Shelah, page 66, explain Rabbeinu Yonah this way. Magen Avraham writes that Rabbeinu Yonah's language does not imply this. Perhaps had he seen that Perishah also explained it this way, he would have agreed. Regarding an ill person or another constraint, Perishah likewise implies that he should contemplate in this manner and not mentally recite the blessing's text. This can be questioned, because if he is not in a filthy place, why should he not contemplate the text of the blessing? This supports what I wrote above, that mental recitation is not equivalent to speech even under constraint. From Rama's words, Taz derives that one who is thirsty at night while in bed, and cannot wash his hands and recite a blessing, should contemplate the blessing in his heart and drink. One can distinguish between Krias Shema, which is an obligation, and a blessing over enjoyment. It also appears to me that in such a case it suffices to clean his hands on a wall, a pebble, or anything that cleans, and he may then recite the blessing, as explained above in siman 4 and siman 92, seif 6. Beit Yosef writes that Kol Bo states that Krias Shema may be recited in any language only with a congregation, but not by an individual, as Rabbi Yochanan said that a person should not request his needs in Aramaic. Beit Yosef responds that he does not know what requesting one's needs has to do with Krias Shema, and therefore sees no basis for distinguishing between an individual and a congregation, as all the poskim likewise make no distinction. To avoid regarding Kol Bo as mistaken, I examined his words and found a novel explanation. Kol Bo writes concerning Rabbi Yochanan's statement that every intelligent person must contemplate it and not mistakenly think that Rabbi Yochanan meant that one should direct his request to an angel, asking the angel to join his prayer and present it before Hashem, contrary to the statement, "Do not cry out to Michael or Gabriel; rather, cry out to Me and I will answer you." Instead, the statement comes to awaken a person to concentrate in prayer. Since Aramaic is foreign and not fluent in a person's mouth, the soul naturally becomes confused by it and proper intent is not readily attained when praying in that language. When the Sages said that the ministering angels do not recognize it, they meant that the good faculties from which good qualities flow are called ministering angels, as in, "If there is an angel over him, an advocate." The evil faculties are called destructive angels. The warning is that because the soul's faculties are not accustomed to that language, one will not concentrate in prayer due to the language's unfamiliarity. Accordingly, Kol Bo's view is understandable. Since his reason is lack of intent, he holds that certainly accepting the yoke of the Kingdom of Heaven, which requires still greater intent, should not be performed in Aramaic. The other poskim, however, understood the statement literally, that actual ministering angels do not recognize Aramaic, as explained below in siman 101, and therefore did not distinguish regarding Krias Shema. Kol Bo's permission with a congregation must be because people then habituate one another to the language and concentrate better, and also because their collective merit is greater.
