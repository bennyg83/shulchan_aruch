Fulfilled. And all the more so that one fulfills [the obligation] by hearing Keriat Shema from another, per Eshel Torah; and likewise the Maharam, part 1, siman 41. And see Magen Avraham, siman 61, se'if kaf-vav, and he wrote Eshel Torah — and it appears that specifically when one understands the language, and even in the Holy Tongue we require specifically that the listener understand; see siman 193, se'if 1, and in She'elat Ya'avetz he brought in the name of Birkat Avraham that specifically when an individual exempts an individual; but when an individual exempts the many, or an individual exempts two people in Birkat HaMazon — even if they do not understand the Holy Tongue, they fulfill [their obligation], per Eshel Torah. The Peri Chadash wrote, and it is reasonable: when the constraint is removed, if the time for Keriat Shema has not yet passed, he is obligated to return and recite, for we hold that thought is not like speech, per Eshel Torah; and Yedei Aharon disagrees with him and ruled that he is not obligated to return and recite Keriat Shema, since he already fulfilled [the obligation] at the outset when the time for Keriat Shema arrived through thought — for at that hour when he was under constraint, thought is like speech, per Eshel Torah.

Yes. From this one may learn regarding one who is thirsty at night in his bed, that he cannot wash his hands and recite a blessing — he should contemplate the blessing in his heart and drink (Taz). (And in Eliyahu Rabbah he disagrees with him, per Eshel Torah, and he also brought in the name of Perishah who disagrees with Rabbi [Yosef Caro], that wherever one is not clean it is forbidden to contemplate the text of Keriat Shema; rather he should only contemplate that he is obligated to recite Keriat Shema and its blessings, and he should grieve that he cannot do so, and he has reward for this.)
