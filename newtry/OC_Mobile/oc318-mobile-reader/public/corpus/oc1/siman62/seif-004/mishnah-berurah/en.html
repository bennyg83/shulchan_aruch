(7) "Fulfilled" — this "fulfilled" is not said completely; for we hold that thought is not like speech. Rather it means: in any case, at a time when he cannot speak, he should contemplate Keriat Shema in his heart and the Holy One, blessed be He, will set reward for him for this; but he does not actually fulfill thereby. Therefore when the constraint passes, if the time for Keriat Shema has not yet passed, he is obligated to return and recite (agreement of most later authorities; see Biur Halachah).

(8) "And even initially," etc. — Taz wrote from this that one may learn regarding one who is thirsty at night in his bed, that he cannot wash his hands and recite a blessing — he should contemplate the blessing in his heart and drink. In Mateh Yehudah he disagrees with him and wrote: behold, he can stand and wash his hands, and it is not comparable to one who is ill or under constraint, for one does not compare possible with impossible; and even if he has no water for washing and drinking, or it is difficult for him to stand because of cold — behold, he can wipe his hands on the wall or on anything that cleans; and this helps even when he knows with certainty that he touched with his hands covered places, as above siman 4, se'if 22 and 23.

(9) "That there should not be," etc. — and if that place is completely filthy, he should not contemplate the text of Keriat Shema or the berakhah; rather he should only think in his heart that he is obligated and cannot fulfill, and grieve over this, and the Holy One, blessed be He, sees the heart to give him reward for the thought, since he is under constraint. And it appears simple that those who drink in a bathhouse improperly act wrongly, for behold there it is forbidden even for thought, and he is not under constraint for this drink; and see below siman 84 for what I will write in the name of Peri Megadim on this matter.
