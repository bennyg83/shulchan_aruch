(3) [Se'if 2] One may read it in any language, etc., and there is no difference between an individual and the congregation. Beit Yosef. Bach. Prisha. Knesset HaGedolah in Hagahot Beit Yosef, letter alef. Olat Tamid, letter bet. Maamer Mordechai, letter alef. And specifically when he understands the language. Magen Avraham, note 1. Pri Chadash, letter alef. Mishna Berurah, letter alef; Shaarei Teshuvah, letter alef; Razah, letter bet. And likewise Rambam, in chapter 2 of Hilchot Keriat Shema, halachah 10. And so appears from the words of the Levush. The same applies to prayer and Birkat HaMazon. But Kiddush, Birkat HaP'ri, Birkat HaMitzvot, and Hallel — one fulfills even if he does not understand the language. Magen Avraham there. Mishna Berurah there. Shaarei Teshuvah there. Razah there. And see below siman 101, se'if 4, whether only the congregation may pray in every language or even an individual — see there. And see Peri Megadim, Eshel Avraham letter alef, who wrote that there is room to question regarding the Holy Tongue: if one does not understand, perhaps one fulfills as with Megillah — in a foreign tongue in Assyrian script one fulfills, but other languages require only Shema with comprehension; or the word Shema means that l'chatchila one must make it heard to his ears from the Torah, not rabbinically — see there. And see Knesset HaGedolah in Hagahot Taz letter alef what he wrote on this — see there.
