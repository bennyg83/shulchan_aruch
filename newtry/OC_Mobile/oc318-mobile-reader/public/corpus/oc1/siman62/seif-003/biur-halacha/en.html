And if he did not make heard to his ears, he has fulfilled — even in Keriat Shema, and all the more so in other mitzvot as stated in Berakhot 15b in the Gemara, and so ruled all the poskim. Rabba wonders at Sha'arei Teshuvah and Birkei Yosef below in siman 185 who cited in the name of Sefer Charedim that most poskim hold that if he did not make heard to his ears he does not fulfill — which is against the explicit Gemara above; perhaps his intent is that he does not fulfill the mitzvah in the optimal manner from the Torah, for in Behag and Rif and She'iltot they concluded on this that it is written "Shema — make heard to your ears," etc. — requires further study.
He has fulfilled — see Mishnah Berurah in what he wrote: therefore when the coercion departs, so is the agreement of Peri Chadash and Agur and Mateh Yehudah and Binyan Olam and Magen Giborim and other Achronim, not like those lenient in this and saying the author rules like the one who says thought is like speech from Beit Yosef in this siman and siman 85 and siman 185 it does not seem so; and so is proven from Beur HaGra that the author himself holds thought is not like speech. And in truth regarding the fundamental law whether thought is like speech or not — it is not proper to doubt this after the view of most Rishonim and almost all lean that it is not like speech: behold Ra'ah, Eshkol, Rabbeinu Yonah, Rosh, Or Zaru'a, Ra'avad, Mordechai, Tur, Rabbeinu Yerucham, and Shulchan Aruch — all ruled thought is not like speech. And so is proven also the view of Rashi in Berakhot 15b s.v. "belibo" — "did not make heard to his ears" implies so, but in thought not; and as Rosh and Rabbeinu Yonah and other Rishonim explained this braita; and so the Maor in chapter "one whose dead" explains this braita thus [only I saw a novelty in his words that it seems somewhat from him he rules like Rav Chisda only because it is a doubt of Torah law — if so according to him in other blessings if he blessed through thought he fulfills bedieved; and from other poskim who wrote anonymously the halacha is like Ra'ah from that sugya in Shabbat 150 — it does not seem so; also he himself wrote there finally Rav Chisda is preferable from that sugya — implies his view is not clear on this; also from Tur and Shulchan Aruch below siman 206 it seems the same in other rabbinic blessings as Rav Chisda]. And so Agur wrote: all the poskim he saw all hold thought is not like speech, except Rambam and Semag who hold that in blessings, even in Birkat Hamazon which is from the Torah, thought is like speech bedieved; and Riaz is extreme on the measure that even in Keriat Shema thought is like speech; and since all Rishonim disagree with them, therefore Shulchan Aruch sealed not like them; and so wrote Bach and Agur, and it is simple. And in Chayei Adam I saw he wrote one who thought the blessing in his heart — requires further study; and in my humble opinion in Birkat Hamazon certainly he returns and blesses, and even in other blessings one who relies on all the Rishonim above certainly did not lose.
