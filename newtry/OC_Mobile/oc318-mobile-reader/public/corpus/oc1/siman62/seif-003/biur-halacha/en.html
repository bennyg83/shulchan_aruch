'And if he did not make it heard to his ears, he fulfills' — even in Keriat Shema, and all the more so in other mitzvot, as stated in Berakhot 15b in the Gemara; and so ruled all the poskim. The Shaarei Teshuvah and Birkei Yosef later in siman 185 cited in the name of Sefer Charedim that most poskim hold that if he did not make it heard to his ears he did not fulfill — this is against the explicit Gemara above; and perhaps his intent is that he does not fulfill the mitzvah in the optimal manner from the Torah, as Bahag, Rif, and Sheiltot concluded on this, for it is written 'Shema — make heard to your ears,' etc. — uncertain.

'Fulfills' — see Mishnah Berurah in what he wrote; therefore when the constraint is removed, so is the agreement of Peri Chadash, Ateret Rosh, Mateh Yehuda, Binyan Olam, Magen Gibborim, and other later authorities, unlike those who are lenient in this; and to say that the author rules like the one who says from the Torah that thought is like speech in Beit Yosef in this siman and in siman 85 and siman 185 — it does not sound so; and likewise it is proven from Biur HaGra that the author himself holds that thought is not like speech. And in truth, regarding the fundamental law whether thought is like speech or not, one should not be uncertain about this after the opinion of most rishonim and almost all of them incline that it is not like speech — behold, the Rosh, Eshkol, R' Yona, Rosh, Or Zaru'a, Raavad, Mordechai, Tur, R' Yerucham, and Shulchan Aruch all ruled that thought is not like speech; and likewise it is proven from the opinion of Rashi in Berakhot folio 15b, in the entry 'b'libo' — 'he did not make heard to his ears' implies, but not through thought; and as Perishah, R' Yona, and the other rishonim explained this baraita; and likewise the Maor in the chapter 'Mi shemeto' explains this baraita so [only that I saw a novelty in his words that it implies somewhat there that he rules like Rav Chisda only because it is a Torah doubt — if so, according to him, in other berakhot if he blessed through thought he fulfills bedieved; and from other poskim who wrote anonymously that the halakhah follows the Rosh from that passage in Shabbat 150 — it does not sound so; also he himself wrote there at the end that Rav Chisda is preferable from that passage — it appears his mind is not clear on this; also from Tur and Shulchan Aruch later in siman 206 it appears the same regarding other rabbinic berakhot]. And likewise Ateret Rosh wrote that all the poskim he saw hold that thought is not like speech, except Rambam and Semag who hold that for berakhot, even in Birkat HaMazon which is deoraita, thought is like speech bedieved; and Riaz is extreme in the matter that even in Keriat Shema thought is like speech; and since all the rishonim disagree with them, therefore Shulchan Aruch was silent unlike them; and so wrote Bach and Ateret Rosh, and it is simple. And in Chayei Adam I saw that he wrote that one who contemplated the berakhah in his heart — uncertain; and in my opinion, in Birkat HaMazon he certainly returns and blesses; and even in other berakhot, one who relies on all the rishonim above certainly did not lose.
