(א) Forbidden in benefit. And if chametz is found on the road immediately after Pesach and we do not know whether it belongs to a non-Jew or a Jew — it is forbidden in eating and permitted in benefit (Beit Chadash and Magen Avraham — see there; and see siman 446 seif 4). Kaf HaChayim disagrees and raised: if the majority are Jews it is forbidden, and if half-and-half, and all the more so if the majority are non-Jews, it is permitted even in eating for one who is not careful about pat akum; and likewise any doubt regarding chametz that passed over Pesach is permitted even in eating — see there.

(ב) The opposite. Taz wrote that in every case we follow the lenient view, for there is no penalty here.
