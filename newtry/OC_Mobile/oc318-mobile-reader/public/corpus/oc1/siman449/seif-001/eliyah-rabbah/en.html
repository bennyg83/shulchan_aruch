(1) chametz found, etc. Magen Avraham wrote: chametz found after Pesach — if the majority of the city are Samaritans it is permitted; and likewise on the road if the majority of passersby are Samaritans; and even if the majority are Jews, if it is in a matter where one can attribute it to having occurred after Pesach, it is permitted. And regarding doubt of chametz that passed Pesach — Bach and Magen Avraham hold it is forbidden in eating and permitted in benefit; Chok Yaakov holds it is permitted in eating, for d'rabbanan doubt is lenient. He explained what Tosafot wrote in Eruvin daf 64 s.v. velamadnu etc. that it should be forbidden from doubt — meaning according to what occurred to him that chametz of Samaritans after Pesach is forbidden in benefit, perforce it is d'oraisa like R' Yehudah in chapter Kol Sha'ah, for Samaritan cooking has no d'rabbanan penalty, and he is correct. Maharsha and Ateret Zekenim wondered for nothing — see there. But what he explained in s.v. sheholkhin etc. — see there — is forced. Also what Maharsha and other commentators troubled themselves to wonder — it seems to me it is no difficulty, for one can say Tosafot holds like Baal HaItur that I brought above: chametz of a Jew that passed Pesach that he nullified is permitted in benefit, and presumably he nullified; and even though it deals when he nullified, nevertheless we say presumably he nullified; and furthermore it should only be a doubt like a doubt-of-a-doubt — requires study. One can also say perhaps Tosafot can prove from that they did not forbid in benefit — perhaps they hold Rabban Gamliel like R' Shimon according to R' Acha in Pesachim daf 29 and permit even d'rabbanan chametz of a Jew that passed Pesach if not that we must say they permit even in eating; and in truth perhaps Rabban Gamliel holds they penalized d'rabbanan only in eating, as Rava holds they penalized even in benefit; if so one cannot prove from that they did not forbid in benefit — requires study.
