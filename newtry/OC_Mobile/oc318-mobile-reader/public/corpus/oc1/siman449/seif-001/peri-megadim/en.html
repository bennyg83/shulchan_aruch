(א) So — Taz Pesachim 41b, and Beit Yosef explains Rabbenu Chananel reads the opposite and we follow the workers and judge as certain. Jewish workers — to forbid in benefit; non-Jewish workers — to permit in eating; it also deals when the inventory is bread and wine, nevertheless we follow the workers entirely; and for Rashi we follow the inventory of whom it belongs entirely — see there. Taz challenged Rabbenu Chananel: granted with non-Jewish workers it is permitted in eating from doubt and it is not a fixed shop for this purpose — still, non-Jewish inventory and Jewish workers, why forbidden and it is not a doubt to permit? This is what Taz wrote: why do we attribute more to workers than the shop, and let it be a doubt to permit in eating? As appears from the beginning of his words that Rashi is satisfied with non-Jewish inventory that it is a doubt and nevertheless permitted in eating — unlike Magen Avraham letter 2, and it will be explained there. And on this Taz wrote that Rabbenu Chananel agrees with Rashi regarding bread inventory — we attribute to whom the inventory belongs; and Rabbenu Chananel deals with inventory of other merchandise; if so according to Rashi and Rabbenu Chananel and Tur and Mechaber it appears they disagree, and he concludes they disagree regarding bread inventory: for Rabbenu Chananel it is a doubt and permitted in eating, for doubt of chametz that passed Pesach is d'rabbanan (where they eat pat akum); for Rashi the inventory is primary and Jewish inventory is certainly forbidden. It follows that regarding inventory of other merchandise, all follow the workers, for the majority is not following the shop owner even though he eats there sometimes. For bread inventory according to Rashi we follow the inventory, and if it belongs to a Jew it is certainly forbidden; for Rabbenu Chananel it is a doubt and permitted. It follows that for the law, in all cases it appears permitted, for it is a legal doubt; except it is like a double doubt — perhaps the halacha is like Rashi and it is certain. If you say like Rabbenu Chananel it is still a doubt perhaps it belongs to a Jew; and in Yoreh De'ah 110 we wrote in s.v. stringently for d'rabbanan (and see Pri Chadash in Klalim s.v. in one matter these stringencies combine — requires study in two rabbinic disputes). Levush explains like Beit Yosef and Bach — see Ateret Zekenim. Baer Heitev letter 2 wrote: Taz wrote in every case leniently — see there; this is for Rabbenu Chananel, but for the law one can say it is like a double doubt as written. Ohr HaChayim wrote Rabbenu Chananel's reason regarding bread inventory that we attribute to workers for they are the majority like coins before cattle merchants — see there; one can say there we follow the majority of buyers not sellers, unlike here where the majority of chametz is the inventory. See Temim De'im siman 245 daf 74b: Maharshal omitted the Rif's baraita of the shop because there are variant texts and all forbid; it implies there even in benefit; and furthermore otherwise it should still bring benefit, and it is difficult: if so, doubt should be forbidden in eating as Magen Avraham wrote regarding benefit, why forbidden — for the halacha is like R' Shimon and d'rabbanan doubt is lenient.

(ב) If one gives his animal after Pesach to a mumar who knows he will certainly feed it chametz that passed Pesach — forbidden; and in doubt during Pesach also permitted, as siman 448 in Magen Avraham 10 and Pri Chadash there regarding one who throws benefit-value to the Dead Sea; and in Yoreh De'ah siman 1 it does not imply so — see there he wrote b'dieved permitted.

(ג) See Pri Megadim siman 10 regarding one who gives his ox to a non-Jew and the non-Jew accepted responsibility — forbidden, for nevertheless it stands in a Jew's domain, unlike the Mechaber in siman 446 seif 4; nevertheless on Pesach it is permitted, for the non-Jew intends for his own benefit if he shares the offspring with him — see there. And likewise one who rents a horse to a non-Jew on the intermediate days of Pesach and knows the non-Jew will give chametz as wine — as explained above, the non-Jew's benefit is suspended, he does not have benefit from this — see there; requires study for the law. And what he wrote there that since the Mechaber wrote the view of those who permit first, it seems his view is so — I wrote in Klalim in Yoreh De'ah this is when writing one view anonymously; but some say letter Aleph and letter Aleph regarding Torah stringently and in the matter of doubt leniently — see Magen Avraham 446 seif 16 and it will be explained further in Notes.
