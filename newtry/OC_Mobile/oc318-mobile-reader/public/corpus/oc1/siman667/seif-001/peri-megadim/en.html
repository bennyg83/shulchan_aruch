Also on the eighth. See the Taz. Sukkah 46b: an esrog is permitted on the eighth, but a sukkah is forbidden. See Rashi: the mitzvah of sukkah is all day, perhaps for strolling and sitting, and perhaps the reading is not 'if a meal happens.' An esrog, once he has fulfilled the mitzvah in the morning, see there. See Beis Yosef, siman 665, regarding an esrog in the name of the Rosh, that we do not say muktzeh retroactively, while with a sukkah he must eat in it and perform an act; see there in siman 661. The Ran answered that lulav on the seventh day is rabbinic and was not fit during bein hashmashos, for a doubt is treated leniently. At first glance, the Rosh and the Ran, of blessed memory, do not disagree, and both are needed. For although an esrog was not fit during bein hashmashos, nevertheless it was set aside because of the doubt of the day, and one must say that we do not say muktzeh retroactively. The Ran's answer is also needed, for if so, the same should apply to sukkah; rather, there it is according to the Torah and here it is rabbinic. However, one can explain differently. The Ran answered that sukkah, being set aside for its mitzvah, is stricter, and we do say muktzeh retroactively. There is no difficulty from esrog, because it does not require an act. One can further say that sukkah, which is according to the Torah and is compared to the Chagigah, is stricter; this is unlike the muktzeh of esrog, which is rabbinic, where one can say that even for its mitzvah we do not say retroactively. See Levush, Eliyah Rabbah, and Eliyahu Zuta; according to what I wrote, it works well as we said. For us, a sukkah is also forbidden on the ninth for this reason, for if a meal happens during bein hashmashos of Shemini Atzeres, he must eat in the sukkah. See Eliyah Rabbah in the name of Maharil in siman 668, letter 5; see there. Although on Shemini Atzeres during bein hashmashos it is a rabbinic doubt and should be treated leniently like lulav, one can say that they enacted it like Torah law, since it is because of the doubt of the seventh day. Although it is like a double doubt, perhaps it is the eighth, since we are expert in establishing the month, and perhaps it is night, nevertheless, regarding anything the Sages forbade because of concern for a Torah prohibition, we do not say that a rabbinic doubt is lenient; see Yoreh Deah 110 regarding cheeses of non-Jews in the Shach.
