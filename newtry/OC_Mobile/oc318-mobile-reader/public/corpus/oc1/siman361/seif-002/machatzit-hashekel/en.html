(א) (s.k. 2) And above, etc., and if its interior, etc., does not need a ladder (Beit Yosef, Hagahot Ashiri, Peri Megadim) — therefore there is a printing error here, for Hagahot Ashiri wrote: it appears that if it were ten high even a ladder is not needed, end of his words; and Peri Megadim wrote: it is not comparable to a pillar in reshut ha-rabbim that is not ten high and the public leans on it, for this roof is ten high from the side of the courtyard — to what is this comparable to a deep pit of seven and an embankment of three completing it to ten, that the embankment is judged a private domain even though it is not higher from reshut ha-rabbim more than three, end of his words; and if so, whence did Magen Avraham derive this leniency of Hagahot Ashiri who wrote that if ten high even a ladder is not needed — it deals when it is ten high also toward reshut ha-rabbim as proven there, and does not deal at all with its interior; and from Peri Megadim it is obvious that not so, on the contrary it appears the opposite, for what he compared the law here to a pit whose interior is ten implies that here even if its interior is ten a ladder is needed; also in siman 345 he wrote plainly that if its interior is ten and from the courtyard side there are not ten, a ladder helps — it appears a ladder is needed; and it is impossible that Magen Avraham disagrees with him to bring his words and dispute him, or to write not like Avodat HaKodesh as is their custom; or it should read: and if its interior is ten even though from the courtyard side there are not ten, a ladder helps; and if also the border of reshut ha-rabbim is ten high, a ladder is not needed; and one law follows from Peri Megadim, for what he compared it to a pit whose interior is ten and on the outer side toward the border of reshut ha-rabbim there is not on any side ten high — it implies that the law here that a ladder helps even in such a case helps, namely when its interior is ten even though outside on no side is there ten;
