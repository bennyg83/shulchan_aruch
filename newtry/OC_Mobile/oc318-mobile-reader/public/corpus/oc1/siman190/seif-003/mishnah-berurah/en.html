(9) Drinking wine—and the same applies to other beverages; there is this doubt.
(10) Whether a kezayit suffices—as with eating, for we rule everywhere the measure of eating is a kezayit [and it is like half a kebeitzah as explained siman 486]; and accordingly for beverages one is obligated in berachah acharonah with a third of a revi'it, for a revi'it is a kebeitzah and a half as is known; and see siman 271 seif 13 what I wrote there in Mishna Berurah and Biur Halacha.
(11) Or a revi'it—as we find in several Torah laws regarding beverages whose measure is a revi'it.
(12) And here, etc.—meaning: in other drinks that are optional one can also remove doubt if he drinks less than a kezayit, for with this all agree he is exempt from blessing after; but here with the cup of birkat hamazon one cannot do so, for since it requires a cup one must at least drink melo lugmav which is rov revi'it, and that is much more than a kezayit.
(13) That he is rov revi'it—meaning for an average person whose measure is so; but for a very large person we estimate melo lugmav according to him; and nevertheless one need not drink more than a revi'it.
(14) A full revi'it—see Taz who wrote that for the essential law we rule melo lugmav; and therefore if he drank only melo lugmav he is obligated in berachah acharonah, but l'chatchila he should see to drink a revi'it to fulfill all opinions. Yet all later authorities rejected his words and raised that whoever did not drink a full revi'it, whether wine or other beverages, may not bless berachah acharonah, as Shulchan Aruch's view; and they also agreed there is no difference between other beverages and aged wine, even though with aged wine people's minds are settled with less than a revi'it—for the Sages did not divide measures; and not like Taz below siman 271.
