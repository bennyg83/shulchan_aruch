Seif 3. The quantity of wine one must drink to become obligated in an after-blessing. Note: Regarding the dispute among the poskim whether the required quantity of wine is a kezayis or a reviis, it appears to me that there is clear proof that a kezayis suffices from an explicit Mishnah in Meilah 15b. It states that five items concerning an olah combine with one another, and six items concerning a todah combine with one another: the meat, the bread, the wine, etc. This is difficult. How can the wine combine with the meat and bread when their required quantities differ, the latter being a kezayis and the former a reviis? The Mishnah later explicitly states that items whose required quantities differ do not combine. I saw that Tosafos in Zevachim 109, cited by Tosafos Yom Tov there in Meilah, noticed this and answered that it refers to bread soaked in wine, which is eaten in the normal manner and therefore has a required quantity of a kezayis. In my opinion, however, their words are difficult. The Gemara concludes in Pesachim, chapter 3, that regarding consecrated matters, permitted substance certainly combines with prohibited substance. If so, why does the Mishnah specify five items concerning an olah and six concerning a todah? With every korban, even ordinary wine, bread, or meat should combine to form a kezayis of piggul, nosar, or tamei, since regarding consecrated matters permitted substance combines with prohibited substance. However, the Gemara in Pesachim raises a similar objection regarding nazir, where it is taught from “anything made from the grapevine” that the prohibitions of a nazir combine with one another. Since permitted substance combines with prohibited substance, it should be obvious that one prohibition combines with another. The Gemara answers that permitted substance combines with prohibited substance only when consumed simultaneously, whereas one prohibited substance combines with another even when consumed successively. The same must apply here. The Mishnah’s statement that five items concerning an olah and six concerning a todah combine refers to successive consumption. Therefore the rule that permitted substance combines with prohibited substance does not apply. If it referred to simultaneous consumption, such as bread soaked in wine, the original difficulty would return: even permitted substance combines with prohibited substance. Necessarily it refers to successive consumption. The question of Tosafos then returns: their required quantities are unequal, so why do they combine? It must be that wine too has a required quantity of a kezayis. This appears to me a strong proof. Examine it carefully.
