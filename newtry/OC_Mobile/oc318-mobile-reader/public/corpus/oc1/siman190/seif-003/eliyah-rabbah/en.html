A full reviit, etc. Lechem Chamudot there emends the Rosh to read 'the majority of a reviit.' The Mordechai and Agudah in Keitzad Mevarchin and in Three Who Ate write in the name of Rabbi Yosi that one who drinks the volume of a kebeitzah recites the final blessing. But they ultimately conclude in the name of the Rambam, Semag, and Rabbenu Shimshon that a full reviit is required. Shibolei HaLeket, folio 20, writes likewise in the name of Rabbenu Simchah and Rabbenu Avigdor. The Rosh's wording can also be reconciled based on Tosafot Eruvin 4; note this well, for I have abbreviated. The Bach agrees, and Rabbenu Yerucham, the Tur, Agur, and Mahari Weil, siman 153, rule likewise. Responsa Rashba, siman 625, states that one who drinks the cup of Birkat HaMazon must drink a reviit. This means for purposes of the final blessing. Perhaps the Rashba holds that even intrinsically the cup of Birkat HaMazon requires drinking a reviit. This requires study. In this matter it seems to me possible to resolve all the objections of early and later authorities against Baal Halachot Gedolot, who writes that one need not recite MeEin Shalosh after the cup of Birkat HaMazon. He may mean that if one actually drinks a reviit he agrees the final blessing is required, but there is no obligation to drink that much. Note this well. I saw that the Taz argues at length that even less than a reviit requires MeEin Shalosh. His principal proof is: why should the final blessing be more stringent than the cup of Birkat HaMazon itself, which he says is also learned from the word 'and you shall bless' and requires only a cheekful? I have never seen or heard that the cup of Birkat HaMazon is derived from that verse. Berachot 48b explains that the verse refers to Birkat HaZan. In any event there is no difficulty, because the cup of Birkat HaMazon is comparable to Kiddush and Havdalah, where a first blessing of Borei Pri HaGefen is certainly required, whereas for a final blessing a reviit is needed. Similarly, a first blessing on food is recited even on less than a kezayit, while the final blessing requires a kezayit. The Taz also questions the Tur in siman 480, which rules that after the fourth cup one recites the final blessing. There is no difficulty, because that case concerns one who drank a reviit, and the Tur relies on what he wrote in the present siman. There the novelty is only that the final blessing is recited specifically after the fourth cup and not after the third. In conclusion, I remain with my ruling that no final blessing is recited unless one drank a full reviit. Even for distilled liquor a reviit is required, because the Sages did not distinguish.
