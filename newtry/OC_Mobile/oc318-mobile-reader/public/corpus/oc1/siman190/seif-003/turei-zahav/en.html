That he is rov revi'it. Explanation: in other drinks that are optional one can remove doubt—meaning if he drinks less than a kezayit then according to all he need not bless after it; and if he drinks a revi'it according to all he is obligated to bless; therefore he will do one of these two. But with the cup of Birkat haMazon it is impossible to arrange matters so he need not bless after it—meaning that he drink less than a kezayit—for since it requires a cup he must drink melo lugmav which is rov revi'it, and that is more than a kezayit; and it turns out he is obligated according to one view and exempt according to another view that requires specifically a revi'it; therefore it is impossible to remove doubt except by the second way that he drink a revi'it and be obligated according to all to bless after it; and it is simple in what Tur wrote siman 472 regarding the Passover cup that one need not drink the whole cup which is a revi'it but its majority; and in siman 190 he wrote regarding a revi'it cup that one blesses after it—and behold according to one view he is not obligated in berachah acharonah except with a revi'it; and one can say the first cups combine—for it is stated in siman 212 that drinkings do not combine if between them is more than the time to drink a revi'it; and if so our master Tur should have ruled that there is a distinction between cups—for the Passover cup the fourth requires a full revi'it to drink as he wrote here; and further it is simple: why does melo lugmav not suffice for berachah acharonah? And is it preferable to the cup of Birkat haMazon which is also learned from a berachah? And therefore it appears there is no absolute safek here—for the safek is as Beit Yosef wrote in name of Ra'ash whether a kezayit suffices for berachah acharonah as for eating; or since it is written "vesavata" and we expound it on drinking—from this it must be satiation measure which is a kebeitzah which is satiation of eating; or perhaps even more than a kebeitzah but satiation measure of a revi'it which is a kebeitzah and a half—for satiation of drinking is more than satiation of eating. And truly it is fitting that the obligation is even with rov revi'it—for the cup is a revi'it and drinking its majority suffices; and therefore Ra'ash first wrote the law of obligation is with melo lugmav which is rov revi'it; but afterward he wrote that Rashi was uncertain about this and wrote on this that it is good to be careful to drink a full revi'it—not so much as absolute safek—for if there were absolute safek here like all safekot he would have had to say it is forbidden to bless until he drinks a full revi'it; but certainly there is no absolute safek here—for certainly its majority suffices; but that there is at least a side to say we require more; therefore it is good for the most preferred side to drink a full revi'it. And in siman 486 I brought clear proof of this from Tur's words; and therefore Agur properly wrote in name of Rashi the Frenchman who explained Beit Yosef, and so it is: it appears one should be stringent to bless after it even if he drank less than melo lugmav—and that is when he drank four-fifths of an egg as we say in chapter Al Pi and that is when he drank the majority of the cup; end of his words. And Beit Yosef wrote on this: and I say this is stringency in a place where they said safek berachot lehakel; end of his words. And according to what I wrote in Kuntres Acharon there is no safek berachot from the essential law but from one side in general one can say here; and truly this Rashi the Frenchman is Rashi Ba'al haTosafot who was French, and Tosafot wrote his view in chapter 3 She'achlu daf 49, and so it is: and regarding drinking it appears to be stringent even with less than melo lugmav, etc.; but Ra'ash wrote that in chapter Al Pi Rashi was uncertain. And truly it is possible he retracted and we do not know which is later; therefore it is good to be careful, etc. And Levush explained the place of the safek above that in any case we require a kezayit—but the safek is whether the kezayit in liquid is like what there is in dry—a kezayit of honey—and from this it would be less than a revi'it; or also in liquid we estimate as if it were thickened and dried—from this in liquid it would be a revi'it as we say in the beginning of chapter HaMotzi'in. And I do not know what he thought to bring proof from a place that contradicts him—for Abaye said there perhaps regarding blood which is thick specifically it is so; but in wine which is thin there is in a kezayit more than a revi'it; and if so Rashi should have been uncertain that perhaps we require even more than a full revi'it; but certainly this safek does not exist at all; but rather as I wrote—from this there is obligation to bless if he drank melo lugmav from a large cup, or even less than this if he drank the majority of the cup as regarding a cup of berachah—as is clear to my mind.
