Which is the majority of a revi'it. Regarding other beverages, which are optional, one can avoid the doubt. If he drinks less than a kezayit, according to all opinions he need not recite a blessing afterward. If he drinks a revi'it, according to all opinions he must recite a blessing afterward. He should therefore choose one of these two quantities. With the cup of Birkat HaMazon, however, he cannot arrange matters so that no blessing afterward is required by drinking less than a kezayit, because a cup of blessing requires him to drink a cheekful, which is the majority of a revi'it and more than a kezayit. He is therefore obligated according to one opinion and exempt according to the opinion that requires specifically a revi'it. The only way to avoid the doubt is to drink a full revi'it, thereby becoming obligated according to all opinions. I find difficulty with the Tur's ruling in siman 472 concerning the cups of Pesach, that one need not drink the entire cup, which contains a revi'it, but only most of it, while in siman 480 he writes regarding the fourth cup that one recites a blessing afterward. According to one opinion, however, no blessing afterward is required unless a full revi'it was drunk. One cannot answer that the earlier cups combine, for siman 612 states that separate drinkings do not combine if more than the time needed to drink a revi'it elapsed between them. The Tur should therefore have distinguished among the cups and ruled that the fourth cup of Pesach requires drinking a full revi'it, as he writes here. There is a further difficulty. Why should a cheekful not suffice for the blessing afterward? Is this cup more important than the cup of Birkat HaMazon, which is likewise derived from the verse “and you shall bless”? It therefore appears that this is not a complete doubt. The Beit Yosef explains in the name of the Rosh that the doubt is whether a kezayit suffices for a blessing afterward, as it does for eating, or whether, since the verse states “and you shall be satisfied” and the Sages apply it to drinking, a quantity that provides satisfaction is required. Perhaps this is a kebeitzah, the quantity that satisfies with respect to eating, or perhaps drinking requires even more than a kebeitzah, namely a revi'it, which is a kebeitzah and a half, because satisfaction from drinking requires more than satisfaction from eating. In truth, it is proper that the obligation should apply even to the majority of a revi'it, because the cup itself contains a revi'it and drinking most of it suffices. Therefore the Rosh initially writes that the required amount is a cheekful, which is the majority of a revi'it. He later writes that Rabbeinu Yitzchak was uncertain about this and that it is proper to be careful to drink a full revi'it. He did not formulate this as a complete doubt. Had it been a complete doubt like other uncertainties in blessings, he should have said that one may not recite the blessing until he drinks a full revi'it. Rather, the primary law is certainly that the majority suffices, although there is a consideration that perhaps more is required. It is therefore preferable to drink a full revi'it. In siman 486 I brought clear proof for this from the Tur's language. Accordingly, the Agur properly cites Rabbeinu Yitzchak of France as saying that one should be stringent and recite a blessing afterward even if he drank less than a cheekful, provided he drank four fifths of a beitzah, as stated in the chapter Arvei Pesachim, namely that he drank most of the cup. End quote. The Beit Yosef comments that this is a stringency in a case where the rule is that in a doubt concerning blessings we are lenient. According to what I have written, however, there is no difficulty, because the primary law is not in doubt. There is merely an additional consideration. This Rabbeinu Yitzchak of France is Rabbeinu Yitzchak, one of the Baalei HaTosafot, who was French. Tosafot cite his view in the third chapter of Berachot, folio 49, saying that regarding drinking it appears proper to be stringent even with less than a cheekful, etc. The Rosh, however, writes that in the chapter Arvei Pesachim Rabbeinu Yitzchak was uncertain. It is possible that he retracted, and we do not know which statement was later. Therefore it is proper to be careful, etc. The Levush explains the doubt differently. In any event, he says, a kezayit is required. The doubt is whether a kezayit of liquid is measured like a kezayit of a dry substance, in which case it would be less than a revi'it, or whether liquid is evaluated as though it were congealed and dry, in which case a kezayit of liquid would equal a revi'it, as stated at the beginning of the chapter HaMotzi. I do not understand how he could bring proof from a passage that contradicts his explanation. Abaye says there that perhaps this applies specifically to blood, which is thick, but regarding wine, which is thin, the volume corresponding to a kezayit might be more than a revi'it. According to the Levush, Rabbeinu Yitzchak should therefore have been uncertain whether even more than a full revi'it is required. This doubt consequently cannot be correct. Rather, the matter is as I have written. One is obligated to recite a blessing afterward if he drank a cheekful from a large cup, or even less than that if he drank most of the cup, as with a cup of blessing. This appears clear to me.
