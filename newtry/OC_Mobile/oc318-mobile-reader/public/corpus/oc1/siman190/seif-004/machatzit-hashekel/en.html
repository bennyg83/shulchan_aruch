(s.k. 4) Drinking wine, etc.—and the doubt whether for satiation which is a revi'it. And meaning: for Rabbi Meir and Rabbi Yehudah dispute the measure of eating for birkat hamazon—Rabbi Meir holds a kezayit suffices and Rabbi Yehudah holds we require a kebeitzah. And the Gemara concludes on daf 49 that they dispute from "veachalta vesavata uvecharta"—Rabbi Meir holds "veachalta" is eating and "vesavata" is drinking, and unstated eating is a kezayit; Rabbi Yehudah holds "veachalta vesavata" is eating whose measure is satiation which is a kebeitzah. And we rule for eating like Rabbi Meir that a kezayit suffices; therefore Ra'ash in chapter 3 wrote he is uncertain whether Rabbi Yehudah also requires a kezayit measure for drinking as for eating.

Or perhaps since for Rabbi Meir we expound "vesavata" is drinking, Rabbi Meir concedes that for drinking we require satiation measure and require a kebeitzah; or perhaps one can distinguish between satiation of eating and satiation of drinking—therefore it is good to be careful to drink less than a kezayit or a revi'it to remove doubt; end of Ra'ash's words siman 24 with some Tosafot explanation. And Peri Megadim challenged and so did many later authorities—for a revi'it, who mentioned its name? For there is no doubt except whether a kezayit suffices, and for drinking is a kezayit satiation measure or a kebeitzah? And Rabbi Yosef ibn Migash in siman 271 wished to reconcile and his words are astounding; see in Magen Avraham. And Bach explained Ra'ash's words in what he concluded "or perhaps one can distinguish between satiation of eating and satiation of drinking"—meaning perhaps satiation of drinking requires more than a kebeitzah and requires a revi'it which is a kebeitzah and a half; for so Beit Yosef wrote in Beit Yosef that one is liable only when drinking a revi'it, as stated on Yoma daf 80; and for Beit HaLevi who is liable with melo lugmav, which is rov revi'it, who holds that with melo lugmav one's mind is settled. And for kiddush a revi'it suffices to settle one's mind; and for Beit Yosef who requires a revi'it one can say because he holds that also for kiddush satiation measure is required for drinking—behold revi'it measure is satiation measure for drinking; so is Bach's reasoning.

And if so there are three doubts: whether a kezayit suffices, or a kebeitzah, or a revi'it; and see Magen Avraham siman 271 s.k. 5.

And some wish to say that for aged wine, etc.; and so Taz wrote siman 271 s.k. 1.
