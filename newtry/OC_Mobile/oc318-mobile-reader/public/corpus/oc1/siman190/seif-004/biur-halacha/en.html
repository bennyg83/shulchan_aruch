One of them tastes, etc.—see in Chiddushei Rabbi Akiva Eiger who wrote that the one who blesses birkat hamazon should not bless Borei peri hagafen and the taster blesses—for perhaps birkat hamazon does not require a cup, and especially since we rule so for the essential law; if so it is not a berachah of mitzvot and he cannot bless for another if he does not bless also for himself—end of his words. And behold one who examines the responsum of Chafetz Chayim brought in Sha'arei Teshuvah—it does not imply so, for according to his words there if another blesses on the cup then the first did not fulfill the mitzvah of the cup at all—see there. And in truth the language of the Mechaber also implies that whoever holds the cup at the time of birkat hamazon is the one who blesses also on the cup [and so is proven more in Beit Yosef from what he brought from siman 271]—only that tasting suffices through another; and what he challenged: according to what we rule for the essential law that birkat hamazon does not require a cup—how can he discharge others if he does not taste himself? One can say: since for all it is in any case a mitzvah min hamuvchar, and as Rama wrote above in siman 182—it is in the category of berachot of mitzvot, for we hold even though yatza motzi; and also from the Mechaber's own words it is proven—for he himself brought many views on this matter in siman 182, and here he is silent and wrote that tasting through another suffices [even though he does not bless on it at all, as we wrote initially]—and he did not write that this matter depends on a dispute, but rather as we said. And know further: even for what Rama ruled later in siman 589 seif 6 that one who discharges women in shofar cannot bless for them if he himself already fulfilled, since they are not obligated in the matter—here it is more preferable, for here there is mitzvah min hamuvchar for all to bless on the cup, unlike there where no hint of mitzvah is mentioned in the Talmud regarding women; and it would be better that they themselves also not bless, but we cannot protest their hands on this; and in any case we certainly do not bless for them, as written there in Darkei Moshe—unlike in our matter.

From the fellow diners—see in Sha'arei Teshuvah in name of Chafetz Chayim that specifically if the fellow diners do not know how to bless themselves; but if they know, it is forbidden for him to bless on condition that others taste if he himself does not taste; and he brought proof for this from Rashi Eruvin 40b—see there. And in truth his words require study and do not imply so in the poskim—for according to his words one does not find this law except with his household or small children, for with them he can discharge them even though he need not bless for himself, as explained above in siman 167 seif 19—see there in Biur haGra and in the later authorities; and the poskim are silent and wrote that whether he himself or another may taste, and they did not divide at all; and therefore it is not comparable at all to that matter in siman 167—for there it is berachat hana'ah and one does not benefit and does not bless, as we say in the Talmud Rosh Hashanah daf 29—see there—unlike berachat hamitzvot, on the contrary we hold even though yatza motzi. And what he brought from Rashi's words—the examiner there in Rashi will see his words there are closed and lacking understanding even per his own words; and from the beginning of his words it appears the opposite—and if so one should not bring proof from there. And one should not bring proof for Chafetz Chayim's words from the Mechaber later in siman 273 seif 4 that also in kiddush, which is berachat hamitzvot, he cannot discharge if he already fulfilled except for one who does not know how to sanctify himself—for one, the Mechaber's words there are rejected from halachah as I brought there in Biur Halacha in name of the later authorities; and further, it is not comparable at all—for that case is different, for he already fulfilled and if so why should he sanctify and bless again for others where they themselves can sanctify—unlike here where he blesses birkat hamazon as obligated and also takes a cup per the enactment of the Sages to bless in birkat hamazon on the cup; and if so he blesses also for himself, only he does not wish to taste—and with this all concede well that another may also taste even though that other could bless himself; know that the Mechaber himself in siman 271 seif 14 is silent and wrote that if one of the fellow diners tasted, they fulfilled—and he did not divide at all whether the fellow diners can bless themselves or not; and so in Beit Yosef there this is not mentioned at all—see there; apparently this law of discharging also in kiddush for oneself in that matter of siman 273 mentioned above, where he sanctifies only for others, is not relevant at all; and so it is clear. And I found in Machatzit haShekel who also wrote that from the words of the poskim it does not imply as Chafetz Chayim's words.
