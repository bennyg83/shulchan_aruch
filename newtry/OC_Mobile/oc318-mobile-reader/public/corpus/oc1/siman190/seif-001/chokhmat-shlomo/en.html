Seif 3: the measure of drinking wine to become obligated in a final berachah. See: behold in law there is a dispute among the poskim whether the measure of wine is a kezayit or a revi'it; it appears in my humble opinion there is clear proof that a kezayit suffices—from an explicit mishnah in tractate Me'ilah daf 15b: five things in an olah combine with each other, and six in a todah combine with each other—the flesh, bread, wine, etc.; and it is difficult: how does wine combine with flesh and bread—behold their measures are not equal, for this is a kezayit and that a revi'it; and it is explicit in the mishnah there later that whatever their measures are not equal they do not combine. And I saw in Tosafot Zevachim daf 109, brought in Tosefot Yom Tov on Me'ilah there, that they sensed this and answered: in bread that soaked in wine, which is a way of eating and is a kezayit—see there. But in my opinion their words are astounding—for behold the Talmud concludes in chapter 3 of Pesachim that in kodashim certainly heter combines with issur; and if so, why mention specifically in the mishnah five things in an olah and six in a todah—behold in all korbanot even wine of non-sacred or bread and flesh of non-sacred also combine for a kezayit for piggul, notar, and tamei, for in kodashim heter combines with issur. But behold the Talmud challenges similarly in chapter 3 of Pesachim regarding a nazir, as taught: from all that is made from the vine, wine for nazirite prohibitions combine with each other—and behold heter combines with issur; issur with issur is obvious; and it answers: heter combines with issur at once; issur with issur even one after the other; and if so, here too in what the mishnah teaches—five things in an olah, six in a todah—it deals with one after the other, and therefore heter combining with issur does not apply here; and if so, perforce it deals with one after the other; and if at once, in bread that soaked in wine the conclusion would be that even heter combines with issur; and perforce it deals with one after the other; and if so Tosafot's difficulty returns to its place—for behold their measures are not equal, and why do they combine? Perforce also wine's measure is a kezayit—and this is a great proof in my humble opinion; and understand well.
