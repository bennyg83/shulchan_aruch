The blessing is an interruption, etc. This reasoning is not correct. What difference does this interruption make between Birkat HaMazon and drinking? The Sages said that the blessing follows immediately after washing, not immediately after drinking. Moreover, the blessing over wine is itself connected to Birkat HaMazon. One could also recite Borei Pri HaGefen first and only afterward wash and recite Birkat HaMazon. It appears that the Levush understood the Rosh cited by the Beit Yosef in this way, but, with respect, he did not analyze the wording carefully. The Rosh means that because Birkat HaMazon itself constitutes a diversion of attention and interruption from eating, it also diverts attention from the blessing on the wine and thereby creates an interruption between Borei Pri HaGefen and drinking. Kiddush and Havdalah, by contrast, do not constitute a diversion from drinking, so they are not an interruption even though one cannot drink and recite the blessing at the same moment.
Unless there is a cup, etc. This means that before the one leading drinks, he should pour from his cup into the cups of the others so that their wine will not be pagum. The common practice of pouring only after the one leading has already drunk is a complete error. The Bach and Perishah write likewise.
[Levush] He should not recite a blessing, etc. If he mistakenly recited the final blessing after drinking, he need not recite a new first blessing before drinking more, since he had intended to continue drinking. I am uncertain about this, because it should be no better than the cup of Birkat HaMazon, which nevertheless constitutes an interruption. Perhaps an actual mistake is different.
[Levush] In the chapter HaMotzi, etc. The Taz writes that he does not understand how the Levush can bring proof from a source that contradicts him. The Gemara there says perhaps the law applies specifically to blood, which is thick, whereas wine is thin and a kezayit of wine may exceed a reviit in volume. If so, the Levush should have remained uncertain whether more than a reviit is required. In my humble opinion there is no difficulty. Abaye says only that perhaps Rabbi Yosi holds this distinction, whereas Rabbi Natan certainly applies the rule even to wine. Moreover, Rav Yosef says that Rabbi Yosi also agrees with Rabbi Natan. I also saw that the Rambam, chapter 18 of Hilchot Shabbat, rules like Rabbi Natan. See Lechem Chamudot, folio 62.
