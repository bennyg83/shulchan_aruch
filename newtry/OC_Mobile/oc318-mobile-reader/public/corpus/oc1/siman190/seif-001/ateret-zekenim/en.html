He recites Borei Pri HaGafen, etc. See Tosafos in Perek Arvei Pesachim and Rosh for the reason Birkat HaMazon differs from other matters that require a cup. In those other cases Borei Pri HaGafen is recited first, whereas here Borei Pri HaGafen is recited last; see there. We say "Savri Maranan" even though the throat is empty, so that the blessing over wine should not be treated differently from one setting to another. The same applies to Kiddush and Havdalah. Several reasons are given why "Savri" is said over wine but not over beer. I found 1 reason: wine brought a curse upon mankind in the days of Noach, when he became intoxicated and the descendants of Canaan were cursed. We therefore say "Savri," meaning: understand that I intend to drink, and with your consent I drink so that no harm should result. For this reason the listeners customarily answer, "Lechaim."
