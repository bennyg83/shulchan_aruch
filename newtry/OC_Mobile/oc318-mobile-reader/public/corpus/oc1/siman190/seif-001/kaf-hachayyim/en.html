1) [Seif 1] After completing Birkas HaMazon, one recites the blessing, etc. This applies even if he already recited a blessing over wine during the meal. Beis Yosef; Levush; Shulchan Aruch HaRav, paragraph 1. Why is Borei Pri HaGafen not recited before Birkas HaMazon as it is before Kiddush and Havdalah? Birkas HaMazon is different because it constitutes withdrawal and a diversion of attention, and would therefore interrupt between the blessing and drinking. Tur; Magen Avraham, paragraph 1. Regarding the practice at Birkas HaMazon during a wedding feast to wait and recite Borei Pri HaGafen after the six blessings, the reason is that since Borei Pri HaGafen was instituted after Birkas HaMazon in such a case, close to drinking, it was likewise instituted at a wedding to be recited after the six blessings. Binyamin Ze'ev, siman 379; Knesses HaGedolah, Hagahos HaTur; Magen Avraham there. Some places, however, practice reciting Borei Pri HaGafen immediately after Birkas HaMazon, before the six blessings. See Maamar Mordechai, paragraph 11, who finds support for this custom in the words of the halachic authorities. Maran, of blessed memory, however, does not hold this view. Rather, at a wedding feast one recites the six blessings before Borei Pri HaGafen, as he writes in Even HaEzer, siman 62, seif 9; see there. In any event, where this practice is established, it should remain. The custom of our holy city, Yerushalayim, may it be rebuilt and established speedily in our days, follows the first opinion cited by Maran there, that the wedding blessings are recited over a separate cup. One cup is placed in the hand of the person leading Birkas HaMazon and Zimun, while another cup is placed before a different person. When the leader completes Birkas HaMazon and says, 'He Who makes peace,' etc., the other person takes the cup before him and recites the six blessings over it. The leader then recites Borei Pri HaGafen over the cup in his hand, over which he recited Birkas HaMazon. The contents of the cups are afterward mixed together. The leader of Birkas HaMazon and the person who recited the wedding blessings each drink from the cup in his hand. Some of the cup of the wedding blessings is given to the groom and bride, and some of the cup of Birkas HaMazon is given to the diners.
