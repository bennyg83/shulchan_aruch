After he drank the cup of birkat hamazon, etc.—see in Mishna Berurah what we wrote in name of Magen Avraham that specifically when his intent is to drink immediately, etc.; and it appears the intent of Magen Avraham is that through this he will need to bless again a first berachah on the drinking he will drink afterward anew—and this follows his reasoning above in siman 184 s.k. 9; but in truth many later authorities agreed there with the view of Even haEzer that whoever did not remove his mind from the eating or drinking—the first berachah is not voided even if he waited all day, as we wrote in Mishna Berurah above—see there; and if so, apparently there is no place for Magen Avraham's words also in this seif. Yet my heart wavers on this—for although if he waited long the first berachah is not voided since he did not divert his mind at all, nevertheless who will tell us he is permitted to do so—for in any case he loses the final berachah of this eating and drinking, and what he will bless at the end after a second eating and drinking does not help at all for this, since it is already digested and is as one who is not; and moreover it often happens that after some time his will changes or he is troubled by something and he does not eat and drink more—and if so he certainly loses his berachah entirely.

He blesses one berachah me'ein shelosh—and if his intent is to drink more he should not bless after it except at the end [Tur]; and Magen Avraham wrote that if he erred and blessed after it he need not bless at the beginning since his intent was to drink more; and the Gra and Chayei Adam in Laws of Kiddush Heaven quoted his words; and see in Chiddushei Rabbi Akiva Eiger in name of responsum Kiryat Melech Rav that specifically when he erred in the law and thought he must bless a final berachah even though he wishes to drink more—but if he forgot he has more to eat and blessed a final berachah, he must bless on what he will eat afterward—end of his words. And see in Avnei Nezer who is uncertain about Magen Avraham's law; and Bigdei Yesha and Maamar Mordechai disagree with Magen Avraham—and their view is that a final berachah is complete removal in all cases; and so sides Nehar Shalom—and therefore in practice requires study.
