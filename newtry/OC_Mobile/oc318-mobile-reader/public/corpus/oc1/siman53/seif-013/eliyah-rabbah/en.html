[18] Torn, etc. This is because of the congregation's honor. Responsa Maharam Mintz, siman 81, writes that the chazzan's clothing must be clean, without dirt or stains, especially his outer robe and cloak. His clothing should be long enough that his legs are not visible. If it is not sufficiently long, he should wear stockings reaching his knees, and it is proper that he have specially designated clean trousers for prayer. He further writes that it is proper for the chazzan to enter the synagogue first and leave last. When he stands to pray, he should clear his heart of every distracting thought and preoccupation, lower his eyes so that he does not look at anyone, and place his hands over his heart beneath his robe, with the right hand folded over the left. He should not separate them except when necessary to turn pages, arrange his tallis, or the like. He should not occupy himself with anything else, unlike some chazzanim whose hands are busy with frivolous matters, adjusting the lamps during prayer, examining their book of customs, or attending to other things. He should not act this way. This is a concise presentation of his words.
