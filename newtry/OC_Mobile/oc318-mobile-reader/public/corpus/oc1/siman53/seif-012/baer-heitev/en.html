**ayin-yod-nun.** And likewise for one who reads chet-tet-nun as ayin-yod-nun; and if they all speak thus, it is permitted for him to be shliach tzibur (Radbaz; Knesset HaGedolah; Magen Avraham; see there). And Tosafos objected at the end of chapter HaKore et HaMegillah: how did Rabbi put R' Chiya down before the ark when he decreed a fast in the workers, and they answered there that when he intended to read the chet he read it properly with effort, see there; but in the workers they answered that this applies where another is possible, but where another is not possible—no, see there. And likewise is the essential ruling: if it were so that he could read the chet with difficulty, how could he say to him "when you reach 'and I waited for the Omnipresent' you will not be found reviling and blaspheming"—perhaps when he reached that verse and the like he exerted himself to read properly and was not reviling and blaspheming; rather the essential ruling is as they answered in the workers. Peri Chadash.
