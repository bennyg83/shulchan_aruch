Alefs like ayins. Meaning that the pronunciation of ayin is stronger and deeper than the pronunciation of alef. The same applies to one who reads ches like hei, or who reads shibboleth as sibboleth. If everyone speaks that way, he is permitted to be shaliach tzibbur. Magen Avraham in the name of the Radbaz. Know further that the same applies to anyone who cannot pronounce the letters properly, such as one who is heavy of mouth or heavy of tongue, that we also do not send him down before the amud. This is implied by the Rambam, chapter 15 of Hilchos Nesi'as Kapayim, halacha 1, and chapter 8 of Hilchos Tefillah, halacha 12; see there in Kesef Mishneh. This requires study from the Yerushalmi, perek Hayah Korei, halacha 4, where it says 'his tongue was arranged'; see Pnei Moshe there, who permits in such a case because nevertheless he does not alter the letters from their form. However, according to how the author of Sefer Chareidim explained the Yerushalmi there, it is resolved.
And ayins like alefs. See Pri Chadash, who concluded that at a time when there is no one else as fit as he, it is permitted to send him down before the amud even if he reads ches like hei. But the above-mentioned Pnei Moshe disagrees and holds that even when there is no other, it is permitted only where, if he concentrates to read it as a ches, he can read it properly with effort. It further appears that even according to the Pri Chadash, it is not permitted to appoint him as permanent shaliach tzibbur, lest tomorrow someone better than he become available. Rather, only for this occasion is it permitted to send him down when there is no one as fit as he, like the incident of Rabbi Chiyya there in the Gemara.
