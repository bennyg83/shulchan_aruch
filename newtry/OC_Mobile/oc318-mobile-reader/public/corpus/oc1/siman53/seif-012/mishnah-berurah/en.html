(37) Alef for ayin, ayin for alef — meaning: the syllable of ayin is stronger and deeper than the syllable of alef; and the same applies to one who reads chet for hei or reads leshibbolet sibbolet. And if everyone speaks thus, it is permitted for him to be shaliach tzibbur [Magen Avraham in the name of Rashbaz]. And know further that the same applies to anyone who cannot pronounce the letters properly—e.g., he is tongue-tied or has a heavy tongue—likewise we do not remove him from before the ark [as is proven from Rambam, chapter 15 of Laws of Raising the Hands 1, and chapter 8 of Laws of Prayer halakhah 12—see there in Kessef Mishneh; and there is difficulty from Yerushalmi chapter "Hayah Korei" halakhah 4, where it states his tongue was arched—see Penei Moshe who permits in this matter because nevertheless he does not change the letters from their essence—see there; but according to what the author of Sefer Charedim there explained the words of the Yerushalmi, it is settled].

(38) And ayin for alef — see Peri Chadash who brought that when there is no other suitable like him, it is permitted to bring him down to the ark even with chet for hei; but Penei Moshe above disagrees and holds that even when there is no other, it is not permitted except where when he intends to read that letter as chet with effort he can read it well. And it seems further that even according to Peri Chadash one may not appoint him as permanent shaliach tzibbur lest tomorrow another better than he be found—only for this time is it permitted to bring him down when there is no one suitable like him, as that incident of Peri Chadash in the Gemara.
