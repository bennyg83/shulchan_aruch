He is permitted to be. For one is not called 'whose period is unseemly' except where he was accustomed to do improper things. But one who encountered an accidental transgression and regrets it is a completely righteous person in all respects, and it is permitted to appoint him initially as shaliach tzibbur.
He did it intentionally. This is not specifically murder, for the same applies to other transgressions, as above in seif 4, where we require that no bad reputation have gone out about him even from his youth. So the Kol Bo wrote explicitly. Eliyah Rabbah.
Nevertheless, a reputation went out, etc. See the Magen Avraham, who brought in the name of many poskim that even if he acted intentionally, as long as he repented he may be a regular shaliach tzibbur. Only regarding a public fast do we rule stringently that no bad reputation should have gone out about him even from his youth. But to be a regular shaliach tzibbur for the rest of the year, it is permitted to appoint him even initially once he has repented. The Peri Megadim brought similarly in the name of Eliyah Rabbah as practical halacha. Eliyah Rabbah wrote that for the Days of Awe one should at least be stringent and judge it like the law of fasts, for in truth even during the rest of the year there are many opinions among the poskim about this; see Kenesses HaGedolah and Biur HaGra. All this concerns not appointing him initially, but all agree that he is not dismissed if he repented. A shaliach tzibbur who many times released treifos from under his hand is dismissed from being shaliach tzibbur, for we again judge him like one who acted intentionally. See Peri Megadim, who is uncertain whether even one time causes us to judge him like one who acted intentionally. Nevertheless, if he repented, he is not dismissed from being shaliach tzibbur, even if he released treifos many times.
