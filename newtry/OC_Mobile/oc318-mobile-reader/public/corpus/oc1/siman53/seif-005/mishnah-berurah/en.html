(19) He takes precedence — even to be permanent shaliach tzibbur, provided he is thirteen years old and brought two hairs.

(20) Permitted to be — for it is not called "his chapter is not seemly" except specifically when he was habituated to do unseemly things; but one to whom an accidental misdeed came and repents—that is a complete righteous person in all his matters, and permitted to appoint initially as shaliach tzibbur.

(21) Did intentionally — not specifically murder; the same applies to other sins; and as above in seif 4 we require that no bad reputation emerged against him even from youth—and likewise in Beit Yosef explicitly [author].

(22) Nevertheless, if a reputation emerged, etc. — see Magen Avraham, who brought in the name of many poskim that even intentionally, if he repented he is permitted to be permanent shaliach tzibbur; for strictly regarding the Days of Awe we are stringent that no bad reputation emerged even from youth; but to be fixed shaliach tzibbur for the rest of the year it is permitted to appoint even initially whoever repented—and Pri Megadim brought in the name of the author for halakhah; the author wrote that on the awesome days at least one should be stringent to judge by the law of fast days; for truly even during the rest of the year there are many opinions among the poskim—see Knesset HaGedolah and Biur HaGra; and all this is not to appoint initially, but we do not remove him according to all if he repented. A shaliach tzibbur who issued many invalid slaughterings from days of youth—we remove from shaliach tzibbur, for we again judge him as intentional; see Pri Megadim, who doubts that even on one occasion we judge as intentional; nevertheless, if he repented we do not remove from shaliach tzibbur even if he issued many times.
