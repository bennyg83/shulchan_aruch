It is permitted to be a shaliach tzibbur. For one is not called "his chapter is not comely" except specifically when he was habituated to do unseemly things; but one to whom an inadvertent action came and who regrets—behold, he is a perfect righteous person for everyone (Darchei Moshe, Orach Chayyim). And from this wording it implies that even if he transgressed once intentionally and repented, it is permitted—until he is habituated to it. And see above, siman 2. And it is difficult why Rama wrote that [if done] intentionally, no; and R. Eliezer Ashkenazi, siman 157, wrote in the name of R' Eliyahu Mizrachi, siman 87, that even if he exchanged [faith] and repented he may be a permanent shaliach tzibbur; and so Machatzit Hashekel in the name of Mahara in responsa Beit Yosef siman 3. And see Raaviah part 2 siman 42, Ri'az, and Machatzit Hashekel part 1 siman 18—he wrote that one who became a tavern keeper and returned may be shaliach tzibbur according to all. And Maharshal in responsa siman 20 wrote that we do not require all the qualities except for those thirty—ibid. And see in responsa Rashba HaLevi section 14 siman 55, and Rashach part 2 siman 107: a shaliach tzibbur from whom non-kosher meat issued many times from his hand—they remove him from shaliach tzibbur (Machatzit Hashekel part 2 siman 75). And see Maharshal siman 20; and it seems to me that if he transgressed a sin under duress, according to all he is permitted, as written siman 128. It is good to seek a shaliach tzibbur who is a righteous son of a righteous man, for the prayer of a righteous son of a righteous man is not comparable, etc. (Bach; Maharshal). One whose arms have fallen off is permitted to be shaliach tzibbur, for the way of the Holy One, blessed be He, is to use broken vessels, as He said in the midrash: "and see that the shaliach tzibbur not be foolish" (Maharshal, first chapter of Chullin). But in the Zohar (Emor, page 173) it is written that this—that the Holy One, blessed be He, uses broken vessels—means one who is depressed and of low spirit; but one who has a blemish is defective. He elaborated on this at length; therefore a priest with a blemish is disqualified for service—and if so, with a shaliach tzibbur too one must be careful. And further: "offer it now to your governor" (Malachi 1:8), etc.
