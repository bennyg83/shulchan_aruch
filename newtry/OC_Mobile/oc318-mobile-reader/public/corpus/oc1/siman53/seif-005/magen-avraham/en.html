He is permitted to be shaliach tzibbur. For the phrase, his past is not becoming, applies specifically where he was habitually prone to do improper things. But one who had an accidental misdeed come to his hand and regrets it is a complete tzaddik in every respect, Darkei Moshe in the name of the Or Zarua. From this wording it implies that even if he sinned once intentionally and repented, he is permitted, until he becomes habitually prone to this. See Yoreh Deah, siman 2. It requires study why the Rama wrote that if it was intentional, he may not. Rabbi Aharon Sasson, siman 157, wrote in the name of Re'em, siman 87, that even one who apostatized and returned may be a fixed shaliach tzibbur. So too the Knesses HaGedolah wrote in the name of Mahara in the responsa of the Beis Yosef, siman 3. See Rav Avraham ben Chayyim, part 2, siman 42, the Maharit, and Mishpetei Tzedek, part 1, siman 18, who wrote that one who became a Muslim and returned may be shaliach tzibbur according to all opinions. Maharshal, responsa siman 20, wrote that we require all these qualities only for a communal fast; see there. See also Responsa Rabbi Shlomo HaLevi, Yoreh Deah, siman 55, and the Roshach, part 2, siman 107. A shaliach tzibbur from whose hand non-kosher meat was found to have gone out many times is removed from being shaliach tzibbur, Mishpetei Tzedek, part 2, siman 75. See Maharshal, siman 20. It seems to me that if he transgressed under compulsion, he is permitted according to all opinions, as written in siman 128. It is good to seek a shaliach tzibbur who is the son of a tzaddik, for the prayer of a tzaddik who is the son of a tzaddik is not comparable, etc., Bach and Maharshal. One whose arms fell off may be shaliach tzibbur, for it is the way of the Holy One, blessed be He, to make use of broken vessels, as stated in the Midrash. They should see to it that the shaliach tzibbur not be foolish, Maharshal, first chapter of Chullin. But in the Zohar, Emor, page 173, it is stated that the Holy One, blessed be He, makes use of broken vessels means one who is crushed and lowly of spirit, but one who has a physical blemish is considered defective; see there, where he elaborates on this. Therefore, a kohen who has a blemish is invalid for the service. If so, with regard to a shaliach tzibbur also one should be careful. Furthermore, the verse says, offer it now to your governor, etc.
