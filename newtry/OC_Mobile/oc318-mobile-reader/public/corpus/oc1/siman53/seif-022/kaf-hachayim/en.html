(22) There—and pleasing to the people. The chazzan should beware: if there is quarrel and strife between two members of the congregation, he should be silent and not act—neither in speech nor in deed, neither openly nor in secret; and all the more so if there is a matter of quarrel between a gentile and a member of the congregation, he should be silent and not act—even if that gentile is his father-in-law or his relative. And if he has any quarrel or enmity with one or with one of the congregation, then one must place the matter before the rabbi or before the congregation to compromise and do whatever seems right to them; and if there will be deficiency and obstruction from the opposing side, then he is innocent—what can he do?
