Sheliach tzibur — see Mishna Berurah; this flows from the responsum of the Rosh. From this a person can see the magnitude of the obligation upon every city to hire a rabbi who instructs them in the mitzvot of the Torah and its laws, how to conduct themselves, and that they should not be like the blind groping in darkness. For even if by this the mitzvah of prayer is entirely set aside among them, this obligation comes first; and in particular if the people of the city can also pray by themselves — certainly this comes first. From this will be filled trembling and fear in some of the towns outside the country that have no rabbi and mohel in their city — and it is a great sin, as we explained. Although they take a shochet and bodek for themselves, nevertheless they need a man of stature who instructs them in the laws of Shabbat and Yom Tov, the strict laws of Passover, the laws of mikveh immersion, and all the details of the laws of niddah and the rest of the laws of our holy Torah that a person should do and live by [unless their shochet and bodek is a great scholar, expert in the chambers of Torah, and has ordination for rulings from the famous great authorities of the country]. And all the more so where some hire a famous sheliach tzibur as a great musician for several hundred rubles a year to play for them in a pleasant voice — at such a rate they could have taken a rabbi and mohel, and also a sheliach tzibur who could pass before the ark on Shabbat, Yom Tov, Rosh Hashanah, and Yom Kippur as in our countries; and certainly their prayer for this would have been accepted on high. Unlike now, when the appointment of the sheliach tzibur is not for the sake of Heaven — on this it is said, "She placed upon me her voice, therefore I hated her." And the end of the matter is that by this the city goes from rejection to rejection, for they have no teacher and leader in the ways of God, and thereby come to desecration of Shabbat and Yom Tov, and eating doubtful chametz, God forbid — and who knows how many more prohibitions incurring karet, God forbid; may God protect us from such people.
