Chazzan. See Mishnah Berurah. This ruling is derived from a responsum of the Rosh, and from it a person should recognize the magnitude of the obligation upon every city to hire a rabbi who will instruct them in the mitzvos and laws of the Torah and teach them how to conduct themselves, so that they not be like blind people groping in darkness. Even if, as a result, the mitzvah of communal prayer would be entirely displaced for them, this obligation nevertheless takes precedence, especially where the residents can also pray on their own, in which case it certainly takes precedence. This should fill with trembling and fear those towns outside our country that have no rabbi and halachic decisor in their city. This is a grave sin, as we have explained. Although they hire a shochet and bodek, they still require a halachic decisor to teach them the laws of Shabbos and Yom Tov, the stringent laws of Pesach, the laws of immersion in a mikveh, all the details of the laws of niddah, and the other laws of our holy Torah, which a person shall perform and live by them. This is unless their shochet and bodek is a great Torah scholar, expert in the chambers of Torah, and has ordination to render halachic rulings from the renowned Torah authorities of the country. All the more so is this true of places that hire a celebrated chazzan, a great singer, for several hundred rubles a year so that he may sing for them in a pleasant voice, when for that sum they could hire both a rabbi and halachic decisor and a chazzan capable of leading the prayers on Shabbos, Yom Tov, Rosh Hashanah, and Yom Kippur, as is done in our country. Their prayers would certainly be accepted on high on account of this. By contrast, when the appointment of the chazzan is not for the sake of Heaven, of this it is said, “She raised her voice against Me; therefore I hated her.” Ultimately, because of this the city deteriorates from one failing to another, for they have no teacher or guide in the ways of Hashem, and thereby come to desecrate Shabbos and Yom Tov and to eat food subject to a concern of chametz, Heaven forbid. Who knows how many other prohibitions punishable by kares they may transgress, Heaven forbid. May Hashem protect us from such people.
