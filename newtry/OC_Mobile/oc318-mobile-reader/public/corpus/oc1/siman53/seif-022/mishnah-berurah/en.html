(63) "For pay is preferable" — for thereby no other unworthy person is permitted to presume to pray; and if it were by donation, permission is given to everyone, and when the matter is open anyone unworthy may go up. Moreover, the sheliach tzibbur himself will be more careful in his prayer and its correction, since he is hired. And even though there is [a cantor] for free, the poor must also give the sheliach tzibbur's wages, for a paid sheliach tzibbur is preferable. [Pri Megadim]

(64) To pray — or to blow [shofar]. [Beit Yosef]

(65) "Without consent" — and a person should not quarrel over any mitzvah, such as rolling a Torah scroll and the like; for we learned: the modest withdraw their hands from the showbread and the gluttons seize it — yet eating the showbread is a mitzvah. It is plain that this is specifically when the mitzvah can be fulfilled through another; but if the mitzvah would be entirely nullified — such as hospitality or supporting Torah study — and one can protest, he is certainly obligated to protest in order to uphold the mitzvah, for by law they compel in such matters, as explained in Choshen Mishpat siman 163 seif 1 in the gloss and end of seif 3.

(66) One does not answer — for this is not a blessing but a curse, as it is said: "He who blesses his friend with a loud voice early in the morning — it will be reckoned to him as a curse" (Proverbs 27:14).
