And it seems to me it is better, etc.—"and those who forsake the Omnipresent shall be consumed," etc. And it seems to me, etc.—except when they call him, etc. Apparently there is no difference between Kaf HaChayyim and Magen Avraham in law, and they do not disagree except in the reason for the matter: Kaf HaChayyim wrote because of "those who forsake the Omnipresent," and Magen Avraham wrote because of shortening of days—but the law does not imply thus; rather they also disagree in law, for even if they call him, he is still between man and man—for they have not yet begun to read from the Torah. And since Kaf HaChayyim wrote he should ascend so as not to be included in "those who forsake the Omnipresent shall be consumed," it must be that Kaf HaChayyim holds that even between man and man, if he goes out, there is concern for "those who forsake the Omnipresent." If so, even if they have not yet called him and he knows they will call him, nevertheless he may not leave. But since Maharil wrote that even if they called him, no portion applies to him—Kaf HaChayyim and from this we know Kaf HaChayyim's reason that even if they did not call him he may leave. But Magen Avraham holds that between man and man there is no concern for "those who forsake the Omnipresent," as written in Eliyah Rabbah siman 146 seif 1; if so, even if they called him he may leave, for he is still between man and man—only if they called him he must ascend for another reason, as written in Berachot page 55a: R' Yehoshua—three things shorten a man's days and years: one to whom a Torah scroll is given to read and he does not read, etc., as it is written "for it is your life and the length of your days"—end quote. Before they called him this does not apply; therefore if he knows they will call him, he may leave before they call him.
