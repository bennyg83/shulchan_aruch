“It appears to me that it is better,” etc. “Those who forsake Hashem will perish,” etc. At first glance there is no practical difference between Knesses HaGedolah and Magen Avraham, and they disagree only about the rationale. Knesses HaGedolah invokes “Those who forsake Hashem,” while Magen Avraham invokes shortening of one's life. The wording, however, does not indicate this. They also disagree in practice. Even after one has been called to the Torah, it is still between one aliyah and the next, since the next reading has not yet begun. Because Knesses HaGedolah writes that he must ascend lest he be included among those who forsake Hashem, he necessarily maintains that even leaving between aliyos falls under that prohibition. Accordingly, even before he has been called, if he knows he will be called, he may not leave. Yet since Maharil writes that even after being called he should not ascend, Knesses HaGedolah disputes him, and from Knesses HaGedolah's rationale we automatically know that even before being called he may not leave. Magen Avraham, however, maintains that leaving between aliyos does not involve forsaking Hashem, as he writes below in siman 146, seif 1. Therefore, even after being called it would still be permitted to leave on that ground, since it remains between aliyos. Once he has been called, however, he must ascend for another reason, as stated in Berachos 55a: Rabbi Yehudah said that three things shorten a person's days and years, including being given a Sefer Torah from which to read and refusing to read, as it is written, “For it is your life and the length of your days.” Before he has been called, this consideration does not apply. Therefore, if he merely knows that he will be called, he may leave before they call him.
