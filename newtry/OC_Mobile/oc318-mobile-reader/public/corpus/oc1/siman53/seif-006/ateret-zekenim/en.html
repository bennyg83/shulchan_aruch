One appoints as chazzan only one whose beard has fully grown, because of the honor of the congregation, etc. Even if the congregation wishes to waive its honor, this is ineffective. The same applies wherever Chazal enacted a rule because of the honor of the congregation, for the congregation is not empowered to waive its honor. Examples include rolling a Sefer Torah in public, a woman reading for the congregation, and a minor reciting Bircas Kohanim (my teacher, the Bach). This is also implied by the Beit Yosef, and so wrote the Rosh, Rashba, Ran, and Tosafos at the end of the chapter Lulav HaGazul, unlike Tosafos at the end of the first chapter of Chullin, whose words imply that it is permitted.
But on an occasional basis, once he has produced two pubic hairs, etc. If he is thirteen years and one day old, he may occasionally lead the prayers without being examined for the hairs, because we rely on the majority who have developed them (Levush). Some say that only a distinguished person should lead the prayers on Rosh Hashanah and Yom Kippur, or in a time of distress, Heaven forbid (Or Zarua). A person whom the attribute of strict justice has struck, such that his arms have fallen away, is especially worthy of serving as chazzan, for the Holy One, blessed is He, desires to make use of broken vessels, as it is written: “A broken and crushed heart, God, You will not despise” (Shelah). A chazzan is not disqualified by any bodily blemish whatsoever, except by insufficient years of age (Maharshal in the name of Maharam). Bodily blemishes disqualify only kohanim.
