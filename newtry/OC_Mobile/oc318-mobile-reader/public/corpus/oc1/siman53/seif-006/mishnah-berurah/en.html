The honor of the congregation. The congregation cannot waive their honor in this matter.
Occasionally. All this is during the rest of the year. But on fasts, Rosh Hashanah, and Yom Kippur, even occasionally we do not send down one whose beard is not fully grown, and even here one should not be lenient through the congregation's waiver.
Once he has produced two hairs. Meaning after he has reached thirteen years, for before then the hairs do not help. See the Magen Avraham, who wrote that presumably one need not check for the hairs in this matter, because for a D'Rabbanan matter we follow the majority, who produce two hairs at that time. See Peri Megadim, who wrote that according to the Rambam, who holds that prayer is according to the Torah, this shaliach tzibbur would be forbidden to discharge others through prayer until we know with certainty that he produced two hairs; see there.
Known. For this is considered fixed for those times. This does not include where the congregation appointed him to be ready to pray if at times the chazzan will not be in the synagogue, and then this one will stand and pray. There is no prohibition in that at all, for it is not called fixed, since it is possible that it will not come to that. They forbade a fixed appointment only for known times, when at that time he is certainly fixed. Taz. The Bach and Eliyah Rabbah disagree and hold that this too is called fixed, and it is not called occasional unless he was not appointed at all.
