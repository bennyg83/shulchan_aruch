For behold, see Taz. Magen Avraham, seif katan 13, likewise writes that thirteen complete years suffice and that an additional full day is unnecessary. See Eliyah Rabbah, seif katan 12, in the name of Responsa Bach, siman 145, that thirteen complete years do not require exact twenty-four-hour periods. Once nightfall arrives, it is sufficient. During bein hashmashos, however, it is not sufficient, because complete years are required until nightfall. Behold, Rambam, Hilchos Tefillah, chapter 8, halachah 11, writes regarding one whose beard has not filled out that the reason is the congregation's honor. There it appears that even occasional service is forbidden, because Rambam writes that he may lead the responsive portions of Krias Shema, as Peri Chadash notes. This may support the view that prayer is D'Oraisa. See siman 106. Otherwise, why may a minor not lead, since in siman 186 we rule that a minor can discharge a Rabbinic obligation? It must be because of the congregation's honor, as Beit Yosef writes in the name of Responsa Rashba citing Raavad. See Levush. If so, the congregation's waiver should be effective. See Beit Yosef regarding Maariv. The difficulty is why the law singles out a minor, since waiver should likewise help for anyone whose beard has not filled out, including a minor. We must therefore infer that prayer is D'Oraisa, and one obligated only Rabbinically cannot discharge one obligated D'Oraisa. This view does not accept Kol Bo's rationale in the name of Rabbi Nassan that the prayer of a man is required. See Beit Yosef and Levush. We would then interpret Rambam's phrase about leading Krias Shema to mean that he may even discharge others in Krias Shema. The Mechaber in seif 6, however, disagrees with Rambam. According to him, prayer may be Rabbinic, and Taz is correct in siman 106, seif katan 2, that it is a positive mitzvah not bound to a specific time because, according to our practice that Maariv was accepted as obligatory, its time extends through day and night. See Magen Avraham, siman 106, seif katan 2. Although women do not pray Maariv, nevertheless for men it is not time-bound. This will be explained, with Hashem's help, in siman 106.
