[6] And that no bad reputation has emerged about him, etc. Specifically to appoint him lekhatchilah; but to remove and dismiss him after he has been established as shliach tzibur—we have no [such leniency], however when a bad reputation has now emerged about him and it is an incessant rumor (kala d'lo paskik), even a single person may protest to remove him—so wrote Knesset HaGedolah in the name of responsum of Ra'avah (Mayim Amukim) siman 41. He also brought later poskim that we do not require all these qualities except on fast days; but on other days, even permanently—whoever is not wicked and is pleasing to the people and his voice is pleasant and he is accustomed to read—they appoint him shliach tzibur even if a rumor emerged in his youth—up to here. And it appears that on the Days of Awe in any case one should be stringent and apply the law of fast days; also during the rest of the year it is good to be stringent per Shulchan Aruch and Levush. Torat Emet wrote: one who goes to idolater courts is not fit to be shliach tzibur on Rosh Hashanah and Yom Kippur unless he has repented.
