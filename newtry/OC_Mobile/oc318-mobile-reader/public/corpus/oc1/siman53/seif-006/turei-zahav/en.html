Out of respect for the congregation. So wrote Rambam; and Beit Yosef — accordingly, if the congregation wishes to waive their honor, it is permitted to accept him; but Rosh does not hold thus, and his words are puzzling, for certainly even for Rambam waiver does not help, for the congregation's honor here is honor of Heaven — intending to honor Hashem through a shaliach tzibbur fit to be an advocate for the congregation before Him, blessed be He; and this is proven from what is stated in the chapter HaNizakin that one does not read from chumashim in the congregation out of respect for the congregation. And in siman 143 the poskim wrote simply that one does not read from chumashim in the congregation and did not mention the reason of congregation honor; and if waiver helped, they should have mentioned the reason so we learn from this that waiver helps — but certainly waiver does not help in these matters that touch honor of Heaven:

But occasionally, etc. In Beit Yosef he brings Tosafot's view in chapter Lulav HaGazul that one who descends on fasts and ma'amadot is like a fixed shaliach tzibbur and his beard must be fully grown — and so Rashba and Rosh. In the Tur it is written in the name of Rosh that the eminence of a shaliach tzibbur does not depend on family lineage — if he is from a disgraceful family but a righteous person, it is good to draw near one from distant seed, as it is said "peace, peace to the far and to the near." Maharshal wrote: nevertheless, if both are equal, certainly one of lineage takes precedence over one without lineage, for the prayer of a righteous son of a righteous man is not comparable, etc. In my humble opinion, from Rosh's writing "good to draw near from distant seed" we learn there is another besides distant seed — for the language "good to draw near" implies there is a choice; and nevertheless it is good to draw near this one who lacks lineage in order to draw him near to that seed for the Divine Presence, for the Merciful One desires the heart, and his prayer will be heard more than a righteous son of a righteous man — and this is learned from the verse "to the far and to the near," as above:

Known times. For this constitutes regularity for those times; and it is obvious that this is not included—if the congregation appointed him that he be ready to pray when sometimes the cantor is not in the synagogue and this one stands to pray—there is no prohibition at all in this, for it is not called fixed at all since it may not come to pass; and they prohibited fixed only for known times when at that time he is certainly fixed.
