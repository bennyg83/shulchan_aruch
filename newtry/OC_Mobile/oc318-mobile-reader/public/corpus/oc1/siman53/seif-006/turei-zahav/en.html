Because of the congregation's honor. Rambam writes this. Beit Yosef writes that according to this rationale, if the congregation wishes to waive its honor, it may accept him. He adds that the Rosh does not agree. His words are puzzling, for certainly even according to Rambam, waiver is ineffective. The congregation's honor in this matter is the honor of Heaven, since they intend to honor Hashem by appointing a chazzan who is fit to serve as an advocate for the congregation before Him. This is evident from the statement in the chapter HaNizakin that one may not read publicly from chumashim because of the congregation's honor. In siman 143, the authorities rule without qualification that one may not read publicly from chumashim, and they do not mention the reason of the congregation's honor. Were waiver effective, they should have mentioned the reason so that we could derive that waiver is effective. Rather, waiver is certainly ineffective in matters that touch upon the honor of Heaven.
But on an occasional basis, etc. Beit Yosef cites Tosafos in the chapter Lulav HaGazul, who maintain that leading the prayers on fast days and at maamados is comparable to serving as a permanent chazzan, and his beard must specifically have filled out. Rashba and the Rosh write likewise. In Tur it is written in the name of the Rosh that the qualifications of a chazzan do not depend upon distinguished lineage. If he comes from a disgraced family but is righteous, it is good to draw near one descended from those who were distant, as it is stated, “Peace, peace, to the distant and to the near.” Maharshal writes that if both candidates are equal, one of distinguished lineage certainly takes precedence over one who is not, for the prayer of a righteous person who is the child of a righteous person is not comparable, etc. It appears to me, however, that since the Rosh writes, “It is good to draw near one descended from those who were distant,” there must be another candidate descended from those who were not distant, for the phrase “it is good to draw near” implies a choice. Nevertheless, it is preferable to draw near the candidate who lacks distinguished lineage, in order to bring one of that lineage close to the Shechinah, for the Merciful One desires the heart. His prayer will thereby be accepted more readily than that of a righteous person who is the child of a righteous person. This is learned from the verse, “to the distant and to the near,” as I have explained.
At designated times. This constitutes a permanent appointment for those times. It is obvious that this does not include a case in which the congregation appointed him to be available to lead the prayers if the regular chazzan is occasionally absent from the synagogue. There is no prohibition in that case, for it is not considered a permanent appointment at all, since he may never actually need to serve. The prohibition against a permanent appointment applies only to designated times at which he is certainly appointed to serve.
