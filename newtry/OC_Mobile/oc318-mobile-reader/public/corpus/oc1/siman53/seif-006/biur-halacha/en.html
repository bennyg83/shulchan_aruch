One appoints only one who, etc.; but occasionally, etc. — see in Bahag, Laws of Community Needs; and from this flow the words of the Rambam, chapter 8 of the Laws of Prayer, who distinguishes between the recitation of Shema and going down before the ark, as stated in the Rambam's code, as above.

But occasionally — see in the Ran [brought in Beit Yosef], who wrote that this applies specifically when there is no one else there but him; and so it is stated in the novellae of the Ramban. The other Rishonim did not mention this. It would appear that one should bring proof that Shulchan Aruch does not hold this — for otherwise, in this manner he would also be permitted to be the sheliach tzibur permanently, as stated in seif 7 that he is permitted to be sheliach tzibur — and this means even permanently, as the Levush wrote there; and so it appears in the Mordechai's statement that occasionally one is permitted even when there is another. But one can reject the proof: in seif 7 it deals when we know, given the nature of the place, that throughout the year they will not have opportunity for a permanent sheliach tzibur whose beard has filled in; therefore it is permitted to appoint a thirteen-year-old son even permanently. Unlike in an unspecified case — one should not appoint him permanently, lest tomorrow they have opportunity for a sheliach tzibur whose beard has filled in.

He may go down [before the ark], etc. — Peri Megadim wrote in siman 332 that even if he is a mourner he should not go down before the ark when permanently appointed if his beard has not filled in, etc.; see there. And it seems to my humble opinion that regarding Maariv one may be lenient even when permanently appointed, for they do not repeat the prayer—only Kaddish and Barchu are said. And it is known that Rambam distinguishes between Keriat Shema and going down before the ark, as Levush wrote; but according to Rambam one should not distinguish between permanent and occasional appointment—only as we wrote. And so wrote Peri Chadash and Peri Megadim and Yeshuot Yaakov according to Rambam. And afterward I found that BeHaG also holds thus. And presumably the congregation forgives for a mourner. One may also combine here the view of Beit Yosef and Shaagat Aryeh, who are lenient in this, as stated above.
