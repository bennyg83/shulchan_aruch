One appoints only one who, etc.; but on an occasional basis, etc. See Halachos Gedolos, Hilchos Tzorchei Tzibbur. Rambam's words in chapter 8 of Hilchos Tefillah, distinguishing between leading the responsive recitation of Shema and descending before the lectern, derive from there, as Lechem Mishneh wrote, as explained above.
But on an occasional basis. See Ran, cited in Beit Yosef, who writes that this applies specifically when no one else is present. The same appears in the novellae of Ramban, while the other Rishonim make no mention of this limitation. At first glance, one may bring proof that Shulchan Aruch does not accept it. Were it so, under those circumstances he could also serve there as a permanent chazzan, as stated in seif 7, which permits him to serve as chazzan, even permanently, as Levush writes there. Maamar Mordechai likewise implies that occasional service is permitted even when another person is present. This proof may be rejected, however, because seif 7 addresses a place where, given the local circumstances, we know that throughout the year they will not find a permanent chazzan whose beard has fully grown. It is therefore permitted to appoint a thirteen-year-old even permanently. Ordinarily, however, one should not appoint him permanently, lest the following day they find a chazzan whose beard has fully grown.
He may descend, etc. Peri Megadim writes in siman 132 that even when he is a mourner, he should not regularly descend before the lectern if his beard has not fully grown, etc.; see there. It appears to me that regarding Maariv one may be lenient even on a regular basis, since the repetition of the Amidah is not recited, and he merely says Kaddish and Barechu. Rambam's view is well known, distinguishing between leading the responsive recitation of Shema and descending before the lectern. As Lechem Mishneh explains, according to Rambam the distinction is not between permanent and occasional service, but rather as we have written. Peri Chadash, Peri Megadim, and Yeshuos Yaakov also explain Rambam this way. I subsequently found that this is also the view of Halachos Gedolos. Moreover, the congregation presumably waives its honor for the mourner. One may also combine here the views of Beit Yosef and Shiltei Gibborim, who are lenient in this matter, as explained above.
