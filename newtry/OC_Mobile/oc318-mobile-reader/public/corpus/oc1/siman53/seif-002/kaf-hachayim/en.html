(2) Magen Avraham wrote at the beginning of this siman in the name of Shelah that all fifteen praises in Yishtabach from "a song and praise," etc., until "until now," one should say in one breath. However, in Zohar, parashat Terumah, 132a, it does not imply thus; rather, the prohibition is if one pauses in speech—and they are only thirteen praises from "a song and praise," etc., until "and the kingdom," as cited—see there. And so Shaarei Teshuvah, page 70 side d. And the Ari did not mention this in Sha'ar HaKavanot that it should be in one breath, as we brought his wording above, siman 48—see there. And so Chida in Kissei Eliyahu, siman 7, note 47, in the name of the siddur of Rabbeinu Yaabetz, that one should not say them in one breath, for they must be said pleasantly, as one who arranges the praise of the king—end quote. And so siddur Beit Yosef, note 13. Sha'arei Teshuvah, note 1; and Eshel Avraham, general rule 18, note 4, in the name of the Gra and Zohar. The text of Yishtabach and who established it—we have already written above, siman 48, in the name of Sha'ar HaKavanot—see there; and so Shaarei Teshuvah, page 70 side d and page 71 side a. Siddur Beit Yosef, note 14. Regarding answering Amen and everything involving sanctity, its law is as Baruch she'amar—see above, siman 51, note 11, and siman 5, note 4—only in the thirteen praises one should be careful not to bring himself to such [a pause].
