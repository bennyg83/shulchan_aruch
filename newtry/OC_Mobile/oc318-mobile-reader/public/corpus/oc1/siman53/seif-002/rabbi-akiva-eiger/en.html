Seif 2. “But rather between Yishtabach and Yotzer.” It is preferable that he recite the blessing over it between Yishtabach and Yotzer rather than interrupt between the zemiros and Yishtabach, because Yishtabach relates to them and is their concluding blessing. These are the words of Levush. Accordingly, one may say that it is specifically between the zemiros and Yishtabach that the blessing relates to them, and it is therefore improper to interrupt between them and their concluding blessing, which should be adjacent to the mitzvah. Earlier in Pesukei DeZimrah, however, he may at least put on the tallis and recite the blessing between sections, since even between sections of Krias Shema the Mechaber holds in siman 66 that he puts it on and recites the blessing. Even according to Rama there, who holds that he does not recite the blessing, putting it on is nevertheless permitted. All the more so should this be permitted between sections of Pesukei DeZimrah. Yet the language of Darkei Moshe here, concerning the words of Kol Bo, is that anyone who did not put on the tallis before Baruch She'amar should wait until after Yishtabach and then put on the tallis and recite the blessing. It is possible that in this respect Pesukei DeZimrah is more stringent than the blessings of Krias Shema, because here one can remedy the matter by waiting until after Yishtabach, since it is not so essential to recite Pesukei DeZimrah while wearing a tallis. For this reason, one may say that the same applies to tefillin. Although Rama rules in siman 66 that one puts on tefillin and recites the blessing even in the middle of a section, perhaps during Pesukei DeZimrah one should not put them on even between sections, since one can wait until after Yishtabach. The practical ruling requires study.
There, in the gloss. “Before he begins Yishtabach, etc.” If the tallis did not come into his possession until after Yishtabach, he wraps himself in it and recites the blessing before Kaddish. Magen Avraham, siman 54, seif katan 4.
