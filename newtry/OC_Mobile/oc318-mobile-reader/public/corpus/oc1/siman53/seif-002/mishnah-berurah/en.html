Unless. For Yishtabach is the concluding blessing established to be said after Pesukei D'Zimrah, and it begins without Baruch because it is adjacent to the blessing of Baruch She'amar. Therefore one should not say it without Baruch She'amar. This is unlike those who err when they are on the road or in another pressing circumstance and begin from Yishtabach, for through this they recite a blessing in vain.
He said Baruch She'amar. Therefore, a mourner or one observing a yahrzeit who came late to the synagogue close to Barechu, and who must at least stand before the amud to say Yishtabach, since it is forbidden to interrupt between Yishtabach and Kaddish, should at least first say to themselves Baruch She'amar and Ashrei. Likewise, on Shabbos, if the chazzan came late close to Barechu, he should at least first say to himself Baruch She'amar, Ashrei, and Nishmas, as above in siman 52 in Mishnah Berurah, seif katan 5.
