Atteret Tzvi challenged Levush not according to law—examine there. Nachalat Tzvi wrote: many err when they are on the road and there is no time to prolong, that they begin from Yishtabach; and one who does so first blesses a blessing in vain—up to here. Levush wrote: what the chazzan says Yishtabach before the ark and does not say it [is] what the zemirot say—that is so that Kaddish follow immediately after it. And now the custom has spread that if the permanent shliach tzibur is in the synagogue during the morning blessings, no individual says aloud the morning blessing and verses of zimrah—only the shliach tzibur says everything; and on days of Selichot whoever prays Selichot says everything. However, on ordinary weekdays, when there is a mourner or yahrzeit or circumcision who can pray with the congregation, the permanent shliach tzibur says the morning blessings, and the mourner or yahrzeit or mohel begins from Yishtabach; and so wrote Piskei Tosafot. And see below siman 133, where the laws of a mourner's prayer and Kaddishim are explained. And the reason they are accustomed to say Baruch She'amar on the bimah when they read from the Torah scroll—it seems to me because it corresponds to the Ten Commandments, which are ten 'Baruch,' as above.
