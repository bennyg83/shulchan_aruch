The Mechaber here retracts his earlier implication, for he cites Rambam's rationale of the congregation's honor and concludes with the Rosh's wording that he should be appointed, etc. This implies that even if the congregation waives its honor, it remains forbidden, as Bach and Magen Avraham, seif katan 9, write. Regarding the proof from siman 143, see Peri Chadash. Beit Yosef there writes that according to Mordechai and Rabbeinu Yerucham, the congregation's waiver is effective, whereas the Yerushalmi implies another reason. See there. According to Rama's gloss there in siman 143, which permits it in pressing circumstances, and Magen Avraham, seif katan 9, who distinguishes between the congregation's honor there and here, it might appear that waiver is effective for ordinary congregational honor. One may answer that here the congregation's honor is the honor of Heaven, and waiver is ineffective. When a Sefer Torah is complete but contains an error and one complete Chumash is available, there is no issue of congregational honor at all. This is straightforward.
Incidentally, Peri Chadash writes that Rambam maintains that even occasionally one whose beard has not filled out may not lead the prayer, based on Rambam, Hilchos Tefillah, chapter 8, halachah 11. Rambam writes that one who is thirteen years old may lead the responsive portions of Krias Shema, implying that he may not lead the Amidah even occasionally. Kesef Mishneh there writes that this implication is not exact, while Lechem Mishneh resolves it differently. One might ask why he did not raise the difficulty from Megillah 24a, where the Mishnah's specification of a minor implies that a thirteen-year-old may lead. It can be answered that a minor may not lead and the congregation's waiver is ineffective, whereas for a thirteen-year-old waiver is effective. We may say that the congregation's honor applies even occasionally, while Rambam would agree that for a permanent appointment waiver is ineffective. Nevertheless, the Mechaber does not hold this way.
