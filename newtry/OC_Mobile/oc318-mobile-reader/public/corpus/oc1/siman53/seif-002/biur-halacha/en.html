One may not say Yishtabach unless, etc. — see in Beit Yosef Taz, note 2, what he wrote regarding one who arrived at the time of Yishtabach; I did not copy it as practical law, because according to what he proved in the responsum of Mishkenot Yaakov with several proofs that one may not skip in any manner the blessing of Baruch She'amar and Yishtabach — and I brought this in Mishna Berurah in siman 52, note 6 — if so, this law follows automatically.

He said Baruch She'amar — and after the fact, one who skipped the blessing of Baruch She'amar and remembered after he had already said the verses of pesukei dezimra: Baruch She'amar, because it was fixed before pesukei dezimra specifically, and he already fulfilled his obligation by saying them — nevertheless he should say Yishtabach; for is one who did not bless before eating not to bless afterward? [from Peri Megadim] And it is obvious that the same applies to the blessings before and after Hallel.

And a few pesukei dezimra — written in Ikrei Dinim in the name of Ginat Veradim: one who must relieve himself before Yishtabach should properly return afterward at least for Tehillah leDavid before Yishtabach, so that the blessing has something upon which to take effect.
