One should not recite Yishtabach unless, etc. See Baer Heitev, seif katan 2, regarding what he wrote concerning one who arrives when the congregation is at Yishtabach. I did not reproduce it as practical halachah, because Responsa Mishkenos Yaakov proves from several sources that one may under no circumstances omit the blessings of Baruch She'amar and Yishtabach, as I cited in Mishnah Berurah, siman 52, seif katan 6. Accordingly, that ruling no longer applies.
He recited Baruch She'amar. After the fact, if one omitted the blessing of Baruch She'amar and remembered only after he had already recited Pesukei DeZimrah, he should not then recite Baruch She'amar, because it was instituted specifically before Pesukei DeZimrah, and he has already fulfilled his obligation by reciting them. Nevertheless, he should recite Yishtabach. Is it conceivable that one who did not recite a blessing before eating should therefore not recite the blessing afterward? (Peri Megadim). It is obvious that the same applies to the blessings before and after Hallel.
And some of Pesukei DeZimrah. The Ikrei Dinim writes in the name of Ginas Veradim that one who is compelled to relieve himself before Yishtabach should afterward repeat at least Tehillah LeDavid before Yishtabach, so that the blessing has something upon which to take effect.
