16) There: What constitutes this? He should be free of transgressions. Zohar, Vayikra, states: Rabbi Yitzchak said, 'If the anointed kohen shall sin'; this refers to the kohen below, who was appointed for the service and in whom a sin was found; 'bringing guilt upon the people.' Woe to those who rely upon him. Similarly, if a shaliach tzibbur sins and a transgression is found in him because the people were not found meritorious before the Holy One, blessed is He, woe to those who rely upon him. Sefer Chassidim writes that regarding shaliach tzibburs who are not complete in Torah and mitzvos yet descend before the lectern, it is said, 'Your advocates rebelled against Me.' Solel Boneh 1; Shalmei Tzibbur, page 72a. Rama writes in explanation of 'Show me your appearance': First correct your deeds, and the Divine image will rest upon your face as it did before you sinned. Afterward, 'let me hear your voice' in prayer and supplication, for then 'your voice is pleasant' before Me 'and your appearance is lovely.' The reason is that if a person merits some spark of the sanctity of the Shechinah, his prayer is accepted. He does not merit this unless complete in all 613 mitzvos, as Tikkunim explains concerning 'Any man in whom there is a blemish shall not approach.' Therefore, if any sin is found in him, woe to those who rely upon him. Solel Boneh, ibid. The shaliach tzibbur must consequently take great care not to interrupt with speech between the silent Amidah and its repetition. Kesher Gudal 18:21 and Siddur Beis Yaakov 10 write in the name of the Arizal's disciples that even for an individual, speaking between the silent Amidah and the repetition is a grave sin. All the more does this apply to the shaliach tzibbur, as Pischei HaDevir 111:3 and Kaf HaChaim by Rav Chaim Palagi 15:52 write; see there. How could he commit a prohibition and then serve as shaliach tzibbur, repeating the prayer before Hashem on the people's behalf? Does he not fear the verse, 'She raised her voice against Me; therefore I hated her'? This will be further explained in our comments to siman 124:1, with God's help.
