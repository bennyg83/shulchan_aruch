And see in responsum Emunat Shmuel siman 16 regarding an old man whose voice is low and weak and prolongs in prayer—that he should be removed. He wrote in responsum of Beit Chadash: tunes that are sung in the synagogue that are sung in houses of idolaters—there is no prohibition except in those tunes designated for them; but if they are not designated, there is no prohibition. Tur wrote: the chazzan is not dependent on yichus, etc.; nevertheless, if both are equal, certainly the one of yichus precedes the one without yichus, and one should be careful about this because the prayer of a righteous son of a righteous person is not similar, etc.; so wrote Beit Chadash in the name of Rashal; they brought Taz and Magen Avraham; and so I saw in Yam Shel Shlomo, first chapter of Chullin siman 48. But in responsum of Rashal siman 20 he wrote that specifically in his own prayer there is a distinction that the merit of his ancestors helps him; but one who prays on behalf of the congregation—on the contrary, it is important before the Omnipresent that he set aside the deeds of his ancestors and walked in the ways of Hashem, blessed be He—up to here; and so wrote Magen Avraham; and likewise in Yam Shel Shlomo itself, chapter Gid HaNasheh siman 17; and so wrote Taz from his own reasoning. And it is puzzling—for he omitted for the great ones mentioned above the words of Rashal mentioned above; and I also proved in Eliyahu Zuta from that which he explained in Asarah Ma'amarot on that which R' Eliezer said twenty-four [requests] and was not answered—R' Akiva went down and was answered; and not that this one is greater than that one, but that this one sets aside his traits. And he explained in Asarah Ma'amarot that setting aside the deeds of his ancestors—for R' Eliezer was of yichus and R' Akiva was not of yichus; and if so, it is difficult from that which the prayer of a righteous son of a righteous person is not similar; rather one must say that the prayer of R' Eliezer and R' Akiva, who would pray for the congregation, is different—if so, if he is also righteous, since he is not of yichus he is better still. But Yitzchak would pray for himself that he merit sons and not for the congregation—a righteous person of yichus whose merit of ancestors helps him is better. And the reason seems to me as I found in responsum of Ramban on that which everyone [is sustained] for Chanina my son, and Chanina my son suffices with a kav of carobs from erev Shabbat to erev Shabbat—and Ramban explained because he needs his merit and it does not help him in this world, but for his generation his merit helps; therefore the whole world is sustained for Chanina, etc. And the same applies here: when praying for himself, a righteous person of yichus whose merit of ancestors helps him is better, because his own merit does not help him; but when praying for the congregation, whose merit helps—a righteous person who is not of yichus whose merit is greater, who sets aside the deeds of his ancestors, is better—so it seems to me. But it is somewhat difficult from what Tur wrote below siman 579 regarding a shliach tzibur of fasts—that there not be among his sons and household and relatives and all associated with him one guilty of a sin—up to here. And one must say that since they are alive it is a demerit, as is stated in Shevuot page 39: you have no family with a tax collector that is not all tax collectors, etc., because they cover for him; and still it needs settlement. Rashal wrote in Chullin there: a chassid precedes a tzaddik, only that he have a pleasant voice; and so wrote Zohar, parashat Vayechi, on the verse 'raise your voice, daughter of waves.' Knesset HaGedolah wrote in the name of responsum of Mahari of Bruna: a shliach tzibur whose voice was disqualified—if it is heard as if he trembles and is broken, he is disqualified. He also wrote: if two chazzanim are equal, a kohen precedes a Levi, a Levi precedes an Israelite, and a Torah scholar precedes an am ha'aretz—even if he is a kohen, and all the more so before a Levi.
