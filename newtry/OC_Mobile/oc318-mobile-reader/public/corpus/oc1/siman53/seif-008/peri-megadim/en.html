Whose garments, see Taz. The Mechaber changed the language of the Mishnah and Tur, while Levush copied Tur's wording, “even in white,” etc. His intent is that the person may not lead at all, whether wearing colored or white garments. The same applies to one who says he will not lead barefoot. In seif 13, “one whose arms are exposed” is discussed. See Ran on Megillah 24a concerning the interpretation of barefoot. We discussed this at length in our chiddushim there, and it will be explained, with Hashem's help, in the laws of prayer. Peri Chadash writes that if immediately after saying that he will not lead in colored garments he explains that only white garments constitute proper honor for Hashem, he may lead. See there. This applies specifically when he subsequently provides a credible explanation. Eliyah Rabbah, seif katan 18, writes in the name of Maharam Mintz, siman 81, that the chazzan's garments should be clean, without stains or dirt. A shochet should change his clothing. The garments should be long, and if they are short he should wear stockings to the knees. This implies that he may pray barefoot, although in a place where one would not stand this way before dignitaries it is forbidden. He should stand in awe and should not handle the lamps to adjust them. See the cited customs.
