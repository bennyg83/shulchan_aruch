17) There: No bad reputation should have emerged concerning him, even in his youth. Re'em, Responsa 1:86, writes that Rambam disagrees with Baal HaTurim. Rambam holds that an ordinary shaliach tzibbur (not one serving during a fast) need not have an unblemished past. If he now possesses good deeds, then even if a bad reputation emerged in his youth, and even if he worshipped idolatry, once he repented he is fit to be a permanent shaliach tzibbur. This is the primary ruling. Rashach 2:107; Rabbi Aharon Sasson 157–158; Mahara di Boton, handwritten responsum 3; and Maharshal, Responsa 20, rule likewise. Maharshal writes that all these qualifications are required specifically on fasts and during maamados. On other days, even for a permanent appointment, anyone who is not wicked, is acceptable to the people, has a pleasant voice, and is accustomed to read may be appointed shaliach tzibbur; see there. Rashdam, Orach Chaim 32 and Yoreh Deah 113, however, writes that Rambam does not disagree with Baal HaTurim; even if he does, Baal HaTurim's view is primary. Mishpat Tzedek 1:18 writes that perhaps even according to Rashdam, one who became an Ishmaelite and then fully repented may serve as shaliach tzibbur, although the question posed to him did not concern an apostate; see there. Raavad 13 writes that this is also evident from Rashdam; see there. Knesses HaGedolah cites this in Hagahos HaTur and Shiyurei Hagahos Beis Yosef 2. Magen Avraham 8 writes that if he committed a transgression under compulsion, everyone agrees he is permitted. Shalmei Tzibbur, page 73c, rules likewise. Yad Aharon, Hagahos Beis Yosef, however, questions Rashdam's aforementioned words and concludes: For an ordinary shaliach tzibbur we do not require an unblemished past. Anyone whom we see has repented is called fit. This is the primary ruling, and most later authorities rule accordingly. Pri Chadash 25; Birkei Yosef 2 in the name of the later authorities, who writes that he also found this explicit in the handwritten Responsa of the great master Mahari ibn Migash 97; Maamar Mordechai 1; and Shalmei Tzibbur, page 73b. Olas Tamid 5 likewise concludes this, adding that if the congregation decrees a fast, then even if it is not presently an established public fast, such a person should not serve; all the more so on Rosh Hashanah and Yom Kippur. Eliyah Rabbah 6 rules likewise, but concludes that during the rest of the year too it is proper to be stringent in accordance with Shulchan Aruch and Levush.
