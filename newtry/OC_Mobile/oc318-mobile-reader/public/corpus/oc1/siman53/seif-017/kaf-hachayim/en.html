(19) Good) there—and that no bad reputation attached to him even in his youth—the Rabbenu Meir wrote, part 1 siman 86, that Rambam disagrees with our Rabbi the author of the Tur and holds that for a regular shaliach tzibbur who is not the fast-day shaliach tzibbur, we do not require that his section be pleasant; rather, anyone who now has good qualities—even if a bad reputation attached in his youth, and even if he worshipped idols—since he repented, he is fit to be a permanent shaliach tzibbur; and so is the main ruling. And so wrote Rashach, part 2 siman 107, and R' Sassoon, simanim 157–158, and Mahara Dei Bouton in responsa part 1 siman 3; and so Rashal in responsum siman 21, that we do not require all these qualities except specifically on fast days and Ma'amodot—but on other days, even as permanent [leader], anyone who is not wicked and who is pleasing to the people and whose voice is pleasant and who is accustomed to read from a Torah scroll—as shaliach tzibbur—see there. But Rashdam, siman 32, and Chayei Adam, siman 113, wrote that Rambam does not disagree with Beit Yosef, and even if he disagrees, the main ruling is as our Rabbi Beit Yosef—see there. Baal Mishpat Tzedek, part 1 siman 18, wrote it is possible that even according to Rashdam, if one became an apostate and repented completely, he can be shaliach tzibbur even though his question was not about one who converted his religion—see there. Ra'avad, siman 13, wrote this is proven from Rashdam's words—see there. Beit Yosef, Knesset HaGedolah in Hagahot Tur, and in Shiurei Hagahot Orach Chayyim note 2—see there. Magen Avraham, note 8, wrote that if he transgressed under duress, all permit. And so Shaarei Teshuvah 273. However, Yad Aharon in Hagahot Beit Yosef wondered at Rashdam's words and concluded: for a regular shaliach tzibbur we do not require a pleasant section, and whoever we see has repented we call him [to lead]—and so is the main point; and most later authorities ruled thus—see there—and so Peri Chadash, note 25, and Birkei Yosef, note 2, in the name of later authorities, and wrote he likewise found in the commentary of HaEshkol Hagadol Rabbeinu Mahari ben Migash in responsa part 1 siman 97. And so Maamar Mordechai, note 1. Shaarei Teshuvah 272. And Avnei Nezer brought this up, note 5, and concluded: it seems that if the community decrees a fast even though there is no communal fast now, nevertheless such a one should not pray as shaliach tzibbur—and a fortiori on Rosh Hashanah and Yom Kippur—see there. And so Ateret Rachel, note 6. However, Ateret Rachel concluded there that also the rest of the year it is good to be strict as ruled by Shulchan Aruch and Levush.
