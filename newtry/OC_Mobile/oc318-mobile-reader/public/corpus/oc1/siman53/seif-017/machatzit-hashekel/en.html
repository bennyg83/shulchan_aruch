(Note 17) "He is not," etc.—Tosafot wrote at the end of chapter 7 of Pesachim, 86b, on what we say there: R' Huna bar Beri—Rav Natan happened to come to the house of Rav Nachman bar Yitzchak; they said, let us set a bench; he sat; they gave him a cup; he accepted it in one time. They said to him: why, when we said set a bench, did you sit? He said: whatever the host tells you, do, etc. Why, when they gave you a cup, did you accept in one time? He said: they refuse the small one and do not refuse the great one. And Tosafot wrote: why, when sitting on a bench, is it not relevant to say "they refuse"—for sitting on a bench is grossness and lordship (for minors sit on stools—as Rashi there)—and he would not sit unless because of "whatever the host tells you," etc.—end quote. And what was needed regarding accepting the cup—for they refuse, etc.—and it would not suffice with "whatever the host says"—see Magen Avraham on 130, siman 170, and in Maharsha, part 1.
