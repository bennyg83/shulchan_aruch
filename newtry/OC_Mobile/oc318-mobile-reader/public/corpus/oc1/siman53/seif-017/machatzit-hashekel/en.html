Seif katan 17: “He should not,” etc. Tosafos at the end of the seventh chapter of Pesachim, 86b, comments on the incident in which Rav Huna son of Rav Nassan visited Rav Nachman bar Yitzchak. They told him, “Sit on the bed,” and he sat. They gave him a cup, and he accepted it immediately. They asked why he had sat when told to do so, and he answered, “Whatever the host tells you, do.” They asked why he accepted the cup immediately, and he answered, “One may initially refuse a lesser person, but one may not refuse a great person.” Tosafos writes that his sitting on the bed could not be explained by the rule against refusing, because sitting on a bed is an act of haughtiness and authority, as younger people sit on benches, as Rashi explains there. He should therefore not have sat had it not been for the rule, “Whatever the host tells you, do.” Regarding acceptance of the cup, however, the Gemara needed the separate reason that one may not refuse a great person. Why the rule concerning the host was insufficient for accepting the cup is discussed by Magen Avraham below, siman 170, and by Maharsha in Chiddushei Aggados.
