(seif 19) there are those who prevent, etc., and even an individual, etc. NB: see Beit Yosef—the reason is that it is like a sacrifice, and one may not offer a person's sacrifice against his will; and it was brought in Avkat Rochel. It is difficult for me—for on the contrary, from the words of Tosafot in Kiddushin [page 28b] regarding 'these kohanim are agents of the Merciful One' it is proven the opposite: from their words it is clear that if they were our agents he could object; implying that if they were agents of the Merciful One he cannot say he does not want that kohen and sacrifice against his will—and behold we hold kohanim are agents of the Merciful One; if so, it is proven the opposite. And this is: that shliach tzibur is different, for he is our agent. In any case, the language of Beit Yosef is not precise in its reason; rather, on the contrary, if it were similar to a sacrifice it would be appropriate that one not be able to object [and see what I wrote at length in the words of Tosafot, with the Omnipresent's help, to the community of Lvov, in my omissions to tractate Nedarim page 35, still in manuscript, see there, examine well]: (ibid.) In the above seif, 'if not that they already agreed on him beforehand.' NB: see in my composition Mishpetei Tzedek, Choshen Mishpat part 2 siman 288, in my composition and in Shulchan Aruch siman 231, what was written in a responsum to the community of Kantshek—that therefore Shulchan Aruch was precise 'if not that they agreed on him beforehand' and did not write 'if he did not protest beforehand'—for one could say that through silence he can afterward appeal and say he erred in the law and thought an individual cannot protest, and now he knows the law that he can protest; only if he agreed explicitly he cannot afterward claim any argument. See there his reason, with the Omnipresent's help, examine well.
