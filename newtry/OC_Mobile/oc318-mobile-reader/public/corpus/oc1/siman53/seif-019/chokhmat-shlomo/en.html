Seif 19: “Some prevent, etc., and even an individual, etc.” Note: See Beit Yosef, who explains that prayer stands in place of an offering, and one cannot offer another person’s sacrifice without his consent. This is cited by Eliyah Rabbah. I find this difficult, because Tosafos in Kiddushin 23b, regarding whether the kohanim are agents of Hashem, imply the opposite. Their words indicate that if the kohanim were our agents, an owner could prevent a particular kohen from offering his sacrifice. It follows that because they are agents of Hashem, he cannot say that he does not want that kohen, and the kohen may offer it against his will. We rule that the kohanim are agents of Hashem, so the proof points in the opposite direction. Granted, one may answer that a chazzan is different because he is our agent. Nevertheless, Beit Yosef’s wording and rationale are imprecise, since if the chazzan were truly analogous to one who offers a sacrifice, an individual should not be able to prevent him. See the lengthy discussion I wrote, with Hashem’s help, concerning Tosafos’ words for the holy community of Lviv. It found its place in the addenda to Nedarim 35a and remains in manuscript. See there and analyze it carefully.
There: In the same seif, “unless he had already consented to him initially.” Note: See my work Mishpat Tzedek on Choshen Mishpat, mahadura dalet, siman 188, and my comments to Shulchan Aruch, siman 231, in the responsum to the holy community of Kontshka. I explained that the Shulchan Aruch deliberately writes “unless he had consented initially,” rather than “unless he had not objected initially.” Mere silence may still allow him later to object and claim that he had erred in the law, believing that an individual had no right to protest, and that he has now learned that an individual may protest. Only where he expressly consented can he no longer raise any claim afterward. See there for the reasoning, with Hashem’s help, and analyze it carefully.
