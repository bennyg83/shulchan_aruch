Even an individual. He may also object to all appointments, even to a Torah teacher, Rashbam, siman 40, Rabbi Baruch, siman 25, and Mishpetei Tzedek, simanim 75 and 76. However, this is specifically where he wishes to accept someone else; see there, Knesses HaGedolah. In Yam Shel Shlomo, first chapter of Chullin, siman 51, he wrote that this applies specifically where there is another person to hire for the wages of this one. Then an individual may object. The Levush wrote that nowadays we follow the majority of those who pay taxes, even if relatives who are invalid as witnesses are among them. There are places where they appoint eleven or thirteen people over this matter, and all of this is so that disputes not increase, and we do not pay attention to an individual. So too the Knesses HaGedolah wrote in the name of Maharshdam and Rabbi Avraham di Boton, responsa of the Beis Yosef, siman 2. It seems to me that specifically in their times, when the shaliach tzibbur discharged the public from their obligation through his prayer, an individual could prevent it, for he cannot be made his agent against his will. This is not so now, when all are fluent, and the shaliach tzibbur is only for the piyyutim. Although he says Kaddish and Barchu, there is not such a great concern. Rav Avraham ben Chayyim, part 2, siman 41, wrote that even according to the opinion that an individual may object, if he comes with a claim of disqualification, he may not object, for there is concern for damaging the person's reputation.
Who already agreed to him. A majority may object even after they agreed, Knesses HaGedolah. Even a minority can compel the majority to accept a Torah teacher, Rabbi Shlomo HaLevi, Knesses HaGedolah.
Before he agreed to him. But if he became his enemy after he had agreed to him, he cannot object. Rather, the shaliach tzibbur is obligated to say explicitly that he removes the jealousy from his heart and will discharge him through his prayer, Maharam Padua there. See siman 581. In Darkei Moshe in the name of Or Zarua: a shaliach tzibbur need not flatter the congregation in matters of Heaven, and he must rebuke them. In the name of Rabbi Yehudah Chassid he wrote that a shaliach tzibbur must be beloved to the congregation, for otherwise, when he reads the rebuke, it is dangerous for one who does not love him. He said that if one hates him, he should not go up, etc. See Maharil, siman 102, who wrote that even when they call him, he should not go up; see there. To me it seems that it is better to go up than to enter the punishment of, and those who abandon Hashem shall perish, Knesses HaGedolah. And to me it seems that this was said specifically about one who leaves the beis haknesses during the Torah reading. But between one person and the next, it is permitted to leave. Rather, when they call him and he does not go up, he shortens his days, as stated in the ninth chapter of Berachos.
