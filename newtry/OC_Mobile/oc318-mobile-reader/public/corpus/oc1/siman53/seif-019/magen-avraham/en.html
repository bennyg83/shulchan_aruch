Even an individual. And also in all appointments one may protest, and even regarding one appointed to spread Torah (Rashbam siman 40, and Rabbenu Yerucham siman 25, and Machatzit Hashekel siman 75, note 6). However, specifically when he wishes to accept another on his Shabbat (Kol Bo). And in Yeshuot Shmuel, chapter 1 of Chullin siman 91, he wrote: specifically when there is another to hire at his expense—then an individual may protest. And Levush wrote: and now they follow the majority of tax payers, even if there are disqualified for priesthood among them; and there are places that appoint eleven or thirteen men for this—and all so that dispute should not multiply, and they do not heed an individual. And so in Kol Bo in the name of Rashdam and Mahara Dei Votin siman 2. And I hold that specifically in their time, when the shaliach tzibbur fulfilled the congregation's obligation in his prayer, then an individual could block—for he is not made their agent against his will; not so now, when all are proficient and the shaliach tzibbur is only for piyyutim—even though he says Kaddish and Barchu, there is not such strictness. And Raaviah wrote, part 2 siman 41: even per the view that an individual may protest, if he comes with a claim of disqualification he may not protest—for there is concern for his disgrace.

Before they agreed upon him. But if he became his enemy after they agreed upon him, he cannot protest; rather, the shaliach tzibbur is obligated to say explicitly that he will remove jealousy from his heart and include him in his prayer [Maharam Padwa ibid.]. And in siman 491: in the name of Or Zaru'a, a shaliach tzibbur need not flatter the congregation in divine matters, and must rebuke them. And in the name of R. Yehuda Chasid he wrote: a shaliach tzibbur must be beloved by the congregation—for otherwise, when he reads the rebuke, it is dangerous for one who does not love him; and he said that if he is his enemy he should not ascend, etc. And see in Maharil siman 102, who wrote that even when they call him he should not ascend on his Shabbat. And I hold that it is better to ascend than to enter punishment—and "those who forsake the the Omnipresent shall come to an end" [Kol Bo]. And I hold that specifically one who leaves the beis haknesset at the time of Torah reading—about him it is said "this"; but between man and man it is permitted to go out, except when they call him and he does not ascend—his days are shortened, as stated in chapter 9 of Berachot.
