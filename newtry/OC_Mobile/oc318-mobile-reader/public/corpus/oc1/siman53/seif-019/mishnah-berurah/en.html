From being shaliach tzibbur. Because he cannot say, 'G-d of our fathers.' But when he prays privately, he should say, 'G-d of the fathers of Israel.'
And their words were rejected. For he can also say, 'G-d of our fathers,' since Avraham is called the father of a multitude of nations, as it is written, 'for a father of a multitude,' because he taught the entire world faith in G-d. A mamzer serving as shaliach tzibbur depends on the dispute whether a mamzer is valid to write tefillin. So it is written in Shiurei Kenesses HaGedolah. According to this, since the Magen Avraham in siman 39 wrote in the name of the Darkei Moshe that he may write tefillin, he may be shaliach tzibbur. However, in Birkei Yosef, Yoreh De'ah 281, he concludes that he should not be shaliach tzibbur, though tefillin that he wrote are valid.
Can object. At the time of appointment. The reason is that prayers were instituted corresponding to the daily offerings, and all Israel have a share in the daily offerings. Who can bring another's offering without his consent? This applies unless he already agreed to him, in which case neither an individual nor half the congregation can remove him, even if they wish to pay his full wages, since he did not act improperly. But the majority of the congregation can remove him if they pay his full wages. Or Zarua. Nowadays, the authority to remove him is only in the hands of the seven communal officers, or the elected representatives in each place according to its ordinance. Peri Megadim.
I do not want. This is specifically where there is another shaliach tzibbur whom the congregation can hire for the same money. But if none is available, or one is available but he would increase the cost, the congregation can say to the objector: we are not able to add, rather he should add from his own funds. Even if he clarifies his claim that the second is superior to the first relative to the additional cost, nevertheless the congregation can say: we do not have sufficient ability to add money.
That so-and-so be chazzan. The same applies to all appointments: he can object, even regarding a scholar who spreads Torah, meaning a rosh yeshiva. This is specifically when he wants to accept someone else. But if he objects because he does not want there to be one who spreads Torah in the congregation, it is obvious that his objection has no substance, and even a minority can compel the majority. Even when he wants to appoint someone else, if the one whom most of the congregation accepts is superior to this other person, he also cannot object. It is possible that even a minority can compel the majority to hire the superior person. Kenesses HaGedolah. The later authorities wrote that this law of the Shulchan Aruch applies specifically in their days. Today, when it is known that, because of our many sins, many people maintain disputes without reason or substance and without intent for the sake of Heaven, if every individual had to be asked regarding appointments, whether for appointing a shaliach tzibbur, one who spreads Torah, an av beis din in the city, and the like, they would never agree. Therefore we follow the majority of taxpayers, even if relatives who are disqualified as witnesses are among them. Magen Avraham. Nowadays the custom is to follow the seven communal officers or the elected representatives of the congregation for this, each place according to its custom. Peri Megadim. All this is so that dispute not increase, and we do not heed an individual. The Magen Avraham wrote another reason that today the law of an individual's objection does not apply regarding a shaliach tzibbur. Specifically in their times, when the shaliach tzibbur discharged the public from their obligation through his prayer, an individual could object, for he is not made his agent against his will. But nowadays, when everyone is knowledgeable and the shaliach tzibbur is only for the piyutim, even though he says Kaddish and Barechu, there is not so much concern.
Initially. If they accepted him for a set time and the time was completed, it is not called having agreed to him initially, for now it is a new acceptance and is as though he had not yet been there. Taz. See what we wrote at the end of the preceding seif katan. Reuven was a shaliach tzibbur hired for a year, completed his year, and began praying for several days of the new year. Some people objected to him, saying that the year for which they hired him had already ended and that they no longer wanted him. The shaliach tzibbur claimed that since he had already begun to pray during the Days of Awe, which are part of the new year, and they had not objected to him, since he is a yearly hire, part of it is like all of it, and they must pay him in full. In Responsa Mishpat Tzedek he ruled that the law is with the shaliach tzibbur. See Leket HaKemach, Yoreh De'ah, page 1a.
The communal officers. Meaning that he presented before them some matter because of which he is not satisfied with him, such as that his character traits are not proper in his eyes and the like, but not a claim of disqualification. Regarding that, all agree that an individual cannot object as long as he has not clarified it, for one must be concerned for damaging the person's reputation.
His enemy. Meaning that it is genuinely known from before that they hate one another. Therefore he can object, for the other is not made his agent for prayer against his will. But if he merely says that he is his enemy, he is not believed by that alone.
Before, etc. But if he became an enemy of the shaliach tzibbur after he had already agreed to him, then the individual can no longer object. Rather, the shaliach tzibbur is obligated to say explicitly that he removes the jealousy from his heart and will discharge him through his prayer. Also, if he intends not to discharge the enemy, even his friends are not discharged. Therefore chazzanim must be careful about this. Peri Megadim. The Darkei Moshe wrote in the name of the Or Zarua that a shaliach tzibbur need not flatter the congregation in matters of Heaven, and he must rebuke them. But in his own matters he should strive to overlook his traits and be beloved by the congregation, as below.
An enemy of the shaliach tzibbur. Meaning that since he hates the shaliach tzibbur, presumably the shaliach tzibbur also does not love him, for as water reflects a face to a face, and perhaps the shaliach tzibbur who reads the tochachah will direct it against him, which is a danger. Therefore he ruled that for this reason, even if they called him, he should not ascend. But Kenesses HaGedolah wrote that it is better that he ascend than that he incur punishment because he disgraces the honor of the Torah. The Gra agreed, and so wrote Sha'arei Ephraim: he must be beloved to the congregation and love them. If it occurred that the reader has hatred toward one of the congregation, it is incumbent on the gabbai not to command that person to be called for the parsha of the tochachah, because it is dangerous. If he knows that the reader hates him and is afraid that they may call him, he should leave the synagogue between aliyos until they call someone else, and afterward reenter. If he did not leave and they called him, he should ascend, since he intends for the honor of the Torah, and one who guards a mitzvah will know no evil matter. It is forbidden for the reader, at the time of his reading, to think of the matter of the curse as directed toward any person; see there. Pischei She'arim wrote that even according to the Magen Avraham, after the fact, if they called him, he should ascend.
