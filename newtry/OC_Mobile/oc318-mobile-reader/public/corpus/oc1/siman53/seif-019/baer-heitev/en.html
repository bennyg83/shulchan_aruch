**convert.** Because he cannot say "God of our fathers," and their words were rejected, as it is written: "for I have made you father of a multitude of nations I have given you." A shliach tzibur who is a mamzer depends on the dispute whether a mamzer is fit to write tefillin (Knesset HaGedolah, gloss of Beit Yosef).

**at first.** And if they accepted him for a set time and the time was completed, it is not called that he anticipated them at first—for now it is a new acceptance, as if he were not there yet (Taz). And Magen Avraham wrote: it seems to me that specifically in their time, when the shliach tzibur discharged the congregation with his prayer, then an individual could delay, for his agency is not made against his will; unlike now, when everyone is proficient and only the shliach tzibur is for the piyyutim—even though he says Kaddish and Barechu, there is not so much concern. Reuven, who was a hired shliach tzibur for a year, completed his year and began to pray a few more days of the new year, and some people objected to him, saying that the year for which they hired him had already ended and they did not want him anymore; and the shliach tzibur claimed that since he had already begun to pray on the Days of Awe, which are of the new year, and they did not refuse him, his being hired for a year—part of it is like all of it—and they should pay him in full. They ruled in responsum 90 that the law is with the shliach tzibur; see Leket HaKemach page 14 side a.

**against him.** But if his enemy was made after they agreed on him, he cannot object; rather the shliach tzibur is obliged to say explicitly that he will remove jealousy from his heart and discharge them with his prayer (Maharam of Padua, there). And Maharshal wrote in the first chapter of Chullin, siman (54) [51]: that which an individual can object—specifically if he is one of those who bear the burden of aliyah and taxes, etc., even though he is not so important; and specifically where there is another shliach tzibur whom the congregation can hire for his fee; but if he is not available, or if he is available but comes to add to the fee, the congregation can tell him that we have no power to add on account of his hatred—rather he should add from his own; and even if he clarifies his claim that this shliach tzibur is superior to the first relative to the addition of the fee, nevertheless the congregation can say that we have no ability to add so much to the fee, etc. And likewise Knesset HaGedolah; see there. And Taz wrote that there is already a custom in most congregations regarding accepting the shliach tzibur, that it follows the majority of tax payers or community leaders, and the rest cannot object, see there; and see in responsum Knesset Yechezkel siman 10. A dayan wrote in the name of Or Zarua that a shliach tzibur need not flatter the congregation, and in matters of Heaven one must rebuke them; and in the name of R' Yehuda Chassid he wrote that a shliach tzibur must be loved by the congregation, for otherwise when he reads the rebuke it is dangerous for one who does not love him; and he said that if he is his enemy he should not ascend even when they call him, see there. And Knesset HaGedolah wrote that it is better to ascend than to enter punishment, and "those who forsake Hashem shall be consumed"; and Magen Avraham wrote that between man and man it is permitted to leave—specifically when they call him and he does not ascend, because of shortening his days, see there.
