If they accepted him for a set time and the time was repeated three times, this is not called that one arose against him at first, for now it is a new acceptance, as though he had not yet been there, Taz. The Magen Avraham wrote: It appears to me that specifically in their times, when the shaliach tzibbur discharged the obligation of the public with his prayer, an individual could then prevent him, for he does not become his agent against his will. This is not so now, when everyone is proficient and the shaliach tzibbur is only for the piyyutim; even though he says Kaddish and Barchu, there is not so much concern. Reuven was a hired shaliach tzibbur for a year, completed his year, and then began praying again for a number of days of the new year. Some people objected to him, saying that the year for which they had hired him had already ended and that they no longer wanted him. The shaliach tzibbur claimed that since he had already begun praying during the Days of Awe, which are part of the new year, and they had not objected to him, since he was hired for the year, part of it is like all of it, and they must pay him in full. In Responsa Mishpetei Tzedek he ruled that the law is with the shaliach tzibbur; see Leket HaKemach, folio 14a.

Rather, the shaliach tzibbur is obligated to say explicitly that he removes jealousy from his heart and will discharge him through his prayer, Maharam Padua there. Maharshal wrote in the first chapter of Chullin, siman 51, that this rule that an individual can object applies specifically if he is among those who bear the communal obligations and taxes, even if he is not so important, and specifically where there is another shaliach tzibbur whom the congregation can hire for the same money. But if he is not available, or he is available but comes to add to the price, the congregation can say to him: We are not able to add because of his hatred; rather, let him add from his own. Even if he clarifies his claim that this shaliach tzibbur is superior to the first corresponding to the added money, nevertheless the congregation can say: We do not have the ability to add so much money. The Taz wrote: There is already a custom in most communities regarding the acceptance of a shaliach tzibbur, that it is according to the majority of the tax-payers or those called to the assembly, and the rest cannot object; see there. See Responsa Knesses Yechezkel, siman 10. The Darkei Moshe wrote in the name of the Or Zarua that a shaliach tzibbur need not flatter the congregation, and in matters of Heaven he must rebuke them. In the name of Rabbi Yehudah HaChassid he wrote that a shaliach tzibbur must be beloved to the congregation, for otherwise, when he reads the rebuke, it is a danger to one who does not love him, and he said that if he hates him he should not go up even when called; see there. Knesses HaGedolah wrote that it is better to go up than to enter the punishment of 'those who forsake Hashem shall be consumed.' The Magen Avraham wrote that between one man and the next one may leave, for only when they call him and he does not go up does he shorten his days; see there.
