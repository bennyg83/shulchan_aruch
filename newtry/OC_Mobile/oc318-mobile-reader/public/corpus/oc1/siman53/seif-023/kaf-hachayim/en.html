(23) The poskim wrote that among all the things required of one who is shaliach tzibbur, he must forgo his [honor and] traits, as we say in the Gemara (Ta'anit 25b): an incident involving R' Eliezer, who went down before the ark and was not answered; R' Akiva went down and said "God of Abraham," and he was answered. A heavenly voice went forth and said: not because this one is greater than that one, but because this one forgoes his traits and this one does not forgo his traits. Shulchan Arukh HaRav, note 2. Shaarei Teshuvah 271 side b. And also that he not be a flatterer, as written in Sotah (41b): R' Eliezer said—every person who has flattery in him brings wrath to the world, as it is said "and the flatterers of heart set wrath"; and moreover his prayer is not heard, as it is said "they cry, but He does not answer, because of the pride of their evil"; and it is written "for what is the hope of the flatterer when he has gained"; and it is written "when distress comes upon him, his cry will be heard by God." Mahari Abuhab already wrote that one of the classes of flatterers is those people who do good deeds in order to receive honor from people—which defilement spoils all a person's service. For they are people who pray and have a pleasant voice, yet ponder in their heart during prayer: how pleasant is my voice, and how many people enjoy hearing me—Shaarei Teshuvah there. And so wrote Shulchan Arukh HaRav, note 5; and see Sha'ar HaKedushah of Maharachav, part 2, gate 4, who wrote that one who reveals his good deeds before people—not only does he not receive reward for them, but he is punished as well—see there. I brought his words in Sefer HaKadosh Beit Ya'akov, derush on the Torah, parashat Vayikra—see there. And see further from what I wrote in Sefer HaKadosh Edut BeYa'akov at the beginning of chapter 2 of Avot—see there.
