23) The poskim write that among the qualities required of a shaliach tzibbur is that he forgo his claims against others, as the Talmud states in Taanis 25b: Rabbi Eliezer descended before the lectern and was not answered. Rabbi Akiva descended, said Avinu Malkeinu, and was answered. A heavenly voice declared: It is not because this one is greater than the other, but because this one forgoes his claims and the other does not. Solel Boneh 2; Shalmei Tzibbur, page 71b. He must also not be a flatterer. Sotah 41b states: Rabbi Elazar said that anyone who possesses flattery brings wrath to the world, as it is written, 'Those who flatter in heart incur wrath.' Moreover, his prayer is not heard, as it says, 'They do not cry out when He binds them.' It is also written, 'For what is the hope of a flatterer when he gains,' and, 'Will God hear his cry when distress comes upon him?' Mahari Abuhav already writes that one category of flatterers consists of those who perform good deeds so that people will honor them. This filth ruins all of a person's service. Some people pray with a pleasant voice while thinking during prayer, 'How pleasant my voice is, and how many people listening to me derive pleasure from it!' Shalmei Tzibbur, ibid.; Solel Boneh 5. See Shaar HaKedushah by Maharchu, part 2, gate 4, which writes that one who publicizes his good deeds before people not only receives no reward for them, but is also punished; see there. I cited his words in my sacred work Beis Yaakov, discourse on the Torah, Vayikra; see there. See also what I wrote concerning this in my sacred work Edus BeYaakov at the beginning of chapter 2 of Avos.
