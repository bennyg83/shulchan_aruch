[23] Even an individual, etc. For who can offer another person’s sacrifice without his consent? This is from Beit Yosef. It accords with what Rabbi David Abudraham writes on page 47: Know that a chazzan stands in place of the kohen who offers the sacrifice, and therefore the chazzan is also called a “karov,” as stated in Midrash Shir HaShirim: When Rabbi Elazar son of Rabbi Shimon died, his generation applied to him the verse, “Perfumed with myrrh and frankincense, with all the powders of the merchant,” because Rabbi Elazar possessed mastery of Scripture, Mishnah, and the sacrificial service, and composed liturgical poems. For this reason, the liturgical poems recited by the chazzan in the midst of the prayers are called kerovos. It is also possible that they are called this because they are recited within the prayers and in their midst. This concludes his words. It follows that the common form “kerovetz” is a corruption. Beit Yosef in siman 68 explains it as an acronym for “The sound of rejoicing and salvation is in the tents of the righteous,” but this is an error. The correct term is kerovos, ending with the letter tav, as Abudraham also calls them on page 21. See Maadanei Melech, page 42, who overlooked the words of Rabbi David Abudraham.
