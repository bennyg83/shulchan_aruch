This agrees with what R' David Abudraham wrote on page 47: know that a shliach tzibur is in place of the kohen who offers, and therefore he is also called a close shliach tzibur, as is stated in Midrash Shir HaShirim: when R' Elazar ben R' Shimon died, his generation called him 'garlands of myrrh and frankincense from all the powder of the merchant'—for R' Elazar was called 'close' and 'appointed' and 'offering' and 'fat pieces.' And for this reason the piyyutim that the shliach tzibur says in the middle of prayers were called kerovot; and it is possible they were called thus because they are said in the midst of prayers and among them—up to here. And from this it is correct that the language that is sometimes corrupted as 'kerovatz' was mistaken; and Beit Yosef explained in siman 68 that it is an acronym 'kol rinah viyeshuah be'ohalei tzaddikim'—and that is an error; rather it should be kerovot with a tav, and so he called them in Abudraham page 21. And see in Ma'adanei Melech page 42 that he omitted the words of R' David Abudraham.
