And he should not wait. Biur HaGra contradicts this from Arachin 11b, which states that a chazzan seeks authorization before beginning the prayer; see there. Nor is it proven from Tur that he need not wait at all, only that he need not initially refuse. Nevertheless, with difficulty one can explain Shulchan Aruch's view by saying that the Gemara refers to a chazzan who was not permanently appointed for prayer, but was appointed for other communal functions. Rashi in Chullin 24b explains that there can be a chazzan appointed to sound the shofar, pronounce bans, and perform other functions; see there. Although the congregation certainly agrees that he should also serve as its agent for prayer, he nevertheless seeks authorization. By contrast, a chazzan whose primary appointment was to lead the prayers need not seek authorization at all. Regarding the leviim there, since this was their first day of appointment to their service, as is evident from the verse, perhaps their situation was inferior to that of a permanent chazzan who always stands ready for this purpose.
