Seif 15: “A regularly appointed chazzan, etc., should not wait until they tell him, etc.” Note: I saw in the Shulchan Aruch of the saintly Gaon of Vilna, of blessed memory, that he found this ruling difficult in light of Arachin 11b, where the Gemara says that even a regularly appointed functionary first seeks the congregation’s consent. See there. In my humble opinion, with due respect, these are two separate matters. Here the Mechaber means that another person who is not regularly appointed should not initiate the matter and ask to lead the prayers, because doing so appears arrogant, as though he considers himself worthy to lead. He must therefore wait until others first ask him, so that they initiate the matter with him. A regularly appointed chazzan, however, displays no arrogance and may initiate the matter by approaching the lectern. Therefore the Mechaber writes that a regularly appointed chazzan descends on his own and need not wait for others to ask him first. Nevertheless, even though he initiates and approaches first, he must still ask the congregation’s permission before beginning. Arachin discusses precisely this, that he initiates by asking them whether he should lead the prayers. This distinction is clear and correct in my humble opinion. Analyze it carefully.
