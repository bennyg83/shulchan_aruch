(59) "For his father" — meaning: his father died and he wants to pray in the manner of mourners, because prayer is a greater mitzvah than saying Kaddish.

(60) "If the congregation wishes" — the reason is that specifically Kaddish he may say for his father and the congregation has no authority to refuse him; but he cannot be the congregation's agent to pray against their will. In this matter it is likewise as with a sheliach tzibbur — an individual does not protest so that dispute not increase, but they follow the majority or those selected for this, as we wrote above in the name of the Acharonim. There are places that have an enactment in the synagogue that they do not allow a mourner to pray all week before the ark except their fixed sheliach tzibbur — one may not set aside the enactment [and it was made because of ignoramuses who do not know and understand the blessings and prayers and say them wrongly, and there is disgrace; therefore they enacted even upon those who know that they not pray, so that dispute not arise]. However, where there is no enactment and there is a fixed custom, it is a mitzvah for the congregation to allow the mourner to pray, for it is comfort for the deceased and saves him from Gehenom; however, certainly when he cannot cut the letters properly and the like, he should not pray before the ark. Magen Avraham wrote: if there is a mourner and a mohel, the mourner is deferred from prayer because of the mohel; and the day on which his father and mother died, which they call yahrzeit, takes precedence over the mohel and over the mourner — all this when the congregation is not particular about it; but the congregation has authority to choose whom they wish.

(61) "He shall say" — and in Maariv prayer, which is not corresponding to the tamid offerings but only to the limbs and fats, it is possible that the owners' intent is not required; and the congregation cannot delay a mourner from praying Maariv, for it is a mitzvah that one whose father and mother died should pray; nevertheless, certainly one should not quarrel over any mitzvah with the congregation, as below in small seif 65.
