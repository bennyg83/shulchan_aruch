(Note 20) Even, etc. And in Yesh Seder—specifically when there is another to hire at his fee; but if they would need to pay another more—even if the rate of what they give him is better—nevertheless he cannot force the congregation to give more, unless the individual wishes to give the extra wages from his own alone. And from his language there it implies: even if they were accustomed among themselves to give higher wages, and now a shliach tzibur happened who does not ask such high wages, and an individual wants them to hire another who is better than he and pay him as they were accustomed—nevertheless he cannot force the congregation to do so, since now they find one who takes less than the first.

And it seems to me specifically in their times, etc.—he wished to give a reason for the custom that Levush wrote; and likewise it is apparently against the law, as Rabbi Yosef wrote in the name of Maharik: since prayer corresponds to the tamid offerings that come from the congregation's property, it is not fitting that a person be their agent to offer their sacrifice without their knowledge and will—end quote from Maharit.

It seems to me he is flawed. For presumably it deals with when the individual has no witnesses on the disqualification—for if he says he has witnesses, let him bring witnesses and they will remove him. And since he has no witnesses, even if he forgives on account of the disqualification, we do not heed him—even though on another claim we would heed him, for even an individual can hold back initially when there is a valid reason. Nevertheless here we are concerned for the shliach tzibur's flaw: if they remove him or do not appoint him initially and rumor spreads that it was because of disqualification, it will be strengthened by the rumor of disqualification—and perhaps it is false.
