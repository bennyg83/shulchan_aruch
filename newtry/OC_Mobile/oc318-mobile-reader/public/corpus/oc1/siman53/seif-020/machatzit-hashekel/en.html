Seif katan 20: “Even,” etc. Yam Shel Shlomo means specifically when another candidate can be hired for the same amount. If the congregation would need to pay another candidate more, even when the other is superior by an amount corresponding to the additional pay, the individual cannot compel the congregation to pay more. He may do so only if he personally wishes to pay the additional wage. His wording there implies that even if the congregation had previously been accustomed to pay a higher wage, but now found a chazzan who requests less, an individual who wishes them to hire a superior candidate and pay him the amount they formerly paid cannot compel them to do so, since they have now found someone who accepts less than the former chazzan.
It appears to me that this applies specifically in their times, etc. He seeks to justify the custom cited by Levush and Knesses HaGedolah, which at first glance contradicts the law stated by Beit Yosef in the name of Maharik. Since the prayers correspond to the communal daily offerings, it is improper for a person to serve as their emissary to offer their sacrifice without their knowledge and consent. This is the wording of Maharit.
For it can be said that they would damage his reputation. The case necessarily concerns an individual who has no witnesses to the chazzan's disqualification. If he had witnesses, he would produce them and the chazzan would certainly be removed. Since he has no witnesses, even though he objects because of the alleged disqualification, he is not heard, although he would be heard were he to advance another legitimate claim, for even one individual may initially prevent an appointment when he has a sound reason. Here, however, we are concerned for damage to the chazzan's reputation. If they remove him, or decline to appoint him initially, and a report spreads that this was because of the alleged disqualification, the rumor of disqualification will thereby be strengthened, although it may be false.
