For his father. This means that his father died and he wishes to pray in the manner of mourners.
Whomever the congregation wants. The reason is that specifically Kaddish he may say for his father, but he cannot become the agent of the congregation to pray against their will, there. For this reason, it seems to me regarding what the Hagahos Maimoniyos wrote in the laws of the Tenth of Teves, that a mohel takes precedence over a mourner in praying, unlike Rav Menachem Mendel. Nevertheless, if the congregation wants the mourner to pray, the mourner has acquired the right. It seems to me that a yahrtzeit takes precedence over a mohel if the congregation is not particular about this, for according to Rav Menachem Mendel, even a mourner takes precedence.
