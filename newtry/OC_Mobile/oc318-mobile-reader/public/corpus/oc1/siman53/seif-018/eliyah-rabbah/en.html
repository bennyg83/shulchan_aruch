[22] Because colored garments, etc. Olas Tamid writes that since the Shulchan Aruch omitted above, siman 39, the law concerning a mamzer, this indicates that a mamzer is fit to write tefillin, and likewise is fit to serve as chazzan. It appears that this applies only when no one else is available, or when his father performed a proper repentance. Otherwise, the Sages have said that the prayer of a righteous person who is the child of a righteous person is not comparable to the prayer of a righteous person who is the child of a wicked person. This concludes his words. This requires study in light of Yoreh Deah, siman 281, where the Shulchan Aruch rules that a Sefer Torah written by a mamzer is invalid. Shach, citing Mordechai, explains that a mamzer cannot discharge the obligation of the public, and anyone who cannot discharge their obligation is unfit to write it. According to the other reasons that Shach gives there, one could distinguish between a Sefer Torah and tefillin, but this reason appears primary. See there. I later found that Magen Avraham, siman 39, writes in the name of Darkei Moshe that a mamzer is fit to write tefillin, and this requires study. As for Olas Tamid’s rejection based on the statement that the prayer of a righteous person who is the child of a righteous person is not comparable, I have already written that with regard to a chazzan the conclusion is the reverse.
