The ark. See Be'er Heitev. And see in She'elat Ya'avetz part 1 siman 75 and part 2 siman 67; and from the words of Chavot Yair it seems that even at the beginning of his words he speaks about the Days of Awe; nevertheless, from the reasons he wrote, one should prevent him even on other days of the year if there is another who is fit and proper like him. And in She'elat Ya'avetz and Moreh u'Ketziah he wrote that he is disqualified. And in Biur Yehoshua and Machatzit HaShekel they ruled for halakhah that on other days of the year he may be shaliach tzibbur, since such is the view of Rambam, Rosh, and other poskim, and Maran. But on the Days of Awe one should be concerned for Chavot Yair's words. And see in Eshel Avraham note 2 that likewise a deaf person — in the name of Rashbash; and Biur Yehoshua wrote that his father also wrote so in Tashbetz part 3 siman 113; and it seems for the Days of Awe his law is as a blind person, even though one can somewhat distinguish — and one could say that one who speaks and does not hear is like any person. Nevertheless, it seems to be stringent on the Days of Awe. And see above note 8; and also that there is some confusion in prayer thereby when he does not hear — then he has no knowledge at the beginning or end of what the congregation answers after him, and how they stand — whether in the middle of the section or between sections. But one who hears only that his hearing nerve was impaired, as with elders whose ears are somewhat heavy from hearing — nevertheless they hear and know from feeling whether the congregation is still standing at their conclusion — we have no concern with this.
