[11] At designated times, etc. This implies that the prohibition applies only when he is appointed to replace the regular chazzan at fixed times, such as to pray in his place on days other than the public assembly days or the like. If, however, he is appointed without fixed times, to pray in the regular chazzan's place whenever the latter is unable to come to the synagogue or feels somewhat weak, it is permitted to appoint him, since this is occasional service. Taz writes likewise. This requires study, for he does not mention Bach, who forbids even this. Since the person has been appointed for this role, Bach considers him permanent, for service is called occasional only when he has no appointment at all.
