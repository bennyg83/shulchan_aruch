(34) Shaliach tzibbur — One who has a pleasant voice should chant praise to the Omnipresent and not other songs; and if he spat, he should wipe his mouth and then pray [Shulchan Arukh HaRav siman 151].

(35) Unseemly — It is found in Shulchan Arukh HaRav: when Rabban Shimon ben Gamliel went out to judgment to be killed, he said to R' Yishmael likewise, "My brother, why am I going out to be killed?" He said to him, "Perhaps you taught Torah in public and rejoiced in your heart and enjoyed words of Torah?" He said to him, "My brother, you have comforted me."

(36) The congregation — Yam Shel Shlomo wrote siman 50: he may not prolong [the service] against the congregation's will, even on Shabbat and Yom Tov; and even with the congregation's consent, excessively is unseemly, for this is neither half to the Omnipresent nor half to yourselves. She'erit Yosef in Knesset HaGedolah cited in the name of responsum of R' Yosef Bruna: a shaliach tzibbur whose voice is disqualified if it sounds as though he is shuddering and broken is invalid. He also wrote: if two cantors are equal—a Kohen precedes a Levi, a Levi an Israelite, and a Torah scholar precedes an am ha'aretz—even if he is a Kohen, and how much more so a Levite.
