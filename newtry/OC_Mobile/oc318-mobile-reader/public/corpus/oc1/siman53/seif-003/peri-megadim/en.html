Tosafos in Sukkah 42a and Megillah 24a write that serving on a fast day is considered occasional service comparable to permanent service, and one may not lead unless his beard has filled out. What is written that the Rosh and Rashba also say this I have not presently found. They write only that a permanent appointment throughout the year is forbidden, not that occasional service on a fast is forbidden. See Magen Avraham, seif katan 9, that on a fast an actual elder is required. In siman 581, however, it is explicit that for Rosh Hashanah and Yom Kippur thirty years of age is at most sufficient. Communal fasts in Eretz Yisrael for rain are treated more stringently. Furthermore, even there an elder is merely especially praiseworthy, while one whose beard has filled out is also sufficient, as is explicit in Rambam, Hilchos Taaniyos, chapter 4. Regarding the statement that it is better to draw near, see Magen Avraham, seif katan 8. Whether the congregation's waiver helps on Rosh Hashanah and Yom Kippur for one whose beard has not filled out is discussed by Eliyah Rabbah, seif katan 10. There is a form of double doubt: perhaps a fast is not equivalent to a permanent appointment, as implied by Tosafos in Chullin according to Beit Yosef's understanding, and perhaps waiver is effective. Nevertheless, one should not be lenient. See siman 581. On Rosh Hashanah and Yom Kippur it is preferable to appoint a righteous chazzan who is the child of a righteous person. There is no proof to the contrary from descendants of disgraced families, for they too are righteous. One disqualified because of a transgression may not serve even occasionally, as Eliyah Rabbah writes in the name of Mishpat Tzedek. This refers to one who has not repented.
