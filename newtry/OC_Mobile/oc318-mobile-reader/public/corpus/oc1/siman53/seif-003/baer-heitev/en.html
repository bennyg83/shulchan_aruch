To Yishtabach. See Responsa Ginas Veradim, Orach Chaim, klal 1, siman 51, and Shiyurei Knesses HaGedolah, and Responsa Mateh Yosef, part 2, siman 11. One who needed to relieve himself during Pesukei D'Zimrah, went and relieved himself, and returned, should wash his hands and recite the blessing, and he puts on tzitzis and tefillin and recites the blessing over them, Ginas Veradim, Orach Chaim, klal 1, siman 51b, see there. But according to what was written above in siman 25, s.k. 16, in the name of the Magen Avraham, it implies that he does not need to recite a blessing; see above, siman 8, seif 14. A group saying Tehillim in the beis haknesses, where it happened that there was no minyan before Baruch She'amar, may not say Kaddish during Pesukei D'Zimrah, Responsa Pnei Yehoshua, Orach Chaim, siman 5. See above, siman 51, s.k. 3, what is stated there in the name of Makor Baruch.
The shaliach tzibbur. But the individual should say Yishtabach immediately. So too on Shabbos, if he finished Pesukei D'Zimrah before the shaliach tzibbur, he should say Yishtabach immediately, Magen Avraham.
And be silent. Meaning that he should not interrupt with speech, and he may wait half an hour with Yishtabach, Maharam Mintz. The Magen Avraham wrote: It appears to me that even if he delayed because of circumstances beyond his control for the time needed to complete all of it, he need not return to the beginning.
