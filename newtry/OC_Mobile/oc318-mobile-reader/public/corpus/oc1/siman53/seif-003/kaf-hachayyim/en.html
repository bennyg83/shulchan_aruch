5) [Seif 3] "One should not recite a blessing over wrapping in tzitzit during Pesukei DeZimrah," etc. Even for the need of a mitzvah it is forbidden to interrupt, as stated in siman 51, seif 4. Magen Avraham, seif katan 3; Shulchan Aruch HaRav, letter 3. Between Yishtabach and Yotzer, however, is more lenient than between Baruch She'amar and Yishtabach, because there one may speak for the purpose of a mitzvah, as stated in siman 54, seif 3. Pri Megadim, Eshel Avraham, letter 3; Shulchan Aruch HaRav there. See below, letter 7, and siman 54, letter 11. 6) There, "one should not recite a blessing over wrapping in tzitzit," etc. The law of tefillin is the same as that of a tallis. Temim De'im; Solel Boneh in Mekor Chaim, letter 1; Yafeh LaLev, siman 54, letter 3. See the following letter. 7) There, "rather, between Yishtabach and Yotzer." Mahari Binyamin holds that the same permission applies between 1 psalm and another. Mahari Zayin cites him in Responsa Gevurat Anashim, Orach Chayyim, rule 1, siman 51; Yad Aharon; Birkei Yosef, letter 4. Mahara HaLevi, however, writes that this applies specifically where the person was compelled by bodily needs. If he was not compelled and tzitzit or tefillin merely became available to him during Pesukei DeZimrah, it is proper not to put them on with a blessing until between Yishtabach and Yotzer. Birkei Yosef cites this and objects that the ruling is overly stringent, because Rambam in Responsa Pe'er HaDor, siman 147, was asked whether a person to whom a tallis and tefillin became available during Pesukei DeZimrah may put them on and bless, and answered that there is no prohibition at all against such a blessing in the middle of the psalms, because this sort of interruption is not prohibited in Pesukei DeZimrah, which is neither the Amidah nor Shema. Accordingly, there are 3 opinions. Rambam permits the blessing on tallis and tefillin even in the middle of a psalm. Mahari Binyamin permits only between psalms. Mahara HaLevi permits doing so within Pesukei DeZimrah only where one was compelled by bodily needs; otherwise he should wait until between Yishtabach and Yotzer. The plain wording of Maran implies that no distinction is made and that the blessing should be recited only between Yishtabach and Yotzer. Kissei Eliyahu, Beit Oved, and Yafeh LaLev write likewise, unlike Knesset HaGedolah. This also follows from the reason given by Magen Avraham and the later authorities cited above in letter 5: the distinction is not between one psalm and another and the middle of a psalm, but between the entire section from Baruch She'amar through Yishtabach and the more lenient interval between Yishtabach and Yotzer. Shulchan Aruch HaRav and Erech HaShulchan likewise imply this. This is not comparable to Maran's ruling in siman 66, seif 2, that between the paragraphs of Shema one may interrupt to put on tzitzit and tefillin. There they are needed for Shema, so that one not be like a person testifying falsely about himself, as Beit Yosef writes in the name of Shibbolei HaLeket. They are not required for Pesukei DeZimrah. Furthermore, here he can put them on during the more lenient interval between Yishtabach and Yotzer, whereas during Shema there is no comparable later place. Even according to the Arizal, who requires putting them on before the passage of the Tamid, once that proper time has already passed and the mitzvah was not performed in its ideal order, one should not interrupt Pesukei DeZimrah. Therefore, one who did not have tzitzit and tefillin and they became available after Baruch She'amar should be stringent not to put them on with a blessing until between Yishtabach and Yotzer. Likewise, regarding one who needs to relieve himself during Pesukei DeZimrah, the ruling of Gevurat Anashim that he wash his hands and recite Asher Yatzar immediately should not be followed; rather, he should avoid interrupting and recite Asher Yatzar after Yishtabach, as we wrote above in siman 51, letter 28. As for his further ruling that one who removed tzitzit and tefillin to enter the restroom must recite new blessings when putting them on again, we already wrote above in siman 8, letter 52, and siman 25, letter 78, that this is disputed. Because of the rule of doubt regarding blessings, he should put them on again without a blessing. 8) There, in Rama: "The shaliach tzibbur, if he did not have a tallis initially, should wrap himself in tzitzit before beginning Yishtabach," etc. This applies specifically to the shaliach tzibbur, who should not have to descend from the lectern after beginning Yishtabach. An individual should wait until after Yishtabach and then wrap in the tallis and bless, because it is preferable to make the interruption there, as explained at the beginning of siman 51. Darchei Moshe; Levush; Knesset HaGedolah; Erech HaShulchan; Pri Megadim; Shulchan Aruch HaRav. See what Kissei Eliyahu writes and Yafeh LaLev, letter 4. 9) There, in Rama: "Before beginning Yishtabach," etc. If the tallis was brought only after Yishtabach, even the shaliach tzibbur may wrap and recite the blessing between Yishtabach and Kaddish. Magen Avraham, siman 54, seif katan 4; Solel Boneh; Shulchan Aruch HaRav; Pri Megadim; Yafeh LaLev. Between Kaddish and Barchu, however, one should not interrupt for anything. All the more so should one not interrupt after the shaliach tzibbur has said Barchu before beginning the blessing of Yotzer. Rama at the end of siman 54. There is no distinction between the shaliach tzibbur himself and another person. Levush. Pri Megadim writes that between Kaddish and Barchu is comparable to between the paragraphs, while between Barchu and Yotzer is comparable to the middle of a paragraph, because Barchu belongs to Yotzer Or. Eliyah Rabbah and Levush likewise imply this. This follows Rama's view in siman 66, seif 2, that one does not bless on a tallis between the paragraphs, as will be explained there. 10) There, in Rama: "Likewise, if there is no minyan in the synagogue, the shaliach tzibbur should wait with Yishtabach," etc. An individual, however, should say Yishtabach immediately. Likewise on Shabbos, if an individual completes Pesukei DeZimrah before the shaliach tzibbur, he should say Yishtabach immediately. Magen Avraham, seif katan 4; Yad Aharon; Eliyah Rabbah; Pri Megadim; Shaliach Tzibbur; Kesher Gudal; Beit Oved. 11) There, in Rama: "And he should remain silent until a minyan arrives," etc. Meaning that he should not interrupt by speaking. Maharam Mintz, siman 8, writes in the name of Maharil that he may wait half an hour before Yishtabach, because this is the measure of time needed to complete the entire section and, according to all opinions, he need not return to the beginning. See the beginning of siman 65. It appears to me that even if an unavoidable circumstance caused him to pause long enough to complete the entire section, he need not return to the beginning. Magen Avraham, seif katan 5; Yad Aharon. Pri Megadim explains that Pesukei DeZimrah is rabbinic and therefore one does not return to the beginning, unlike Shema, which is Torah-level. Shulchan Aruch HaRav; Beit Oved. 12) There, in Rama: "And he should remain silent until a minyan arrives," etc. If they said Yishtabach immediately and only afterward the number of people increased to a minyan, Kaddish may nevertheless be said provided there was no interruption, even though there was no minyan at the time Yishtabach was said. Eliyah Rabbah in the name of Responsa Maharam Mintz, siman 8; Beit Oved in the name of Chazon LaMoed, siman 17. 13) A group reciting Tehillim in the synagogue that did not have a minyan before Baruch She'amar may not say Kaddish in the middle of Pesukei DeZimrah. Responsa Pnei Yehoshua, Orach Chayyim, siman 5; Yad Aharon; Shaliach Tzibbur; Kesher Gudal; Beit Oved. Likewise, if there was no minyan before Hodu and the Kaddish ordinarily said before Hodu was omitted, once they have begun Baruch She'amar and Pesukei DeZimrah they should not say that Kaddish in the middle of Pesukei DeZimrah. This follows from Shaar HaKavanos, discourse concerning Kaddish, which explains that Kaddish was instituted between each spiritual world and the next. Once its proper time has passed, there is no reason to interrupt Pesukei DeZimrah in order to say it. Pnei Yehoshua and Beit Oved write likewise, unlike Mahari Azulai; see Shaarei Teshuvah, siman 51, letter 3. Likewise, in a place where mourners customarily say the Kaddish after Yishtabach, if 1 mourner has not yet said Yishtabach and is still in the middle of Pesukei DeZimrah, he should not interrupt to say that Kaddish. The principal Kaddish that benefits the deceased is specifically the final Kaddish said before Aleinu, as explained in Shaar HaKavanos. Furthermore, Kaddish was instituted only between one spiritual world and the next, and this person has not yet completed the rectification of the spiritual world in which he presently stands so as to elevate it. Rav Pealim, part 2, siman 14, permits the mourner to say that Kaddish because he says it for the congregation. In my humble opinion this is incorrect. Since he has not yet completed the rectification and elevation of his own prayer, how can he elevate the prayer of others? Even a shaliach tzibbur who elevates the prayers of others does so together with his own prayer. If his own prayer has not yet been rectified, how can he rectify others? Rav Pealim further writes that once a person has begun the zemirot he already has a connection with Yetzirah and may therefore say Kaddish. This too appears incorrect. The ascent of the 7 heichalot of Yetzirah to their own Holy of Holies takes place through the 5 Hallelujah psalms, and in Halleluhu El Bekodsho they ascend to the Holy of Holies, as explained in Shaar HaKavanos, Pri Etz Chaim, Shaar HaZemirot, chapter 6, and the siddur of the Rashash. Since their rectification has not yet been completed so that they may ascend to their Holy of Holies, how can they ascend to Beriah through Kaddish? Furthermore, since the rectification of Yetzirah itself is not yet complete, how can he rise from it? Therefore the principal ruling is not to interrupt.
