5) [Se'if 3] One should not recite a berachah over wrapping in tzitzis during Pesukei DeZimrah, etc. It is forbidden to interrupt even for a mitzvah, as stated in siman 51:4. Magen Avraham 3; Shulchan Aruch HaRav 3. Between Yishtabach and Yotzer is more lenient than between Baruch She'amar and Yishtabach; there one may speak for a mitzvah, as stated in siman 54:3. Pri Megadim, Eshel Avraham 3; Shulchan Aruch HaRav, ibid. See below, paragraph 7, and siman 54:11.
6) There: One should not recite a berachah over wrapping in tzitzis, etc. The law of tefillin is the same as that of a tallis. Temim De'im; Solel Boneh in Makor Chaim 1; Yafeh LaLev 54:3. See the next paragraph.
7) There: Rather, between Yishtabach and Yotzer. The same applies between one mizmor and another. Mahari Binyamin, cited by Mahari Zayin in Responsa Gevuras Anashim, Orach Chaim 1:51; Yad Aharon, Hagahos Beis Yosef; Birkei Yosef 4. Mahara HaLevi, siman 52, writes that this applies specifically when one was compelled by bodily needs. If he was not so compelled but tzitzis and tefillin became available to him, it is proper not to put them on or recite their berachos within Pesukei DeZimrah, but to wait until between Yishtabach and Yotzer; see there. Birkei Yosef cites him and comments: 'The Rav went too far in this ruling. See Rambam's responsa in the newly printed Pe'er HaDor 147, which rules the opposite: A person who obtains a tallis and tefillin while in Pesukei DeZimrah; may he put them on and recite the berachos? Answer: There is no prohibition whatsoever against reciting the berachos over tzitzis and tefillin during Pesukei DeZimrah. Such an interruption is not forbidden in Pesukei DeZimrah, which is neither the Amidah nor Krias Shema, where such an interruption would be forbidden.' Thus, three opinions exist. Rambam permits interrupting for the berachos over tallis and tefillin during Pesukei DeZimrah, apparently even in the middle of a mizmor. Mahari Binyamin permits it only between the mizmorim. Mahara HaLevi permits it only if one was compelled by bodily needs; if tzitzis and tefillin merely became available, it is proper not to put them on or recite the berachos until between Yishtabach and Yotzer. Maran's unqualified wording also implies no distinction: the berachos should be recited only between Yishtabach and Yotzer. Kisei Eliyahu 3; Siddur Beis Yaakov 9; and Yafeh LaLev 3, unlike Shiyurei Knesses HaGedolah, Hagahos Beis Yosef 4. The rationale given by Magen Avraham and the later authorities cited above in paragraph 5 likewise implies no difference between the middle of a mizmor and between mizmorim. The distinction is only between Baruch She'amar and Yishtabach on the one hand and between Yishtabach and Yotzer on the other; see there. Shulchan Aruch HaRav 3 and Erech HaShulchan 1 rule likewise. It is astonishing that Birkei Yosef says Gevuras Anashim went too far. See Zechor LeYitzchak Harari 8, who also questions Birkei Yosef. This does not resemble Maran's ruling in siman 66:2 that between sections one may interrupt to put on tzitzis and tefillin. There, they are required for Krias Shema so that one not resemble a person bearing false testimony against himself, as Beis Yosef writes in the name of Shibolei HaLeket. They are not needed for Pesukei DeZimrah. Moreover, here he can put them on between Yishtabach and Yotzer, which is more lenient; there is no other place available in Krias Shema. Even according to the Arizal's words cited above, that they must be put on before the passage of the Tamid, once that time has already passed and the mitzvah was not performed in its ideal order, one should not interrupt Pesukei DeZimrah. One who lacked tzitzis and tefillin and obtained them after Baruch She'amar should therefore act stringently and put them on with their berachos only between Yishtabach and Yotzer. Gevuras Anashim also writes that one who attended to bodily needs during Pesukei DeZimrah should wash and recite Asher Yatzar. The proper practice, however, is not to interrupt for that berachah until after Yishtabach, as stated above in siman 51:28. Regarding Gevuras Anashim's underlying ruling that one who attended to bodily needs and entered the lavatory must repeat the berachos over tzitzis and tefillin, we wrote above in siman 8:52 and siman 25:78 that the matter is disputed and doubt concerning berachos is resolved leniently; see there. Accordingly, one who attended to bodily needs during Pesukei DeZimrah, removed his tzitzis and tefillin, and entered the lavatory need not repeat their berachos after leaving and washing his hands. He should put them on without a berachah, because doubt concerning berachos is resolved leniently.
8) There, in the gloss: If the shaliach tzibbur initially had no tallis, he should wrap himself in tzitzis before beginning Yishtabach, etc. This applies specifically to the chazzan, who should not descend from the lectern after beginning Yishtabach. An individual should wait until after Yishtabach, and then wrap himself in tzitzis and recite the berachah, because interrupting then is preferable, as stated above at the beginning of siman 51. Darkei Moshe; Levush; Shiyurei Knesses HaGedolah 4; Erech HaShulchan 1; Pri Megadim, Eshel Avraham 3; and Shulchan Aruch HaRav 3. Regarding Kisei Eliyahu's words, see Yafeh LaLev 4.
9) There, in the gloss: Before beginning Yishtabach, etc. If the tallis was brought only after Yishtabach, even the shaliach tzibbur should wrap himself and recite the berachah between Yishtabach and Kaddish. Magen Avraham 54:4; Solel Boneh 4 there; Shulchan Aruch HaRav 3 there; Pri Megadim, Eshel Avraham 3 in this siman; and Yafeh LaLev 3. Between Kaddish and Barchu, however, one should make no interruption whatsoever. All the more so should one not interrupt after the shaliach tzibbur says Barchu and before beginning Yotzer. Rama's gloss at the end of siman 54. There is no distinction between the shaliach tzibbur and anyone else. Levush's gloss to this siman and his comments to siman 54:3; Shulchan Aruch HaRav 3 there. See Pri Megadim, Eshel Avraham 3 in this siman and paragraph 4 in siman 54. He writes that between Kaddish and Barchu is like between sections, whereas between Barchu and Yotzer is like the middle of a section, because Barchu relates to Yotzer Or; see there. Eliyah Rabbah 54:5 writes likewise, and this is also implied by Levush there. This follows Rama's view in his gloss to siman 66:2 that one does not recite the berachah over a tallis between sections. The matter will be explained there with G-d's help.
10) There, in the gloss: If no minyan is present in the synagogue, the shaliach tzibbur should wait at Yishtabach, etc. An individual, however, should recite Yishtabach immediately. Likewise, on Shabbos, if he completes Pesukei DeZimrah before the shaliach tzibbur, he should say Yishtabach immediately. Magen Avraham 4; Yad Aharon, Hagahos HaTur; Eliyah Rabbah 4; Pri Megadim, Eshel Avraham 4; Shalmei Tzibbur, page 70d; Kesher Gudal 7:45; and Siddur Beis Yaakov 11.
11) There, in the gloss: He should remain silent until a minyan arrives, etc. This means that he should not interrupt with speech. Maharam Mintz 8 writes in Maharil's name that one may wait half an hour at Yishtabach, because this is the time required to complete all of Pesukei DeZimrah, and according to everyone he need not return to the beginning. See the beginning of siman 65. Nevertheless, it appears to me that even if he waited under compulsion long enough to complete the entire section, he need not return to the beginning. Magen Avraham 5; Yad Aharon, ibid. Pri Megadim, Eshel Avraham 5, explains that Pesukei DeZimrah is rabbinic and one does not return to the beginning; it does not resemble Krias Shema, which is from the Torah. See there. Shulchan Aruch HaRav 3; Siddur Beis Yaakov 10.
12) There, in the gloss: He should remain silent until a minyan arrives, etc. If Yishtabach was recited immediately and a minyan afterward assembled, Kaddish is nevertheless recited, provided there was no interruption, even though no minyan was present when Yishtabach was said. Eliyah Rabbah 4 in the name of Responsa Maharam Mintz 8; Siddur Beis Yaakov 12 in the name of Chazon LaMoed 17.
13) A group reciting Tehillim in the synagogue that did not have a minyan before Baruch She'amar may not recite Kaddish during Pesukei DeZimrah. Responsa Pnei Yehoshua, Orach Chaim 5; Yad Aharon, Hagahos Beis Yosef; Shalmei Tzibbur, page 67c; Kesher Gudal 7:36; and Siddur Beis Yaakov 12. Similarly, if no minyan was present before Hodu and the Kaddish preceding Hodu was not recited, but Baruch She'amar and Pesukei DeZimrah have begun, Kaddish should no longer be recited during Pesukei DeZimrah. This is implied by Shaar HaKavanos, Derush HaKaddish, which says that Kaddish was instituted between each world and the next; see there. Since its time has passed, one need not interrupt Pesukei DeZimrah to recite it. Pnei Yehoshua, ibid., writes likewise. Siddur Beis Yaakov 13 agrees, unlike Mahari Azulai; Shaarei Teshuvah 51:3. Likewise, in a place where orphans customarily recite the Kaddish after Yishtabach, if one of them has not yet recited Yishtabach and is still in Pesukei DeZimrah, he should not interrupt to recite Kaddish. The principal Kaddish that benefits the deceased is specifically the final Kaddish before Aleinu, as Shaar HaKavanos states there. He should therefore not interrupt Pesukei DeZimrah for this Kaddish. Moreover, Kaddish was instituted only between one world and the next. He has not yet completed the aspect of that world in order to elevate it, as is known. Rav Pe'alim 2:14 writes that since he recites it for the congregation, doing so is permitted. In my humble opinion, this is incorrect. Since he has not yet completed the rectification of his own prayer in order to elevate it, how can he elevate the prayers of others? Even the shaliach tzibbur elevates others' prayers only together with his own; if his has not yet been rectified, how can he rectify others? Rav Pe'alim further writes that because he began the songs, he has a connection to the world of Yetzirah and may recite Kaddish. This too appears incorrect. The ascent through the seven palaces of Yetzirah to their Holy of Holies occurs through the five Halleluyahs, and through 'Praise God in His holiness' they ascend to the Holy of Holies, as stated in Shaar HaKavanos and Pri Etz Chaim, Shaar HaZemiros, chapter 6, and explained in Rashash's siddur. Since their rectification to ascend to their Holy of Holies is not yet complete, how can they ascend to Beriah through Kaddish? Moreover, since the rectification of Yetzirah is not complete, how can it ascend? The principal ruling is therefore not to interrupt.
