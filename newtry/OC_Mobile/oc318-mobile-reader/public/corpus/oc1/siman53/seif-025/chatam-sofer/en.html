There: In the Mechaber, seif 25, there in the gloss. Note: See Responsa Chasam Sofer, Orach Chayim, simanim 11 and 205. In siman 205 he writes in the middle of the responsum: “I have already written elsewhere that the customary practice of specifying a term of three years in documents appointing a rav and his subordinate officials does not establish a fixed limit. Who has ever heard that a community would bring a rav or the like from a distant place and afterward remove him from there? Rather, Jewish custom is Torah. The term is written for the benefit of the rav and the subordinate officials, so that should he wish to leave after three years, he has permission to do so. Although an ordinary laborer whose work involves preventing a loss may not retract during the middle of his term, and this rav’s term is in principle lifelong, he may nevertheless retract after three years. The reason is that one may not hire himself out for more than six years, and some say for more than three years, because that resembles the status of a Hebrew servant.” See Shach, Choshen Mishpat, siman 333, seif katan 17. Study that responsum carefully, as well as the responsum following it.
