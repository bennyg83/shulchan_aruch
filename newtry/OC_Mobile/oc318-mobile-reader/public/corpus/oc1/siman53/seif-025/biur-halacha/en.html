If witnesses came against him — see Mishna Berurah regarding kala d'lo pasik; and see Biur HaGra, who cites Gittin chapter 9, page 1a; Ketubot 36b — and this is what he said in the Gemara there: "A name of harlotry went out in the city — we are not concerned for it." And it appears that even kala d'lo pasik — for otherwise the Gemara's difficulty there would fall; see there. And in Kol Bo, from which this law is sourced, it states "valid witnesses" — it appears that all the more so kala d'lo pasik alone certainly does not help. And in the responsa of Chavot Yair, siman 11, he brought these words of Kol Bo and concludes that even kala d'lo pasik — if the whole congregation is satisfied to retain him as cantor — one need not protest against them. However, if one of them protests and says, "I believe this kala d'lo pasik," he may protest to remove him. He also wrote there that sometimes they remove him through kala d'lo pasik even without a protester — such as when a report went out that the ugliness was done before witnesses and they went overseas; even though there are no witnesses before us, since there is kala of witnesses on the side that was hidden and the report originally went out through valid witnesses, they remove him from his appointment. But a rumor that was not through witnesses, where the report originally went out through a slave, maidservant, and women — for this they do not remove him unless some individuals are found who protest against him, as above. And kala d'lo pasik is called such if a day and a half the whole city speaks ill of him and he has no enemies in the city whom we could say raised the report [Yevamos 25a]. And in the responsum of Mahari Weil, siman 188, it implies that it is not called kala d'lo pasik except with a voice that the majority agree upon and that does not cease — for presumably one would not say that this report went out for nothing. But if we know that only one person originally raised the report and the voice spread in the city, we do not place a defamatory name upon him. And it is possible that the same applies in our case. And afterward I found in Chavot Yair, part 1, siman 25, that it implies thus; see there. However, it would appear very difficult: how does any kala d'lo pasik help to remove a cantor? For he automatically has a monetary loss as well, as demonstrated in the commentaries on Chullin, end of first chapter, see there. And regarding monetary loss in general it is simple and clear that no kala helps, as implied by the conclusion of the Gemara, Gittin chapter 9, page 1a, top of the column; and likewise there, "he consecrated his property" or "he declared his property ownerless" — it also teaches anonymously, see there. And even a clear assessment does not help to extract money, as implied in Choshen Mishpat, siman 408 — and all the more so an ordinary kala. And it is possible that the reason is what is stated in Gittin chapter 9, page 1a — therefore "a name of harlotry went out for her, we are not concerned," because perhaps general promiscuity was attributed to her and thereby this report arose. And regarding a cantor it is possible that presumably people would not wish ab initio to appoint a man with so much promiscuity that he would come to kala d'lo pasik for such harlotry, and he should have guarded himself not to come to this. And regarding if one sees in him several signs of promiscuity — it is obvious that one must warn him, as Rama wrote regarding a sheliach tzibur who defiles his mouth.
