Unless. See responsum in Yoreh De'ah siman 29—a shliach tzibur who swore not to play with dice and violated his oath: we remove him; and all the more so we do not appoint him lekhatchilah. Nevertheless he concludes that we remove him only when two witnesses testify against him, and his confession alone does not help. And see responsum Devar Moshe siman 3 and siman 5, and Yoreh De'ah siman 28, and Maharit, and Maharshal, and responsum Edut BeYehosef siman 27 on this matter—see there; and responsum Penei Moshe siman 16—many laws branch out in this case. And responsum Noda B'Yehuda siman 23, and he concludes there that if one swore and transgressed regarding a matter involving desire for money—even if he was flogged, fasted, and made many fences—he does not return to fitness until he appeases his adversary; see Choshen Mishpat siman 34. And see in responsum Noda B'Yehuda siman 17 regarding one suspected of one of the Torah transgressions.

His son—a slaughterer who held his trade and died and left young sons, and another held it after him; and when they grew up they claim their father's chazakah—there is no claim and proof from parashat Tzav: "and the anointed priest in his place from his sons"—his son precedes any man, even if he does not fill his father's place; it is written "and whoever fills his hand"—while he fills his place, no other may come; and if not, let another come and serve—and it did not say "serve in his place" until his son is fit. It implies together: once he is pushed aside, he is pushed aside. And see responsum Radbaz siman 59, and responsum Rashba, part 1 siman 300; and see responsum Ohel Yosef 62. And in responsum Lev David siman 31 he raised that all this is only when the congregation wishes to appoint an assistant first—then the son precedes; but one must not reject the assistant for the son of the shliach tzibur. He also wrote that if after the death of the shliach tzibur they wished to appoint a shliach tzibur, the son precedes; nevertheless the assistant in his place remains. Rav—when weakness from old age overcame him, he appointed his son to preach every Shabbat without anyone opening his mouth. And when the elder's soul rested, they sought to reject the son; he raised in responsum Darkei Moshe siman 1 that they have no power to remove him, even though in that congregation there are others more distinguished than he; and see Yoreh De'ah siman 245 in the words of Rama, and responsum Rashba siman 300; and see Knesset HaGedolah and Rashdam, part 1 siman 35, and Rashal, first chapter of Chullin siman 51.
