Unless. See Responsa Beit Yehudah, siman 29, concerning a chazzan who swore not to gamble with dice and violated his oath. He is removed, and all the more so should he not be appointed initially. Nevertheless, he concludes that he is removed only where two witnesses testify against him; his own admission alone is insufficient. See Responsa Devar Moshe, simanim 3 and 5; Beit Yehudah, siman 28; Maharit; Maharshal; and Responsa Edus BiYehosef, siman 27, concerning this matter; see there. Responsa Penei Moshe, siman 16, contains many laws that branch from this case. See also Responsa Nechalas Shivah, siman 23. He concludes there that if one swore and violated the oath in a matter involving desire for money, then even if he receives lashes, fasts, and establishes many safeguards, he does not regain his fitness until he appeases the opposing party. See Choshen Mishpat, siman 34. See also Responsa Nechalas Shivah, siman 17, regarding one suspected of one of the Torah's transgressions.
His son. A shochet who had established possession of his position died and left minor sons, and another person took possession of the position. When the sons matured, they claimed their father's established right. Their claim is invalid. A proof may be brought from Toras Kohanim on parashas Tzav: “The anointed kohen from among his sons who succeeds him.” One might have thought that his son takes precedence over every other person even if he is unfit to fill his father's place. Therefore the verse states, “who is installed to serve,” teaching that when he is fit to fill his father's place, he succeeds him, but if not, another person comes and serves. It does not say that another should serve temporarily until the son becomes fit. This explicitly implies that once he has been displaced, he remains displaced. See Responsa Radbaz, siman 59; Responsa Rashba, part 1, siman 300; and Responsa Ohel Yosef, siman 2. Responsa Beit Yehudah, siman 31, concludes that all this applies only where the congregation initially wishes to appoint an assistant, in which case the son takes precedence. But an existing assistant is not displaced in favor of the chazzan's son. He further writes that if, after the chazzan's death, they wish to appoint a chazzan, the son takes precedence, but the assistant nevertheless remains in his position. A rabbi became weak because of old age and appointed his son to deliver the sermon every Shabbos, without anyone voicing an objection. When the elderly rabbi died, they sought to remove the son. Responsa Devar Moshe, siman 1, concludes that they lack the authority to remove him, even though that community contains scholars greater than he. See Yoreh Deah, siman 245, in Rama; Responsa Rashba, siman 300; Knesses HaGedolah; Responsa Maharashdam, part 1, siman 35; and Yam Shel Shlomo, first chapter of Chullin, siman 51.
