(73) A chazzan from his position - The same applies to any appointment in a place where they were not accustomed to appoint for a known term. The reason is so people not suspect them of having found a disqualification in him.
(74) A disqualification in him - Through witnesses. Then they do not accept him until he repents completely, without cunning or deceit; but a mere oath that he will not go in those ways does not help, for in any case he is already sworn from Mount Sinai.
(75) And one does not remove - But to appoint him initially, even because of mere rumor, one should refrain, even if the rumor about him was only in his youth, as above in seif 4.
(76) Who was caught, etc. - Nevertheless, one should be concerned for the rumor and seat a beit din over this. A shaliach tzibbur whose daughter committed adultery while married should not be appointed initially; but if this happened after he was already appointed, it appears one should not remove him because of this.
(77) Or who informed on a person - A shaliach tzibbur who threatens a person to hand him over to gentiles but did not hand him over: although the matter is ugly even for someone who is not a shaliach tzibbur, he is not disqualified for this. Rather, they rebuke him and warn him that if he continues to speak such words, they will remove him from his stature; and if he persists in his rebellion, it is proper to remove him.
(78) Witnesses about him - From Magen Avraham above in seif katan 7 it implies that even with a persistent rumor, an individual may protest against him to remove him. So too it implies in Yam Shel Shlomo, Chullin, end of siman 52. See Biur Halachah.
(79) They remove him - If he repented, this helps so he is not removed, even if he transgressed intentionally. But regarding appointing him, it is explained in seif 5 in the gloss that for intentional transgression repentance does not help, for his past is not proper; see there in Mishna Berurah seif katan 22. If he sinned in his youth and has still not repented, even if they appointed him, they remove him, for one disqualified by sin is disqualified for testimony, as Peri Megadim writes.
(80) The soiled garments - A shaliach tzibbur who, because of illness beyond his control, cannot clean himself to be pure from a nocturnal emission, and does not purify himself at all, while serving as shaliach tzibbur: perhaps on the Days of Awe one should rebuke him to immerse, and at least to remove the soiled garments from upon him. If he will not listen to the words of the Sages to wash well and remove the soiled garments from upon him, it is proper to remove him.
(81) To change - It is good that he change his dirty clothes all day when he goes among people, so he not be among those who make themselves repulsive; just as a Torah scholar may not go out to the marketplace in dirty clothes, so too a shaliach tzibbur, even if he is not a Torah scholar.
(82) With songs of gentiles - Meaning a tune that gentiles sing for their idol worship. Bach in responsa siman 127 wrote this is specifically a tune designated for this. One who goes to gentile courts is disqualified from being shaliach tzibbur on Rosh Hashanah and Yom Kippur unless he repented, as Eliyah Rabbah writes in the name of Torat Emet. It appears to me that this speaks where no money of his fellow remained in his hand as a result, such as where their law was like our law; nevertheless he is disqualified at least for Rosh Hashanah and Yom Kippur, for it is a great sin in itself, as stated in Choshen Mishpat siman 26. Otherwise, if the disqualification were because of sin, even on other days of the year he should not be shaliach tzibbur, as above in seif katan 14. Perhaps in our times, because of our many sins, when the prohibition has been forgotten by many people and perhaps he is inadvertent in the matter, one should not disqualify him, as a similar idea appears in Choshen Mishpat siman 34 seif 4; see there. In any case, regarding shaliach tzibbur on Rosh Hashanah and Yom Kippur, one should certainly be stringent. A shaliach tzibbur who was a shochet and was found to have sold treifah as kosher inadvertently, or who was removing forbidden fats and forbidden fat was found after him: although one does not remove him from being shaliach tzibbur, nevertheless one should not appoint him initially, as Knesset HaGedolah writes. If he repented, it is permitted even to appoint him, as above in seif 5 in the gloss. A community that divided the prayers of Rosh Hashanah and Yom Kippur as customary, if one of them became ill and cannot pray, he is not permitted to appoint another person in his place; it is specifically in the hands of the community to appoint him, as Sha'arei Ephraim siman 31 writes. See responsa Knesset Yechezkel siman 13.
(83) A shaliach tzibbur who grew old - The same applies to all appointments: his son has priority, even if others are greater than he, provided he fills his fathers' place. Some say that regarding a scholar appointed to teach Torah or judge, we do not say their sons have priority; others disagree. See Magen Avraham, Knesset HaGedolah, Olat Tamid, and responsa Chatam Sofer, Orach Chayim siman 13.
(84) And wants, etc. - Rashba wrote there that the same applies in all appointments: if the appointee wants someone to assist him in that appointment from time to time, he has permission. Knesset HaGedolah wrote that this is specifically if he is fit for it, both regarding the work and regarding fear of Heaven.
(85) Pleasant - Provided that his voice is not unusual. One should not remove a shaliach tzibbur for another whose voice is pleasant. If the majority want another, both should serve together.
