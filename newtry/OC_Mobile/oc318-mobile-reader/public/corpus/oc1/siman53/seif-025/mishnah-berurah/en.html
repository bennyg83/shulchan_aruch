(73) A cantor from his craft — and the same applies to every appointment in a place where they are not accustomed to appoint for a known period, lest they suspect that disqualification was found in them.

(74) Disqualified in it — in witnesses. Then they do not accept him until he returns in complete repentance without guile and deception; but a mere oath that he will not go in these ways does not help, for in any case he is already sworn and standing from Mount Sinai.

(75) And they do not remove him — but to appoint him initially, even for mere rumor, one should prevent it; even if the rumor was only about him in his childhood, as above in seif 4.

(76) That he was caught, etc. — nevertheless there is concern for rumor to seat a court over this. A sheliach tzibbur whose daughter committed adultery under her husband — he is not appointed initially; but if this happened after he was already appointed, it appears they do not remove him for this.

(77) Or if a person — a shaliach tzibbur who threatens on his life to hand over [another] to a non-Jew and did not hand him over: even though the matter is ugly, even for a person who is not a shaliach tzibbur he is not disqualified for this, but they rebuke him and warn him that if he continues to say such things they will remove him from his position; and if he persisted in his rebellion, he should be removed:

(78) Witnesses are upon him — and from the words of Magen Avraham above in seif kaf zayin it implies that even with an unceasing rumor, a single individual may protest against him to remove him; and so is implied in Yeshuos Shabbos, Chullin at the end of siman 52; and see Beur Halakhah:

(79) They remove him—and if he repented it helps not to remove him even if he transgressed intentionally; but to appoint him, it is explained in Shulchan Aruch in the gloss that for intentional sin repentance does not help, for his chapter is not seemly—see Mishna Berurah there, note 22. And if he sinned in his youth and has not yet repented, even if they appointed him they remove him, for he is disqualified due to sin for testimony [Peri Megadim]:

(80) Soiled garments—a sheliach tzibbur who from his illness is compelled and cannot cleanse himself to be pure from an emission at night, and he does not purify himself at all—being a sheliach tzibbur, possibly on the Days of Awe one should rebuke him to immerse, and at least to remove soiled garments from upon him; and if he does not listen to the words of the Sages to wash well and remove soiled garments from upon him, he should be removed:

(81) To change—and it is good that he change his soiled garments all day when he walks among people so that he not be among those who cause hatred, for just as a Torah scholar may not go out to the market in soiled garments, so too a sheliach tzibbur, even if he is not a Torah scholar:

(82) In gentile songs — meaning in a melody that gentiles play for their idol worship; and Bach in responsum siman 127 wrote specifically a melody designated for this. One who goes to the courts of idolaters is disqualified to be shaliach tzibbur on Rosh Hashanah and Yom Kippur unless he did repentance [Eliyah Rabbah in the name of Torat Emet]; and it seems we discuss when no money of his fellow remained in his hand through this — such as when their law in this was like ours; nevertheless he is disqualified at least on Rosh Hashanah and Yom Kippur, for it is a great sin on its own account, as stated in Choshen Mishpat siman 26 — for otherwise, is not one disqualified due to a sin forbidden to be shaliach tzibbur even on the rest of the days of the year, as above in seif kaf dalet? And perhaps in our time, in exile, the prohibition is forgotten among many people, and it is possible he is shogeg in the matter — one should not disqualify him, as stated similarly in Choshen Mishpat siman 34 seif 4, see there; but at least regarding shaliach tzibbur on Rosh Hashanah and Yom Kippur one should certainly be stringent. A shaliach tzibbur who was a slaughterer and was found to have sold treifah under presumption of kashrus b'shogeg, or who was a poultry examiner and milk was found after him — even though they do not remove him from shaliach tzibbur, nevertheless one should not appoint him l'chatchila [Knesset HaGedolah]; and if he did repentance, it is permitted even to appoint him, as above in siman 55 in the gloss. A congregation that divided Rosh Hashanah and Yom Kippur prayer as is customary — if one of them became ill and cannot pray, he is not permitted to place another man in his place; only the congregation may place him [Shaarei Ephraim siman 31, and see responsum Knesset Yechezkel siman 13]:

(83) A shaliach tzibbur who aged — and the same applies to all appointments: his son takes precedence, even if there are others greater than he, since he fills his fathers' place. Some say regarding a sage appointed to disseminate Torah or to judge — we do not say his sons take precedence; and some disagree; see Magen Avraham, Knesset HaGedolah, Eshel Avraham, and responsum Chavat Yair Orach Chaim siman 13:

(84) And if he wishes, etc. — Rashba wrote there likewise that in all matters of appointments, if the appointee wishes that someone assist him in that appointment for periods, the authority is in his hand; and Knesset HaGedolah wrote specifically if he is fit for it — both in the work and in fear of Heaven:

(85) Pleasant — provided his voice is not strange. One should not remove a shaliach tzibbur on account of another whose voice is pleasant. If the majority want the other, both serve as one:
