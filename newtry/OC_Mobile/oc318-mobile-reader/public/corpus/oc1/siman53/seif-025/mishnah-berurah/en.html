A chazzan from his craft. The same applies to any appointment in a place where they are not accustomed to appoint for a known time. The reason is so that they not be suspected that some disqualification was found in them.
A disqualification in him. By witnesses. Then we do not accept him until he repents with complete repentance, without cunning or deceit. But a mere oath that he will not go in these ways does not help, for in any case he is already sworn and standing from Mount Sinai.
And we do not remove. But with respect to appointing him initially, even because of mere rumor one should refrain, even if the rumor about him was only from his youth, as above in seif 4.
Who was caught, etc. Nevertheless one must be concerned for the rumor and convene a beis din over the matter. A shaliach tzibbur whose daughter committed adultery under her husband should not be appointed initially. But if this occurred after he had already been appointed, it appears that he should not be removed because of it.
Or who informed on a person. A shaliach tzibbur who threatens a Jew that he will inform on him to the non-Jews, but did not inform, although the matter is ugly even for one who is not a shaliach tzibbur, he is not disqualified because of this. Rather, they rebuke him and warn him that if he continues saying such things they will remove him from his position. If he persists in his rebellion, it is proper to remove him.
Witnesses about him. From the words of the Magen Avraham above in seif katan 7, it implies that even with an uninterrupted rumor, an individual can object to remove him. So too it implies in Yam Shel Shlomo, Chullin, end of siman 52. See Biur Halacha.
We remove him. If he repented, this helps so that he is not dismissed, even if he transgressed intentionally. But regarding appointing him, it is explained in seif 5 in the gloss that when it was intentional, repentance does not help, because his period is not seemly; see there in Mishnah Berurah, seif katan 22. If he committed a transgression in his youth and still did not repent, even if they appointed him, they remove him, for he is disqualified for testimony because of the transgression. Peri Megadim.
The soiled garments. A shaliach tzibbur who because of illness and compulsion cannot clean himself so as to be pure from a nocturnal emission, and does not purify himself at all, if he is a shaliach tzibbur, perhaps on the Days of Awe one should rebuke him to immerse, and at least to remove the soiled garments from upon him. If he does not listen to the words of the Sages to wash well and remove the soiled garments from upon him, it is proper to remove him.
To change. It is good that he change his soiled clothes all day when he goes among people, so that he not be among those who cause others to hate. For just as a Torah scholar is forbidden to go out in the marketplace in soiled garments, so too a shaliach tzibbur, even if he is not a Torah scholar.
With songs of the non-Jews. Meaning with a tune that non-Jews use in their idol worship. Bach wrote in a responsum, siman 127, that this refers specifically to a tune designated for this. One who goes to the courts of non-Jews is invalid to be shaliach tzibbur on Rosh Hashanah and Yom Kippur unless he repented. Eliyah Rabbah in the name of Toras Emes. It appears to me that this speaks where his fellow's money did not remain in his hand through this, such as where their law in this matter was like our law. Even so, he is at least invalid on Rosh Hashanah and Yom Kippur, for it is a grave sin in itself, as stated in Choshen Mishpat, siman 26. Otherwise, one disqualified because of a transgression should not be shaliach tzibbur even during the rest of the year, as above in seif katan 14. Perhaps in our times, because of our many sins, when the prohibition has been forgotten by many people, it is possible that he is inadvertent in the matter and should not be disqualified, similar to what is stated in Choshen Mishpat, siman 34, seif 4; see there. At least regarding being shaliach tzibbur on Rosh Hashanah and Yom Kippur, one should certainly be stringent. A shaliach tzibbur who was a shochet and was found to have sold treifah as kosher inadvertently, or who was a menaker and prohibited fat was found after him, although we do not remove him from being shaliach tzibbur, nevertheless we do not appoint him initially. Kenesses HaGedolah. If he repented, it is permitted even to appoint him, as above in seif 5 in the gloss. A congregation that divided the prayers of Rosh Hashanah and Yom Kippur according to custom, if one of them became ill and cannot pray, he may not appoint another man in his place. Only the congregation has authority to appoint him. Sha'arei Ephraim, siman 31; see Responsa Kenesses Yechezkel, siman 13.
A shaliach tzibbur who aged. The same applies to all appointments as well, that his son takes precedence, even if others are greater than he, provided he fills the place of his fathers. Some say that regarding a scholar appointed to spread Torah or to judge, we do not say that their sons take precedence, and some disagree; see Magen Avraham, Kenesses HaGedolah, Ba'er Heitev, and Responsa Chasam Sofer, Orach Chaim, siman 13.
And wants, etc. The Rashba wrote there that the same applies to all types of appointments: if the appointee wants someone to assist him in that appointment at intervals, he has permission. The Kenesses HaGedolah wrote that this is specifically if he is fit for it, both in the work itself and in fear of Heaven.
Pleasant. Provided that his voice is not unusual. One should not remove a shaliach tzibbur because of another whose voice is pleasant. If the majority desire another, both should serve together.
