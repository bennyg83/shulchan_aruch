**disqualified.** Then they do not accept him, even if he swears that he will not walk in these ways until he returns in complete repentance (Rashdam, part Orach Chayyim siman 32; Magen Avraham; see there).

**singing.** But to appoint him at the outset—even mere singing in general—one does not appoint him at the outset; and this is what the author wrote above, seif 4, that no bad reputation should come upon him (see there; and see above, note 7, in the name of Shach).

**the gentile woman.** And nevertheless one must convene a court on this (Maharit; Knesset HaGedolah). A shliach tzibur whose daughter committed adultery under her husband—if it is not possible with another, it is considered after the fact and he is fit (Mahari of Bruna, Knesset HaGedolah). And see there implies that even if he was already accepted as permanent shliach tzibur, they remove him if there is another; and all the more so one does not appoint him at the outset, see there; and see above siman (129) [128], note 71, what was written in the name of Knesset HaGedolah and in the name of Shevut Yaakov regarding a kohen. A shliach tzibur whose voice is disqualified and sounds as if he is trembling and drunk is disqualified as shliach tzibur (Mahari of Bruna, Knesset HaGedolah).

**they remove him.** And if he repents, they do not remove him (see there, see there).

**soiled garments.** A shliach tzibur who, because of his illness, is forced and cannot keep himself pure from the cold of night and does not purify himself at all—and he is shliach tzibur—on the Days of Awe it is possible to rebuke him that he should immerse; and at least that he remove the soiled garments from upon him; and if he does not heed the words of the Sages to wash well and remove the soiled garments from upon him, it is fitting to remove him (Mahari Yitzchaki, may his memory be blessed, in his responsum, Zera Avraham part Orach Chayyim section 6, see there).

**in songs of gentiles.** Meaning in a melody that he plays for idolatry (Magen Avraham in the name of Terumat HaDeshen). And Bach in responsum section 127 wrote: specifically a melody designated for a house of idolatry. Rashal wrote in responsum siman 20: a shliach tzibur who was a slaughterer and it was found that he sold treifah meat believing it kosher inadvertently, or who was a shochet and chelev was found after him—even though they remove him from his trade, they do not remove him from shliach tzibur; and see above note 7 what I wrote in the name of Mishpetei Tzedek part 2 siman 75; and Knesset HaGedolah wrote that Maharshal acknowledges that one does not appoint him at the outset. One who went to gentile courts is not fit to be shliach tzibur on Rosh Hashanah and Yom Kippur unless he repented (Torat Emet siman 157). A congregation that divided the prayer of Rosh Hashanah and Yom Kippur as is customary—if one of them fell ill and cannot pray, he is not permitted to appoint another in his place; rather it is specifically in the hands of the congregation to appoint him (responsum Sha'ar Ephraim siman 31; and see responsum Knesset Yechezkel siman 13). Reuven, who was established to pray regularly on the Days of Awe, and afterward became a widower, and some objectors arose saying that he could not pray because he had no wife—in responsum Penei Moshe siman 54 he inclined now here and now there, and finally concluded that he should not be removed on account of this matter, for there is no sin in it (Mishpetei HaDevarim HaMe'akvim); and he further added there to the branch that that man had already become betrothed, see there; and see in Leket HaKemach page 11 side b.

**sweet.** Provided that his voice not be strange. And a great principle in all appointments: the son takes precedence; and appointments that are the crown of Torah—their sons do not take precedence, only from Hillel the Nasi onward they were accustomed that their sons take precedence. One should not remove a shliach tzibur on account of another whose voice is sweet; and if the majority wants the other, both should serve as one. See Knesset HaGedolah, Magen Avraham, and see there.
