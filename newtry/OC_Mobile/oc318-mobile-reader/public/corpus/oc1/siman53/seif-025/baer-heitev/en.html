Invalid. Then we do not accept him, even if he swears that he will not go in these ways, until he returns in complete repentance, Maharshaldam, Orach Chaim, siman 32, Magen Avraham, and Olas Tamid.
A rumor. But to appoint him initially, even on the basis of a mere rumor, one should not appoint him initially. This is what the Mechaber wrote above, seif 4, that no evil rumor should have gone out about him, Olas Tamid. See above, s.k. 7, what is written there.
The non-Jewish woman. Nevertheless, a beis din should be seated over such a matter, Maharit, Knesses HaGedolah. A shaliach tzibbur whose daughter committed adultery under her husband, if no one else is possible, is considered like after the fact and is valid, Mahari Bruna, Sheyarei Knesses HaGedolah. The Olas Tamid wrote: It implies that even if he was already accepted as a regular shaliach tzibbur, we remove him if there is someone else, and all the more so one should not appoint him initially; see there. See above, siman 128, s.k. 71, what I wrote in the name of Sheyarei Knesses HaGedolah and in the name of Shevus Yaakov regarding a kohen. A shaliach tzibbur whose voice became invalid, so that it sounds as if he is trembling and drunk, is invalid to be shaliach tzibbur, Mahari Bruna, Sheyarei Knesses HaGedolah.
We remove him. But if he repented, we do not remove him, Olas Tamid; see there.
The soiled garments. A shaliach tzibbur who, because of his illness and against his will, cannot cleanse himself to be pure from a seminal emission, and he does not purify himself at all, and he is the shaliach tzibbur, perhaps on the Days of Awe one should rebuke him that he immerse, and at least that he remove the soiled garments from upon him. If he does not listen to the words of the Sages to wash very thoroughly and remove the soiled garments from upon him, it is proper to remove him, Rabbi Yitzchak Isaacchi, of blessed memory, in his responsum Zera Avraham, Orach Chaim, end of siman 6; see there.
Songs of non-Jews. That is to say, with a melody that he uses to sing for idol worship, Magen Avraham in the name of Terumas HaDeshen. The Bach, in a responsum at the end of siman 127, wrote that this applies specifically to a melody designated for a house of idol worship. Maharshal wrote in Responsa, siman 20, that a shaliach tzibbur who was a shochet and it was found that he had sold tereifah as kosher inadvertently, or who was a menaker and cheilev was found after him, even though we remove him from his profession, we do not remove him from being shaliach tzibbur. See above, s.k. 7, what I wrote in the name of Mishpetei Tzedek, part 2, siman 75. Knesses HaGedolah wrote that Maharshal agrees that he should not be appointed initially. One who went to the courts of non-Jews is not fit to be shaliach tzibbur on Rosh Hashanah and Yom Kippur unless he repented, Toras Emes, siman 157. A congregation divided the prayers of Rosh Hashanah and Yom Kippur as customary. If one of them became ill and cannot pray, he is not permitted to appoint another person in his place; only the congregation has the authority to appoint him, Responsa Shaar Ephraim, siman 31. See Responsa Knesses Yechezkel, siman 13. Reuven was established to pray regularly on the Days of Awe, and afterward he became a widower, and some objectors arose against him, saying that he could not pray because he has no wife. In Responsa Pnei Moshe, siman 54, he argues this way and that way, and in the end concluded that he should not be removed because of this matter, since there is no sin in it according to the law of the matters that prevent one; and he also joined there as an additional factor that the man had already betrothed a woman; see there, and see Leket HaKemach, folio 11b.
Pleasant. Provided that his voice is not unusual. A great rule regarding all appointments: the son precedes. But appointments that are the crown of Torah, their sons do not precede; only from Hillel the Nasi onward was the custom that their sons precede. One should not remove a shaliach tzibbur for another whose voice is pleasant. If the majority desire another, both should serve together. See Knesses HaGedolah, Magen Avraham, and Olas Tamid.
