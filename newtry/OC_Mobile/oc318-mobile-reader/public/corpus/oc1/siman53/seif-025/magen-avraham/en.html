And the congregation cannot object. This speaks of a case where they accepted him as shaliach tzibbur for all the days of his life. If so, he certainly stipulated with this in mind, that if at times he would be unable to pray, he could appoint another to assist him. Therefore his son takes precedence. Furthermore, even if the father had not been fit for this, the line of law is that his son takes precedence, for the custom of that place was that sons are appointed in place of fathers. They stated a great principle regarding all appointments, that the son takes precedence, even regarding a Kohen Gadol, and even if there are others greater than he, end quote from the Rashba, siman 300. So too it is in the Sifrei, that for all appointments, their sons take precedence. So too wrote the Knesses HaGedolah. Maharshdam in Yoreh Deah, siman 81, wrote that with a scholar appointed to teach Torah publicly or to judge, we do not say this, end quote. In the book Yefeh Mareh, from Chelek Daled, part 2, chapter 19, he wrote that all appointments that are the crown of Torah, their sons do not take precedence, and only from Hillel the Nasi onward did they practice that their sons take precedence.
