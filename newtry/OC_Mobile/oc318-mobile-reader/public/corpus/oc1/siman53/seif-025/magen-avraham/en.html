And it deals with one whom they accepted as shaliach tzibbur for all his days; if so, certainly he stipulated that if sometimes he cannot pray, he will appoint another to assist him—and if so, his son precedes. And further, even if the father was not fit for this, the rule of law is that his son precedes—for the custom of the place was that sons are appointed in place of fathers; and they stated a great principle in all appointments that the son precedes, even in the Sanhedrin, and even if others are greater than he—end quote, Rashba siman 300; and so in Sifri that all appointees—their sons precede; and so in Sanhedrin; and Rashdam in Yoreh De'ah siman 281 wrote that for a sage appointed to spread Torah or to judge, we do not say thus—end quote. And in Yad Malakhi part 1 chapter 2 paragraph 19: all appointments that are the crown of Torah—their sons do not precede, except from Hillel the president onward they practiced that their sons precede; see chapter 13 of Ketuvot.
