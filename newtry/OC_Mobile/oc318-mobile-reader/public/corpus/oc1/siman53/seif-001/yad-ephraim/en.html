(In Taz note 1) And it seems to me that the Tur and Shulchan Aruch also did not intend, etc. Many find this difficult, for behold it is not according to Bach's words that he brought at the beginning of his words—and what did he innovate in this? Also what he wrote "to stand before the ark at its beginning," etc.—the word "at its beginning" has no meaning; and it seems to me there is a scribal error here, and it should read thus: the shaliach tzibbur who must stand before the ark to say Barchu should precede himself at his beginning by reciting Yishtabach, and he should also say this standing, etc. And he innovated in this that according to Bach the entire core of this law did not come because the shaliach tzibbur says Yishtabach—for that is an obvious matter—but only came to inform us of the law that it must be said standing; if so, at first glance standing would be on account of saying Yishtabach itself. However Bach was forced to say that what he wrote—that one must say standing—is on account of the Kaddish after it; and this is somewhat strained if we interpret that the core of the law is to inform us that Yishtabach must be standing, for it would have been fitting that he not make an interruption between Yishtabach and Kaddish, and we would thereby know that he did not sit in his place while saying Yishtabach (see what he wrote, viz.: if he says Yishtabach seated, then he interrupts between Yishtabach and Kaddish for the entire time that he rises from where he was sitting and goes down before the ark and sets himself to begin Kaddish; and it is not proper to interrupt anything between Kaddish and Yishtabach; therefore the shaliach tzibbur must stand and prepare himself to go down before the ark before he begins Yishtabach, so that immediately when he finishes Yishtabach he says the Kaddish after it standing, etc., see there)—for the preparation to go down before the ark to say Kaddish is an interruption; therefore Taz wrote that the intention in this law is not mainly to inform us of the standing for Yishtabach, but because we would not know whether the shaliach tzibbur has to go down before the ark and say Yishtabach, since the core of the shaliach tzibbur's prayer to discharge the congregation is through saying Barchu; therefore they came to inform us that the shaliach tzibbur who prepares himself to say Barchu should not begin before the ark with Kaddish, but only has to precede himself by saying Yishtabach also before the ark; and incidentally he wrote that he should also say it standing so that it is joined to what he says after without interruption—and this is the Tur's intention: "and the shaliach tzibbur stands and begins Yishtabach"; and likewise Shulchan Aruch, who wrote "and the shaliach tzibbur says Yishtabach standing," to inform us that the shaliach tzibbur's core [role] is only from Barchu onward; nevertheless he precedes himself to begin from Yishtabach and must also say it standing so as not to interrupt; and since the core of the law is not on account of interruption but only taught you that before he begins Barchu he must show that he is the shaliach tzibbur before the ark through his saying Yishtabach, therefore he used this wording of what the shaliach tzibbur must say Yishtabach before the ark—it is possible it does not depend on the reason of interruption at all, but out of propriety that he not come before the ark to begin with Kaddish while the congregation needs to fulfill [their obligation] and their intention will be confused; therefore it is good to precede himself by saying Yishtabach so the congregation knows he is the shaliach tzibbur; and since his reason is not dependent on interruption he phrased it thus; and nevertheless that he must say it standing is on account of not needing to interrupt—and as Bach also wrote; and I expanded to explain the matters for students who find difficulty understanding the matters, explained well, and they are clear in my humble opinion.
