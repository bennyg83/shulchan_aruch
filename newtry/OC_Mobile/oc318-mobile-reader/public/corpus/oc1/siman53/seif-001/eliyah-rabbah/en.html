[1] shliach tzibur, etc. Thus is the language of the Tur, and it implies that the shliach tzibur stands and not the individuals. Beit Chadash wrote, and likewise Derishah wrote—this is his language: what we change on Shabbat to say, the congregation in a loud voice 'Yishtabach' seated; and it seems to me because they enacted that after the shliach tzibur says 'in the mouth of the upright You are praised,' etc., the congregation says in assemblies; and it is appropriate to say to the congregation, and in assemblies he does not explain what the praise is but that they are obligated to praise. Therefore they enacted that the congregation say in a loud voice 'Yishtabach,' and the congregation have no reason to say standing; but the chazzan says standing so that he say Kaddish over it—up to here. And that which Rama and Levush wrote at the end of siman 51 that the custom is to stand—one must say that there it is from custom and here he speaks from law.
