Hagahos Maimoniyos certainly also bases the ruling on placing Kaddish immediately afterward, since Yotzer Or itself does not require standing and the Kedushah within Yotzer may be recited while seated, as stated by Magen Avraham, siman 59, seif katan 2. Rather, it appears that the congregation also practiced standing for Kaddish. Taz therefore cites Maharil, who holds that an individual need not stand for Kaddish. See Magen Avraham. Eliyah Rabbah writes that in siman 56, seif 6, the practice is a mere custom rather than strict law, and only the chazzan is required to stand. See there. Levush uses Tur's wording, “The chazzan shall begin,” whereas the Mechaber intentionally changed the wording because the chazzan must begin standing from Baruch She'amar. I do not know why Levush retained Tur's formulation. Levush also writes that all of Pesukei DeZimrah must have been recited, but Nachalas Tzvi objects that reciting part is sufficient. Eliyah Rabbah writes that with a congregation one must recite all of Pesukei DeZimrah. Certainly, when someone can be found who recited Baruch She'amar and all of Pesukei DeZimrah, he is preferable. It also stands to reason that an individual should not recite Yishtabach unless he recited at least some verses. This refers to reciting it as a blessing over those verses. The text of Yishtabach itself, however, may be recited independently. See what I wrote in siman 52 and see Magen Avraham.
