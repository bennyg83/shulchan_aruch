He says Yishtabach: the shliach tzibur stands so as not to interrupt between Yishtabach and Kaddish (Tosafos Yeshanim). From this the custom spread that on Shabbat the congregation sits when they say Yishtabach, because the chazzan draws out the melody at the end of the Yishtabach blessing and they do not stand until Kaddish begins; but on weekdays the congregation also stands with the shliach tzibur to hear Kaddish standing from the mouth of the shliach tzibur and to answer Amen immediately; likewise Barechu is also [said] standing. But because of the blessing of Yishtabach one need not stand; nevertheless men of deed are accustomed to stand when they say Yishtabach, both on Shabbat and on weekdays, even when praying alone. And likewise anything involving sanctity requires standing, as stated in Yerushalmi on the verse "a matter of God came to you, O king, and you stood up." And above in siman 8 I wrote that one must stand for every mitzvah blessing; only for blessings of enjoyment need one not stand.
