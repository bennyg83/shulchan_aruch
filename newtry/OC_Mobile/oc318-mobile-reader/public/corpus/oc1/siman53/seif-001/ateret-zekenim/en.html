The chazzan recites Yishtabach while standing, so that he not interrupt between Yishtabach and Kaddish (Tosafos Yom Tov). From this developed the custom that on Shabbos the congregation sits while Yishtabach is recited, because the chazzan chants at the end of the blessing of Yishtabach, and they do not stand until he begins Kaddish. During the week, however, the congregation also stands together with the chazzan, in order to hear Kaddish while standing from the mouth of the chazzan and immediately answer, “Amen, yehei Shemei rabbah,” while standing as well. Standing is not required because of the blessing of Yishtabach itself. Nevertheless, people of pious practice customarily stand when reciting Yishtabach, both on Shabbos and during the week, and even when praying alone. Likewise, one must stand for every matter of sanctity, as stated in the Yerushalmi concerning the verse, “I have a word of God for you,” whereupon the king arose. Above, in siman 8, I wrote that one must stand for every blessing over a mitzvah; only blessings over enjoyment need not be recited standing.
