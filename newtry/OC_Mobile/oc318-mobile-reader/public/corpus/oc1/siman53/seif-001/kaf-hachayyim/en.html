1) [Se'if 1] The shaliach tzibbur recites Yishtabach while standing. This is so that he may immediately follow it with Kaddish. Beis Yosef; Bach; Taz 1; and Magen Avraham 1. An individual, however, need not stand while reciting Yishtabach, as stated above in siman 51:42. Regarding Magen Avraham's statement that the end of siman 51 implies one must stand, Eliyah Rabbah 1 writes that the ruling there must concern custom, while the ruling here concerns the strict law; see there. Pri Megadim, Mishbetzos Zahav 1, cites him. We already wrote above that the universal custom among us is that an individual does not stand while reciting Yishtabach; see there.
2) Magen Avraham writes at the beginning of the siman, in Shelah's name, that all fifteen praises in Yishtabach (from 'song and praise,' etc., through 'from now') should be said in one breath. Zohar, Terumah 132a, however, does not imply this. Rather, the prohibition is to interrupt with speech, and there are only thirteen praises, from 'song and praise' through 'and kingship'; see there. Shalmei Tzibbur, page 70d, writes likewise. The Arizal also does not mention in Shaar HaKavanos that they should be recited in one breath, as we quoted him above in siman 48; see there. Chida, Kesher Gudal 7:47, writes in the name of Yaavetz's siddur that they should not be recited in one breath, for they must be said melodiously, like one setting forth the praise of a king. Siddur Beis Yaakov 13; Shaarei Teshuvah 1; and Chayei Adam 18:4 in the names of Gra and Zohar rule likewise. We already discussed the Yishtabach text and its author above in siman 48 in the name of Shaar HaKavanos; see there. See also Shalmei Tzibbur, pages 70d and 71a, and Siddur Beis Yaakov 14. Regarding answering Amen and any sacred response, its law is like Baruch She'amar; see above, siman 51:11 and siman 5:4. During the thirteen praises, however, one should take care not to place himself in such a situation.
3) 'Life of the worlds' is pronounced with a patach. Birkei Yosef 207; Kesher Gudal 7:49. This is the common practice. See below, siman 54:6.
