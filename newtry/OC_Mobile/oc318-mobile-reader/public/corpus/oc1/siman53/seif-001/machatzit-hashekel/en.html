(Note 1) "While standing," etc.—it implies when praying alone. For certainly when praying in congregation, the individual must also stand like the shaliach tzibbur—even though for the shaliach tzibbur the reason is so that Kaddish adjoins him: if he sat during Yishtabach and would need to stand for Kaddish, there would be some delay and interruption between Yishtabach and Kaddish—nevertheless, the individual must stand at the time of Kaddish (at least at the saying of "Amen yehei shemei rabba"), as below, siman 56, note 1; which is not so when the individual prays not in congregation—because of Yishtabach itself he need not stand. And likewise on Shabbat, when the chazzan sings and prolongs at the end of Yishtabach in saying "Baruch Hu uvaruch Shemo"—then there is delay for the individual praying in congregation to stand while the chazzan sings, and then the individual need not stand for Yishtabach. But in Shulchan Arukh, siman 51 (note 7 in the gloss), it implies one must stand—for the rule in an individual's prayer: Yishtabach with Baruch she'amar and "and David blessed" implies their laws are equal; and also for Yishtabach one must stand because of Yishtabach itself, like Baruch she'amar and "and David blessed."
