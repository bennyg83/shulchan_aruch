The chazzan recites it. This implies that only the chazzan recites it standing, but not an individual. My father-in-law, of blessed memory, explained that standing is not required because of reciting Yishtabach itself. Rather, since Kaddish must be recited standing immediately afterward, if the chazzan recited Yishtabach while seated, he would need to interrupt between Yishtabach and the following Kaddish in order to stand. This explanation, however, does not appear from the Hagahos Maimoniyos cited by Beit Yosef: “One who speaks between Yishtabach and Yotzer Or bears a sin. Therefore, the practice is to recite Yishtabach standing, like Yotzer, since one may not interrupt between them.” This implies that the ruling applies to every person. Rama already wrote in siman 51 that the practice is to stand when reciting Yishtabach. Maharil records that Rabbi Yaakov Segal would not stand for Kaddish or Barechu, but whenever Kaddish began while he was already standing, he remained standing until he finished answering “Amen, yehei Shemei,” etc. I likewise saw this in the sefer of the Arizal's kabbalistic intentions. It appears to me that Tur and Shulchan Aruch also did not intend to require standing because of the recitation of Yishtabach itself. Rather, since the chazzan must stand before the lectern when he begins Barechu, they intended that he should anticipate this by also reciting Yishtabach standing, so that it is joined to what follows without interruption.
