"Saying — shaliach tzibbur." It implies specifically the shaliach tzibbur says it standing, but not an individual. And the late Mahari Chai z"l gave a reason: there is no need to stand from saying Yishtabach himself, but rather because Kaddish must follow standing; and when the shaliach tzibbur says Yishtabach seated he would need to interrupt standing between Yishtabach and the Kaddish after it. But it does not imply so in Hagahot Maimoniyot that Beit Yosef brings, and thus: "One who speaks between Yishtabach and Yotzer has violation in his hand" — therefore they are accustomed to say Yishtabach standing like Yotzer, for it is forbidden to interrupt in between, end quote. It implies it applies to every person. And Rama already wrote in siman 51 that they are accustomed to stand when saying Yishtabach. And in Maharil it is written that Mahari Segal would not stand neither for Kaddish nor for Barchu; but every Kaddish he began standing, he remained standing until he finished Amen yehei, etc. And so I saw in Sefer HaKavanot of the Ari z"l. And it seems Tur and Shulchan Aruch did not mean one must stand for saying Yishtabach, but they meant that the shaliach tzibbur, who must stand before the ark at his beginning to say Barchu, should precede himself by saying Yishtabach — that he too say it standing, so that it join to what he says after without interruption.
