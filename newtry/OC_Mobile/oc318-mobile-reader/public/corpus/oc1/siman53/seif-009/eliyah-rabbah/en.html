[9] Deliberately, etc. It appears that murder is not meant specifically, for the same applies to other transgressions, as stated above in seif 4. It is difficult to say that the earlier ruling concerns one against whom a bad reputation emerged and who did not repent. Kol Bo, page 156, likewise indicates that even if he repented from other transgressions, he is still considered one against whom a bad reputation emerged. Kol Bo there further implies that the deliberate sinner remains disqualified despite repentance only for a communal fast, but during the rest of the year it is permitted even to appoint him initially. Rabbi Eliyahu Mizrachi writes similarly in Responsa, siman 88. Knesses HaGedolah writes in the name of Nimukei Ramah: One who struck his pregnant wife with a piece of splitting wood, and she died before nightfall in a manner clearly attributable to that blow, is unfit to serve as chazzan. This is his wording. I require study of his source.
