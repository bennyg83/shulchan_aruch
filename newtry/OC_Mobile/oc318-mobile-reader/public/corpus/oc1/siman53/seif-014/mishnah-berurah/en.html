Blind. Even in both eyes, and he is obligated in all mitzvos according to the Torah, for we rule like the Sages against Rabbi Yehudah. In the responsum Chavos Yair, siman 176, he concluded that nevertheless a blind person should not pray as shaliach tzibbur on the Days of Awe, even if blind in one eye, where there is someone else equally proper and fit; see his reasoning there. In Eliyah Rabbah, letter 10, he disagrees with him. It appears to me that even according to the Chavos Yair this is only not to appoint him initially, but not to remove him from being shaliach tzibbur if he became blind. A shaliach tzibbur who is hard of hearing and cannot hear at all, Rabbi Akiva Eiger concludes in his novellae that he should not be shaliach tzibbur, for although if one did not make his own ears hear he has fulfilled his obligation, nevertheless, since initially one must make his ears hear, he does not discharge others initially.
