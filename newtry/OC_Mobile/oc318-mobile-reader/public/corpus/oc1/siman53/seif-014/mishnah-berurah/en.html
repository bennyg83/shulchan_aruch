(41) A blind person — even with both his eyes, and he is obligated in all mitzvot from the Torah, for the halakhah follows the rabbis against R' Yehuda. In responsum Chavot Yair siman 176 he brought that nevertheless a blind person should not pray on the Days of Awe, even with one eye, where there is another suitable and fit like him—see there his reason. Eliyahu Rabbah in letter 10 disagrees with him, and it seems to me that even according to Chavot Yair, this means only not to appoint him initially, but not to remove him from shaliach tzibbur if he became blind. A shaliach tzibbur who has heaviness in the ears and does not hear at all—Re'em in Chiddushei Re'em concludes he should not be shaliach tzibbur, for even though if he did not make it audible to his ear he has fulfilled his obligation, nevertheless since initially he must make it audible to his ear, he does not discharge others initially.

(42) That he should not read — see below siman 139, seif 4, in the gloss.
