14) [Se'if 4] A shaliach tzibbur must be fit, etc. Scripture says, 'She raised her voice against Me; therefore I hated her.' Mar Zutra bar Tuvyah said in Rav's name (and some say Rabbi Chama said in Rabbi Elazar's name) this refers to an unfit shaliach tzibbur who descends before the lectern. Taanis 16b; Tur. Regarding the verse's feminine language, Kisei Shlomo on Taanis, page 116b, writes: It is known that every prayer is called rachamim. The shaliach tzibbur must arouse mercy, which comes from the right, the masculine side, as those familiar with the mystical wisdom know. An unfit shaliach tzibbur not only fails to arouse the masculine side, but even transforms the attribute of mercy into the attribute of judgment, which comes from the feminine side. The verse therefore uses the feminine, 'She raised,' etc. See there for his ethical rebuke of an unfit shaliach tzibbur. Machazik Berachah 1; Shalmei Tzibbur, page 71c; Shaarei Teshuvah 6. See also below, paragraph 16.
14) There: A shaliach tzibbur must be fit, etc. Abudraham explains why the shaliach tzibbur is customarily called a chazzan: the Aramaic translation of 'he saw' is 'chaza,' because he must see how he reads. Eliyah Rabbah 5.
