(12) It is necessary — for it is written, "She put upon me her voice, therefore I hated her," and Chazal said: this is a shaliach tzibbur who is not fit. And the books extended greatly on the magnitude of the disgrace of those who appoint a shaliach tzibbur who is not fit, for they thereby withhold good from Israel.

(13) Fit — and it is fitting that the shaliach tzibbur's garments be long so his legs are not visible, and he enters the synagogue first and leaves last. He should not be foolish or a simpleton, but able to speak on communal matters as needed. It is good to prefer for shaliach tzibbur a righteous son of a righteous person, for the prayer of a righteous son of a righteous is not like the prayer of a righteous son of a wicked. What Rosh wrote—that the elevation of the shaliach tzibbur does not depend on family pedigree—for even if from a disgraced family and righteous, it is good to draw near from distant seed; meaning also only that he is not of distinguished lineage, but nevertheless his father is not wicked [later authorities]. Blemishes disqualify only for kohanim and not for shaliach tzibbur; on the contrary, "a broken and crushed heart, God, You will not despise." Some are stringent about this initially where there is one fit and proper like him—see below in se'if kaf alef regarding a blind person what we wrote there.

(14) From transgressions — one who transgresses an oath—his house is not empty; and even though witnesses have not yet testified about him, nevertheless there is concern for rumor to convene a court on account of this; and even if he only transgressed a vain oath for the future, nevertheless it is not fitting to appoint him shaliach tzibbur—see there [author]. He also wrote, citing Mishpat Tzedek, that one disqualified due to a sin is disqualified to be shaliach tzibbur even incidentally. Pri Megadim wrote this is specifically when he did not repent.

(15) And that no bad reputation emerged, etc. — specifically to appoint him initially; but to transfer and remove him after he was established as shaliach tzibbur we have no [grounds], since we hear nothing against him now. However, when a bad reputation has now emerged against him—which is an incessant rumor—even one individual may protest to remove him [Knesset HaGedolah in the name of Rabbeinu Avraham Chaviv]. See below in seif 25 in Beur Halakhah what we wrote on this. And even if clarified by witnesses that he sinned, if he repented we do not remove him according to all.

(16) In his youth — even if it becomes clear to us that he then repented on that matter, nevertheless he is not appointed initially as shaliach tzibbur. A shaliach tzibbur who was already removed and stood thus days and years with the congregation's consent, and now they want to appoint him—it is like an initial acceptance, and even a minority can protest. If the protesters are the leaders, it is as though the whole congregation protests; unless in a place where there are arbitrators and a regulation not to change their decree—then the protest of the leaders is ineffective.

(17) For the nation — Maharam of Mintz wrote in responsum: the cantor should beware if there is quarrel and fight between two homeowners that he be "sit and do not act"—neither in speech nor in deed, openly or secretly; and all the more if there is a quarrel with another person not of the city with a homeowner. If he has any quarrel or hatred with one of the congregation, he must bring the matter before the rabbi or congregation to compromise and do as seems right to them; and if there is a defect and obstacle from his opponent's side, then he is innocent—what should he do?

(18) And accustomed to read — so that the verses interwoven in prayer are arranged in his mouth; also, if not accustomed to read, he is included in "turns his ear from hearing Torah," and his prayer is an abomination [author]. Pri Megadim wrote in siman 581 seif 1: we find regarding Rosh HaShanah and Yom Kippur we require that he be married and thirty years old; and it implies for the rest of the year even if not married; nevertheless it seems to me married takes precedence in choosing, even if his beard is full.
