Must. For it is written, 'She raised her voice against Me, therefore I hated her,' and Chazal said that this refers to an unfit shaliach tzibbur. The books wrote at great length about the severe disgrace of appointing an unfit shaliach tzibbur, for through this they withhold good from Israel.
Fit. It is proper that the garments of the shaliach tzibbur be long, so that his legs not be seen, and that he enter the synagogue first and leave last. He should not be foolish or ignorant, but should be able to speak about communal matters as needed. It is good to seek a righteous shaliach tzibbur who is the son of a righteous person, for the prayer of a righteous person son of a righteous person is not comparable to the prayer of a righteous person son of a wicked person. What the Rosh wrote, that the stature of the shaliach tzibbur does not depend on family lineage, and that even if he is from a lowly family but is righteous it is good to draw near those from distant stock, means only that he lacks distinguished lineage, but nevertheless his father is not wicked. Blemishes disqualify only kohanim, not a shaliach tzibbur. On the contrary, 'a broken and crushed heart, O G-d, You will not despise.' Some are stringent about this initially where there is someone else equally proper and fit. See below in seif katan 41 regarding a blind person, where we wrote about this.
From sins. One who violates an oath, his house is not empty. Although witnesses have not yet testified about him, nevertheless one must be concerned for the rumor and convene a beis din over the matter. Even if he violated only an oath of expression concerning the future, nevertheless it is not proper to appoint him as shaliach tzibbur; see there. Eliyah Rabbah further wrote there in the name of Mishpat Tzedek that one who is disqualified because of a transgression is disqualified from being shaliach tzibbur even occasionally. The Peri Megadim wrote that this is specifically when he has not repented.
And that no bad reputation went out, etc. This is specifically with respect to appointing him initially. But to remove and dismiss him after he has already been established as shaliach tzibbur, we do not do so, since nothing is now heard about him. However, when a bad reputation has now gone out about him and it is an uninterrupted rumor, even an individual may object to remove him. See below, seif 25, in Biur Halacha, what we wrote about this. Even if it was clarified by witnesses that he sinned, if he repented, all agree that he is not removed.
In his youth. Even if it was clarified to us that he then repented for that matter, nevertheless one should not initially appoint him as shaliach tzibbur. A shaliach tzibbur who was already removed and remained that way for days and years with the consent of the congregation, and now they wish to appoint him, this is like an initial acceptance, and even a minority can object. If those who object are the communal leaders, it is as though the entire congregation objects, unless it is a place that has arbiters and an ordinance not to change their decree; then the objection of the communal officers is ineffective.
To the people. Maharam Mintz wrote in a responsum that the chazzan should be careful, if there is a quarrel and dispute between two householders, to remain passive, whether in speech or in action, openly or secretly. This is all the more so if there is a dispute involving a person from outside the city with a householder, that he should remain passive, even if that person is his mechutan or relative. If he himself has any quarrel or enmity with one of the congregation, he must present the matter before the rav or the congregation to mediate and act as they see proper. If the shortcoming and obstruction is from the other side, then he is innocent, and what can he do?
And is accustomed to read. So that the verses interwoven within the prayer will be ordered fluently in his mouth. Also, if he is not accustomed to read, he is included in 'one who removes his ear from hearing Torah, even his prayer is an abomination.' Eliyah Rabbah. The Peri Megadim wrote in siman 581, seif 1, that regarding Rosh Hashanah and Yom Kippur we require that he be married and thirty years old. It implies that on other days of the year, even if he is unmarried, nevertheless it appears to me that a married man precedes a bachur, even if the bachur's beard is fully grown.
