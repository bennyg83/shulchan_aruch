Seif 4: A prayer leader must be fit, etc. Note: It appears that this applies even when he is to lead the prayers on a single occasion, and not specifically to a regularly appointed prayer leader. The proof is that Shulchan Aruch changes its wording below, in seif 6, and writes, "One appoints only one whose beard has filled out." Why did it not use the expression "one appoints" here as well? This proves that the requirements stated here apply even without an appointment, when he leads only on one occasion. This is further demonstrated by the fact that Beis Yosef writes that the Tur derived this ruling from the Gemara in tractate Taanis; see there. One who examines that Gemara will see that it discusses even leading the prayers on a single occasion and does not discuss accepting someone as the prayer leader permanently. This proof could, however, be rejected. One could say that there the discussion concerns a communal fast at a time of distress, when the prayer leader must possess all these qualities, but there is no proof concerning a time without distress. Rather, one could say that the Tur discusses appointing him permanently, which is even more compelling than a communal fast: over the course of a permanent appointment, it is inevitable that the community will at times require mercy and the like, and ordinarily the regularly appointed prayer leader will lead the prayers. The Tur therefore derived from there that he must possess all these qualities. Nevertheless, the wording of the Tur and Shulchan Aruch proves their view that these qualities are required even for an occasional prayer leader, for below they write, "One appoints only," whereas here they did not use that expression. I have ruled this way in actual practice. This appears correct in my humble opinion. Analyze this carefully.
