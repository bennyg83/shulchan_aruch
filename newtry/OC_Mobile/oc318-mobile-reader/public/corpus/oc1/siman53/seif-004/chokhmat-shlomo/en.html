seif 44: the shliach tzibur must be proper, etc. NB: It seems this speaks even of praying occasionally, and not specifically a permanent shliach tzibur—and proof from what Shulchan Aruch later changed its wording in seif 6 and wrote 'one does not appoint except one whose beard has filled in'—why did he not write that here in the wording 'one does not appoint'? It is proven from this that here we are strict even without appointment, only occasionally. And it is further proven—for Beit Yosef wrote that the source of this law they learned from Tur from the Gemara in Taanit, see there; and one who examines the Gemara there sees it speaks even of prayer occasionally and does not deal with permanent acceptance of shliach tzibur. However, this could be rejected—one might say that in truth there is no proof from there; one might say there it deals with a public fast, and in time of distress it requires all these qualities; but not in time of distress there is no proof. Only one might say the Tur speaks of his acceptance as permanent, and this is preferable to public fast—for on a permanent basis he will not escape that the congregation will need mercy, etc., and per the majority a permanent shliach tzibur prays—therefore for Rabbi it follows he should have all these qualities. Nevertheless, from the language of Tur and Shulchan Aruch, who wrote below in the wording 'one does not appoint' and here did not write thus—it is proven they hold that here one requires even occasionally. And so I ruled halakhah lema'aseh as in my humble opinion—correct, examine well.
