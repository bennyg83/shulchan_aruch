Fit. Maharam Mintz wrote: The garments of the shaliach tzibbur must be clean, without dirt or grease, and his garments should be long so that his legs are not visible. He should enter the beis haknesses first and leave last; see there, siman 81. He should not be foolish or ignorant, but should be capable of speaking regarding the needs of the congregation as necessary, Maharshal.
In his youth. In Responsa Re'em, siman 86, he writes that for an ordinary shaliach tzibbur, who is not the shaliach tzibbur for a fast day, it is not necessary that his youth have been proper. Rather, anyone who now has good deeds is fit to be a regular shaliach tzibbur, even if in his youth an evil rumor went out about him, and even if he worshiped idols, since he repented. So wrote the Rashach, part 2, siman 107, and Rabbi Aharon Sasson, siman 157. Maharshal, in Responsa, siman 50, also agreed that this requirement applies specifically on a fast day and at ma'amados, but on other days, even as a permanent appointment, anyone who is not wicked, is acceptable to the people, has a pleasant voice, and is accustomed to reading, is counted for being a shaliach tzibbur. So the author of Yad Aharon concluded as halacha, that for an ordinary shaliach tzibbur we do not require that his youth have been proper, and anyone whom we have seen repent is called fit; and so ruled most later authorities. See Rabbi Shmuel HaLevi, Yoreh Deah, siman 28. So too the Olas Tamid concluded, and he finishes: It appears to me that if the congregation decrees a fast, even though it is not now a public fast, nevertheless such a shaliach tzibbur should not lead the prayers, and all the more so on Rosh Hashanah and Yom Kippur; see there. The Olas Tamid further concluded that even the Mechaber, when he ruled that no evil rumor should have gone out about him in his youth, was stringent only that he not be appointed initially. But to remove and dismiss him after he has already been established as shaliach tzibbur, we should not be stringent; and so wrote the Magen Avraham, and Responsa Ra'anach, part 2, siman 41. See seif 25. He wrote further there that if an evil rumor went out about him now, and it is an unceasing rumor, even an individual may object to remove him. If they already removed him and he remained so for days and years with the agreement of the congregation, and they now wish to appoint him, this is now like an initial acceptance, and even a minority can prevent it. If those objecting are the communal leaders, it is as though the entire congregation objects, unless it is a place where there are arbitrators and a cherem not to change their decree; then the parnasim do not help, Magen Avraham in the name of Mishpetei Tzedek, see there, and Knesses HaGedolah. In Responsa Chut HaShani, siman 75, he wrote that since the malicious waters passed over him, he should be removed. But in a case where he was invited to convert out and did not convert of his own will, and he returned in full repentance, and they had already accepted him upon themselves as shaliach tzibbur, he is not removed; see there. See Chavos Yair, siman 176, Shaar Ephraim, siman 31, and Edus BiYehosef, siman 87. According to everyone, if he transgressed under coercion and repented, it is permitted even initially; and so wrote the Magen Avraham. A shaliach tzibbur from whose hand a tereifah emerged many times is dismissed from being shaliach tzibbur, Mishpetei Tzedek, part 2, siman 75. It is good to seek a shaliach tzibbur who is the son of a righteous person, for the prayer of a righteous person who is the son of a righteous person is not comparable, etc., Bach, Maharshal. But the Taz, s.k. 3, disagrees and wrote that one should draw close the one who is not of distinguished lineage, to bring those who are distant close to the Shechinah, for the Merciful One desires the heart, and his prayer will be heard more than that of a righteous person who is the son of a righteous person. One whose arms fell off may be shaliach tzibbur, for the way of the Holy One, blessed be He, is to use broken vessels, as it is written, 'a broken heart,' etc., Maharshal, first chapter of Chullin. The Magen Avraham, s.k. 8, disagrees with him and wrote that one must be careful that the shaliach tzibbur not be blemished, and furthermore, 'Offer it now to your governor.' In the sefer Eliyah Rabbah he brought several proofs that a blemished person may be shaliach tzibbur; see there.
