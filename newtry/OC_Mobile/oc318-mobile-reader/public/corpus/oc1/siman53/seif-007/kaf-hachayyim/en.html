Mahari Binyamin, cited by Mahari Zayin in Responsa Gevuras Anashim, Orach Chaim 1:51; Yad Aharon, Hagahos Beis Yosef; Birkei Yosef 4. Mahara HaLevi, siman 52, writes that this applies specifically when one was compelled by bodily needs. If he was not so compelled but tzitzis and tefillin became available to him, it is proper not to put them on or recite their berachos within Pesukei DeZimrah, but to wait until between Yishtabach and Yotzer; see there. Birkei Yosef cites him and comments: 'The Rav went too far in this ruling. See Rambam's responsa in the newly printed Pe'er HaDor 147, which rules the opposite: A person who obtains a tallis and tefillin while in Pesukei DeZimrah; may he put them on and recite the berachos? Answer: There is no prohibition whatsoever against reciting the berachos over tzitzis and tefillin during the Tehillim. Such an interruption is not forbidden in Pesukei DeZimrah, which is neither the Amidah nor Krias Shema, where such an interruption would be forbidden.' Thus, three opinions exist. Rambam permits interrupting for the berachos over tallis and tefillin during Pesukei DeZimrah, apparently even in the middle of a chapter of Tehillim. Mahari Binyamin permits it only between Tehillim. Mahara HaLevi permits it only if one was compelled by bodily needs; if tzitzis and tefillin merely became available, it is proper not to put them on or recite the berachos until between Yishtabach and Yotzer. Maran's unqualified wording also implies no distinction: the berachos should be recited only between Yishtabach and Yotzer. Kisei Eliyahu 3; Siddur Beis Yaakov 9; and Yafeh LaLev 3, unlike Shiyurei Knesses HaGedolah, Hagahos Beis Yosef 4. The rationale given by Magen Avraham and the later authorities cited above in paragraph 5 likewise implies no difference between the middle of a chapter of Tehillim and between Tehillim. The distinction is only between Baruch She'amar and Yishtabach on the one hand and between Yishtabach and Yotzer on the other; see there. Shulchan Aruch HaRav 3 and Erech HaShulchan 1 rule likewise. It is astonishing that Birkei Yosef says Gevuras Anashim went too far. See Zechor LeYitzchak Harari 8, who also questions Birkei Yosef. This does not resemble Maran's ruling in siman 66:2 that between sections one may interrupt to put on tzitzis and tefillin. There, they are required for Krias Shema so that one not resemble a person bearing false testimony against himself, as Beis Yosef writes in the name of Shibolei HaLeket. They are not needed for Pesukei DeZimrah. Moreover, here he can put them on between Yishtabach and Yotzer, which is more lenient; there is no other place available in Krias Shema. Even according to the Arizal's words cited above, that they must be put on before the passage of the Tamid, once that time has already passed and the mitzvah was not performed in its ideal order, one should not interrupt Pesukei DeZimrah. One who lacked tzitzis and tefillin and obtained them after Baruch She'amar should therefore act stringently and put them on with their berachos only between Yishtabach and Yotzer. Gevuras Anashim also writes that one who attended to bodily needs during Pesukei DeZimrah should wash and recite Asher Yatzar. The proper practice, however, is not to interrupt for that berachah until after Yishtabach, as stated above in siman 51:28. Regarding Gevuras Anashim's underlying ruling that one who attended to bodily needs and entered the lavatory must repeat the berachos over tzitzis and tefillin, we wrote above in siman 8:52 and siman 25:78 that the matter is disputed and doubt concerning berachos is resolved leniently; see there. Accordingly, one who attended to bodily needs during Pesukei DeZimrah, removed his tzitzis and tefillin, and entered the lavatory need not repeat their berachos after leaving and washing his hands. He should put them on without a berachah, because doubt concerning berachos is resolved leniently. See citations 100, 210.
