(7) There—except between Yishtabach and Yotzer. And likewise between psalm and psalm—Mahari Binyamin; and they brought Mahari Zein in responsa Gur Aryeh, part Orach Chayyim, general rule 1, siman 51. Yad Aharon in Hagahot Beit Yosef. Birkei Yosef, note 4. And Mahara HaLevi there in siman 52 wrote that this is specifically under duress for his needs; but if he was not under duress and tzitzit and tefillin happened to be available to him, it is fitting that he not wear them and bless on them within the verses of pesukei dezimra, but only between Yishtabach and Yotzer—see there. Beit Yosef [cited] Birkei Yosef there, and wrote on it: and the rabbi overdid in this ruling—go out and see in the important words of Rambam in Sefer Pa'ar HaDor newly printed, siman 147, who ruled the opposite: whoever happens to have tallit and tefillin and he stands in the verses of pesukei dezimra—may he wear them and bless on them? Answer: there is no prohibition in blessing on tzitzit and tefillin within the psalms, for such an interruption in pesukei dezimra is not forbidden, and it is not prayer nor Keriat Shema, until such interruption would be forbidden—end quote. It turns out according to his words that there are three disputes in this matter: according to Rambam, one may interrupt to bless on tallit and tefillin within pesukei dezimra—and from his words it implies even in the middle of the psalms; according to Mahari Binyamin, specifically between psalm and psalm; according to Mahara HaLevi, specifically if under duress for his needs—but if tzitzit and tefillin happened to be available, it is fitting not to wear and bless on them except between Yishtabach and Yotzer. And so is implied from the words of Maran—that there is no distinction, and one blesses on them only between Yishtabach and Yotzer, as Kissei Eliyahu, note 3, wrote; and in siddur Beit Yosef, note 9; and Yafeh Lelev, note 3. And not as Knesset HaGedolah wrote in Hagahot Beit Yosef, note 4. And so is implied from the reason Magen Avraham and the later authorities gave, who wrote above, note 5, that the distinction is not between psalm and psalm versus the middle of a psalm, but the distinction is between Baruch she'amar and Yishtabach, and between Yishtabach and Yotzer—as cited—see there. And so from the words of Razah, note 3—see there. And so Erech HaShulchan, note 1. And it is astonishing on Birkei Yosef, who wrote on Gur Aryeh that he overdid in this ruling; and see Zechor LeYitzchak Harari, siman 8, who also wondered on Birkei Yosef—see there. And it seems to me according to what Maran wrote in siman 66, seif 2, that between the chapters one interrupts to put on tzitzit and tefillin—for there it is different, because they are required for Keriat Shema so he not be like one who testifies false testimony against himself, as Beit Yosef wrote in the name of Shiltei HaGibborim; but they are not required for pesukei dezimra. And further, here it is possible to put them on between Yishtabach and Yotzer, which is more lenient—not so there, where there is no other place. And even per the Ari, whom we wrote above that one must put them on before parashat HaTamid—here the intent is that the time has already passed and he did not perform the mitzvah properly, so he may not interrupt in pesukei dezimra. Therefore one should be strict: one who did not have tzitzit and tefillin and they became available to him after Baruch she'amar should not wear and bless on them except between Yishtabach and Yotzer. And so Gur Aryeh wrote there regarding one who needed to relieve himself within pesukei dezimra—that he wash his hands and bless Asher yatzar; it is correct not to interrupt and bless except after Yishtabach, as written in siman 51, note 28. However, regarding the essential law that Gur Aryeh wrote—one who needed to relieve himself and entered the beis haknesset should return and bless on tzitzit and tefillin—we have already written above, siman 8, note 52, and siman 25, note 78, that there is a dispute in this, and the practice is to be lenient—see there. If so, here too: one who needed to relieve himself within pesukei dezimra and removed tzitzit and tefillin and entered the beis haknesset—when he leaves the beis haknesset and washes his hands, he need not return and bless on tzitzit and tefillin, but should wear them without a blessing, because of the practice to be lenient.
