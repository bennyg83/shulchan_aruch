That he should not, see Taz. In siman 141, seif katan 3, a blind person may receive an aliyah and recite the blessing while the chazzan reads from the Torah. The blind person himself, however, may not recite written words from memory, for one may not recite orally matters that are written. The Mechaber's wording here is difficult for me, for it implies that if he has memorized the reading it is permitted. See siman 49. Yet we rule that even one letter may not be recited without a written text. See siman 139, where public Torah reading must specifically be from a valid Sefer Torah, and even printed Chumashim are insufficient. All the more so one may not read by heart. It is true that Kesef Mishneh, Hilchos Tefillah, chapter 12, halachah 8, writes that one may not read even one letter orally from matters that are written. This presently requires study.
