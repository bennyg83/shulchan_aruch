An aging chazzan. Rashba was asked about this case and ruled in favor of the father for two reasons. First, when a person holds an appointment, such as serving as a public scribe, a baker, or the like, and wishes to have another person assist him in that appointment from time to time when he is unable to perform it, he has the authority to do so, and the congregation cannot prevent him. Second, even if his strength has entirely failed and he can no longer perform the work at all, so that another person must be appointed, if his son is fit for the position, the son takes precedence over every other person, even if others are greater than he. See there. This explains the gloss here. As for Magen Avraham's statement that the case is where they accepted him as chazzan for his entire lifetime, one who studies Rashba carefully will see that Rashba wrote this only as an additional point, to prove that the congregation certainly intended from the outset that if, in his old age, he would occasionally require assistance, he would have the right to appoint someone for that purpose. The same law nevertheless applies even if he was hired for only two or three years and occasionally requires assistance. Beur HaGra also appears to imply this.
