A sheliach tzibur who has aged — in this case the Rashba was asked, and the Rashba vindicated the father for two reasons. First: one who is appointed — such as a scribe for the public or a notary — and everything of this kind, who wants someone to assist him in that appointment for periods when he is unable — the authority is in his hand, and the congregation has no power to detain him. Second: even if his strength has waned and he cannot do it at all and they are forced to take another — if his son is fit, he takes precedence over any person, even if there are others greater than him; see there. And by this you will understand the words of the gloss here, and what Magen Avraham wrote: it deals with one who was accepted as sheliach tzibur for all his days. One who examines there will see well that the Rashba wrote there only for clarity, to prove that certainly the congregation's intent from the outset was that if in his old age he would need help for periods, it would be in his authority to take one for this. Nevertheless, the same applies even if he was hired for two or three years and sometimes needs help — the authority is in his hand. And a little of this is also implied in Biur HaGra.
