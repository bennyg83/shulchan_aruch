(Note 26) Without desire and one should not quarrel, etc.—regarding lechem hapanim: even though its eating is a positive mitzvah, and as is stated in Yoma page 39a—nevertheless it is stated there: every forty years that Shimon HaTzaddik served, blessing was sent, etc., on lechem hapanim. And every kohen who received an olive's measure—some ate and were satisfied, and some ate and left over. After his death curse was sent, etc., on lechem hapanim. And every kohen received a double portion. The modest withdrew their hands and the gluttons took and ate. And Rashi explained: every kohen received double that is not an olive's measure—since they saw it does not satisfy, the modest withdraw their hands, etc.; and in the days of Shimon, those who received double did not withdraw their hands—end quote. And likewise Rashi in Kiddushin page 53a: the modest, etc., and every kohen who received double—the modest withdraw their hands from him and it does not help them at all, etc.—end quote. Behold he did not give the reason as quarreling but because it does not satisfy and does not help them. Even on Rashi himself it is difficult: since there is a mitzvah in eating, even if it does not satisfy, they should not withdraw their hands. Rather surely Rashi intended the words of the old Tosafot in Yoma, who wrote: R' Yehuda said—specifically when he receives double; but for an olive's measure they would not withdraw their hands, because they thought to fulfill the mitzvah of eating; but here there was no complete mitzvah of eating, since it is only double and they were not satisfied—end quote. Behold the main mitzvah is when one eats and is satisfied; but when one is not satisfied, there is no complete mitzvah (but nevertheless there is some mitzvah even if not satisfied—and see at the end of the old Tosafot's words). And this is Rashi's intent in what he wrote: since they saw it does not satisfy, they withdraw their hands—meaning since it does not satisfy, there is no main mitzvah. And so one may say Rashi's intent in Kiddushin. In any case, since nevertheless there is some mitzvah even if not satisfied, as the old Tosafot wrote at the end of their words—see there—they should not withdraw their hands; if so, it is because they will not need to quarrel with the gluttons. And accordingly, when there is a complete mitzvah, even though there will be quarreling, nevertheless one should not withdraw his hand. And it also implies in the old Tosafot: otherwise why did they need to write that since he does not fulfill the mitzvah properly, since they were not satisfied they withdrew their hands—let it teach to be saved from quarreling? Rather surely they hold that when there is a complete mitzvah, perhaps one may not leave because of quarreling; and perhaps specifically there it was not clear they would come to quarrel. But when he will certainly come to quarrel in any case, he may withdraw his hand.
