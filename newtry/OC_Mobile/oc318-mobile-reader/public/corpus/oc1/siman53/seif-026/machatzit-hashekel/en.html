Seif katan 26: Without willingness, and one should not quarrel, etc. This is learned from the lechem hapanim, even though eating it is a positive mitzvah. Yoma 39a states that throughout the forty years in which Shimon HaTzaddik served, blessing was present in the lechem hapanim, and every kohen who received an olive-sized portion ate and was satisfied, and some even left over. After his death, a curse was present in the lechem hapanim, and each kohen received only a bean-sized portion. The modest kohanim withdrew their hands, while the gluttonous ones seized and ate. Rashi explains that when each kohen received only a bean-sized portion, which is less than an olive, the modest ones withdrew because they saw that it did not satisfy them. In the days of Shimon HaTzaddik, however, even one who received only a bean-sized portion did not withdraw his hand. Rashi similarly explains in Kiddushin 53a that the modest ones withdrew because the portion did not benefit them. He does not say that they withdrew because of contention. Rashi's own explanation requires clarification, for since eating the lechem hapanim is a mitzvah, why should they withdraw merely because the portion did not satisfy them? Rashi must mean what Tosafos Yeshanim write in Yoma: specifically when one received only a bean-sized portion did the modest ones withdraw, but not when one received an olive-sized portion, because with an olive-sized portion they could fulfill the mitzvah of eating. With only a bean-sized portion the mitzvah was not fulfilled completely, since they were not satisfied. Thus, the principal mitzvah applies when one eats and is satisfied. When one is not satisfied, the complete mitzvah is absent, although some mitzvah still remains, as Tosafos Yeshanim state at the end of their discussion. This is Rashi's intent in saying that once they saw it did not satisfy them, they withdrew their hands, meaning that the principal mitzvah was absent. The same explains Rashi in Kiddushin. Nevertheless, since some mitzvah remained even without satisfaction, they should not have withdrawn entirely. Necessarily, they did so to avoid quarrelling with the gluttonous kohanim. It follows that where a complete mitzvah is present, one should perhaps not withdraw merely because contention may arise. Tosafos Yeshanim also imply this, for otherwise they could simply have explained that the modest kohanim withdrew to avoid quarrelling. Their need to explain that the mitzvah was not fulfilled properly because the portion did not satisfy them shows that where a complete mitzvah is present, one should not necessarily abandon it because of possible contention. This may apply only where it is not certain that a quarrel will arise. Where contention is certain, one may withdraw in every case.
