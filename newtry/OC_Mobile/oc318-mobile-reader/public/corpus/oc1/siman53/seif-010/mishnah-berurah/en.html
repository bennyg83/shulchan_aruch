The evening prayer. Because they do not discharge the public from their obligation, since they do not repeat the prayer, but only say Barechu and Kaddish. There are also other reasons; see the Beit Yosef. All this is pressed only to uphold the custom, but he himself holds that initially it is not proper to do so.
On Motzaei Shabbos. The same applies to weekday Maariv, but the custom practiced in those places was on Motzaei Shabbos.
In the evening prayer. Because of the Barechu in it. The same applies that he should not lead the responsive Shema for one who did not hear Barechu in the morning, for in truth Barechu is also a mitzvah for the entire congregation to hear in the morning and evening, and a minor who is not obligated cannot discharge them in this.
For he is still. Meaning, in places where they customarily pray the Shabbos service on Friday while it is still day because of accepting Shabbos early, nevertheless his thirteen years are not completed until Friday night, since the addition to Shabbos does not help regarding the years of a youth. But if he prays Maariv at night after the emergence of the stars, he may descend before the amud. When the phrase 'thirteen years and one day' is used everywhere, it does not mean that one needs one additional day, but rather that one needs thirteen complete years, from day to day, excluding a deficiency of even a few hours. Therefore, immediately at the beginning of the night his years are completed and he is an adult. Even if he was born on Rosh Hashanah before twilight, he becomes bar mitzvah at the beginning of the night of Rosh Hashanah of his fourteenth year.
