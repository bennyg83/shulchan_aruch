[10] On an occasional basis. Bach and Nachalas Tzvi conclude that on fast days and the Days of Awe, even occasional service is forbidden. Olas Tamid writes: If the congregation wishes to waive its honor, practical determination is required as to whether they have the authority to do so. I am astonished, for Bach concludes with clear proofs that the congregation cannot waive this even according to Rashba, and that this is the view of Shulchan Aruch. Ateres Tzvi and Sheyarei Knesses HaGedolah write likewise, and this also appears to me from Chiddushei Rashba at the end of the first chapter of Chullin. See there, for I have abbreviated. Perhaps Olas Tamid is uncertain only regarding occasional service on Rosh Hashanah and Yom Kippur. Yet it appears that occasional service on Rosh Hashanah and Yom Kippur is equivalent to permanent service during the rest of the year, where waiver is ineffective, as is explicit in Bach and Sheyarei Knesses HaGedolah there. Magen Avraham writes that siman 579 implies that on fast days an actual elder is required, and a beard that has merely filled out is insufficient. Responsa Mishpat Tzedek writes that one disqualified because of a transgression may not serve even occasionally. Maharam writes: You asked whether a man whose arms have fallen away is fit to serve as chazzan. It is obvious that he is fit. Physical blemishes disqualify only kohanim, not a chazzan. See below, siman 128, where kohanim are disqualified because people stare at them, a ruling originating in the Gemara. On the contrary, appointing such a person is a particularly choice fulfillment, for the King of kings desires to use broken vessels, as it is written, “A broken and crushed heart,” etc. Maharshal and Bach cite this. Derishah concludes: This is also my view, with proof from a blind person who may lead the service. All this implies that he is fit even as a permanent chazzan. This is explicit in Yam Shel Shlomo, Chullin, chapter 1, siman 48, and in his proof from leviim, who are fit for the musical service despite bodily blemishes. This is unlike Responsa Mahari Bruna, cited in seif 3, which permits him only occasionally. See Zohar, Emor, page 173, which implies that a chazzan with such a blemish is forbidden, as reflected in certain customs. A distinction may be drawn. See Chavos Yair, siman 176, who overlooked all the sources cited above.
