Olat Tamid wrote: however, if the congregation wish to forgive their honor, it requires practical investigation whether the authority is in their hands—up to here. And I am amazed—for Beit Chadash concludes with clear proofs that the congregation cannot protest, even against Rashba; and such is the view of Shulchan Aruch; and so wrote Atteret Tzvi and Sheyurei Knesset HaGedolah; and so it is proven to me from the novellae of Rashba at the end of the first chapter of Chullin—examine there, for I abbreviated. And perhaps specifically occasionally on Rosh Hashanah and Yom Kippur is in doubt; but it implies that occasionally on Rosh Hashanah and Yom Kippur is like permanent on the rest of the days of the year, that forgiveness does not help; and so is explained in Beit Chadash and Sheyurei Knesset HaGedolah there. Magen Avraham wrote that it implies in siman 579 that we require an actual elder on fasts and it does not suffice that his beard has filled in. Mishpetei Tzedek wrote in a responsum: one who is disqualified because of a sin—even occasionally—is forbidden. Maharam wrote: you asked about a man whose arms fell off whether he is fit to be shliach tzibur—it is obvious he is fit; and he is fit, for blemishes disqualify only kohanim (see below siman 128, that they disqualify because they look at him—and that is from the Gemara) and not a shliach tzibur. Rather, it is a mitzvah from the choicest that the King of kings of kings desires to use broken vessels, as it is written 'a broken and crushed heart,' etc.—up to here; they brought Rashal and Beit Chadash and Perishah, end. And so it seems to me—and a blind man who goes down before the ark is proof—up to here. And from all this it implies that even for a permanent shliach tzibur he is fit; and so is explained in Yam Shel Shlomo, first chapter of Chullin siman 48, and the proof he brings there from Levites who are fit for song service; and not like the responsum of Mahari of Bruna that Sheyurei Knesset HaGedolah brought, that specifically occasionally it is permitted. And see in Zohar, parashat Emor page 173, that it implies a shliach tzibur is forbidden like in customs—and one may distinguish. And see Chavot Yair siman 176, that he omitted all that was mentioned above.
