Who has already, see Taz. See Levush, who writes that nowadays an individual cannot protest, and a chazzan is accepted by the taxpayers and elected representatives, or by the seven communal trustees. See siman 581. Eliyah Rabbah, seif katan 23, writes that the chazzan stands in place of the kohen who offered the daily offerings and is called a “karov.” Rabbi Elazar was called “karov,” and liturgical poems are called “kerovos,” with a final tav, because they bring Israel close to their Father in Heaven and because they are placed within, in the middle of, the prayer. Beit Yosef writes in siman 68 that the acronym “kerovatz” stands for “A voice of song and salvation is in the tents of the righteous.” It may be that the word became corrupted from “kerovos” to “kerovatz.” Abudraham, page 21, also has this reading.
