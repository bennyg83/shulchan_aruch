In seif 10, in the gloss: “For he has not yet reached thirteen years of age.” Note: See Magen Avraham, who writes that when they pray Maariv at night, he may lead the congregation. See also Shach, Choshen Mishpat, siman 35, Sema there, and what I wrote in my work there. At present, however, this matter requires study in light of the Gemara, Rosh Hashanah 6b, which asks: “How can a year without festivals occur?” The question is difficult. It can occur where one vowed on the first night of Pesach. Upon the arrival of the first night of Pesach the following year, a complete year has already elapsed, while three festivals have not yet elapsed, because an entire festival is required, and here the festival has only begun. This accords with Tosafos below, 7b, s.v. veharei regalim, who write that the prohibition does not take effect from the evening. Thus, whether he vowed on the first night of Pesach or on its first day, as soon as the next Pesach night begins a year has elapsed, but three festivals have not. It must therefore be that thirteen full years and one additional day are required before one is considered thirteen years old, just as one is not considered one year old until the following day. Accordingly, according to the view that the festivals need not be in their proper order, the same applies within a single festival. If part of the days of Pesach pass in the following year, once the number of days omitted from the first Pesach has passed, those days join with the earlier days of Pesach and the three complete festivals are counted. It therefore cannot occur that a year passes without the festivals. If he vowed on the first day of Pesach, the year is not complete until that day has passed, and by then three festivals have also passed. This is a clear and powerful proof. See what I wrote on this matter in my novellae to Choshen Mishpat there. Analyze this carefully.
