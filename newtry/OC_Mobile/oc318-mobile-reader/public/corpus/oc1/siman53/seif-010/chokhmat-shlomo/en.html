NB: see Magen Avraham, who wrote that if they pray Maariv at night it is permitted to go before the ark; and see Shach Choshen Mishpat siman 35, and Semag, and what I wrote in my composition there. However, now to my view the matter requires study from the Gemara Rosh Hashanah chapter 1 page 6b, which asks 'but a year without regalim—where do you find it?' and it is difficult—what does it ask? Behold you find it if he vowed on the first night of Passover—for then when the first night of Passover of the coming year arrives, a full year has passed for him, and three regalim are not yet present, since we require an entire festival and here the entire festival is still a festival, as Tosafot wrote below there page 7b s.v. 'and behold regalim,' that they do not take effect from the evening—see there. If so, three regalim are not present and a year is present—whether in a vow on the first night of Passover or on the first day, and immediately when the beginning of the coming Passover night arrives a year is present and regalim are not. And perforce it is proven that we require thirteen years and one extra day—then he is considered age thirteen and a day of his year. If so, for one who holds we do not require them in order, the same applies to a festival—we do not require them in order; and if part of the days of Passover passed for the coming year after he reached the partial days missing from the first Passover, we complete those days to the days of the past Passover and it counts as three complete regalim—then you do not find a year without regalim; for if he vowed on the first day of Passover it is not considered a completed year until the first day of Passover has passed, and there are already three regalim. And this is clear and strong proof. See what I wrote in my novellae to Choshen Mishpat there on this, examine well.
