"And with a metal one." See Ateret Zekenim. Regarding a slave who did not undergo brit milah and immersion, according to the first view, which holds that there is a Torah prohibition against his performing melacha for his master, one may say that he may not go out with a metal seal. If the seal breaks, he will bring it to his master, acting for his master's benefit, and this is forbidden, as Levush states explicitly. With a clay seal attached to his garment, however, where he folds the garment because he fears his master and his master derives no benefit from that, it is permitted for such a slave. Accordingly the law is reversed with a metal seal: where his master made it, it is forbidden whether it hangs from his garment or neck, because the master is particular about the broken pieces and the slave will bring it to the master for the master's benefit. If the slave made it for himself, it is permitted in every manner, because he is allowed to perform melacha for himself. A slave who underwent brit milah and immersion, however, is forbidden in every case when he made it for himself, whether the seal is clay or metal, as we explained.
