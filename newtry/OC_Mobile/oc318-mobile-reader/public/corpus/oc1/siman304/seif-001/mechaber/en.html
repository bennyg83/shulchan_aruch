Concerning which slave one is commanded regarding his rest. It contains three se'ifim.
A person is commanded concerning the rest of his slave who was circumcised and immersed for the sake of servitude and accepted upon himself the mitzvos that apply to a slave. But if he was not circumcised and immersed, but only accepted upon himself the seven Noahide mitzvos, he is like a ger toshav, and it is permitted for him to perform melachah on Shabbos for himself but not for his master; and it is forbidden for any Jew to tell him to perform melachah on Shabbos for the need of a Jew, even one who is not his master. And if he did not accept upon himself any mitzvah, but is still a full-fledged non-Jew, his law is equivalent to one who accepted upon himself the seven mitzvos. Accordingly, the needs of a sick person who is not in danger — regarding which we hold that one may tell a non-Jew and he does it — and likewise a corpse on the first day of Yom Tov, regarding which we hold that non-Jews may occupy themselves with it — it is forbidden to tell a Jew's slave to do, even if he is an idolater; for since the slave's melachah is forbidden by Torah law, it was not permitted for something that involves no saving of life. And some disagree and permit in this. Nevertheless, if he was performing his master's melachah without [his master's] knowledge, and it is evident that he is not doing it for [his master's] benefit, it is permitted, and he need not stop him {even if he accepted upon himself the seven mitzvos}. And for another Jew who is not his master, even if he does it for the Jew's benefit, it is permitted — as long as there is no directive from a Jew, and provided that the Jew derives no benefit on Shabbos from that melachah. And some say that whoever has not accepted upon himself the seven Noahide mitzvos, since he is a full-fledged non-Jew, his master is not admonished concerning him; and accordingly, for the needs of a sick person who is not in danger, and likewise the needs of the dead on the first day of Yom Tov, it is permitted to tell them to do it. {Rama: And every slave concerning whose rest one is commanded may not go out with a seal that his master made for him to show that he is his slave; and if it is of clay, it is permitted to go out with it when it hangs on his neck, but not on his garment; and one of metal is forbidden in any case (Tur). And if the slave made the seal for himself, even one of clay is forbidden in any case (Maggid, chapter 19).}
