1) [Seif 1] A person is commanded to ensure that his slave rests, etc. Just as a person is commanded to ensure that his animal rests on Shabbos, he is commanded regarding his male and female slaves. Although they possess understanding and act of their own volition, we are commanded to supervise them and prevent them from performing labor on Shabbos, as it is stated, 'So that your ox and donkey shall rest, and the son of your maidservant and the stranger shall be refreshed.' Rambam, chapter 20, halachah 14. The slave himself is likewise forbidden to perform labor, even for himself, because he is obligated in everything that obligates a woman. The novelty is that his master is also commanded to prevent him, just as he is commanded to ensure the rest of all members of his household. Levush Serad. See the following paragraph.
2) Know that there are three types of slaves, apart from a Jewish bondsman, who has the status of a Jewish person in every regard except that one aspect of marriage to a non-Jewish maidservant is permitted to him. The first is a slave who underwent bris milah and immersion for the sake of slavery. He is obligated in all prohibitions like a fully Jewish person and in every positive mitzvah that obligates women, because his law is like that of a woman. The second is an uncircumcised slave who stipulated that he would not undergo bris milah but would observe the seven mitzvos commanded to the descendants of Noach. He is called a resident slave, and in some places an uncircumcised slave, and his law is like that of a resident alien. The third is an uncircumcised slave who practices his own idolatry. Maggid Mishneh there.
3) There: A person is commanded to ensure that his slave rests, etc. When the slave performs labor, the master transgresses, just as regarding the requirement that his animal rest. Maggid Mishneh there; Magen Avraham, paragraph 1; Tosefes Shabbos, paragraph 1. Although he transgresses a prohibition, he does not receive lashes because he does not perform the act himself. Sefer HaChinuch, mitzvah 33. This is also implied by Rambam, because no lashes are administered for the requirement that one's animal rest. Eliyah Rabbah, paragraph 1. Nevertheless, one may rent his slave to a non-Jew during the weekdays if he stipulates that the non-Jew perform no labor with him on Shabbos. This differs from one's animal, which siman 246, seif 3, forbids renting to a non-Jew under such a condition because the non-Jew is not trusted. The non-Jew cannot compel the slave to perform labor, and the slave is trusted regarding this. Eshel Avraham, paragraph 1; Mishnah Berurah, paragraph 2.
