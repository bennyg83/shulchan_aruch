Seif katan 1, "concerning his slave," etc. Two verses are written. One says, "so that your servant and maidservant may rest like you." This refers to a slave who underwent brit milah and immersion for the sake of slavery and accepted all the mitzvot incumbent upon slaves, meaning all mitzvot in which a woman is obligated. Shabbos is included among these mitzvot. Therefore such a slave may not perform melacha on Shabbos even for himself. The second verse says, "and the son of your maidservant and the stranger shall be refreshed." The Sages expounded this as referring to a gentile slave who is bodily owned by a Jew but did not undergo brit milah or immersion, and accepted only the 7 Noahide mitzvot. Every Jew is forbidden to instruct such a slave to perform melacha on Shabbos. For his own needs, however, the slave may perform melacha, because Shabbos is not included among the 7 Noahide mitzvot. A slave who is bodily owned by a Jew but did not undergo brit milah or immersion and did not accept any mitzvah, not even the 7 Noahide mitzvot, is the subject of a dispute among the authorities whether we are likewise commanded concerning his Shabbos rest, as will be explained.
