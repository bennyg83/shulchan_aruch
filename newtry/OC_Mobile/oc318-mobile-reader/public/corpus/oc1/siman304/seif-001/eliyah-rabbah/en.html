[1] [Levush] That he not compel him, etc. Rambam at the end of chapter 20 writes: just as a person is commanded regarding the rest of his animal on Shabbos, so he is commanded regarding his male and female slave. Even though they possess understanding and act on their own initiative, we are commanded to guard them and prevent them from performing melachah. See siman 247, seif 2. Although violating this is a negative commandment, there are no lashes because the master does not perform the act himself (Sefer HaChinuch, mitzvah 33). Rambam likewise implies this, since there are no lashes for failure to rest one's animal.
[2] And it is prohibited to every Jew. They are biblically warned regarding him, as Maggid Mishneh implies. Even rabbinic melachah is prohibited (Radbaz).
[3] And some disagree, etc. Bach, Radbaz, and Mas'at Binyamin are stringent like the first view. Responsa Rashba, siman 59, explains that the slave is no less than one's ox or donkey. Magen Avraham leaves Shulchan Aruch's apparent repetition unresolved. In my humble opinion, at first Shulchan Aruch writes that some disagree and permit, and the qualification is the final phrase, that this applies when the slave practices idolatry. If he does not practice idolatry, it is prohibited because he desecrates Shabbos for the sake of idolatry. This is Rashi's view cited by Beit Yosef. Afterward Shulchan Aruch writes that some say that so long as he has not accepted the seven Noachide commandments, etc., meaning that even if he does not practice idolatry, since he has not accepted the seven commandments it is permitted. This is Rosh's view in Beit Yosef. This explanation appears correct.
