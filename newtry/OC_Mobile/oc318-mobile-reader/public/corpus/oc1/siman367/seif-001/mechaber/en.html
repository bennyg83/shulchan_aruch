A man's wife may make an eruv for him without his knowledge
— even if he protested to her not to make an eruv, and even if he is not accustomed to make an eruv with them — when this applies: when he forbids them, such as when the house opens only to that one courtyard; but if it opens to two courtyards, in one of which he is accustomed to go in and out frequently so that he forbids them, and in one he is not accustomed to go in and out — in the case where he is accustomed to forbid, she makes an eruv without his knowledge even if he is not accustomed to make an eruv; and when he is not accustomed to go in and out there also, even though he does not forbid — if he is accustomed to make an eruv with them, she makes an eruv without his knowledge; but if he does not forbid and also is not accustomed to make an eruv with them, she does not make an eruv without his knowledge. And as long as he did not protest to her explicitly — even if he is not accustomed to make an eruv with them and he does not forbid — she makes an eruv without his knowledge, for presumably it is agreeable to him. But she cannot acquire from his property for others without his knowledge. And if the husband and wife are not in the city, the members of the household may make an eruv without his knowledge if the house opens only to one courtyard; and even if it opened to two courtyards, if he is accustomed to make an eruv with one of them, they make an eruv for him; but if he is not accustomed — no. But the courtyard residents cannot take his bread from his house to make an eruv without the knowledge of one member of the household — even if accustomed with them and he forbids them. And some say that his wife makes an eruv only without his knowledge; but if he says he does not wish to make an eruv with them — no. And if he is accustomed to make an eruv with them and now he does not wish — the courtyard residents enter his house and take from him by force; and if he is not accustomed, they cannot take from him by force; but they compel him in beit din to make an eruv with them, or beit din descends to his property.One who is not accustomed to make an eruv and made an eruv with courtyard residents and retracted from his eruv must go back and re-acquire; but if he is accustomed to make an eruv, it is an eruv against his will [Rosh, chapter HaDar].]:
