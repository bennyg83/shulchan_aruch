(1) A man's wife, etc. — because his wife is like his body; and they were lenient even when he protests to her, because Chazal were concerned lest he intend to provoke the residents to forbid them.

(2) She makes an eruv for him — from his own [property].

(3) When he forbids them — the courtyard if he will not make an eruv with them.

(4) In the case where he is accustomed to forbid, etc. — as below in siman 386 seif 9, see there.

(5) Accustomed to make an eruv, etc., without his knowledge — meaning even when he protests to her; and the reason is because of ways of peace, since until now it was his way to give a share in the eruv; therefore the eruv takes effect in every manner, and afterward it is permitted to carry utensils that remained in his house to this alleyway.

(6) But she cannot, etc. — meaning even for courtyard residents whom he forbids and he is accustomed to make an eruv with them.

(7) To acquire from his own for others, etc. — and from her sustenance she can acquire, for it is hers.

(8) Without his knowledge — meaning the husband does not know of this.

(9) Members of the household can, etc., without his knowledge — and if they know from him that he does not want, even though he did not protest to them explicitly, they cannot make an eruv.

(10) To one courtyard — for it is a benefit for him and certainly he will not object to them; but if it opens to two courtyards, they cannot make an eruv for him without his knowledge, for it is certainly not a benefit for him, for perhaps he does not wish to make an eruv with this courtyard but with that one, unless as Mechaber concludes.

(11) Accustomed to make an eruv, etc. — for presumably his intent is also now for this.

(12) Without the knowledge of one member of the household, etc. — meaning without the knowledge of one member of the household; for if it were with their knowledge it would be permitted, as long as they did not hear from the homeowner that he does not want an eruv; and see in Biur Halacha that we brought in the name of several Acharonim who rule that courtyard residents can take his bread without his knowledge [as long as they do not know he protests the matter], even without the knowledge of members of the household.

(13) But if he says he does not want, etc. — even if the house opens only to one courtyard that he forbids them by preventing the eruv.

(14) To make an eruv with them — no; and even if he said so after she made an eruv, her eruv is nullified retroactively.

(15) Courtyard residents, etc. — and the same applies to his wife; and it deals with when by preventing the eruv he forbids them.

(16) But they would compel him in beit din — meaning, even though they themselves cannot judge for themselves, beit din would compel him to make an eruv with them so he not forbid them. And this law of beit din compelling applies according to all, even according to the first view.

(17) They descend to his property — and make partnership.

(18) And he retracted from his eruv — meaning he said: I retract from the eruv and I do not wish to make an eruv and participate with you anymore.

(19) He must return and acquire — for his eruv and previous partnership are nullified, even though the bread is still placed in its place; and therefore if he wishes to be a partner with them again, he must participate anew; and see in Biur Halacha.

(20) It is an eruv by force — meaning he cannot return and nullify his eruv; and as above, where he is accustomed to make an eruv with them, courtyard residents come and take from him by force; and all the more so here, since the bread is already placed in their hands; and even according to the first view, who holds that without the wife's knowledge courtyard residents cannot take from him by force — here it is lighter, because the bread is already placed under their control.
