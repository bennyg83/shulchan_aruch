She cannot acquire. And it appears to me that from her sustenance she is permitted to acquire; see in Orach Chayyim siman 363.

Members of the household to make an eruv. Bach wrote that even if he does not wish to make an eruv, they can make an eruv unless he protests to them explicitly, see there; and see what I wrote siman 371 seif 4, that specifically regarding a non-Jew we say thus, but regarding a Jew they cannot make an eruv if he does not wish; and likewise in Beit Yosef here; and see siman 382 in Beit Yosef in the dispute between Rashi and Rashba.

To one courtyard — for it is a benefit for him.

Courtyard residents enter. And it appears to me that the same applies to his wife; and with this the sugya of the Gemara is well settled — 'accustomed — no, not accustomed — yes' — and one need not emend as Beit Yosef wrote.

It is an eruv by force. It appears to me that this is specifically according to the 'some say' that courtyard residents take by force; but according to the first view, they can never make an eruv without the wife's knowledge; requires study, for in chapter Halon Rosh wrote that always we require the wife's knowledge, and if so Jew and non-Jew are equal; and in Rosh chapter HaDar he wrote as Rama wrote here that courtyard residents take by force; and if so they contradict one another.
