A man's wife — see Taz. Behold the five levels: wife, hired worker, gleaner, household member, courtyard member, and another person. Protested to her explicitly. Revealed his intent that he does not want. Generally without his knowledge — behold three. House open only to this courtyard — necessarily he forbids them, whether accustomed to eruv with them or not accustomed to eruv. Open to two courtyards — in one he is accustomed to go out and come in — he forbids them, whether accustomed to eruv or not accustomed to eruv; and in a courtyard where he is not accustomed to go out and come in on weekdays — when not accustomed to eruv with them, all agree he does not forbid; but when accustomed to eruv with them, as appears there is a dispute between Levush and Taz — here it implies whoever is not accustomed to go out and come in, even if accustomed to eruv, does not forbid, only that they should not come to quarrel; since he is accustomed to eruv, the Sages enacted his wife is like his body and she eruvs by force of the husband — her ways are ways of pleasantness; but Perishah explains in the Tur that when accustomed to eruv even though not accustomed to go out and come in — he forbids; and in Rosh one may strain and explain as Perishah wrote in the Tur that he does not forbid from the going but forbids when accustomed to eruv; and he brought s.v. accustomed — see there. And in Rashi 'accustomed and intends to forbid' — one may strain 'accustomed to participate' meaning because he forbids that he is accustomed in going; and it is strained that he said accustomed and now changes to show he does not want — nevertheless they take by force — for if so from where do we challenge Shmuel against Shmuel, for here he said he is not accustomed yes, and above anonymously he said even when not accustomed — behold accustomed is greater — by force even though he revealed his intent he does not want. And Taz wrote that protest to her explicitly — she eruvs from her own intent; and this is Shmuel — accustomed to participate without by-force of him and with his wife's intent at least as Rosh and Tur wrote; and from where do we know — perhaps there is a difference between he does not want but did not protest to her, like that case of the Turzina who did not want to rent his house, and the Gemara compares rental of a non-Jew's house to eruv of an Israelite by force of him — behold he protested to her explicitly — say it does not help her intent; and answer that there is proof for them that whoever does not want is like protest — otherwise why challenge Shmuel against Shmuel, for above he said his wife eruvs without his knowledge implying even when not accustomed, and here specifically when accustomed — perhaps here when he protested to her. And although Rosh wrote that if by force of his wife there is no reason to take his eruv by force, but when he protested to her and she wants to eruv — there is no clear reason neither for prohibition nor permission; perhaps one may say from this we learn the Gemara holds whoever does not want is like protest to her and one may not distinguish at all between them. (And that which it challenges Shmuel against Shmuel — perhaps above without his knowledge his intent is not known — if so why specifically his wife, even household member too as we need to say below — if so.) This is the law of his wife; and hired worker and gleaner too like his wife in this — siman 382 Taz note 13, as proven from Tosafos there s.v. they came who wrote that from his renter and gleaner one may bring — and one may say it brings from his wife even though he protested explicitly; and that case of Turzina — possibly it was thus we learn even protest to his renter — he eruvs and from renter, etc. — and if so explained there further. And household member who is not his renter and gleaner — in general they eruv for him in every matter — and this is as Mechaber wrote, either when he forbids them or when he does not forbid only accustomed to eruv with them (Perishah in name of Perishah). And one courtyard member — Tur wrote as Mechaber here — even in general they cannot take bread from the homeowner and eruv with them, even accustomed to eruv with them and he forbids them, and not with knowledge of household member; and he challenged the Tur from what is written in siman 368 (and what he wrote why household member is better than courtyard member — one may say household member when accustomed to eruv even though he does not forbid) — in any case the Tur's words contradict each other; therefore Bach explained household member when the homeowner does not want — eruvs with knowledge of household member, and with explicit protest he is no worse than his wife in this; and courtyard member specifically in general helps, and here it deals when he does not want, and in siman 368 in general; and Taz was not satisfied with this for the Gemara proves that whoever does not want is like explicit protest from the challenge Shmuel against Shmuel, and household member when he does not want does not help — therefore in general it deals — from this courtyard member in general also does not eruv; and it is difficult from siman 368 (Tur wrote here generally courtyard member eruvs without his knowledge, and in Shulchan Aruch if there is no city, etc.) — and Taz answered that certainly household member and courtyard member are different as Mechaber wrote here, and they are equal regarding general — that wherever the homeowner's intent is not known, then his household member and courtyard member eruv — meaning at least knowledge of courtyard member is needed here; but when the homeowner does not want, even though he did not protest explicitly — likewise household member and courtyard member do not eruv; and with his wife she eruvs from her intent even though he protested to her. And in my humble opinion requires study what differs his wife from household member — see Levush and Tosafot Yom Tov that his wife is like his body, etc.; and therefore one may say his renter and gleaner too are only like household member; and one may say for a non-Jew it is lighter, for it helps also his renter and gleaner in siman 382 seif 11, and siman 371 in Magen Avraham note 6 and Magen Avraham here note 2 — and if so explained there further on this:
