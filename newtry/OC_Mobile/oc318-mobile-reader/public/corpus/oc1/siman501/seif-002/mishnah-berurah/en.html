One should not split at all. That is to say: not only to split them into very fine pieces, which involves concern for grinding, but even to make them into pieces that are not fine is also forbidden, since one can cook his pot without splitting; it is unnecessary exertion on Yom Tov and is forbidden. And the meticulous are accustomed to be careful even on Chol HaMoed, preparing all the small wood needed for cooking fish before Yom Tov, for one can burn it without splitting. (And even to split large wood on Chol HaMoed itself, which one cannot cook with as it is, since it is possible to prepare before Yom Tov, one should prepare before Yom Tov unless he did not have it beforehand.) However, in a place where the price of wood is expensive and there is loss and something perishable if he burns them as they are, it is a mitzvah to cut them into fine pieces.
And even to break them by hand, etc. For since they are fit for kindling without breaking, this too is unnecessary exertion.
There is one who forbids. And so several later authorities agreed.
