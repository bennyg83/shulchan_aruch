And even to break them by hand, etc. So the Tur wrote, that from the language of the Rosh it appears that it is forbidden; and Beis Yosef wrote that he learned it from the Rosh's writing "one should not split them," since one can cook and bake with them without splitting, so it is unnecessary exertion, and it is an av melacha, like that of one who shreds shreddings, who is liable on account of grinding, etc. And our master holds that even by hand one is liable on account of grinding. And Maharshal disagrees with this proof and said: on the contrary, since the Rosh hung the reason on its being an av melacha, it implies that by hand it is permitted, for there is no grinding except with a vessel; and even pepper, regarding which grinding applies, you will not find a prohibition by hand but only with a vessel. Therefore it appears that it is permitted according to all opinions; until here his words in chapter HaMevi. And it appears to me that the Tur learned it from the first reason that the Rosh wrote, that it is unnecessary exertion; in this there is reason to forbid even by hand. And this fits the language of the Rosh, for one may scrutinize why he needed to write that it is unnecessary exertion, which is rabbinic, and then conclude upon it "and it is an av melacha," for among two hundred there is a maneh; and if he meant to say in the manner of "not only this but also that," he should have said "and not only that, but it even involves an av melacha." Rather, it appears that his intent with these two reasons is that even in a place where one cannot say it is an av melacha, namely where one is not splitting into very fine pieces and it is unlike that of shredding shreddings (meaning fine wood to kindle the fire), which is an av melacha on account of grinding, and that is only with very fine wood in the manner of kindling a fire, which is not so here where one does not split into very fine pieces, for it is not the way to cook with such fine pieces, but only that they not be very thick, in the manner of placing on the fire after the fire has been kindled, to cook the pot, and there grinding does not apply; therefore he wrote that it is forbidden at least on account of exertion. And if so, he included in this two matters: if one splits into somewhat fine pieces there is unnecessary exertion, and if very fine there is an av melacha. And this is proper and reasonable to include in the Rosh's words; and if so one should forbid even by hand on account of the first reason. And thereby I am settled regarding what one may say from that of Rav Yehuda who strips bark into strips; and that is the law the Tur wrote in siman 519, that it is permitted to snap spice wood, and likewise on Shabbos in siman 322 that it is permitted to snap even with a knife straw to pick between one's teeth, for there it is for a need, which is not so here where we consider it unnecessary exertion. And according to this I am also settled regarding what the Tur wrote, that from Rashi it appears that it is permitted to break by hand; and Beis Yosef wrote that he did not know from where [the Tur] learned this from Rashi's words, and also Rashi weighed this and it did not appear to him from Rashi to forbid. And according to me it is fine: the main prohibition according to the Rosh is on account of exertion, and Rashi explained explicitly that according to the questioner the reason was exertion, but according to the conclusion we do not forbid on account of exertion but on account of muktzeh; and if so there is no prohibition by hand in something that is not muktzeh, namely what broke from erev Yom Tov. This appears to me in the Tur's words; and it is proper to be concerned in practice to forbid breaking even by hand in a place where it is forbidden with a knife. And Maharshal wrote: and the meticulous were accustomed to forbid even on Chol HaMoed, and they prepare all the small wood needed for cooking fish before Yom Tov, for they are concerned about exertion, since one can burn it thus without splitting, and one should not be lenient; but in a place where wood is expensive they were not concerned about exertion, for there is great loss; end quote. And it appears that to break by hand one should not be stringent at all on Chol HaMoed. He wrote the Ran: and they raise a difficulty here, why did they permit to split by means of a change, for these are preparations that one could do from erev Yom Tov; and Raavad answered that since one cannot cook without wood, they treated it like pounding salt, which they permitted by means of a change. And it appears to me that whatever one benefits from the body of the thing itself is like ochel nefesh itself, and it is unlike preparations from which one does not benefit, such as a knife or a spit, but only from what was prepared by their means; which is not so regarding wood, from which one benefits in themselves, for they make a fire and one warms oneself opposite them. And since splitting them to make a fire is permitted even though it was possible from yesterday, even to cook with them is permitted by way of "since" (mitoch). However, since people split for making vessels, they permitted only by means of a change; end quote. And Beis Yosef wrote that Mahari Abuhav raised a difficulty: for warming by a fire was permitted only by way of "since," that burning was permitted for the need of ochel nefesh; where then do we say "since burning was permitted for a fire, we permit splitting wood," and likewise permit them to cook, given that the fire was permitted for warming only by way of "since" cooking was permitted; end quote. And it appears to me that the Ran himself holds that in truth there is no permission for warming by way of "since," etc., but that it itself is ochel nefesh, as he wrote here. And that the Ran wrote, brought by Beis Yosef in siman 511, that these two matters, namely the benefits of warming by a fire and hot water for his feet, are by force of "since": he wrote thus even according to Raavad, who here does not hold that warming is like ochel nefesh itself; but according to the Ran's own reason here, one needs the reason of "since" only regarding hot water for the feet, not regarding warming opposite [the fire].
