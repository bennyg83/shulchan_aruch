2) There. Nor from a beam that broke on Yom Tov. Although it is presently intended for fuel, at twilight it was not intended for that purpose. Rashi, Beitzah 31b. Beis Yosef cites this and writes that Rashi means it is nolad. Levush writes likewise. Yam Shel Shlomo, chapter 4 of Beitzah, siman 7, also writes that even according to Rama in the gloss above, siman 495, se'if 4, that muktzeh is permitted on Yom Tov as well, he nevertheless agrees that a beam that broke on Yom Tov is forbidden because it is nolad, and nolad is forbidden, as Rama writes there. Olas Shabbos, subsection 1. Magen Avraham, subsection 2, likewise writes that according to those who forbid nolad on Yom Tov, as written in siman 495, it is obvious that this beam is forbidden because it is nolad. Shulchan Aruch HaRav, subsection 1; Chayei Adam, rule 101, subsection 8, write likewise.
