One may not bring wood, etc. These are the words of the Rambam; and in the Mishnah it is stated: R' Eliezer says, a person may take a twig from in front of him (according to the Rambam's explanation, that is, next to him) to pick between his teeth, and may gather from the courtyard and kindle, for everything in the courtyard is prepared; and the Sages say he gathers from in front of him and kindles. And in a beraisa it is stated: Our Rabbis taught: he gathers from the courtyard and kindles, for everything in the courtyard is prepared, provided he does not make piles upon piles; and R' Shimon permits. In what do they argue? The first Tanna holds that it appears as one who prepares for tomorrow, and R' Shimon holds that his pot proves about him. And the Rambam ruled like the Sages of the Mishnah; and here there is a stringency and a leniency. A stringency: that even in a courtyard there is no permission except from in front of him, that is, next to him, not like the Sages of the beraisa. And a leniency: that since the Sages of the Mishnah did not mention a courtyard explicitly, it is implied that even in a field it is permitted from in front of him if he kindles there. But the Tur implies that he rules like the first Tanna of the beraisa, and there is a distinction between a courtyard and a field: in a courtyard it is permitted from the whole courtyard, provided he does not make piles, but from the field in all ways it is forbidden. And the Tur does not distinguish in a courtyard between fine wood and coarse wood; even though one needs to distinguish thus so that the Sages of the Mishnah, who permitted only from in front of him (and that even in a courtyard), not contradict the Sages of the beraisa who hold everything in the courtyard [is prepared]. Rather, certainly one must distinguish: the Sages of the Mishnah deal with fine wood that are nullified to the ground, and in the beraisa they deal with coarse wood; and so the Ran distinguished. Nevertheless the Tur does not hold so; rather there is no distinction between fine and coarse according to the Sages of the beraisa, that everything in the courtyard is prepared, like R' Eliezer in the Mishnah; and the Sages of the beraisa disagree with the Sages of the Mishnah and do not hold that everything in the courtyard is prepared. And therefore the Tur ruled that it is forbidden to make piles, and that is both coarse and fine, like the Sages of the beraisa. And thereby Beis Yosef's wonder at the Tur is answered, that he did not bring the distinction of fine wood, and likewise that the Rambam and Rif did not bring the law that one should not make piles: for that applies when bringing from place to place, but when taking from in front of him in the place where he kindles, it does not apply at all; therefore they did not bring this, for according to them there is no permission except from in front of him.
