Gathered there from the evening. From this language it is implied that even if we prepared them from the evening it is forbidden; and the reason may be that it is weekday labor to go to the field to bring wood to his house. And some explain because it is confused with scattered wood, and scattered is forbidden because it appears as gathering for tomorrow's need and is like me'amer; and for this reason even in a private domain it is forbidden to collect them when they are scattered, as below.
In the field, from in front of him. That is, near the place of cooking his pot, for it is not weekday labor; and also according to the second reason above it is likewise permitted, for his pot proves about him that he is not gathering for tomorrow's need. And the view of Maharshal, the Bach, and other later authorities is to forbid gathering in the field even in this manner, for it appears like gathering in the place of its growth, since it is in the field, and one is liable on account of me'amer.
He takes from in front of him. That is to say: even in a courtyard he gathers only from in front of him for the need of cooking his pot now; but to gather from the whole courtyard to set aside is forbidden, and there is no distinction between small twigs and large ones.
A frame. For when it has no frame, even though it was gathered from the evening, it is like a field.
And it should be within the Shabbos techum. And it is implied that even near the city one also needs a frame; and many poskim disagree with this, and according to them, whatever is near the city within seventy amos and a bit more does not require a frame; and where we require a frame is from seventy amos onward until within the techum, which is two thousand amos; and so is the law (Acharonim).
One of all these. That is, it was not gathered, or it lacked a frame, or it was outside the techum.
Behold, they are muktzeh. According to the reasons we wrote above, that it is like weekday labor or because of concern for me'amer, this law is according to all, even according to the poskim who permit muktzeh on ordinary Yom Tov; in this the Sages made it muktzeh. And some explain this law simply: if one of these conditions is missing, presumably he diverted his mind from them so as not to use them on Yom Tov, and it becomes muktzeh; and according to this, for one who permits muktzeh on Yom Tov it is permitted also in this case. And for practical law it is proper to be stringent.
