One may not. On the Taz: in my humble opinion there is intent in it. For I found difficult what Beitza folio 31b poses, and as Rashi explained, that the questioner held "this, and it need not be said that," and the conclusion is "not this," and if so even muktzeh is forbidden, and the Mishnah is R' Yehuda, as inferred on folio 2b. And perhaps the Mishnah is like R' Shimon regarding Shabbos? And this is the meaning: one may not split either beams that broke from erev Yom Tov and one can kindle thus; that is obvious that it is forbidden, for preparations that one could do from erev Yom Tov, even if it broke on Yom Tov, are forbidden, since there is no need to split, for one can kindle thus; see siman 509 in Magen Avraham (2). And when he splits where one cannot cook thus, let him split with a hatchet, as written here in se'if 1 and se'if 2. And to this he said: for if so, it should have said one may not split either from beams that broke from erev Yom Tov or on Yom Tov; and since it taught "not from beams and not from a beam that broke on Yom Tov," it is implied that the opening clause is where it did not break, and for the reason of monetary loss; and what broke on Yom Tov is muktzeh like R' Yehuda, for presumably there is one reason that applies to both. And further, if so, why specifically on Yom Tov; even from erev Yom Tov, since the reason is that one can kindle thus? Which is not so according to the questioner, for whom it was "this and it need not be said that"; and presumably like R' Yehuda. And see the Yerushalmi, this version: "this you said on Yom Tov; but from erev Yom Tov it is permitted," which appears like that which you said regarding fragments of utensils; see there. And the commentators explain that one should not take the word "on Yom Tov" as a separate matter from whole beams or those broken from erev Yom Tov, that one may not kindle on Yom Tov; or as Rashi explained here, that the questioner held "this and it need not be said that"; therefore he explains that specifically where it broke on Yom Tov is it forbidden, but if it broke from erev Yom Tov it is permitted. And see the Gaon the commentator (David Dissauer), "did," that is to say; and according to what I wrote it is fine. And see Magen Avraham (2) and (12), and God willing it will be explained further there. See Rashi on Beitza folio 2; he added "not from the beams"; perhaps he intended the order of the Yerushalmi, "one may not split from the beam," and in Shabbos folio 157, "one may not split from the beam," and in Rashi, "the beams"; and understand. From an old beam, not "the beam," that is, a weak one; see Magen Avraham letter 2, see there well.
