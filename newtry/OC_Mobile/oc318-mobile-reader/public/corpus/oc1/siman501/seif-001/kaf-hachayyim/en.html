1) [Se'if 1] Wood may not be split from beams designated for construction. This applies even according to Rabbi Shimon, because the beams are muktzeh due to potential monetary loss, regarding which Rabbi Shimon agrees. Tur and Beis Yosef; Levush; Magen Avraham, subsection 1. Therefore, it is forbidden to move them even for use of the object itself or its place. Eshel Avraham, subsection 1. See above, siman 308, se'if 1, and our words there, with Heaven's help.
2) There. Nor from a beam that broke on Yom Tov. Although it is presently intended for fuel, at twilight it was not intended for that purpose. Rashi, Beitzah 31b. Beis Yosef cites this and writes that Rashi means it is nolad. Levush writes likewise. Yam Shel Shlomo, chapter 4 of Beitzah, siman 7, also writes that even according to Rama in the gloss above, siman 495, se'if 4, that muktzeh is permitted on Yom Tov as well, he nevertheless agrees that a beam that broke on Yom Tov is forbidden because it is nolad, and nolad is forbidden, as Rama writes there. Olas Shabbos, subsection 1. Magen Avraham, subsection 2, likewise writes that according to those who forbid nolad on Yom Tov, as written in siman 495, it is obvious that this beam is forbidden because it is nolad. Shulchan Aruch HaRav, subsection 1; Chayei Adam, rule 101, subsection 8, write likewise.
3) There. Even if it was unstable before Yom Tov, etc. This law depends upon the dispute between Rabbi Yehudah and Rabbi Shimon regarding muktzeh, as is evident from the Talmud. Maran follows his position above, siman 495, where he rules in accordance with Rif and Rambam that muktzeh is forbidden on Yom Tov. Even according to the alternative opinion mentioned there by Rama, that muktzeh is permitted even on Yom Tov, it is nevertheless forbidden here because it is nolad. Rama therefore added no gloss. Maamar Mordechai, subsection 1. See the following subsection.
4) There. Even if it was unstable before Yom Tov, etc. We do not say that since it was unstable, he had in mind to use it as fuel if it broke. Regarding anything that was not fit at twilight, we do not say that he had it in mind, and it is considered nolad. Levush. This is also the view of Maharshal in Yam Shel Shlomo there and of Bach. Olas Shabbos there. Magen Avraham there likewise writes that even if it was unstable, it is called nolad, because initially it was a utensil and now it is a broken utensil. See there. Shulchan Aruch HaRav there; Mishnah Berurah, subsection 2, write likewise.
5) There. But if it broke before Yom Tov, etc. It is no longer fit for construction. Pri Chadash in the name of Rashba; Maamar Mordechai, subsection 3; Chemed Moshe, subsection 1.
6) There. Large pieces may be split from it. Although this could have been done before Yom Tov, it is not a melachah but only a weekday activity. Small pieces, however, may not be split, because this could have been done before Yom Tov. The chapter Klal Gadol states in Shabbos 74b that one who cuts wood into small chips is liable for the melachah of grinding. Mordechai, chapter HaMeivi. Ran writes in the name of Raavad that since cooking and baking are impossible without wood, the Sages treated splitting it like crushing salt, which they permitted when performed in an unusual manner. Ran himself gives another resolution. See there. Beis Yosef, Taz, subsection 2, and Magen Avraham, subsection 3, cite this. See there. See also the following subsection.
7) There. He should split it neither with an axe, etc. Why are an axe and similar implements forbidden? It is so that he not act in the manner he does on a weekday, since he could have split the wood before Yom Tov. Why, then, was splitting not forbidden entirely? He may encounter a thick piece of wood that he cannot ignite and consequently refrain from cooking. The Sages therefore permitted splitting in an unusual manner. For this same reason, regarding all similar matters they permitted what they permitted and forbade what they forbade. Rambam, chapter 4 of Hilchos Yom Tov, law 10. Beis Yosef cites this. Levush, se'if 2.
