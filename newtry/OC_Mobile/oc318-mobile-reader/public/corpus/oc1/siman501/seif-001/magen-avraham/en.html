And close to breaking. This is according to R' Yehuda; but according to R' Shimon it is permitted when weak (Tur). And it appears to me that even according to what I wrote in siman 324, se'if 7, that a sound animal that died is permitted according to R' Shimon, that is because nevertheless he sits and waits, hoping that a deaf-mute, mentally incompetent person, or minor will slaughter it, as Tosafos wrote in Chullin (and see siman 318); but a sound beam that broke is forbidden according to all, for the anonymous Talmud on folio 30 raises a difficulty and does not say "this works for the one who holds," as it wrote on folio 26; rather, necessarily all agree here that it is forbidden. And for those who forbid nolad on Yom Tov, as written in siman 495, it is obvious that it is forbidden, for it is nolad; and likewise all broken utensils; see siman 308, se'if 6. And it appears to me that even if it was weak it is called nolad, for originally it was a utensil and now it is a broken utensil; and so Beis Yosef wrote in the name of the Yerushalmi: even a utensil that broke from erev Yom Tov and still has the form of a utensil, and then broke on Yom Tov, is forbidden. However, in the Yerushalmi of the Krakow printing it brought: if it still has the form of a utensil, even if it broke on Yom Tov it is permitted to kindle with it; and so Beis Yosef himself wrote at the end of this siman; and it is dealing where they still perform a function similar to their original use, such as fragments of a trough to pour porridge into them, or fragments of glass to pour oil into them, as stated in the Gemara. And it appears to me that regarding a utensil, even if it was whole yesterday and broke on Yom Tov, it is permitted according to R' Shimon, for a utensil is made to break, which is not so regarding a beam; and so it is implied in the Gemara on folio 32, where it poses a contradiction between the two beraisos against each other, and it does not say "here where it was sound from erev Yom Tov, here where it was weak," but rather necessarily regarding a utensil there is no distinction, as I wrote.
Large ones. And even though these are preparations that one could make from erev Yom Tov, it is permitted, for it is not a melacha but only weekday labor; but fine ones are forbidden, for one is liable on account of grinding, as we say in chapter 7 of Shabbos (Mordechai; and so it is implied in the Rosh; see siman 519). And the Ran wrote that it is like ochel nefesh itself, for one may warm oneself with them; and since splitting them is permitted for warming oneself with them, it is likewise permitted to split them to cook with them. However, since people commonly split them to make vessels from them, a change is needed; end quote. And Mahari Abuhav raised a difficulty: for a fire to warm oneself was permitted only by way of "since" (mitoch), as the Ran himself wrote, which implies that it is not ochel nefesh (Beis Yosef). And I do not know what difficulty he has, for the Ran wrote that it is like ochel nefesh, meaning that in truth a fire to warm oneself is not ochel nefesh, but it was permitted by way of "since"; and if so, since a fire is permitted, it is permitted to split wood for a fire, because one benefits from the wood itself, and it is unlike preparations such as a knife or a spit, from which one does not benefit in themselves but only from what is done by their means, as the Ran wrote explicitly. And since splitting was permitted for the need of warming, it is likewise permitted for the need of cooking, as it appears to me.
But only with a knife. And the meticulous are accustomed to forbid even on Chol HaMoed, and they prepare all the small wood, even for cooking fish, before Yom Tov, for one can burn it without splitting, as the Rosh wrote, and one should not be lenient; but in a place where wood is expensive they were not concerned about extra exertion, and it is permitted to split it, for there is great loss; end quote, Yam Shel Shlomo, siman 7.
