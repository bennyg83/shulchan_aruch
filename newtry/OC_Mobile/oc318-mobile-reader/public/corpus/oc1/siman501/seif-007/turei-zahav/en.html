And torches that remained, etc. Beis Yosef brought the words of the Rosh who permits this, for there is no preparation in this; and so the Mordechai wrote in the name of R' Yitzchak, that since the body of the wick and the wood of the fire already exist in the world and there is here only a repair, it is like baking and cooking. And in the name of Ba'al HaItur the Rosh wrote to forbid on account of preparation, for it is better to light when they were already lit and went out; therefore on Shabbos and Yom Tov and the two days of Rosh Hashanah it is forbidden to light on the night of Yom Tov what went out on the first, unless they were lit and went out on a weekday; and likewise the torches that went out on the first, unless he adds other wood upon them and nullifies them in the majority, provided he does not touch them, but he may turn them after they were nullified in the majority; end quote. And Beis Yosef raised a difficulty on this: according to his words he should have forbidden on Yom Tov of Rosh Hashanah leftover wick and torches that went out on that very day, whether you say the prohibition on the second day is because they have one sanctity, or whether you say it is because of preparation, just as an egg laid on that day is forbidden; and likewise he should have forbidden lighting on every Yom Tov the wicks that went out on that day; and this requires study; end quote. And I wondered greatly at a great man like him that he left this difficulty as requiring study, for in my humble opinion it has no beginning: for certainly the prohibition is on account of preparation, as Ba'al HaItur wrote, that Shabbos may not prepare for Yom Tov; likewise the two days of Rosh Hashanah, according to the one who holds they are two sanctities, one may not prepare from one sanctity for its fellow; but for that very day itself it is obvious that one prepares from Yom Tov for that same day itself, for this is not within the prohibition of preparation. And it is unlike an egg, which is forbidden on that day, for the reason stated in the first chapter of Beitza according to Rabbah, that an egg laid on Yom Tov is forbidden on account of preparation, namely on Yom Tov that falls after Shabbos, for we say that every egg laid today was finished from yesterday, so of its own accord Shabbos prepares for Yom Tov; and we raise there: if so, ordinary Yom Tov should be permitted; and it answers: a decree on account of Yom Tov after Shabbos, meaning if we permit what was laid on ordinary Yom Tov they will come to permit also what was laid on Yom Tov after Shabbos; and all this is regarding what was laid on Yom Tov. But here with torches and a wick, a decree does not apply, for in truth it is permitted even on Yom Tov after Shabbos if it went out today, for the preparation is today and not from yesterday; and we do not decree what was "born" on Yom Tov on account of what was "born" on the Shabbos before it. And something like this I wrote in siman 512 at the end of the siman regarding a chick. This appears simple and clear; and without examination Beis Yosef wrote that it requires study. And regarding practical law the Rama ruled leniently; and so the Mordechai that Beis Yosef brought wrote that the custom is that they light a measure of wick and oil that burned on Yom Tov of Rosh Hashanah to light on the second night. And in Terumas HaDeshen he shows reason to forbid lighting even on the other side that has not yet been lit, on account of a decree lest one light on the other side, as written regarding slaughter with a knife of which one side is notched; and Maharshal decided to forbid in this, and so my master and father-in-law of blessed memory; and Beis Yosef in siman 514 ruled to permit. And on the two days of diaspora Yom Tov it is permitted according to all to light on the second night what went out on the first day; only on Yom Tov after Shabbos and Rosh Hashanah, where the reason is not doubt, do the aforementioned poskim disagree. And certainly it is proper for each person to fulfill all views and make new wicks; and he may move them on the second night toward the old wick; and even though the Mordechai in the name of Ravyah forbids even the moving, in this we certainly rely on those who are lenient, as it appears to my humble opinion.
