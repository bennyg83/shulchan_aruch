One may not kindle with their shells. And even if they are fit for animal food, nevertheless they are nolad, for yesterday they stood for human use (so the Rosh wrote at the end of Beitza, not like the Bach); and see what I wrote in siman 494, se'if 4.
With the nuts. For they are not prepared for kindling at all (Beis Yosef). And this requires study: what differs from utensils that one kindles with, even though they are not designated for kindling, as Beis Yosef wrote in siman 502 in the name of the Ran, since the status of a utensil is upon them, all the more so foods? One must say that foods are different, for it is not the usual way at all to leave them on the fire. However, in the Yerushalmi it is stated that one kindles with food; and see what I wrote in siman 518.
Torches. For preparation does not apply, since the body [of the wick or wood] already existed in the world, and it is like baking and cooking, which are not called preparation (Mordechai). And it appears to me that even according to what I wrote in siman 527, that preparation applies regarding cooking, that means it is forbidden to cook from one Yom Tov for its fellow; but if one already cooked, it is not forbidden in benefit, for preparation is not called [forbidden preparation] except regarding an egg that was not in the world at all. And Yam Shel Shlomo ruled that torches are forbidden, and the same applies to wicks that went out; and he wrote in Terumas HaDeshen, siman 81, that if it was lit on Shabbos and went out, it is forbidden to move it on Yom Tov even for the need of its body or its place; even though a wick is a utensil, nevertheless it is considered as if he actively set it aside by hand from the Yom Tov that follows Shabbos, on account of preparation; until here. And it appears to me that this is not called actively setting aside by hand except on Shabbos, when it is forbidden to move a burning lamp; but on Yom Tov it is not called actively setting aside by hand; end quote, Yam Shel Shlomo. And so the Bach wrote. And regarding what Beis Yosef wrote, that if it is considered preparation, then even on that very day it should be forbidden to light it, like an egg that was laid: he answered in Darkei Moshe that since it is an inferior preparation, it is not forbidden on that very day; end quote. This implies that he holds it is not Torah-level preparation. And it appears to me that from the outset there is no difficulty, for according to the strict law even an egg laid on Yom Tov is permitted, except that we decree because sometimes Yom Tov falls on Sunday and they would also permit the egg and would not know to distinguish that it was finished from yesterday; but here there is no place to decree that if they come to light those that went out today they will also light those that went out yesterday on Shabbos, for people do not err between Yom Tov and Shabbos. For if you do not say so, regarding wood that fell on Yom Tov, why do we need the reason "lest one go up and detach"; we should have said the reason is a decree regarding Yom Tov after Shabbos (see siman 507, se'if 2)? Rather, necessarily it is as I wrote. And it is written in Terumas HaDeshen that on Yom Tov after Shabbos it is forbidden to light it even on the other side, for since it is Torah-level we decree lest one come to do so on the other side; but on the second day of Yom Tov that follows Shabbos, or on the second day of Rosh Hashanah, it is permitted to light it on the other side, and therefore it is permitted to move them; until here.
