That broke on Yom Tov: one may not kindle. For it is forbidden to move them; and even to burn them in their place is forbidden, for it is forbidden to benefit from muktzeh. But when one kindles a whole utensil, even though it becomes ruined at the end, the benefit comes afterward of its own accord. A non-Jew who carved a kav-measure into a log: it is nolad, and one may not kindle with it (Shabbos folio 29); and see what I wrote in siman 586, se'if 22.
Whole ones. The Ra'em in Sefer Yereim inferred from this that even absolute demolishing does not apply to utensils; but for us, who hold that there is absolute demolishing regarding utensils (as written in siman 314), one must say it is dealing with a loosely joined utensil (Turei Even); and this requires study, for even though it is permitted to remove a loosely joined utensil, nevertheless it is forbidden to break it completely. And further, a non-Jew who carved a kav into a log is not a loosely joined case; so why do we need the reason of nolad? And it appears to me that there is no difficulty from the outset, for kindling itself is an av melacha and was permitted for a need; the same applies to demolishing. And granted that it is forbidden to split a utensil for the need of ochel nefesh, that is because it is a melacha in its own right; and it is possible that it is permitted to split it just as they permitted splitting wood in se'if 1, for one who demolishes is not liable unless he does so in order to build; and if so, for a need it is entirely permitted, as it appears to me. And in any case one must say that he has no wood, for otherwise he transgresses "do not destroy," as stated in Shabbos folio 129. A stick with which one stirs the fire that broke: it is forbidden to kindle with it, for it is nolad (Yam Shel Shlomo, Gemara); and specifically where it was designated for that purpose.
