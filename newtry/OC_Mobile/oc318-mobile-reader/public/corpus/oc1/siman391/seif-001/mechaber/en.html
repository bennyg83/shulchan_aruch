The laws of relinquishing domain for those who forgot to make an eruv. It contains 2 seifim: All the laws of relinquishing domain that apply to one who forgot and did not join the eruv in his courtyard likewise apply in an alleyway. Thus, if one courtyard forgot and did not participate in the shituf, it may relinquish its domain to the other residents of the alleyway, or they to it, even though they are many, because the residents of a courtyard are considered, relative to the alleyway, like a single individual in a courtyard. And all the laws governing Jews in a courtyard together with a non-Jew likewise apply in an alleyway or in a city: carrying is not prohibited in an alleyway or in a walled city until there are 2 courtyards of Jewish houses in the city; but one courtyard does not suffice, even if many Jewish houses open into it. And this applies when the city is enclosed by a wall for residential purposes; ordinary towns are enclosed for residential purposes, and ordinary fortresses are not enclosed for residential purposes. And when there are 2 courtyards of Jewish houses in the city, one must rent from each and every courtyard of non-Jews, and it is not sufficient to rent from the city official. {Rama: And some say this is specifically regarding bringing objects into and out of the non-Jew's domain; but for carrying in the alleyway one may rent from the official, since the roadway of the alleyway belongs to the official and he can remove all the non-Jews from there. [Rivash, siman 710]} When does this apply? Regarding an official who does not own the houses and also has no right at all to use the houses of the city's residents, even in wartime. But in a place where all the city's needs are carried out only through the official or his appointee, renting from that official or from his agent certainly helps, because he has the right to place his men and weapons in the residents' houses in wartime without their consent.
