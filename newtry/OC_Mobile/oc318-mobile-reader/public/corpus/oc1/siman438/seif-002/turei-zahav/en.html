An olive's bulk of chametz on a beam, etc. In Tur he wrote: further it asks in the Gemara — bread on a beam whether a ladder is required to lower it, etc.; and if it is in a pit, etc.; and the law of these is like the first case: if he nullified it is a rabbinic doubt and one need not check; if he did not nullify it is a Torah doubt and one must check. But Raavad wrote these ask whether it is l'chatchila — whether he must remove or not — for certainly he must, otherwise it is like hiding in pits; rather it speaks where he checked and nullified, and after its prohibition he found — it asks whether he must remove or not; and his words do not appear correct, for there is no hiding here; and since he nullified it is not his; and that we say one should not hide in pits — is when he did not nullify; and so Rambam on that case of bread that one nullifies and that suffices, etc.; Raavad wrote he erred; and it appears to me he did not err — end Tur. And Beit Yosef inferred from the language "if he did not nullify he must check" — it implies nullification does not help that he nullify again, for once doubt preceded nullification it is a Torah doubt and even if he nullifies afterward — see there; and accordingly he challenged what Rambam wrote — behold he disagrees with Rambam too, for Rambam holds if he did not nullify he should nullify and that suffices — see there at length. And I say: Tur wrote its law where he did not know of the chametz until after its prohibition time; and this is the meaning: if he nullified before prohibition time — it is a rabbinic doubt and one need not check; if he did not nullify before prohibition time and now he cannot nullify since it is after prohibition time — automatically it is a Torah doubt and checking is required. In truth if it happened before prohibition time — even if he did not nullify he may still nullify and it is a rabbinic doubt — and this is what Rambam wrote; and Rambam speaks where he found before prohibition time — if so Tur and Rambam do not disagree, as Beit Yosef wrote there are three disputes in the matter; and one need not ask why Tur did not write the law before prohibition time — we learn from this that even if he did not nullify he may still nullify; nevertheless he distinguishes between nullified and not nullified — it would have been proper to distinguish between found before prohibition time and after prohibition time; and all where he did not nullify: if before prohibition time he may still nullify and it is a rabbinic doubt; if after prohibition time it is a Torah doubt. And one may say he speaks not as a question: not as a question where he has not yet nullified and it is before prohibition time that he will still nullify — in that nullification his intent will be per the explanation on this chametz; but even if he already nullified and one might think it does not help on this chametz since he did not know of this chametz at all in his nullification — it teaches that nevertheless it helps; and the essence of the dispute of Raavad and Tur: it appears they hold if before prohibition time, even if he nullified — he must check because it is like hiding in pits, since nullification touched hefker; and in this hefker does not apply because a person's hand does not control a pit and it is still in his domain; unlike if after prohibition time when he already nullified before — therefore retroactively the nullification applies since it is not in his domain; and Tur holds it is not like hiding except specifically if at the time he nullified his intent was that it not be nullified; unlike stam; and that we say one should not hide in pits — is when he did not nullify — meaning he explains his intent was not to nullify. And what Tur and Raavad wrote "these ask" — means pit and beam; for regarding a mouse one cannot say it is like hiding — see there as explained in my humble opinion.
