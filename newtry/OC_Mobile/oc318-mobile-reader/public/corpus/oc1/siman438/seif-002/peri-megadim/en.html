Yehudah's distinction on a mouse in meat one does not leave over but bread one leaves over — rather like Rava; and the same for a toddler — only where there are some crumbs there is presumption, etc.; and enough for the whole loaf was explained in sign 1; and "some say" specifically the whole loaf — meaning another entered immediately after with indicia to the matter as Tosafot; and this is for Rashbag; Av Beit Din; and as Tur in 439; but for Rambam there he ruled like Rabbi — see there; possibly not required immediately; and accordingly the Mechaber used Rava's language and includes them together; and whether partial or whole loaf — not required immediately; understand. And with nullification to permit — the same; and in Magen Avraham and Kaf HaChayim in 439 (8); and in Mishnah Berurah it implies somewhat that it is not so — and it will be explained in 439.

(ב) And see Beit Yosef there sign 1 on a toddler and he did not nullify — requires study; and Kaf HaChayim sign 6 brought proof from Tosafot: "because if he nullified Rashbag also agrees"; and wrote it is clear proof — see there; and one may say Tosafot explain the dispute as they explained the words "and nevertheless"; but for practical law, even without nullification for a toddler — per Rambam partially and per Tur for the whole loaf — as explained; and see Eshel Avraham 2; Peri Chadash agreed with view 1; and in truth in most places the halachah is like view 1; and we already wrote what may be explained in view 1.

(ג) See Derishah: he reads in the name of Maharil "loaf in its mouth" and in all our texts "in his hand"; and at first glance it appears he ate and found partial crumbs — all agree no bedikah is required; and "in his hand" then there is dispute; and if we saw him eating — possibly even without finding crumbs no bedikah is required, for presumably he ate it all.
