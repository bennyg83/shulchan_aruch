(א) And a loaf in its mouth, etc. — even if the loaf was small enough that a mouse could eat it, and even if he nullified chametz — nevertheless we do not rely leniently because of a rabbinic doubt, since once we know certainly the mouse dragged chametz before us, doubt does not remove from a presumption of definite prohibition.
(ב) And found crumbs — but if he found a whole loaf — no bedikah is required, for we rely that this is the loaf the mouse brought; specifically where he nullified; but without nullification, which is a Torah matter — we do not rely leniently and must still check the whole house unless they recognized this is what the mouse took.
(ג) Even enough for the whole loaf — and all the more if there were only a few crumbs — certainly we do not presume it ate and the crumbs fell while eating.
(ד) He must return and check, etc. — specifically where we saw a mouse enter with chametz; but if he found plain chametz in a house after his bedikah — he need not return and check the whole house because of this (Mekor Chayim).
(ה) The house — see Magen Avraham: "house" means a room; one need not check except that room we saw entered; but other rooms open to that room we rely leniently and need not check them if he nullified chametz or the loaf was small; and if we did not know at all which room it entered — see siman 439 seif 2 in the gloss and what is written there.
(ו) That it is not its way — and the same: a dog or chicken do not crumble.
(ז) And found crumbs — specifically in this manner where he found crumbs that support that they ate; but if he did not find crumbs — we do not presume they ate, for doubt does not remove definite chametz; and he must return and check that room where he saw the toddler entered; specifically a toddler without understanding; but if he has understanding he is believed to say he ate it all (Magen Avraham).
(ח) That we presume he ate it — even if he did not nullify.
(ט) For a toddler's way is to crumble — and per this view there is no difference whether there were only a few crumbs or enough for the whole loaf.
(י) That if there are not enough crumbs in the crumbs, etc. — for otherwise we are concerned the toddler left over.
(כ) He must return and check — and the main view is the first opinion; but if he did not nullify and the time already passed when he cannot nullify — one should be stringent to return and check. Mekor Chayim wrote: a gentile who entered with chametz into a checked house need not return and check the house — specifically for a mouse and toddler we are concerned their way is to hide; unlike a gentile whose way is not so — rather either to eat it or take it to his house.
