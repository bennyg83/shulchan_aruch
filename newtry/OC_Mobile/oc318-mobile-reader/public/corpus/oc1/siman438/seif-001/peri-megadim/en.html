(א) Mouse. Taz — per my common view there is great reflection here. For there are three views: (1) Tosafot and Tur on a toddler — specifically enough for the whole loaf; (2) Rambam chapter 2 law 8 per its plain sense — its way is to crumble at the time of eating; if so, enough for a loaf requires bedikah; (3) Mechaber omitted on a toddler and in all cases no bedikah is required. And what Rambam wrote "at the time of eating" — to say even not enough for a loaf we presume they ate, even though doubt does not remove certainty; nevertheless there are stronger indicia that its way is very much to crumble while eating; and enough for the whole loaf too — since another entered immediately after, as Tosafot wrote; and Av Beit Din; and in Rambam in any case he ruled like the first view of Rabbi: this that was lost is this that was found — as we need to say below — see there. And in Darkei Moshe he brought the third view; and here he was silent and did not emend because the Mechaber's view on a toddler is correct for the whole loaf — Rif and Rosh agree no bedikah is required; Rif states it plainly and Rosh — since he wrote on a mouse "even the measure of a piece of bread" — necessarily a toddler need not in such a case; and Rambam's words may be explained thus: plainly the halachah is not like view 3; and even without the whole loaf — the halachah is no bedikah is required; in Rambam it is clear; and in Rif plainly, otherwise he would have to explain until it would seem difficult on Tur why he ruled like Tosafot; and in Rosh plainly it implies even without the whole loaf no bedikah is required — except since he wrote "and even the measure of a piece of bread" — one might think it is an addition on Rif and Tosafot to argue; rather to rely on a toddler specifically when enough for the whole loaf; and therefore he brought "some say"; nevertheless the halachah is like view 1 in the plain ruling, per Hagahot Taz.

(ב) Behold — in Tur and Shulchan Aruch and Levush there is a change of wording: in Tur: "he must check the whole house," etc., and "must check after the loaf the mouse brought"; and Levush added the word "all": "and must check after all the loaf the mouse brought." And accordingly Tur's question: must he check the whole house even if he nullified? And "perhaps" for siman 439 — with nullification he ruled leniently that it is not its way to crumble — certainly from elsewhere, etc.; and we further inform: or must he check until he finds the loaf and recognizes the mouse brought it — no more is required even though these crumbs are from elsewhere we are not concerned to require the whole house, even without nullification; and "perhaps" for siman 439 where we saw a mouse enter with a loaf and found — as Rashbag: we saw enter with a loaf, etc.; and Levush added: possibly if he recognizes half a loaf that this is it — nevertheless we are concerned it hid half in a hole — see Pesachim 12. Indeed the Mechaber used different language; and Eshel Avraham there, etc.; and it implies the Mechaber's view: specifically after the loaf it brought; if not, even if he found a whole loaf and nullified — it is worse and requires the whole house since crumbs come from elsewhere.

(ג) Further — reflect on Rambam's language there chapter 2 law 89: "he left ten and found nine — he checks" (implies even if he nullified, for doubt does not remove certainty); and in Beit Yosef: likewise he saw a mouse, etc. — "it cannot be he already ate the loaf and these are the crumbs; rather we are concerned perhaps it left it in a hole and crumbs were there." And in Tur: he saw a toddler with a loaf in hand, another entered after and found crumbs — no bedikah; we presume he ate it and these crumbs fell at eating, for a toddler's way is to crumble while eating; and it is not a mouse's way to crumble — end. Behold he considered greater stringency when some crumbs are found — for here crumbs are evidence at eating; nevertheless bedikah is required; and all the more for enough for a loaf — for certainly a mouse does not crumble except while eating; and Taz cited the greater stringency of the whole loaf and nullification as siman 439. And "these crumbs certainly from elsewhere"; and the word "we are concerned perhaps it left in a hole" — it could be it also ate. Indeed since Beit Yosef did not write "it is not a mouse's way to crumble" except in law 9, and in Beit Yosef he did not mention "another entered after"; and in Beit Yosef he mentioned "he saw" — it appears specifically for a toddler without the whole loaf: we presume he ate and behold crumbs that fell at eating are good evidence at eating for the whole loaf; but another entered immediately after — no, for he does not hold like Tosafot "indicia to the matter," etc.; and in the first view he wrote this is worse since it is not its way except while eating. And in Beit Yosef: no bedikah is required since it is not its way — otherwise the doubt would be balanced and doubt would require bedikah, for we do not say "they ate" even if he nullified. And in Tur: without the whole loaf for a toddler — we presume matters appear so; for a mouse its way is not so. And Rif wrote: where it entered, etc. — apparently copied from Beit Yosef or She'altot; and why did he not copy two statements of Rava as his way, but to explain Rava's statement that a mouse — even if he did not find enough for a loaf — has no presumption; and from it regarding a toddler specifically where there is not the whole loaf; and he inverted Rava's words; and possibly the text should read thus: "toddler," etc. "mouse," etc. — for enough for the whole loaf it is plain a mouse certainly does not crumble — and what does it teach? And accordingly the Mechaber wrote: for a toddler we presume he ate and they fell at eating — we do not presume; meaning without the whole loaf; if the whole loaf — he did not mention because it is not clear to him; and he also omitted "at eating" because it is not clear; but Levush explained view 1: even if not enough for the whole loaf, at eating one may explain in two aspects. And Rashi Pesachim 10b: "and entered and we do not presume the loaf in it," etc. — this is not the loaf the mouse brought; one may say like Tur's greater stringency: even crumbs like the whole loaf — we do not presume this crumb-loaf in it regarding a mouse that brought this loaf; and these crumbs; and all the more without a loaf; and from it regarding a toddler specifically — the whole loaf; understand.

(ד) And see Bach at the beginning of siman 439; and here if he found a whole small loaf — one may say there is doubt whether this is it and whether it ate it; unlike crumbs which are certainly from elsewhere — only doubt of eating; doubt does not remove from certainty even if he nullified. And in Magen Avraham 439 sign 3: doubt whether this, etc. — one may distinguish between there and here; see there (1). And in Kaf HaChayim sign 3 he wrote Tur's words in 439 were overlooked — see there; and behold Beit Yosef — light of Israel — did not overlook, for below he speaks of a large loaf and brings proof from here "mouse enters and leaves" without nullification — that he checks even a small loaf; as Darkei Moshe wrote it is doubt whether this is also doubt whether this; and he left in doubt; and Av Beit Din 439: everything that was established — one may say nullification of doubt does not help — see there; and see Magen Avraham there — novelty of law that there is a difference between finding a whole loaf unrecognized or recognized, and crumbs — already explained above; and it will be explained further in (2).
