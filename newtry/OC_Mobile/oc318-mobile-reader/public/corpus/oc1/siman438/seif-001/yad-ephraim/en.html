(Magen Avraham s.k. 1 siman 438) meaning, etc. — requires explanation; and behold Bach wrote: Rambam wrote: a mouse entered and afterward crumbs are found — we do not say, etc.; therefore he returns and checks; if he found nothing — this one checks the whole house, etc.; and it appears to me what Rambam wrote "this one checks the whole house" — our Rabbi also speaks of a mouse that entered a house and we did not see to which place in the house the mouse entered; but when we saw it entered to a place among the places in the house — one need not check except that place alone; and we are not concerned the weasel or mouse dragged from place to place, for many times on other days of the year they leave ten and find nine, etc.; nevertheless on the night of the 14th one need not check a place where they do not put, etc.; the same when we did not see to which place in the house the loaf was brought — except that some room doors are locked — one need not check those rooms, for certainly it did not enter there; and we are not concerned it entered through a hole from room to room — for then there is no end, and it is unreasonable to check all rooms and attics in a large courtyard and hall; but those rooms that are open must be checked even if chametz is not used there — there is doubt to be concerned the weasel dragged there since we do not know to which place the loaf was brought, etc.; and it is simple that in this law there is no difference between nullified and not nullified: for a mouse one must check even if he nullified; and for a toddler one need not check even if he did not nullify, etc.; end. And so siman 434 on Riaz's words: I say one need not check every place except when we did not see to which place it was taken — specifically in rooms whose doors are not locked; but when we saw to which place it was taken we are not concerned perhaps a weasel dragged from place to place; and likewise in those rooms that are locked one need not check there; and we are not concerned perhaps through a hole it was taken from room to room, etc.; and what Rambam and our Rabbi wrote on a mouse that entered a checked house must check the whole house — meaning also specifically in such a case where we did not see the place it entered and their doors are not locked, etc.; end. And behold what he wrote "and so our Rabbi" — even though Tur did not mention in siman 438 that one must check the whole house — it appears his intent is on what Tur wrote siman 439: he saw the loaf brought to the house and another entered after and checked the whole house, etc.; therefore Bach wrote it speaks where it entered the house and we did not see to which place in the house the mouse brought the loaf — and "house" is an inclusive term for many rooms, as we call all rooms inclusively "house" in German. Therefore if there were several rooms there and we know it entered but do not know to which place the Rav brought — meaning which room among the rooms — then he must check all rooms, specifically those open; but locked ones need no bedikah since there is no doubt that when it disappeared from our sight it entered there — only we are concerned it entered the open ones and from there through a hole to locked ones — for this we are not concerned; but if we saw which room in the house it entered — one need not check the whole house, i.e. all rooms, but checking that room alone suffices, for we are not concerned for dragging through a hole; and also through openings — it is said it went out from this place we saw it enter and came through the opening to the second room — on such they said we are not concerned perhaps it dragged, etc. We find we learn from Bach's words several things: (1) he explains Rambam and Tur "checks whole house" — all rooms there; and it speaks where we did not see which room it entered — and all unknown require bedikah if not locked; (2) if we saw which room it entered, then other rooms that have no opening between them — we are not concerned it dragged through a hole — in every case no bedikah is required even with a large loaf and without nullification; (3) if they are open to each other it appears he holds we are concerned — specifically through a hole we are not concerned, unlike through an open opening which is considered one domain; and Magen Avraham's view is not so — rather he holds "house" means room; he explains what Rambam and Tur wrote "checks whole house" — that room where it entered; on the contrary it speaks where known it entered that room and he says one must check all of it; also regarding other rooms he disagrees with Bach in two matters: (1) if the loaf is large and he did not nullify — one may not be lenient; also what for Bach in rooms open to each other the language is "concerned" and for Magen Avraham even here "not concerned" — it is like a courtyard close to a house (and possibly Magen Avraham agrees to Bach where rooms are not open to each other we are not concerned perhaps dragged from place to place; and what Magen Avraham wrote "but other rooms," etc. — means those rooms open to the room we saw entered — meaning the Gemara's question; this truly resembles courtyard and house; and from Bach it implies in such a case all require bedikah — specifically dragging through a hole we are not concerned, unlike through openings — from his words it appears even with nullification it does not help; on this Magen Avraham disagrees; and though Magen Avraham did not mention Bach's implication — his way is to abbreviate). And from what is explained for Bach: it speaks where we did not see where it entered and requires bedikah for all open rooms in every manner — therefore Magen Avraham said: this is the case of two houses and we do not know which it entered — it is explained there the distinctions whether they belong to one person or two who came to ask in Beit Din, etc. — as explained in my humble opinion in Magen Avraham's words. A short way to explain Magen Avraham: behold Bach wrote locked rooms need no bedikah and we are not concerned it went room to room through a hole; and accordingly what Bach wrote that open ones require bedikah — whether open to outside and we do not know which it entered, only that it is open from there to another room — specifically through a hole we are not concerned; and what he wrote in Bach's name "and if we saw," etc. — means they are not open to each other, only open outward; and we saw which it entered — the other room need not bedikah, for we are not concerned it exited through its entrance and came to another room. And behold Bach was forced to explain what Rambam and Tur wrote "whole house" — all rooms there — for he could not explain "house" means room and that he checks the whole room we saw entered — for in truth other rooms open thereto also require bedikah; and therefore he says "checks whole house" — all open rooms; and certainly it is strained to explain it speaks where all rooms open to this room; therefore Bach preferred to explain it speaks where we did not see where it entered — then he must check all since unknown which it entered; and if so in every case where they are open one must check all; and Rambam did not need to explain it speaks of open ones — a simple matter that locked ones have no concern of entry. And on this Magen Avraham said: in my humble opinion "house" means room, etc. — meaning always what Rambam and Tur wrote "checks whole house" means the whole room we saw it entered; nevertheless there is no difficulty, for sometimes other rooms also require checking since open thereto — this is the Gemara's question, etc.; and if the loaf is not large or he nullified — no need to check; and here Rambam wanted to teach that in every case that room requires bedikah; and regarding other rooms he does not speak, for sometimes no bedikah is required; and since it speaks where we saw it entered — if we did not see we are in two houses and we do not know, etc.; unlike Bach who established "we saw" and always requires bedikah in all rooms — but where we saw it speaks and refers to that room — as explained in my humble opinion.
