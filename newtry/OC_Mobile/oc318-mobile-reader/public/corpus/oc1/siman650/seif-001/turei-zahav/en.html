The measure of the hadas, etc. The dispute among the opinions in this matter is as follows. It is stated in the Gemara: the measure of a hadas and aravah is three tefachim. Rabbi Tarfon says: with an amah of five tefachim. Shmuel said: the law is like Rabbi Tarfon. We explain that statement of Rabbi Tarfon as follows: take before you an amah of five tefachim, divide them into six, take from them three for the hadas, and the rest for the lulav. How much is this? Two and a half. Rashi explained: take for yourself an amah of five average tefachim and divide it into six parts of small tefachim, and with them the measure is three for the hadas and aravah and four for the lulav. Rabbi Tarfon is being lenient, and this is what Rabbi Tarfon says to the first Tanna: those three tefachim by which we measure, and ordinary three tefachim are half an amah, should be small tefachim, and should be only half an amah within an amah of five tefachim. We challenge this: if so, these three tefachim are only two and a half average tefachim, for each small tefach is one sixth less than an average tefach; but did Shmuel not say three average tefachim? The answer is that Shmuel was not precise, in the stringent direction, and gave a larger measure. There are opinions about this regarding the fourth tefach of the lulav. The first opinion holds that this tefach too is a small tefach, one sixth less than an average tefach. Each tefach is four thumbs, so the two and a half tefachim of the hadas are ten thumbs, and the fourth tefach, which is also small, contains three thumbs and one third of a thumb. Its sign is 13 thirds, meaning thirteen thirds. The middle opinion holds that specifically the three tefachim of the hadas are small, namely ten thumbs, but the fourth tefach of the lulav is large, like the ordinary average tefach, namely four thumbs. It follows that the total is fourteen thumbs. The third opinion holds that the law is not like Shmuel, who ruled like Rabbi Tarfon, but we maintain like the first Tanna, who holds that all four tefachim are specifically average. It follows that all together there are sixteen thumbs. Even Shoev wrote that one should bind the aravah on the left, the hadas on the right, and the lulav in the middle, and there is a secret in this. It is written in Tolaas Yaakov that there is one who takes the spine of the lulav toward his face and the face of the lulav outward, in the manner of "you shall see My back," etc.
