[1] And so is the practice, etc. The Bach rules this way as a matter of law. The Shelah wrote: Even Shoev wrote that the face of the lulav should be toward him and the spine outward, but in Tolaas Yaakov he wrote the opposite, and the secret is "you shall see My back, but My face shall not be seen." Since the author of Tolaas Yaakov was a great master of the wisdom of Kabbalah, it is proper to practice like him, end quote. This is how the world practices. But in Eliyahu Zuta I wrote that the primary view is like Even Shoev, for I saw in the Darkei Moshe and Mateh Moshe that they agreed with him. So too wrote Abudraham, Rabbeinu Saadiah Gaon, and Shiltei Gibborim in the name of Sefer Eshkol. The Maharik and the Shelah did not see the agreement of the Geonim mentioned above. One should also add what the Beis Yosef wrote at the end of siman 645, that its face is the side of the spine and its back is the other side. If so, even according to the secret of "you shall see My back," etc., the spine must be outward. However, I found in Shibbolei HaLeket, siman 118, this language: and in the bundle, the spine of the lulav must be opposite the heart. Mateh Moshe and the later authorities wrote that one should bind the aravah on the left, the hadas on the right, and the lulav in the middle. What is written in the writings of the Arizal, to bind three hadasim and aravos, I have not seen anyone practice this way.
