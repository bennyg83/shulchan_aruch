[1] Any unavoidable circumstance, etc. For example, he has a wound on his arm, or he must set out on a journey and cannot delay long enough to put on both tefillin, or he has no unavoidable circumstance but inadvertently or deliberately put on only one of them. In all these cases, he fulfills the mitzvah of the tefillin that he put on. Levush implies that even on the right arm he should not put it on, since it cannot be placed on the left arm, and this is worse than the case of a left-handed person discussed in siman 27, seif 6. However, I found in the Mechilta, parashas Bo: Abba Yose says, we find that the right hand is called “hand,” as it says, “Israel stretched out his right hand.” Why then does the Torah say “upon your hand”? To include one whose left arm is amputated, who places it upon his right arm. This clearly shows that when it is impossible to place it on the left arm, he places it on the right. Nevertheless, it appears that this follows the view of Acherim in Menachos 36a, who expound “your hand” to include one whose arm is amputated. The halachah, however, follows the first Tanna, who interprets “your hand” as the left hand. Magen Avraham likewise writes this regarding Sifrei, parashas Vaeschanan. Responsa Beis Yaakov discusses this at length and disputes Magen Avraham. He brings the phrase, “and he has none of them,” but this is irrelevant, because here the verse specifically says “your hand,” as he himself writes. Yet according to Tosafos there, who maintain that the Tannaim do not disagree, this answer is unavailable. The Mechilta itself also requires explanation. What is the connection between its statement that the right hand is called “hand” and its exposition of “your hand”? On the contrary, if “hand” ordinarily means right, the verse “your hand” should be needed to indicate the left. It appears to me that the left hand is actually derived from another exposition, as stated in the Gemara there. The Mechilta asks why the Torah nevertheless uses the general term “hand,” which ordinarily implies the right hand. It answers that this includes one whose left arm is amputated, who places it on the right arm. This is why the Torah uses the general word “hand.” In our Gemara, however, the inclusion of one whose arm is amputated is derived from the spelling “your weakened hand,” rather than the ordinary form “your hand,” indicating even where there is an interruption. According to that interpretation, he places it upon the remaining portion of the left arm. Examine this carefully. This appears to be the practical halachah. Responsa Rama, siman 122, was asked whether one who lacks a left arm should put tefillin on the right. He discusses the matter at length and concludes, based upon conceptual arguments, that he should not put them on at all. With respect, all the sources cited above escaped his attention. Responsa Beis Yaakov, siman 149, writes at length that Beit Yosef, siman 651, explicitly rules in the name of Baal HaIttur that one fulfills the mitzvah with the right hand. This is not precise, because that passage concerns a left-handed person. His citation of responsa Maharam, and his question upon Rama in siman 651, are likewise incorrect to anyone who examines responsa Maharam of Rothenburg, siman 12, and responsa Rashba, siman 1120. There is no need to elaborate.
