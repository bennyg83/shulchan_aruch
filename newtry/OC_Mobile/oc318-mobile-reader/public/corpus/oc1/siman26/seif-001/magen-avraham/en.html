Any unavoidable circumstance. For example, he must set out on a journey and cannot delay long enough to put on the tefillin of the arm. He should put on only the tefillin of the head, and we do not concern ourselves with the statement that so long as the tefillin are between your eyes, there should be two upon him. Rosh. This is difficult. From where did Rosh derive this? Perhaps the Mishnah concerns specifically a complete unavoidable circumstance, such as a wound on his arm. Mere delay of his journey should not permit him to neglect the mitzvah of the tefillin of the arm. One may answer that he will nevertheless put them on afterward. The novelty is only that although he cannot presently put on the tefillin of the arm, he should still put on the tefillin of the head alone. See what I wrote in siman 38, seif 9.
