(Seif katan 1) Any unavoidable circumstance, etc. Magen Avraham asks: from where did Rosh derive this? Rosh writes this concerning the Mishnah in Menachos: the tefillin of the arm do not prevent fulfillment of the tefillin of the head, and the tefillin of the head do not prevent fulfillment of the tefillin of the arm. Rabbi Chisda initially thought that this applied only where one possesses both, but if he has only one, fulfillment with that one is prevented. The Sages would have enacted such a decree lest, if the remaining tefillin did not prevent fulfillment, he become negligent and fail to purchase the missing tefillin. Rabbi Chisda later retracted and concluded that whether he possesses both or only one, they do not prevent fulfillment of one another, and we do not decree lest he become negligent. Rosh found the case in which he possesses both difficult. Why would he actually put on only one? Are we dealing with wicked people? He therefore answers that the case involves an unavoidable circumstance, such as where he must set out on a journey. The concern that “so long as they are between your eyes, there should be two,” discussed below in siman 28, seif 2, is not taken into account in such a case. Magen Avraham asks: from where did Rosh know that such a relatively minor unavoidable circumstance permits him to wear only the tefillin of the head? Perhaps the case of one who possesses both refers specifically to a complete unavoidable circumstance, such as a wound upon his arm. Magen Avraham therefore answers that Rosh did not mean that because of this minor circumstance he may entirely neglect the mitzvah of the tefillin of the arm. Rather, he will put them on later. The novelty is that although he cannot presently put on the tefillin of the arm, he should nevertheless put on the tefillin of the head alone.
And through a minor unavoidable circumstance such as this we permit it. One should not ask from the statement, “so long as they are between your eyes, there should be two.” Although one could still ask how Rosh knew that this rule is disregarded because of a minor unavoidable circumstance, one must say that Rosh understood this from his own reasoning, since this prohibition is not especially severe. Nimukei Yosef maintains that if one encounters the tefillin of the head first, he should put on the tefillin of the head first so as not to pass over a mitzvah, even though he thereby violates the rule that so long as they are between your eyes there should be two. This necessarily shows that the latter rule is relatively minor. Although we do not rule in accordance with Nimukei Yosef, as explained above in seif 6, it nevertheless proves that the rule is not severe.
See what Magen Avraham writes in siman 38, seif 9. Meaning, one should not say that where he must set out on a journey, Rosh exempts him from putting on the tefillin of the arm merely because he is preoccupied, just as a bridegroom is exempt because he is preoccupied with a mitzvah. Magen Avraham therefore directs us to his comments there in seif 9, where he explains that preoccupation of this kind does not exempt him, because he must compose his mind. See Magen Avraham there, seif katan 12.
