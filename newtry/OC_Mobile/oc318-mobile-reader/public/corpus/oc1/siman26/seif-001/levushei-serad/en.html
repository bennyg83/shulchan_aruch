Magen Avraham, seif katan 1. “At present, the tefillin of the arm.” He carefully writes that the person cannot presently put on the tefillin of the arm. Certainly, mere concern for delaying his journey would not permit him to neglect a Torah requirement, namely that so long as the tefillin are between your eyes, there should be two tefillin upon him. Rather, the case is where setting out on the journey will cause him distress if he puts on the tefillin of the arm, such as because of the cold or a similar circumstance. We rule that one who is distressed is exempt from tefillin. Accordingly, the principle that so long as the tefillin are between your eyes there should be two does not apply, because at that time he is entirely exempt from the tefillin of the arm and obligated only in the tefillin of the head. This is why Magen Avraham concludes, “See what I wrote in siman 38,” where the law that one who is distressed is exempt from tefillin is explained. This appears to be his intent.
