1) Se'if 1: If he has only one of the tefillin, he puts on the one he has, etc. The same principle applies if he has both and is under no compulsion, etc. If he put on only one, although both were available and he had no compelling excuse, he fulfilled the particular mitzvah he performed. Nevertheless, it is certainly forbidden to put on the head-tefillin before the arm-tefillin, and all the more so to neglect either one without compulsion. These are Maran's words in Beit Yosef. From this, the distinguished and pious R' Yaakov Molcho, one of the elder sages of earlier generations in our holy city Jerusalem, proved in manuscript responsum 85 that a person who mistakenly put on the head-tefillin before the arm-tefillin need not remove it, since he already fulfilled its mitzvah. Birkei Yosef, section 1, cites him. Taz, siman 682, se'if katan 4, writes likewise, rejecting Abudarham's statement: 'Since the teaching says they are indispensable, it implies that if he put them on in this order he did not fulfill the obligation, for they must be put on in the order of their mitzvah.' Taz questions him and writes that the Gemara he cites appears nowhere in the Talmud; see there. This wording of Abudarham appears in Hilchos Berachos, first gate, 119a, in the name of Rabbeinu Gershom. Its source is Yerushalmi, Maaser Sheni 5:5: 'One Tanna teaches: All the mitzvos in the Torah prevent the declaration; another Tanna teaches: All the mitzvos in this passage prevent it. R' Acha bar Pappa asked R' Zeira: Even if he put the head-tefillin before the arm-tefillin? He answered: I too think so.' Penei Moshe explains: One Tanna holds that every mitzvah in the Torah prevents the declaration, because one who transgressed any mitzvah cannot say, 'I have acted according to every commandment You commanded me, etc.' The other Tanna holds that only the mitzvos stated in this passage prevent it. R' Acha asked R' Zeira, according to the view that all Torah mitzvos prevent it, whether this includes putting the head-tefillin before the arm-tefillin. The Torah does not explicitly say to put on the arm-tefillin first; this is derived from 'and totafos between your eyes'; whenever the tefillin are between your eyes, there shall be two. Does this too count among the mitzvos stated in the Torah? R' Zeira answered that according to that opinion, this too prevents the declaration. Abudarham's wording cited by Taz is thus not the Gemara's own wording, but his inference from it. Under Penei Moshe's explanation, however, the passage does not address whether one fulfills the mitzvah of tefillin. It means that because he reversed the order and initially put on the head-tefillin first, failing at that moment to fulfill 'whenever they are between your eyes, there shall be two,' he cannot say, 'I have acted according to every commandment You commanded me.' He nevertheless fulfills the mitzvah of putting on tefillin; he merely failed to observe the derived rule at the beginning. Even if he then removes the head-tefillin, puts on the arm-tefillin, and replaces the head-tefillin, he still cannot make the declaration, because this is another performance and does not correct his earlier failure. Taz therefore writes that no such ruling is found in the Talmud and questions Abudarham. See Erech HaShulchan, siman 684, section 3. Eliyah Rabbah, siman 684, section 2, likewise writes that once one has put on the head-tefillin, he need not remove it but should immediately put on the arm-tefillin. Peri Megadim cites this there in Mishbetzos Zahav, section 4. Responsa She'eilas Yaavetz, siman 125, agrees, unlike Halachos Ketanos, part 1, siman 53. Birkei Yosef cites this in siman 25, Shiyurei Berachah, section 3, and rules likewise in Kesher Gudal, siman 3:3. See Erech HaShulchan, siman 25:6 and siman 684:3; Shalmei Tzibbur 37b; Zecher LeAvraham, part 1, under the letter tav; Beit Oved Siddur, laws of putting on tefillin, section 25; and Shaarei Teshuvah, siman 25, section 13. Shaarei Teshuvah writes that if he put on the head-tefillin because he had no arm-tefillin and later obtained one, even Halachos Ketanos agrees that he need not remove the head-tefillin before putting on the arm-tefillin; see there. See also Pesach HaDevir, section 2. Arizal writes in Shaar HaKavanos, discourse 5 on tefillin, that the arm-tefillin precedes the head-tefillin, and gives a kabbalistic reason. This applies specifically before putting them on, when one must follow the order of arm and then head. If he already erred and reversed the order, putting on the head-tefillin first, he need not remove it so as to repeat the order. Once the order was reversed, that moment has passed; what is gained by subsequently arranging them correctly after the reversal already occurred? Moreover, Arizal writes there that whenever holiness and spirituality depart, a small residual trace nevertheless remains there permanently and never departs. Here too, even if he removes the head-tefillin, its residual spiritual trace remains. Why should he remove it and suspend the mitzvah during the time needed to put on the arm-tefillin? This apparently explains why Chida rules that the head-tefillin need not be removed despite Arizal's teaching: No correction is achieved through removal, as explained. If, however, he has not yet tightened the head-tefillin or positioned it properly when he remembers, he must remove it, put on the arm-tefillin first, and then the head-tefillin. Since the mitzvah was not yet completed, he should perform it in the proper order. See what we wrote above, siman 25, sections 41 and 90.
