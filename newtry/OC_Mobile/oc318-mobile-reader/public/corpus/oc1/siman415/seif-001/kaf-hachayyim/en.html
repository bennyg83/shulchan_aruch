1) [Se'if 1] One may establish an eruv techumin only for a mitzvah purpose, etc. This applies specifically when establishing the eruv with bread. By personally establishing residence at the location, however, one may establish an eruv even for an optional purpose. Rabbeinu Yehonasan on the Rif; Beis Yosef. This is also the opinion of Magen Avraham, subsection 12, and in siman 444, subsection 13. Chayei Adam, rule 77, subsection 12, likewise rules this way. Even HaOzer, however, writes that Tosafos in the chapter Elu Ovrin, Pesachim 49, Rosh, and Ran there clearly maintain that even when personally establishing residence, one may not do so for an optional purpose. He writes that this also appears from the Yerushalmi and that the Shulchan Aruch therefore omitted the distinction. See there. Eliyah Rabbah, subsection 1, likewise writes that this is implied by Rabbeinu Yerucham and Maharil, and therefore rules that one should be lenient only in a case of great need. See there.
2) There. Of a wedding. The same applies to a betrothal celebration. Avodas HaKodesh; Rabbeinu Yerucham; Eliyah Rabbah, subsection 2; Tosefes Shabbos, subsection 3; Eshel Avraham, subsection 1. Gra, however, writes that only going to perform the betrothal itself is considered a mitzvah purpose, as stated below in siman 444, se'if 7. See there. Nevertheless, one may rule leniently according to the first opinion because many authorities hold it.
3) There. Of a wedding. A wedding between the daughter of a kohen and an am ha'aretz, or between the daughter of a Torah scholar and an am ha'aretz, is not considered a mitzvah celebration, as stated in Pesachim, chapter 3. Magen Avraham, subsection 1; Tosefes Shabbos, subsection 2. See Even HaEzer, siman 2, se'if 8.
4) If songs are recited at the wedding of a Torah scholar's daughter to an am ha'aretz, it is considered a mitzvah meal. Mordechai, chapter 4 of Pesachim; Magen Avraham, siman 670, subsection 4; Tosefes Shabbos in this siman, subsection 2. Machatzis HaShekel's attempt to reject this because the person could recite songs at home is not compelling. Alternatively, the celebration there also includes gladdening the groom. Examine this carefully. See Responsa Chavos Yair, siman 70, who writes that nowadays we do not have the category of am ha'aretz described by our Sages, just as we do not have the am ha'aretz concerning whom six characteristics were stated in Pesachim, chapter 3. See there, where he discusses at length the definition of a mitzvah meal.
