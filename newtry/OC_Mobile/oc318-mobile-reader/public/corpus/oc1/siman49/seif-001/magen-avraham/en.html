In everyone's mouth. If a passage is not fluent in everyone's mouth, it is forbidden to recite it by heart even if it is fluent in one's own mouth. The permission to write tefillin by heart because the passages are well reviewed means that they are fluent in the mouths of all scribes. Darkei Moshe and Or Zarua. Bach rules like Tur that one should not recite Scripture by heart to discharge others. This is also implied by Taanis 28a. For this reason, the custom is that the congregation recites the verses “Who is like You” and “Hashem shall reign” themselves. In earlier times the chazzan prayed by heart and could not discharge them in these verses. There are differing views on the matter; see Beit Yosef. It therefore appears to me that one should be careful not to recite anything by heart except what is mentioned here in Shulchan Aruch. Tosafos in Bava Kama states that a blind person may recite by heart because, “It is a time to act for Hashem; they have violated Your Torah.” Regarding reading the weekly portion by heart while the chazzan reads, I personally am careful not to do so, but I do not protest against others. Radbaz, part 1, siman 135. See there: according to one explanation, the prohibition applies specifically to reciting the weekly portion twice and its translation by heart. Knesses HaGedolah.
