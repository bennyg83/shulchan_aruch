Siman 61 "although" etc. that things in writing… NB: Here in the law of things forbidden to say by heart there are many views—some distinguish whether one reviewed them or not, and some distinguish whether an individual reads to fulfill the obligation of the many or not—see there. And I in my humble opinion found explicit proof from the Gemara that it is forbidden only to fulfill the many's obligation, and this is explicit Gemara, and thus it says in Menachot: "an individual reads it by heart"—R' Yossi said: and can an individual read Torah by heart in public? Rather they all enter and read it by heart as one reads Shema after praying—and behold there those parashiyot in Bereishit were not reviewed, and nevertheless R' Yossi did not challenge except "and can an individual read Torah by heart in public?"—implying that only in public is it difficult for him, but by himself it would be permitted to read, and it is proven explicitly to this view that only to fulfill the many's obligation is it forbidden, but between himself and himself it is permitted. And also from what the Gemara answers "rather they all enter and read it by heart as one reads Shema" it is proven explicitly to the view of the Mordechai that the Darkei Moshe brought in this siman—that if the whole congregation reads by heart there is no concern, for behold this is the essence of his answer: "and can an individual read by heart in public?"—and necessarily his challenge is only because of "things in writing you may not say by heart," and on this it answers that the whole congregation reads thus by heart—proven explicitly to Mordechai's view, yes; and I wonder that I did not see anyone who noticed this Gemara—and thus is my humble opinion, examine.
