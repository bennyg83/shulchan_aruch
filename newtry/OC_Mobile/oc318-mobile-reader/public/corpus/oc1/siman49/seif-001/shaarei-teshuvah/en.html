All of it. See Baer Heitev and Chavos Yair there. Since Tehillim arouses mercy, it is like prayer, and one may rely on Tosafos Yom Tov's statement in Berachos. Verses of Ma'amados, however, may not be recited by heart even when fluent in one's mouth; see there. Shevus Yaakov writes that according to Chavos Yair, who permits Tehillim by heart, Pesukei D'Zimrah, which people recite constantly, is certainly permitted. One may also say that since the Sages established it as an obligation, it is like prayer. Birkei Yosef cites Knesset HaGedolah that this restriction does not apply to Targum; on the contrary, in their time they were particular not to read Targum from writing, were it not for the principle 'It is a time to act for Hashem.' See the end of Megillah, in the chapter HaKorei Omed; Tosafos at the beginning of Bava Kamma; and several other places, as well as Responsa R' Shlomo ben Rashbatz, siman 277. Machazik Berachah adds a source from Tanchuma, Parashas Vayeira. See Responsa Maharanach, part 3, siman 72. R' Chaim Vital writes: My master, of blessed memory, always read the prayer from the siddur and not by heart. He recited every Scriptural passage with the cantillation of that passage, and Mishnah in the melody of Mishnah. Even during the seated portions of prayer, such as the zemiros and the like, he did not raise his voice, thereby displaying submission before Hashem. On Shabbos alone he raised his voice slightly more, with awe, in honor of Shabbos.
