Ba'er Hetev and see there in Chavat Yair that Tehillim, since it is to arouse mercy, is like prayer, and one may rely on what the Kaf HaChayyim wrote in Berachot; but verses of maamad it is forbidden to say them by heart even if they are fluent in his mouth, see there. And in Shulchan Tziyyon he wrote that according to what Chavat Yair permits saying Tehillim by heart, all the more so verses of pesukei dezimra that one is accustomed to frequently, see there; and also one may say that since they established them as obligatory it is like prayer—and see in Beit Yosef in the name of Knesset HaGedolah that in translation this does not apply, and on the contrary in their days they were particular not to read translation from a written text if not for "a time to act"; and see at the end of Megillah chapter "one who reads stands," and in Tosafot beginning of Bava Kamma, and in many places, and in responsum of Rashba son of Rashbatz siman 277, and in Mishna Berurah he brought more from Midrash Tanchuma parashat Vayera; and see in responsum of Maharanch siman 72 volume 3; and Maharachav wrote: my teacher, may he rest in Eden, always recited the prayer from within the siddur and not by heart, and he also said every matter of Scripture with the cantillation of that parashah, and likewise Mishnah with the melody of Mishnah, and even the order of prayer that is settled such as zemirot and the like he did not raise his voice and show submission before Him, may He be blessed, except on Shabbat he would raise his voice a little more in awe because of the honor of Shabbat.
