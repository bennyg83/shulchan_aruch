(s.k. 1) In the mouth etc.—for what we say regarding tefillin—the meaning is that this is the proof of the poskim that if fluent in his mouth it is permitted, from what we say that a scribe may write not from the scroll, even though from the law's perspective he should write specifically from the scroll before him—and the reason is because he reviewed them and they are fluent in the scribe's mouth, and as above siman 32 seif 29, and the same here. If so, even if not fluent in everyone's mouth, if it is fluent in his mouth it suffices—for also the parashiyot of tefillin are not fluent in any person's mouth, only in scribes' mouths.

That all scribes etc.—in tefillin we deal with writing. If so, we require that all writers and scribes have them fluent in their mouths, and this suffices, for we do not care about other people—for other people do not write; but here we deal with speech, and all Israel are among those who speak—therefore it requires that they be fluent in the mouth of all who speak.

To fulfill others etc.—even if fluent etc. In Taanit page 28: we taught there, the men of the maamad would fast four days of the week and pray each day the Musaf prayer and read in the Torah each day from Bereishit in Shacharit and Musaf as we read from a Torah scroll publicly during the week, but at Minchah because of the weakness of the fast they were lenient on them not to bring a Torah scroll but an individual would read that parashah by heart. R' Yossi said: and can an individual read Torah by heart in public? Rather they all enter and read them by heart as they read Shema—and Rambam ruled in chapter 6 halakhah 4 of Laws of Temple Vessels like R' Yossi—behold that for R' Yossi the individual does not fulfill the congregation by his reading by heart, and therefore those parashiyot were fluent in their mouths; for otherwise how could R' Yossi say "rather they all enter and read them by heart" etc., and how were they permitted if not that they were fluent, and nevertheless the individual cannot fulfill the congregation.

The verse "Who is like You" and "Hashem will reign forever"—these are the Tur's words, for in the Tur's time the shaliach tzibbur still fulfilled the many's obligation and the congregation would hear from the shaliach tzibbur and would not say; but these two verses are in Shacharit in Truth and Firmness, and in Maariv in Truth and Faith—the congregation would say for the aforementioned reason. And in our time when everyone prays the whole prayer, this ancient custom is practiced: when the congregation reaches these two verses they wait one for another until the shaliach tzibbur also reaches there, and the congregation answers the verses together in a loud voice as the ancient custom—except the Bach challenged why the shaliach tzibbur would say the verse "He who makes the great lights" completely and from the verse "the Name, our soul lives" and from the verse "for Hashem redeemed Jacob" etc., as Tosafot wrote that one need not be particular except regarding biblical verses in the Chumash and not regarding what is in Nevi'im and Ketuvim—except that in Sefer Avodat Yisrael he challenged that we do not hold thus like Tosafot, see there. Only what is mentioned here in Shulchan Aruch. And in responsum Chavat Yair siman 175 he permitted reading all of Tehillim by heart and relied on Tosafot of the Rashba that anything obligatory one may say by heart, and since saying Tehillim is to arouse divine mercy it is like prayer and an obligatory matter has this, and also since they are said every day and fluent in the mouth of the majority of the majority as a whole (and if so it depends on local custom whether they say Tehillim every day—also one may doubt this); but verses of maamad it is forbidden to say by heart even for one fluent in his mouth, see there.
