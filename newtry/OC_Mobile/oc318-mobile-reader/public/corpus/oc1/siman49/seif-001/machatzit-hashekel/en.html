(Seif katan 1) In the mouth, etc. This is based upon what we say regarding tefillin. The poskim prove that if a passage is fluent in one's mouth it may be written without consulting a text, from the rule that a scribe may write tefillin without copying from a written text. Strictly speaking, he should have written only from a text before him, but the passages are well reviewed and fluent in the scribe's mouth, as explained above in siman 32, seif 29. The same reasoning should apply here. Even if the passage is not fluent in everyone's mouth, it ought to suffice that it is fluent in this person's mouth. The passages of tefillin are also not fluent in every person's mouth, but only in the mouths of scribes.
That all scribes, etc. In the case of tefillin, the discussion concerns writing. It is therefore sufficient that the passages are fluent in the mouths of all writers and scribes, since other people do not write them. Here, however, the discussion concerns recitation, and all Jews are included among those who recite. Therefore, the passage must be fluent in the mouths of all who recite it.
To discharge others, etc. Even when the passages are fluent, etc. In Taanis 28a, the Mishnah states that the men of the maamad would fast four days each week. Every day they prayed an additional prayer and read the account of Creation from the Torah at Shacharis and at the additional prayer, like our twice-weekly Torah reading. At Minchah, however, because of the weakness caused by the fast, they were lenient and did not bring a Sefer Torah. Instead, an individual would read that passage by heart. Rabbi Yose said: Can an individual read words of Torah by heart for the congregation? Rather, they all enter and read it by heart, as they read Krias Shema. Rambam, chapter 6, halachah 4, of Hilchos Klei HaMikdash, rules like Rabbi Yose. This proves that according to Rabbi Yose, an individual cannot discharge the congregation by reading Scripture by heart. Those passages were certainly fluent in their mouths. Otherwise, how could Rabbi Yose say that they should all enter and read them by heart? Evidently, the passages were fluent, yet an individual still could not discharge the congregation.
The verses “Who is like You” and “Hashem shall reign” are Tur's own words. In Tur's time the chazzan still discharged the congregation in prayer, and the congregation listened rather than reciting the entire prayer themselves. They would nevertheless recite these two verses themselves: “Who is like You” in Emes VeYatziv at Shacharis, and “Hashem shall reign” in Emes VeEmunah at Maariv, for the reason explained above. Nowadays, when everyone recites the entire prayer, the earlier custom remains. When the congregation reaches these two verses, they wait for one another until the chazzan also reaches them, and the congregation responds together aloud, in accordance with the earlier practice. Bach asks from other verses that the chazzan would recite, such as “Who makes great lights,” “Who keeps our soul alive,” and “For Hashem has redeemed Yaakov.” He answers like Tosafos: one must be particular only concerning verses written in the Chumash, not those in the Prophets and Writings. Eliyah Rabbah challenges this because we do not rule like Tosafos on this point. See there. Magen Avraham therefore concludes that one should recite by heart only the passages explicitly mentioned here in Shulchan Aruch. Responsa Chavos Yair, siman 175, permits reciting all of Tehillim by heart. He relies upon Rabbeinu Yonah's explanation that anything obligatory may be recited by heart. Since Tehillim is recited to awaken Hashem's mercy, it has the status of prayer and an obligatory recitation. He adds that because these chapters are recited daily and are fluent in the mouths of most people, the majority is treated like the whole. This may therefore depend upon local custom, whether Tehillim is recited every day, and even this reasoning can be questioned. The passages of the maamados, however, may not be recited by heart even by one in whose mouth they are fluent. See there. See Tosafos, Bava Kama 3b, s.v. “as translated,” etc.
