(1) Matters in writing—a blind person may read by heart because of "a time to act for the Omnipresent, they have voided Your Torah." And the same if he is in prison and cannot obtain a Chumash there.

(2) You may not—Radbaz volume 1 siman 135 wrote: to read the parashah by heart when the shaliach tzibbur reads—I am careful but I do not object to others, because there are many poskim who hold that the prohibition is specifically if he fulfills others' obligation thereby, and so wrote the Eitan Tzvi and Biur HaGra.

(3) To say them by heart—one who preaches publicly with many verses from the Torah and it is difficult for him to search every hour in the Chumash because of the honor of the congregation, it is possible that one may be lenient. See Yoma (page 70) in old Tosafot s.v. "and on Yom Kippur" in the first answer and in the last answer, and in Biur HaGra he agreed to the law of the last answer.

(4) And fluent—but if it is not fluent in everyone's mouth, even though fluent in his mouth, it is forbidden.

(5) Such as Shema—and likewise the verses of "Who is like You" and "the Omnipresent will reign."

(6) And parashat HaTamid—Magen Avraham wrote: one who examines Beit Yosef will see that there are differing opinions regarding the reason for permitting saying these matters by heart; therefore one should be careful not to say anything by heart except what is mentioned here in Shulchan Aruch. And in responsum Chavat Yair he permits saying all of Tehillim by heart, since it is to arouse divine mercy it is like prayer, and it seems one may rely on him—for otherwise the view of the Eitan Tzvi and the Gra is like the poskim that only to fulfill the many's obligation is it forbidden.

(7) It is permitted—and when fulfilling others by his recitation of the scriptures, Magen Avraham leans to be strict not to say by heart in any manner, and so is implied from the words of the Gra in his commentary.
