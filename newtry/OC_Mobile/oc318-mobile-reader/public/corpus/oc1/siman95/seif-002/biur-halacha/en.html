He must bow, etc.; his eyes downward toward the ground - See Mishnah Berurah, which states that anyone who does not close his eyes, etc. This is from the Knesses HaGedolah cited in Baer Heitev. See Maamar Mordechai, who writes that under the law of the Gemara there is no prohibition against keeping one's eyes open, provided that they are directed downward toward the ground, as is proved from siman 90, seif 23. However, according to what the later authorities write in the name of the Zohar, that one must close his eyes during prayer, it would appear that the holy Zohar disagrees in this matter with our Gemara; see there. Chayei Adam, klal 22, din 11, implies that even according to the Zohar the primary prohibition applies specifically when, with open eyes, one looks at other things. If his eyes are directed downward toward the ground and he does not intend to look within four cubits, it is permitted according to all opinions. Indeed, Chayei Adam there uses the expression that he brings the Angel of Death upon himself prematurely, which is taken from the Zohar. Thus, there is no dispute at all. Nevertheless, ideally it is proper to be stringent and close one's eyes, so that he will not look at other things.
