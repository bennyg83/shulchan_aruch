He must bow his head, etc., his eyes downward to the earth — see in Mishna Berurah that anyone who does not close his eyes, etc., is from She'eirei Knesset HaGedolah brought in Be'er Heitev; and see in Sefer Maamar Mordechai who wrote that per the Talmud's ruling there is no prohibition with his eyes open, only that his eyes should be downward toward the earth, and proof from above siman 90 s"k 23. However per what the later authorities wrote in the name of the Zohar that one must close his eyes during prayer, it appears the holy Zohar disagrees with our Talmud — see there. And in Chayei Adam general rule 22 law 11 it is implied that the main prohibition even per the Zohar is specifically when he looks with his open eyes then at other things; but if his eyes are downward toward the earth and he does not intend to look at something else, in the view of all it is permitted [for Chayei Adam there wrote before it from Magen Avraham and it is from the Zohar] and they do not disagree at all. Nevertheless lechatchilah it is proper to be stringent and close his eyes so that he not look at something else.
