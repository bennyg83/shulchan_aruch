He must bow his head. And he must also think of the exaltedness of God, may He be blessed, and the lowliness of man, and he should say in his heart: What am I — poor and despised — coming to request from the King, King of kings, the Holy One, blessed be He. And at the time of prayer he should cast his eyes downward; but before praying the eighteen blessings he must cast his eyes to the windows in that wall (Bach, and thus implied in the commentary of Tosafos that the Beit Yosef wrote in the name of Rabbeinu Yehuda ibn Attar). And in prayer while seated he should not lean backward nor tilt to the sides because this is a way of arrogance; nor should he extend his legs nor place one foot upon the other — for all of this is a way of arrogance. Rather he sits with his head bowed so that he does not see the face of one sitting opposite him except within his four cubits; and his two elbows on the hip bone where they join the body, to here and to here; his hands under his garment — the right on the left — as a hint to increase the attribute of mercy over the attribute of strict justice. And he should sway his entire body back and forth to fulfill what is said "All my bones shall say," etc. But during the eighteen blessings it is forbidden to sway one's body (Maharam Alshich siman 113).
