[4] His eyes downward toward the ground, etc. Tzeror HaMor writes that when one prays the Amidah, the Shechinah is opposite him, and therefore it is forbidden to open his eyes and look at the wall. Shiyarei Knesses HaGedolah writes: I heard in the name of the Zohar that anyone who does not close his eyes during the Amidah will not merit seeing the face of the Shechinah when his soul departs, and from then onward my practice has been to close my eyes. Mateh Moshe and Shelah, page 255, likewise warn concerning this. This requires some consideration in light of siman 90, seif 23, which rules that one should not pray opposite illustrated garments; see there. I saw in Piskei Tosafos that pious and accomplished people would raise their eyes upward when mentioning Hashem's Name in prayer, but during the rest of the prayer they would not look toward the wall even momentarily. This resolves why one may not pray opposite illustrated garments, for when mentioning Hashem's Name he might look at them. The Levush's words, however, do not imply this, for were it so he could have resolved the question why windows are required by referring to the mention of Hashem's Name. Rather, it appears that one looks upward only at the beginning of prayer, as is also implied by the Bach. Accordingly, one may also say that because of the beginning of prayer one should not pray opposite illustrated garments. See above, siman 90, seif 4. I explained this further in Divrei Eliyahu on Tehillim 123. The Arizal would pray from a siddur so that he could concentrate intensely. Magen Avraham, siman 53, writes that everything depends upon what a person senses in his state of holiness. Maharil held a siddur in his right hand and prayed from it, while with his left hand he held his cloak over his heart.
