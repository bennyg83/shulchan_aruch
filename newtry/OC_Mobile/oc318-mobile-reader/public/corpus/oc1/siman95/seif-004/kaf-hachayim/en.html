(4) There — one should position one's feet next to each other, etc. See what Knesset HaGedolah wrote in Hagahot Shulchan Aruch in the name of the midrash that a person is obligated to sway himself during prayer, as it says "all my bones shall say," etc. And in the name of R' Moshe ben Machir z"l, siman 113, he wrote that it is forbidden to sway one's body during the Eighteen Blessings; and he z"l wrote to distinguish that the words of the midrash apply only to other prayers, not to the Eighteen Blessings, and he wrote that thus it appears to distinguish from the responsa of R' Moshe ben Machir z"l — see there. And likewise Olat Tamid, note 2, and Pri Megadim at the end of this siman. See Peri Megadim and what Nishmat Adam wrote on it, note 1, and see there in Nishmat Adam that he elaborated on this and concluded that one who sways during the Eighteen Blessings — they do not rebuke him, for he has something to rely on; only that every person should refrain himself from swaying during prayer — see there. And see our words above, siman 48, note 7, that thus wrote in the name of the author, and in note 9 we proved from the words of the Zohar that one should not conduct oneself casually except when occupying oneself with Torah, but not during prayer — see there.
