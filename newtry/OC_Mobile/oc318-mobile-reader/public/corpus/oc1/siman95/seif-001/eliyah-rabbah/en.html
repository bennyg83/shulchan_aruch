[1] Aligned as though they were only one, etc. This excludes the opinion in the Yerushalmi that it suffices to place the heel beside the big toe. It teaches that this is insufficient, and actual alignment, one foot beside the other, is required. Nevertheless, if one already prayed with the heel beside the big toe, the Bach and Olas Tamid write that he need not pray again. If he prayed without aligning his feet at all, however, they maintain that he must pray again. In my humble opinion, Rambam, chapter 5 of Hilchos Tefillah, implies that aligning the feet is only an ideal requirement, and one need not pray again even if he did not align them at all. Nevertheless, it appears that he may voluntarily pray again without introducing a new element, as stated above in siman 94, seif katan 9. Rabbeinu Yonah writes that some have the practice of leaving space at the top near the toes, because the verse states, "The sole of their feet was like the sole of a calf's foot." Olas Tamid writes that although Rabbeinu Yonah concludes that this is unnecessary, since our Gemara makes no mention of it, one who follows this practice acts with piety. In my humble opinion, however, Rabbeinu Yonah's words, "This is nothing, for the Gemara mentioned only that they should appear as one," imply that those who leave space at the top act improperly, since they must specifically appear as one. This is also implied by Abudraham, page 35.
[2] To resemble the angels, etc. Furthermore, when one stands in this manner he does not stand forcefully, which demonstrates that he stands in awe and submission. Mahari Abuhav writes that it alludes to the removal of his ability to move, either to flee or to attain any desire apart from Hashem. Rabbeinu Bachya on Parshas Korach concurs, writing that one thereby shows as though his feet are bound, that his desire does not depend upon himself, and that he cannot accomplish his will unless the Master consents. Since movement of the feet is more significant than movement of the hands for drawing benefit near and repelling harm, our practice is to align the feet, but not the hands. See Be'er Sheva, page 11.
[3] Some say that when he stands, etc. Nachalas Tzvi writes: I do not know why the Levush omitted this. It is possible that the Levush maintains that this applies when one is standing in his place at home, but if he has already left his place in order to pray, and certainly if he walks to the synagogue, it is unnecessary. It also appears that one need not step backward in order subsequently to walk forward, such as when he is standing near the wall. Rokeach explains the reason from the three instances of approaching that are written regarding prayer: "Avraham approached," "Yehudah approached," and "he approached him." It is also written, "and their feet were a straight foot": "their feet" indicates two, "foot" indicates one, and afterward "straight," meaning that he straightens his feet. The letters of "straight" form "song," and its numerical value equals "prayer."
