(1) One should position one's feet, etc. — and after the fact, even if one did not position one's feet at all, one has fulfilled one's obligation.

(2) To resemble the angels — since one speaks with the Divine Presence, one must remove all bodily thoughts from one's heart and resemble as if one is an angel; and even if one is sitting in a carriage, nevertheless one should position one's feet [Peri Megadim]. They also wrote that one should not lean on the back, and not be inclined to the sides, and not stretch out one's legs, and not cross them one over the other, because all of this is a way of arrogance; rather one sits with one's head bowed [Ateret Tzvi].

(3) He should walk before him — and one need not turn backward in order to walk forward; thus wrote Eliyah Rabbah. But the custom of the world is to walk backward. Darkei Moshe wrote that Maharil would habituate standing in the morning for the Eighteen Blessings when the prayer leader began Tehillot, and in Minhah when the prayer leader descended before the ark, and in Arvit when the prayer leader began Kaddish; and he would also remove at that time phlegm and nodding and anything that disturbs one's concentration [later authorities].
