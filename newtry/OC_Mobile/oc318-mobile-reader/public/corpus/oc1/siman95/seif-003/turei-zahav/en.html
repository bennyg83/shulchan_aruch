The right over the left. This alludes to subduing the yetzer hara, which is represented by the left side. Beit Yosef. The Gemara's statement that Rav Ashi would fold his hands during a time of communal distress is unrelated to placing the right hand over the left. Rambam wrote this placement based on his own reasoning. Were it connected to Rav Ashi's practice, Rambam should have distinguished here between a time of communal distress and a time of communal peace. Rather, folding one's hands is a different act associated with distress, Heaven protect us, like a person who clasps or breaks his fingers. The placement mentioned here is merely a posture of submission, and one should therefore do it at all times. Beit Yosef did not recognize this distinction, and therefore wrote that one must fold his hands even during a time of peace. His proof was that the Gemara did not state that during a time of peace Rav Ashi did not fold his hands, but only dressed and covered himself. This is no proof, for when there was no change the Gemara had no need to mention that he did not act differently. It mentioned only what he changed: during a time of wrath he folded his hands, and during a time of peace he changed his clothing in honor of prayer. Heaven forbid that one should fold his hands during a time of peace, for by doing so he brings judgment upon himself, Heaven forbid, as is known.
