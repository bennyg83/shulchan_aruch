Right over left — a hint to subjugate the yetzer hara which is hinted at on the left side (Beit Yosef). And what is in the Gemara that Rav Ashi in a time of distress in the world would break his hands — is not relevant here regarding placing right over left; and it is logical in one's soul — thus the Rambam, for otherwise here he would have had to distinguish between distress in the world and peace in the world; rather breaking his hands is another matter from distress in Bava Metzia, as a person who breaks his fingers. But placing that he mentioned here is a way of subjugation in the world; therefore one should always do so. And in Beit Yosef he did not descend to this; therefore he wrote what he wrote here — namely that one must break one's hands even in a time of peace; and his proof is from that which it did not say at a time of peace that he did not break his hand but wore clothing — and this is not a proof at all, for whatever there was no change there was no need to mention that there was no change; he only mentioned what changed, such as at a time of anger he broke his hands, and at a time of peace he changed his garments for honor. Heaven forbid to break one's hands at a time of peace, for one brings judgment upon oneself thereby, God forbid, as is known.
