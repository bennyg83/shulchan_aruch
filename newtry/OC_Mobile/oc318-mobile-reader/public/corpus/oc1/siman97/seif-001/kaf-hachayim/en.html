(1) [Seif 1] He shall not yawn and shall not gape, etc. — and if he does so willingly, behold this is arrogance; and if he needs to do them from unavoidable compulsion, he shall place his hand on his mouth so that its opening is not seen — Tur, R' Yehuda Abuhav, and this is from Gemara Berachos folio 24b. Rashi there on amud alef explains: yawning (gihuk) in Old French ritayer — sometimes a person brings from his body to his mouth a puff of air from his satiety and its smell is like the smell of the food he ate; gaping (pihuk) in Old French anayer — he opens wide from his jaws to expel the breath of the mouth, full, like a person who wishes to sleep or who has risen from sleep. Some expound by notarikon: gihuk — "he straightened his body" (go hikim); pihuk — "his mouth straightened" (piv hikim), end of discussion. Beit Yosef brought this and wrote: behold, per the first explanation gihuk in Old French is rigoldar, pihuk is bostizar; per the second explanation gihuk in Old French is dish pirizar, and pihuk as in the first explanation, end of discussion. See Perishah siman 1, Taz, Kaf HaChayyim, Eliyah Rabbah, Rabbeinu Zalman, Kitzur Shulchan Aruch siman 18.
