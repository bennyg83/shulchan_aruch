The second explanation of “gihuk” is that one stretches and extends his body and arms. See also the Kesef Mishneh, Hilchos Tefillah 4:11, who explains that “pihuk” means emitting a sound like gluttons, and Rashi before us explains “gihuk” in this manner. What the Shulchan Aruch writes, that if one yawns involuntarily, applies equally to belching: if it is impossible to restrain it, it is permitted, so that he will not be distracted during his prayer. However, he need not place his hand over his mouth except when yawning, since he opens his mouth widely. See Eliyah Rabbah and Berachos 24a, where Rabbi Yehudah HaNasi belched and yawned. See there. According to the second explanation there, it is well understood that one who yawns places his hand upon his chin. According to the first explanation, stretching one’s body may be forbidden. See Rambam, Hilchos Tefillah 4:11, that one should examine himself beforehand. See there.
