1) [Seif 1] One should not belch or yawn, etc. If one does so intentionally, he is among the arrogant. If he must do so because he cannot prevent it, he should place his hand over his mouth so that its opening will not be seen. Tur and Beis Yosef. This is from the Gemara in Berachos 24b. Rashi explains there according to one interpretation: "Belching" is called reter in the vernacular; at times a person expels from his body into his mouth air caused by satiation, and its odor resembles the food he ate. "Yawning" is called bailler in the vernacular; the palate of his jaws opens to expel a mouthful of air, like a person who wishes to sleep or who has risen from sleep. Some interpret the Hebrew terms as compounds: the word for belching means "he raised his body" (estrelier in the vernacular) while the word for yawning means "he raised his mouth." End of Rashi's words. Beis Yosef cites them and writes that according to the first explanation, belching is regoldar in the vernacular and yawning is bostezar. According to the second explanation, belching is desperezar in the vernacular, while yawning is as in the first explanation. End quote. See Perishah, paragraph 1; Taz, seif katan 8; Eliyah Rabbah, paragraph 1; Shulchan Aruch HaRav, paragraph 1; Kitzur Shulchan Aruch, siman 18, paragraph 9.
2) There: If he must yawn because he cannot prevent it, etc. Tur writes, "If he must do them," etc., as stated in the preceding paragraph. This implies that whether he must belch or yawn, he must place his hand over his mouth. The wording of Shulchan Aruch, however ("If he must yawn," etc.) implies that one must place his hand there specifically when yawning, because he opens his mouth widely, but not when belching. Eliyah Rabbah, paragraph 1; Maamar Mordechai, paragraph 1; Nehar Shalom, paragraph 1; and Mishbetzos Zahav, paragraph 1, write accordingly. Levush, however, writes, "If he must do them," etc., following Tur. So too Solet Belulah in Mekor Chaim, paragraph 1. This also appears from the words of Chida in Kesher Gudal, siman 12, paragraph 27; Siddur Beis Yaakov, paragraph 43; and Kitzur Shulchan Aruch there. It appears that they explain that Shulchan Aruch's word "yawn" is not precise, and that the same rule applies to belching; Shulchan Aruch mentions only one of the two. This follows from the fact that Beis Yosef quotes Tur's wording in Beis Yosef and writes that this too is stated in the Gemara, without commenting upon it at all, implying that he agrees. Moreover, see Beis Yosef there, who adduces proof for Tur from the Yerushalmi in the chapter Mi Shemeiso. Rabbi Chanina said: "I saw Rebbi yawn and sneeze, and he placed his hands over his mouth." This implies that he placed his hands over his mouth both when yawning and when sneezing. See also Rosh, chapter Mi Shemeiso, siman 39, whose version of the Gemara does not read, "When he yawned, he would place," etc., but merely, "and he would place," etc. Maharsha there likewise writes that this implies that he would place his hand over his mouth for all of them; belching, yawning, and sneezing. One should therefore be stringent.
3) There: He should place his hand over his mouth, etc. Even if no person can see him, he should place his hand over his mouth out of respect for Hashem. Tiferes Shmuel on Rosh there, paragraph 39.
4) There, in the gloss: He should take care not to place his hand upon his chin, etc. This means the lower jaw. Beis Yosef and Bach. See what we wrote above in siman 95, paragraph 15. While standing in prayer before the King of kings, the Holy One, blessed is He, one must avoid anything that appears arrogant. As for the cantors in these countries who, when raising their voices in song, place their hands upon the jaw, there is no concern, because they do so not out of pride or arrogance but in order to project and raise their voices in song. Outside the time of singing, however, they should certainly be stopped from doing so. If they would make their voices pleasant and sweet without blaring, they would improve matters in two respects: first, they would have no need at all to place a hand upon the chin; second, they would not make themselves unsightly by opening their mouths and moving their heads. Such conduct is unseemly, as we find regarding Rebbi's yawn according to Rashi's explanation and the Yerushalmi. We have already heard that in other countries the cantors sing only with a pleasant voice, without blaring and shouting. Lechem Chamudos on Rosh there, paragraph 122; cited by Magen Avraham, seif katan 8; Ateres Zekeinim, paragraph 1; Eliyah Rabbah, siman 95, paragraph 6; and Solet Belulah there, paragraph 2. See there the words of rebuke he writes concerning this.
