(5) "And if it is impossible," etc. — thus wrote Beit Yosef in the name of Rabbeinu Yonah; but from the Tur and other decisors it implies that behind him and to his left are equivalent, and likewise in Kol Bo. Rokeach siman 324 brought from the Midrash on Vayechulu — this is its wording: "I saw the warrior give before You; he stands in prayer and saliva reaches him; he spits to the sides and acts with awe and fear, and the Holy One blessed be He humbles his enemies" — until here its wording. It implies that ab initio he spits to the sides. The Midrash mentioned above is puzzling; I explained it in my commentary on the Torah. Magen Avraham wrote that in supplications after prayer the law is as during prayer, for as long as he has not stepped away.
