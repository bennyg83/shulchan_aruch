(4) In the gloss: "and he shall beware not to place his hand on his chin," etc. — that is, the lower cheek (Beit Yosef, Bach); and see our words above siman 95. And everything that appears to involve arrogance one must beware of when standing in prayer before the King, the Holy One blessed be He. As for what cantors in those countries do when they raise their voices while singing with music — they place their hands on the cheek — there is nothing wrong with this, since they are not doing so for display or arrogance, but only to carry the tune and raise the voice in song; but when not at the time of music it is certainly proper to protest to them. And also if they sweeten and temper their voice so they do not shout, they improve in two ways: first, that they will not need at all to place the hand on the chin; second, that they will not disgrace themselves by opening their mouths and moving the head — and this is disgraceful, as we find regarding Rabbi in his gaping, per Rashi and Yerushalmi. I have already heard that in other countries cantors do not conduct themselves in their singing except with pleasantness of voice alone, and not with blast and cry — Lev Chaim on the Rosh there, letter kaf-kaf-bet; they brought this, Magen Avraham seif kaf-chet, Etz Chayyim letter alef, Eliyah Rabbah siman 95 letter vav, Shaarei Teshuvah there letter bet; and see there what he wrote, reproof of Megillah 7a, see there.
