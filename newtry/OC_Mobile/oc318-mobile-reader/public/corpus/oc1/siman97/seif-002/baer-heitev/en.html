To spit: even in supplications after prayer it is forbidden, for as long as he has not stepped away it is considered as standing before the King. Magen Avraham.

Visible: specifically on an undergarment; but on an outer garment, since the saliva is visible, it is forbidden (Terumat HaDeshen). In our country where they wear a handkerchief (petzelis), even if the saliva is visible it is permitted since it is made for that purpose. Magen Avraham, and above siman 92 seif kaf 17.

To his left: Beit Yosef raised a difficulty — here preference is given to man's right, and below regarding three steps he gives more honor to his left because it is the right of the Shekhinah, etc. Later authors answered that here at the time of spitting the honor of the Shekhinah is removed from him, as written regarding "honor yourself," etc.; therefore we follow man's right and left, unlike three steps where one bows to the Shekhinah, therefore we follow the right of the Shekhinah.

That it is forbidden: and if it is impossible for him to spit except before him or to his right, and he suffers in prayer because he cannot spit — he is permitted to spit before him or to his right. Bach and Peri Megadim.
