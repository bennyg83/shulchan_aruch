To spit. Even during the supplications following the prayer it is forbidden, for as long as he has not stepped back, he is considered as standing before the King. Magen Avraham.
Visible. This applies specifically to an undergarment, but with an outer garment, since the saliva is visible, it is forbidden. Rabbeinu Yonah. In our countries, where people wear a cloth called a patchil, it is permitted even though the saliva is visible, since it is made for that purpose. Magen Avraham. See above, siman 92, seif katan 10.
To his left. The Beit Yosef asks that here preference is given to a person’s right side, whereas below, regarding the three steps, greater honor is accorded to his left because it corresponds to the right side of the Shechinah. See there. The later authorities answer that here, when he spits, he removes from himself the honor of the Shechinah, as written concerning “Honor yourselves, honored ones,” and therefore we follow the person’s own right and left. This is unlike the three steps, when he bows to the Shechinah, and therefore we follow the right side of the Shechinah.
For it is forbidden. If he can spit only in front of himself or to his right, and he is distressed during prayer because he cannot spit, he may spit in front of himself or to his right. Bach and Olas Tamid.
