(2) There: "and if he needs to gape from compulsion," etc. — in the Tur he wrote "and if he needs to do them," etc., as in the previous section; and it implies that for yawning one should understand for gaping — he must place his hand on his mouth. But from the wording of the Shulchan Aruch, which wrote "and if he needs to gape," etc., it implies specifically for gaping one needs placing the hand, because he opens his mouth more, but not for yawning — thus Eliyah Rabbah, Maamar Mordechai, Nishmas Shabbos, Mishbetzos Zahav. But the Levush wrote "and if he needs to do them," etc., as the Tur's words; and likewise the Sedei Chemed in Maareches Kaf HaChayyim, and so from the words of the Chida in Kitzur Shulchan Aruch siman 12. It appears they explain that what the Shulchan Aruch wrote "to gape" is not precise, but the same applies to yawning, and it cited one of them; and this is from what Beit Yosef brought the Tur's wording in Beit Yosef and wrote on it that the Gra in the Gemara (as cited) did not speak about it at all — it implies he agrees to his words. And further see in Beit Yosef, which brought proof for the Tur from Yerushalmi chapter Mi sheMeto, that R' Chanina said: I saw Rabbi gaping and sneezing and his hands were placed on his mouth — it implies that both for gaping and for sneezing he would place his hands on his mouth. And further see in the Rosh, chapter Mi sheMeto siman 34, that he did not have the reading in the Gemara "when he gapes he places," but only "and he places," etc.; and likewise Maharsha there, that it implies for all he would place his hand on his mouth — whether yawning, whether gaping, whether sneezing — and so one should be stringent.
