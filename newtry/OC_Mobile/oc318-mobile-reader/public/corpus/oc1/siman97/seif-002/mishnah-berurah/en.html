(3) To spit — and whatever is forbidden within the prayer is forbidden even in the supplications after the prayer, for as long as he has not stepped back, he is considered as standing before the King.

(4) If it is impossible for him, etc. — for example that he will suffer distress by this and will be distracted in his prayer; and after spitting he should wait the time of four cubits, as above in siman 92 seif 9.

(5) That it should not be visible — such as in an undergarment; but in an outer garment, since the saliva is visible, it is forbidden. And in our country where they wear handkerchiefs, even though the saliva is visible, it is possible that it is permitted, since it is made for this purpose there is no issue of disgust — so wrote the Magen Avraham; but other later authorities wrote that also in this case one must fold the place of the spit so it is not visible.

(6) And he cannot, etc. — meaning it is disgusting to him to absorb it in his garment, or he cannot keep it in his mouth until he absorbs it, or it is fine clothing and if we would require him to absorb it in it, that too would distract him.

(7) He casts it behind him — meaning that he turns his face behind him, or throws it with his hand behind him, but not upward, for that appears like denial.

(8) And all the more so before him — because the one praying has the Shechinah before him; nevertheless, if it is impossible for him to spit except before him or to his right, and he suffers distress in prayer because he cannot spit, he is permitted to spit to his right, and even before him.

(9) It is possible to say the reason, etc. — and the later authorities explained simply that here, at the time of his spitting, the honor of the Shechinah withdraws from him, as written in siman 3 regarding "honor the honored," etc.; therefore we follow the right and left of the person, unlike in siman 123 which discusses bowing, where he bows at the end of his prayer toward the Shechinah — therefore we follow the right of the Shechinah.

(10) And he is opposite the Shechinah — meaning that therefore his intention then, at the time of bowing, is to bow toward the Shechinah which is above ten handbreadths, for it has not descended below.
