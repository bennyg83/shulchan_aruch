(3) To spit. Everything forbidden during the prayer is forbidden even during the supplications following the prayer, for as long as he has not stepped back, he is considered as standing before the King.
(4) It is impossible for him, etc. For example, if this will distress him and distract him during his prayer. After spitting, he should wait the time needed to walk four amos, as stated above in siman 92, seif 9.
(5) So that it will not be visible. For example, into an undergarment. With an outer garment, however, since the saliva is visible, it is forbidden. In our countries, where people wear patchiles, even though the saliva is visible, it may be permitted, because since they are made for that purpose there is no repulsiveness. This is the ruling of the Magen Avraham. Other later authorities, however, write that even in this case he must wrap the place of the saliva so that it will not be visible.
(6) And he cannot, etc. Meaning, it is repulsive to him to absorb it into his garment, or he cannot retain it in his mouth until he can absorb it, or it is a fine garment and requiring him to absorb it there would also distract him.
(7) He should cast it behind him. This means that he turns his face behind himself, or casts it behind himself with his hand, but not upward, for that appears as though he denies Hashem.
(8) And certainly not in front of himself. This is because the Shechinah is before the person praying. Nevertheless, if he can spit only in front of himself or to his right, and he is distressed during prayer because he cannot spit, he may spit to his right and even in front of himself.
(9) One may explain that the reason, etc. The later authorities answer simply that here, when he spits, he removes from himself the honor of the Shechinah, as written in siman 3 concerning “Honor yourselves, honored ones,” and therefore we follow the person’s own right and left. This is unlike siman 123, which discusses bowing at the end of his prayer to the Shechinah, and therefore we follow the right side of the Shechinah.
(10) And he is opposite the Shechinah. Meaning, at that time, when bowing, his intention is necessarily to bow toward the Shechinah, which is above ten handbreadths, since it did not descend below that point.
