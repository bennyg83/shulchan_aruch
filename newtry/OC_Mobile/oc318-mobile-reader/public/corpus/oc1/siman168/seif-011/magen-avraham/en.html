And one does not bless. This requires study, for in siman 461, seif 4, it is written that one fulfills the obligation with soaked matzah that was not dissolved, implying even if it was soaked a great deal; if so, he should not need to recite Birkas HaMazon, as written in seif 7. One can answer that he must soak the whole olive-bulk together and eat it, as written at the beginning of siman 475, and then he recites HaMotzi and Birkas HaMazon, as written in seif 12. The Maharshal wrote: one who soaks his bread in wine or in some beverage called zupa, Rabbeinu Shlomo wrote that the bread exempts it, and he is never obligated to recite Borei Pri HaGafen or Shehakol unless he drinks the liquid in the manner of drinking. So too it appears from Shiltei Gibborim, page 29, and so wrote the Tanya in his name, concluding: therefore it is good to bless first on the liquid and afterward soak his bread in it. Likewise, when they put wine in a bowl with salt, called seitza, in order to dip meat into it, one need not bless, for the meat is primary and the meat has already been exempted by the blessing on HaMotzi. This follows his own view that liquids require a blessing within a meal. The practical difference for us is if it lacks the form of bread and one recites Borei Minei Mezonos on it; then the bread does not exempt the liquid, so one must bless on the liquid, and so one should practice. Afterward I found in Tosafot, chapter 4, that with rice and wine one blesses on the rice and exempts the wine, for the rice is primary. However, if his main intention is for the soup, the wine is primary, and if his intention is for both, he blesses on both; see what I wrote in siman 177. Maharil wrote in the name of Maharam Shalom that bread that fell into wine during the meal requires Borei Pri HaGafen. It appears to me that he derived this from Tosafot in Berachos 41, that according to Rashi, when one soaks his bread in his wine, bread does not exempt it; see there. However, we hold that the bread exempts it when he soaks into it and eats the bread, as Tosafot explain; see there and siman 212. It appears to me that if one crumbles a little bread very finely into hot beer to give it flavor, he recites only Shehakol on the beer; see siman 208, seif 6. The Rambam writes in chapter 3: any cooked dish into which one mixed one of the five species, whether flour or bread, one recites Borei Minei Mezonos, provided that it is not subordinate, etc. See the end of siman 204. Here the bread is subordinate, for his primary intention is to drink the beer, and the bread has no substance or importance.
