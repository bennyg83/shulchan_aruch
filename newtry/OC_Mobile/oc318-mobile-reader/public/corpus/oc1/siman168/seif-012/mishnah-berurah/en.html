In red wine. Specifically red wine, because since the bread became red through the wine, it lost the form of bread. But if one soaked his bread in white wine, we require specifically that the wine change from its appearance because of the bread soaked in it, as above in seif 11.
But Borei Minei Mezonos. Meaning, to exclude blessing HaMotzi on it. Regarding the blessing over the wine: if his main intent is for eating the bread, and the wine comes only to sweeten the eating, the wine becomes secondary, and he blesses only Borei Minei Mezonos over the bread. The same is the law for one who soaks pas kisnin in wine or liquor. Certainly one who soaks slices that contain a kezayis, or soaks bread in white wine, where he blesses HaMotzi even on slices that do not contain a kezayis, as above, the wine certainly becomes secondary to the bread. But if his intent is also for drinking the wine, for he wants to eat and drink together, it is proper that he first bless Borei Pri HaGafen over a little wine by itself, and afterward bless the blessing over the soaked bread. If his intent is only for the wine in it, and the bread comes only to sweeten the drinking, the bread becomes secondary to the wine, and he blesses only Borei Pri HaGafen. According to this, regarding the coffee drinking customary among us, where one dips pas kisnin into it, since his intent there is also for both, it also appears proper that before he blesses on the pas kisnin he should bless Shehakol on the coffee and drink a little. Better still, he should first bless on the pas kisnin alone without soaking, and afterward first bless Shehakol over a little sugar to exempt the coffee. Maamar Mordechai.
