(Seif katan 3) So that, etc. But if it appears, etc., see siman 366. This means that the law comes from the Rokeach, who holds that joining helps, based on siman 366. There, although one may not make an eruv with a slice, if two halves of bread were joined and appear whole, one may make an eruv with it. The Rokeach holds the same applies here. Just as in siman 366, if the joining is apparent it is not called whole, so too here. (Seif katan 4) He may, etc. The Rokeach wrote in this language, etc. The Shulchan Aruch's wording implies the novelty that even on Shabbos he may join them and there is no prohibition of fixing. But the Rokeach's wording is clear that he may also join them for Shabbos so that through this it will be considered a whole loaf for lechem mishneh.
