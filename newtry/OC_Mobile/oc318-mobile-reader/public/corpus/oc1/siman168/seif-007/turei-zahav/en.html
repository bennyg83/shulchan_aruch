Some explain that it is bread, etc., which they fill, etc. Rashi explains pas haba'ah bekisnin in terms of the filling. The dough itself is kneaded with water, but it is filled with sugar, honey, or nuts. This includes the broad folded dough made on Purim and filled with sesame seeds. There is no distinction between a fruit filling and a meat or fish filling. See what I write in seif 16 of this siman. The “some say” is Rambam, who holds that kisnin is determined by the dough itself, which was kneaded with honey or spices. The Beit Yosef was uncertain whether this means specifically that it was kneaded with much honey and little water, so that a minority of honey would be nullified to a majority of water and the product would be full bread, or whether even a small admixture of honey, whenever its taste is discernible in the dough, is called kisnin. He writes that the latter appears primary. As a matter of practical halachah, since this is a D'Rabbanan matter, he rules leniently in accordance with all the opinions. Thus, pockets filled with honey or almonds, and dough into which fruit juice or spices were mixed, all have the law of kisnin. My father-in-law objects: Why is this called a leniency? If he does not recite the proper blessing, either way it is a stringency, for he violates “You shall not take.” In my view there is no difficulty. Borei Minei Mezonos certainly covers every form of dough, since it provides nourishment. Only because of bread's importance did the Sages establish Hamotzi and three blessings of Bircas HaMazon. Therefore, whenever it is uncertain whether the product possesses that importance and is outside the category of kisnin, one is lenient and does not require Hamotzi. There is also a stringent aspect in avoiding an unnecessary Hamotzi and three blessings.
And some say that this is called full bread. This means where it contains only a small amount of honey, even though its taste is discernible. Rather, it must contain much spice or honey and only a minority of water, so that the honey or spice is virtually primary. This is measured by taste, such that the flavor of the honey and spice is more strongly sensed than the flavor of the dough. Only then is it kisnin. Rama writes regarding this that such is the custom. It is obvious that if it contains much honey and little water, all agree that it is kisnin. The same applies if it was kneaded with milk and little water, but not to products made with only a small amount of butter or fat, even though their taste is discernible, for those are full bread. Therefore, the practice of baking shemaltz cakes for lechem mishneh on Shabbos is proper, since they contain only a small amount of fat. It appears that Rama disagrees only with this point in the Shulchan Aruch, that discernible taste alone creates the status of kisnin. He does not disagree with the earlier definition based on filling, nor with the later definition, “Some explain,” and so forth. Otherwise he should have written that all of those are full bread, especially if he disputed everything cited by the Shulchan Aruch. My father-in-law cites Maharshal that one should not recite Borei Minei Mezonos even over lekach unless the spice and honey are primary and there is only a small amount of flour. It appears that one need not be concerned for this, since it is an individual opinion. My father-in-law also writes that Purim kreplach are kisnin, and that the principal matter depends on the kneading: if kneaded with mostly honey and little water, they are kisnin.
Like dry cakes. Meaning, attractively shaped like fine rolls, as Rashi explains in Keitzad Mevarchin, page 39. The phrase “and people chew them,” and so forth, refers to the cakes served at meals to stimulate the appetite and not to satisfy hunger. It appears to me that regarding kisnin created by a filling, as mentioned immediately above, even if the filling is removed and only the dough remains, it retains the law of kisnin. For this reason it is called pas haba'ah bekisnin, bread that comes with kisnin, rather than simply pas kisnin. The reason is that we follow its state at the time of baking.
