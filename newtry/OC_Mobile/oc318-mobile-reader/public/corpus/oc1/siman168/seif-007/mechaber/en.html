Pat haba'ah b'kisnin: Some explain that this means bread made in the form of pockets that are filled with honey or sugar, walnuts, almonds, and spices, which are called rusklayish and dialchashu. Others say that it is dough into which honey, oil, milk, or various spices were mixed and which was then baked, provided that the taste of the fruit juice or spices is perceptible in the dough. Some say that this is nevertheless considered ordinary bread unless it contains a large quantity of spices or honey, as in types of cake called lekach, where the honey and spices are nearly the principal ingredients, and this is the custom. [Rashi; Aruch; Rambam can also be understood this way.] Others explain that it means bread, whether spiced or unspiced, made into dry cakes that are chewed, which are called biskunis. The halacha follows all these views, and all of these foods are governed by the laws stated regarding pat haba'ah b'kisnin.
