Honey. Meaning, Rashi explains that pas haba'ah b'kisnin is defined by its filling, while the dough itself was kneaded with water, but it was filled with sugar, honey, or nuts. Included in this is what they make on Purim, a broad folded dough filled with sesame. The opinion cited as 'some say' is the Rambam, who holds that kisnin is defined by the dough itself, which was kneaded with honey or spices. The Beit Yosef was uncertain whether this means specifically that it was kneaded with much honey and little water, but if there is only a little honey it is nullified to the majority water and is complete bread, or perhaps even through a small admixture of honey, as long as its taste is recognizable in the dough, it is called kisnin. He wrote that for practical halacha, since it is a D'Rabbanan matter, one should be lenient like all the opinions. See what the Magen Avraham wrote regarding the Rambam, and see Responsa Panim Meiros, siman 68.
Complete bread. Meaning, if it contains only a little honey. Therefore those who bake shmalz kuchen on Shabbos for lechem mishneh do well, for there is only a little fat there. The Taz wrote that it appears the Rama disagrees only regarding this point, but he does not disagree with what the Shulchan Aruch wrote at first regarding filling, nor with what he later wrote, 'and some explain,' etc. It appears to me that regarding kisnin defined by the filling, even if the filling is removed and only the dough remains, the law of kisnin still applies to it, because we follow the time of baking. Taz. Purim kreplach are considered kisnin.
Lekach. The Bach, in the name of the Maharshal, wrote that even on lekach one does not recite Borei Minei Mezonos unless the honey is primary and the flour is little. The Taz wrote about this that it is an individual opinion, and the primary factor depends on the kneading: if it was kneaded with a majority of honey and a little water, it is kisnin. See there.
Those. This refers to the kichlach made during the meal for appetite, and not in order to satiate.
As kisnin. The reason is that the blessing Borei Minei Mezonos covers everything, even dough, since it nourishes. Only because of the importance of bread does one establish HaMotzi and the three blessings for it. Therefore, whenever it is not clear to us that it has such importance and is not included in kisnin, one should be lenient and not require HaMotzi and the three blessings. Taz. The Bach is stringent even when they contain spices or honey, requiring that they be eaten only within a meal unless the honey and spices exceed the flour. See there. The Taz rejected his words. See Responsa Ginas Veradim, klal 1, siman 24, page 13.
