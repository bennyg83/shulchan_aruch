That they fill them, etc. Meaning, even if it was initially kneaded with water like other bread, since the entire dough is filled with these items, and although the filling is primary for him, nevertheless grain is significant and is not nullified. Therefore he blesses Borei Minei Mezonos and exempts everything, as written in siman 208, seif 2 and 3. However, if he does not eat the dough and eats only the fruit inside, he blesses the blessing appropriate to the fruit. The Taz wrote that if the fruit was removed and he eats only the dough, nevertheless he blesses Borei Minei Mezonos and not HaMotzi, for it has the law of kisnin, since it was baked with fruit filling, and one does not normally establish a meal on such bread.
And nuts and almonds, etc. It means either one or the other. It is reasonable that it also speaks where the taste of the filling is noticeable in the dough, as he concludes at the end.
That he mixed into it. At the time of kneading, and this is even if the majority was water, since he also mixed these ingredients into it, and through this the taste changed, it is called pas kisnin.
Noticeable in the dough. For otherwise it is actual bread. Bread into which saffron is placed to flavor and beautify it, or into which a few raisins are placed, is according to all opinions actual bread. Peri Megadim.
For this is called. This refers to where he mixed only a little honey and milk into it during kneading; the name bread is not removed because the taste is felt.
Actual bread. And one blesses HaMotzi on any amount, and Birkas HaMazon on a kezayis, like the law of bread.
Much spice or honey, etc. Meaning, so much spice is mixed into them that because of this the spice is more noticeable in taste than the flour. Likewise with honey, oil, and milk, the majority must be from them and a minority water, so that through this the taste from them is felt very strongly, until they are primary and the taste of the dough is secondary. Likewise in the first case written by the Shulchan Aruch, where they are filled with them, the filling must also be so abundant that because of this the taste is felt very strongly.
And so is the practice. That for lechem mishneh they bake bread with a little oil and spice. Darkei Moshe. The Acharonim also agreed in halacha with the ruling of the Rama.
Dry ka'avim. That is their name in Arabic, and in Spanish they are called buskigos. They are made from the five species of grain and water alone, but in their baking they become so dry that they crumble, and this is not called food but something that one crunches, and people do not ordinarily eat much of it. Therefore it does not have the law of bread.
