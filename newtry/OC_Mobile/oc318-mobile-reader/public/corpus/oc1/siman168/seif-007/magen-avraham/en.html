This is difficult, for the Rambam himself ruled in the sixth chapter of Chametz U'Matzah that matzah kneaded with fruit juice fulfills one's obligation on Pesach, and the Gemara implies somewhat that if it is called bread regarding matzah, the same applies regarding HaMotzi. So too it appears from the Rambam, chapter 6, law 7; see there, and in Berachos 37, and what I wrote in siman 196. It is also difficult, for if so, one could eat this on Pesach and would not need to recite Birkas HaMazon, which does not seem so from siman 188. Perhaps the Rambam specifically mentioned here honey, oil, and milk, over which one recites Borei Minei Mezonos, because even according to the Rambam one does not fulfill his Pesach obligation with them, since it is rich matzah. But with other fruit juices, indeed one recites HaMotzi and Birkas HaMazon, because they do not remove it from the category of bread. According to this, wine is like honey, as written there. Alternatively, here it is speaking where there is no water at all, for he wrote 'kneaded with honey' or 'mixed with spices and baked,' meaning that it was kneaded entirely with honey, whereas there it is speaking where there is a little water, as the Maggid Mishneh wrote there. This requires study regarding the Beit Yosef, who changed the Rambam's language in chapter 5 and wrote 'mixed,' etc. The words of the Rama are primary.
