(Seif katan 9) Whichever, etc. This implies specifically because he was careful, since the Agudah did not write Rabbeinu Shimshon's practice immediately after writing that if one is not careful he blesses on whichever he wants. Rather, it comes adjacent to the case of one who is careful.
Since there is a dispute, etc. Meaning, the Yerushalmi states that where there is clean impure bread and coarse pure bread, one blesses on whichever he wants. The poskim compare Jewish bread to pure bread and bread of non-Jews to impure bread, and therefore rule that with clean bread of a non-Jew and coarse Jewish bread one blesses on whichever he wants. The two opinions cited by the Agudah dispute how to understand the Yerushalmi: one holds it refers to someone who does not eat ordinary food in purity, so it can be learned for bread of non-Jews only where he is not careful about such bread; the second holds it refers even to someone who eats ordinary food in purity, and therefore even one careful about bread of non-Jews may bless on whichever he wants. Thus, when he is not careful, Rabbeinu Shimshon would not command removal, for no one disputes that the Yerushalmi permits blessing on whichever he wants.
Nevertheless, it implies there that even when one was not careful, Rabbeinu Shimshon would remove it. The reason is that perhaps the Yerushalmi permitted blessing on whichever he wants only regarding impure bread, but bread of non-Jews is worse.
That the Beit Yosef did not want to rule like him means not to rule as implied by Terumas HaDeshen in the name of Rabbeinu Shimshon. The Beit Yosef relied on the Agudah, which implies that Rabbeinu Shimshon's practice applied specifically to one who was careful. Although the Beit Yosef in his work did not cite the Agudah at all, but only Terumas HaDeshen, still, for one who is not careful there is no need to remove it, since the Yerushalmi explicitly permits it and no one disputes this. The Beit Yosef did not accept the above distinction between impure bread and bread of non-Jews. Know that what was written above in the name of the Bach, that one who is careful need not remove it, is because he holds that since the careful person does not intend to eat from it and must break on the Jewish bread, why should he need to remove it? This accords with the Rama in the gloss to seif 1.
