Even though it became obligated in challah. One might ask: It is written in Yoreh Deah, siman 329, that in such a case it is not obligated in challah. It appears to me that here the case is where he originally intended to bake it and later changed his mind and decided to fry it, for in such a case it is obligated in challah. One should not ask: If so, why did the Beit Yosef write that the Tur's ruling here in accordance with Rabbeinu Tam is surprising? Why not say that all agree in this case, since it concerns one who changed his mind? The answer is that his words must nevertheless follow Rabbeinu Tam, because he made the law of Hamotzi dependent upon the law of challah, whereas according to Rabbeinu Shimshon there is a distinction, as written here in the name of those who disagree.
And the custom is to be lenient. This means in accordance with the first opinion, that one need not recite Hamotzi and the three blessings of Bircas HaMazon. This is called a leniency, as the Beit Yosef used this expression regarding pas haba'ah bekisnin: “As a matter of practical halachah, since this is a D'Rabbanan doubt, we follow all the opinions leniently,” and so forth. See also what I wrote above in seif katan 4. The Beit Yosef likewise writes here that the general custom is not to recite Hamotzi over sufganin, and so forth, and Levush writes the same. My father-in-law understood “the custom is to be lenient” to mean that the custom is to recite Hamotzi over them, and he therefore raised an objection. He was not precise.
Only when it has, etc. This is unlike seif 10, where the appearance of bread is not required when there is a kezayis, because there it was originally complete bread.
And one should not eat it unless he has recited, etc. The reason is that one opinion at the beginning of this seif follows the law of challah. Here there is an obligation of challah, since the dough was originally formed with a thick batter. Accordingly, these pastries and kreplach, although afterward fried, require Hamotzi. Another opinion holds that regarding Hamotzi the matter depends on baking, and accordingly they are exempt from Hamotzi here. One should therefore eat them only during a meal. This is difficult, however, for Rama immediately writes that the custom is to be lenient like the first opinion, and consequently it should not be necessary to eat them during a meal. One must say that Rama means that a God-fearing person should not rely on the custom. Although the Shulchan Aruch already wrote this, Rama repeats it here regarding kreplach fried in honey, according to his view that this is not pas haba'ah bekisnin. In my humble opinion, this does not appear correct, for the reason I will write immediately. Moreover, Rama's distinction here, that the dough was kneaded with oil and honey, requires study. It is certainly pas haba'ah bekisnin even if it was kneaded without honey and merely fried in honey, because we follow the final preparation, and at that stage it was fried in honey. Mordechai writes an even greater novelty, which I cite below in seif katan 13, that bread whitened by water loses the status of bread and resembles kisnin. All the more so here, where pas haba'ah bekisnin does not depend upon the appearance of bread, it should acquire that status by being fried in honey. My father-in-law likewise writes that hoizen blasen have the law of kisnin, and they have the same law as these kreplach. It appears that Rama needed to write this because he was troubled by the entire discussion whether one recites Hamotzi over a fried item. It should apparently be pas haba'ah bekisnin in any event. He therefore distinguished between the cases. In truth there is no difficulty, for even pas haba'ah bekisnin requires Hamotzi if one establishes a meal upon it. The dispute here is whether Hamotzi has any relevance to it at all. If one eats it in the manner of kisnin, its blessing is plainly Borei Minei Mezonos. If one establishes a meal upon it, the law depends on the dispute cited at the beginning of this seif. Rama and the Beit Yosef already testify that the custom is to be lenient. I therefore know of no remaining doubt regarding eating a small quantity, except where it was fried in oil or honey and he wishes to eat enough to constitute an established meal, or where it was kneaded with water and therefore is not kisnin, in which case this law applies even to a small quantity. A God-fearing person should not eat an established quantity except during a meal. See also seif 17 below for more on this matter.
