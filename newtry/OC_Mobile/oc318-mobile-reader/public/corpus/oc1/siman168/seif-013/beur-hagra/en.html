Seif 13: If he cooked it, etc., even though it has upon it, etc. This excludes the second view, which obligates if he cooked it, specifically where it has the form of bread, as written in the gloss. This is unlike the Magen Avraham, who wrote that with something cooked the form of bread is not relevant; he overlooked the words of the Rosh there in Pesachim and there in Hilkhot Challah, who wrote: Furthermore, Rabbeinu Tam would say, etc., but something whose kneading was thick, etc., even if its end is to cook them in water, etc., here too one recites hamotzi over it after cooking, etc. The same applies to those cooked in water, for it is not reasonable to distinguish between water and oil, etc. R. Meir conducted himself, etc., in order to cook it, etc., after it was cooked, etc.; see there. Although he wrote in seif 10 that what the Rash wrote, that cooking cancels baking, we do not rule like him, nevertheless here perhaps one may rule like him, for there it is specifically where he baked it first, as the Rosh wrote there in Hilkhot Challah, and so too regarding kanubkaot, etc. This is what he wrote: since he baked it in an oven or pan and it became bread, etc.
And some disagree. This is the opinion of Rabbeinu Tam: that which R. Yochanan and Resh Lakish dispute in Pesachim 37a is specifically with a thin batter, like sufganin, etc., for there if its beginning and end are sufganin it is exempt, but if its end is dough it is liable, as written in chapter 1 of Challah. They dispute whether pan-preparation is considered bread or not. But with a thick dough, all agree it is liable, even if he cooked it in liquid, for although R. Yochanan agrees there, nevertheless since its beginning was dough it is liable, as stated there, "its beginning was dough," etc. Rabbeinu Tam further wrote that the same applies regarding hamotzi, from what is written in Menachot 75 and Berakhot 37, "he took them to eat them," etc., implying even pan and deep-pan offerings, although fried in oil, and the same applies if he cooked it in water. See Tosafot there, s.v. le-khulei alma, and there in Berakhot, s.v. lechem, and Tosafot in Pesachim 36b, s.v. perat, and below where it discusses, etc. The Rash and Ramban disagreed with him and wrote that the Mishnah's statement "its beginning was dough" means that he initially made it with intent for complete bread and afterward changed his mind to make sufganin; but if he made it initially with intent to make sufganin, that is the first clause there, "its beginning and end are sufganin." A proof is from Berakhot there: bread made for kutach is exempt. Tosafot there also asked this. What it says there that kavim are liable is because they can be confused with other dough, and this is what they said there: its actions prove, etc.; or alternatively, perhaps he will change his mind and make bread, as written in the Yerushalmi there: a woman asked R. Mana, because I want to make my dough into noodles, should it be exempt from challah, meaning my dough will be sufganin. He said to her: Why not? meaning it is obvious it is exempt. He came and asked his father, who said: It is forbidden, lest she change her mind and make dough, etc. Rav said: dough for kutach is liable in challah. R. Bun said: perhaps he will change his mind and make it into a white cake. R. Mana said: We must announce to those who make avitzin, meaning chavitzin, that they should make them less than the required measure, for they think it is exempt but it is liable. The reason is because of a decree, as above. Do not say the Yerushalmi disagrees with our Gemara, for there it speaks where he took a little from it to bake, and we decree regarding all of it, while our Gemara speaks where he took none from it; so too kutach follows its usual manner. But regarding kavim, all agree it is liable, for there the decree is more applicable. See Yoreh Deah siman 329 seif 2 and seif 7. Another proof is from what is written in the Yerushalmi there and at the beginning of chapter 4 there: one who makes dough intending to divide it while dough is exempt from challah. He said to him: But we learned that a baker who made sourdough to divide is liable in challah. He said: Do not answer me from a baker; with a baker the matter does not depend on his intent but on the customers' intent, perhaps he will find customers and return and make it dough. This is also the reason with thanksgiving-offering loaves, as written in the Yerushalmi there and in the Gemara, Perek Kol Shaah, regarding matzah. Thus we follow his intent. Likewise regarding dog dough, they said there: What is dog dough? Resh Lakish says: any dough into which bran was mixed. The baraita says so: when shepherds eat from it; sometimes shepherds do not eat from it. R. Yochanan said: any dough made like kavim; and so it was taught: if he made them like kavim they are liable, if he made them like limudin they are exempt. R. Ba in the name of Shmuel, R. Ami in the name of R. Chiyya: most of it, even if he made it like fine loaves. But we learned: if shepherds do not eat from it? Explain it as where he made it from the outset so the shepherds should not eat from it. Thus we follow his intent. Even Resh Lakish and R. Yochanan dispute only because it can be confused, as with dough for kutach. See Ran in Perek Kol Shaah. So too regarding hamotzi it is exempt. That which we say "he took it to eat," etc., is with a flour offering, or even with pan and deep-pan offerings, because their oil is little and they are like baked items. Talmidei Rabbeinu Yonah wrote that even something liable in challah is nevertheless exempt regarding hamotzi, for challah depends on kneading, unlike hamotzi, like the first opinion of Rabbeinu Tam in Berakhot in the above dibbur, except that Rabbeinu Tam retracted because of that baraita, "he took it," etc., and it has already been answered as above. So wrote Rabbeinu Yerucham. This is what Shulchan Aruch wrote in the first opinion: even something, etc., and even, etc., meaning where he changed his mind, nevertheless regarding hamotzi it is exempt, as he wrote and as above. The Rash and Rosh in Perek Kol Shaah further brought proof that one does not recite hamotzi from what is written at the end of Perek Kol Shaah: R. Yosi says one fulfills, etc., but not with cooked, etc., and we rule like R. Yosi. That which is said in Berakhot there, "when the pieces remain intact," follows R. Meir. This requires study, for there on 38b we conclude: R. Yosi said only, etc. Although in Pesachim there we do not conclude so, Tosafot there wrote that this follows the sugya that over cooked vegetables one recites shehakol, but we rule that one recites borei peri ha-adamah, and we also rule like the above baraita and like "when the pieces remain," as above in seif 10.
