(2) And some disagree. This is the view of Rabbenu Tam, for that which Rabbenu Yonah and Rabbenu Levi disagree in Pesachim 37a specifically regarding a soft batter like sufganin — for there with sufganin the beginning and end are exempt, the end is obligated dough as is written in the first chapter of Challah; and they disagree in the case of alfes whether it is bread or not; but in a thick batter all agree it is obligated, and even if boiled in liquid — although Rabbenu Yonah agrees — nevertheless since its beginning is obligated dough, as was stated "its beginning is dough," etc. And Rabbenu Tam further wrote that the same applies regarding hafrashah, as is stated in Menachot 76a and Berachot 37b "they took them to eat them," etc., and forty — even from a pan and while moving, although fried in oil; and likewise if boiled in water. And Tosafos there s.v. lechol and there in Berachos s.v. lechem and Tosafos in Pesachim 36b s.v. prat — and below deals with this; and Rosh and Ramban disagreed with him and wrote that what we learned "its beginning is dough" means that he made it from the outset for complete bread and afterward changed his mind to make sufganin; but if he made it from the outset to make sufganin, that is the beginning there — beginning and end sufganin; and proof from what is written in Berachos there: bread made for kutach is exempt; and likewise Tosafos challenged there; and from what is written "thick batter is obligated" — because it is confused with other dough; and this is what they said there "its actions prove," etc.; or perhaps he may change his mind to make bread, as is written in the Yerushalmi there — a woman asked R' Mana: because I want to make my dough strips — shall it be exempt from challah? Meaning my sufganin dough. He said: why not? Meaning it is obvious it is exempt. She asked her father; he said: forbidden, lest he change his mind to make dough, etc. Rav said: kutach batter is obligated in challah. R' Bun said: lest he change his mind to make it white bread.
