(67) Whose dough was thick. Meaning that after he poured the water into the flour, he kneaded it into a thick dough like bread dough.
(68) If he cooked it, etc. This means cooking it in water or frying it in oil.
(69) One does not recite, etc. This applies even to pieces that contain a kezayis. It is not comparable to what was written in seif 10, where cooking does not remove their status as bread, because there they were originally complete bread that had been baked. Here, however, they were never baked, and therefore cooking or frying removes their status as bread. See below, seif 14 in the Rama's gloss, that if one places only a small amount of oil in the pan so that the dough will not burn, this is not considered frying in liquid, but full-fledged baking.
(70) The appearance of bread. Even if he ate a large quantity and established his meal upon them, they are like porridge. He recites Borei Minei Mezonos beforehand and the one blessing abridged from three afterward.
(71) Even if it became obligated in challah. Meaning, even if it is considered bread regarding challah, such as where at the time of kneading he did not intend to cook or fry it and only afterward changed his mind, in which case all opinions agree that it is obligated in challah, as explained in Yoreh De'ah, siman 329, he nevertheless does not recite Hamotzi.
(72) The time of baking. Something is called bread only when baked. Since it was not baked in an oven or in a pan without liquid, it does not bear the name of bread. Regarding challah, however, everything follows the rolling and kneading of the flour with water. At that point the obligation to separate challah takes effect because it has become dough, and the obligation is not subsequently removed through cooking or frying.
(73) There are those who disagree, etc. They hold that since the dough was originally thick and it now has the appearance of bread, one recites Hamotzi. According to them, this applies even if one eats it casually and does not establish a meal upon it, because they consider it complete bread, as stated below in the Rama's gloss.
(74) The custom is, etc. Meaning, the custom follows the first opinion, that one need not recite Hamotzi and the three blessings of Bircas HaMazon.
(75) To be lenient. Magen Avraham writes that this applies only when one did not eat to satiation, in which case his obligation to recite Bircas HaMazon is only rabbinic, and we therefore follow the first opinion leniently. If he ate to satiation, his obligation to recite Bircas HaMazon is from the Torah, and he must recite it because of the doubt. This is also implied by the Gra. All this applies where, at the time he kneaded the thick dough, he intended to bake bread, but later changed his mind and made it into fried cakes. If from the outset he intended to cook or fry it, then even if he ate to satiation he recites only the one blessing abridged from three afterward.
(76) A God-fearing person should satisfy, etc. This applies even where, at the time of kneading, he intended to cook or fry it.
(77) He should not eat, etc. Meaning, he should not eat even a small amount outside a meal.
(78) After baking. The wording is not entirely precise. It means after cooking it in a pot or frying it in a pan. Many Acharonim agree that this applies only if the pieces each contain a kezayis. If they do not, cooking or frying completely removes their status as bread regarding Hamotzi, as stated above in seif 10.
(79) The appearance of bread. This is not comparable to seif 10, where the appearance of bread is not required when there is a kezayis, because there it had initially been complete bread.
(80) Pashtida and kreplach, etc. Meaning, they are filled with meat, as written in the Rama's gloss at the end of this siman. If they are filled with fruit, they are pas haba'ah b'kisnin, as stated in seif 7, and one initially recites Borei Minei Mezonos when he does not establish a meal upon them, even if they were baked in an oven. All this follows the Rama. Taz, however, disagrees and holds that bread filled with meat, fish, or the like is also included in pas haba'ah b'kisnin. Accordingly, when one does not establish a meal upon them, all agree that he recites Borei Minei Mezonos beforehand and the one blessing abridged from three afterward. The practical ruling will be explained at the end of the siman.
(81) It is considered to have the appearance of bread, and one should not, etc. This refers to a God-fearing person who wishes to satisfy the opinion of those who disagree, where the item was cooked in water or fried in oil.
(82) All this, etc. This refers to the dissenting opinion, which holds that it is complete bread and requires Hamotzi even if one did not establish a meal upon it. That view applies only to dough that was not originally kneaded with oil, honey, or the like. Since it was originally complete dough like ordinary bread, they hold that its name as bread is not removed regarding Hamotzi even though it was later fried in oil and became similar to pas haba'ah b'kisnin. If, however, it was originally kneaded with honey or the like before frying, it never bore the status of ordinary bread dough. Therefore, even according to the dissenting opinion it is not complete bread requiring Hamotzi when one does not establish a meal upon it, for it is pas haba'ah b'kisnin. If one establishes a meal upon it, according to the first opinion he still recites only Borei Minei Mezonos because frying completely removed its status as bread, while according to the dissenting opinion he recites Hamotzi and Bircas HaMazon, as with pas haba'ah b'kisnin.
