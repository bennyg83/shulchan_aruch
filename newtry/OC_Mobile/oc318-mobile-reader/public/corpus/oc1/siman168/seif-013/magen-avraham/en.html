The form of bread. Meaning, for example, he baked it in a pan; even so it is not bread. This is not comparable to what is written in seif 10, for there it was bread initially, and therefore as long as it has the form of bread it does not leave the category of bread. See seif 14 in the gloss, that a small amount of liquid is not called liquid.
And even if it became obligated. Meaning, even according to the opinion that it is obligated in challah, but we rule that it is exempt, as written in Yoreh Deah, siman 329. The Bach wrote that if his intention was to bake it and he reconsidered and cooked it, Rabbeinu Shimshon agrees that one recites HaMotzi, etc.; see there. In my humble opinion this is not so, for regarding challah, the obligation arises at the time of kneading, and then he intended to bake. But HaMotzi comes at the time of baking. Furthermore, if he baked a little of it and cooked the rest, one does not recite HaMotzi on the cooked portion. So wrote Rabbeinu Yerucham, that Maharam baked a little of it to obligate it in challah and ate it during the meal because of doubt regarding the blessing; see there. So too is the view of Maharam Shalom, who brings proof from a cooked wafer, that although it had been complete bread, once he cooked it, it left the category of bread, and certainly when he only intended to make bread from it. It appears to me that the language of the Beit Yosef misled the Bach, for at the end of this siman he wrote in the name of Rabbeinu Shimshon that if one made it like cakes, it is not obligated in challah unless he takes from it a cake and bakes it, for we decree lest he reconsider regarding the whole dough, and the Beit Yosef wrote that the same applies regarding HaMotzi. The Bach understood this to mean that regarding HaMotzi too, if he baked a little, he blesses HaMotzi on the rest. This has no reason, for how does a decree apply at the time of HaMotzi? Also, if so, why did he write plainly in seif 16 that one recites Borei Minei Mezonos and not distinguish? Rather, the Beit Yosef's intent must be that the same applies regarding HaMotzi, that it is exempt even if made like cakes, just as it is exempt regarding challah; and that which obligates in challah if he baked a little is because of a decree, which does not apply to HaMotzi. See there.
And some disagree. They hold that since its kneading is thick and it has the form of bread, one recites HaMotzi.
And the custom is to be lenient. Meaning, one does not recite HaMotzi but only Borei Minei Mezonos, and so it appears at the end of this siman, unlike the Bach. See the Levush. Nevertheless, it appears to me that if he is satiated from it, it is a doubt according to the Torah, and he must recite Birkas HaMazon out of doubt, as written in siman 184, seif 4.
That he should bless on bread. Since their kneading is thick, they are not filled with anything, and they were not kneaded with anything, bread exempts them. Shelah. However, in seif 7 he wrote that dry cakes are also kisnin. Rather, the primary explanation is that here it speaks of pastida filled with meat or kreplach, as written above, and they come as a cooked dish and for nourishment, not for dessert; therefore bread exempts them, as written in seif 8.
After baking. It appears to me that he mentioned baking because here he is speaking of a pastida baked in a pan with liquid or water. If he cooked it in a pot, it is obvious that it does not have the form of bread, even if it had been complete bread and was cooked afterward, as written in seif 10. Therefore he mentioned baking. See what I wrote in siman 471. The Shelah emended it unnecessarily.
Pastida and kreplach. Meaning, they are filled with meat, as written at the end of this siman, for if they are filled with types of fruit they are kisnin, as written in seif 7, even if baked in an oven.
Were kneaded with them, etc. Nevertheless, the practical difference is that there, if he fixed his meal on it, he recites HaMotzi according to all opinions; but if he fried it in liquid, there is a dispute whether fixing a meal is effective, since according to the first opinion it is not bread at all, because it was cooked or fried. So too the Tanya wrote that a cooked dish made from flour and honey, or with milk and other liquid, even if he fixed his meal on it, requires Borei Minei Mezonos.
