(Seif katan 13) To fix on it, it appears to me that if he fixed, etc. The wording 'he fixed' is not precise, for it does not depend on him, but on whether others fix a meal on it, as in the Shulchan Aruch and the Gemara. He used this wording because of the end of the statement.
So too the language of the Gemara implies: anything on which others fix a meal. Since 'fix on it' means that it is a measure of satiation, the Gemara should have said, 'anything from which others are accustomed to be satiated.' Rather, if it had said that, it would have implied that they must be satiated from the pas haba'ah b'kisnin alone. In truth, even if they are satiated only through the food eaten with it, it is called a fixed meal. Therefore it used the language of fixing a meal on it, which can mean that they are satiated by combining what they eat with it, while the main fixed element is the pas haba'ah b'kisnin.
And if he ate it alone and did not eat accompanying foods with it. We also require that others would be satiated, for it does not depend on him alone. If we would measure according to accompaniment, then when he ate accompaniment we need not measure, for we see that he was satiated from it, and presumably others would also be satiated if they ate it with a similar amount of accompaniment. But when he ate it alone, we would have to estimate whether others would be satiated if they ate accompaniment with it, and through this we would not know the measure of bread, since depending on how much accompaniment there is, the amount of bread eaten increases or decreases.
See the first chapter of Eruvin, chapter 8. The Mishnah asks the measure of food for two meals for an eruv techumin. Rabbi Meir says it is weekday food, not Shabbos food, and Rabbi Yehudah says Shabbos food, not weekday food, and both intend to be lenient. Rabbi Yehudah holds that on Shabbos each meal is smaller because one must eat three meals, while Rabbi Meir holds the opposite, that on Shabbos one eats more because there is more appetite and the cooked foods are tastier. He holds that the bread is the same on Shabbos as on weekdays, but the dish eaten with the bread is tastier on Shabbos, and through this one eats more bread. If so, how can we measure according to Rabbi Yehudah a Shabbos meal, since we need to know the measure of bread alone, while on Shabbos he eats it with accompaniments and cooked foods, and depending on their amount the bread eaten changes? We must say that we measure the accompaniment according to the way most people eat it at most meals. The same applies here.
And if he is satiated from it and others, etc. He recites HaMotzi, etc. Meaning, the Beit Yosef cited the Raavad, who holds that it is enough if he fixed a meal on it even if others do not. Although we do not rule like him, but require that others fix a meal on it, nevertheless in such a case, where others would be satiated from it if they ate it with accompaniment, the Magen Avraham left it in doubt whether to recite HaMotzi. Therefore, when combined with the fact that he himself was satiated from it alone, which also has the Raavad's opinion, it was clear to the Magen Avraham that he recites HaMotzi. Regarding the measure on which others fix a meal, I saw in Sefer Kapos Temarim on Sukkah, and so wrote later authorities, that it is three or four eggs. This is because below, in siman 368, there is a dispute between Rashi and the Rambam regarding the measure of food for two meals for an eruv: according to Rashi it is six eggs and according to the Rambam eight eggs. Accordingly one meal is three eggs according to Rashi and four according to the Rambam. Since that is a meal measure for each according to his view, it is called fixing a meal on it.
