He blesses on the larger one. The Nachalas Tzvi wrote that this is specifically when both are equal in quality; but if the smaller one is fine bread and the larger one is coarse bread, he blesses on the smaller one, end quote. It seems to me that there is proof to this from the Tosefta cited by Tosafot there: if there is a whole loaf of fine bread and a whole loaf of a householder's bread, he blesses on the whole loaf of fine bread; if there is a piece of fine bread and a whole loaf of a householder's bread, he blesses on the whole loaf of the householder's bread. If it were so that he blesses on the larger whole loaf, it should have stated everything with whole loaves, with a greater novelty, for example: a small whole loaf of fine bread and a large whole loaf of a householder's bread, he blesses on the householder's bread. Rather, it is proven that in that case he blesses on the small whole loaf of fine bread. It is possible that this is what the first clause of the Tosefta discusses, and that is why it needed to teach that he blesses on the whole loaf of fine bread.
