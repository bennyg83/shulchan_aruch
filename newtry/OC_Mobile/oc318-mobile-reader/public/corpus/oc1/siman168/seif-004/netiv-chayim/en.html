Magen Avraham, s.k. 32: ‘In my humble opinion, this is not, etc.’ Note: This is difficult. If so, how does a small quantity of challah exempt it because it is matzah ashirah? It should suffice that it is not considered bread with respect to HaMotzi. It therefore appears to me that according to Rabbeinu Shimshon one certainly recites HaMotzi when he changed his mind. His reasoning is the same as will be stated according to Rabbeinu Tam: since he made a thick dough of the kind ordinarily used for bread, even if he fried or cooked it, it nevertheless remains bread because it retains the appearance of bread. Consequently, even if a small amount is baked in the manner of bread in an oven while some is cooked or fried, it is nevertheless fully considered bread. Rabbeinu Shimshon challenges Rabbeinu Tam that, if cooking necessarily removes the status of bread, this follows from the case of a cooked wafer. Yet when his original intention was to bake it and he subsequently changed his mind and cooked it, Rabbeinu Shimshon agrees that we say he is acting on his original intention: he wishes to eat it after cooking while it retains the status of bread, since it has the appearance of bread, and it is therefore fully considered bread. This differs from a cooked wafer, where his intention through cooking was to remove its status as bread and make it into a cooked dish. The same applies to chavitza. The Tur’s words are therefore correct, as Bach wrote. Magen Avraham’s explanation of Beis Yosef’s words is also forced; the explanation I have given will be shown to be correct. Moreover, even according to Magen Avraham’s own approach, the words of Beis Yosef remain valid. Rabbeinu Yerucham distinguishes between challah and HaMotzi, yet Beis Yosef cites him as ruling that when bread made for kutach was formed as thick loaves, one recites HaMotzi, since it was made in the shape of loaves; see there and consider this carefully.
