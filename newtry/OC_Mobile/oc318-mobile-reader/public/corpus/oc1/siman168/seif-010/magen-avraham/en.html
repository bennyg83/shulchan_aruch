It appears to me that since there is a dispute in Yoreh Deah, siman 68, whether pouring cooks like a kli rishon, one should avoid eating crumbs over which one poured from a kli rishon when they appear to have the form of bread, and certainly when he eats a great deal until he is satiated from them, for there is a doubt according to the Torah whether he should recite Birkas HaMazon. Therefore he should first bless on other bread, as written in siman 177.

Recognizable and known, etc. The Mordechai concludes: that it was not completely dissolved. The Beit Yosef intentionally omitted this, for the Mordechai follows Rashi, who holds that even if it was cooked it is called the form of bread unless it was completely dissolved. But according to us, who hold that even if it was not dissolved it does not have the form of bread once cooked, then likewise, when it was not cooked, if it is not recognizable as bread, it is not called the form of bread. See seif 12. Know that in all these cases, even if he fixed his meal on it, he recites Borei Minei Mezonos, since it is not bread at all; see seif 6. The Gemara says that if one crumbled bread until it became like fine meal, then kneaded it together again and baked it again, he must recite HaMotzi, even if it lacks the form of bread, for according to Rabbi Yehudah it is called crumbs that contain an olive-bulk, and with an olive-bulk the form of bread is not required. It implies there even if they were first fried in oil, similar to menachos, as Tosafot wrote in Berachos 37. Accordingly, those who make frimzelach from crumbled bread or from bread soaked in water must recite HaMotzi, since its beginning was bread and it does not leave the category of bread until each piece lacks an olive-bulk. Although according to what the Beit Yosef wrote for the Rambam, if the form of bread passed away completely through cooking, even if they contain an olive-bulk one does not recite HaMotzi, nevertheless we do not rule so, as written in the Shulchan Aruch. Still, if he mixed flour into them and fried them in fat, or kneaded them, it requires study, see seif 13, whether we follow the majority. However, those who fry whole slices plainly recite HaMotzi, as written here in the Shulchan Aruch. In Kenesses HaGedolah, in the name of Rashach, part 1, siman 162, he wrote that the fritters made from crushed matzah, mixed with eggs and fried in oil, even though while it is crushed one must recite HaMotzi, nevertheless when he joined it with water and eggs it changed and he recites Borei Minei Mezonos, and certainly after frying. This requires study.
