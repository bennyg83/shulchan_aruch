And if. See Taz. His sacred words require careful study and close analysis, and they are difficult to understand. First, he infers from the Mechaber that Mordechai derives the law of small rolls eaten during a meal by an a fortiori argument from pas haba'ah b'kisnin. Regarding pas haba'ah b'kisnin, Berachos 41b states that no food requires a blessing afterward but not beforehand except pas haba'ah b'kisnin. Tosafos there, s.v. ela, explain that all the more so this applies to small rolls, meaning dough kneaded with oil. According to Rashi, establishment of a meal does not help for such dough. Even according to our ruling that establishment does help, when one does not establish a meal it is no better than pas haba'ah b'kisnin: ordinary bread eaten at the meal does not exempt it, one recites Borei Minei Mezonos before it, and Bircas HaMazon afterward exempts its concluding blessing. Wafers, according to our ruling, are full-fledged bread when eaten without establishing a meal, and the bread of the meal exempts them. The Mechaber omitted the case of pas haba'ah b'kisnin and mentioned small rolls, which could lead one to think that the law applies only to small rolls. Taz answers that the Mechaber wished to teach a greater novelty. He then writes that when small rolls are eaten during a meal without establishing a meal upon them, the requirement to recite Borei Minei Mezonos does not depend upon one's intent, whether he seeks satiation or dessert, but upon whether he ate the quantity upon which others ordinarily establish an independent meal. He proves this from Mordechai's a fortiori argument from pas haba'ah b'kisnin. Taz understands this as deriving from Berachos 42a, where our Gemara states that one who eats small rolls without establishing a meal does not recite HaMotzi but Borei Minei Mezonos. From there the law is derived for pas haba'ah b'kisnin. Rif writes, “It is reasonable that where he ate it in the manner of kisnin,” and so forth. Since Rif explicitly rules that one who eats pas haba'ah b'kisnin without establishing a meal recites Borei Minei Mezonos, it follows that even during a meal, where he did not establish a meal upon it, ordinary bread does not exempt it and he must recite Borei Minei Mezonos. All the more so this is true of small rolls, for according to Rashi establishment does not help for kneaded dough. Accordingly, the matter certainly depends not upon his intention but upon the quantity upon which others establish a meal, as in seif 7. This appears to be Taz's intention. Rif, however, is certainly discussing food eaten outside a meal. See Chemed Moshe; according to what I have written, his words are understandable. Still, it is difficult, for Mordechai derives the law from Berachos 41b, where pas haba'ah b'kisnin eaten during a meal requires a blessing before it, as stated above. See there also regarding the differing expressions “he ate” and “they brought before him.”
I later saw that words of living water flow from the Sanctuary, sweet and pleasant. Beit Yosef understands that Mordechai disagrees with Rabbeinu Meir, or Rabbi Yitzchak. Rabbeinu Meir holds that ordinary bread exempts kneaded dough eaten during a meal, whereas Mordechai derives by an a fortiori argument from pas haba'ah b'kisnin that ordinary bread does not exempt it. Bach, in seif 5, holds that ordinary bread fails to exempt it only when it is eaten after the meal, but during the meal ordinary bread does exempt it. This appears from Tosafos, Berachos 41b, s.v. ela, who disagree with Rashi and explain that the discussion concerns food eaten before Bircas HaMazon. The Mechaber holds that even if everything is eaten during the meal, a blessing must nevertheless be recited before it. There is therefore no proof from Berachos 41b, and Mordechai must derive the law from page 42. Since Rif states that pas haba'ah b'kisnin requires Borei Minei Mezonos when eaten without establishing a meal, the same applies during the meal, and all the more so to small rolls. It follows that the matter certainly depends upon the quantity eaten, as I wrote. Regarding Taz's reference to seif 13, seif katan 19, with Hashem's help it will be explained there.
