Was cooked. Meaning, he placed the bread in a kli rishon. But if he placed the crumbs in a bowl and poured boiling water over them, this is not called cooked. Beit Yosef, Mordechai. The Magen Avraham wrote: it appears to me that since there is a dispute in Yoreh Deah, siman 68, whether pouring cooks like a kli rishon, one should refrain from eating crumbs over which one poured from a kli rishon when they appear to have the form of bread, and certainly when he eats much, until he is satiated from them, for then it is a doubt according to the Torah whether he should recite Birkas HaMazon. Therefore he should first bless on other bread, as written in siman 177.
An olive-bulk. Meaning, each individual slice contains an olive-bulk.
Borei Minei Mezonos. In all these cases, even if he fixed his meal on it, he recites Borei Minei Mezonos, since it is not bread at all. See above seif katan 17. The Gemara states that if one crumbled bread until it returned to the form of fine meal, and then kneaded it together again and baked it again, he must recite HaMotzi. The Magen Avraham wrote: therefore, those who make frimzelach from crumbled bread or from bread soaked in water must recite HaMotzi, since its beginning was bread and it does not leave the category of bread until each piece lacks an olive-bulk. Nevertheless, if he mixed flour into them and fried them in fat, or kneaded them, it requires study whether we follow the majority. See seif 13. The Kenesses HaGedolah in the name of the Shach, part 1, siman 163, ruled plainly that one recites Borei Minei Mezonos; see there. However, those who fry whole slices plainly recite HaMotzi, as written in the Shulchan Aruch. Magen Avraham, see there.
