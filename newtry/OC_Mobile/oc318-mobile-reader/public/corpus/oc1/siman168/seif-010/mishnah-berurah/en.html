Chavitza, etc. I will preface this seif with a brief introduction: there are three forms in this matter. First, bread that one crumbled into pieces and cooked: this depends on whether the crumbs are large, containing a kezayis. If they contain a kezayis, the name bread is not removed from them, even if through cooking their bread-form disappeared. If they do not contain a kezayis, even if it appears that they have the form of bread, he blesses Borei Minei Mezonos, for the name of a cooked dish is upon them. Second, if he did not cook them, but only crumbled them into a bowl and the crumbs became joined together through honey or broth: if they still have the form of bread, he blesses HaMotzi over them, even if the crumbs do not contain a kezayis; and if they do not have the form of bread, he blesses Borei Minei Mezonos over them, unless the crumbs contained a kezayis, as above regarding cooking. Third, when he neither cooked them nor joined the crumbs together by liquid: even if the crumbs are fine like semolina, he blesses HaMotzi over them, for the name bread is upon them. Now we will explain the seif, with the help of Heaven.
That is, bread crumbs that stick together. When cooked, there is no difference whether they stick together or not. He mentioned this because of the second form, which speaks without cooking.
Together. The same applies if they were merely soaked in it for a long time, until the water became whitened through them; its law is like pieces stuck together, as below in seif 11.
If it was cooked, etc. This means that he placed the bread in a kli rishon while it was standing on the fire, or at least when it was yad soledes bo. But in a kli sheni it is not considered cooking, and its law is like the second form, that if it has the form of bread, he blesses HaMotzi even with less than a kezayis. This is specifically if the water was not whitened by the bread, but if the water became white, as commonly happens when bread is soaked in hot water, it is explained in seif 11 that through this the bread is considered to lack the form of bread. If he placed the crumbs in a bowl and poured boiling water over them from a kli rishon, the Acharonim wrote that it is doubtful whether this has the law of cooking or not. Therefore, if the crumbs do not contain a kezayis and they have the form of bread, he should first bless over another bread.
If they contain, etc. This does not mean all of them together, but even if one slice contains a kezayis, he also blesses HaMotzi and Birkas HaMazon over it, and automatically he may also eat the other smaller pieces.
A kezayis. Meaning, in the slice itself over which he blesses there was a kezayis beforehand, and it was not reduced from this measure through the cooking. We do not combine with it what stuck to it from its companions or what swelled through the liquid.
Even though it does not have, etc. For since they contain a kezayis, the name bread is not removed from it.
That appears, etc. Since it was cooked, it is not considered the form of bread. If he did not cook them in a pot but fried them in liquid in a pan, it is implied from the Magen Avraham, seif katan 36, that this is not like cooking, and the form of bread helps for it, as below in the second form. According to the view of the other Acharonim there, there is no proof for his words. The proper practice is that when it was fried and it has the form of bread, one should eat it only within the meal. All this is where they do not contain a kezayis, but if he fried slices that contain a kezayis, even if the form of bread disappeared, it is obvious that he blesses HaMotzi, as above regarding cooking, and all the more so here.
He does not bless, etc. Know that in all these cases mentioned in this seif and in the following seifim, even if he established his meal on them and ate to satiety, he blesses Borei Minei Mezonos and afterward a blessing resembling three, because they are not in the category of bread at all, but only like ordinary cooked dishes.
If there are in the slices. Meaning, even in one of them, as above.
If they contain, etc. The reason this is preferable to the first form is that since it was not cooked, its bread-form is significant, for it is more recognizable and known. What he wrote, and Birkas HaMazon, refers to where he ate several crumbs up to the measure of a kezayis. If one crumbled bread until he returned it to semolina, and afterward kneaded it again with fat and the like, which in our language we call kneidlach or chremslach, see what the Magen Avraham wrote about this. The agreement of many Acharonim is that if he cooked them in a pot or fried them in a pan with liquid, which is considered like cooking, even if each one contains a kezayis or more, he blesses Borei Minei Mezonos over them, even if he ate a lot; and so is the practice of the world. If he baked them, it is proper not to eat them except within the meal, unless it was kneaded with mostly fat or honey, in which case he blesses Borei Minei Mezonos over them, as above in seif 7 in the gloss. If he kneaded them with water alone and baked them, they have the law of actual bread.
And not the form of bread. This means that because it is crumbled very finely, like semolina, the form of bread is not recognizable upon it. The reason is that since it is bread by itself and is not joined, it never leaves the category of bread. See the Acharonim, who agreed that even if one soaked the crumbs in water, since they did not join together, it does not leave the category of bread. This is specifically if he soaked them for a short time; but if the crumbs remained in it until the water became whitened through them, it is explained in the following seif that he blesses Borei Minei Mezonos over it and a blessing resembling three.
