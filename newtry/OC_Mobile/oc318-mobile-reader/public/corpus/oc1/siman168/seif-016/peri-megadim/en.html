And even. See Taz. Rabbeinu Tam's view is stated in Tosafos, Pesachim 37b, s.v. dekulei alma. Thick dough is obligated in challah immediately upon kneading even if one intends to cook it in a pot, because Scripture says, “your doughs.” Likewise, thin batter that is baked in an oven is obligated because Scripture calls it “bread.” Regarding HaMotzi, thick dough prepared with the intention of cooking in a pan, whether with liquid or much oil, is considered bread so long as it retains the appearance of bread. This differs from seif 10, where cooking in a pot is not considered to retain the appearance of bread, unlike frying in a pan; see Magen Avraham seifim katanim 31 and 36. One therefore recites HaMotzi. Rabbeinu Tam proves this from Berachos 37b, which speaks of one standing and offering meal-offerings, implying that even a pan-offering or deep-pan offering, which one intended to fry in much oil, requires HaMotzi. The Rosh, however, in the first chapter of Challah, mishnah 5, holds that dough is obligated in challah only if it was prepared with the intention of baking, and one later changed his mind. Even then, regarding HaMotzi, if one intended to bake it but later fried it in a pan with much oil or liquid, he recites Borei Minei Mezonos. The passage concerning meal-offerings refers to an oven-baked offering. The statement that one ground, baked, and cooked it and still recites HaMotzi when an intact slice remains, in Berachos 37a, follows Rabbi Meir, who holds that cooking does not nullify baking, whereas we rule like Rabbi Yose that it does. So writes the Rosh, and he writes similarly in Pesachim. It is explicit that even if one intended to bake the dough and actually baked it, subsequent frying in a pan nullifies the baking. The statement that food prepared in a pan is obligated applies where there is only a small amount of oil, as stated in seif 14 in the gloss. The Tur writes here that thick dough is obligated in challah, and that one recites HaMotzi where it retains the appearance of bread. Beit Yosef objects that in Yoreh Deah 329 the Tur rules like Rabbeinu Shimshon that the dough is obligated only when one intends to bake it. Bach answers that here too the Tur speaks of dough intended for baking, where Rabbeinu Shimshon agrees that it is obligated in challah and requires HaMotzi. This cannot be correct, as explained above. Perishah likewise writes that according to the Rosh, even dough intended for baking is obligated in challah but does not require HaMotzi, as is evident from the Rosh's statement concerning one who ground, baked, and then cooked it. Taz writes that the Mechaber's ruling here is understandable: although it is obligated in challah, that is because it was intended for baking, as in Yoreh Deah 329:13. In regard to HaMotzi, however, one does not recite it, following Rabbeinu Shimshon in the first chapter of Challah. This remains true even though according to our ruling in seif 10, where a kezayis remains one recites HaMotzi because cooking does not nullify baking. We distinguish the case of one who roasted the korban and then cooked it, and the matzah of siman 461, because the taste of matzah is required, as I wrote in seif katan 12 and in Magen Avraham seif katan 25. Nevertheless, we rule that one does not recite HaMotzi over dough fried in a pan. Seif 10 is different because it was initially full-fledged bread. See Magen Avraham seif katan 31, where with Hashem's help this will be explained. Beit Yosef's objection to the Tur remains, and one cannot answer as Bach does that the case is one in which the dough was intended for baking, for the Tur nevertheless rules unlike Rabbeinu Shimshon, as explained. Yet I cannot interpret Taz's words accordingly, since he connects the law of challah with HaMotzi. One might suggest that according to Rabbeinu Shimshon one indeed does not recite HaMotzi, while the Tur disagrees with him on that point. But then what is the difficulty in connecting challah with HaMotzi? According to Rabbeinu Tam too the laws are not fully identical, since challah is required from the time of kneading even without the appearance of bread, while HaMotzi requires the appearance of bread. One could then answer like Bach. In conclusion, I, the humble writer, have not fully understood Taz's intention here. See what I will write, with Hashem's help, in Magen Avraham seif katan 31; there is no need to elaborate further here.
