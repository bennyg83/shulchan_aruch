Even coarse bread, etc. This is the language of the Semak, siman 151: even if it is sourdough bread. See Nachalas Tzvi, who wrote at length to rule that if the piece is better and larger than the whole loaf, he should place the piece beneath the whole loaf, as with a piece of wheat bread. So it also seems in my humble opinion from Tosafot, Berachos, folio 39, s.v. kol, etc., who wrote as follows: why should he bless on the large one, since they are not as clean as this one, end quote. This implies that if it is clean, he blesses on the larger piece. However, this requires study, for if so the words of Tosafot would be contradictory, since in s.v. aval, etc., it implies like the ruling of the Shulchan Aruch and Levush. I then found it explicitly in Piskei Tosafot, as follows: if wheat bread and barley bread are whole, or both are of one species and one is better than the other, end quote. Thus, he places the piece beneath the whole loaf, as with a piece of wheat bread, and this requires study. Nevertheless, as a matter of law it is proper to do so.
Wheat, etc. See seif 4, that spelt is not included, according to Malbushei Yom Tov. The Perishah wrote: it seems that rye bread, like most of our bread, and spelt bread, the spelt bread takes precedence, for spelt is a type of wheat and rye is a type of barley; the same applies to the others, end quote. See Yoreh Deah, siman 324. The Magen Avraham wrote: it seems to me that if the rye is whole, he need not place it inside the piece, end quote. To me it seems that even if it is spelt, the law is the same, for only with whole barley does he place it; see below, seif 4.
He places the piece, etc. The Magen Avraham, the Shelah, the Bach, and all the later authorities wrote in the name of the Maharshal that this is specifically when one eats alone; but if two are eating, one should bless on the piece of wheat bread and one on the whole barley bread, end quote. The Magen Avraham wondered: all agree that the piece of wheat bread is preferable, but we say that a God-fearing person should fulfill both views. If so, how can one bless on the barley and leave the wheat? end quote. In my humble opinion, it is implied from the Ashri that according to R. Zalman there is a dispute, and this is what the Levush wrote, that they are equivalent; so it also implies in the Bach. What he challenged from siman 298, seif 4, that even a beis midrash is cancelled so that one person should bless for all, is not difficult at all, for HaMotzi is different, since it requires joining together, as above in siman 166. Even regarding a guest, it seems to me that the law is the same, and so it implies in the Bach, unlike the Magen Avraham. However, when both are of one species, the Bach wrote that one should not do this, since we hold that whole takes precedence. See what I wrote above regarding a case where the piece is cleaner than the whole loaf.
