(Seif katan 1) Coarse and small bread, at first glance it implies from the Tur, etc. The Tur's language differs from that of the Shulchan Aruch, for the Tur writes that one blesses on the whole loaf even if it is coarse, while the pieces are clean bread, and even if the whole loaf is smaller than the pieces. Since he did not combine them and say, 'even if it is coarse and small,' as the Shulchan Aruch writes, one might say the Tur meant either one deficiency only: either it is coarse while equal in size, or it is small while equal in cleanness. But from the Gemara it appears otherwise, for it concludes, 'but slices,' etc., and speaks of large slices, since this is the case of the dispute. The Magen Avraham's proof is that, according to Rabbeinu Tam, the dispute of Rav Huna and Rabbi Yochanan is about large pieces against whole loaves; therefore the later case of a wheat slice and a barley whole loaf must also involve a larger slice. If so, the slice has two advantages, being wheat and large, yet whole barley would have been preferable were it not that wheat has precedence in the verse. Tosafot, however, also give other reasons to reject Rashi's explanation, and from the Rosh it appears that the wheat slice is smaller than the whole loaf, so the Magen Avraham's words require explanation. Still, Tosafot's wording, that even if the pieces are of clean bread and the whole loaves are coarse bread, one blesses on the whole when they are of one type, supports the Shulchan Aruch, and this seems to be the Magen Avraham's intent, though there may be a textual error in his wording.
