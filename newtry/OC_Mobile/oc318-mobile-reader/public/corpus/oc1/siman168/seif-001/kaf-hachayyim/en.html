1) [Seif 1] He recites the blessing over the whole loaf, etc. Some say that he recites the blessing over the piece because it is cleaner bread. Rabbeinu Yonah and Kol Bo. Some say that he places the piece inside the whole loaf and breaks bread from them. Rashi and the Ra'avad, as written in the Beit Yosef; see there. Sefer Kovetz on the Rambam, Hilchos Berachos, chapter 8, writes the same, and Pis'chei Teshuvah cites it. Eliyah Rabbah, paragraph 1, likewise writes this in the name of HaNetz; see there. Most poskim, however, agree with Maran, of blessed memory, that one should recite the blessing solely over the whole loaf. Levush; Taz, paragraph 1; Magen Avraham, paragraph 1; Maamar Mordechai, paragraph 2; Shulchan Aruch HaRav, paragraph 1; Chayei Adam, klal 42, paragraph 5; Beis Menuchah, paragraph 1; Derech HaChaim, paragraph 1; Kitzur Shulchan Aruch, siman 41, paragraph 8; Mishnah Berurah, paragraph 2.
2) There: He recites the blessing over the whole loaf, etc. If one took a piece of bread in order to recite the blessing over it and meanwhile a whole loaf was brought to him, he should place the piece inside the whole loaf and recite the blessing. If he already recited the blessing over the piece, his hands should break that piece. Mahari Molcho in a handwritten responsum, siman 84; Birkei Yosef, paragraph 1; Shaarei Teshuvah, paragraph 1. It appears to me that the instruction to place the piece inside the whole loaf and recite the blessing applies specifically if he already began the blessing over the piece. If he had not yet begun the blessing when the whole loaf was brought, he should recite the blessing over the whole loaf alone.
3) There: He recites the blessing over the whole loaf, etc. Even if the piece has many advantages, being cleaner, larger, and Jewish-baked bread, while the whole loaf is non-Jewish bread, if he is not careful to refrain from non-Jewish bread, the whole loaf takes precedence. Peri Megadim, Mishbetzos Zahav, paragraph 1. See below, seif 5.
4) There: He places the piece beneath the whole loaf, etc. Even if the piece of wheat bread is larger than the whole loaf of barley bread, he nevertheless places both together. Peri Megadim, Mishbetzos Zahav, paragraph 2; Mishnah Berurah, paragraph 4.
5) There: He places the piece beneath the whole loaf, etc. It appears that this law applies specifically to one eating alone, as implied by the wording, 'If pieces were before him.' If two people are eating, it would be preferable for one to recite the blessing over the pieces of wheat bread and the other over the whole barley loaves. Maharshal writes this and says that he saw this practice. When both are of the same species, however, they should not do so, since we rule that a whole loaf takes precedence. Bach. Mateh Moshe, siman 284; Shiyurei Knesset HaGedolah, Hagahot Beit Yosef, paragraph 1; Olas Tamid, paragraph 1; Ateres Zekeinim, paragraph 1, write the same. Magen Avraham, paragraph 2, however, questions the words of Maharshal and Bach and leaves them requiring further study; see there. Erech HaShulchan, paragraph 1, likewise concludes that one should not separately bless over the whole loaf, unlike Eliyah Rabbah, paragraph 3; see there. Therefore, since some question this ruling and Maharshal and Bach did not state it as an obligation, but only as the preferable practice, one person should recite the blessing over both, as written in the Shulchan Aruch. If they are a host and guest, the host first breaks from both for himself, and then again breaks from both for the guest. If both are hosts, or both are guests, and each recites his own blessing, with a piece and whole loaf before each one, each breaks from both the whole loaf and the piece. If one has a whole barley loaf and the other has a piece of wheat bread, each recites the blessing over his own bread.
6) There: He places the piece beneath the whole loaf, etc. If one is rye bread, he need not place it together with the piece, because rye is not one of the seven species. This is all the more true if the wheat bread is Jewish-baked and the rye bread is non-Jewish bread. Magen Avraham, paragraph 2. The same applies if one is spelt bread. He need not place it together with the piece, for specifically a whole barley loaf is placed with it. Eliyah Rabbah, paragraph 2; Eshel Avraham, paragraph 2; Chayei Adam, klal 42, paragraph 7; Derech HaChaim, paragraph 3. It appears that the same applies when the piece is barley and the whole loaf is rye or spelt. He recites the blessing over the piece of barley bread alone, since it is one of the seven species. Derech HaChaim there and Mishnah Berurah, paragraph 3, write the same.
7) There, in the gloss: All this applies when he wishes to eat from both, etc. The Rama writes the same below in siman 211, seif 5, in the gloss; see there. The later authorities agree.
8) There, in the gloss: One need not be concerned for the other, etc. Even though the other bread is before him and is mentioned earlier in the verse, is preferred, or is more significant, he is not obligated to eat from it merely because of this precedence. Taz, paragraph 3. Seif 5 states that he removes the non-Jewish bread because he wishes to eat it but refrains only as an act of piety, and therefore removes it. Eliyah Rabbah, paragraph 10; Peri Megadim, Mishbetzos Zahav, paragraph 3.
