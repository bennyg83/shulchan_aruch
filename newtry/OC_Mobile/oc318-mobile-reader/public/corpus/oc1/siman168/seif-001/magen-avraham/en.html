Coarse and small bread. At first glance, the Tur implies that when the pieces have two advantages one does not bless on the whole loaf; see there. But from the Gemara it appears as written in the Shulchan Aruch, for it concludes, 'but slices,' etc., and it is speaking of slices larger than the whole loaf, since that is the case of the dispute. Only wheat is different, because the verse gives it precedence. But here, where they are of one type and the pieces are clean and large, even so the whole loaf is preferable.
He places the slice. If another person is reclining with him, one should bless on the whole loaf and one on the slice. Mateh Moshe, Bach, and Shelah in the name of Maharshal. This requires study, for how can we make a compromise not mentioned in the Gemara? Even the beis midrash is suspended so that one should bless for everyone because 'in a multitude of people is the King's glory,' as written in siman 298, seif 14. Moreover, there is no dispute here, for all agree that the wheat slice is preferable, only that we say a G-d-fearing person satisfies both breads. If so, how can one bless on the barley and leave the wheat? This requires study. It appears to me that at least if one of them is a guest, the host should break bread on both, for he is permitted to eat bread of a non-Jew because of this, as written in seif 5. It further appears to me that if one is rye, he need not place it inside the slice, as written in seif 4, since it is not of the seven species, and certainly if the wheat is Jewish bread and the rye is bread of a non-Jew, for according to Rashi and those who agree with him one need not place a slice inside the whole loaf at all, and we should not add further.
