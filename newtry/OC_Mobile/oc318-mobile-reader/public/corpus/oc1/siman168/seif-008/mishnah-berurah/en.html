The Acharonim wrote that this refers to what in Russian is called nalisilke, where flour is mixed with a great deal of water in a pot like porridge, poured onto vegetable leaves, and baked in an oven with the leaves. The same applies when they are baked on a pan without oil. This is specifically with those that are very thin and soft, but if they are not so soft and thin, it is explained in seif 14 that the law of bread applies to it and one blesses HaMotzi. Magen Avraham.
And if he established, etc. The poskim wrote that those called valaplatkes in Prague, which are also made from a soft batter, but because they spread out during baking they become much thinner and slighter than the nalisilkes mentioned above, one should not bless HaMotzi on them even if he established a meal on them, for they do not have the status of bread at all, and they resemble terita in seif 15.
His meal on them, etc. What is called establishing a meal is explained above in seif 6 regarding pas kisnin, and the same applies here.
And if he eats them, etc. Meaning these soft and thin ones. The same applies to all pas haba'ah b'kisnin explained in seif 7. Magen Avraham. See Biur Halacha, where several Acharonim disagree with him.
Not because of the meal. Meaning, he did not eat them to satisfy his hunger, but only for dessert and sweetness. For if he ate them to fill his hunger, even if he ate only a little of them, they are included in the meal and do not require a blessing.
They require a blessing. Since he does not eat this for the sake of the meal, it is not exempted by the blessing of HaMotzi. If he eats a large amount, the measure of a fixed meal explained in seif 6, even if he ate them merely for sweetness, they are exempted by the blessing of HaMotzi, for since if he ate them outside the meal he would need to bless HaMotzi, automatically within the meal they are exempted by HaMotzi.
Before them. All this is regarding forms of bread. But regarding cooked grain dishes that always come for nourishment and as cooked food, they come because of the meal, as explained in siman 177. Even if they are filled with fruit, like those called apple fladen and the like, the fruit becomes a cooked food inside the filling, the flour is primary, and the fruit is secondary to it. One should not be stringent to take a little of the apples from inside them and bless Borei Pri HaEtz, for that would be a blessing in vain. Acharonim.
But, etc. This refers to the main law of pas haba'ah b'kisnin, and it teaches that this is not included in it, for one does not bless over the dough at all because it is secondary to the preserve, as he explains.
They are secondary. For they do not intend to eat the wafers, and they make them only so that their hands not become dirty from the preserve. Therefore the dough is considered secondary, as below in siman 212. The Acharonim there wrote that in our countries, where preserves are placed on honey cakes, called lekach fladen, and the honey cakes are good to eat by themselves, their intent is also for eating the honey cakes; automatically they are primary, and he blesses Borei Minei Mezonos over them and exempts the preserve. It is obvious that this is specifically when they are baked together at the time of baking. But if he baked the honey cakes alone and afterward places the preserve on top of them, the preserve does not become secondary to them, for his intent is to eat both, and the preserve does not come to accompany the honey cakes, so he must also bless over the preserve.
Exempts them. If he ate the preserve on top and left the wafers and ate them by themselves, he must bless Borei Minei Mezonos over them. But if he ate them together and a little wafer without preserve remained, he need not bless over it, since the main manner of his eating was together, and it is considered secondary to the preserve; we are not concerned for the end of the eating.
