Ring-shaped crackers. See Taz. Even if they are full-fledged bread made from one of the five species of grain and kneaded only with water, since they are made solely to be nibbled for pleasure and are not ordinarily eaten in large quantities, the Sages did not impose the stringencies of a kezayis or kebeitzah. See Rashi, Berachos 38a, regarding ring-shaped crackers, and Beit Yosef here in the name of the Aruch, citing Rav Hai Gaon, on the verse, “and their bread for the journey was dry, it was dotted,” explaining that this refers to kisnin. See Beit Yosef there. Regarding the statement that whether or not one removes the filling he recites Borei Minei Mezonos, see what I will write, with Hashem's help, in seif katan 11. The term kisnin derives from kis, a pouch, as the Tur writes. See Rashi, Berachos 41b, s.v. pas haba'ah b'kisnin. With Hashem's help, this will be explained in siman 177.
