Flour and water. Its batter is very soft, as written in seif 8. Nevertheless, those baked in an iron mold, even though they are gathered in the mold and in Prague are called aflatkes, since after baking they are very thin wafers, even if one fixed a meal on them it is not considered a fixed meal, and he recites Borei Minei Mezonos. Shelah, Bach.
And even if he fixed. In the Tur it is written that if he fixed a meal on it, he recites HaMotzi, and the Beit Yosef disagrees with him. It appears to me that the law follows the Tur, for the Rosh, Rambam, and Rif did not distinguish regarding HaMotzi, and they were lenient regarding trisa only concerning challah, that it is not called bread because it is poured; but after baking it is complete bread if he fixed a meal on it. It is comparable to dough kneaded with fruit juice, which according to the Rosh is exempt from challah, yet if he fixed a meal on it he recites HaMotzi. The Beit Yosef copied the words of Rabbeinu Yerucham incorrectly. Rabbeinu Yerucham wrote: on truknin one recites Borei Minei Mezonos, and if one fixed a meal on them, he recites HaMotzi, for it is like something whose beginning is sufganin and whose end is sufganin, which is exempt from challah and requires Borei Minei Mezonos, but trisa is exempt from challah and one recites Borei Minei Mezonos. Plainly this is a textual error, for an explicit Gemara says that truknin is obligated, and so wrote Rabbeinu Yerucham in the laws of challah. Rather, it should read: something whose beginning is sufganin and whose end is dough is obligated in challah, but something whose beginning is sufganin and whose end is sufganin is exempt from challah, etc. So it is explicit in the Rosh, and it is known that Rabbeinu Yerucham was his student and copied his words as they were. If so, there is no distinction between truknin and trisa except regarding challah, but regarding the blessing they are the same, as appears to me. Analyze and you will find it so, especially if he eats it to satiety, for that is a doubt according to the Torah, as written above.
