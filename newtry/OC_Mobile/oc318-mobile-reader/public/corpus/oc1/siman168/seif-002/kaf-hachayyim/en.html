9) [Seif 2] He recites the blessing over the larger loaf. This applies specifically when both are equally fine. If the smaller loaf is clean bread and the larger is coarse bread, he recites the blessing over the smaller loaf. Eliyah Rabbah, paragraph 4, writes this in the name of HaNetz and brings a proof; see there. Erech HaShulchan, paragraph 2; Chayei Adam, klal 42, paragraph 7; Chesed LaAlafim, paragraph 1. The same applies if both are pieces and the smaller piece is cleaner than the larger. He recites the blessing over the smaller piece, as implied by Eliyah Rabbah there. Chesed LaAlafim there and Derech HaChaim, paragraph 1, write the same. See below, paragraph 20.
10) There: He should join them together with a piece of wood. If challah was removed from a loaf, one part in forty-eight or less, Minchas Yaakov at the end of the sefer, siman 12, writes that it is considered whole, as for an eruv in siman 366, seif 6. Eliyah Rabbah in siman 167, paragraph 2, however, writes that he proved in a responsum that its law is that of a piece; see there. Erech HaShulchan, paragraph 3. See above, siman 167, paragraph 12.
11) There: He should join them together, etc. Magen Avraham, paragraph 4, writes that perhaps on weekdays one need not join them at all, and this is the custom. Eliyah Rabbah, paragraph 5, however, writes that this does not appear correct. Eshel Avraham, paragraph 4, writes the same.
12) There: So that it will not appear, etc. If it is apparent that the pieces were joined, it is not considered whole, as written in siman 366, seif 6. Magen Avraham, paragraph 3; Pri Chadash, paragraph 2; Eshel Avraham, paragraph 3.
13) There: Even on Shabbos, etc. Although Shabbos requires lechem mishneh and whole loaves, this is considered whole. Magen Avraham, paragraph 4; Shulchan Aruch HaRav, paragraph 3. If one has a genuinely whole loaf, he should not rely upon this, for one can say that the joined loaf is considered whole only regarding an eruv chatzeiros. Shulchan Aruch HaRav there. Eliyah Rabbah, paragraph 5, writes the same.
14) There: Even on Shabbos, etc. When joining the pieces on Shabbos, one must take care not to use a piece of wood that is muktzeh. Magen Avraham there; Eliyah Rabbah, paragraph 5; Shulchan Aruch HaRav there. He may use a utensil whose primary use is prohibited, such as a tool used for knitting stockings, or a muktzeh needle, because a utensil whose primary use is prohibited may be moved when its body or place is needed. Eshel Avraham, paragraph 4.
15) There: Even on Shabbos, etc. If one's Shabbos loaf broke into several pieces, he may not join them with splinters in order to break bread from it. The novelty of the Shulchan Aruch's permission applies only to two pieces and no more. Rather, he should endeavor to obtain another whole loaf. Responsa Chasam Sofer, Orach Chayyim, siman 46; Orchos Chaim, paragraph 3.
