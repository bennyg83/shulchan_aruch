(Seif katan 2) He places, etc. This requires study, how we can make such a compromise. The compromise of the Gemara still has one person bless and discharge the other, preserving 'in a multitude of people is the King's glory.' How could Maharshal's compromise, that each blesses for himself, cancel that value? Moreover, there is no dispute here. According to Rabbeinu Tam, everyone agrees that the wheat slice takes precedence; the G-d-fearing practice is only to satisfy both breads, so one who blesses on the whole barley loaf alone would be acting improperly.
It appears to me that at least if one of them is a guest, etc. Meaning that in such a case even Maharshal agrees.
For it is permitted to him, etc. This is because the host discharges the guest with his blessing, and it is as though the guest is blessing. Since the guest is not careful about bread of non-Jews, he may bless on the clean bread of the non-Jew. Therefore even the careful host was permitted to bless on the non-Jew's bread, and we do not say that the guest should bless on the clean non-Jewish bread and the host on the coarse Jewish bread. This shows that in such a case Maharshal's reasoning, that the guest follows the host, does not apply.
For according to Rashi and those who agree with him, etc. According to Rashi, Rav Huna and Rabbi Yochanan disagree where the pieces are larger than the whole loaf: Rav Huna requires blessing specifically on the slice, and Rabbi Yochanan specifically on the whole loaf. Therefore a G-d-fearing person satisfies both opinions in that case, where the slice is larger and both are of the same species. But where the slice is wheat and the whole loaf is barley, the Gemara concludes that everyone agrees he blesses on the slice, and the G-d-fearing compromise does not refer to that case. Although we rule like Rabbeinu Tam, who explains differently and says that in a case of one species and a larger slice, one who blesses on the whole loaf satisfies all opinions, Rabbeinu Tam applied the G-d-fearing solution to the wheat slice only because that is stated explicitly, and we do not add further cases.
