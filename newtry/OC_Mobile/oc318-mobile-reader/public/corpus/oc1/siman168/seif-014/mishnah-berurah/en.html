That afterward he baked it in an oven. The same applies if he baked it in an ilpas, as in the Gemara, without liquid.
It is actual bread. So that you should not say that since he initially scalded it, it is not in the category of bread; this teaches us otherwise.
And he blesses HaMotzi over it. Even if its kneading was soft, if he scalded it in boiling water and afterward baked it in an oven, he blesses HaMotzi. Magen Avraham in the name of the Tur. The Magen Avraham further brought that even if he fried it in oil and afterward baked it, he blesses HaMotzi, and it is evident there from his words that he holds that even if he did not establish a meal on it he blesses HaMotzi. However, the Taz and many other Acharonim agreed with him that frying in honey or oil and the like makes it like pas haba'ah b'kisnin, just as if it was kneaded with these kinds, and when he did not establish a meal he blesses Borei Minei Mezonos and Al HaMichyah. However, know that it is not called pas kisnin unless he fried it in so much oil that the oil is primary in taste relative to the flour, as the Rama wrote in the rule of pas kisnin in seif 7; see there in the Mishnah Berurah.
And he blesses HaMotzi over it. Even when he did not establish a meal on it. See the Magen Avraham above in seif 8, that this is specifically if they were not extremely thin, for otherwise it is included in pas kisnin. See what we wrote there.
And fried it in liquid. Meaning, he did not bake it on an oven, but in an ilpas with liquid. Therefore, according to all opinions, even according to the view of those who disagree above, it is not bread, since the name dough never took effect upon it.
