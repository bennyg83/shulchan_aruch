Whichever of them. If someone is reclining with him, each one should break bread on one loaf; see seif katan 1, what I wrote. The Bach wrote that one who is not careful should remove pas palter of non-Jews, while one who is careful need not remove it, as is explicit in the Mordechai, and so is the view of Tosafot. In the Agudah it is written: he blesses on whichever he wants if he is not careful, and some say even if he is careful; Rabbeinu Shimshon was accustomed to remove the white bread of a non-Jew. This implies specifically because he was careful, since there is a dispute, he would remove it; but if he is not careful, since the Yerushalmi states explicitly that he blesses on whichever he wants, he need not remove it. See Beit Yosef in the name of Terumas HaDeshen in the name of the Mordechai, chapter Kol Sha'ah, that the Yerushalmi refers to both cases, even one who is careful; see there. Nevertheless, it implies there that even when he was not careful, Rabbeinu Shimshon would remove it. Perhaps the Beit Yosef did not want to rule like him in this, since the Yerushalmi explicitly mentions that he blesses on whichever of them he wants.

He should break from the fine bread. Meaning, if he wants to eat it, he should bless on it, for his soul desires it, and he refrains only out of pious separation. Therefore, if he can find a basis to eat it permissibly and need not practice that separation, such as here, where he cherishes the mitzvah, the Sages permitted it to him. Terumas HaDeshen. See Yoreh Deah, siman 112, where they discuss this at length.
