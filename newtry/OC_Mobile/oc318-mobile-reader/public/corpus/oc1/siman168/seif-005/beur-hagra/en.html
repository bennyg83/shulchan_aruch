Seif 5: Bread, etc. The Yerushalmi, cited by Tosafos and the other poskim, states: If there is clean but impure bread and coarse but pure bread, Rabbi Chiya said in the name of Rabbi Acha that one may recite the blessing over whichever he wishes. Tosafos write that the same applies in an analogous case. Mordechai writes that this applies where one is not careful to avoid non-Jewish bread, and Tosafos, Mordechai, and the other poskim explain the Yerushalmi similarly. However, the minister of Coucy instructed that it be removed. The Rosh ruled like the first opinion, and the Shulchan Aruch rules accordingly where one is careful. He holds that the minister of Coucy was certainly careful, and therefore did not act in accordance with the Yerushalmi. See Magen Avraham and Taz.
And if, etc.; and one does not, etc., need, etc. As written in seif 1 in the gloss.
And if the homeowner is careful, etc. He holds that the Yerushalmi cited above discusses one who is careful. This is the meaning of, “He should break bread,” etc.: the choice is in his hands if he wishes. The permissibility of eating it is what is taught here. See Magen Avraham.
And since, etc. As stated in Yevamos 7a, “Since it was permitted,” etc.; and at the beginning of chapter 4 of Eruvin, “Once it entered, it entered.” One may do so even initially, as stated there: “If he is wise,” etc.
Specifically, etc. As written in seif 1 in the gloss; and in Arvei Pesachim, “When bread was dear to him,” etc.
