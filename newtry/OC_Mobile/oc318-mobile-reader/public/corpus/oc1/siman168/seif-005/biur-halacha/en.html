The head of the household should break bread, etc. Know that although Terumas HaDeshen, from whom the Mechaber derived this ruling, implies that even without concern for the guest's honor they permitted bread baked by a non-Jew when it is cleaner and finer and lies before him, because of the desirability of the blessing, I did not reproduce that ruling because many later authorities strongly question it. See Bach, Taz, and Shach in Yoreh Deah, siman 112. The Gra, seif katan 12, also implies that this ruling contradicts the Mechaber's view at the beginning of the seif, where he rules that one who is careful must remove it. Beis Meir questions it even more strongly; see there. According to his words, one must first obtain release from the practice, as with any mitzvah practice one adopted without originally stipulating that it was without a vow. Peri Megadim also notes this point. According to them, even having a guest reclining with him would not help. The Gra likewise somewhat implies that he does not accept the distinction that the presence of a guest is different, for otherwise he would not have needed to explain that the Yerushalmi cited above concerns one who is careful; see there. In any event, without the reason of honoring the guest, one certainly should not be lenient, and this is the conclusion of Eliyah Rabbah. Know also that Shach in Yoreh Deah, siman 112, gives the reason as avoidance of ill will. This would appear to imply that the same law applies even when the host does not discharge the guest through his blessing, unlike Machatzis HaShekel, whose words we reproduced in the Mishnah Berurah. According to the Taz's explanation of the reason, however, as we wrote in the Mishnah Berurah, there is room for Machatzis HaShekel's view. The wording of the Shulchan Aruch here likewise accords with the Taz, for it says, “Since the mitzvah rests upon the head of the household,” etc.
