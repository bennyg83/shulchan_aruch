Clean bread of a non-Jew, etc. But if they are equal, it is obvious that Jewish bread takes precedence for the blessing. Eliyah Rabbah.
Over whichever of them he wants. For each one has an advantage: this one is Jewish bread, for non-Jewish bread was permitted only under pressure, and this one is clean.
He removes it, etc. This law derives from the words of the Sar of Coucy, who was concerned that if it is lying on the table, at the time of the blessing it will be dear to him because it is clean, and perhaps because of this he would need to bless over it. Many Acharonim question this greatly, for since he is careful and treats this as forbidden, what does its dearness help? Nevertheless, initially it is good to be concerned for his words and remove it.
And his intent is not, etc. Meaning, although at the beginning of the seif it wrote that he blesses over whichever he wants, that is where he intends to eat the meal from both of them. But this one, whose intent is not to eat at all except the bread of a non-Jew, need not bless over another bread, even though it is more significant, as above in seif 1 in the gloss. And although he discharges the members of his household with the blessing of HaMotzi, and they do not eat from this but only from kosher bread, nevertheless since he wants to eat only this, and he is the head of the household, he is primary and we follow him, and he blesses over the bread that he eats.
Upon the host. Meaning, to break bread and discharge others with his blessing, as they said: the host breaks bread so that he should break generously. Since the guest will eat from non-Jewish bread that is fine and clean, they permitted the host also to break over the non-Jewish bread because of the honor of the guest. But without a guest, it is not permitted at all for one who is careful about non-Jewish bread to bless over this bread; rather, he should remove it from the table, as above. This is the conclusion of the Shach and Taz in Yoreh Deah 112, see there. See Biur Halacha. Know that today, when the practice is that each person blesses HaMotzi for himself, seemingly this law does not apply at all; rather, each one should bless over the bread that he eats. Machatzit HaShekel. See Peri Megadim regarding what he noted on the main ruling of the Shulchan Aruch.
But if it is not dear, etc. Meaning, all this is when this bread is intrinsically dear to him and he refrains only because it is non-Jewish bread; here they permitted it because of the guests. But if this bread is not intrinsically dear to him and he does not desire it, he should bless over his kosher bread.
