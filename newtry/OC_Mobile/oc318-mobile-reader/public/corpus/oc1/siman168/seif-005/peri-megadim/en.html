If one is careful, etc. See Taz and Tosafos, Berachos 39b, s.v. aval. Regarding the doubt of the Sar of Coucy, it is possible that the distinction applies specifically to ritually impure and pure bread, where there is a clear preference because one may not eat the impure bread in purity, and so forth. Bread of a non-Jew, however, may perhaps be displaced only in favor of Jewish bread, so that an observer should not establish this as the halachah. He therefore instructed that it be removed, similar to what Magen Avraham writes in seif katan 9. Alternatively, he may have been uncertain whether one should prefer specifically Jewish bread or specifically the non-Jewish bread, since the prohibition of non-Jewish bread is more lenient, as stated in Yoreh Deah 112:15 in the gloss, and therefore it was necessary to remove one of them. It is also possible that the Mechaber's words, that one who is careful should remove the non-Jewish bread, refer back to the first case as well. Thus, whether he is generally careful or not, if he is eating Jewish bread he should remove the non-Jewish bread, lest it appear disgraced when he chooses the cleaner bread and leaves it aside. But if he is not careful and eats the non-Jewish bread, he need not remove the Jewish bread, for there is no disgrace in choosing the cleaner bread, and the superiority of Jewish bread is not evident from that choice. See Eliyah Rabbah on this. See also what I wrote in Magen Avraham, seif katan 9, and Taz here. See Yoreh Deah, Shach seif katan 21 and Taz seif katan 8, that when a guest reclines with him there may be hostility if he does not break bread over the non-Jewish loaf. It was therefore permitted to break bread over it and use it for the entire meal. This appears to be merely optional; see there. However, in seif 16 in the gloss and in Magen Avraham seif katan 12, we wrote that Terumas HaDeshen and Beit Yosef imply that it is a mitzvah to give it precedence.
Now, where one is not careful and the non-Jewish loaf is whole, even if the Jewish bread is cleaner and larger but consists of pieces, the whole loaf takes precedence over ten other advantages, as explained in seif 1 and Magen Avraham seif katan 1. If the non-Jewish loaf is wheat bread and the Jewish bread is barley bread, or the like, it appears that he should place both together, as in seif 1. It is implied there that even against several advantages, the advantage of the species mentioned earlier in Scripture is weighed equally. A God-fearing person should satisfy both views. See Levush, seif 1, regarding what he writes about equal considerations. If one is careful regarding non-Jewish bread, but that loaf is whole while the Jewish loaf is a piece, the matter requires study. It appears that a God-fearing person who wishes to satisfy both views should place them together but break bread only from the kosher loaf. Although some say he should break from both, that is impossible here, and there too some say he should break only from the piece. See Nechalas Tzvi and Eliyah Rabbah. I have written what appears correct to me, and there is no need to elaborate.

One may discuss a case in which one person is careful and another is not, while the third ate only a kezayis of a grain dish or vegetables, as in siman 197; see there. There is no concern of hostility unless most of those eating partake of non-Jewish bread, not where only one person does so. See Shach, Yoreh Deah 112, seif katan 21. See also Yoreh Deah 88, Shach seif katan 2, which implies that one who is careful regarding non-Jewish bread, especially where he accepted this by vow, may not place such bread upon the table at which he eats.
