With kisnin, etc. In the next seif it is explained what this is. Although with ordinary bread one blesses HaMotzi on any amount, and on a kezayis one blesses Birkas HaMazon, these are different, for people do not ordinarily establish their meal on them, but eat a little of them casually. Therefore their blessing is only Borei Minei Mezonos, and afterward a blessing resembling three, unless he ate the amount of a fixed meal, in which case he blesses HaMotzi and Birkas HaMazon, for in any event it is bread.
That others are accustomed to establish. The Acharonim wrote that if he ate it alone, he must eat an amount from which others are accustomed to be satisfied when eating it alone. If he ate it with meat or other things with which one eats bread, it is enough if he eats an amount from which others are accustomed to be satisfied when they also eat it with other things. However, if he ate it alone and was satisfied from it, even though others would not be satisfied from it alone, nevertheless, since if they ate it with other things they would also be satisfied from it, we do not say that his view is disregarded, and he must bless HaMotzi and Birkas HaMazon over it. Regarding eiruvei techumin, in siman 368 it states that the measure of a meal is three or four eggs, and several Acharonim wrote that the same applies here and this is considered the amount of a fixed meal. But several Acharonim, including the Gra, disagree and hold that one blesses HaMotzi and Birkas HaMazon only for the amount of a fixed meal, meaning the evening and morning meal, as the Beit Yosef wrote in the name of Shibbolei HaLeket. I also found in the Eshkol that it is evident that he holds this way, and the language of the Shulchan Aruch also leans more this way. Nevertheless, initially it is good to be concerned for the stringent view not to eat four eggs, and certainly where the item is one about which there is halachic doubt whether it is pas haba'ah b'kisnin; although in seif 7 it is explained that we are lenient in this, one should certainly be concerned for the stringent view mentioned above.

He blesses Birkas HaMazon over it. Since he completed the measure of a fixed meal. But he need not bless HaMotzi on what he decided afterward to eat, since what is before him after he decided does not contain the measure of a fixed meal, except together with what he already ate. If in truth what he has not yet eaten does contain the measure of a fixed meal, he must wash his hands, bless HaMotzi and Al Netilas Yadayim, and recite Birkas HaMazon.
