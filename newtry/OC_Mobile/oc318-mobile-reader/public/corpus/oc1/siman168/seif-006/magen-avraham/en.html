To fix on it. It appears to me that if he fixed his meal on it, even though he ate meat and other foods with it, and had he eaten it alone he would not have been satiated from it, nevertheless he recites HaMotzi and the three blessings. This also appears from the language of the Gemara, 'anything on which others fix a meal,' and somewhat from Tosafot, who wrote regarding small loaves: if he fixed a meal on them, as on Purim. In Semak it is written: on the night of Purim and on the night of a bris milah. Presumably on Purim they do not fix the meal on them alone. If he ate it alone, we require that he eat so much that others are accustomed to be satiated from it alone, for if we measure the amount people eat with other foods, you have made the matter variable. See the first chapter of Eruvin, where it is possible to measure, and this requires study. If he is satiated from it, and others are also accustomed to be satiated from it when they eat something with it, then even if he ate it alone he recites HaMotzi, since he was satiated from it, as appears to me.
He recites Birkas HaMazon on it. This implies that even though he reconsidered in the middle and decided to eat more, he need not recite HaMotzi, for the remaining portion does not contain the required measure. So wrote Rashach, part 1, siman 163. It is obvious that if, after eating a little, he reconsidered and decided to eat more, the amount of a fixed meal, he must wash and recite HaMotzi.
