The answer given is that here it concerns baking without liquid, where all agree that one recites Hamotzi. This is difficult, for the Shulchan Aruch concludes above in seif 7 that dough containing a filling has the law of pas haba'ah bekisnin, and there is no greater filling than that of a pie. I saw in Emek Berachah that he distinguishes between a filling of spices and a filling of meat or fish, because meat and fish are eaten as an accompaniment to bread and are therefore secondary to the bread, unlike fruit. This is not correct. The Beit Yosef cites this law in the name of Agur: “Although the meat, fish, or cheese is primary, one recites Hamotzi over it, because grain is significant.” He concludes that we follow the five species. This shows that meat and fish are not regarded as secondary, for otherwise there would have been no need for the reason of the importance of grain and the five species. It therefore appears to me that they did not explain this properly. Certainly this has the law of kisnin, and if one establishes a meal upon it he recites Hamotzi. The teaching here is that one might have thought that even if he establishes a meal upon this pie he should not recite Hamotzi because the meat is primary; it therefore teaches that this is not so. Although the Shulchan Aruch does not explicitly state that it concerns one who established a meal upon it, that is no difficulty. Had the Shulchan Aruch intended to teach that this does not have the law of kisnin and that there is a distinction between a meat filling and a spice filling, it should have taught this above in the laws of kisnin, where it stated that a filling creates the law of kisnin. It is therefore clear that there is no distinction. Just as meat and fish may accompany bread, so fruit may accompany bread, as stated in siman 177 concerning fruit eaten with bread. Here it is certainly pas haba'ah bekisnin and requires Hamotzi if one establishes a meal upon it. The teaching is that even according to the opinion cited immediately above, that an established meal is ineffective for certain fried items, here it is effective because this item has a connection to Hamotzi. My final conclusion is that frying in liquid produces the law of kisnin, as stated above, and the same applies to a filling of meat or fish. Rashi mentioned a filling of sugar and spices only because that is the ordinary case, while pies are ordinarily filled with meat and similar items. Even when baked in an oven, it is kisnin like any other pas haba'ah bekisnin baked in an oven.
