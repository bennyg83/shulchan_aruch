With meat or with fish. And the like, even if he did not fix a meal on it, for these do not nullify the category of bread, since they themselves are nourishing; see the beginning of siman 177. This is unlike fruit, which is dessert; see seif 7. So too it appears from Shiltei Gibborim, and so wrote the Shelah. This requires study, for the Tanya wrote regarding it: specifically when he fixed his meal on it; see there. If so, its law is like pas haba'ah b'kisnin, and this requires study. So too it appears from the plain wording of the poskim. However, Shibolei HaLeket wrote plainly that the five species are primary, as with cooked grain dishes; see there. It is possible that anything filled with fruit also has this law. It appears to me that the same applies to dough kneaded with egg water without fat and baked: it is complete bread, for eggs are also nourishing and do not nullify it from the category of bread. So too it appears in Beitzah 16 from Tosafot, s.v. kimcha ikar. From here comes the custom to take non-Jewish bread called knevait and oblias, for the flour is primary, it is thick, and it is oven-baked, etc. From their being kneaded, since one does not recite HaMotzi on them, there is a concern of bishul akum, etc. This indicates that bread kneaded with eggs requires HaMotzi. See Beit Yosef in Yoreh Deah, siman 113. However, Shibolei HaLeket wrote that when it is kneaded with eggs it is like being kneaded with honey. Principles of this siman: 1. dough kneaded with fruit juice and afterward baked, in seif 7; 2. if fried in fruit juice or cooked in water, in seif 13; 3. if filled with fruit, in seif 7; 4. if filled with meat and fish, in seif 16, and all this is with thick kneading; 5. soft batter, in seif 8, seif 14, and seif 15; 6. complete bread that one soaked and afterward cooked, in seif 10.
