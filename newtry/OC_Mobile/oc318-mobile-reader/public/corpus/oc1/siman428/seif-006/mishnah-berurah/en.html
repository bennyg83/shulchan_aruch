(1) One does not stop at them - and this authority I will read Damsli Dativ Moser 4 my sons do not get tired and do not stop at his rebuke, and if it was that the reader stops at them and they had to happen otherwise, it would seem like this is the end of the rebuke of the Shi'i:
(ב) And they begin, etc. — Chazal said the Holy One said it is not proper that the children bless Me for their troubles; rather one reads all, begins one matter and ends one matter, and can bless beginning and end; for this alone it would suffice to begin one verse before the rebuke and one after; but since we hold one does not begin a parashah with fewer than three verses and does not end adjacent to a parashah with fewer than three, therefore one must begin three before the rebuke and three after (and in BeChukotai there are three before the end of the parashah; in Ki Tavo one verse suffices — "Eileh divrei haberit" ends that parashah).
(ג) In the verses before them — and where the practice is that the rav av beit din is third, even in BeChukotai — if so, nothing remains to begin three before reading the rebuke — one should begin three retroactively; but correct that the rav waive honor and go up fourth that Shabbat. Maharil was strict about one going up for rebuke — specifically the shamash hired for this there is no strictness; nevertheless if they called him he must go up (lest shortening life — but no evil befalls one who goes up for honor of Torah). In many places the shaliach tzibur who reads goes up himself — correct; even if he is a kohen he may read from beginning of BeChukotai until after the rebuke and the other readers finish from there; in Ki Tavo he shortens so readers complete until there and he is last — see Biur Halacha.
(ד) One may interrupt in them — they are not as severe, stated only in singular and Moshe said them from himself [though all was by divine command as "these are the words of the covenant" — nevertheless when he said them it was from himself]; unlike Torat Kohanim where the utterances were from the Almighty speaking from Moshe's throat — "I will send," "I will appoint," "I will cast" — One who can do so.
(ה) Whoever wishes — lest one not want to go up when called and come to despise rebukes — see Biur Halacha.
