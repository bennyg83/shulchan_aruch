(א) Shim'u — if he erred and read as haftarah on the first Shabbat the parashah of the day, he reads on the coming Shabbat Divrei Yirmiyahu; also Shim'u because adjacent — Magen Avraham in name of Tzitz Eliezer. What chumashim write haftarah Vayishlach Vayivrach Yaakov is error — in Vayetzei they read from Vayivrach until end of Hoshea, then verses from Yoel "Ve'achaltem achol" etc. "Veyda'tem" etc., because one must end with good matter; in Hoshea it ends "ufosh'im yikashlu bam" — and in Vayishlach they read from "Ve'ami telu'im" and part of Vayivrach Yaakov until "Umoshi'a ein zulato" — per Gra they read in Vayishlach Chazon Ovadyah — Chayei Adam.
(ב) They always read Shuva — because in Hoshea that haftarah ends "ufosh'im yikashlu bam" and one does not end on that — therefore they add verses. Some write they add Micha "Mi Kel kamocha" etc.; some add Yoel "Tek'u shofar" etc.; in Avodat HaRav Prague they added Micha and Yoel; Dagul Merevavah ruled one need not add both — rather when reading Vayelech on Shabbat Shuva one ends with Micha [parallel to Vayelech "vacharah afi" and Micha "lo hachzik la'ad apo"]; when reading Haazinu on Shabbat Shuva one ends with Yoel [parallel "ya'arof kamatar likchi" and "veyored lachem geshem morah"]. See Shaarei Teshuvah — all per custom.

(ד) They read Shuva — proper to cry out to man both before judgment and after judgment.
(ה) Which is the haftarah of Acharei Mot — because the haftarah of the second parashah mentions Jerusalem's abomination; not so when separate, for they already read Halo kevnei Kushiyim in Acharei Mot and must read judgment in Kedoshim. Levush disagrees with Rama: even when combined they read last haftarah of Kedoshim; but Bach and Shakh wrote the custom spread in all communities like Rama; likewise if Shabbat was Acharei Mot and erev Rosh Chodesh they read Machar Chodesh — on Shabbat of Kedoshim they read Halo kevnei Kushiyim — Chayei Adam.
