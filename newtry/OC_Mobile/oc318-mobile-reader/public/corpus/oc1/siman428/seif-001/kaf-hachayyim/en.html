1) [Se'if 1] These are the days on which they are not fixed, etc. The general principle is that care must always be taken that Yom Kippur not occur on Sunday or Friday, so that two sacred days will not be adjacent, and that Hoshana Rabbah not occur on Shabbos, so that the mitzvah of the aravah will not be displaced. Magen Avraham, beginning of the siman. Regarding the statement that Yom Kippur should not occur on Sunday, etc., a reason for this is given in Rosh Hashanah 20a. See there.
2) Rosh Hashanah does not occur on Sunday, Wednesday, or Friday. If Rosh Hashanah occurred on Sunday, Hoshana Rabbah would occur on Shabbos. If it occurred on Wednesday, Yom Kippur would occur on Friday. If it occurred on Friday, Yom Kippur would occur on Sunday. This is impossible, and therefore Rosh Hashanah does not occur on Sunday, Wednesday, or Friday. Once this rule is stated, it follows automatically that Yom Kippur does not occur on Tuesday, Friday, or Sunday, for if Yom Kippur occurred on Tuesday, Hoshana Rabbah would occur on Shabbos.
3) There. Purim does not occur on Shabbos, Monday, or Wednesday, and Pesach does not occur on Monday, Wednesday, or Friday, etc. Once it is stated that Purim does not occur on Shabbos, Monday, or Wednesday, it follows automatically that Pesach does not occur on Monday, Wednesday, or Friday. Ordinary Purim is on the fourteenth of Adar. If Purim occurred on Friday, Pesach would occur on Monday; if Purim occurred on Monday, Pesach would occur on Wednesday; and if Purim occurred on Wednesday, Pesach would occur on Friday. Since Purim does not occur on Shabbos, Monday, or Wednesday, Pesach consequently does not occur on Monday, Wednesday, or Friday. Shavuos also does not occur on Tuesday, Thursday, or Shabbos, since Shavuos falls on the same weekday as the second day of Pesach, as explained below in se'if 3. Once it is stated that Pesach does not occur on Monday, Wednesday, or Friday, it automatically follows that Shavuos does not occur on Tuesday, Thursday, or Shabbos: Tuesday follows Monday, Thursday follows Wednesday, and Shabbos follows Friday. The reason Pesach does not occur on Monday, Wednesday, or Friday is explained in se'if 3: if it did, Yom Kippur would occur on Friday or Sunday, or Hoshana Rabbah would occur on Shabbos.
4) There. Chanukah does not occur on Tuesday. This is because Chanukah falls on the same weekday as Shavuos, as explained below in subsection 7. Since Shavuos does not occur on Tuesday, Thursday, or Shabbos, Chanukah consequently does not occur on Tuesday.
5) There. Taanis Esther does not occur on Sunday, Tuesday, or Friday. Taanis Esther occurs on the thirteenth of Adar and Purim on the fourteenth. If Taanis Esther occurred on Sunday, Tuesday, or Friday, Purim would occur on Monday, Wednesday, or Shabbos: Monday follows Sunday, Wednesday follows Tuesday, and Shabbos follows Friday. Since Purim does not occur on Shabbos, Monday, or Wednesday, Taanis Esther consequently does not occur on Sunday, Tuesday, or Friday.
6) There. The fasts of Tammuz and Av do not occur on Monday, Wednesday, or Friday. The seventeenth of Tammuz and Tishah B'Av fall on the same weekday as Pesach, as explained below in se'if 3. Since Pesach does not occur on Monday, Wednesday, or Friday, the fasts of Tammuz and Av likewise do not occur on those days.
7) There. Chanukah occurs on the same weekday as Shavuos. This is the correct text. Chanukah following Shavuos occurs on the same weekday when the intervening months are in their regular or deficient arrangement. When they are full, Chanukah occurs on the day after the weekday of Shavuos. For example, if Shavuos occurs on Sunday, Chanukah occurs on Monday. Magen Avraham, subsection 1. This implies that when the months are regular or deficient, Chanukah occurs on the same weekday as Shavuos and therefore does not occur on Tuesday, Thursday, or Shabbos. Although the Shulchan Aruch states only that Chanukah does not occur on Tuesday, it can occur on Thursday or Shabbos when the months are full. The preceding Shavuos would then have occurred on Wednesday or Friday, and Chanukah would occur on Thursday or Shabbos. Eshel Avraham, subsection 1.
