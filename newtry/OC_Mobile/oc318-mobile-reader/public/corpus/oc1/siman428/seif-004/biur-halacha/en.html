Tzav before Pesach. Because in Tzav the Torah discusses kashering vessels regarding notar of kodashim, and kashering vessels from chametz is learned from kodashim. Also in a leap year, when Metzora is read before Pesach, it contains "and an earthenware vessel shall be broken," which is similar to kashering vessels, as Eliyah Rabbah writes in the name of Abudraham.
And one always reads, etc., before Atzeret. This is so that this parashah always interrupts between Bechukotai, which contains the curses of the tokhachah, and Shavuot, which is the day of judgment for tree fruits; the curses should not be read adjacent to the day of judgment, giving the Satan an opening to prosecute, Heaven forbid, as Levush writes.
Bamidbar Sinai before Atzeret. Sometimes even parashat Naso is read before Atzeret, such as when that year is a leap year and Rosh Hashanah falls on Thursday, so Acharei Mot is read before Pesach, whether Cheshvan and Kislev that year are full or deficient. Then Naso is read before Atzeret, as explained in the poskim.
I will copy before the reader from the Gra's Biur, so he may know which parashiyot are joined and which are separated. A sign for the joined parashiyot:
Vayakhel-Pekudei, Tazria-Metzora, Acharei Mot-Kedoshim, and Behar-Bechukotai are always joined in a regular year and separated in a leap year, except in the case of heh-sheleimah, meaning Rosh Hashanah fell on Thursday and the year is complete, with Cheshvan and Kislev full; then even in a regular year Vayakhel and Pekudei are separated, because one additional Shabbat is added before Pesach. Chukat-Balak are joined only when Shavuot falls on Shabbat, adding one parashah, since on Yom Tov the weekly parashah is not read; this applies both in a regular and leap year. Matot-Masei are separated only when the year is a leap year and Rosh Hashanah falls on Thursday, whether the year is complete or deficient; then Acharei Mot is read before Pesach and Naso before Atzeret, and because one parashah is missing, Matot and Masei are separated. Nitzavim-Vayelekh are separated only when Rosh Hashanah falls on Monday or Tuesday, as written in Shulchan Aruch; there is no difference in this between leap and regular years, or between complete and deficient years.
I will also copy briefly before the reader, from the Gra's Biur, the main rules for fixing the months:
When Rosh Hashanah falls on Sunday or Tuesday, all the months always proceed in order, one full and one deficient: Tishrei full, Cheshvan deficient, Kislev full, Tevet deficient, Shevat full, Adar deficient, and so through the year, both in a regular and leap year; in a leap year, however, the first Adar is also full, as explained in the poskim. When the first day of Rosh Hashanah falls on Monday or Shabbat, the two months Cheshvan and Kislev cannot be in order; sometimes both are full and sometimes both deficient, whether the year is regular or leap. Likewise, when the first day of Rosh Hashanah falls on Thursday and that year is a leap year, these two months also cannot be in order, but are sometimes both full and sometimes both deficient. When the first day of Rosh Hashanah falls on Thursday in a regular year, the year cannot be deficient, meaning those two months cannot both be deficient; rather, sometimes Cheshvan is deficient and Kislev full, and sometimes both are full. Thus there are seven types of fixed years in a regular year: Monday deficient, Monday complete, Tuesday in order, Thursday in order, Thursday complete, Shabbat deficient, Shabbat complete; and seven types in a leap year: Monday deficient, Monday complete, Tuesday in order, Thursday deficient, Thursday complete, Shabbat deficient, Shabbat complete. They further gave a sign: the first day of Shavuot is the first day of Chanukkah afterward, hinted by "for a mitzvah is a lamp and Torah is light," unless the year is complete, in which case the second day of Shavuot is the first day of Chanukkah. So writes Magen Avraham. The first day of Chanukkah is the day of Purim, but only when the year is regular and deficient. These are the Gra's words, and all these rules are also evident in Levush.
Tishah BeAv before. The reason is that they read parashat Devarim, which contains Moshe's rebukes, before Tishah BeAv, so that the haftarah may be Chazon, Yeshayahu's rebuke over the destruction. VeAtem Nitzavim is read before Rosh Hashanah because it contains matters of teshuvah, as Geza Shai writes.
