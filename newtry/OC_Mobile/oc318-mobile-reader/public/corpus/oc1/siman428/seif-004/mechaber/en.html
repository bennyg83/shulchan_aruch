We always read "Tzav es Aharon" before Pesach in a regular [non-leap] year, and "Metzora" in a leap year — except in a leap year [whose Rosh Hashanah began on] Thursday {and a leap year [of the type] hei-shin}, when we read "Acharei Mos" before Pesach. And we always read Parashas Bamidbar Sinai before Shavuos, [the parashah preceding] Tishah B'Av before "Va'eschanan," and "Atem Nitzavim" before Rosh Hashanah. Therefore, when Rosh Hashanah is on Monday or Tuesday, such that there are two Shabbosos between Rosh Hashanah and Sukkos, one must divide "Nitzavim" and "Vayelech," so that they read "Vayelech" between Rosh Hashanah and Yom Kippur, and "Ha'azinu" between Yom Kippur and Sukkos; and the mnemonic is "Beis-Gimel: the king, bread, and Vayelech." But when Rosh Hashanah is on Thursday or Shabbos, then there is only one Shabbos between Rosh Hashanah and Sukkos, on which they read "Ha'azinu"; then "Vayelech" [is joined] with "Nitzavim" before Rosh Hashanah. And a mnemonic for a regular year: "pikdu u'pischu" [count (Bamidbar) and then Pesach…]; and for a leap year: "sigru u'pischu, manu ve'atzru, tzumu ve'tzalu, kumu u'seku."
