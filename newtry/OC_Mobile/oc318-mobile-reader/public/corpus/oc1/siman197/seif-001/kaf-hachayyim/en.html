1) [Seif 1] Two who ate together and finished, etc. The same applies if one person ate and finished, and two then arrived; they may make a Zimun. Maamar Mordechai, paragraph 1. Nevertheless, there is no obligation for the first person to wait until they eat and make a Zimun unless two people ate together and finished, and a third then arrived, as stated in the Shulchan Aruch.
2) [Seif 1] Whenever, if something were brought to them, etc. This applies even to dessert, such as truffles, mushrooms, or poultry. Gemara; Magen Avraham, paragraph 1. It applies even if they can eat only the smallest amount. Shulchan Aruch HaRav, paragraph 1.
3) There: He can eat some of it, etc. This applies although they ate nothing after he joined them. Shulchan Aruch HaRav there. Even if that item is not before him, we nevertheless say that if it were before him he would eat it. Derishah, paragraph 1. Regarding what Derishah writes there about Levush, see Eliyah Rabbah, paragraph 1, who resolves it; see there.
4) There: They are obligated to give him, etc. This is because it is a mitzvah for them to seek a third person to join them in Zimun, as stated above in siman 193, seif 1. Below, siman 479, states that it is a mitzvah to seek Zimun on Pesach nights, implying that during the rest of the year there is neither a mitzvah nor an obligation. One may answer that there one must invite enough people for Zimun before the meal, whereas here, only if the situation arises during the meal must they give him food so that he can join. Maamar Mordechai, paragraph 3.
