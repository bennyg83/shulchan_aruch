All. Taz in siman 196 ot 2 challenged: why Kohen and non-Kohen with gentile bread do not combine, and in Eliyah Rabbah 196 ot 3 he answered somewhat—see there; and ostensibly it implies they can eat even though they are not desirous; and see Levush and Perishah. But if they are so satiated they cannot eat at all, he does not combine. And know: if they finished their meal and a third came—if they said explicitly they do not wish to fix a meal, then there is no zimun, even though they do not act properly, for they are obligated to return after zimun as in siman 193; but if they were silent and the third came and ate a kazayit, they are obligated, for it is fixing a meal; and what is written in 193 seif 2 that they are fixed at finishing the meal—this too is included, since they can eat; and so Rashi Berachot 47a s.v. akilna: "you do not obligate us in zimun"—and he was forced to explain thus, for Rav in 45b holds two—it is optional to invite; and if it were R' Shimon bar Yochai also with them to say the zimun blessing, alternatively he does not combine—he can answer "Blessed are You... who has fed us from His bounty," as Rashi 45b explained "he came and found them"—since he did not eat; and see siman 198 in Magen Avraham 1 (and to discharge himself with their birkat hamazon R' Shimon bar Yochai would not protest for this)—it teaches obligation of zimun for all of them, and great is the mitzvah etc.; and it is difficult for Maharal who holds all who were not fixed with haMotzi are not obligated—see siman 193 in Tur from here; and Orach Mishpat chapter 5 of Berachot halacha 9 wrote: and a third came and ate etc.—obligated in zimun; and in Shulchan Aruch he wrote l'chatchila; and from Kohen and non-Kohen one can say it is as if they said explicitly they do not wish to fix a meal—unlike here where they were silent; and all this in our innovations there.

In Eliyah Rabbah 4—he is uncertain if when one can eat and the other cannot, whether they combine; and practical difference: if one said "hav venevarekh"—see there. And I am uncertain whether we require he can eat specifically a kazayit or anything at all; and Orach Mishpat chapter 5 halacha 9—even anything at all, and even from other foods—one can say all the more so other foods is meant. Also what is written "they can eat" and not "they can drink" requires study.
