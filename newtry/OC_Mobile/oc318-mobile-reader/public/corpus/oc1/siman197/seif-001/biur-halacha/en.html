They can eat from it—thus is the reading in old editions of Shulchan Aruch, and the intent is specifically if both can eat from it; and so it implies from Rambam's wording; and therefore it is obvious that if one washed hands [or clearly diverted his mind that he would not eat anything, per what we wrote in siman 179 in Biur Halacha s.v. ve'afilu, and even per Beit Meir] automatically the third cannot join; and I found in Eliyah Rabbah that he too sides thus for the law, but afterward he is somewhat uncertain; and from the text of Chot Yair in Rif who reads "if they would bring me ardilia" etc. it also implies as we wrote; afterward I found in Ma'amar Mordechai who wrote clearly as our words, and he greatly wondered at Eliyah Rabbah who is uncertain—see there.

He joins with them—meaning they are obligated in zimun [and it is not optional], and as the Mechaber ruled in siman 193 that it depends on finishing the meal, and this too is called finishing a meal; it implies from several poskim who ruled thus for the law, and not like Rabbeinu Yonah who holds this passage is only regarding optional zimun; and so it implies there in Rashi s.v. akilna that they are obligated in zimun. And know further that it implies from the Rishonim that this third can invite and discharge them from zimun as well.

But if they said etc.—see Magen Avraham that per the second view explained above in siman 179 seif 1, "hav lan venevarekh" too is not complete diversion of mind regarding eating; therefore here they combine—all who have not washed mayim acharonim; and so it implies in several Acharonim [see Derishah and Eliyah Rabbah and Beur HaGra above in siman 179 seif 1] that this depends on that; however in Shulchan Aruch HaGraz tends to say our law is for everyone—see there his reason; and see Peri Megadim who also wants to side thus, and he left it requiring further study.

He does not join—see Or Zaru'a who wants to innovate that this is specifically when there were two and a third came; but where three ate and were already obligated in zimun and again one came and ate a kazayit—even if they washed hands or said "hav lan venevarekh"—he also joins with them and invites for them and discharges them from the obligation of zimun; and with this is resolved what the Rishonim challenged [see Pesachim 103b Tosafot s.v. keivan] from that of Shimon ben Shetach—see there; however from other Rishonim who explained otherwise it implies they do not hold thus.

With them—see Peri Megadim who sides that in zimun of three, wherever there is doubt for the law they combine [meaning it is permitted for them to invite]; but in zimun of ten where there is mention of the Name he is uncertain whether it is permitted because of the severity of "do not take [the Name in vain]"; and so Chayei Adam tends.

And washing etc.—see Magen Avraham s.k. 3 what he brought in the name of Bach and I did not copy it, for Eliyah Rabbah already challenged it and it is brought in Sha'arei Teshuvah; and see Taz siman 193 s.k. 8 who also holds like Eliyah Rabbah's view.
