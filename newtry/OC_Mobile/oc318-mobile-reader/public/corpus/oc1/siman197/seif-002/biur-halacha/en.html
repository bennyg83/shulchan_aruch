Kazayit of vegetable—and in Beur HaGra it implies his view that we do not require a kazayit for joining ten; and so it implies in Chiddushei HaRa'ah, for he brings proof for Rif's ruling that specifically for ten one combines—from the Mishnah that teaches "they invite zimun until a kazayit," and our Mishnah deals with joining three—see there; behold it is proven that joining for ten is even with less than a kazayit; and in Shibolei Leket he also brings proof from Tosefta that for joining ten it helps even with less than a kazayit of grain—see there; and ostensibly the same applies to less than a kazayit of vegetable [and it is possible that even for grain one does not bless any final blessing on less than a kazayit, nevertheless it is considered more than vegetable; and in any case for grain his view is clear that even less than a kazayit helps]; and nevertheless in practice it requires study to be lenient against Shulchan Aruch's view, for Mordechai—from whom Shulchan Aruch took its source as explained in Beit Yosef—is not a lone view, for it is reasonable that all these poskim who do not distinguish between three and ten for joining hold thus, that in any case we require the measure of a kazayit—for behold it states in the Mishnah at the beginning of "three who ate" that the attendant who ate less than a kazayit—they do not invite zimun upon him; for even if we say per their explanation—even with any vegetable—it is proven from this in any case that we require specifically a kazayit even for joining [as all Rishonim explained that this Mishnah deals with joining]; and per them certainly one should not distinguish between three and ten [as is proven from the proof those who hold this view brought from "one of them went to the market," for on the contrary with ten we are stricter]; and if so certainly one should not be lenient per them with less than a kazayit even for ten; and since we came to this, why should we say that per Rif and his supporters who are stricter regarding joining, they would be lenient for ten to join even with less than a kazayit—and also why should we make a new dispute between them; and further Rashba who agrees with Rif regarding joining three that specifically with grain it is clear to him, also regarding joining ten he is lenient with vegetables—and such that in any case the one who joins must be obligated in some final blessing, for otherwise joining for zimun is not applicable at all—see Berachot 48; and if so in any case we require a kazayit that he be obligated in a final blessing. And that Tosefta Shibolei Leket brings as proof is not complete proof even for grain that less than a kazayit suffices, for it is possible it deals where there was a kazayit by dipping in brine, as he himself somewhat rejected there—see there.

That contains a revi'it—see Beur HaGra who wrote this is Mordechai's view and as his view, and it implies his view that we do not require a revi'it; and as Beur HaGra is lenient regarding kazayit of vegetable; but I already wrote it is difficult to be lenient, for several Rishonim hold thus. And in Eliyah Rabbah he brought in the name of the book Tzedah LaDerech that even a majority of a revi'it suffices, and he wrote he did not find thus in other poskim; and in my humble opinion one should not so reject his words, since Beur HaGra and his colleagues hold we do not require a kazayit, and automatically in this too it suffices even with a very small measure—and in particular a majority of a revi'it, which is an important measure for kiddush; and there are many poskim uncertain regarding final blessing who hold the measure of a kazayit suffices for drinking, which is a third of a revi'it—and all the more so with a majority of a revi'it; and even though l'chatchila we are strict not to bless a final blessing on less than a revi'it, as stated below siman 210, nevertheless for joining it is possible to be lenient with less than this; and l'chatchila it is certainly better to be careful that he drink a revi'it, but b'dieved it is possible to be lenient.
