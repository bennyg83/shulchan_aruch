(2) [Levush] Above siman 193 etc. Malbushei Yom Tov wrote it is not there because of general obligation; and on the contrary below siman 479 it is proven that specifically on Passover nights one must return after three; but Rosh's words in chapter "three who ate" that if one comes one must give him—and therefore it is not difficult against him, for in chapter Arvei Pesachim he ruled that even on Passover nights they do not have the custom to return after three—until here. And I did not know what difficulty he has on Levush, for there is no doubt that instead of siman 192 one should say siman 193, for in seif 1 it is explained there like Levush that they must return after zimun; and what he challenged from siman 479—it seems to me it is no difficulty at all, for there it teaches us it is a mitzvah to return after zimun to answer Hodu, and the practical difference is they should not separate after birkat hamazon; or even if he had no obligation to bless birkat hamazon, nevertheless he returns after zimun to answer Hodu. One can also say that what Levush wrote—mitzvah to return after three—and indeed in Ma'adanei Melech there he wrote: one could say that in chapter Arvei Pesachim he proved there is no mitzvah to return after three except on Passover nights, and even on Passover night he concludes they did not have this custom—until here his language. And in any case we see Malbushei Yom Tov resolves this wonder that I brought, and it is reasonable to him that the same law applies—if three come to him he must return after him that he eat and invite zimun with him. It also seems to me to resolve the main wonder of Ma'adanei Melech on Rosh and his difficulty on Levush—that on Passover night even if one eats he must return after zimun, unlike other days of the year when one is obligated only when they are two, as above siman 193—and understand well. And in the essence of this law I am uncertain, and ostensibly it implies in the Talmud [Berachot] 47 that from Shmuel's statement "if they would bring me ardilia and gozalia to eat" etc.—one alone does not suffice but we require both. And if so, even if one says "hav lan venevarekh" or washed hands and the second intends to eat more, the third does not join with them; but in poskim it somewhat implies that even when one's mind is to eat more he combines—and per this one must say Shmuel stated the straightforward truth, and it requires study.
