[Levush] Or even, etc. Malbushei Yom Tov asks why the Levush says 'even,' since this follows directly from the beginning of siman 179. Shiyarei Knesset HaGedolah reports that the Beit Yosef questioned the Rama's statement that mayim acharonim is equivalent to saying 'Let us bless,' because washing appears more decisive than verbal declaration. Above in siman 101 I cited the Rashba that saying 'Let us bless' is actually more decisive than mayim acharonim. One can therefore say that the Rama teaches that for this law all agree the two are equivalent. It also seems to me possible to explain based on the Bach, who holds that even after saying 'Let us bless' there is a mitzvah to resume eating, wash again, recite HaMotzi, and thereby make zimun with the newcomer. The Rama therefore writes that mayim acharonim is equivalent to saying 'Let us bless': although in siman 179 he rules that after mayim acharonim one may not eat again even after washing and blessing, here for the sake of the mitzvah of zimun one may rely on the poskim who permit it. See there, where I concluded that one may eat even when not needed for zimun. Nevertheless, the Bach's principal rule that there is a mitzvah to resume eating, also cited by Magen Avraham, is not clear to me. I found no source for it, and the Gemara and poskim imply otherwise. It is reasonable that once a new first blessing is required, the later eating is an entirely new eating. If they eat again and then make zimun, it is as though they began a new meal together. The Derishah also implies this. See above siman 193, seif katan 10.
