Mitzvah that he bless. For from Torah he is not obligated to bless unless he ate to satiety; and as Beit Yosef—that if each blesses to himself in a whisper and only they invite zimun, even one who ate a kazayit can bless; and see siman 183 seif 7.

He can discharge. For from Torah he can discharge his fellow even though he did not eat, since his fellow already ate and is obligated to bless, and all Israel are guarantors for one another—as written end of siman 196; except he cannot say "who has fed us" unless he ate a kazayit (Tosafot, Beit Yosef); and Ateret Zekenim did not look in Beit Yosef, and it ostensibly implies if they are two he need not say "who has fed us"—he can discharge his fellow even though he did not eat; and nevertheless in beginning of siman 193 it implies he does not discharge his fellow unless he ate—and so in Tosafot in the name of Yerushalmi.

And some say he is not obligated. And per their words, if he ate a kazayit he is obligated d'oraisa to bless; and per Rabbeinu Yehudah it is only d'rabbanan, for that which we expound "and you shall eat"—eating measure of a kazayit—is merely an asmakhta, for d'oraisa we require actual satiety; and so say most poskim, and Bach did not understand Rabbeinu Yehudah's words on this—see there; and if some ate to satiety and did not drink, and some did not eat to satiety and drank—it is better they bless over those who ate to satiety, for they are obligated d'oraisa per most poskim, for we are not concerned with drinking; and see end of siman 186. One who eats coarse eating discharges others in birkat hamazon if his throat enjoyed that eating (Shulchan Gavoah chapter 7); see siman 602 seif 6.
