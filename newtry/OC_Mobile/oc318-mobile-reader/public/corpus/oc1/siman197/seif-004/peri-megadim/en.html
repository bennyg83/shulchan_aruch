If. See Taz. We rule that one who eats to satiety is obligated by Torah law, as in siman 184:6 and 186:2. Some authorities follow Rabbi Meir that an olive-sized portion suffices, while others interpret “and you shall be satisfied” as drinking. Each interpretation creates both a leniency and a stringency. Rama therefore writes that it is preferable, although the principal view is that satiety is required and drinking is unnecessary. See Magen Avraham, seif katan 11. Mordechai may hold in this case that one who is obligated only Rabbinically cannot discharge a Torah obligation, because mutual responsibility applies only to an obligation one cannot avoid, unlike optional eating. According to our ruling, however, one with a Rabbinic obligation may discharge one with a Torah obligation because of mutual responsibility, as I wrote in letter 3. Rama therefore writes only that it is preferable, for two independent reasons. This is not the place to elaborate.
