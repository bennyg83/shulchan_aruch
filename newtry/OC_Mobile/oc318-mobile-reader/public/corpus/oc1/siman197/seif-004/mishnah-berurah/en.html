(23) Mitzvah etc.—for since many of them ate to satiety and are obligated in birkat hamazon from the Torah, it is better he discharge them also—he who ate to satiety and is obligated from the Torah like them. Acharonim wrote all this if he discharges them in birkat hamazon; but if each blesses by himself and only one invites zimun upon them—we are not so strict; and l'chatchila one can invite zimun upon them even one who did not eat except a kazayit.
(24) He can discharge etc.—for from the Torah he can discharge his fellow in birkat hamazon even though he did not eat at all, since his fellow already ate and is obligated to bless and all Israel are guarantors for one another; except because he speaks falsely in saying "who has fed us" when he did not eat, the Sages required he eat a kazayit first; and see Magen Avraham who is uncertain that per this, if they are only two—for otherwise they do not invite zimun—it is possible he can discharge his fellow who does not know how to bless even if he did not eat at all; and he sides to be strict; and so in Eliyah Rabbah—that d'rabbanan in all cases one does not discharge his fellow in birkat hamazon unless he too is obligated in the matter; and so is proven explicitly in Chiddushei HaRa'ah to Berachot and Ritva Rosh Hashanah 29, and in Shitah Mekubetzet Berachot 48—see there.
(25) Some say he is not obligated etc.—for they hold that what R' Meir expounds in the gemara "and you shall eat" refers to eating and "and you were satisfied" refers to drinking—meaning if he drank after eating then he is obligated to bless, and if not he is not obligated except d'rabbanan.
(26) And he is thirsty to drink—for if he is not thirsty to drink, for everyone he is obligated d'oraisa even without drinking.
(27) And it is good to be careful l'chatchila—meaning per Shulchan Aruch's ruling above that if there is one obligated in the matter d'oraisa who knows how to bless, it is a mitzvah he bless to discharge others; it is good to be concerned also for this view that one who drank after his eating bless to discharge others, for he is certainly obligated d'oraisa—unless those who drank do not know how to bless, then one who did not drink can discharge them, as the Mechaber also wrote regarding satiety.
(28) That one who drank bless—and nevertheless if some ate to satiety and did not drink, and some did not eat to satiety and drank—it is better they bless over those who ate to satiety, for they are obligated d'oraisa per the poskim even if they did not drink; and Mordechai's view in the name of Ra'avan is a lone view. One who was satiated before he ate and ate coarse eating he did not need—nevertheless if his throat enjoyed that eating he blesses before and after it and discharges others; and if his soul was disgusted by it and his throat did not enjoy it, he is not fit to bless neither before nor after it, for this is not considered eating at all for any mitzvah in the Torah, as explained in siman 476 and siman 612.
