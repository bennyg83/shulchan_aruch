If he did not drink and he is thirsty to drink. So Beit Yosef in the name of Mordechai; and his reason: since we hold like R' Meir who said "and you were satisfied" refers to drinking—if so he is not obligated d'oraisa unless he drank if he needs to drink etc.; therefore one who ate and did not drink cannot discharge others with it, for the Sages do not come and produce d'oraisa from this; and Beit Yosef challenged this, for Tosafot chapter "three who ate" wrote the verses are merely asmakhta; and further—even per those who hold the verses are not asmakhta but kazayit is d'oraisa—from where do we know birkat eating depends on drinking—perhaps the verse means when you eat or drink you bless Heaven your Heaven; and so all poskim who did not write this, and so we rule that even if he desires to drink he discharges others with it—end of his words; therefore Rama wrote it is good to be careful l'chatchila etc.; and in my humble opinion it is difficult on Mordechai's words—why can he not discharge others without drinking, for we already said nearby that even if he did not eat at all he can discharge others in this mitzvah, except we require a kazayit of grain so he can say "who has fed us"—and here he can well say "who has fed us," even though he is not obligated in Borei nefashot rabot per one view since he did not drink—nevertheless he is within "who has fed us" and can discharge others who drank because of guarantors as written nearby; and if you say he cannot say "who has fed us" since he did not drink—if so it should have said "who has fed us and given us drink," the language of Scripture that mentioned drinking separately and not within "and you shall eat."
