He may. See Taz and Tosafos, Berachos 48a, s.v. ad, and Rosh Hashanah 29a. See Magen Avraham, seif katan 11, that where two are unlearned and one is learned, the learned person may recite the blessing even if he ate nothing. Levush, seif 4, cites the Yerushalmi quoted by Beit Yosef: “You shall eat and bless,” one who ate blesses; “those who derive benefit,” one who wishes to derive benefit must bless. Accordingly, not only for Bircas HaMazon may one not discharge others unless he ate an olive-sized portion of grain, but even for Borei Nefashos he may not discharge them unless he ate. We rule even in such a case, let him not derive benefit and not bless. Although he has already eaten and is obligated, it remains an act of benefit, and he should not have eaten unless he learned the blessings. See siman 167, Magen Avraham seif katan 40, and here seif katan 11. Levush writes specifically an olive-sized portion of bread, not porridge. With Hashem’s help this will be explained further in Magen Avraham.
