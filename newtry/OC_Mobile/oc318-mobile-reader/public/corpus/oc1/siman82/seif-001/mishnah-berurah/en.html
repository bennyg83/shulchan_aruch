(1) It crumbles. Specifically, it must crumble into fine fragments; breaking into two or three pieces is not sufficient. During cold weather, when excrement freezes solid like stone and emits no odor, the Magen Avraham was uncertain whether it is permitted to recite Krias Shema opposite it. Perhaps we assess it according to its present state, or perhaps it retains the designation of excrement because it can return to its original state when the weather becomes warm. The later authorities unanimously rule that it is prohibited. Even after the fact, one who recited Krias Shema must repeat it, and one who prayed must repeat the prayer as a voluntary prayer. If the excrement is beneath frozen water, it may be considered covered. Although water itself is ineffective, as explained in siman 76, a layer of ice or frost may be included in the requirement of 'you shall cover it' and may resemble a glass barrier; in that case, we assess its present condition. Excrement covered by snow is considered covered as long as the snow has not melted. Shulchan Shlomo writes that one should likewise be stringent not to recite Krias Shema opposite frozen urine. This point requires study.
