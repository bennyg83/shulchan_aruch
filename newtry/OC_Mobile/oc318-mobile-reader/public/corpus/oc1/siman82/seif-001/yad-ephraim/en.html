(In Magen Avraham, subsection 2) Nevertheless requires study regarding excrement like pottery, etc., by analogy to an old privy, etc.—apparently one must emend "and again by analogy to an old privy," for that is a different proof; nevertheless requires study regarding the proof from an old privy, for we see that if one throws it and it crumbles apart, there is no excrement in it; and if so, if we say hardened excrement is comparable, there is no proof from an old privy. And in my humble opinion, his intent is toward what I wrote: although it can return to its former state, nevertheless we follow the present state—and on this he wrote that study is required. Excrement like pottery without bad smell is forbidden so long as it does not crumble apart—thus we do not follow the present state, for now it is like pottery and has no smell; rather we follow the original state and say the name of excrement was not nullified from it, and we judge it according to its end. And on this he wrote what is stated regarding an old privy—that it is forbidden; for even though it has no excrement, since initially it was forbidden by Torah law, we do not say in it "go after the present" but follow the original state. Here too one should judge it according to its end, not the present—and it is not comparable to throwing it so it crumbles apart, for there it is mere dust and the name of dust applies to it, and it is not fit to follow the original state; and so according to its end one judges it, or judges it that it will never return to its former state. Not so with hardened excrement—it is not fit to call it dust since it will return to its former state; therefore the name of excrement was not nullified from it.
