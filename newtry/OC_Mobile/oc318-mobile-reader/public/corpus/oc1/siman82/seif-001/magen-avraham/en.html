"It crumbles apart." But if it breaks into two pieces, it does not help (Kesef Mishnah, Rambam).

"And there are those who say it is not, etc."—and the first reasoning holds that it crumbles more easily by rolling than by throwing, and both opinions are for stringency. And it seems to me that in cold weather when the excrement has hardened greatly like a stone, it is permitted to recite Shema opposite it even though it will later return to its former state in warm weather; nevertheless we follow the present state, and Kesef Mishnah [says] somewhat in Taharot chapter 3. Nevertheless requires study, for it implies excrement like pottery is forbidden even though it has no smell, because excrement still remains on it until it is thrown and crumbles apart; if so, here too—even though it has hardened, excrement still remains on it since it can return to its former state, by analogy to an old privy: even though it has no excrement, it is forbidden since initially it was forbidden by Torah law, as Rabbenu Yonah wrote in the name of Tosafos Ri, siman 83—requires study.
