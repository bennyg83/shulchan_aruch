It crumbles. Merely breaking into two pieces is not sufficient, as is implied by the Rambam.
And some say that it is not, etc. The first opinion maintains that excrement crumbles more readily through rolling than through throwing, and both opinions intend to rule stringently. It appears to me that in cold weather, when the excrement freezes solid like stone, it is permitted to recite Krias Shema opposite it, even though it will later return to its original state when the weather becomes warm. We assess it according to its present state, as is somewhat implied in Taharos, chapter 3. Nevertheless, this requires study, because the law that excrement like earthenware is prohibited even when it has no odor implies that it retains the designation of excrement until it can be thrown and crumble. Here too, although it has frozen, it may still retain the designation of excrement because it can return to its original state. This resembles an old privy, which is prohibited even when no excrement is presently inside it, since it was initially prohibited by Torah law, as Beit Yosef writes in the name of the Talmidei Rabbeinu Yonah. See siman 83. This requires study.
