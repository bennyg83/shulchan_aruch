And some say that it is not considered earth unless, etc. The Gemara presents two versions that disagree on this point. The first opinion likewise intends to rule stringently, but maintains that excrement crumbles more readily when rolled than when thrown.
And this is the primary ruling. This implies that, when we have seen that it crumbles through rolling, that is sufficient, and we need not be concerned that perhaps it would not crumble if thrown. Eliyah Rabbah, however, writes in the name of the later authorities that one must also be stringent in accordance with the Mechaber's opinion. See Peri Megadim for what he further writes on this in the name of the Derishah. See also the novellae of the Rashba to Berachos 25, citing the Raavad, which imply that, when one sees it splitting apart, it has reached the point at which it would crumble through rolling. This is preferable to excrement that is like earthenware, which we rule is prohibited. Nevertheless, according to Eliyah Rabbah's stringency that one must take the Mechaber's opinion into account, one should not be lenient in this case. I later found that the Aruch, under the entry kaf lamed, and Or Zarua, siman 29, maintain that even this degree of dryness has not yet reached the status of excrement like earthenware, and it remains prohibited. This also appears from all the poskim who do not cite the Rashba's statement at all.
