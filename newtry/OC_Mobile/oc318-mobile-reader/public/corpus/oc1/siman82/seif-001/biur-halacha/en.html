"And there are those who say it is not like dust only, etc."—In the gemara two versions dispute this, and the first opinion also tends to be stringent in this; but it holds that it is easier to crumble by rolling than by throwing.

"And such is the main ruling"—It implies from this that he holds that if we saw it crumble by rolling, that suffices, and one need not concern that perhaps it would not crumble by throwing; but in Baer Heitev he wrote in the name of the later authorities that one must be stringent also, like the author's opinion. See in Peri Megadim what he wrote further on this in the name of HaDrisha. See in Rashba's novellae [Berachot 25] in the name of Raavad that it appears from him that if one sees it splits apart extensively, then it has reached the measure that it should crumble by rolling—and this fits well with excrement like pottery, which we hold is forbidden; yet according to what Baer Heitev was stringent to concern for the author's opinion, one may not be lenient in this. I again found in Aruch, entry "kol," and Or Zarua siman 29, that they hold this measure has not yet been reached even for excrement like pottery, and it is forbidden; and so appears from all the poskim who did not bring this Rashba view at all.
