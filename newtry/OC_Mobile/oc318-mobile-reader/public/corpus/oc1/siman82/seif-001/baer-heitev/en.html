"It crumbles apart." But if it breaks into two pieces, it does not help (Kesef Mishnah). And in cold weather when the excrement has hardened greatly like a stone, it is doubtful whether it is permitted to recite Shema opposite it—whether we follow the present state, or whether one must forbid because excrement still remains on it since it can return to its former state in warm weather. See Magen Avraham; and in Baer Heitev before me he wrote that it seemed to him a proof for prohibition from Mishnah 2, chapter 3 of Taharot—see there and investigate, thus far. And I examined there and analyzed, and I cannot understand his intent that there would be proof from there for prohibition in this law; on the contrary, it leans more toward permission, as Magen Avraham wrote—see there.
