Seif 6. There are those who object to the custom of completing the verses of Shofaros with the verse, 'And on the day of your rejoicing,' because shofar is not mentioned there, only trumpets; but the Rosh and the Ran wrote to uphold the custom. Note: The reason of the Rosh is that he wrote as follows: I say that one should not change the formula of blessings practiced in earlier days; just as a teruah without the word shofar is called shofar, so too a tekiah is called shofar, and because the verse says trumpets it is no worse, end quote. This is astonishing to me from the Talmud in Rosh Hashanah 29, where it says that kohanim, Leviim, and Yisraelim are obligated in shofar-blowing. It asks: is this not obvious? It answers: kohanim were necessary for me; one might have thought that since it is written, 'it shall be a day of teruah for you,' only one who has only a one-day tekiah is obligated, but these kohanim, since they have the tekios of the whole year, as it is written, 'you shall blow with trumpets,' perhaps they are not obligated; therefore it teaches us otherwise. It then asks: are they comparable? There it is trumpets, and here it is shofar, etc. What is the question? In any event they have tekios, and tekios can also be with a shofar; if so, they have tekios the whole year. It is forced to distinguish between performance and mention, for what difference is there between them? In mention too we require shofar and not trumpets, yet because the word tekiah is written, it includes even shofar. If so, also in performance let us say that since in any event they have tekiah, and tekiah is shofar, we do not care that in fact it is trumpets. This requires analysis. While engaged in this, I had another difficulty: why does the Talmud ask, 'is this not obvious?' Let us say as follows, according to what was written above on folio 27, that all shofars are valid except that of a cow, because it is called a horn. Ulla says there that the reason of the Sages is because a prosecutor cannot become a defender. The Gemara asks there: but the Mishnah teaches, 'because it is a horn.' It answers: the Sages said one reason and another: one reason is that a prosecutor cannot become a defender, and another is that it is a horn and not a shofar. If so, why does it ask here? Perhaps the intent of the tanna is this: all are obligated specifically in shofar-blowing and do not fulfill the obligation with that of a cow, which is a horn. One might have thought that the main reason we require shofar is because a prosecutor cannot become a defender, and if so, kohanim and Leviim, who did not sin with it, would fulfill even with that of a cow. Therefore it teaches that the main reason is because it is a horn, and they too do not fulfill with that of a cow. Even according to the other amoraim who answer there differently, one may say that this is according to the truth, because they were not comfortable saying this in the reasoning of the Sages due to the Gemara's challenge there from the spoon and fire-pan and from the golden garments, and they were not comfortable with the answer there. Nevertheless, in the initial assumption it could properly have been said so, and one might have thought that kohanim and Leviim would be permitted even with a horn; therefore it teaches that in truth that reason is not so, but the reason is that it is called a horn. This requires analysis; examine it. I am astonished at the Ran and the Rosh, and at those who object, that they did not see the words of the Sifrei brought in Yalkut on Parashas Behaaloscha, on the verse 'you shall blow with trumpets,' as follows: Rabbi Nassan says: This is not necessary; 'you shall blow with trumpets' means shofars; see there. Thus it is explicit that this is considered Shofaros. I wrote the explanation of the Sifrei's intent in my sermon for Rosh Hashanah of the years 584, 595, and 601; see there and analyze it well.
