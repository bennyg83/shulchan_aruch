6) In a place where the congregation prays together with the shaliach tzibbur, and the shaliach tzibbur also sounds the shofar and errs, it is forbidden to speak or rebuke him. The blasts sounded during the order of the blessings are not indispensable, and certainly individuals do not need to hear them during the order of the blessings. Even if they did not hear the blasts sounded while seated, after the prayer they should repeat only the series in which he erred, and that is sufficient. Rashba, siman 295; Magen Avraham, s.k. 2. This refers to a place where they do not pray silently at all, but the shaliach tzibbur begins the prayer aloud and the congregation recites it with him word for word, as written by the Levushei Serad and Eshel Avraham, see there. The same applies according to the custom stated by the Arizal, of sounding the shofar during both the silent prayer and the repetition, as written above, siman 585, os 28. If the shofar blower errs during the silent prayer, it is forbidden to speak or rebuke him. The custom is merely to signal to him, whereupon he realizes that he erred and corrects it. See below, siman 592, os 2.
