One who changes the prescribed text, etc. The Rivash likewise wrote that one should not say ‘for the seed of Yaakov,’ because the unqualified ‘seed of Yitzchak’ means Yaakov, as the Sages expounded: ‘For through Yitzchak shall offspring be considered yours’; through Yitzchak, but not all of Yitzchak, thereby excluding Esav. In Zevachei Shelamim by the author of Pardes, however, I saw that he disputed this. He said that in Yevamos we find that invalid offspring are also called ‘offspring’ regarding the verse ‘and she has no offspring,’ and in prayer one must be as explicit as possible. He cites support from the Zohar’s account of Yaakov’s prayer. End quote. One may say ‘for the seed of Yaakov,’ but not ‘for his seed,’ because the term ‘seed’ is traced to Yitzchak specifically through Yaakov. This is the primary view, and it is proper to recite it in this manner.
