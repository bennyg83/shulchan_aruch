7) [Seif 2] The widespread custom among all Sephardim is not to mention the verses, etc. The verses of the Mussaf offering should be said only on Shabbos and Rosh Chodesh, with which people are familiar and will not come to err. On other festivals, with which they are not familiar, they may come to err. Therefore, they do not mention them, but say, "As written in Your Torah," etc., thereby fulfilling the requirement of Malchuyos, Zichronos, and Shofaros, as stated in seif 4. Taz, s.k. 1; Levushei Serad; Ateres Zekeinim. This is from the words of the Tur. It further appears to me that there is another reason. On Shabbos, the section of the Shabbos offering is not read from the Torah because, for the reason stated by the poskim above, siman 283, it does not contain three verses. Therefore, it is recited in Mussaf. On Rosh Chodesh, although its section is read from the Torah scroll, it is nevertheless repeated in Mussaf because in the Torah reading that section is not read by itself, but together with the section of the daily offering. Therefore, the Rosh Chodesh offering is repeated by itself in Mussaf, lest people err and say that everything read from the Torah constitutes the offering of the day. However, on the three festivals, since the second Torah scroll contains only the offering of the day, there is no need to repeat it in Mussaf, for it was already read from the Torah with a blessing. Shiyarei Knesset HaGedolah, siman 488, os 10. However, what he wrote, "lest people err," etc., requires further study, for the verse explicitly says, "On the Shabbos day," etc. It is possible that his principal reason is that the section is not read by itself. The reason there is a distinction concerning whether it is read by itself is that when it is read by itself, people know that this reading is in place of the Mussaf offering and intend through this reading as though we had offered the Mussaf appropriate to that day, as it is written, "Let our lips substitute for bulls." When it is read together with other sections, people do not direct their attention to this, but regard it merely as a reading concerning the day, as on the other festivals. Therefore, they repeat it in Mussaf. All this follows the Sephardic custom stated above.
