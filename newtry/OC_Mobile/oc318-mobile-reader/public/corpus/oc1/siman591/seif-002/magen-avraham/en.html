Although the shaliach tzibbur discharges others on Rosh Hashanah and Yom Kippur, even one who is expert, nevertheless not every person can listen from beginning to end, as written in siman 124 [Tur]. Also, according to Rashi and Tosafos, he exempted only one who was under constraint and could not arrange his prayer. According to the explanation of the Ran, he discharges only from two blessings, but the seven blessings one is obligated to pray like the rest of Yom Tov. Therefore it is better for each person to pray silently all nine blessings. It also seems to me that even after the fact he did not fulfill his obligation with the shaliach tzibbur's prayer, since nowadays we interrupt with piyyutim; he has not fulfilled his obligation at all. See what I wrote in siman 68, and see siman 124, seif 10, and siman 592, seif 2. And we do not say the verses, etc. Even before the offerings, we do not say it [Tur, Minhagim, Levush, siman 582].
And we also say 'the Mussaf offerings,' etc. Although the Beis Yosef wrote this nearby in seif 3, the Rema wrote it here to inform us that even according to our custom, where we say the verses, one should say 'Mussaf offerings,' unlike the Bach, who was pressed not to say 'Mussaf offerings'; see there. This is also implied by the Ran, who wrote not to say the verses of the Mussaf of Rosh Chodesh, implying that the verses of Rosh Hashanah are said, and nevertheless he wrote to say 'the Mussaf offerings,' etc.; see there. In a place where the congregation prays together with the shaliach tzibbur and the shaliach tzibbur blows and errs, it is forbidden to speak and rebuke him, for the blasts according to the order of the blessings are not indispensable. All the more so, individuals need not hear them according to the order of the blessings. Even if they did not hear the sitting blasts, they should go back and blow after the prayer the set in which he erred, and that is sufficient [Rashba, siman 295].
