(There, s.k. 4, 'for the seed of Yaakov'). Note: See Chasam Sofer, Orach Chaim, siman 168. There he wrote regarding the words of the Magen Avraham that Edom is called the seed of Yitzchak. He then wrote that the Yerushalmi brings proof from Hashem saying to Yitzchak, 'To your seed I will give these lands,' yet He did not give them to Esav; and he rejected this, for He in fact gave him the Keini, Kenizi, and Kadmoni. This is astonishing in my eyes, for it is an explicit Talmud in Pesachim 119b: in the future the Holy One, blessed is He, will make a feast on the day He bestows kindness upon the seed of Yitzchak, and it is known that Esav is not included. If so, it is proven from the language of Chazal that 'the seed of Yitzchak' means specifically Yisrael. This is also proven from the language of the verse, 'on the day Yitzchak was weaned,' which refers to the day He bestows kindness upon Yitzchak. If so, Esav has no merit in this, for he is not included in the meaning of 'Yitzchak and his seed.' One cannot reject this by saying that it means part of his seed and that Esav is still called his seed, for if so, he should briefly have rejected the proof from the Yerushalmi. Rather, this must not be the meaning of the expression, and at first glance this requires great analysis. There is a slight way to reject it: the Talmud in Pesachim refers to the future, when this will already have been fulfilled and no remnant of the house of Esav will remain; see Avodah Zarah 10b. This is unlike now, in our prayer, where one must be concerned that Esav is included.
