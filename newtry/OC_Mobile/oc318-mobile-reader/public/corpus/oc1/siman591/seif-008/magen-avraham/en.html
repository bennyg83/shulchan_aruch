But he may pray Shacharis, since there is a congregation praying Shacharis in all places, and his prayer is not rejected [see what I wrote in siman 90, seif 9]. Mussaf, however, since its time is the whole day, some pray it in the morning and some delay it [Rashi]. If so, it appears that nowadays, when the custom throughout the dispersions of Yisrael is to pray Shacharis early, an individual should not pray Shacharis after the first hour, for certainly the congregation has already prayed. Although the shaliach tzibbur may still be saying piyyutim, this is not called the time when the congregation is praying, for even if they are praying Shacharis and he is praying Mussaf it is not called the time of the congregation, and all the more so piyyutim. If we would say that he should wait until after three hours, the time for Shema will already pass; and if he reads Shema at the end of the third hour and prays immediately, it is impossible to be precise. Therefore it seems to me that he should be very careful to pray in the first hour, and if he did not do so, he should pray in the second hour, for Rashi wrote that in the first three hours it is a time of anger, while Tosafos wrote that the anger is in the third hour; see there. If so, it implies that in the first and second hours it is permitted to pray, and this is also implied in Beis Yosef, who brought Tosafos. But the Tur and the Shulchan Aruch hold that he should not pray during all three hours, because it is difficult on Rashi's words: why did the Gemara need to say, 'If you say Torah, of which it is written truth,' etc.? Let it derive from the fact that it is a time of anger. Rather, necessarily the reason is not because of the anger, but because when a person prays and requests his needs, his deeds are examined; and since the Holy One, blessed is He, is engaged in Torah, He does not act beyond the letter of the law, and his prayer will be rejected. Therefore in a case like this one may rely on Rashi, as it seems to me. If you ask: do we not say at the end of siman 98 that only when one says, 'It is fitting that the Holy One, blessed is He, should fulfill my prayer,' are his sins mentioned, implying that when he prays simply, his sins are not mentioned? How then do we say here that perhaps his deeds will be examined? One may answer: there they certainly mention his sins, while here it is only a possibility. Rashi explained that the Mussaf prayers of the whole year are praise and narration of events, but on Rosh Hashanah, since he says Malchiyos, Zichronos, and Shofaros, the judgment is examined more, end quote. According to this, one must say that the Gemara's question, 'If so, in the morning too,' refers to Shacharis of the whole year, which is also a request for one's needs and is not praise and narration of events; one should likewise be concerned that the judgment is examined. It answers: since there is a congregation, etc. If so, one must be careful the entire year not to pray Shacharis alone in the first hour, for certainly the congregation does not pray at that hour nowadays in summer; and in winter the reverse is true, for after two hours all places have certainly prayed. This requires analysis for practical halacha. But Tosafos at the beginning of chapter 4 of Berachos wrote that the whole year it is permitted. Alternatively, one may say that Rashi wrote this according to the second answer, which answers: Torah, of which it is written truth, etc. We learn from there that although He is not engaged in judgment, nevertheless, since the person is praying, they examine his deeds; if so, the same would apply the whole year. But according to the first answer, specifically on Rosh Hashanah, when He is actually judging, they examine his deeds; on the rest of the days of the year He judges only for assignment or mere examination, and there is no concern at all, as it seems to me. If so, the Gemara properly asks, 'If so, in the morning too.' Sefer Chasidim, siman 763, wrote: since the Holy One, blessed is He, judges every day for three hours, and so too on Rosh Hashanah and Yom Kippur, what is the difference between them and other days? Rather, on Rosh Hashanah He judges the upper beings and lower beings regarding what will be for many years, such as seven years of famine, etc.; see there. One should say, 'and there is no explaining the concealment of Your Name,' and not say, 'and there is no explanation for the concealment of Your Name,' because there is an explanation for the concealment of His Name [Tashbetz in the name of Maharam].
