In the Shulchan Aruch: there are those who object, etc. Maharikash wrote as follows: He concludes, 'Blessed are You, Hashem, Who hears the sound of the teruah of His people Yisrael today with mercy'; on Shabbos he should not say 'today.' In Gan HaMelech, siman 2, he wrote about this that on Shabbos one should say, 'Who hears the remembrance of the teruah of His people Yisrael today with mercy.' Our custom is that both on weekday and on Shabbos we say neither the word 'today' nor the word 'remembrance'; rather, both on weekday and on Shabbos we say, 'Who hears the sound of the teruah of His people Yisrael with mercy.' See Shiyurei Knesses HaGedolah, who wrote that it is proper to say the word 'today'; see there, where he also wrote in the name of Maharach Alfandari that it can be explained as referring to the teruah made by the mouth. In Sheyarei Chagigah he wrote in the name of Beis Din that they relied on the fact that in the Mikdash and in the place of the beis din they would blow on Shabbos, as the Tur wrote in siman 588. In the name of the book Beis Tefillah he wrote another reason; see there. See above, siman 582, s.k. 5, regarding one who erred and said on Shabbos 'a day of teruah'; see there. See Tashbetz, part 3, siman 290 and siman 309, that in the text 'Today the world was conceived' and likewise 'This day is the beginning of Your works,' one should not change at all, even when it falls on Shabbos, and Birkei Yosef brought this; see there. See Seder HaYom, who wrote that when saying 'Today the world was conceived,' the custom is to bow oneself and say it with bent posture. The reason is that since they come to mention the great significance of the day, one must say it with fear and submission, so that God's mercy be aroused over him, etc.
