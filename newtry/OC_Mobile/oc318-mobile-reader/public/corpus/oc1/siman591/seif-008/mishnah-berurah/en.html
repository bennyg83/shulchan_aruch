(13) He should not pray, etc. This law can be imagined for an individual who lives in a settlement and cannot go to a place where there is a minyan, for in a city, even after three hours he should not pray alone.
(14) Alone, etc., until after three hours. During the first three hours, the Holy One, blessed is He, judges His world, and perhaps his judgment will be examined individually, and who can be found righteous? But when he prays with the congregation, 'the mighty God will not reject.' The reason Rosh Hashanah is mentioned, even though every day the Holy One, blessed is He, judges His world during the first three hours, is that there it is only an assignment for that day, whereas on Rosh Hashanah the Holy One, blessed is He, judges for the whole year and sometimes for many years. Specifically Mussaf, whose time is the whole day, people commonly delay until after three hours. But Shacharis people commonly pray in the morning within the first three hours, and therefore when he prays at that time, his prayer will be accepted together with the prayer of the congregation. In places where the custom is to pray Shacharis early, within the first or second hour, he should be precise to pray his prayer then also within that time [Magen Avraham and other authorities].
(15) The day. Likewise, an individual should be careful not to blow the mitzvah shofar blasts until after three hours of the day, because they may examine his judgment, as we wrote in siman 588, s.k. 2, in Mishnah Berurah.
