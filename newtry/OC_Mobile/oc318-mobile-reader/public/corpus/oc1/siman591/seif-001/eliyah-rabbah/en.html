[1] [Levush] 'Praiseworthy is the people,' etc. But in the Tur it is written until 'raise our horn,' end quote. Thus the custom is to say two more verses after 'Praiseworthy': 'In Your Name they rejoice all day, and through Your righteousness they are raised,' and 'For You are the glory of their strength, and through Your favor You raise our horn.' So too wrote Abudraham. Mateh Moshe wrote the reason: they correspond to three principles. 'In Your Name they rejoice,' etc., indicates the existence of Hashem, for His Name indicates His existence. 'The glory of their strength' refers to Torah, for there is no strength except Torah. 'Through Your favor You raise' means that the elevation is through Your providence, end quote. See siman 585, s.k. 6. One may also say that it alludes to the shofar of the revival of the dead, as the Rokeach wrote in siman 203, that whoever hears the shofar that the Holy One, blessed is He, will blow will live. Therefore he said, 'Praiseworthy is the people who know the teruah; Hashem, in the light of Your countenance they shall walk,' alluding to the incident at the end of Perek Hashoel that the righteous are truly alive in the grave, except that they have no permission to walk until the verse is fulfilled, 'for I am Hashem when I open your graves.' It seems to me that this is why we say, 'In Your Name they rejoice all day,' as the Rokeach wrote there, that the Holy One, blessed is He, will reveal to them the explicit Name. Therefore we say, 'For You are the glory of their strength,' meaning that in the future new explanations and reasons of the Torah will be revealed, as is known.
