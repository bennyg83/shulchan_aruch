1) [Seif 1] The congregation should pray silently, etc. Although we rule like Rabban Gamliel, who says that the shaliach tzibbur discharges their obligation, it is nevertheless preferable that individuals pray. For one who wishes to fulfill his obligation through the prayer of the shaliach tzibbur must concentrate on everything the shaliach tzibbur says, and if he fails to concentrate on even one word, he has not fulfilled his obligation. Not every person is capable of this. This is certainly so according to the Baal HaIttur, who wrote that the shaliach tzibbur discharges only one who is not proficient, but does not discharge one who is proficient even if he is in the synagogue. Therefore, it is preferable that every individual pray, and this is the practice. Tur. Furthermore, according to the explanation of Rashi and Tosafos, he discharges only one who was under duress and could not arrange his prayer. According to the Ran's explanation, he discharges him only from two blessings, but one is obligated to pray the other seven blessings as on every Yom Tov. Therefore, it is preferable for each person to pray all nine blessings silently. It further appears to me that even after the fact one has not fulfilled his obligation through the prayer of the shaliach tzibbur, because nowadays they interrupt with piyutim, and he has not fulfilled his obligation at all. Magen Avraham, beginning of the siman; Shulchan Aruch HaRav, os 3 and os 4. In a place where the custom is to interrupt the audible prayer with piyutim, even the shaliach tzibbur has not fulfilled his obligation after the fact if he did not pray silently. If he erred in the silent prayer, he must repeat the prayer like any individual who erred, and may not rely on the prayer he will repeat aloud, since it is interrupted. Mateh Ephraim, os 1. However, regarding what he wrote, "If he erred in the silent prayer," etc., the Eliyah Rabbah, os 3, left this requiring further study. Eshel Avraham, beginning of the siman, cited his words. It is possible that since we rule leniently in a doubt concerning blessings, one may be lenient. Nevertheless, one who cannot pray himself, such as a sick person, an elderly person, or the like, should concentrate on the repetition of the shaliach tzibbur and intend to fulfill his obligation, since no other option is possible. Regarding the order of concentration on the prayer of the shaliach tzibbur, see above, siman 124, seif 1, and our words there, with God's help.
