1) [Seif 1] The congregation should pray silently, etc. Although we rule like Rabban Gamliel, who says that the shaliach tzibbur discharges their obligation, it is nevertheless preferable that individuals pray. For one who wishes to fulfill his obligation through the prayer of the shaliach tzibbur must concentrate on everything the shaliach tzibbur says, and if he fails to concentrate on even one word, he has not fulfilled his obligation. Not every person is capable of this. This is certainly so according to the Baal HaIttur, who wrote that the shaliach tzibbur discharges only one who is not proficient, but does not discharge one who is proficient even if he is in the synagogue. Therefore, it is preferable that every individual pray, and this is the practice. Tur. Furthermore, according to the explanation of Rashi and Tosafos, he discharges only one who was under duress and could not arrange his prayer. According to the Ran's explanation, he discharges him only from two blessings, but one is obligated to pray the other seven blessings as on every Yom Tov. Therefore, it is preferable for each person to pray all nine blessings silently. It further appears to me that even after the fact one has not fulfilled his obligation through the prayer of the shaliach tzibbur, because nowadays they interrupt with piyutim, and he has not fulfilled his obligation at all. Magen Avraham, beginning of the siman; Shulchan Aruch HaRav, os 3 and os 4. In a place where the custom is to interrupt the audible prayer with piyutim, even the shaliach tzibbur has not fulfilled his obligation after the fact if he did not pray silently. If he erred in the silent prayer, he must repeat the prayer like any individual who erred, and may not rely on the prayer he will repeat aloud, since it is interrupted. Mateh Ephraim, os 1. However, regarding what he wrote, "If he erred in the silent prayer," etc., the Eliyah Rabbah, os 3, left this requiring further study. Eshel Avraham, beginning of the siman, cited his words. It is possible that since we rule leniently in a doubt concerning blessings, one may be lenient. Nevertheless, one who cannot pray himself, such as a sick person, an elderly person, or the like, should concentrate on the repetition of the shaliach tzibbur and intend to fulfill his obligation, since no other option is possible. Regarding the order of concentration on the prayer of the shaliach tzibbur, see above, siman 124, seif 1, and our words there, with G-d's help.
2) One who is under duress and cannot come to the synagogue, such as the people in the fields, an elderly person, or a sick person, is discharged by the shaliach tzibbur even though he did not come to the synagogue. Tur; Shulchan Aruch HaRav, os 2. This applies if they do not know how to pray or are unable to pray. If they are able to pray, they should pray in their own locations even though they cannot come to the synagogue, for the reason we wrote in the preceding os.
3) Ibid. Nine blessings. The Tur cites a dispute whether the congregation should pray seven or nine blessings. He wrote in the name of his father, the Rosh, of blessed memory, that one who prays seven recites a blessing in vain, because he omits some of the blessings. The custom of our forefathers is Torah and should not be changed, see there. So too wrote the Beit Yosef: "Since this does not arise as a practical matter, because the widespread custom throughout the world is to pray nine, I did not see fit to elaborate," end quote. He therefore ruled accordingly in the Shulchan Aruch. This is also the consensus of the Acharonim, and this is now the established custom in all places, that the congregation also prays nine blessings. This is likewise required according to the mystical meaning, as written above, siman 585, os 28, see there.
4) Ibid. Nine blessings. An individual who prayed Mussaf of Rosh Hashanah and, upon reaching "V'hasieinu," followed his habitual wording and said "Retzei," "Modim," and "Sim Shalom," must return to the beginning, unlike some who expressed doubt regarding this. Pachad Yitzchak, entry Mussaf of Rosh Hashanah; Ikkarei HaDat, siman 30, os 34; Pitchei Teshuvah.
5) Ibid. The shaliach tzibbur should also pray together with them. So wrote the Beit Yosef in the name of responsa Rivash, siman 37, that there is no doubt that the proper custom is for the congregation to pray first and for the shaliach tzibbur to pray silently with them, in order to arrange the prayer, and afterward for the shaliach tzibbur to repeat the prayer aloud, as stated at the end of Rosh Hashanah. This is the custom in Catalonia and in every place whose inhabitants are Torah scholars, see there. Sefer Chassidim, siman 784, wrote that the prayer should be fluent in his mouth, like R' Chanina ben Dosa, for when his prayer was fluent in his mouth it was accepted, end quote. We have already written above, siman 585, os 28, that according to the mystical meaning, both the silent prayer and the repetition are necessary, see there. So too we wrote above, siman 124, os 2, see there.
6) In a place where the congregation prays together with the shaliach tzibbur, and the shaliach tzibbur also sounds the shofar and errs, it is forbidden to speak or rebuke him. The blasts sounded during the order of the blessings are not indispensable, and certainly individuals do not need to hear them during the order of the blessings. Even if they did not hear the blasts sounded while seated, after the prayer they should repeat only the series in which he erred, and that is sufficient. Rashba, siman 295; Magen Avraham, s.k. 2. This refers to a place where they do not pray silently at all, but the shaliach tzibbur begins the prayer aloud and the congregation recites it with him word for word, as written by the Levushei Serad and Eshel Avraham, see there. The same applies according to the custom stated by the Arizal, of sounding the shofar during both the silent prayer and the repetition, as written above, siman 585, os 28. If the shofar blower errs during the silent prayer, it is forbidden to speak or rebuke him. The custom is merely to signal to him, whereupon he realizes that he erred and corrects it. See below, siman 592, os 2.
