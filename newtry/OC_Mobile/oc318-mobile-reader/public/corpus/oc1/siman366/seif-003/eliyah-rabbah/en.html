[6] [Levush] And likewise a minor, etc. A non-Jew may not collect the eruv, as stated by the Magen Avraham. The Maggid Mishneh in chapter 1 says a woman and servant may do so. One must say a non-Jew is worse because he lacks halachic reliability. I later found Lechem Mishneh in Perek BeKol Me'arvin state that one who does not acknowledge eruv does not qualify even for eruv chatzerot. See siman 409, seif 8.
[7] They may not change its location, etc. This remains true even if there is some reason to change it, but if there is a substantial reason they may change it. If the homeowner sold his house, we have no proof that the buyer acquires the privilege, as stated by Rav Avraham ben Chaim, responsum 70. This follows the Rambam's explanation of the Mishnah that the reason is benefit. The Gemara, however, explicitly gives the reason of suspicion: if people do not see the eruv there, they will suspect the residents of carrying without an eruv. According to that reason, even after sale it should remain there, as stated by the Magen Avraham. Perhaps because the property is now in another person's possession, people will not suspect, since they know one person's established privilege does not bind another owner. Tosafot in Gittin 60 writes similarly. See Choshen Mishpat at the end of siman 149. Moreover, Retzof Ahavah explains the Gemara even according to the Rambam as meaning that the original homeowner may suspect that the eruv was removed from his house specifically to deprive him of the benefit.
[8] And place it in another house. This is prohibited even if the original homeowner died, even if the second homeowner offers money to the courtyard residents to place it in his house, and even if he is a Torah scholar while the first was an unlearned person, as stated by the Radbaz, responsum 14. If at the time the eruv was first placed they stipulated that its location could be changed, it may be changed. If this is a service for which payment must be made, they may change and pay another person. In all these cases, if they transgressed and placed it in another house, we do not compel them to return it to the original house. Nevertheless, if they wish to return it, they may. Knesset HaGedolah leaves this point requiring study.
[9] The eruv in the synagogue, etc. One may carry within the synagogue without an eruv because no one resides there, as stated by the Magen Avraham. If the synagogue, or the house in which the eruv is placed, is itself in a situation where carrying there is prohibited, such as lacking an alleyway eruv or lying outside the eruv, the eruv is ineffective, because one cannot view the participants as though they reside there, as stated by the Taz.
