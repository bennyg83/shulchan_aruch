(18) Need not place bread — for the eruv is only that one consider as if all dwell there, and behold he dwells there:
(19) And need not, etc. a perutah — to exclude the one who says in the Gemara eruv is because of acquisition, that the homeowner transfers his house for the bread — and therefore the bread needed to be worth at least a perutah to be considered money; it teaches us it is not because of acquisition but merely partnership of dwelling; and from this reason also must give specifically bread and not suffice with other items such as a vessel and the like that are not food, for a person's primary dwelling is where his bread is — all explained in the Talmud:
(20) To exclude a gatehouse, etc. — for although made like houses, since they are traversed by all courtyard members they are not considered dwelling:
(21) A gallery — a high place in the courtyard with steps ascending to upper rooms; this place is high, enclosed by building, and people dwell there:
(22) Four amot by four amot — then it is fit for dwelling; and if wider than four but short in length and has enough for a quarter of four by four — there are opinions among poskim whether it is called dwelling; therefore preferable l'chatchila to place in another larger house; and see Biur Halacha:
(23) And even if it belongs to a minor — meaning the house where they place the eruv belongs to a minor; it teaches not to say one must transfer the house to them and a minor cannot receive transfer — it teaches it is not because of acquisition but because of dwelling; therefore permitted to place even in a minor's house:
(24) Accustomed to place it always — to exclude if not yet accustomed to this house, they need not object if they change to another house:
(25) They may not change it, etc. — and even if they have some reason in the matter, unless they have a great reason — then they may change:
(26) And place in another house — and even if the homeowner died; and even if the second gives money to courtyard members to place in his house; and even if he is a Torah scholar and the first an am ha-aretz; if at the start of giving they stipulated to change, permitted to change [Knesset HaGedolah]; and some Acharonim question this:
(27) Because of ways of peace — ways of peace here is because of suspicion [Gemara]; and Rashi explained: since the eruv was accustomed in that house, if you come to change its place, those entering that house and not seeing the eruv there will suspect courtyard members of carrying without eruv; and Tosafos explained the suspicion is they will suspect this homeowner steals bread from the eruv and therefore removed it from him to another house, and thereby come to dispute with them; per Rashi certainly one should be stringent even if he sold the house to another not to change the eruv's place; however per Tosafos one may say the buyer will not come to suspicion by removing the eruv from his house after the eruv was never in his hand; and all this in eruv where the custom is to place it in a house for free and the like; but a matter where they need to give money to this one, they may change to give to another, even if the second must also give money; and in every case if they transgressed and placed in another house they are not compelled to remove from there; however if the giver wishes to return and place where he was originally accustomed, the authority is in his hands; nevertheless requires study in practice [Magen Avraham in name of Knesset HaGedolah]:
(28) And even a minor — all from the above reason it is not because of acquisition requiring a person of understanding to transfer; and although a minor's act is nothing, nevertheless the eruv is made automatically when placed in the house; nevertheless through a non-Jew forbidden to collect l'chatchila, for he is suspected to steal and exchange good for bad:
(29) And it appears to me the reason, etc. — meaning although in synagogue not fit for dwelling because of its sanctity — for dwelling is a place of eating, and forbidden to eat in synagogue unless learners there as siman 151 — nevertheless one may uphold the custom as he concludes that our eruvei chatzerot have the law of joining alleys — meaning all courtyards and houses in the alley participate together in this eruv; and joining alleys need not be placed at all in a dwelling house, only in a guarded place as siman 386:
(30) And need not place in a dwelling house — and therefore since synagogue is not considered a dwelling house, we consider it only as the partners' courtyard, and permitted to carry from it to another courtyard without eruv chatzerot, for all courtyards are one domain as explained siman 372:
