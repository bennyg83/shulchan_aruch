[1] [Levush] From the houses, etc. The same applies from one house to another, as stated by the Magen Avraham, until they make an eruv. Without an eruv, one may pass an object from hand to hand in increments of less than four amot, as stated by Maharshal, responsum 51.
[2] [Levush] Bread, etc. Specifically bread. This differs from siman 386, which concerns eruv techumin, as stated by Knesset HaGedolah. Shiyarei Knesset HaGedolah explains at length that this is Rashi's view at the beginning of Perek BeKol Me'arvin. See there.
[3] It is prohibited to carry, etc. The boat has the status of a karmelit. From the sea into it, however, carrying is permitted. If there is a house aboard the boat, it is prohibited to transfer from the house to the boat. If the boat has walls ten handbreadths high, no eruv is needed because there is only one house. Small boats that are broad above and narrow below to a knife-edge are nevertheless private domains if their walls are ten high, because we extend the walls downward. Some say that if the boat is not four handbreadths wide within the lower three handbreadths of its height, it is like the sea and one may carry in it only four amot, as stated by the Magen Avraham. Know that the requirement of walls ten handbreadths high is measured from the boat's floor even if they do not rise ten above the water.
[4] And they do not place, etc. The Taz asks from Eruvin 49 why the Gemara does not say this is a practical difference between the opinions. He answers that the opinion based on acquisition also accepts the reason of residence; only when one explicitly says the eruv is based on acquisition does a utensil suffice, as Tosafot writes there. This is difficult, because then if he explicitly says so, according to Shmuel the eruv should work even in a house unfit for residence.
[5] That contains four amot, etc. Even if it does not actually have four by four amot but can contain a square of four by four, as written in Yoreh Deah siman 286, seif 13. Although some disagree there, regarding the rabbinic law of eruv it appears to me one may be lenient.
