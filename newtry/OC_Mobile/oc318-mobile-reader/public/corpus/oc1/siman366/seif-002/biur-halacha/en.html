Or encampment — see Mishna Berurah. And all this in time of peace; but when Israel went out to war, even milchemet reshut they are exempt from eruv chatzerot, and even upon their return from war [poskim]:

But a caravan — see the Rav HaMaggid, and it appears from him that the difference between encampment and caravan is that encampment means they encamp many days, and caravan means they encamp only one day — namely because of Shabbat; and some commentators copied this; but this is not required by the wording of Rambam's words; and in truth, from where do we know this? On the contrary, reason dictates that likewise if the caravan encamps some days, as is common among desert travelers who must wait some days for some reason — they also need not eruv, for this too fits Rambam's reasoning that those tents are not fixed for them; and I further say it is possible that even in an encampment where they encamp days, if they are only traveling on the road, they are like a caravan, and this reasoning also applies that those tents are not fixed for them now and they stood only to rest or for another reason; and in my humble opinion it appears clear that the matter is as we explained in Mishna Berurah — encampment means what we call in Ashkenazic language Lager, and it does not deal at all with a troop going from place to place, but only with those who encamp in a place — except they do not encamp in houses but in the field, as is known that the king's soldiers encamp in summer in the field, only they set up like tents; and it appears that even tents of reeds are also included in houses, and the decree of taking out from domain to domain applies; and all this in the manner I depicted — at least they encamp with permanence; but not for those traveling on the road — there is no difference at all between encampment and caravan, nor between one day and two days; rather whoever does not fix themselves for a long time — certainly they did not decree in this. And know that the essential law of caravan is not clear, and most early poskim disagree on this; see Rashba and Ritva what they wrote in name of R' Moshe Kohen and Or Zarua and R' Yonatan; and so in truth Rashi's view appears in chapter Kol Gagot (Eruvin daf 93) regarding three enclosures — Rashi wrote like those who eruv'd see there; and although there it deals with caravan, evidently caravan is not exempt from eruv chatzerot in essence. And incidentally we come to this — it is astonishing in the Mechaber's view here he rules anonymously like Rambam, and in siman 370 seif 3 he copied Rashi's wording that deals with caravan that eruv'd — and this is the opposite of Rambam's view; and possibly this is the intent of Gra that wrote on this law of caravan see 370 — requires study; and Taz is a mistake in his words — it must be siman 370 seif 3, and he intended what I challenged; and possibly one could distinguish and say Rambam spoke of caravan exempt only specifically when they surrounded the entire caravan outside with one partition, but inside each erected tents; and Rambam wrote they are exempt from eruv chatzerot because since they surrounded the entire caravan with one partition they are joined, and we do not regard how they divided inside with tents because those tents are not fixed — like one with no tents at all who dwell in a courtyard or one enclosure where carrying is permitted throughout; unlike siman 370 seif 3 that deals when each surrounded himself with a separate partition — then one cannot say they are like joined, for on the contrary each divided for himself; only there is an opening from one to another — if so, no better than two houses open to each other that need eruv; and so in truth appears from precision of Rambam's wording, which is the Mechaber's wording, that wrote because all are joined, etc.; and at first glance one could say Rashi also concedes to caravan law in the manner of siman 366; but to analyze well we see it is not so, for regarding four things exempt in encampment, one of them eruv chatzerot, Rashi also wrote like when each surrounded partitions separately with opening, etc. — exactly like daf 93; and specifically there because they go to war they exempted them, but otherwise they are obligated; and so Or Zarua concludes on encampment exempt from eruv chatzerot — it deals when they made partitions, etc.; but in caravan, etc.; and in truth I found in Shibolei Leket two explanations on encampment exempt from eruv chatzerot — one Rashi's explanation, and afterward R' Shimon ben HaItum's explanation as Rambam depicted, that they surrounded with one partition and divided inside with tents see there siman 96:

Houses on a ship — see what I wrote in Mishna Berurah in name of Magen Avraham and Eliyah Rabbah; and in truth at first glance from where is this, since many people dwell on a ship and it is fit to decree lest one take out from his house to the ship lest it be taking out from reshut ha-yachid to reshut ha-rabbim like dwelling in houses — and this was already discussed in Peri Megadim see there; and at first glance they took their words from Shibolei Leket siman 111, from which Rama copied his words here, which read there in that siman: and my brother R' Binyamin wrote that since we rule houses on a ship are obligated in eruv, their law is like a courtyard — if two Jews are divided in two houses they need eruv, and if not divided in two houses they need not eruv, etc. end there — except in truth there is no proof from there, for there he did not descend to this but because the ship belongs to a non-Jew as he concludes there; and in a non-Jew one does not forbid one Jew or even two Jews if they are in one house as explained below siman 382 seif 1; therefore he wrote and required two houses — but not that they need eruv specifically for two houses, but whenever there are domains of many people it forbids if there are not houses at all, for they did not decree on a bare courtyard; and possibly the reason is one should not decree on a ship more than on a courtyard, and in a courtyard it is known a courtyard is called such only when it has at least two houses open into it — therefore they decreed on a ship only in this manner:
