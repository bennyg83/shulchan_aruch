(5) Tent-dwellers, etc. — such as desert dwellers and the like:
(6) Or encampment — a place of encampment of soldiers in the field that we call in our language Lager; and see Biur Halacha:
(7) That they surrounded it with a partition — meaning there was a partition ten tefachim high encircling the tents and booths:
(8) One may not carry from tent to tent — and the same from tent to courtyard, namely to that area within the partition:
(9) Until they eruv — and for that reason we wrote above in seif 1:
(10) But a caravan — travelers going by desert road:
(11) That surrounded it with a partition — and they have within the enclosure tents for each separately:
(12) Because all, etc. — meaning since those tents are not fixed for them for dwelling but only temporarily when they stand to rest they erect tents — it is not called division of dwellings, and like all joined in one house it is considered; and see Biur Halacha. And see Biur HaGra who brought in name of commentators who disagree — their view is tents in a caravan need eruv, and in encampment exempt from eruv; but caravan is called only when less than ten, and from ten and above included in encampment:
(13) Houses, etc. — belonging to designated people; and the reason it is not inferior to tent-dwellers, etc. who need eruv chatzerot — for here too the houses are fixed for them for the entire duration of their sea journey; and see Magen Avraham and Eliyah Rabbah — specifically when there are some houses belonging to Jews; but if the ship has only one house and the rest dwell in the ship itself, no eruv needed at all, and permitted to take out from the house to the ship; and see Biur Halacha:
(14) Need eruv — and thereby permitted to carry from house to house and from houses to ship; and without eruv forbidden in all this; but in the ship itself permitted to carry throughout like a courtyard. And likewise those ships without houses but dwelling in the ship — they are kosher in one courtyard, permitted to carry throughout without eruv:
(15) That the ship has partitions — of ten tefachim, and its law is reshut ha-yachid:
(16) And if, etc. partitions — of ten tefachim, and we measure from its floor even though some tefachim are in water:
(17) Forbidden to carry in the ship — for it is karmelit; and in this manner permitted to carry from the sea into it and from it into the sea, for permitted to carry from karmelit to karmelit. Magen Avraham wrote if there are houses on the ship and the ship itself has no partitions of ten tefachim, forbidden to take out from the houses to the ship, for from reshut ha-yachid to karmelit it is stringent; and see Biur Halacha what I further brought in name of Magen Avraham:
