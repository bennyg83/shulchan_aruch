(55) Through his minor son and daughter — because their hand is as his hand, and the matter has not left his domain:

(56) And not through his slave, etc. — also because the slave's hand is as his master's hand and it has not yet left his domain:

(57) Even if they are dependent — and although in this manner their found object belongs to the father as explained in Choshen Mishpat siman 270 — this is only because of enmity lest he withhold sustenance if their found object were for themselves; but in our case where the father wishes to acquire for others through them, they acquire well, for since they are adults they have independent hand:

(58) Even if they are minors — and although a minor does not acquire for others everywhere, here for eruv which is merely d'rabbanan they were lenient regarding his slave and maidservant; nevertheless they did not wish to be lenient except regarding slave and maidservant, not regarding his minor children, as above because their hand is literally as his hand:

(59) And through his wife — for when another gives her a gift on condition the husband has no authority over it, it helps — behold she has hand to acquire for herself; likewise he can acquire through her for others:

(60) And even if she has no house — he used this wording to exclude the one who says in the Gemara specifically where she has her own house in her courtyard where she eats, then we say migo that acquisition in eruv works for her house it also works to acquire for others in eruv for their houses:

(61) And some say, etc. — they hold eruv resembles found objects — wherever for found objects one holds he has no independent hand and it belongs to father, or a woman who finds and it belongs to husband — likewise for eruv they have no independent hand and it has not left his domain:

(62) Through his son and daughter, etc. — meaning after she reaches maturity and is independent in all matters for her domain; nevertheless since she is dependent on his table and her found object belongs to father, also for eruv she has no hand for herself:

(63) As long as she has not reached maturity — for then her found object belongs to father:

(64) Who supports her — for then her found object belongs to husband:

(65) Or he said to her go out, etc. — and it deals with one who pledges her earnings for sustenance — for if she does not pledge for sustenance her found object does not belong to husband:

(66) And even if she has a house — meaning and eats there the sustenance her husband gives her; one might think migo she acquires for herself in this eruv for her house she also acquires in eruv for others — it teaches not; and the reason: in truth she herself needs no eruv at all, for she follows her husband as below siman 366 seif 6, and there is no migo:

(67) Even if he is a minor — since for found objects he has hand to acquire for himself when not dependent on his father's table:

(68) Who reached maturity and is not dependent — meaning specifically in this manner, not like the first view that holds when she reaches maturity even if dependent she acquires through her hand; and likewise through his wife specifically where he does not support her — for then her found object is for herself and not for husband:

(69) On the words of the lenient in eruv — meaning whether minor and not dependent on his table, or adult even if dependent; and likewise through his wife even though he supports her:

(70) Who has a wife, etc. — who does everything per his own will and not per his father's:
