At the time he acquires, etc. — see in Mishna Berurah; and behold from Beit Yosef and Bach in siman 395 it appears the blessing must be before the other raises to acquire on their behalf — then it is called prior to performance; and for b'dieved certainly he may bless even after acquisition, as Magen Avraham and Sh'ar Efraim wrote, because the essence of eruv acquisition occurs at Shabbat entry, and especially if still before the eruv was placed in its place it is certainly still prior to performance, as Perishah and Mo'ed Katan wrote; and even l'chatchila one may study that perhaps better to bless after acquisition — for when collecting eruv from others one may bless even when beginning to collect since eruv action already started through collection the beadle raised from some people; unlike when he wishes to eruv from his own for all before acquisition — still no eruv name on the bread at all, for it has not left his domain at all, like still in his basket — and the essence eruv name is made through acquisition making all partners through acquisition for them through another; and when blessing before acquisition it is prior to prior — still lacking the complete act of acquisition, without which the bread lacks the essence of eruv act [like one cannot bless on tefillin before the parshiyot are placed in the housings]; and in truth siman 527 Tur and Shulchan Aruch explicitly mention blessing after acquisition; and although there on erev Shabbat indeed dissenting views on this — one may say it is different there because eruv tavshilin name applies even before acquisition, for he too alone needs eruv tavshilin; unlike our case where the bread has not left his domain — no eruv name at all, and one cannot bless here that the essence act is still lacking entirely, as said; and possibly Beit Yosef and Bach's intent is when he gave the bread to the other for eruv and before the other raises to acquire for all the giver blesses — this is not prior to prior, since he already gave to another for eruv name — eruv name applies even though he has not yet acquired for anyone; similar to when the beadle began collecting, he may bless on mitzvot of eruv; nevertheless from siman 527 it is proven explicitly he may bless l'chatchila even after acquisition; and so Perishah and Mor U'Ketzia here; and as said he may bless even after acquisition and before placement; therefore one who practices thus certainly should not be objected to; and it further appears even one who practices blessing before acquisition — the formula of this eruv is good to say after acquisition:
