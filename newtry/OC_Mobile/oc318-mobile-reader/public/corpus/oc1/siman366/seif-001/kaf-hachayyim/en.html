1) [Se'if 1] A courtyard into which many houses open, etc. This refers to a case in which two or three householders live in the houses in that courtyard. If only one householder lives in that courtyard, an eruv is unnecessary, as stated below at the beginning of siman 382. See there.
2) There. The Sages prohibited carrying, etc. Under Torah law, they may carry throughout the courtyard and from their houses into the courtyard, for it is a complete private domain. The Sages, however, prohibited carrying from their houses into the courtyard until they establish an eruv. This is because the houses are separate domains, each designated for its own owner, while the courtyard is a domain shared by everyone and therefore somewhat resembles a public domain. If they were to carry from the houses into the courtyard, people might say that it is permitted to carry from a private domain into a public domain. Tur; Levush; Olas Shabbos, subsection 1; Shulchan Aruch HaRav, subsection 1.
3) There. The Sages prohibited carrying from their houses into the courtyard, etc. Within the courtyard itself, however, it is permitted to carry throughout the courtyard utensils that began Shabbos within it. It is likewise permitted to carry from one courtyard to another, as stated below at the beginning of siman 372. See there.
4) There. From their houses into the courtyard, etc. The same applies to carrying directly from one house to another without passing through the courtyard, which is forbidden. Magen Avraham, subsection 1; Eliyah Rabbah, subsection 1; Tosefes Shabbos, subsection 1; Shulchan Aruch HaRav, subsection 1.
5) Two houses belonging to two people, with no courtyard between them, are likewise forbidden to carry from one to the other without an eruv. Tosafos Yom Tov, chapter 7 of Eruvin. Responsa Dvar Shmuel, siman 182, rules likewise. Rabbi Moshe Zacuto there and Shibolei HaLeket, however, disagree. Since no area remains in which they have equal rights, they maintain that an eruv is unnecessary. Their words are cited in Kovetz on Rambam, Hilchos Eruvin, chapter 1, which concludes that one who is stringent should in any event not recite a blessing. See there. Pischei Teshuvah.
6) There. Until they establish an eruv. If they did not establish an eruv, it is forbidden even to pass an object from hand to hand, one person to another, within four amos. Responsa Maharshal, siman 51. The later authorities cite his words.
7) There. Namely, they collect bread, etc. Specifically bread is used. This is unlike siman 386, which concerns eruvei techumin. Knesses HaGedolah and Shiyurei Knesses HaGedolah in Hagahos HaTur; Eliyah Rabbah, subsection 2. See below, siman 386, subsection 4.
