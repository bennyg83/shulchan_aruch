[15] And with this intention, etc. This implies that the synagogue attendant need not formally grant ownership to everyone, unlike siman 395, which says that without such acquisition there is no eruv. Nachalat Tzvi and the Taz there rule accordingly. The Magen Avraham writes that initially it is preferable to be stringent and have another person acquire it for everyone. The Taz at the end of this siman writes that the attendant should give the eruv to the rabbi, who should acquire it on behalf of the entire congregation. If night fell at the beginning of the first day of Yom Tov and this acquisition was not made, one may not perform the acquisition on Yom Tov except conditionally, as in siman 528. If Yom Tov is not adjacent to Shabbos, the eruv should not be made until Shabbos. Nevertheless, if they forgot to grant ownership until Shabbos, carrying is permitted.
