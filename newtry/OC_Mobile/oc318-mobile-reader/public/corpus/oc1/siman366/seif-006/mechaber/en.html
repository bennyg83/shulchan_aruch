One does not make an eruv with a slice even if it is very large; but with a whole loaf, even if very small, one makes an eruv — provided there is in them enough that there is in them the required measure; however, if there was taken from it the amount of a baker's challah, which is one forty-eighth, one makes an eruv with it even if it was not set aside for challah; and if it was sliced and joined with a toothpick that he inserted one end inside the loaf and the second end inside the slice — if it is not recognizable that it was sliced, one makes an eruv with it:And there are those who explained that the reason one makes an eruv only with a whole loaf is that the entire eruv together should be one whole loaf; therefore the custom is to gather from each house a little flour and make one whole challah and make an eruv with it (Maggid and Hagahot Raavad chapter 1 of laws of Eruvin); and so the custom is widespread in all these lands; and one must be careful that the challah have the measure explained below siman 368 seif 3; and even if flour remained and' the challah was not made from all of it, nevertheless it is an eruv, for it is no worse than if one grants ownership to all; and with the intent that they gave their flour from the outset thus it appears to me:
