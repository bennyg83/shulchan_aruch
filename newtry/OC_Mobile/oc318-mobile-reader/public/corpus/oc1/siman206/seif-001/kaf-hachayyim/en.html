1) [Seif 1] If one recited Borei Pri HaAdamah over tree fruits, he fulfilled his obligation. The reason is that the tree's principal source is the earth. Berachos 40b; Beis Yosef; Olas Tamid, paragraph 1. Even if he intentionally recited Borei Pri HaAdamah over tree fruit, he fulfilled his obligation. Eshel Avraham, paragraph 1.
2) There: Borei Pri HaAdamah, he fulfilled his obligation. The Tur rules accordingly. Tosafos there, Rashba, and Rosh write the same, as stated in Beis Yosef. Beis Yosef nevertheless writes that the Rambam in chapter 8 rules that he did not fulfill his obligation. Kesef Mishneh there, chapter 8, law 10, however, writes that one textual version of the Rambam states that he did fulfill his obligation; see there. The version before us likewise says that he fulfilled his obligation. The later authorities agree that he fulfilled his obligation, and this is our practical ruling.
3) There: Borei Pri HaEtz, he did not fulfill his obligation. Regarding items growing on a tree whose blessing is Borei Pri HaAdamah because the fruit is incomplete or because they are not the principal fruit, if one recited Borei Pri HaEtz, he fulfilled his obligation. Magen Avraham, paragraph 1; Shulchan Gavoah, paragraph 1; Yad Aharon, Hagahos HaTur; Birkei Yosef, paragraph 2; Chayei Adam, rule 51, paragraph 12; Chesed LaAlafim, paragraph 2. See Eliyah Rabbah, paragraph 1, Maamar Mordechai, paragraph 1, and Even HaOzer, who question Magen Avraham's cited ruling. Nevertheless, since all the aforementioned authorities agree with him, and in cases of doubt concerning blessings we rule leniently, we follow Magen Avraham. Likewise, regarding something whose proper blessing is Borei Pri HaEtz or Borei Pri HaAdamah, but over which one recites Shehakol because it was crushed, if after the fact one recited the blessing appropriate to that species, he fulfilled his obligation. Chayei Adam there.
3) Likewise, regarding ground produce whose law is to require Shehakol, such as fruits that are better cooked than raw, if one recited Borei Pri HaAdamah, he fulfilled his obligation. Panim Meiros, volume 1, siman 58; Birkei Yosef, paragraph 3.
4) There: Therefore, if he is uncertain, etc., he recites Borei Pri HaAdamah. Olas Tamid, paragraph 1, writes that since the Rambam disagrees and holds that one who recited Borei Pri HaAdamah over tree fruit did not fulfill his obligation, it is preferable to recite Shehakol, thereby fulfilling his obligation according to all opinions; see there. Rishon LeTziyon, folio 14, writes the same; see there. Birkei Yosef, paragraph 4, however, writes that since the Rambam's view is unclear and some textual versions state that he fulfilled his obligation, the primary ruling follows Maran; see there at length. Shaarei Teshuvah cites this. Mateh Yehudah, paragraph 1, agrees and challenges Olas Tamid; see there. The later authorities concur: Shulchan Aruch HaRav, paragraph 1; Chayei Adam, rule 58, paragraph 4; Chesed LaAlafim, paragraph 1; Kitzur Shulchan Aruch, siman 56, paragraph 2.
5) There: Over everything, if he said Shehakol, etc. This implies that even over the fragrance of spices, if one said Shehakol, he fulfilled his obligation. See below, siman 216, paragraph 32.
6) There: If he said Shehakol, he fulfilled his obligation, etc. Likewise, if one recited Borei Minei Mezonos over any item, he fulfilled his obligation, for in the Torah's language everything except water and salt is called nourishment. Chayei Adam there, paragraph 3; Ben Ish Chai, parashas Balak, paragraph 13.
