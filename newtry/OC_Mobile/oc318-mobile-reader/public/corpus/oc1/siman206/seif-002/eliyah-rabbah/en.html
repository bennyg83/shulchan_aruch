If there were before him, etc. Taz wonders why Shulchan Aruch requires that both fruits be before him, since even if a tree fruit was brought after he recited the blessing, if he intended to exempt it he has fulfilled his obligation. In my humble opinion, the phrase "before him" teaches a greater novelty: even when both are before him, specific intent is still required; a blessing recited without such intent does not exempt the tree fruit. Beit Yosef writes that if an etrog and an olive, one of the seven species, were before him and he blessed over the etrog, then unless he intended to exempt the olive he must bless again over the olive. Likewise, if nuts and apples were before him and he blessed over the nuts, he must bless over the apples unless he intended to exempt them. If the apple is more beloved to him than the nuts, it is improper that it be exempted incidentally unless he had it in mind initially. See below, end of siman 246.
