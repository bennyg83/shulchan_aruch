Regarding items growing on a tree whose blessing is Borei Pri HaAdamah because the fruit is incomplete or because they are not the principal fruit, if one recited Borei Pri HaEtz, he fulfilled his obligation. Magen Avraham, paragraph 1; Shulchan Gavoah, paragraph 1; Yad Aharon, Hagahos HaTur; Birkei Yosef, paragraph 2; Chayei Adam, rule 51, paragraph 12; Chesed LaAlafim, paragraph 2. See Eliyah Rabbah, paragraph 1, Maamar Mordechai, paragraph 1, and Even HaOzer, who question Magen Avraham's cited ruling. Nevertheless, since all the aforementioned authorities agree with him, and in cases of doubt concerning blessings we rule leniently, we follow Magen Avraham. Likewise, regarding something whose proper blessing is Borei Pri HaEtz or Borei Pri HaAdamah, but over which one recites Shehakol because it was crushed, if after the fact one recited the blessing appropriate to that species, he fulfilled his obligation. Chayei Adam there.
3) Likewise, regarding ground produce whose law is to require Shehakol, such as fruits that are better cooked than raw, if one recited Borei Pri HaAdamah, he fulfilled his obligation. Panim Meiros, volume 1, siman 58; Birkei Yosef, paragraph 3.
