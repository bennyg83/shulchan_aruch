If one took a fruit in his hand to eat it, recited the blessing over it, and it fell from his hand and was lost or became repulsive, he must recite the blessing again, even though more of the same species was before him when he blessed over the first one. {Rama: This applies only when he did not have in mind to eat the other fruit. [Hagahot Maimoniyot, chapter 4; Kol Bo; Agur; Abudarham; Responsa Maharil, siman 92]} He must say, 'Blessed be the name of the glory of His kingdom forever and ever,' because he uttered the Divine Name in vain. If, when the fruit fell, he had already said, 'Blessed are You, Hashem,' but had not yet said 'our God,' he should conclude, 'teach me Your statutes,' so that it appears that he is reciting a verse and he has not uttered the Divine Name in vain. But one who stands beside a flowing water channel recites a blessing and drinks, even though the water he drinks was not before him when he recited the blessing, because that was his intention from the outset. {Rama: See above, seif 1, regarding the law when one recited a blessing in error.}
