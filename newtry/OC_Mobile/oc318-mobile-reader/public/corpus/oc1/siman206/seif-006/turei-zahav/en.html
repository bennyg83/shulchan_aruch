Only if he had not intended to eat it. I have found here a new dispute between the early and later authorities that Beit Yosef did not mention. Beit Yosef cites the Rosh, who derives from the Yerushalmi that if fruits were before a person and he intended to eat them, but the item over which he blessed fell from his hand, he must recite a new blessing over the fruits he now eats. Thus, his prior intention at the time of the blessing is insufficient. The Tur's wording, adopted here by Shulchan Aruch, likewise implies this, for it states that even though more of the same species was before him when he blessed over the first item, he must bless again. Hagahos Maimoniyos, however, cites Rabbeinu Tam that if one blessed over a cup and it spilled, and another cup was brought, the first blessing suffices, for what difference is there between this cup and another? Beit Yosef explains that Rabbeinu Tam refers to a case where he intended the second cup from the outset. Rabbeinu Yonah writes in the chapter Keitzad Mevarchin that the Yerushalmi teaches that one who blessed over an item that fell from his hand need not repeat the blessing when he again eats that species. His wording somewhat implies that he had not originally intended to eat more, for the phrase “when he again eats” suggests a new act not contemplated initially. Beit Yosef, however, writes after citing the Rosh, “Rabbeinu Yonah writes likewise.” I do not know how he derived this, for according to the Rosh, even prior intent to eat more does not help. There is thus a dispute among the early authorities. In siman 209 it will be explained that Maharil and Mahari Weil also disagree about this. Rama's ruling here, that the matter depends on prior intent, therefore requires explanation. Rambam, Tur, and Shulchan Aruch already rule that if one blessed and afterward additional fruit was brought, he must bless again. That law is derived from the same Yerushalmi, yet Rama does not state there that the matter depends on prior intent. In truth, both cases concern the same circumstances and one is learned from the other.
For this was his original intention. This is difficult in light of seif 5, which rules that if additional fruit was brought after the blessing, he must bless again, although he initially intended it. According to Rabbeinu Tam, that case may concern one who did not initially intend the later fruit, as explained above. According to the Rosh, however, prior intention does not help even when the item over which he blessed was lost or became repulsive, as explained above. The same should therefore apply where he blessed and additional fruit was then brought, since that ruling is derived from the same Yerushalmi concerning one who took a lupine and it fell from him. One might say that the Rosh does not accept the ruling that when fruit is brought after the blessing a new blessing is required where he initially intended it. The lost item is different, because the very object over which the blessing was recited disappeared. Where he blessed intending that an item be brought and that item was brought, no new blessing is required. The difficulty remains, however, against Tur and Shulchan Aruch, who rule here that no new blessing is required over water flowing in a channel, yet rule that fruit brought afterward requires a new blessing. One must distinguish that the arrival of the water is certain, whereas fruit to be brought later may be prevented by some unforeseen circumstance. Levush writes similarly. Yet this seems to make the law dependent upon uncertain degrees. If the person bringing the fruit stands in an adjacent room and no foreseeable obstacle exists, would no new blessing be required? One cannot answer that the Sages made no distinction, for one may not invoke such a principle to justify an unnecessary blessing. Beit Yosef cites Shibolei HaLeket that one who blessed intending to eat but could not do so immediately, and was delayed longer than kedei dibbur, must bless again. Since this ruling is not settled, if one blessed and afterward fruit was brought before him, he should bring another species requiring the same blessing and intend to discharge these fruits as well.
