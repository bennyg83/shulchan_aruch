(1) If he comes — but lechat'chilah it is proper to come to the synagogue early so that he need not skip, for the books wrote that the Maggid warned Beit Yosef to come to the synagogue early so he could pray in order and not with skipping, because one who does so reverses the channels; and many men of action practice praying in order for this reason even if they were late coming to the synagogue. But in the responsum of Chacham Tzvi siman 36 he wrote that what is written in Sefer HaZohar that one should pray in order — that is when he is not praying with the congregation; but if he was late coming to the synagogue and came when the congregation is praying, all agree he skips in order to pray with the congregation; and likewise the Pri Chadash in siman 56. And even one who is not accustomed to praying with the congregation regularly and does not wake so early — nevertheless if it happened to him some time that he came to the synagogue and found the congregation at the end of Pesukei Dezimra, he should do as written in the Shulchan Aruch, for at least this time he has merit to pray with the congregation. And see in Sha'ar HaTevunot that specifically if he can say at least Baruch She'amar, Ashrei, and Yishtabach — and see what we wrote below in the name of Mishkenot Yaakov.

(2) At the end of Pesukei Dezimra — that is, after he places the tefillin on his head, the congregation will be standing at the end of Pesukei Dezimra. And see in Chayei Adam who wrote that if he was late coming, he should at least see to recite before he begins to pray the blessing of Asher Yatzar and Elokei Neshamah and birchas haTorah [for if not so, the opinion of the Pri Chadash regarding Elokei Neshamah is that he should not say it after the prayer because he fulfilled it in the blessing Mechaye hametim — and see at the end of this siman what we wrote on this; and in Beit HaTefillah there are opinions on this in siman 47 seif 8 if he did not learn immediately after prayer — see there; and netilat yadayim is established only before prayer].

(3) He says, etc. — so that he may pray with the congregation, for prayer with the congregation is desirable and accepted before the Holy One, blessed be He.

(4) More — that is to say, more than the measure of reciting Tehillah l'David and all the Halleluyahs until Kol haneshamah tehallel kah; they precede by law the recitation of Hodu, for they are the essence of pesukei dezimra [Magen Avraham and the Gra]. And the Magen Avraham also wrote that reciting Vayevarech David until le-shem tifartekha is likewise before Hodu la-the Omnipresent kiru, but not before Halleluyah.

(5) Until "ve-hu rachum" — the Chayei Adam wrote that on Shabbat one should skip the psalms added on Shabbat from Lamenatzeach until Yehi kevod, and recite as on a weekday because they are frequent; and if he has more time he should say Lamenatzeach ledavid beshanoto and Tefillah le-Moshe, and they take precedence over the other psalms [as stated in the Zohar]. He also wrote that on Shabbat one is obligated to say Nishmat, which is called [Pesachim 118] the blessing of the song; and it is better to skip [other] psalms; and if he has no time at all he should say Baruch she'amar, Tehillah l'David, Nishmat, and Yishtabach — see there.

(6) He should read Shema — and in the responsum of Mishkenot Yaakov he elaborated on this and proved that the blessing of Baruch She'amar and Yishtabach is an ancient enactment from the days of the Tannaim; therefore it is better to pray individually than to skip Baruch She'amar and Yishtabach entirely. However, if he need not skip entirely — so that he can say Baruch She'amar, Ashrei, and Yishtabach while the chazan prolongs the words from Yotzer onward — all agree he should do so, for the main concern is that he pray the Shemoneh Esreh with the congregation in a whisper; but it is plain that all this is on condition that he not thereby lack the mitzvah of Krias Shema as explained below in siman 61, for we do not push aside fulfilling a mitzvah in its proper form for the sake of prayer with the congregation.

(7) And he should pray with them — but if he will not reach the congregation's Shacharit, even if he would begin at Yotzer Or he should not begin at all; rather, all agree he should pray in order without the congregation, and he should not pray the Shacharit Amidah alone with the congregation, for juxtaposing geulah to prayer in the morning is preferable to prayer with the congregation [Beit Yosef and Darkei Moshe in siman 236].

(8) After them — that is, Baruch She'amar and Yishtabach, for according to most poskim they were enacted only before the prayer.

(9) And nevertheless he should say [them] — that is, he is obligated to say them after the prayer if he did not say them before the prayer. And see above in siman 47 seif 8 regarding birchas haTorah, and in Mishna Berurah there in se'if kaf-zayin what we wrote in the name of Ateret Tzvi; and see in Pri Chadash who wrote that also the blessing Elokai Neshamah one should not say after the prayer, for he already fulfilled it in the blessing Mechaye hametim; and see in Peri Megadim who wrote that from the words of the Rama it does not imply so; and likewise in Sefer Maamar Mordechai disagrees with him; and likewise in Sha'ar HaTevunot in siman 6 he wrote that it seems he did not fulfill [it]; and likewise it is implied from the explanation of the Gra in this siman — and see in Biur Halacha. And nevertheless it is better lechat'chilah to fulfill according to all — that is, if he already began to pray and forgot beforehand the blessing Elokai Neshamah (=Elokei neshamah=), he should intend expressly in the blessing Ve-ne'eman attah that he does not wish to exempt through it the blessing Elokai Neshamah (=Elokei neshamah=), and then all agree he blesses it after the prayer; for although blessings do not require intention to fulfill, nevertheless where one expressly intends not to fulfill, it is valid — see there further [Peri Megadim]. And see in Biur Halacha what we wrote: if he forgot birchas haTorah and began the blessing of Shema, he should intend expressly in the blessing Ahavah Rabbah to exempt birchas haTorah and should learn immediately after prayer, and thereby he fulfills according to all.

(10) All the blessings — and see in Sefer Maamar Mordechai and in Sefer Nehar Shalom that the time for all the blessings is all day bedieved; and likewise wrote Sefer Maaseh Rav to the Gra z"l, and he added there further that even at night until the time of sleep is their obligation if he forgot to say them beforehand. And it appears to me that if sleep seizes him, all agree he may no longer bless the blessing Ha-maavir sheinah [compare in Be'er Heitev in siman 484 se'if 5, see there]. And nevertheless lechat'chilah one must be careful not to delay the blessings more than four hours into the day; but bedieved at least he may bless them until midnight, for so it is proven from many acharonim that he did not lose the blessings after four hours; and one who is lenient to rely bedieved on the above-mentioned great ones and bless them after midnight — one should not protest against him — and see in Biur Halacha.
