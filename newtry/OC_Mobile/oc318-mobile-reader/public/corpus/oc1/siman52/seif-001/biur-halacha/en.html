And nevertheless he shall say, etc.—See in Mishna Berurah what we wrote, that Maggid Mishneh disagrees with Peri Chadash; although in Sha'arei Teshuvah above in siman 6 in the name of Shulchan Tziyyon he wrote that Maggid Mishneh only doubts him—the one who examines it will see that it is not so; and Shulchan Tziyyon derived this from what Maggid Mishneh wrote there at the end, that nevertheless once it happened to him that he could not recite the blessings before prayer and he was careful at least to bless Elokai Neshamah and birchas haTorah in order to escape the concern of Peri Chadash as well. But in truth he does not retract this at all from his earlier words, for it is certainly better to do so lechat'chilah, but not that there should be a delay bedieved. And the Sha'arei Teshuvah above, who did not disagree completely in practice with Peri Chadash—it appears he relied on Shulchan Tziyyon, who wrote that Maggid Mishneh, who also disagrees, did not have the heart to disagree in practice. And I say in this matter as well: one who wishes to rely bedieved on Peri Chadash should not be protested, since Chayei Adam and Darchei Hachaim copied his words; but one who wishes to bless and rely on all those many authorities we wrote likewise did not lose. And one who wishes to escape doubt—it appears to me he should sleep a little during the day, and then when he awakens from his sleep he should bless Elokai Neshamah, for Beit Yosef in siman 231 wrote that the Agur in the name of R' Yehuda of Speyer wrote that one who awakens from the sleep of daytime sleep must bless Elokai Neshamah; and likewise in Shibolei HaLeket in the name of Rabbeinu Simcha and Rabbi Avigdor Cohen; and likewise in Sefer HaTanya—only that Beit Yosef concluded there on this and they did not practice so; if so, here one should do so for everyone. And likewise in Sefer Toras Avigdor. And know further that Peri Megadim gave this advice mentioned in Mishna Berurah regarding birchas haTorah: if he forgot and began the blessing of Shema and has not yet said birchas haTorah, he should intend explicitly in Ahavah Rabbah not to fulfill through it the mitzvah of birchas haTorah—and then he is not exempt from birchas haTorah according to everyone. And in my humble opinion this requires investigation according to what Mordechai wrote, that therefore they said in the Gemara that he already fulfilled it in Ahavah Rabbah, because Ahavah Rabbah itself is birchas haTorah; and in truth his words are proven in Yerushalmi chapter 1 of Berachos, that one calls there Ahavah Rabbah birchas haTorah, because Shema is also Torah—if so, how can he divide the intention between them? That is, regarding Shema he should intend that this blessing be for the sake of a blessing on it [for although blessings are not indispensable, nevertheless lechat'chilah it was established for its sake]; and regarding the rest of words of Torah he should intend that this blessing is recited on condition of not fulfilling through it birchas haTorah. And see above what I wrote in siman 47 in Biur Halacha in the name of Terumat HaDeshen on this matter, which seemingly is proof for Peri Megadim—and it requires investigation in practice. And in my humble opinion it appears better if he forgot birchas haTorah and began the blessing of Shema that he intend explicitly in the blessing of Ahavah Rabbah to exempt through it birchas haTorah, and he should learn immediately after prayer, for Ahavah Rabbah will be counted for him in place of birchas haTorah according to everyone—as written in siman 47, see there in Mishna Berurah seif kaf vav; and with this advice he also emerges completely clean from concern of a blessing that is unnecessary:

**All the blessings**—see in Mishna Berurah what we wrote in the name of the poskim regarding the time of blessings. And behold in Darchei Hachaim, Laws of Onen, law 5—it is implied from it regarding Magen Avraham in siman 70, seif kaf alef: birchas hashachar for every person is only until four hours of the day, similar to the time of the blessing of Shema alone for birchas haTorah [if not fulfilled through Ahavah Rabbah] and the three blessings "who did not make me a gentile, slave, and woman"—for their time is all day. And this is not so, for Magen Avraham did not say only for one who was an onen at the main time of his obligation in the morning, when he was then exempt—therefore exempt after four hours; but for one who at the beginning of his time of obligation was liable, he is certainly liable even after four hours. So it is implied from his language explicitly, and so it is implied in Levushi Serad and in Sefer Chochmas Adam and in Sefer Shulchan Shelomo; and so he wrote explicitly in Sefer Machatzit HaShekel. And it could be that for every person the opinion of Magen Avraham is like the Gra and Nehar Shalom and Maamar Mordechai, that its time bedieved is all day—but until midnight at least it is proven explicitly from his language that he did not lose the blessings; and it could be that Darchei Hachaim also did not speak there only regarding one who was at the beginning of his obligation exempt—and although on this too they disagree, Nehar Shalom and Maamar Mordechai on Magen Avraham—nevertheless in Sha'at and in Gemara they upheld his words as halacha. But if so, Darchei Hachaim was very brief in his language emerging from all this, that until midnight one should certainly not be concerned that he will lose the blessings for this; and even after midnight one may rely bedieved to be lenient, for I did not find one of the poskim who gives a time for blessings according to what is practiced today—as the poskim who follow worldly custom practice, he blesses. After writing all these I found the responsum of Rashba [brought in siman 4 in Beit Yosef, beginning "and it is implied from the words of the Rosh," etc.]—and it appears there seemingly from his language that the main birchos hashachar were established in the morning, and for this reason they also established netilas yadayim specifically in the morning—see there. Therefore it appears that lechat'chilah one should certainly be stringent to bless them in the morning at the time of prayer; and bedieved one may rely on the above-mentioned elders at least until midnight, for prayer itself is obligatory in essence until midnight, as below in siman 89, seif alef, see there; and one who blesses them afterward bedieved should not be protested—and as above:
