Pesukei DeZimrah. The Maggid warned the Beit Yosef to come to the synagogue early, so that he would be able to pray in the prescribed order and not by skipping passages, for one who does so reverses the channels of divine influence. This is stated in the Arizal's order of prayer, and many people of pious conduct therefore customarily pray in the proper order even when they arrive late at the synagogue. However, Responsa Chacham Tzvi, siman 36, writes that the Zohar's statement that one must pray in order applies when one is not praying with the congregation. If, however, he came late to the synagogue and arrived while the congregation was praying, all agree that he should skip passages in order to pray with the congregation. See there. Peri Chadash rules likewise in siman 50. See there.
They proclaimed. Keep this rule in hand: all the passages beginning with Hallelujah take precedence over "Give thanks to Hashem, proclaim His Name." Thus, when there is insufficient time to recite both the Hallelujah passages and "Give thanks to Hashem, proclaim His Name," the latter is deferred in favor of the Hallelujah passages, because the essential Pesukei DeZimrah extend from "A Tehillim of praise by David" through "Let every soul praise Hashem." "David blessed" likewise precedes "Give thanks to Hashem, proclaim His Name," but it does not precede the Hallelujah passages. See Magen Avraham.
