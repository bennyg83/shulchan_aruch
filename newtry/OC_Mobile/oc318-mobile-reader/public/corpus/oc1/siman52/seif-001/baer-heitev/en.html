**dezimra.** The Maggid warned Beit Yosef to come to the beis haknesses early in the morning so that he could pray in order and not by skipping, for one who does so reverses the channels of the Ari's siddur; and many men of deed are accustomed to pray in order for this reason even if they were late in coming to the beis haknesses. But in the responsum of Chacham Tzvi siman 36 he wrote that what he wrote in the Zohar—that one should pray in order—was when he is not praying with the congregation; but if he was late in coming to the beis haknesses and comes when the congregation is praying, everyone agrees that he should skip in order to pray with the congregation, see there; and likewise wrote Peri Chadash in siman 59, see there:

**"call out."** Take this rule in hand: all halleluyahs precede "hodu l'Havayah kera'u"; that is, if there is no time to pray, halleluyah and "hodu l'Havayah kera'u"—postpone "hodu l'Havayah kera'u" because of halleluyah, for the main pesukei dezimra are from "Tehillah leDavid" until "and it was established." And "May David bless" also precedes "hodu l'Havayah kera'u," but not halleluyah. Magen Avraham.

**"compiled."** And in Sefer Lechem Chamudos, chapter "Ein Omedin," he expanded on this and concluded that if there is time, he should also say Mizmor leTodah, see there:

**"and their blessings."** And if he arrives close to [time of] Shema, if he can begin with Yotzer and reach praying the shemoneh esreh with the congregation, he should do so, for the main concern is that he pray shemoneh esreh with the congregation. Glosses; Magen Avraham.

**"after them"**—that is, Baruch she'amar and Yishtabach:
