Pesukei D'Zimrah. See Baer Heitev. Shalmei Tzibbur cites Magen Chayil that no distinction applies: Even a person who does not regularly pray with the congregation because he does not rise early enough should, on an occasion when he comes to the synagogue and finds the congregation at the end of Pesukei D'Zimrah, follow the Shulchan Aruch's instructions, so that at least on this occasion he merits communal prayer. Yosef Ometz comments on the Shulchan Aruch that a person who is not conscientious about rising early, and who will consequently be unable most days to recite the fixed zemiros before prayer and will lose the blessings Baruch She'amar and Yishtabach, should not alter the formulation established by the Sages for the blessings. Shalmei Tzibbur explains that Beit Yosef speaks only of such a case, where this occurs most days, and objects specifically to losing Baruch She'amar and Yishtabach entirely because the person arrives so late that he must begin with Yotzer Or. One who can recite the blessings with some of the zemiros should follow the Shulchan Aruch, as Chacham Tzvi writes; see there. All this concerns one who arrives late. A person who normally prays slowly and with concentration, and cannot keep pace with the congregation even when arriving at the beginning, gains nothing by praying at their speed, for he would lose his concentration. He may pray in order at his own pace and should arrange to answer Barechu and Kedushah with them between sections, rather than be in the middle of a section. Hashem examines every heart.
