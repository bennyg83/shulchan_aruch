On pesukei dezimra — see Be'er Heitev. And in Shulchan Tziyyon he wrote in the name of Mahari Chai that there is no distinction in this: even one who is not accustomed to pray regularly with the congregation, who does not rise so early, nevertheless if it happened once that he came to synagogue and found the congregation at the end of pesukei dezimra, he should do as written in the Shulchan Aruch, for at least this time he has [the opportunity] to merit praying with the congregation, etc. And in Sefer Yosef Ometz he wrote on the words of the Shulchan Aruch: one who is not careful to rise early for prayer — if he prays as stated, then usually he will not be able to say the fixed zemirot before prayer and will lose the blessing of Baruch She'amar and Yishtabach; then he should not change from the coin that the Sages coined with blessings. And in Shulchan Tziyyon he wrote that Beit Yosef spoke only in such a case where usually, etc., and also where he was particular only about Baruch She'amar and Yishtabach — that is, he is late until he must begin Yotzer Or, whereby he loses the blessings entirely. But anyone who can say the blessings with some zemirot should do as the Shulchan Aruch, and as the Chida z"l wrote, see there. However, all this speaks of one who is late; but one whose way is to pray slowly and with kavanah — even if he comes at the beginning of prayer, he cannot pray equally [with others] — it will not help him to pray with them together, since he proceeds slowly and deliberately; and if he prays with them equally he will lose kavanah — he is permitted to pray in order, and he should intend that for Barchu and Kedushah he speaks with them between the sections, and not stand in the middle of a section; "with all your hearts" Hashem requires.
