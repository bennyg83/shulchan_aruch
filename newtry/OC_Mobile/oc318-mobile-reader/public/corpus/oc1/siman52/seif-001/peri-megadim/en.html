Nevertheless he should recite them, meaning after the prayer. Taz. Regarding Bircas HaTorah, siman 47, seif 8, explains that one must be careful. See Beit Yosef here. Accordingly, he should not recite Bircas HaTorah after the prayer, for perhaps he studied immediately afterward, and Krias Shema itself may suffice, or the like. It must be considered whether, if he intends not to fulfill Bircas HaTorah through the blessing Ahavah Rabbah, this is effective. Although blessings do not require intent, see siman 60 in Magen Avraham, seif katan 3, and siman 209, seif katan 3, and with Hashem's help this will be explained, nevertheless, where he explicitly intends not to fulfill the obligation, that intent is effective, and he may afterward recite Bircas HaTorah. One might object that he is not permitted to multiply blessings. That is incorrect, for only gratuitous multiplication of blessings is forbidden. Here, where there is a doubt, it is proper to intend not to fulfill the obligation through Ahavah Rabbah, as stated. Peri Chadash writes that even so he should recite Bircas HaTorah, following his own view in siman 47, seif 6. We, however, follow the Mechaber's ruling there. Peri Chadash further writes there a novel ruling: If one did not recite Elokai Neshamah, he should not recite it after the prayer, because he has already fulfilled it through the blessing "You are mighty, Who revives the dead." He wrote similarly at the beginning of siman 46, based in part on the Yerushalmi. The implication of the Mechaber and Rama is not so. Here too, one may intend not to fulfill the obligation, as stated. Levush writes in a gloss that even Bircas HaTorah may be recited afterward, following his own view in siman 47. As explained there, he holds that Krias Shema does not constitute Torah study. According to the Mechaber there, however, the matter remains doubtful. Olas Tamid writes that even Bircas HaTorah may be recited, but Eliyah Rabbah disputes him for the reason stated.
