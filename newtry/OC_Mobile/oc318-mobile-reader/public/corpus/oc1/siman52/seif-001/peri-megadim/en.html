And nevertheless he shall say — meaning after the prayer. The Taz: and birchas haTorah is explained in siman 47 seif 8 that one must be careful. See there here; and if so he should not say birchas haTorah after the prayer, for perhaps he read in place and Krias Shema suffices, and the like. And behold one must see if he intends not to fulfill through the blessing Ahavah Rabbah [his obligation in] birchas haTorah — that is valid; for although blessings do not require intention (see siman 60 in Magen Avraham ot gimel and Taz ot gimel, and it will be explained), nevertheless where one intends expressly not to fulfill, it is valid, and he blesses birchas haTorah afterward. And one who wishes to multiply blessings is not permitted — no, that is not so; only gratuitously one may not multiply [blessings]. However in such a case where there is doubt, it is valid to intend expressly not to fulfill through Ahavah Rabbah, as we said. And the Pri Chadash wrote that nevertheless he should say birchas haTorah, and he follows his view in siman 47 se'if 6; but we hold as the Mechaber's ruling there. He also wrote there a novel law: if he did not say the blessing Elokai Neshamah, they are not said after prayer, for he already departed through the blessing Atah gibor mechaye hametim; and likewise wrote at the beginning of siman 46 in the name of the Yerushalmi somewhat — and the implication of the Mechaber and Rabbeinu Bechor Shor is not so; and one may also intend not to fulfill, as stated. And in the Levush he wrote in his hagahah: even birchas haTorah, according to his view in siman 47; and as he wrote there, that he holds Krias Shema is not Torah study, unlike the Mechaber there, where it is doubtful; and Bach wrote: even birchas haTorah and Ahavah Rabbah — they challenged from what we said.
