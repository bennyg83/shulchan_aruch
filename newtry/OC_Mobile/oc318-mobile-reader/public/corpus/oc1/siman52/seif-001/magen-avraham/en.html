Semag writes: He recites Baruch SheAmar, skips to Tehillah LeDavid, then skips to “Praise God in His sanctuary,” continues through “the name of Your glory,” and skips to Yishtabach. This is his language. The other poskim, however, imply that all the Halleluyah psalms precede Vayevarech David. He should say Hodu LaShem, etc. This implies that Hodu LaShem Kir’u Bishmo precedes the other Halleluyah psalms. Semak states this explicitly. Rif, however, writes that the principal Pesukei DeZimrah extend from Tehillah LeDavid through Kol HaNeshamah Tehallel Yah, implying that all the Halleluyah psalms precede Hodu LaShem. Tur likewise appears to hold this way. Rama’s statement, “If he has more time,” may also be explained to mean after he has recited all the Halleluyah psalms, since Rama afterward mentions none of the remaining Halleluyah psalms. It further appears to me that Vayevarech David precedes Hodu LaShem, since Semag placed it earlier. If the congregation has begun Yishtabach, he should listen to Yishtabach from the chazzan. Mateh Moshe. If he arrives close to Shema and can begin Yotzer in time to reach Shemoneh Esrei together with the congregation, he should do so, because the principal concern is to pray Shemoneh Esrei with the congregation. Hagahos Yesh Nochalin.
