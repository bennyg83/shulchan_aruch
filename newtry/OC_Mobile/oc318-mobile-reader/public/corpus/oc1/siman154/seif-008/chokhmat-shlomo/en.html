In the Rama gloss they practice enjoying many kinds of benefit from holy objects — not addressed; see Taz at note 7 what he wrote "were it not that I fear," etc.; see my chiddushim on Yoma 12b, as the gemara states there that "two" is not fit neither for the High Priest nor for an ordinary priest, because of "elevate in holiness and do not lower" — there I proved against his view; and especially what he said there (4) that he would have trouble in life, and it would be an inference that he would never be a priest — yet nevertheless "elevate in holiness and do not lower" applies to him; understand well. And what Taz wrote in the name of Maharshal that it is not called lowering as long as the sefer is inside it — and so wrote Magen Avraham in his name; and Bach brought that even this is lowering from holiness; understand well. And see my chiddushim on the Laws of Chanukah siman 671 what I wrote on this — proof for Bach that even this is lowering from holiness; understand well what I wrote on this and examine. And it appears to my humble opinion to bring proof to Maharshal's view that Taz brought above — that lowering from holiness for a time does not apply — from what we learn in chapter 4 of Sukkah 44b: since you say it is only taken by itself, obviously he is obligated in the willow in the lulav. And it is difficult: why does he ask — let him say it teaches us that even if he took it from the lulav he does not fulfill with it? And they answered: the reason is, in truth he does not fulfill this way if it is by itself; but one can say: since we hold it is forbidden to lower from grave holiness to light holiness, it is proven that this is contempt of a mitzvah; and therefore mitzvah objects may be discarded — that is, after their mitzvah time has passed; but during their mitzvah time they are forbidden for benefit and to use them from the power of contempt of a mitzvah, as written in Ne'ot Sukah and the like. And if so, even to use them for a light mitzvah is also included in contempt of a mitzvah. And if so, one can say: since the lulav is from the Torah in the borders one day, and the willow has no fundamental source from the Torah in the borders, and they make the lulav all seven as a remembrance of the Temple, and the willow one day — and if so it was a light mitzvah, and therefore one should not remove it from grave holiness to light holiness. And necessarily, with the lulav — even though this is not contempt, as is proven here, that while the books are still inside it is permitted to place in it also other books — either he cannot fulfill the willow, since it is only taken by itself; or if he took it from the lulav, again it is lowering from holiness for a time, and it is forbidden to remove it from grave to light, since it is still fit for the grave today. And one cannot say that in any event b'dieved why does he not fulfill — and behold the Tanna said he does not fulfill his obligation with the willow in the lulav, which implies even b'dieved; let one say it is a mitzvah that comes through transgression — and it is difficult for most poskim who hold Pasul even b'dieved. And what does he ask — perhaps while it is still fit for the mitzvah, as it is forbidden for mundane use, so too it is forbidden to change it to light holiness, which is also included in contempt of a mitzvah; and necessarily lowering for a time is not lowering; and since it is still possible to return it to the lulav, it is not considered lowering; and even in holiness it is permitted in such a case — how much more so with mitzvah objects. And it is proven like Maharshal's view, not like Bach; examine well.
