Lamps. See Taz. This applies even when they are permitted for ordinary benefit in the manner discussed in Yoreh Deah 139, after they were nullified. Nevertheless, they remain repulsive for a mitzvah because they came from idolatry. Regarding their use in one’s home for Shabbos and Yom Tov lamps, Eliyah Rabbah, letter 15, is uncertain, because the lamp itself was made for honor, as in siman 151, seif 9, unlike a chandelier. For this reason, I permitted benches from a house of idolatry to be placed in a synagogue, because they serve human needs, namely, to sit upon while praying, and are comparable to the wooden markers mentioned in seif 6. See Magen Avraham, letter 17, where this will be explained, with Hashem’s help. In Yoreh Deah 259, Shach, seif katan 11, cites Mordechai and Bach, which implies that a menorah is a mitzvah object. See siman 153, Magen Avraham, letter 15, that synagogue benches possess the sanctity of the synagogue, and letter 28 there, following Ran, that a synagogue possesses sanctity, unlike Ramban, who regards it only as a mitzvah object. Nevertheless, benches from a house of idolatry may be permitted. This will be further explained in Magen Avraham, with Hashem’s help.
