A condition helps for it: This means when they stipulated at the beginning of its making that it would be permitted to use it. There is no distinction between the time when the Torah scroll is resting in it and afterward.
Even for mundane use: Nevertheless, for a degrading use a condition does not help. Magen Avraham above in siman 151, seif katan 14.
And it is impossible to be careful: See Terumas HaDeshen, where it is evident that this applies specifically to a synagogue, but not to an individual's Torah scroll that he has in his house.
He makes a condition about them initially: This is specifically regarding something for which the custom to be lenient is clear. But regarding something that is not the custom, we may not be lenient on our own, for perhaps they did not stipulate about this.
Although they did not stipulate: Terumas HaDeshen concludes, I exerted myself in order to justify somewhat why the world is not careful. It is implied there that it is proper for every person, when donating an item to the synagogue, or for the treasurers when purchasing something, to stipulate that they may use it. Magen Avraham.
