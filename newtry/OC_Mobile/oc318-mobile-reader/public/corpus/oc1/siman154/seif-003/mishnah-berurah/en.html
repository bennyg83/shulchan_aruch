In which they place a Torah scroll: This teaches that we should not say it was made merely for protection and not for honor, and that the chest therefore does not have the status of an accessory of sanctity. Gemara. Those boxes designated for placing our books, where the books are covered with paper, leather, or boards, are called an accessory of an accessory. Sefer Shiurei Berachah in the name of the responsum of Rabbi Yehudah ben HaRosh. Although regarding a chest in which a Torah scroll is placed we hold that even if the Torah scroll rests in a case the chest is nevertheless an accessory of sanctity, as explained in Yoreh Deah siman 282, seif 12, perhaps a chest is different, since it is made for the honor of the Torah scroll, as stated in the Gemara, unlike these boxes, which are made only for mere protection.
And a chair, etc.: This is the table that stands on the bimah. Although a cloth rests upon it and the Torah scroll rests upon the cloth, so that the table is only an accessory of an accessory, nevertheless it has the law of an accessory of sanctity, because sometimes the Torah scroll is placed upon it without a cloth. Gemara.
A curtain that is hung: This is the paroches. This refers to the practice in the time of the Talmud, when they would sometimes place the Torah scroll upon it or cover the Torah scroll with it. It appears that this was common in their time, for otherwise it would be only an occasional use, and it would not have the status of an accessory of sanctity. But nowadays, when we do not practice this way, it is only an accessory of an accessory. This is what the Rama glosses below in seif 6. Acharonim.
They have sanctity: The same applies to the dust of books and the dust of their coverings, meaning the decay of the books and coverings caused by worms that eat them. Shabbos 90a.
And they must be placed in genizah: This means after they have worn out and no longer stand for their use. Even so, they have sanctity, and it is forbidden to use them or throw them away; therefore one must place them in a guarded genizah. This is unlike an accessory of an accessory, from which one may benefit even while it still exists, as above.
Specifically the item, etc.: This is the rule: anything that actually touches the sanctity without an intervening object, even if it is not so much for honor but only for some protection, such as a chest and the like, excluding a cabinet in a wall, which is merely for protection; and anything made for honor, even if it does not touch in that way, such as silver plates and a cover over the boards, since it rests on the sanctity even with an intervening object, is an accessory of sanctity. This is unlike our paroches in seif 6, for although it is an expensive and honorable item, nevertheless it is like a partition and does not rest on the sanctity at all. Peri Megadim. See what we wrote in Biur Halacha in the name of the Gra on this.
The cover over the boards: Their practice was to make a case of boards for the books, and upon them they would place a cover for beauty and honor. Since it is made for the honor of the sanctity, it is considered an accessory of sanctity, although it does not touch the sanctity itself.
Built into the wall: This means that there is a hollow in the wall like a cabinet. The same applies if it was made this way in a wooden wall. In all such cases it is like a mere room, since it was made only for protection and not for honor. But if they make a wooden cabinet inside the hollow of the wall, that cabinet is an accessory of sanctity. Likewise, when they spread a piece of cloth inside the wall so the books not become ruined, that cloth has the status of an accessory of sanctity. Peri Megadim.
Become ruined: If so, it was not fit from the outset, and the name of sanctity did not take effect upon it, for it is not called its accessory but its damaging agent. In this manner, even if it is made for beauty, since it ruins the item it does not have the status of an accessory of sanctity.
That it is permitted to remove them from there: Meaning, he should take the Torah scroll away, and it is permitted to demolish that place. It is not like other accessories of sanctity, which may not be ruined, as above at the end of siman 152; see there.
A covering of a Torah scroll: The same applies to all accessories of sanctity.
With urine: The same applies to any foul-smelling substance, such as water in which flax was soaked and the like.
