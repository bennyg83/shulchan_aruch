3) A room adjoining a beis midrash was not permanently designated as an annex to the beis midrash, but at times, when many people were present, they prayed there. They now wish to construct in that room a spring-fed mikveh for men and women. See Responsa Maharam Schick, end of siman 106, where he inclines toward leniency and permits it; see there. Orchos Chaim, paragraph 1, cites him. However, from what we wrote above in paragraph 1 in the name of Maharit and Mor U'Ketziah, it appears that this should be forbidden. Since people pray there whenever space is limited, the sanctity of a synagogue takes effect upon it. I later saw that Toras Chaim, paragraph 1, writes the same. He concludes that the straightforward solution is for the seven trustees of the town, in the presence of the townspeople, to sell that room and transfer its sanctity to the money. They may then buy it back, after which they may even make it into a bathhouse, as explained in siman 153, seif 9; see there. This applies specifically to a village synagogue. A synagogue in a large city has no remedy, as explained there in seifim 7 and 9; see there.
