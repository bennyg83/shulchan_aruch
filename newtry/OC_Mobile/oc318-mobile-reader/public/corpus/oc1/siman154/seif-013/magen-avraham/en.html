A synagogue candle. This is the language of the Beit Yosef: Rabbeinu Yerucham, netiv 2, wrote that a synagogue candle may be used for reading by its light, whether Talmud or any of one's needs, for his intent is for illumination, to increase the light of the synagogue; the more he reads, the greater the mitzvah, and he receives reward. So wrote Rav Natronai in a responsum. Although the Kol Bo wrote that the custom was not to light from a synagogue candle, since they were set aside for their mitzvah, and the early authorities practiced a great prohibition, and even to write or study they did not light from them, the world practiced according to Rav Natronai. Nimukei Yosef wrote in the laws of tzitzis that it is forbidden to light from them while they are burning for their mitzvah for a weekday need, but a Shabbos candle or Chanukah candle for Torah study, for a woman in childbirth, or for a sick person is permitted. However, they did not have the practice to be careful about this. This is the wording of the Beit Yosef. He understood that according to Rabbeinu Yerucham it is permitted to read by it even a weekday matter, and all the more so to light from it a weekday candle; according to the Kol Bo, even for a mitzvah one may not light from it; and according to Nimukei Yosef, if it is for a mitzvah it is permitted, but for a weekday matter it is forbidden. It requires study how it entered the mind of the Gaon to explain the words of Rabbeinu Yerucham that one may read weekday material, for he wrote, 'the more he reads, the greater the mitzvah, and he receives reward.' What mitzvah and reward is there in a weekday matter? Furthermore, he did not copy the words of Rabbeinu Yerucham as written. This is his language: oil that they vowed to light in the synagogue may be used for reading by its light, for the only reason they forbade reading by a Shabbos candle was not because of sanctity but because perhaps he will tilt it. Therefore on a weekday it is permitted to read by its light, whether Talmud or any of his needs. And even though a Chanukah candle has sanctity, and we conclude that one should not treat mitzvos disgracefully, there the mitzvah is to light a Chanukah candle; here he intends illumination, to increase the light of the synagogue, and the more he reads, the greater the mitzvah, and he receives reward, etc. This is his wording. It is thus clear that what he wrote, that it is permitted to read, etc., all of his needs, refers to a Shabbos candle, meaning that he may read by it on a weekday for all his needs if some of it remained, as the Beit Yosef wrote in siman 677 in the name of Tosafot and the Ran. But by a synagogue candle it is forbidden to read a weekday matter by it, as with all uses of a synagogue; only to study by it is permitted. We do not say that he did not donate with this in mind, for the more he reads, the greater the mitzvah. If so, according to everyone it is forbidden to light a weekday candle from it, as written in seif 14. Thus what is written here, that it is permitted to read by its light, means specifically a matter of study, as is also implied by the fact that in seif 14 he forbids even lighting from it. However, this is specifically where one vowed a candle to the synagogue, for that is what he discusses. But the candles that the treasurers make, where the practice is that on Motzaei Shabbos each person takes a candle home and uses it for weekday use, the heart of beis din stipulates regarding them. But those that stand on the menorah, it is forbidden to read weekday matters by them.
