A synagogue candle: This means that someone vowed oil for lighting in the synagogue.
It is permitted to read: This means words of Torah, but not mundane reading. This is not comparable to a Chanukah candle, for according to most poskim even learning by its light is forbidden, as below in siman 673. There it is different, because it must be recognizable that it was lit for the mitzvah of the Chanukah candle and not for anything else. Here, however, the intention of the one who vowed was only to increase light in the synagogue; therefore, the more people read words of Torah by its light, the more mitzvah there is, and he receives reward. However, this applies specifically to one who vowed a vow to the synagogue, as above. But in places where the treasurers make candles and the practice is that after Shabbos each person takes a candle to his home, even if he uses them for mundane use it is permitted, for this is considered as though the condition of beis din was made concerning them. But those candles that stand on the menorah may not be used for mundane reading.
