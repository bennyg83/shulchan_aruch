(Note 11) He raises an objection, etc. And it is implied from Mordechai that even one built in a wall, as written in seif 1, is forbidden. And this is Mordechai's language in the chapter Bnei HaIr — they asked him about the law of the ark built in a wall explained in seif 1: it appears to me that there is no issue of holy objects; for although an ark certainly has the status of holy objects, etc., nevertheless regarding this case that we come to, it appears to me that there is no issue of the Holy Ark — for I say that it is only called the Holy Ark when it is made like a kind of chest for honor and not for preservation. And you should know that Rava called it a box, for Rava said (Megillah 26b): the chest of arpita (Rashi: an ark that was damaged) — make a box from it; a small one — permitted; a seat — forbidden (and this is the law explained here in Shulchan Aruch seif 7) — hence in such a case it is not called the Holy Ark; but this one, which is made in the building of the wall for preservation, is made not for honor, etc.; end of Mordechai's words. It is implied that Mordechai equates the law of an ark built in a wall to this law of Rava, that it is not called the Holy Ark; and why did Rava call it a box, yet Rava said one may not make from a box a seat? If so, likewise one may not make from an ark built in a wall a seat. And even though Magen Avraham wrote at note 4 that it has only the holiness of a synagogue — one must say not specifically, but rather that it does not have the holiness of the Holy Ark itself; but nevertheless it is superior to the holiness of a synagogue.
