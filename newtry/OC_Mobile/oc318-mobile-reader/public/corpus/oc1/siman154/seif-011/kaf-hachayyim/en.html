11) Now, some make a wooden aron within a recess in the wall. One can say that it is an accessory of sanctity. Sometimes pieces of cloth are spread inside the wall so that the sefarim will not be damaged. If the cloth tears and is no longer fit for that purpose, it may be made into covers for the tables upon which people study in the beis midrash, to spread upon them in honor of Shabbos. One can say that the rabbinical court stipulates concerning them. According to the Taz, paragraph 7, whenever an item is no longer fit for its original purpose it is permitted. Eshel Avraham, paragraph 3. Toras Chaim, paragraph 2, similarly writes that a wooden aron built into a recess in the wall is the primary aron kodesh and is called an accessory of sanctity. But if it stands independently and is not built into the wall, it is like an aron kodesh built into the wall that was made only for protection. Magen Avraham, paragraph 2, writes that the same applies to one built into a wooden wall, for it is considered merely a room. Toras Chaim rejects the words of Maharam Schick at the end of siman 80; see there. It appears to me that everything depends upon its intended purpose. If it was made to honor the Sefer Torah and not for protection, it is an accessory of sanctity. See below, paragraph 65. All this applies to those whose custom is not to make a case for a Sefer Torah, but only handles, so that they roll it in a cloth and, after the reading, store it in the aron kodesh. Those whose custom is to make a case for the Sefer Torah, covered with silk or with silver and gold fastened by nails, never remove the Sefer Torah from that case, even during the reading. After the reading they place it together with its case in the wall recess on the side facing the congregation, which is called the heichal. According to all opinions, that recess serves merely for protection, and its sanctity is like the sanctity of the synagogue. Yoreh De'ah, siman 282, seif 12, states that an aron or tower in which a Sefer Torah is placed requires genizah even if the Sefer Torah is placed there only while in its case. That refers to an aron in which the Sefer Torah is sometimes placed without its case, as is implied by the Beit Yosef there, and the Bach explicitly writes so there. This is unlike what the Gra writes there, paragraph 28. See the Beit Yosef there regarding the tower, which is a bench, and Be'er HaGolah there writes the same. This refutes the distinction written by the Gra; see there and examine it carefully.
