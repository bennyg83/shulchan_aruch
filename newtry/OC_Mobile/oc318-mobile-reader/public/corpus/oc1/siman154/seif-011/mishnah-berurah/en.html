That a Cuthean gave them to idol worship: Meaning to a house of idol worship for lighting.
And their attendant extinguished them, etc.: That is, then they are permitted for ordinary use, as written in the gloss, because the status of adornment of idol worship was removed from them through their extinguishing. Even so, they are forbidden for sacred use because they are repulsive. The same applies to wax that drips from their candles, which is forbidden. Chayei Adam. If they had never yet been lit and their attendant sold them to a Jew, they are permitted even for sacred use, for the status of adornment of idol worship never took effect upon them.
In the synagogue: The same applies to any mitzvah lighting, such as Shabbos candles and Chanukah candles, or to learn by their light; it is forbidden. The same applies to all mitzvah matters, that one does not make them from something made for idol worship; see siman 11, seif 8, and siman 586, seif 3. Regarding making a house of worship of idolaters into a synagogue, the Magen Avraham wrote in the name of the Re'em to be lenient. The reason is that the house itself was never worshipped. The view of the Eliyah Rabbah inclines to be stringent about making it a fixed synagogue; see there. It seems that the common practice is to be lenient. See Biur Halacha, where we clarified that this is specifically when their practice is not to place their idols there. But where they place their idols there, even if they removed them and sold the house to a Jew, so that the house is then permitted for ordinary use because it has already been nullified from the status of a house of idol worship, nevertheless for a synagogue it remains forbidden.
Although they are permitted for ordinary use: Meaning, because of nullification, as above. Even so, for sacred use it is forbidden because it is repulsive. The same applies to candelabra, called leichters, that were in a house of idols and that a Jew acquired after the idolater nullified them; they are permitted for ordinary use, but nevertheless forbidden for a synagogue because they are repulsive. The same applies to lighting with them at home for a mitzvah need, such as Shabbos candles and the like. Peri Megadim, explaining the words of the Taz. Although the Eliyah Rabbah inclined to be lenient, the Peri Megadim and Rabbi Akiva Eiger incline to rule like the Taz. Regarding benches from houses of idols, which are made merely for sitting and not as adornment for idol worship, the Peri Megadim ruled that they are permitted for a synagogue even without nullification.
Wax or a candle: The same applies to other things.
It is forbidden to light it: For it is comparable to a korban, which we do not accept from him. The same applies to an apostate who publicly desecrates Shabbos, or an apostate acting out of spite even with respect to one of the other transgressions; since we do not accept a korban from them, we do not accept anything from them for the synagogue. However, if they gave money to write a Torah scroll with their names, it is permitted. Magen Avraham.
