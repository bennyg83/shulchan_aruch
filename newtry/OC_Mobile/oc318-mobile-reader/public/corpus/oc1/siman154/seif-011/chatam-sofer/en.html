In Avodah Berurah in the name of Shulchan Aruch. Note: and it implies a general rule of law: charity for the poor — they accept from an apostate, but not a sacrifice; and the reverse regarding gentiles — they vow vows and free-will offerings, but not charity, because it is said "in its harvest it withers," etc. But a candle and a Torah scroll and synagogue needs — they accept whether from an apostate or from a gentile; and what Tosafot wrote in Bava Batra 8a s.v. he sat, etc., that from it Mahari Weil brings proof — one can say it is not specifically that it was a lamp like a sacrifice, but to exclude that it is not like charity for the poor; and it is difficult. But in Magen Avraham here and in Yoreh De'ah siman 254 it implies that a lamp and the like is like a sacrifice, but charity — they certainly accept from them.
