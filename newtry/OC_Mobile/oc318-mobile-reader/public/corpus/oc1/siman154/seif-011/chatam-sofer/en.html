Note: see Responsa Orach Chaim, siman 42, regarding candles of a house of idolatry that were never lit, whether they were not forbidden merely by that designation so that one may not light them in a synagogue; and regarding those that were lit and extinguished, where the Shulchan Aruch in Orach Chaim and Yoreh Deah states explicitly that they are forbidden because they are repulsive. If the wax was melted down and changed, is it permitted to make another candle from it? In the responsum I wrote that mere designation is not mentioned in the Shulchan Aruch as a prohibition, and the law is with him, for we conclude in Temurah 29a that an item is not muktzeh until an act is done with it, etc. According to both the Rambam and the Raavad there is no basis to be stringent at all, and one need not be concerned that they were originally made actually for idolatry, for the ordinary thought of a non-Jew is not for idolatry; he makes them to sell in the market. However, regarding candles that already burned and were extinguished, and became forbidden and repulsive for a synagogue, whether melting them helps, he concludes at the end of the responsum as follows: as a matter of law, it appears to me that according to the implication of the Shulchan Aruch and Chavos Yair, simanim 161 and 162, it is permitted; and from Ramban on Parshas Vayakhel and the Gemara in Avodah Zarah there is no proof to forbid. Therefore one who is lenient loses nothing, and blessing should come upon one who is stringent.

Note: the general rule of the matter appears to be that charity for the poor is accepted from an apostate, but not a korban; and the reverse applies to non-Jews, who may vow vows and voluntary offerings, but not charity, because it says, when its harvest withers, etc. But a candle, a Torah scroll, and synagogue needs are accepted both from an apostate and from a non-Jew. What Tosafot wrote in Bava Batra 8a, s.v. yativ, from which Ri Weil brings proof, can be explained as not meaning specifically that a lamp is like a korban, but only to exclude that it is not like charity for the poor. This is difficult for me. But in the Magen Avraham here, and in Yoreh Deah siman 254, it implies that a lamp and the like are like a korban, though charity certainly may be accepted from them.
