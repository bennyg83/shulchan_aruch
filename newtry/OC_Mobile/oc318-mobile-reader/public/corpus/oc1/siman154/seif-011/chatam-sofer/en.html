And in a responsum I wrote that mere invitation is not mentioned in Shulchan Aruch as a prohibition, and the law is with him, for we conclude in Temurah 29a that there is no muktzeh until an action is done to it, etc.; and whether per the Rambam or per the Ra'avad one should not be stringent at all, and one need not be concerned they were made initially for actual idolatry, for a gentile's unstated thought is not for idolatry but to sell in the market. However, regarding candles that already burned and were extinguished and became disgusting for the synagogue — whether melting helps — I conclude at the end of the responsum, and this is its language: for the practical ruling it appears to me per the implication of Shulchan Aruch and Chavot Yair siman 161 and siman 162 that it is permitted; and from the Ramban in parashat Vayakhel and the gemara in Avodah Zarah there is no necessity for prohibition; hence one who is lenient has not lost, and one who is stringent — blessing shall come upon him.
