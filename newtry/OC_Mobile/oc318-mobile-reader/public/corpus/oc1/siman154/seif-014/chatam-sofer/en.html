There note 23 — and there is no hand to consecrated property. Note: as Rashbam and Tosafot wrote in Bava Batra 79a, and Ra'avad, and Tosafot Yom Tov in tractate Me'ilah chapter 3 mishnah 46. And what Tosafot Yom Tov asked — in any case there is an agent for consecrated property — one can say: since he owes others, no. But requires study: should the power of a layman not be more stringent than consecrated property, as we say in Kiddushin 29a; and in the Talmud there is no need at all that there is no hand, as written in Ramban's chiddushim there — that Nebuchadnezzar acquired for consecrated property, but there is no misuse in it. And further requires study: who cannot say it deals with a courtyard that is not guarded for the knowledge of consecrated property; and it does not deal there except with the law of growth of consecrated property, not the law of a courtyard; and so explained from mishnah 4 chapter 5 of Shekalim; and see Choshen Mishpat siman 404:8; and see responsa Chasam Sofer Orach Chayim siman 44 [regarding a homeowner who has a shop open to the public street to pour wine, and many gentiles are commonly there, and the serving boy found a purse with money inside known to have been lost by the gentiles — whether the boy acquired it or the homeowner, whose courtyard acquires for him; and he concluded his responsum: nevertheless in this case we rule in our incident that the serving boy acquired, for we hold a worker's find and a hired worker's find belong to himself, unless the homeowner told him "collect this find for me"; and see Shach end of siman 278. And what they say that the custom is a servant's find belongs to the homeowner — idle talk, let them shut their mouths, and there is no place for this. And what is written in Shulchan Aruch siman 267 — see what Beit Shmuel wrote siman 247 and Turei Zahav].
