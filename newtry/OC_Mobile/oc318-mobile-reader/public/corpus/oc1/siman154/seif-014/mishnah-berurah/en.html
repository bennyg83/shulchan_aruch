One may not light, etc.: Because they have the sanctity of a synagogue upon them.
Of an ordinary person: This excludes lighting from it for a Shabbos or Chanukah candle, or for the need of Torah study, which is permitted.
When one needs to extinguish them: The reason is that they are no more stringent than Chanukah candles, regarding which, after they have burned for the required measure of time, it is permitted to benefit from them. The Gra.
For a great need: Regarding lighting the pipe of tobacco from a synagogue candle, see Sha'arei Teshuvah, who rules that it is forbidden from a prayer candle, but from a yahrtzeit candle that burns for the entire twenty-four-hour period there is room to permit. Sefer Chasidim, siman 813, wrote that one should not take a candle to search for his money; but if he fears that thieves have hidden in the synagogue, he may take a candle and search. Eliyah Rabbah. In my humble opinion, after the Rama wrote that for a great need we say that the condition of beis din applies, it is reasonable that it is also permitted to take a candle to search for money that fell from him.
