Seif katan 14: Which are not, etc. As a sign so that they not err. Meaning, they know which Sefer Torah to remove from the ark for the reading, either because that Sefer Torah is rolled to the required passage or because it is known to be properly corrected. Likewise, the pointer made for a Sefer Torah shows the reader where he is reading so that he not err.
The Bach wrote: However, it requires study where they removed, etc. Taz similarly discusses the custom on Hoshana Rabbah, after all the Sifrei Torah are removed from the heichal, to place a lit candle inside and say that this honors the Torah, alluding to “Torah is light.” Taz writes that this is insufficient to justify lowering the ark from its sanctity. See there.
It appears to me plainly that it is forbidden, etc. Bach proves this from the teaching in Megillah 27a that one may wrap a Sefer Torah in the mantle of Chumashim, but may not wrap Neviim or Kesuvim in a Sefer Torah mantle. The first clause necessarily concerns temporary use, after which the mantle will again be used for Chumashim. Otherwise, if it meant permanently designating the mantle for a Sefer Torah, the ruling would be obvious. Since the first clause concerns temporary use, the latter clause likewise prohibits wrapping Chumashim, Neviim, or Kesuvim in a Sefer Torah mantle even temporarily. Temporary use is therefore still considered a lowering of sanctity. Bach brings additional proofs; see there.
Who says that this is an established and proper custom? Maharshal brought proof from the custom of placing invalid parchment sheets in the ark. Bach responds: Who says, etc.?
And regarding an ark built, etc. It is plainly permitted, although seif katan 11 states that one may not make it into a chair because that lowers its sanctity. Temporary lowering is permitted here, because there is at least an argument to permit it even for a full aron kodesh, although we do not rule that way; an ark built into the wall is therefore permitted. Alternatively, one may permanently place invalid parchment sheets in it. Only transforming it into a chair changes the object and is prohibited; leaving it as an ark and placing such sheets inside is permitted.
And in Megillah 26b, etc. Rava initially thought that where a corpse lay in a room adjoining the synagogue and an open window connected them, preventing kohanim from entering the synagogue, one could place the ark in the window to block tumah. He thought that the ark containing the Sefer Torah was a wooden vessel made to remain stationary, meaning it stayed permanently in one place and was not moved, and therefore did not contract tumah because a wooden vessel must resemble a movable sack. Anything incapable of contracting tumah can interpose against tumah. The Gemara rejected Rava’s view because the ark is sometimes moved and therefore does contract tumah.
Although it serves a Sefer Torah. Meaning, this would prove that temporary lowering is permitted. Alternatively, placing it in the window is not considered lowering at all. Tosafos in Bava Basra prove from this incident that an object can interpose against tumah even if it is placed there only temporarily. Rava certainly did not intend the ark to remain in the window permanently, for that would lower its sanctity. Nevertheless, he thought it interposed, and they disagreed only because it contracts tumah. Tosafos must therefore hold that such placement, where the ark is not used for anything, is not considered lowering.
What difference is there, etc. This was permitted only temporarily. Permanent use in this manner would still be forbidden, since it is difficult to say that Tosafos disagree with all the authorities cited above.
And in Taz, Yoreh Deah, etc. What was written above implies that it is permitted. Meaning, this is not lowering at all; what difference is there whether the object rests here or there?
As written in siman 315, seif 7. On Shabbos one may place one book on one side, another on the other side, and a third across them. This is not forbidden as making a tent because one does not need the space beneath them. According to Taz, it should nevertheless be prohibited because it lowers the upper book from its sanctity. Taz answers in siman 315 that the case is one in which the person needs all three books for study. See there.
And in Sefer Chassidim, etc. One should not rule lines, etc. This implies that if the booklet already contains writing, one may place it on a sacred book. Meaning, he may place the booklet upon the book and rule lines in the portion not yet written, since Sefer Chassidim forbade it only because the booklet had not yet been written upon.
Know that Taz wrote as follows: Were I not afraid, I would say, etc. The rule that one may not convert a greater sanctity into a lesser one applies so long as the object remains fit for the greater sanctity. If it is no longer fit for that use and can serve only a lesser sanctity, it is preferable to use it for the lesser sanctity rather than leave it unused and bury it. See there.
They said in Shabbos, etc. Rashi explains that “arna” was the term they used for their towers, but “aron,” etc. Although aron and arna differ only in that aron is Hebrew and arna is Aramaic, the word arna became specifically associated with towers. It is therefore degrading to call the aron kodesh an arna. The term aron is not uniquely associated with towers.
