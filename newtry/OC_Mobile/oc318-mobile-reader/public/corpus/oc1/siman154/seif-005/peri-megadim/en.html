Even. See Taz and Megillah there. Regarding a cloth, the text says “they may make,” implying permission, whereas regarding a Sefer Torah it implies an obligation. Although one might say that regarding the cloth the teaching is needed to inform us that this is not considered lowering its sanctity, see Taz, letter 7, while regarding a Sefer Torah it must therefore mean an obligation, it remains difficult because both were stated there in the same formulation. Perhaps the cloth too is obligatory, as appears from the Tur, Yoreh Deah 282. See Beit Yosef there. Rambam, chapter 10 of Hilchos Sefer Torah, halachah 3, likewise implies that it is obligatory. This requires study. Nevertheless, regarding a Sefer Torah, it appears that it should specifically be buried in the grave of a talmid chacham, even one who did not serve Torah scholars; burying it directly in the ground is not sufficient. This requires study, for I have not seen this practiced.
