Note: see responsa Orach Chayim siman 42 [regarding candles of an idolatrous house that were never lit — whether they are forbidden by the invitation of that house not to light them in the synagogue; and regarding those that were lit and extinguished, which is stated explicitly in Shulchan Aruch Orach Chayim and Yoreh De'ah as forbidden because they are disgusting. If the wax was melted and changed — whether it is permitted to make from it another candle. And in a responsum I wrote that mere invitation is not mentioned in Shulchan Aruch as a prohibition, and the law is with him, for we conclude in Temurah 29a that there is no muktzeh until an action is done to it, etc.; and whether per the Rambam or per the Ra'avad one should not be stringent at all, and one need not be concerned they were made initially for actual idolatry, for a gentile's unstated thought is not for idolatry but to sell in the market. However, regarding candles that already burned and were extinguished and became disgusting for the synagogue — whether melting helps — I conclude at the end of the responsum, and this is its language: for the practical ruling it appears to me per the implication of Shulchan Aruch and Chavot Yair siman 161 and siman 162 that it is permitted; and from the Ramban in parashat Vayakhel and the gemara in Avodah Zarah there is no necessity for prohibition; hence one who is lenient has not lost, and one who is stringent — blessing shall come upon him.
