And the custom is. See Taz and Magen Avraham, letter 14. See Beit Yosef concerning the Yerushalmi, where they placed a hammer in the ark. This implies that even when a Sefer Torah is present, one may not place other objects there unless there was a stipulation. A stipulation, however, is effective for all accessories of sanctity, allowing them afterward to be used entirely for mundane purposes. Sacred books alone retain their sanctity. The statement that lowering sanctity is forbidden specifically while the object remains fit for its former use requires some study. The case of a damaged ark and the like implies that even when it is entirely unfit to serve as an ark, it may not be made into a chair. It is not merely a case where it remains fit for a smaller ark; even when it is unfit, the conversion is forbidden. This requires study.
