And this is now the practice because the beis din is presumed to have stipulated, etc. At first glance, according to what we wrote in the Mishnah Berurah, seif katan 35, in the name of Terumas HaDeshen, that the presumed stipulation of beis din does not apply to an individual’s Sefer Torah kept in his house, here too the mantle had already become sanctified for this particular Sefer Torah in his house before he donated the Sefer Torah to the synagogue. How, then, can a presumed stipulation of beis din apply? Perhaps the case is one in which, when he purchased the mantle, he already intended eventually to donate the Sefer Torah to the synagogue, so he never designated the mantle exclusively for that one Sefer Torah. If so, however, Rama should have stated this. His unqualified language implies that it is permitted in every case. This requires study.
