And they stipulated, etc.: Without a stipulation, one need not give anything to the one who leaves, for they acquired it with the understanding that it would remain here.
Only what he gave: Presumably they stipulated only with respect to the defined portion at that time, not to make another appraisal. Even if there were room to doubt what they meant by their wording, the law is also so, since in any event the Torah scroll remains with those who remain; they only need to return money, and there is doubt how much they must return. They can say, the Torah scroll is certainly ours, and if you claim money, bring proof and take it. Beit Yosef. According to this latter reason, even if the books decreased in value, they need give him only according to the lower price. Levush. If their condition was that if he wished, he could give them their money and take the Torah scroll, then even if he later agreed to give them the Torah scroll, nevertheless they are not called the ones in possession, since he has the ability to remove them by payment. Magen Avraham.
Only: All this is in a case like this, where without the condition he would have no right at all to take money for his share, as above, and therefore the Torah scroll is in the possession of those who remain, except that the condition causes them to give him money. They can say, bring proof how much is due to you because of your condition, and take it. But partners who bought a house together, and there was a condition between them that they would give him his share whenever he wished, and afterward the value increased, and one of them wants to withdraw and take his share from those who remain, they certainly must give him according to what it is worth now. The condition does not weaken his power; it came only to improve his power, so that he would not need to wait until the time they wanted. This is unlike here, regarding a Torah scroll, where by law he has no right in the Torah scroll apart from the condition.
