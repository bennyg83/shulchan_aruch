[15] Wax candles, etc. The Taz wrote: it seems obvious that the same applies, and all the more so, to the candelabrum called a leuchter that is in a house of non-Jews, that one may not use it in a synagogue, and even not in his home. This is his wording. I am uncertain, for a candle is different, since it is a mitzvah item, as above in siman 151, whereas candelabra are no worse than one who wears clothing that he worshipped while praying, and even a house in which worship was performed on a fixed basis is permitted for prayer, as Re'em wrote in siman 79. Although Magen Avraham wrote that the reason there is because it is attached, see there, nevertheless I examined the responsum of Re'em itself, and it does not imply this. However, to make this house into a permanent synagogue, it appears that Re'em agrees it is forbidden. See Yoreh Deah, siman 151, seif 9. In one's home, it is obvious that there is no prohibition regarding the candelabra mentioned above. Responsa Chavos Yair, siman 161, wrote that a cloth into which the Togarmah people bring their children when initiating them into their faith and religion is forbidden to make into a cloth for a Torah scroll, but it is permitted to wrap children in it for circumcision.
