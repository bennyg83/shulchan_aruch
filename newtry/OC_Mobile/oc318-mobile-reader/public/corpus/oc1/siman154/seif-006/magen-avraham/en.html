From an ark into a chair. For in an ark the Torah scroll is always placed, whereas on the chair they place the Torah scroll only sometimes. It implies from the Mordechai that even one built into the wall, as written in seif 1, may not be made into a chair; see there.
A pouch for a Chumash. All the more so it is forbidden to wrap the cloth of a Torah scroll around a haftarah book, as stated in the Gemara. A cloth spread over the table may not be spread over the lectern before the chazzan. Maharil.
It does not have the sanctity of an ark. For they never place the Torah scroll upon it without a cloth. Maharam Padua, siman 82, likewise wrote that it is permitted to make from a paroches a cloth for the table. Nevertheless it requires study why they have the practice to spread it as a canopy for bridegrooms. Perhaps the heart of beis din stipulates regarding them, as written in seif 8; see the end of the siman.
Which are not sacred. For they are neither for beauty nor for clothing, but only as a sign so that they not err. There. Nowadays the practice is to hang them on the Torah scroll for beauty. Cloths spread over the Torah scroll may be hung on the wall of the synagogue, and silver utensils placed on the Torah scroll may be placed on the pillars in the synagogue. Binyamin Ze'ev, siman 174. The Bach wrote in siman 153 that the Maharshal wrote: it appears that it is not called lowering from sanctity if other books are placed in the ark alongside the Torah scroll, for its sanctity still remains. Know this, for we always place invalid sheets in the ark, and the same applies to Chumashim and other books. However, it requires study where they removed the Torah scroll from it, whether one may place other books in it, for since there is no Torah scroll there, perhaps there is a lowering from sanctity temporarily, or perhaps not. This is his wording. To me it appears obvious that it is forbidden even temporarily, and even placing them next to a Torah scroll does not appear easy to permit, for who says to us that this is the practice of the pious. This is his wording. In Sefer Chasidim, siman 834, it says that it is forbidden to place invalid sheets inside it. Perhaps it is permitted because the heart of beis din stipulates. In an ark built like a room, it is obvious that it is permitted, as written at the end of seif 1. In Megillah we say that it is permitted to place the ark in the window to seal it so that impurity not enter the synagogue, even though this is mundane use. One must say that since he does not use it for anything, what difference is there whether it lies here or here? Tosafot in Bava Batra 20 wrote that since he does not nullify it to be there forever, it is permitted. In the Taz in Yoreh Deah it is written that it is forbidden to take a book and place it under another book to raise it so that it be convenient to see and examine it, unless the book is already lying there; then it is permitted to place the second on top of it. This is his wording. From what I wrote above it implies that it is permitted, and certainly when he does so to study from it, as written in siman 315, seif 7. In Sefer Chasidim, siman 898, one should not score lines on booklets upon a book, because booklets have no sanctity until something is written in them. This implies that once something is written in them, one may place them upon it. Afterward he wrote that if the sun is shining and he cannot study, it is permitted to place one book to serve as shade, but not for a need unrelated to study, for it is forbidden to benefit from it; see there. One should not place booklets in a book, nor write his accounts in a book, nor test the quill in it. This is his wording. We say in the second chapter of Shabbos: women die for the sin of calling the Aron Kodesh 'arna,' for one must say Aron Kodesh. However, many times in the Gemara and poskim the term 'aron' appears. One must say that when engaged in study it is different, for everything is a sacred matter. Rashi wrote: 'arna' is what they called their chests, but 'aron' is a name designated also for the Aron Kodesh. I am astonished at the great men of the generation who do not protest against those who make covers for sacred books from pages of sacred writings when binding them. Certainly this is a complete prohibition, for they lower them from their sanctity, and it is proper to put them in genizah, so that they not lie in disgrace.
