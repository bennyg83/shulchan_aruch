And a paroches. It is permitted to make a table covering from a paroches. Eliyah Rabbah permits even making from it a cloth for the lectern, and this also appears from Responsa Bach. It is permitted to cut old silver plaques, whether whole or broken, that are no longer used in the synagogue, in order to make a candlestick for lighting on Shabbos and Yom Tov. Chavos Yair, siman 171. Responsa Bach, siman 17, permits making a cloth for the lectern from a paroches, but forbids making a wedding canopy from it. Magen Avraham is lenient because the beis din implicitly stipulates concerning such items. They should not be given to a non-Jewish launderer; rather, a Jew should wash them, and not together with other items. Knesses HaGedolah.
As sacred as they are. Cloths spread over a Sefer Torah may be hung on the synagogue wall. Likewise, silver objects placed on a Sefer Torah may be placed upon the synagogue pillars. Radbaz. Binyamin Ze’ev, siman 174. Maharshal writes: It appears that placing other sacred books in the ark alongside a Sefer Torah is not considered lowering its sanctity, since its sanctity remains. Proof is that we always place invalid Torah sheets in the ark; the same should apply to Chumashim and other sacred books. Nevertheless, it requires study whether, after the Sefer Torah has been removed, one may place other sacred books in the ark. Since no Sefer Torah is then present, perhaps this constitutes a temporary lowering of sanctity, or perhaps not. Bach writes regarding this: It appears obvious to me that it is forbidden, for even a temporary use is called a lowering of sanctity. It likewise does not appear proper to permit placing them alongside a Sefer Torah. Sefer Chassidim, siman 938, states that invalid sheets may not be placed in the ark. Taz writes that according to this, the practice on Hoshana Rabbah of removing all the Sifrei Torah and placing a burning lamp there is improper, for the wall of the aron kodesh thereby becomes an accessory to that lamp. See there. He further writes that the sanctity of an ark applies specifically when it was made to house a Sefer Torah permanently. If it was made temporarily, with the intention of later constructing a proper aron kodesh, then after the permanent ark has been constructed, the sanctity of the first one ceases, and sacred books may be placed within it. He further writes: Were I not afraid, I would propose a novel principle. The rule that one may not convert an object from a higher sanctity to a lower sanctity applies only while it remains fit for the higher sanctity. If it is no longer fit for that purpose, but only for a lesser sanctity, it is preferable that at least it be used for the lesser sanctity rather than remain unused and be placed in genizah. Thus far Taz. Magen Avraham writes regarding Bach that perhaps it is permitted because the beis din implicitly stipulates concerning it. Where the ark is constructed like a room, it is plainly permitted, as stated above in seif katan 3. Taz in Yoreh Deah, siman 282, writes that one may not take a sacred book and place it beneath another book in order to raise it to a convenient height for study, unless the lower book was already lying there, in which case the second may be placed upon it. Magen Avraham writes that it is permitted, especially when done for purposes of study. Sefer Chassidim states that one should not rule lines on a booklet while it rests upon a sacred book, because the booklet has no sanctity until words have been written in it. Magen Avraham writes that this implies that once words have been written in it, it may be placed upon the book. If sunlight prevents one from studying, one may place another sacred book so that it provides shade, but one may not derive benefit from a sacred book without need. Sefer Chassidim, siman 898. One should not place booklets inside a sacred book, write accounts in a sacred book, or test a pen upon it. Sefer Chassidim, ibid. Magen Avraham writes: I am astonished at the great authorities of the generation who do not protest against those who use pages of sacred books as binding material. This is certainly a complete prohibition, for they lower those pages from their sanctity. Eliyah Rabbah offers some justification for the custom. Responsa Chavos Yair writes that it is doubtful whether one may cut a silver plaque to make a candlestick for the synagogue. Some permit this when the plaque is broken.
