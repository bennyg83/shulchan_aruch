From a chest to a chair: A chest is the Aron Kodesh. Although a chair is also an accessory of sanctity, as above in seif 3, nevertheless it is forbidden, because the Torah scroll rests in the chest constantly, while on the chair, meaning the table, the Torah scroll is placed only sometimes; this is a lowering of sanctity.
A pouch for a Chumash: For the curtain sometimes serves the Torah scroll itself, as above, and a Chumash, even if made in scroll form, has lighter sanctity than a Torah scroll.
It is forbidden: All the more so it is forbidden to wrap a Torah scroll cloth around Nevi'im. A cloth spread over the table may not be spread over the lectern before the chazzan. Cloths spread over the Torah scroll may be hung on the synagogue wall, and likewise silver utensils placed on the Torah scroll may be placed on the pillars in the synagogue. The reason is that he does not hang them there for use; what difference is there whether they rest here or there.
It does not have the sanctity of an ark: For in our times they never place a Torah scroll upon it without a covering, and it is permitted to make from a paroches a cloth for the table. It requires study why the practice is to spread it as a canopy for bridegrooms. Perhaps the condition of beis din is understood to apply to them, as written in Sefer Chasidim.
Only the sanctity of a synagogue: Although it is only an accessory of an accessory, nevertheless it is no worse than other synagogue utensils, whose law is like that of a synagogue. But other accessories of an accessory that are not in the synagogue, such as those of tefillin and the like, have no sanctity at all. Peri Megadim.
That are not sacred: They are neither for beauty nor for clothing, but only as a sign so that people not err; therefore this would be a lowering from the sanctity of the synagogue. Nowadays, when the practice is to hang the pointer on the Torah scroll for beauty, it is certainly an accessory of sanctity, and it is permitted to make it from the poles. It is stated in Sefer Chasidim, siman 935, that it is forbidden to place invalid sheets inside the Aron Kodesh, for this is a lowering of sanctity. The Magen Avraham wrote that perhaps it is permitted because the condition of beis din is understood to apply to them. The Eliyah Rabbah likewise wrote in the name of Sheyarei Kenesses HaGedolah that the practice is to place invalid sheets in it. But one must be careful not to place Chumashim or other books in it, even temporarily, whether the Torah scroll is resting inside it or not. In an ark built into the wall, according to what was explained above in seif 3 in the gloss, that it has only the status of a synagogue, there is room to be lenient.
