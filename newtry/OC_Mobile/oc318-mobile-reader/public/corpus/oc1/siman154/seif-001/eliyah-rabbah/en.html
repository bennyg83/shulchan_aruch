[1] Houses and courtyards, etc. Our courtyards are no better than these. Perhaps, since people pray in them constantly whenever they are crowded, they become sacred. A garden, orchard, or the platform surrounding it, even though it opens to the synagogue, has no sanctity; but if it opens opposite the heichal, one should be stringent not to use them. Maharit, part 4; Magen Avraham. The Taz wrote: because of our sins synagogues were destroyed by empty people, and upon our return we made a small ark for the Torah scroll until Hashem will broaden our ability to make a proper Aron Kodesh. It seems that after the second was made as permanent, the sanctity of the first was nullified, similar to a house rented for a synagogue. Therefore it is permitted to place books inside it. Were I not afraid, I would say that what we say, that it is forbidden to make from a higher sanctity a lower sanctity, applies only when it is fit for the higher sanctity; but if it is fit only for a lower one, this is preferable to its being placed in genizah. This is his wording, and it requires study. Also, at first glance he should have permitted the ark mentioned even for mundane use, similar to houses. Perhaps with respect to an Aron Kodesh he holds one should be more stringent, for the case mentioned above is like permanence.
[2] Built into the wall, etc. The same applies to a wooden wall, for it is like a mere room. Shiyarei Knesses HaGedolah wrote: it is forbidden to place Chumashim and books in the ark, but invalid sheets are customarily placed there. Magen Avraham wrote: in an ark built like a room, it is obvious that it is permitted. Perhaps this refers only to sheets, and not to other books; this requires study.
[3] Houses that they rent, etc. One should not rent it if the area above it is not clean, as above at the end of siman 151.
