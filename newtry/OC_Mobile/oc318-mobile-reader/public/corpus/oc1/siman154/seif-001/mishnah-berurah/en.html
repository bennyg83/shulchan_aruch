The city square: This is the place where they gather on fast days to pray, because then the gathering is large and the synagogues cannot contain them.
Where they gather in them, etc.: This means even an empty house in which no one lives. Needless to say, if they gather to pray in a house in which people live and use it for their needs, it certainly does not have the status of a synagogue, even if they pray there regularly, since it is not designated for prayer alone.
Occasionally: Our courtyards are no better than houses and courtyards in which people pray only when they are crowded. Nevertheless, one may say that since they pray there regularly whenever they are crowded, although it is occasional with respect to this worshipper, the place becomes fixed for those who are lax. Maharit, part 2, siman 4. In large cities where the practice is to pray regularly in the courtyard, it is obvious that it has all the sanctity of a synagogue. But a garden, orchard, or surrounding bench, even if open to the synagogue, has no sanctity. If it was open opposite the heichal, one should be stringent not to act there with levity, for although the place is non-sacred, since it is aligned opposite the sacred area there is a degradation. Ibid.
Those who rent a house: For a fixed time, for a month or a year. The reason is that when the rental period ends, the owner may choose not to rent it to them again; therefore it is only temporary and has no sanctity. This reason is evident from the Levush, though it does not appear so from the Be'er Heitev. See Biur Halacha.
And pray in it: This is specifically if it is only for prayer. But if it is a beis midrash, it has sanctity. Sha'arei Teshuvah in the name of the responsum of Kenesses HaGedolah. I do not know his reason. Perhaps since it is made fully fixed for praying and learning in it, like the manner of a beis midrash, it is not called temporary.
Accessories of sanctity, etc.: This means that although with accessories of a mitzvah we hold in siman 21 that after their mitzvah has passed it is permitted to discard them, this is not so with accessories of sanctity. Rather, even after they are worn out and no longer fit for their use, they still have sanctity and must be placed in genizah. It is also forbidden to use them for any use. This applies specifically to an accessory of sanctity itself, but something that serves the accessory of sanctity, which is called an accessory of an accessory, has no sanctity, and it is permitted to use it even while it still exists. Peri Megadim.
