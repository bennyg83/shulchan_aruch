10) There, in the gloss: It is permitted to remove them from there. Magen Avraham, paragraph 4, writes: 'Meaning, it will have the law of synagogue sanctity.' His intent is that the phrase 'it is permitted to remove them,' used by the Rama, of blessed memory, is not exact. What prohibition could there be in taking the Sefer Torah from this aron and placing it in another aron? Rather, he teaches us the status of sanctity of that place. It is permitted to remove the sefarim from it, and the vacated place retains the law of synagogue sanctity, so that other synagogue articles may be placed there. This also appears to be the intent of the Levush, who writes, 'It is permitted to remove them for other uses.' He means that one may remove the sefarim and leave the place available for other synagogue uses. See Olas Tamid, paragraph 2, and Maamar Mordechai, paragraph 1, who struggled to explain the Rama's words; see there. Eshel Avraham, paragraph 4, similarly writes that all accessories of accessories have the law of synagogue sanctity, as stated in seif 6 in the gloss; see there. See below, paragraph 143.
