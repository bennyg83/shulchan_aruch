If. See Taz. In Peri Chadash there we wrote that there is no dispute. If the substance is repulsive to him, it is forbidden for him to consume because of “You shall not make yourselves repulsive,” as explained in Yoreh Deah 116. If it is not repulsive to him, though it is repulsive to others, it is permitted even according to Taz. Taz’s point is only that when there is less than sixty times its quantity, even if it is not repulsive, so long as the taste of a non-kosher species is perceptible, it is forbidden for synagogue use. According to this, the ruling here concerning oil is difficult, for unstated “oil” means olive oil, and Yoreh Deah 104 explains that oil imparts a detrimental taste. Rather, where it is repulsive, Taz agrees that it is forbidden, as stated, and where there is less than sixty in fat, as written there in Yoreh Deah. This still requires study, for perhaps anything repulsive to others is forbidden for synagogue use because of “Offer it, if you please, to your governor,” whereas in one’s home it may be used for Shabbos lamps, Chanukah lamps, a yahrzeit lamp, and the like, provided it is not repulsive to him. Incidentally, I saw fit to mention what Beit Yosef writes in the name of Rabbeinu Yerucham: if it fell into a hot substance, it is forbidden; if into a cold substance, one passes it through a strainer. At first glance, this implies that even where there is a doubt whether it remained soaking long enough to become absorbed, it is permitted by a combination of two doubts, as in Shach’s question in Yoreh Deah 104, seif katan 4. See Taz, Yoreh Deah 105, letter 3, concerning this.
