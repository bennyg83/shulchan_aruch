[14] They protest, etc. In a case like this we do not say that the heart of beis din stipulates. Levush. This implies that if one stipulates explicitly, the stipulation is effective, as with a Torah scroll; so Magen Avraham wrote. In the opening case, where it is placed on the head of the reader, it appears that we say that, presumably, the heart of beis din stipulates; so it is implied from the responsum of the Rashba cited by the Beit Yosef. This excludes the Olas Tamid, who understood that in the opening case a double stipulation is needed, and in the latter case even an explicit stipulation does not help, and therefore he was astonished at the Shulchan Aruch. This is not so. It is also possible that a crown that one places on his head for his honor is different, and worse.
