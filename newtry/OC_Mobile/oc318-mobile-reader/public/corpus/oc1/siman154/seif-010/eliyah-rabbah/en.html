[10] A pouch for a Chumash, etc. It implies that our paroches is permitted. Cloths spread over a Torah scroll may be hung on the wall of a synagogue, and silver utensils placed on the Torah scroll may be placed on the pillars in the synagogue. Binyamin Ze'ev, siman 174. The Taz wrote in siman 282 that it is forbidden to take a book and place it under another book in order to raise it so that it be convenient to examine it, for this is worse than placing a book on his lap and his two elbows upon it, which is forbidden, unless the lower book is already lying there; then it is permitted to place the second upon it. Likewise, if he needs to study from all of them, it is permitted. The view of Magen Avraham is that it is always permitted. Sefer Chasidim wrote, from siman 894 until 914: one should not score lines on a booklet upon a book until he writes in it. If the sun is shining and he cannot study, it is permitted to place one book to serve as shade. One should not place unwritten booklets inside a book, nor write his accounts in a book, nor test the quill in it, nor place the quill or a knife in a book to store it when he goes, so that it be ready. But when copying, he may place it on the line so that he be ready to find the word. But with a written booklet or a page from a book he should not do so, for it is not proper that one book serve another book. See further there for stringencies regarding the quill and ink with which he writes, and regarding the stick with which they show children the words and letters. Magen Avraham wrote: I am astonished at the great men of the generation who do not protest against those who make book covers from pages of sacred books when binding them. This is a complete prohibition, for they lower them from their sanctity, and it is proper to place them in genizah. In my humble opinion, perhaps they relied on what I wrote in seif katan 1 in the name of the Taz, but his words require study as a matter of law. In Responsa Chavos Yair, siman 162, he is uncertain whether it is permitted to melt a silver plate in order to make from it a candlestick for lighting in the synagogue; and regarding a broken plate, some permit it. See there; it requires study.
