It does not have the law of a synagogue. Beit Yosef writes this in the name of Mahari Chaviv. Under the Turkish government they lacked permission to establish a permanent synagogue and had to hide in basement rooms. Such a place therefore did not have the law of a synagogue, and residential quarters could be situated above it, provided that those upper rooms were kept clean. See what I wrote at the end of siman 151. Accordingly, one should not rent a room for prayer if the area above it is not clean.
