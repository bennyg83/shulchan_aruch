Those who rent a house, etc. See the Mishnah Berurah for the reason, which I wrote according to the Levush’s explanation. Olas Tamid, however, indicates that this ruling applies only to a case like that of Mahari ben Chaviv, where under the Turkish government they had no permission to designate a permanent place for prayer and therefore hid in basements to pray. Such a place is certainly only temporary, since today or tomorrow it may be discovered and the arrangement cancelled before the rental period ends. This differs from people who rent openly in the ordinary manner. For the rental period the premises are effectively sold to them, and they certainly acquire sanctity, since the place is fixed for prayer throughout that entire period and not used merely occasionally. Although the owner may refuse to renew the lease afterward, this is not merely casual use, because during the rental period they have no other fixed place. Knesses HaGedolah, cited in Shaarei Teshuvah, likewise implies that if it is established with the king’s permission, meaning openly and not secretly, it has sanctity. In my opinion this is the correct practical ruling. Even in Mahari ben Chaviv’s case, Maharit, part 2, siman 4, writes that the matter is not clear to him and he inclines to rule stringently throughout the rental period. Therefore, where a place is rented openly in the ordinary manner, one certainly should not be lenient.
