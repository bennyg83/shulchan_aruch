“Hollow.” Meaning hollow organs; one should not say chalalim, for “corpse” (chalal) is not an apt term for creation.

“That is to say.” Because the reading “if one of them were blocked he could not survive even an hour” is hard, for a person keeps his mouth closed many hours—therefore it says “that is to say,” etc. But our custom is not to say even “one hour,” as the Rambam writes, and so Agudah, which counts forty-three words and also reads “and known before the Throne,” etc., not “before You”—see there; some writings count forty-five words.

“Sick.” Bach in the name of Ramaz, Shelah, and Mateh Moshe wrote not to say “choli” (sick) but only “Rofei kol basar,” and so Agudah.

“Spiritual.” Kavvanos writes that the soul benefits from the spirituality of the food and the body from the physicality of the food, and through this they are bound together by means of the food.
