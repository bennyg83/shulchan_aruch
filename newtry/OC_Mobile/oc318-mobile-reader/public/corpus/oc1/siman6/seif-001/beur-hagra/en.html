Seif 1, "that the creation of man," etc. So the Beis Yosef explained according to the Tur, who did not explain it as referring to "with wisdom," for it is known that man's creation was with wondrous wisdom.

"And some explain," etc., "as below," etc. Rashi explained there that "Who acts wondrously" means that man, etc.; this is wonder and wisdom. But Tosafos explained "Who acts wondrously" like Rashi, and as stated at the beginning of Bereshis Rabbah: Rabbi Tanchuma on the verse "For You are great and do wonders"; Rabbi Tanchuma said: this skin-bag, if it has a hole like the eye of a needle, its air leaves it; but this human is made with cavities and cavities, openings and openings, and his air does not leave him. He expounds this on "does wonders." "With wisdom" means as stated in Tanchuma: "God blessed man"; Rabbi Bun said: with wisdom, for He prepared his sustenance and afterward created him, as stated in Sanhedrin 38a, gloss beginning "so that he should enter," etc.; the parable, etc., as it says, "Wisdom built her house," etc., and all needs of the world, etc.

"Hollow ones." This is the reading of the Rif and Rosh, unlike our reading and Rashi. So wrote the Tur. The Beis Yosef wrote that "cavities" refers to the hollow itself, to which creation does not apply, but "hollow ones" refers to the hollow organ. This is what is meant by "many hollow organs."

"Meaning many openings and also," etc. That is, therefore it says "openings" twice and "hollow ones" twice, to indicate their abundance.

"Such as," etc., "like," etc. Rashi there.

"For if one were stopped," etc. This is the Rambam's reading; but our reading, and that of Rashi, Rif, and Rosh, places "if one were opened" first.

"That is," etc. Meaning, the Rosh and Tur read "even for one hour." The Rada and Kol Bo wrote in the name of the Ramam that this should not be read, for a person can close his mouth for many hours and survive. The Tur upholds it, explaining that it refers to the human's emergence from his mother's womb, when he could not survive, as stated at the end of Niddah chapter 3: were it not so, etc. Still it is difficult: what does "if one of them were opened" mean, since it refers only to the mouth? Therefore he wrote that among the openings there is one opening, etc. But one can ask: why did it first say "openings" in the plural, if it refers only to one of them? Rather, since "if one were opened" refers to all the hollow organs, it also said "many openings" and refers to "and created." This is what he wrote: "and the hollow organs, if," etc. All this follows the Tur's explanation. Against this the Beis Yosef objected: if so, "one of them" regarding stopping means only one, whereas "one" regarding opening means any one; and further, "if one were opened" refers to emergence into the world's air and not to relieving oneself. Therefore he wrote: "and one can further explain that the limit," etc., not emergence into the world's air, and it refers to all the openings. Although if the mouth were stopped it is not an act of relieving oneself, nevertheless the anus and membrum are actual relieving oneself, even for one hour. This is what he wrote: "and since included," etc. Still, even according to this, "if one were opened" is not relieving oneself; rather, "hollow ones" is for relieving oneself, namely the stomach and intestines, and it is related to the blessing. This is what he wrote: "and included among the hollow organs," etc. Since this did not satisfy him, he wrote: "and perhaps further," etc., that "opened" also concerns relieving oneself. All this was forced by the reading "even one hour." But in our reading it is absent and none of this is needed. Also "if one were opened" concerns relieving oneself: if it were open and there were no retaining force until matter exits through the openings, one could not survive. This is primary.

"Healer of the ill." This is the reading of the Rif.

"On account of," etc. The Rada.

"Because," etc. Like Rashi and Tosafos, as above.

"And one can further explain that He acts wondrously," etc. Meaning, through food.
