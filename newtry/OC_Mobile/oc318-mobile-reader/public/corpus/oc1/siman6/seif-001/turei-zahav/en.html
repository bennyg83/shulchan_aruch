“And created in him openings,” etc. First explain the Tur’s view, then what calls for scrutiny in the Shulchan Aruch’s wording. The Tur wrote “chalulim chalulim” (hollows) and not “chalalim chalalim.” One might ask why he rejected the version chalalim, when our Gemara also has that reading — and the Beit Yosef explains that for chalalim “creation” does not apply, for there is no substance there except through the organ around which the cavity exists; therefore he reads chalulim, referring to organs that are hollow. One could ask: if so, he should also say nekuvim (perforated) rather than nekavim (openings). Also, the Beit Yosef’s explanation of the Tur — “if he opens,” etc., referring to the beginning of creation — does not fit the hollow organs but the perforated ones, namely mouth, anus, nostrils. Incidentally one resolves why he doubled chalulim. It appears to me that the Tur does not explain like Rashi: according to Rashi the reading chalulim refers to creating the cavity Hashem made for heart, intestines, and belly — sealed on every side — whereas nekavim are organs perforated outward, e.g. mouth, nostrils, anus; but the Tur explains the reverse — e.g. mouth and navel: in the mother’s womb the mouth is called hollow and closed and the navel perforated and open, and afterward it is reversed. Therefore he said “two openings [nekavim]” — one corresponding to what was perforated in the womb and the second to what was perforated afterward — and upon those very openings rests “chalulim chalulim”: to teach that those openings we mentioned are also “hollow hollow” — sometimes this one is hollow and that perforated and sometimes the reverse — therefore do not read chalalim, for then it would be a separate topic; whereas chalulim indicates attachment — that the openings themselves are also hollow. And now “if he opens or seals” is explained properly afterward — namely the change between the womb and afterward — and thereby one mentions the Creator’s wisdom; and because a person’s bodily elimination is also through these openings, they anchored this blessing to that hour — such is the Tur’s view. It requires study regarding the Shulchan Aruch, who explains chalulim like Rashi and “if he seals” like the Tur — the two do not sit well together.
“Even one hour.” In the Beit Yosef citing Kol Bo, Maharam did not say this matter, for one can live a day or two; and others said it refers to the hour of formation — then if what was closed opened or the reverse it would not endure even one hour. Accordingly it appears to me this depends on the interpretation of the blessings we cited — Maharam like Rashi and others like the Tur — if so both readings are correct and one must not erase any version found among these two.
“Healer of the illness of all flesh.” The Gemara asks what does he conclude — Rav said “healer of the sick”; Shmuel said to him, Abba made everyone harvesters [ketzirei] — rather say “healer of all flesh”; Rav Papa cites similarly afterward — therefore one should not read “rofei choli kol basar” but “rofei kol basar,” and so the Rif — but Ashkenazi texts have “rofei choli kol basar,” etc., and so the Beit Yosef. One must explain according to the Rosh that specifically when one says “rofei cholim” it implies they are all [ketzirei]; but when one says “rofei choli kol basar” the meaning is that the waste should not rot — and that is “illness of all flesh” by nature, meaning there is already waste of all flesh and that is the “illness,” only Hashem removes it — whereas when one says “cholim” the implication is people who are sick. From Darchei Moshe it appears one could read “rofei choli kol basar”; Maharshal wrote in the name of Rashbash one could read “rofei kol basar.” I say both versions are valid — Heaven forbid to erase one and emend like the other — each has a basis to rely on.
