“And created in him,” Taz: Behold the Beit Yosef’s view is explained in the Shulchan Aruch; and the word “with wisdom” is like Rashi Berachot 60b s.v. umefaleh — this is the wonder and wisdom — or like Tosafot there s.v. asher; and the Tur omitted both. The Beit Yosef explains that man’s creation is certainly wisdom, and it is difficult why he chose his own path and omitted Rashi and Tosafot. Also the explanation that if the designated one — the mouth — were sealed when emerging into the world’s air, even a moment he could not survive, and “will be sealed” in the future, since afterward too if sealed excessively; but “one hour” refers to emerging into the world’s air that it would have remained so; and “if he opens” refers to many organs, any of which if opened even after emerging into the world’s air he could not live even one moment. Taz challenged: why not say chalalim, for “creation” does not apply — likewise nekavim does not apply to “creation”; and Bach challenged further: behold “Who creates darkness” is on absence of light. I would say: certainly “creation” applies to something from nothing, like openings and darkness — nevertheless it is called making, for through openings one brings in and out, and removal of light was a making and thereby there was darkness; unlike hollows, which in any case the cavity existed, for through forming the organ the cavity in the middle came to be in any case (see Rama on Eichah “they were considered as clay vessels, the work of a potter’s hands” — see there). This is what Beit Yosef and Shulchan Aruch say: openings — mouth, etc.; hollows — organs, etc.; and for openings he did not mention “organs,” for it refers to openings literally and “creation” applies to them as I wrote; and he said “and created in him” not “formed,” for with openings the term is “formation” (formation only for substance, and “creation” applies also to substance, as “and He created man”). He further challenged Beit Yosef: the Tur brought the Gemara Niddah — in his mother’s womb his navel is open and mouth sealed, etc. — implying that “if he opens” refers to the navel at the hour of emerging into the world’s air, as if the mouth were sealed it would remain sealed, so too the navel would remain open (as he took the letter alef as future). And this is that hollow organs if they open afterward too he could not survive a moment; and you would say it refers only to emerging into the world’s air, for afterward in any case he would live until eight days like a treifah — unlike Beit Yosef who explained hollow organs even afterward; if so why take “opens” and “will be sealed” as futures? (And this is what Taz said — hollow organs do not apply except to mouth, etc., and incidentally he mentions nose and anus.) Therefore Taz’s explanation: the Tur does not explain like Rashi that hollow organs refer to heart, stomach, nose, intestines, and openings to mouth and anus, but the reverse (unlike Beit Yosef who explained the Tur like Rashi — see there) — meaning only mouth and navel, which is the end of the intestines; and conversely openings to intestines: in his mother’s womb navel open and thereby intestines open, and the oral cavity in his mother’s womb sealed — thereby there is a cavity in it — and one mentions the Creator’s wisdom. This is “Who formed with wondrous wisdom”: in his mother’s womb mouth must be hollow and sealed and intestines and navel open specifically, and into the world’s air the reverse specifically — and this is well hinted in the Tur’s words “with wisdom,” see there. And do not say He certainly created openings in mouth and navel except that not at one time — this in his mother’s womb and that at emerging into the world’s air; and hollows likewise in such a case; therefore he said doubling “openings, openings” — not that both openings exist at one time, but there is a time when one is perforated, etc.; and “hollows” — those organs themselves, and mouth and navel we spoke of, are called openings and also called hollows — this in his mother’s womb, etc. — but it is correct to say chalulim to clarify it refers to those organs themselves, lest you err that it refers to other organs. And now do not say “if he opens or seals one of them” is future; and what is “upon” in his mother’s womb too — if one opens it is the mouth, if one seals it is the navel — he could not survive one hour in his mother’s womb; and because it refers also to afterward they adjoined this to relieving oneself. He challenged the Mechaber who explained the Tur’s words: hollow organs like Rashi and openings like Niddah on the mouth alone — and accordingly “if one of them seals” is the designated mouth and “if one opens” would be any one, and this is impossible (see Tzohar HaTeivah on interpreting echad ha’am with patach or segol kamatz — see there). See Rashi Berachot there s.v. o yiftach — “opens” refers to hollow organs, “seals” to openings; seemingly what does this teach us except to negate Beit Yosef’s explanation that hollow organs caused organs, etc., and “opens an organ” — therefore as “seals” refers to openings not perforated (even for Beit Yosef), so explain “seals” of hollow organs not of organs. We extended this according to our short understanding; to grasp the holy ones’ view I need to see Mishnah Berurah.
