(12) A blessing of thanksgiving. It is not a blessing that must immediately follow another blessing. Therefore, upon arising in the morning one may recite the blessings through the words preceding Elokai Neshamah, and begin Elokai Neshamah in the synagogue. So writes the Tur. Nevertheless, it is best to place Elokai Neshamah immediately after Asher Yatzar. See the commentary of the Gra to siman 46, who likewise holds that Elokai Neshamah is connected to Asher Yatzar. It is therefore proper to be careful about this initially. If one did not recite Elokai Neshamah until after praying Shemoneh Esrei, he should not recite it afterward, because by already saying “Who revives the dead” he has expressed the idea of restoring souls. So rules Peri Chadash. Shaarei Teshuvah strongly questions this ruling and concludes that, at least if when reciting the blessing of “Who revives the dead” he intended to recite Elokai Neshamah after the prayer, he may recite it afterward. See there for the reason. This is also implied by Peri Megadim in siman 52; see what we wrote there in Biur Halachah. In the text of the blessing one should not say, “You created it within me,” but rather, “You created it; You breathed it into me.”
