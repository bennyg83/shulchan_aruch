Comment 3, "ill," etc. That one should not say choli. For in the Gemara we say: Rav would conclude "Healer of the ill." Shmuel said to him: you have made us all short and sick. From this, one should not say even choli kol basar, and the view of the aforementioned is choli with twenty-two. Their reason is specifically regarding Rofeh choli without qualification — on this Shmuel was particular; but choli kol basar may well be said — the intent is Healer of that illness which is in all flesh, namely the waste in every person's body. As if one eats excessively that food will sicken him, or if he eats harmful food the physician must give him potions and remedies to remove that food from his body; and as long as he has not received healing, so too he is ill. So too the waste produced from excess food is likewise ill until it is expelled from his body; the Holy One, blessed be He, implanted in his nature to expel it — this is called "Healer of the ill of all flesh," because He implanted a repulsive force in the esophagus to reject it. For four powers are given to the esophagus: the power drawing food from above downward; the power holding food so it remains until digested; the digestive power; and the power repelling waste after digestion — and these are opposing forces. Therefore in the esophagus were created ligaments running lengthwise and crosswise, warp and woof — and this is the wisdom the Rabbeinu Yonah mentioned at the end, see there, "Who selects the good," etc.

So too in the Agudah. For if so there are forty-four words — why did he write that there are forty-three words? As written in the Magen Avraham in the comment before this.
