Sephardim did not practice so; rather each one blesses at home on netilas yadayim and Asher Yatzar, and in the synagogue they begin from Elohai Neshamah (Beis Yosef), and he wrote that this is the essential law. Nevertheless, per the Arizal one must adjoin the blessing Elohai Neshamah to the blessing Asher Yatzar, because it corresponds to Imma, which is constantly joined with Abba — see Shaar HaKavvanos, discourse on Birkas HaShachar. So too in Sefer Pri Etz Chayyim, Shaar HaBerachos chapter 5, and Mateh Moshe, page 6; Sedei Chemed, letter 1; Kesser Godel, siman 5, letter 6; so too Ben Ish Chai, Parashat Vayeshev, letter 2. Nevertheless one must not protest those who practice not to adjoin it. Shaarei Teshuvah, page 45, paragraph 13; Knesset HaGedolah there, letter 7. The custom of the Arizal was to arrange all eighteen blessings upon rising from his bed at home before going to the synagogue — see Shaar HaKavvanos there. When someone would bless and arrange the blessings in the synagogue, he would answer Amen after him; and even if he was in the middle of his prayer he would interrupt to answer Amen after them, even if many were blessing — Shaar HaKavvanos there; Shaarei Teshuvah, letter 6.
