“A berachah of praise.” It is not a berachah consecutive to another, so he may say in the morning upon rising up through Elohai Neshamah, and in synagogue begin with Elohai Neshamah (Tur). Bach wrote that nonetheless he should say Elohai Neshamah immediately, like wayfarer’s prayer in siman 110:6; so too Levush. Mishnah Berurah wrote to say all the berachos through la’amo Yisrael, because netilas yadayim applies specifically to prayer—see there—and it is not compelled; I have not seen elderly rabbis do so, for the Gemara says first all the berachos and then netilas yadayim, which implies even if he does not pray immediately, as on 11a regarding Rav. Seder Hayom wrote: one who rises early and will need to sleep again should say Elohai Neshamah and HaMapil, etc., without the closing blessing, and when he rises a second time say it with the closing formula; for some say even by day when he sleeps he says Elohai Neshamah, as Beis Yosef siman 231.
