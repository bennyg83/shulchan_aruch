“Some have the practice to wait,” etc. The reason: since for the other blessings we do not recite them immediately adjacent to washing and leave them for Baruch Hu — netilas yadayim is postponed with them as well; Sephardim hold netilas yadayim must always precede the act, as with every mitzvah blessing — only they wait until after washing because it is unbecoming for a man [gevara lo chazi]; but the others, which are blessings of praise, one may say after doing them. Darkhei Moshe wrote that he conducted himself like Sephardim.

“Even in such a case,” etc. That is what most people practice — to pray Slichos before dawn on Slichos days; several verses are said there and afterward they bless netilas yadayim and the Torah. For Birkas HaTorah one can justify that those verses one reads only as prayer and they did not obligate Birkas HaTorah on them; but regarding netilas yadayim, interrupting with such a gap requires study — and especially puzzling: if one already relieved himself before morning he surely needed then to bless netilas yadayim and Asher Yatzar — how bless afterward a second time? Darkhei Moshe wrote on this that if he had strength he would abolish [Slichos] so as not to bless twice; then he somewhat justified and concluded: one who wishes to remove doubt should go outside after Slichos end, pour water, rub, and then may bless with the congregation — and even in this there is room to question, as below siman 7. And it appears to me to defend the sheliach tzibbur for Slichos: his blessing a second time is to discharge those not expert in blessings — and even though there may be no non-expert in synagogue, nevertheless he acts properly, as the Beit Yosef wrote siman 124 citing Abudarham in the name of the Rambam regarding the cantor’s repetition in synagogue even when all are expert — originally it was instituted for perhaps some might not be expert — see there — and so here; except that the congregation answering after him on netilas yadayim has no basis if they blessed first — why bless twice? And if they did not bless first it is a large interruption — still it is better they not bless first than make a blessing in vain — and this is obvious, as above.
