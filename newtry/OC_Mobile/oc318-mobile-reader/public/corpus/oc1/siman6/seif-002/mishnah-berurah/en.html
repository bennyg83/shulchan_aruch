(7) To wait. There is a tradition from Rabbi Yehudah HaChasid that one who leaves his home for the synagogue before praying should recite the verse of Shema Yisrael. He should intend not to fulfill thereby the positive mitzvah of Krias Shema, unless he fears that the time for Krias Shema will pass. See what we wrote below in Mishnah Berurah at the end of siman 46.
(8) To recite the blessing. This applies specifically if he goes immediately to the synagogue. If he studies after washing, and certainly if he interrupts with idle conversation and only afterward goes to the synagogue and recites the blessing over washing the hands there, he does not act properly according to any opinion, because there is a lengthy interruption between the washing and the blessing, as explained below. Therefore, those who recite Tehillim before the blessing over washing the hands do not act properly, because this constitutes a lengthy interruption. Levush writes that those who recite Tehillim would do better to recite them before the prayer; see there for the reason.
(9) This custom was not adopted. The later authorities agree that one should follow the Sephardic practice and not initially interrupt between washing the hands and reciting the blessing. Rather, immediately after washing he should recite the blessing over washing the hands. If, however, when he washes he needs to relieve himself, so that he is forbidden even to speak words of Torah, then even if he can restrain himself for the distance of a parsah he should not recite the blessing until after relieving himself. He should then recite the blessing over washing the hands and Asher Yatzar. See Shaarei Teshuvah, and below, siman 92, seif 1 in the gloss and Mishnah Berurah there. See also above, siman 4, where in Mishnah Berurah we cited Chayei Adam that initially it is preferable to delay the blessing until after cleaning oneself and preparing to pray, for then one fulfills the requirement according to all opinions. In Biur Halachah there we wrote that it is proper at that time to arrange, after the blessing over washing the hands and Asher Yatzar, the other morning blessings as well. This is the case to which Chayei Adam refers when he writes that one fulfills the requirement according to all opinions. See Biur Halachah there, where Maaseh Rav likewise rules in accordance with Chayei Adam. Nevertheless, one who follows Shaarei Teshuvah also has authorities upon whom to rely.
(10) Who already recited them at home. See Magen Avraham, Peri Megadim, and the other later authorities, who agree that even a chazzan who recited the blessings at home should not repeat them in the synagogue. Nowadays everyone is proficient, each person recites the blessings for himself, and no one intends to fulfill his obligation through the chazzan. Therefore, in a place where the custom is for the chazzan to recite all the blessings publicly, as in our communities on the Yamim Noraim, when he publicly recites the blessing over washing the hands, Asher Yatzar, and the blessing over Torah study, he should take care initially not to recite them at home. If he already recited them at home, he should seek one listener who has not yet recited them and ask him to intend to fulfill his obligation through the chazzan. The chazzan must then be very careful to articulate the entire blessing audibly, unlike the practice in some places where he makes only the beginning and end audible. Since he intends to discharge the listener, the listener must hear the entire text of the blessing.
(11) In such a case, etc. This refers to the widespread custom among the public to recite Selichos before dawn during the Selichos period. There they mention several verses, and afterward recite the blessing over washing the hands and the blessing over Torah study. Regarding the blessing over Torah study, this can be reconciled somewhat, because those verses are recited only as prayer and, according to some authorities, do not require the blessing over Torah study, as explained in siman 46, seif 9 in the gloss; see Mishnah Berurah there. Regarding washing the hands, however, the insertion of such a lengthy interruption requires study. The later authorities agree that one should not act this way. Rather, he should recite the blessing at home immediately upon arising and should not repeat it in the synagogue, as explained in seif 1 of this siman. Alternatively, when he arises he should not recite the blessing over washing the hands. After completing Selichos he should check whether he needs to relieve himself, or urinate and clean his hands, and then recite the blessing over washing the hands and Asher Yatzar and proceed to pray, as we wrote above in the name of Chayei Adam.
