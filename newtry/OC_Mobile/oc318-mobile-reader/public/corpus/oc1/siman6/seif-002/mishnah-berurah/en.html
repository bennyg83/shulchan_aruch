(ז) To wait — a kabbalistic practice from R. Yehudah he-Hasid: one leaving his house for synagogue before praying should say the verse Shema Yisrael, but intend not to fulfill [the obligation of] Keriat Shema thereby unless he sees that the time for Keriat Shema will pass; see below at the end of siman 46 what we wrote in Mishnah Berurah there.

(ח) To bless — specifically if he goes immediately to synagogue; but if he learns after netilas yadayim, and all the more so when he interrupts with idle talk and afterward goes to synagogue and blesses there al netilas yadayim — according to all opinions he does not act properly, for there is a great interruption between netilah and blessing, as below; therefore those who say Tehillim before al netilas yadayim do not act well, for it is a great interruption. Levush wrote: those who say Tehillim had better say them before prayer, for this reason.

(ט) They do not do so — and the latter authorities agreed to practice like Sephardim, not to interrupt ab initio between the blessing al netilas yadayim and netilas yadayim, but as soon as he washes he must bless al netilas yadayim; however if at the time he washes he needs to relieve himself — for even in Torah matters it is forbidden, and even if he can restrain himself up to a parsa — he should not bless al netilas yadayim until he relieves himself and blesses al netilas yadayim and Asher Yatzar [Shaarei Teshuvah]; see below siman 92 seif 1 in Hagahah and Mishnah Berurah there; and see above siman 4 in Mishnah Berurah where we wrote in the name of Chayei Adam that ab initio it is better to wait from blessing al netilas yadayim until after he relieves himself and wishes to pray, for then he fulfills according to all; see Biur Halachah there where we wrote it is good then to arrange after al netilas yadayim and Asher Yatzar also the other morning blessings — in this manner Chayei Adam speaks who wrote one fulfills according to all; see Biur Halachah there that in Maaseh Rav he also wrote like Chayei Adam; nevertheless one who acts like our times also has on what to rely.

(י) Who blesses them at home — see Magen Avraham and Peri Megadim and other latter authorities who agreed that even a sheliach tzibbur who blessed at home does not bless again in synagogue, for in our days everyone is expert and each blesses for himself and does not wish to fulfill through the sheliach tzibbur’s blessing; therefore where the custom is that the sheliach tzibbur recites all blessings with the congregation, as in our places where the custom is so on the Days of Awe that he blesses with the congregation also al netilas yadayim and Asher Yatzar and Birkas HaTorah — he should be careful from the outset not to bless them at home. If it happened he already blessed at home, he should try to ask one of the listeners who has not yet blessed to intend to fulfill through his blessing; but then the sheliach tzibbur must be very careful to enunciate every word of the blessing, not as practiced in some places where the sheliach tzibbur enunciates only the opening and closing words of the blessing, for since his intent then in the blessing is to exempt, the listener must enunciate every word of the blessing.

(יא) In such a case, etc. — namely what most people practice, to pray Selichos before dawn on Selichos days, and there several verses are mentioned and afterward they bless al netilas yadayim and Birkas HaTorah; regarding Birkas HaTorah one can somewhat justify that those verses one says only as prayer and they are not obligated in Birkas HaTorah according to some poskim, as explained siman 46 seif 9 in Hagahah — see Mishnah Berurah there; but regarding netilas yadayim, that one interrupts with such an interruption — difficult to justify; the latter authorities agreed not to do so, but one should bless at home immediately upon rising, and again not bless al netilas yadayim in synagogue, as explained this siman seif 1; or upon rising not bless al netilas yadayim but after finishing Selichos examine himself for his openings or pour water and rub, then bless al netilas yadayim and Asher Yatzar and pray, as we wrote above in the name of Chayei Adam.
