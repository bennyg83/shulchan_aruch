To wait. Kabbalah from R' Yitzchak Chasid: one who leaves his house for the synagogue before praying should say the paragraph of Shema Yisrael. Minchat Tam:

For the synagogue. Bach wrote only when he goes immediately to the synagogue. And in Baer Heitev he wrote that those who say Tehillim before washing the hands do not act properly, for it is a great interruption; see siman 46. Levush wrote that those who say Tehillim should say them before prayer; see there for the reason:

In this regard there are those accustomed, etc. That is, what the majority of the people are accustomed to — to pray selichot before morning light on selichot days — and there they mention several verses and afterward bless on washing the hands and on the Torah; and the Taz wrote regarding the blessing on the Torah, it can be explained that those verses are not said as obligatory text but as prayer, and they are not required for the blessing on the Torah; but regarding netilat yadayim — that they interrupt with such an interruption — it is difficult to explain; and it is especially puzzling if he already relieved himself earlier in the morning, for he certainly needed to bless then on netilat yadayim and Asher Yatzar — so how can he bless again afterward? See there. And Bach ruled that one should bless in his house immediately upon rising and should not bless in the synagogue. And a shaliach tzibur who blessed in his house should not return and bless in the synagogue. Magen Avraham §462, Beit Yosef, Yad Aharon — unlike Shlah and Taz, who gave merit to the shaliach tzibur to bless a second time in order to discharge those who are not proficient; and see responsum of Yad Eliyahu siman 44.
