To wait. This is a tradition from Rabbi Yehudah HaChasid. One who leaves his home for the synagogue before praying should recite the verse of Shema Yisrael. Mateh Moshe.
To the synagogue. Bach writes that this applies specifically when he goes immediately to the synagogue. Olas Tamid writes that those who recite Tehillim before the blessing over washing the hands do not act properly, because this constitutes a lengthy interruption; see siman 46. Levush writes that those who recite Tehillim should do so before the prayer; see there for the reason.
In such a case some have the custom, etc. This refers to the widespread custom among the public to recite Selichos before dawn during the Selichos period. There they mention several verses, and afterward they recite the blessing over washing the hands and the blessing over Torah study. Taz writes that regarding the blessing over Torah study this can be reconciled, because those verses are recited only as prayer and therefore do not require a blessing over Torah study. Regarding washing the hands, however, the practice of inserting such an interruption requires study. It is even more difficult if he already relieved himself before dawn, for he certainly had to recite then the blessing over washing the hands and Asher Yatzar. How, then, can he recite the blessing again afterward? See there. Bach rules that one should recite the blessing at home immediately upon arising and should not recite it in the synagogue. A chazzan who recited it at home should not repeat it in the synagogue. So rule Magen Avraham, Olas Tamid, Halachah Berurah, and Yad Aharon, contrary to Shelah and Taz, who defended the custom of the chazzan reciting it again to discharge one who is not proficient. See Responsa Yad Eliyahu, at the end of siman 30, and Sefer Birkas Avraham, simanim 19 through 27.
