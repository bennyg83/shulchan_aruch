“To wait.” Kabbalistic teaching from Rabbi Yehudah Chassid: one leaving home for synagogue before praying should say Shema Yisrael (Mateh Moshe).

“To bless.” Specifically when he goes straight to synagogue (Bach). Mishnah Berurah writes that those who recite Tehillim before netilas yadayim do poorly, for it is a great interruption—see there and siman 47. Levush wrote that those who say Tehillim should say it before prayer—for that reason.

“They did not adopt.” Darkei Moshe wrote that he personally follows Sefardi custom; so too Maharil in a responsum—to say the berachos of Selichos immediately upon rising. Mishnah Berurah wrote that the shaliach tzibbur should bless at home before Tehillim and then bless in synagogue to discharge those not proficient; even though now all are proficient, the enactment of the ancients nonetheless stands. It appears to me this is not comparable: only for Shemoneh Esreh was there an enactment of the ancients, and also because of kedushah, as Beis Yosef siman 59 cites the Rosh—but these berachos were instituted for each person at home, only that the practice is to say them in synagogue; in that connection siman 197 records that he enacted the shaliach tzibbur to say them in synagogue, and some disputed him—see there. Why then say a blessing in vain where there is no congregation? Even according to siman 59 that one who heard may recite Shema for one who did not hear—there the fellow listens in silence and is discharged by his friend’s berachah; here each blesses for himself, so it is a blessing in vain. So too Darkei Moshe wrote that on Selichos days one should wash netilas yadayim before Selichos—yet the practice is to bless again; one might say they hold a second washing is needed, but that is not correct, for one need not bless again, as siman 4:14, etc. If so they must hold it is forbidden to bless again in public, and if no one present failed to bless, they should not say the berachos at all, like Sefardi custom—all the more so according to Mishnah Berurah here that they do not intend to be discharged by another.
