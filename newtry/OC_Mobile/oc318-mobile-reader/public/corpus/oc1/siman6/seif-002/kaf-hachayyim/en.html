2) [Seif 2] Some practice waiting, etc. All the later authorities agree that one should not interrupt between the blessing Al Netilas Yadayim and the washing. Rather, immediately after washing one must recite Al Netilas Yadayim. Some write that one should recite the blessing after drying, as stated above in siman 4, but the practice is not so; see what we wrote above in siman 4, paragraphs 1 and 8. If, at the time he washes his hands, he needs to relieve himself; so that even words of Torah are forbidden, and even if he can restrain himself for the time needed to walk a parsah; he should not recite Al Netilas Yadayim until he has relieved himself. He should then recite Al Netilas Yadayim and Asher Yatzar. Shaarei Teshuvah, paragraph 5; see there. Although in this case he does not recite Al Netilas Yadayim immediately after washing, he must nevertheless raise his hands upward toward his head, etc., while reciting Al Netilas Yadayim, as stated above in siman 4, paragraph 16. This is obvious.
3) There: The Sephardim did not adopt this practice. Rather, each person recites Al Netilas Yadayim and Asher Yatzar at home, and in the synagogue they begin with Elohai Neshamah. Beis Yosef writes that this is the primary practice. According to the Arizal, however, one must place the blessing Elohai Neshamah immediately after Asher Yatzar, because it corresponds to Imma, who is constantly joined with Abba. See Shaar HaKavanos, the discourse on the morning blessings. So too in Pri Etz Chaim, Shaar HaBerachos, chapter 5; Matzas Shimurim, page 6; Solet Belulah, paragraph 1; Kesher Gudal, siman 5, paragraph 6; and Ben Ish Chai, parashas Vayeishev, paragraph 2. Nevertheless, one should not protest against those whose practice is not to place them together. Shalmei Tzibbur, page 45c; Kesher Gudal there, paragraph 7. The Arizal's practice was to recite all eighteen blessings in order when he arose from his bed, at home, before going to the synagogue. See Shaar HaKavanos there. When someone recited the blessings in order in the synagogue, he would answer Amen after him. Even if he was in the middle of Pesukei DeZimra, he would interrupt to answer Amen after the blessings, even when many people were reciting them. Shaar HaKavanos there; Shaarei Teshuvah, paragraph 6.
4) There, in the gloss: In any event, he should not recite the blessing twice, etc. This law is not applicable among us, because our practice follows the Arizal: each person recites the blessings at home upon arising from his bed, before coming to the synagogue, and the prayer leader likewise does not recite the morning blessings in the synagogue. We have therefore not discussed this at length.
