Some. See Taz and Beit Yosef in the name of Mahari Abuhav, who writes that the blessing over washing the hands should be recited before drying, so that it precedes the completion of the act. Regarding washing for a meal this is correct, because drying is indispensable, since one who eats without drying is considered as though he ate impure bread. Morning washing differs, because drying is not indispensable. See Magen Avraham, siman 4, seif katan 6, and siman 158, seif 12. Nevertheless, whenever the blessing can be placed close to the act, it should be. Even a blessing of praise should ideally be recited immediately after seeing the event that prompts it. If one did not recite it immediately, however, one may still recite it later, as Taz writes in siman 227, seif katan 2, based on the Yerushalmi. Consequently, with blessings such as “Who gives the rooster understanding,” where immediate recitation is impossible, the Sages did not establish a precise measure of proximity, and they may be arranged in the synagogue in order to discharge others. The blessing over washing the hands, by contrast, can be recited immediately after washing, before drying, and should therefore be recited then. This is especially clear according to Rama in siman 46, who holds that the morning blessings are recited over the general order of the world. Therefore he followed the Sephardic custom. Beit Yosef explains that Ashkenazic communities follow Rashba, who regards the blessing over washing the hands as a blessing of praise as well. See Taz, seif katan 4, Magen Avraham, seif katan 1, and siman 6, seif katan 1, and understand.
