It is permitted to move a ladder, etc. I must briefly clarify the sugya with Rashi's explanation and explain it with God's help, for I saw explanations in it that were not clear to my humble opinion. In the mishnah: Beis Shammai say one may not take the ladder from dovecote to dovecote, and Beis Hillel permit; R' Chanan bar Ami said: the dispute is in a public domain, for Beis Shammai hold the onlooker will say he needs to plaster his roof and do melacha on Yom Tov, and Beis Hillel hold its dovecote proves concerning it; and there is another version in the Gemara, but the practical law is like this version; and in a private domain it is permitted according to both. And we say in a beraisa after the dispute of Beis Shammai and Beis Hillel: R' Yehuda said, when was this said? Regarding a dovecote ladder; but an attic ladder, all agree it is forbidden. Rashi explained: for regarding that one certainly says he needs to plaster his roof. R' Dosa says: he tilts it from window to window. And we say in the Gemara: R' Chiya forbade to move even a dovecote one; for what the first Tanna permitted, that is a dovecote one for carrying, R' Dosa forbade, and came to say carrying is forbidden and tilting is permitted; and regarding an attic one he did not speak at all. And this is the language of the Rosh: and we conclude that an attic ladder is forbidden to move; and it is difficult from that of chapter "Window," where we say that an Egyptian ladder is something movable on Shabbos; and one cannot say that that sugya is like the version that said in a private domain it is permitted, for even according to that version that is specifically a dovecote ladder; or perhaps since Beis Shammai forbade a dovecote ladder in a private domain, the same applies that an attic ladder is permitted according to everyone in a private domain (it appears simple that there is a scribal error here, and one must say: or perhaps since Beis Shammai permit a ladder, etc.). However it is difficult that R' Chiya himself who forbids here: it is implied there that he holds Babylonian ladders have no fixed place and are taken on Shabbos. And Rabbeinu Tam answered that there it deals with house ladders that it is the way to move from corner to corner, and not similar to attic ladders that were large and fit to plaster a roof. And R' Eliezer of Metz answered that here on Yom Tov it is permitted to take out a ladder to a public domain, and the onlooker says he needs to plaster his roof; and since it is forbidden in a public domain, even in innermost rooms it is forbidden; but on Shabbos there is no way to take it out through a public domain, therefore in the house it is permitted. And according to this answer it is forbidden to move a ladder on Yom Tov; end quote. And this is the language of the Tur: it is permitted to move the ladder from dovecote to dovecote even in a public domain; and when was this said? A small ladder such as of a dovecote; but a large one such as of an attic is forbidden to move even in the house; end quote. And Beis Yosef challenged: here it is implied that he rules like Rabbeinu Tam's answer, for he wrote "but a large one such as of an attic is forbidden," and that is like Rabbeinu Tam's explanation that attic ladders are forbidden because they are large; and it is difficult that in siman 308 it appears he holds like R' Eliezer of Metz's answer, for he wrote even an attic ladder, though large, it is permitted to move in a courtyard on Shabbos, and that is like R' Eliezer, for according to Rabbeinu Tam both on Shabbos and on Yom Tov it is forbidden; end quote. And upon this he elaborated in answers and to distinguish between house and attic and dovecote, and in the end he left it requiring further study. It appears that his memory of blessed memory explained Tosafos and the Rosh who wrote according to this answer that our ladders are forbidden to move: that is even though they are small; and this is the novelty that R' Eliezer was stringent; and so Maharshal and my master and father-in-law of blessed memory understood. And to my humble opinion this is not the way at all regarding the Rosh's intent; rather this is the explanation: at first the Rosh challenged that here it is implied an attic one is forbidden, and in chapter "Window" we say an Egyptian one is permitted and it too is large like an attic one; and he answered that there it deals with a private domain, and in this even Beis Shammai permit; and afterward he challenged Rabbeinu Tam who forbids like R' Dosa, that is even regarding a dovecote one he permits there Babylonian ladders, and that is even in a public domain; and Rabbeinu Tam answered that those ladders a person always keeps in the house, which it is the way always to move from corner to corner, have no prohibition at all even in a public domain, and of this chapter "Window" deals; but those ladders that are always on the street standing for roof repair, those of attics we forbid here; and the matter does not depend on small and large, for certainly even those kept in the house are larger than a dovecote one mentioned in the Gemara and fit to plaster a roof; rather the permit is because it is their way to take them from corner to corner, and the onlooker will not say he needs to plaster his roof; which is not so of those always outside; and small and large are only a mere distinguishing mark that those outside make them larger than those kept in the house; and if so those in the house even if very large may be moved in the house, for there is no suspicion since they have a distinction from attic ones and all see that such a ladder belongs to the house. But R' Eliezer did not distinguish in this but between Shabbos and Yom Tov regarding moving in the house: on Shabbos there is no concern even if it is large, since he cannot plaster his roof, for it is forbidden to take out on Shabbos from private to public domain; but on Yom Tov there is concern of taking it out to plaster, then there is a distinction between a small one like a dovecote, regarding which they did not decree because of plastering, and a large one that is certainly always for plastering, therefore forbidden even in the house lest they err and say he moves it in order to take it out. And it emerges that there is leniency and stringency according to R' Eliezer's explanation: leniency regarding a very large attic ladder, permitted [on Shabbos] in the house, which is not so according to Rabbeinu Tam who only permits one that belongs and is recognizable as of a house; and this the Tur mentioned in siman 308, for he holds like R' Eliezer's explanation and that is essential; and stringency regarding one that always belongs to the house: Rabbeinu Tam permits since it is its way for house use, and according to R' Eliezer it is forbidden since one can take it out on Yom Tov and they will say [he needs] to plaster, etc. And the Rosh only mentioned the stringency alone, and therefore wrote: and according to this answer it is forbidden to move our ladder on Yom Tov, that is our ladders that are always inside the house; for in Talmudic times their roofs were flat and they plastered clay there as Rashi explained in the Gemara, therefore they kept special ladders outside for plastering; which is not so of ours, for we have no outside ladders for this but keep the ladder inside the house, and in this it would be permitted according to Rabbeinu Tam since it is always its way in the house, but according to R' Eliezer forbidden even in this since it is permitted to take it out. And in Tosafos they also wrote this matter that the Rosh wrote, except that they concluded in this language after R' Eliezer's answer: and according to this it is forbidden to move our ladder on Yom Tov even in his house, for perhaps the law is like R' Dosa; and their intent is even if we have small ladders it is forbidden, for perhaps we hold like R' Dosa who forbids even a dovecote one, and that is because in any case they are also fit to plaster a roof, for for this reason Beis Shammai forbade in the Gemara regarding a dovecote one. It emerges there are two stringencies: (1) even in the house; (2) even small ones like a dovecote, for according to Rabbeinu Tam a large one of a house is permitted; but the Rosh did not copy the second stringency, for he did not rule like R' Dosa but like the first Tanna that Beis Hillel permit a dovecote one like R' Yehuda, and an attic one is specifically forbidden, and that is on Yom Tov; therefore the Rosh only mentioned the stringency according to R' Eliezer's explanation regarding even one designated for the house being forbidden, against Rabbeinu Tam, and that is regarding a large one; and this distinction the Tur also mentioned, and it is according to R' Eliezer's explanation. And the Tur need not mention the stringency of our ladders always in the house, for it is obvious one should not be lenient for that reason; and the Rosh only wrote this because he first wrote Rabbeinu Tam's explanation who is lenient for that reason, so he needed to mention that according to R' Eliezer it is forbidden in this; but the Tur did not mention Rabbeinu Tam's explanation at all, and therefore need not mention that we are not lenient because it belongs to the house, for whence would one say so? And Maharshal also distinguished three distinctions among ladders and emended Tosafos's words: and further, perhaps the law is like R' Dosa; and according to what I wrote everything is comfortable and there is no forced stretch, with Heaven's help. But the Shulchan Aruch's words here who hold that an attic one is forbidden because it has no status of a vessel, and also on Shabbos he does not hold like R' Eliezer's explanation, and that is the Rambam's view; except that the Rambam forbade even a dovecote one to carry from dovecote to dovecote, only tilting alone. And it appears that for the practical law: on Shabbos it is permitted to move the ladder in our houses to take out or carry some thing from the attic downward, for this is permitted both according to Rabbeinu Tam and according to R' Eliezer; but on Yom Tov one should forbid the carrying, for perhaps R' Eliezer's explanation is essential and there is a prohibition lest they say [he needs] to plaster, etc. And even though nowadays we need not plaster our roofs because they are sloping, nevertheless a decree of the Sages is not nullified, for nevertheless one can err and say he wishes to take it out for some need of repairing his roof; rather only tilting should be permitted, as appears to my humble opinion.
