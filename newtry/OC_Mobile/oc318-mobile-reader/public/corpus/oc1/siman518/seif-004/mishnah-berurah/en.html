{25} It is permitted to move. In order to take nestlings for the need of Yom Tov, and even if it was possible for him from yesterday.
{26} From dovecote to dovecote. And the same law applies if it stands in another place, that it is permitted to set it up for a dovecote.
{27} Even in a public domain. Meaning that the dovecotes were in a public domain, or that he carries the ladder through a public domain; and we are not concerned lest they say he is moving the ladder for the need of doing melacha, for ladders of dovecotes are recognizable and designated, and people know that he is bringing it for his dovecote. And it deals with when he prepared the birds from while it was still day; and regarding birds for which preparation helps, according to the manners explained above in siman 497, se'if 9.
{28} But an attic ladder is forbidden, etc. For the onlooker will say that to plaster his roof he is moving this ladder, as is always his way on a weekday; and even to move it only in a private domain is also forbidden, for wherever something is forbidden because of marit ayin, even in innermost rooms it is forbidden. And the poskim wrote that even without moving it to another dovecote, merely to tilt it from this nest to another nest in that same dovecote itself is also forbidden. And ladders that are designated for use inside the house, which they always move from this corner to the opposite corner: if they carry them about, there are opinions among the poskim. Some say it is permitted, for only an attic ladder, which is large and fit to go up on it to the roof to repair it, do we fear they will say he needs it to plaster his roof; which is not so of a ladder in the house, which are not fit to go up on them to the roof: its law is like a dovecote ladder. And there are those who are stringent about this, and their view is that they were lenient only regarding a dovecote ladder, which are recognizable by their form, and everyone knows that he brings them to a dovecote; but not regarding an ordinary ladder, for even when they are small, sometimes one leans them on projections sticking out from the walls and goes up to the roof. And for the practical law: in a private domain one may rely to be lenient to move a ladder that is in the house; and in a public domain it is proper l'chatchila to be stringent.
{29} By moving. But without moving it is permitted to go up on it to his attic or to his dovecote. And know that from the language of the Mechaber it appears at first glance that an attic ladder has the status of muktzeh; but the Vilna Gaon in his commentary inclines that it is not muktzeh, and it is not forbidden by ordinary moving and shifting it from its place, but rather by moving of carrying it for some use, or even tilting for that purpose.
