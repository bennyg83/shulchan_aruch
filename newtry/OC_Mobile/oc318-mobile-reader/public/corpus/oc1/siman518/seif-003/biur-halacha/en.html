He took wood that is not designated, etc. - But if it is designated for roasting, whether of wood or of metal, it has the status of a vessel, and it is permitted to move it in any manner he wishes [Beis Yosef; and so Maharshal wrote; see there]. And from Peri Megadim it is implied that only for the need of its body and its place is it permitted, like all vessels [that is, whose melacha is for a forbidden purpose]. And see in the novellae of the Rashba and in the novellae of the Ra'ah, who explain the sugya regarding a metal spit; see there what they elaborated on this. And we explained according to the Tur's interpretation, and as the Mechaber copied it; and this requires further study.
Rather he removes it - See Mishnah Berurah, and what we distinguished between our case and that of the thorn, and this is to resolve the Taz's difficulty [and his answer is a great stretch]. And similarly to this Chemdat Moshe wrote, and so is proven from the Meiri. However, there is still a difficulty from that of siman 308 above in se'if 6 in the gloss, and its source is the Kol Bo: that broken vessels in a place where they can cause damage, such as glass broken on the table or in the house in a place where people walk, it is permitted to move the shards, and there he did not require moving from the side. And it is possible that glass has more certain damage and they were not stringent to make a change (and one can align this with the Meiri's words that I brought in Sha'ar HaTziyun). And it is more correct to say that if we are stringent here like the poskim that even according to Ravina one must specifically remove by dragging, the same applies there as well. And it is possible that the Kol Bo wrote there the essence of the law, and incidentally in his flow he did not descend there to explain the details. And likewise I found there in Olas Shabbos who wrote: however, if he can take the glass through moving from the side, he should not move it directly; see there; and this requires further study.
Even if there is no meat on it at all - See Mishnah Berurah s.k. 22. And it is explained in Avodat HaKodesh of the Rashba that when there is meat on it, even less than a ke-zayis, it is permitted to remove it even in a place where there is no concern of damage; and his source is from that of Rav Malkiya who said "he removes it," and upon this R' Chiya bar Ashi said "and that is when there is meat on it"; therefore it is not for the reason of damage, for that is the statement of Ravina after this who said "even if there is none on it," etc., "just as one may move a thorn." And it implies that the earlier Amoraim did not tie it for the reason of a thorn; rather since there is a little meat on it, even though it is not an important measure, one should not forbid it. And he has the reading in the Gemara "and that is when there is meat on it," and so is the version in the Ittur and in Or Zarua and in Shibbolei HaLeket and in the novellae of the Ra'ah. And according to this the Rashba further adds there that when there was a ke-zayis of meat on it, one need not even make a change and may move it directly, even not in a place of damage, as we say regarding a hide in the mishnah in the first chapter of Beitzah: if there is a ke-zayis of meat on it he may move the hide on its account; and so the Ran wrote, and so the Ra'ah's view is implied; see in his novellae. And know that according to our version in the Gemara, which is also the version of the Rif and the Rosh, the Rashba's later ruling has no place. And I found in Eliyah Rabbah who noted that of the Rashba and remained with a difficulty requiring further study, since he did not see all these poskim whom we brought; and see in the novellae of the Rashba.
