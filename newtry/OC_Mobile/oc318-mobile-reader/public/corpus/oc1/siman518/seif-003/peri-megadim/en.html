Rather. See Taz; see Eliyah Rabbah: regarding a thorn there is greater concern of damage than regarding a spit. And what the Taz wrote, if he wishes to roast a second time [on it], it is kosher and permitted: that means to take afterward the wood and roast on it; but before that it is forbidden. And see Beis Yosef regarding a metal spit and the like that is a vessel: it is implied that even after roasting it is permitted to move it for the need of its place like all vessels; and Rabbeinu Yeruham forbids, since it is not truly a vessel but he merely performed an act like a thorn; see there. And see Yam Shel Shlomo, Perek HaOleh, siman 24; and in Tosafos Beitzah 28b s.v. "Ravina"; and in Rashi there regarding that which they roasted on Yom Tov: at first glance, if on Yom Tov with a vessel-spit, for Shmuel there is no muktzeh; which is not so on Yom Tov, where it is like a kind of nolad. And see Olas Shabbos; and in Tosafos one can explain it in two ways; and see Yam Shel Shlomo. And according to what I wrote, one can say that the Tur explains it as the plain sense, and Rabbeinu Yeruham explains that he performed an act like a thorn; see there. Pnei Yehoshua Tzahav at first glance depends on the dispute; however the Mechaber ruled in the Shulchan Aruch specifically regarding wood that is not designated, and on Shabbos too it is permitted to bore into detached [wood].
