But see Olat Shabbat — there is greater concern of damage from a thorn than from a spit; and what the Taz wrote: if he wishes to roast again in the evening it is permitted — meaning he may take the wood afterward and roast with it, but before that it is forbidden. And in Beit Yosef regarding a metal spit and similar which are utensils — it is implied that even after roasting it is permitted to move for the need of its place like all utensils; and Ri Migash forbids, since it is not truly a utensil but merely the act of a thorn he performed — see there. And see Olat Shabbat siman 24 and in Tosafot Beitza 28b s.v. Ravina and in Rashi there who roasted on Yom Tov — at first glance, if he wants on Yom Tov a utensil-spit, Shmuel has no muktzeh; unlike on Yom Tov where it is like a type of nolad — see Olat Shabbat. And in Tosafos there are two explanations — see there. And one can say: the Tur explains literally and Ri Migash explains he merely performed the act of a thorn — see there. Pnei Yehoshua Zahav at first glance depends on the dispute; however the Mechaber ruled in Shulchan Aruch specifically on wood that is not designated, and on Shabbat it is also permitted to gouge detached wood.
