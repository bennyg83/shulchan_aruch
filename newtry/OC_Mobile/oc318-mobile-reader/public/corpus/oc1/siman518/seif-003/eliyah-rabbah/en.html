[3] [Levush] That they permitted their end, etc. It is difficult: if his heart is not troubled lest it be stolen, then he would not refrain at the beginning; and if you say that his heart is troubled lest it be stolen, then why do we speak of permitting the end for the sake of the beginning? Even if [the items] had been there from many days past it would also be permitted, as he writes afterward regarding other vessels of which he is afraid, etc. And in truth in the Shulchan Aruch he did not write that it is permitted to return [them]; and the Mordechai who wrote thus does not hold [the same] regarding other vessels. And it appears to me that he holds as the Bach wrote: specifically regarding important vessels about which his heart is troubled if they are stolen is it permitted; but regarding unimportant ones, where his heart is not troubled when they are stolen, it is forbidden; therefore he wrote here the reason that they permitted their end, etc., for according to this even unimportant things are permitted; and study this carefully. And more apparently from what the Levush wrote, "and likewise a key or other vessels of which he is afraid," etc.: it deals with vessels needed that day that he need not take out, for when he comes home he will need them; nevertheless, since [if left] they would be stolen, it is permitted; but vessels not needed that day are forbidden even if he fears theft. And so here it deals [with machzorim] that he no longer needs for learning from them; therefore he needed the reason that they permitted their end, etc.
