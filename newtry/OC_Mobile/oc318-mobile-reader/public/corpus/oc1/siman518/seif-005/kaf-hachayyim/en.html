5) There: And a Torah scroll. The same applies to bringing books along to the synagogue and even returning them, for the Sages permitted the return so that people would bring them initially. The Rosh there, cited by Beis Yosef; Olas Shabbos, paragraph 1; Eliyah Rabbah, paragraph 2; Chemed Moshe there; Shulchan Aruch HaRav there; Mikra'ei Kodesh there. This means that if one fears they will be stolen, he may return them; if there is no concern of theft, returning them is forbidden. Even when theft is a concern, if they had already been left there for many days, returning them is forbidden. Yam Shel Shlomo, first chapter of Beitzah, siman 34; Olas Shabbos there; Magen Avraham, paragraph 2; Shulchan Aruch HaRav there; Chayei Adam, rule 96, paragraph 1; Mikra'ei Kodesh there. This applies when one does not intend to study from them again that day. Olas Shabbos there. If he wishes to recite songs and praises from machzorim on Yom Tov, however, he may always bring them. Chayei Adam and Mikra'ei Kodesh there. The same applies to other books: if he wishes to study them, he may carry them in every circumstance. This follows from the poskim, and Mishbetzos Zahav, paragraph 1, explicitly writes that books from which to study a fixed lesson are a need of that day.
