For we say: since carrying out was permitted for the need of ochel nefesh, it was also permitted without need; and that means that at least there is some need of the day in it, such as a child and a lulav, but not stones. And in the Yerushalmi we say it is permitted to move the key of foods, but not of vessels; and they challenged: yet R' Abahu sat and taught with a key of the palace in his hand; and they answered: he had peppers with him. And the Rosh wrote in the name of a responsum of the Geonim that for the honor of Yom Tov it is permitted if it is an ornament; but carrying out not for the need of eating and not for the need of an ornament, such as those who go out with keys tied in their belts, they act improperly. And so the Tur wrote, and this is his language: and likewise a key with which he locks the foods, if he is afraid to leave it in his house, he may take it with him; and R' Shimshon wrote: specifically to take it in his hand, but not with a strap in weekday fashion; and it does not appear [correct] to distinguish, for it does not so appear like a weekday act that it hangs on a strap; end quote. And from this it is implied that a key of vessels that have no need of ochel nefesh, except that he fears lest they be stolen, it is forbidden to take with him. And so it is in Maharil who took out with him a key from his cupola on Yom Tov, for he said that his fish that he brought in for Yom Tov are in the cupola there; and so Maharshal ruled. But Beis Yosef brings Hagahos Sefer Mitzvos Katan, siman 282: that a key of a chest that has only money in it, since his heart is troubled because of loss, it is considered some need; and this the Rama ruled as law. And I do not know why, since most Rishonim and Acharonim only ruled specifically for a key needed for ochel nefesh or an ornament. And in the Mordechai it is also written that machzorim it is permitted to take with him and to return them, for they permitted their end for the sake of their beginning, for otherwise he would not take them with him; implying that if they were already there before Yom Tov it is forbidden to take them out of the synagogue. And therefore it appears that even though the elder Rama already ruled to be lenient in this, every conscientious person should be stringent for himself where there is no eruv, not to take with him anything but what is needed somewhat for ochel nefesh on that very day; but not on account of fear of theft, especially where he can give the key to someone trustworthy in his house, for then it is forbidden according to everyone.
And it is permitted to play with a ball, etc. Maharshal wrote that it is astonishing to permit, for there is no need on Yom Tov at all except children's play; but for adults it appears in my eyes a bad custom; and even though he wrote thus in the name of Rabbeinu Tam, it is not comfortable to me, for it is possible he only came to resolve what they practiced thus; and if my strength were sufficient I would abolish it, for the Rosh did not copy this. Maharshal wrote: and regarding carrying knives, in my youthful days I saw that they were careful when going to stroll outside the city on Yom Tov not to carry the knives with them, for the reason that knives after eating have no need of the day, for it is not the way to eat further that day; but nevertheless outside the eruv they would carry, for the Sages only instituted eruv chatzeros on Shabbos when it is forbidden to carry out any thing; but on Yom Tov when it is permitted to carry out any thing except what has no need of the day at all, for this reason they did not institute eruv chatzeros; and so is the view of the Rif and Tosafos; and the Ran distinguished on Yom Tov that there is no eruv chatzeros there, except that the Rashba, etc.; and there is proof in the Gemara for his reasoning, etc. And it is fitting for every God-fearing person to place eruv chatzeros on Yom Tov as on Shabbos, except that one should not change the custom; but nevertheless according to what they practiced to place an eruv on Erev Pesach for the whole year, he should include in it also Yom Tov and say "for all the Shabbosos and Yom Tov of this year," to take himself out of the dispute in a matter that has no monetary loss, and it is not called a change since he is not performing a new act; end quote. In this that he wrote to add in the blessing it appears correct, for the Rama also wrote here that there is benefit in an eruv to carry out whatever has the status of a vessel, etc. But what Maharshal wrote to be stringent regarding knives does not appear correct to my humble opinion, for certainly knives throughout the day are called a need of ochel nefesh, for if some fruit presents itself to him he will eat with them; and this I also proved regarding Shabbos in siman 308, se'if 4, that it is forbidden to move even in the house something that has no need of the day at all; and if so how do they carry knives all day tied in the belt on Shabbos? Rather certainly it is also for need throughout the day. Also I do not know how to distinguish between outside the city or outside the eruv, for even outside the city there is only a rabbinic prohibition, for it is karmelit; rather Tosafos in the first chapter of Kesubos wrote that one may not learn from a courtyard that is not joined by an eruv a leniency for karmelit, for it is more stringent; Beis Yosef brought them. Therefore one should be more careful in karmelit regarding something that has no need of ochel nefesh; and all our alleyways are karmelit. The Rambam wrote in chapter 2: whatever is forbidden to eat or to make use of on Yom Tov because it is muktzeh is forbidden to move; until here. Meaning: but not that it is forbidden because of outside the techum, for it is permitted by moving, as written in siman 515.
