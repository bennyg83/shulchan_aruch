Since carrying out was permitted - See in Beis Yosef where he elaborated to bring the words of the poskim who disagree on this. The core of the matter with additional clarification is as follows: according to Tosafos and the Rosh, carrying out with no need at all, such as carrying stones and the like, is forbidden according to the Torah, and they only permitted it where there is some slight need. However, according to Rashi, carrying out was permitted through "mitoch," etc., even with no need at all, and carrying stones is only forbidden by rabbinic law. And so is the view of the Rif according to how the Ran inclines at the end of his words; and so wrote the Maggid Mishneh regarding the view of the Rif; and so appears the view of the Rambam; and so is the view of R' Yeshaya HaRishon, cited in Shibbolei HaLeket and in the words of the Riaz; and likewise the view of the Riaz himself. The Ran further inclines that according to Rashi, and likewise to the Rif, there is not even a rabbinic prohibition on carrying out without need when he is not carrying it for tomorrow's need, and the Sages only forbade stones and the like which are in any case muktzeh. And so is the view of the Maggid Mishneh in the Rambam's position [see there, where he wrote that the Rambam was silent and did not explain that stones are forbidden, because he relied on what he had already written that whatever is forbidden on Shabbos, etc.; see there; and it is proven from this that specifically stones and earth and the like are forbidden, whereas regarding vessels and the like, even on Shabbos it is permitted for some need; see siman 308, se'if 4]. And so wrote Maamar Mordechai regarding the Maggid Mishneh's view; see there. And see in Beis Yosef that from the words of other poskim this is not implied; rather, according to all positions it is at least forbidden by rabbinic law wherever there is no need at all. And so is also proven from Rashi's position according to what Tosafos brought in our sugya, s.v. "thus is Rashi's version"; see there. And so is also implied in Rashi on the reverse side, s.v. "kindling" (and it is forced to distinguish between carrying out and kindling). And so is clarified in the Riaz, the holder of this view, that at least by rabbinic law it is forbidden unless for the need of that day. And even his grandfather there who is lenient nevertheless also requires at least some need, such as the need of the vessel lest it be stolen and the like; see there. And see in Penei Yehoshua in the sugya where he elaborated to prove that according to everyone, with no need at all it is forbidden. Now, the Mechaber was silent following Rashi's position, since the Rif and the Rambam stand in his position. And from the silence of his language, writing "and vessels" without distinguishing between for need and without need, and excluding from the general rule only stones, it implies that he ruled to permit even l'chatchila without need, as the Ran inclines for the holders of this view. According to this one must say that the Rama who emends "for need" is his own view; and we find several times that the Rama inserts his words into the Mechaber's words and does not bring them in the name of "some say." And so also inclines the view of the Vilna Gaon in explaining the Mechaber's words. However, it is difficult according to this what the Mechaber brings in se'if 2, that it is forbidden to carry out for the need of a non-Jew; and this is specifically according to those who hold that at least by rabbinic law it is forbidden, for according to the one who permits even l'chatchila without need, the same would apply that for the need of a non-Jew it is permitted, as Beis Yosef himself wrote above in siman 512 in the name of the Maggid Mishneh; unless we say that the Mechaber himself holds that for a non-Jew it is worse, as we brought above in siman 512 in Biur Halacha. [In truth the Maggid Mishneh's view is difficult: what difference is there between one who carries out for the need of a non-Jew, or cooks and other melachos? For he himself wrote that we do not say "mitoch" only regarding carrying out, but the same applies to other melachos; and if so, if this law is not according to the Rambam, then the same applies to cooking as well; yet the Rambam copied as law that one may not cook, etc., and were it not for the possibility that guests might arrive he would be liable to lashes; therefore it must be that for a non-Jew it is worse, and this is a decree of Scripture, as wrote Korban Nesanel and Penei Yehoshua; see there.] And it is forced, for Beis Yosef brought no one who disputes the Maggid Mishneh's reasoning. Therefore it appears more that even according to the Mechaber it is at least forbidden by rabbinic law even regarding vessels when it is with no need at all. And so is implied from Darkei Moshe HaAroch that Beis Yosef's conclusion is to be stringent. And with this it fits better that he emended in the Shulchan Aruch and did not write in the language of "some say." And why the Mechaber excluded from the general rule only stones: because stones are presumably with no need at all, whereas ordinary vessels have some need, as Olas Shabbos wrote. And all this is according to the Mechaber's position who ruled like Rashi, the Rif, and the Rambam. However, in truth this ruling requires further study, for nearly all the poskim disagree with it, and according to them carrying out stones and the like, something with no need at all, is forbidden according to the Torah, and one is liable to lashes for it, as the view of Tosafos and the Rosh; for they are Rabbeinu Chananel and Rabbeinu Tam and the Riva [cited in Or Zarua, and it is implied that this is also his view; and the Rashba also brought this in the name of Rabbeinu Chananel, and so it is in Rabbeinu Chananel before us]; and likewise the view of the Ittur and the Semag and the Ra'az and R' Shimon [cited in Eshkol]; and the Ramban in Toras HaAdam; and the Ra'ah in his novellae; and the Rashba and Ritva and Meiri and Maharam Chalawa [see there in the novellae of the Ritva and Maharam in Pesachim, first chapter]. And moreover the Rosh explains even the Rif's words according to his own position; see there; and not according to the Ran's explanation. And also Peri Chadash hesitates regarding the Rambam's view and explains it like the other poskim; see siman 495 [and see in Nehar Shalom and Chemdat Moshe there and in Penei Yehoshua and Korban Nesanel who greatly strained to resolve the sugyos according to this position]. And it is possible that this is also the Rama's view, and so it appears somewhat that the Vilna Gaon explained his words thus. And if so there remains to us no one whose view is to be lenient except Rashi and R' Yeshaya and the Riaz, against all these poskim; and certainly one should be stringent and adopt the view of those who are stringent; and so Yam Shel Shlomo ruled.
A child - See Mishnah Berurah. And all the more so to take out an eight-day-old child in order to circumcise him: certainly it is permitted l'chatchila, for it is a mitzvah need belonging to that day, as explained in Tosafos. And after eight days, when it is a milah not in its time, it is forbidden to take him out, for it is forbidden to circumcise him on Yom Tov. And the Ra'ah in his novellae wrote that in this one is liable to lashes for taking him out, like stones, for there is neither a need of ochel nefesh nor a mitzvah need. [And a wondrous thing: I saw in Or Zarua who wrote that even after eight days it is permitted to take him out on Yom Tov to circumcise him, for it is a mitzvah need; yet there is an explicit mishnah in Shabbos 137 that a milah not in its time does not override Yom Tov. And the proof he brought from the Yerushalmi that says that even an adult it is permitted to take out: the intent is not after eight days in order to circumcise him, but to bring him to some place, as we write below in the name of the Meiri. And perhaps Or Zarua's intent is to take him out in order to circumcise him tomorrow morning, in a manner that tomorrow it will not be possible to do so; but this too is difficult, for how is it better than preparations for a mitzvah than the mitzvah of milah itself, which we learn there from a verse that it does not override Yom Tov, as stated in Shabbos 133? And this requires great further study.]
And a Sefer Torah - And the same applies to machzorim and other books to learn from them on this day (Eliyah Rabbah in the name of the poskim). And for the need of some other mitzvah matter that is not for the need of that day, it is implied from several poskim that one should be stringent about this [the Mordechai in the first chapter of Beitzah regarding burning of kodashim, even though it is a mitzvah; and Maharam Chalawa in the first chapter of Pesachim; and likewise the Ritva there in his novellae: that "mitoch" only permitted a mitzvah belonging to this day, but something he could have done beforehand, even though it is a mitzvah, is like a milah not in its time which does not override Yom Tov; see there; and so is proven from the Rashba, Shabbos 24, regarding burning of kodashim; see there; and so Magen Avraham wrote above in siman 446, s.k. 3, in the name of the Kesef Mishneh; see there]. And so Bach and Eliyah Rabbah ruled. And in the Meiri he inclines that in this manner there is no negative commandment, only a prohibition; and so is implied in the Me'or. [And what we permitted to take out books in order to learn is because Torah study is called something belonging to that day.] And know that all these poskim whom we brought to be stringent about this are those who hold that "mitoch" only permitted when he has some slight need, and this is not called a need according to them; but according to Rashi and those who agree with him, it is possible that this does not apply.
That are needed by him - And it is proven from Tosafos Kesubos folio 7 that even if it was only for a rabbinic mitzvah need it is also permitted; see there well.
Somewhat - Now in the mishnah it is stated that Beis Hillel permit a child to be taken out, and it is stated in the Yerushalmi that according to Beis Hillel even an adult it is permitted to take out; and that which we learned "a child" is to inform you of the strength of Beis Shammai's stringency, that even a child they forbid. And that is to say: even an eight-day-old child for whom there is a mitzvah in order to circumcise him is also forbidden according to Beis Shammai, since it is not a need of ochel nefesh; but according to Beis Hillel the same applies to an adult if he cannot walk and they need to bring him to some place. And that which we say in our tractate, specifically an adult whom many need, meaning to expound in public: because the need of bringing an adult is not common except in such a case [Meiri in his novellae]. And see above what I wrote in the name of Or Zarua regarding this.
And if he placed an eruv it is permitted to move, etc. - This comes to exclude the view of the Ritva cited in Shittah Mekubetzes, who holds that eruv chatzeros only helps for Shabbos, but for Yom Tov: things that have need are already permitted even without an eruv, and they did not institute at all for things that have no need, and even if he made an eruv it does not help.
