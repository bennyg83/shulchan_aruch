(א) "Since carrying was permitted" — see in the Beit Yosef where he elaborated to bring the words of the poskim who disagree on this matter. The core of the matter with additional clarification is as follows: according to Tosafos and the Rosh, carrying without any need whatsoever — such as carrying stones and similar items — is forbidden by Torah law (d'oraisa), and they only permitted it where there is some slight need. However, according to Rashi, carrying was entirely permitted through "mitoch" etc., even without any need at all, and carrying stones is only forbidden by rabbinic law (d'rabbanan). And so is the view of the Rif according to how the Ran inclines at the end of his words, and so wrote the Maggid Mishneh regarding the view of the Rif, and so appears to be the view of the Rambam as well, and so is the view of R' Yeshaya HaRishon, cited in the Shibbolei HaLeket and in the words of the Riaz, and likewise the view of the Riaz himself. The Ran further inclines that according to Rashi, and similarly per the Rif, there is not even a rabbinic prohibition on carrying without need when one is not carrying it for tomorrow's need, and the Sages only prohibited stones and similar items which are in any case muktzeh. And so is the view of the Maggid Mishneh regarding the Rambam's position [see there, where he wrote that the Rambam was unspecific and did not explain that stones are forbidden, because he relied on what he had already written that anything forbidden on Shabbos etc., see there; and it is proven from this that specifically stones and earth and similar items are forbidden, whereas regarding utensils and similar items, even on Shabbos it is permitted for any purpose — see siman 308, seif 4]. And so also wrote the Maamar Mordechai regarding the view of the Maggid Mishneh, see there. And see in the Beit Yosef that from the words of other poskim this is not implied; rather, according to all positions it is at least forbidden by rabbinic law where there is no need whatsoever. And so is also proven from the position of Rashi according to what Tosafos cited in the sugya, s.v. "thus is the version of Rashi," see there. And so is also implied in Rashi on the second side, s.v. "hava'arah" (and it is a forced distinction to differentiate between carrying and burning). And so is clarified in the Riaz — the proponent of this position — that it is at least forbidden by rabbinic law unless it is for the need of that day. And even the lenient one cited there nevertheless also requires at least some need, such as the need of the utensil lest it be stolen and similar situations, see there. And see in the Penei Yehoshua in the sugya where he elaborated to prove that according to all opinions, carrying without any need whatsoever is forbidden. Now, the Mechaber was unspecific following the position of Rashi, since the Rif and the Rambam stand with his position. And from the unspecified language he used — writing "and utensils" without distinguishing between for need and not for need, and excluding from the general rule only stones — it is implied that he ruled leniently even l'chatchila when there is no need, just as the Ran inclines for the proponents of this position. According to this, one must say that the Rama who emends "for need" is his own opinion, and we find several times that the Rama inserts his own words into the Mechaber's words without presenting it in the name of "some say." And so also inclines the view of the Vilna Gaon in his explanation of the Mechaber's words. However, this is difficult on account of the fact that the Mechaber brings in seif 2 that it is forbidden to carry for the need of a non-Jew — and this is specifically according to those who hold that it is at least forbidden by rabbinic law — for per the one who permits even l'chatchila without need, similarly for a non-Jew's need it would be permitted, as the Beit Yosef himself wrote above in siman 512 in the name of the Maggid Mishneh — unless we say that the Mechaber himself holds that for a non-Jew it is worse, as we brought above in siman 512 in the Biur Halacha. [Actually, the view of the Maggid Mishneh is difficult: what difference is there between carrying for the need of a non-Jew and cooking and other melachos? For he himself wrote that "mitoch" is not said only regarding carrying, but rather the same applies to other melachos. And if so, if this law does not apply per the Rambam, then the same would apply to cooking as well. Yet the Rambam did rule that one may not cook etc., and were it not for the possibility that guests might arrive, he would be liable to lashes. Therefore it must be that for a non-Jew it is worse, and this is a Scriptural ruling, as wrote the Korban Netanel and the Penei Yehoshua, see there.] And it is a forced interpretation, since the Beit Yosef cites no one who disputes the reasoning of the Maggid Mishneh. Therefore it appears more likely that even per the Mechaber, it is at least forbidden by rabbinic law even regarding utensils when it is without any need at all. And so is also implied from the Darkei Moshe HaAroch that the conclusion of the Beit Yosef is to be stringent. With this it also fits better that the Rama emended the Shulchan Aruch without writing in the language of "some say." And the reason the Mechaber excluded from the general rule only stones is because stones are presumably without any need at all, whereas ordinary utensils have some need, as the Olat Shabbat wrote. And all this is per the position of the Mechaber who ruled like the view of Rashi, the Rif, and the Rambam. However, in truth this ruling requires further study, since nearly all the poskim disagree with it, and in their view, carrying stones and similar items — something without any need whatsoever — is forbidden by Torah law (d'oraisa), and one is liable to lashes for it. Such is the view of Tosafos and the Rosh, and they include: R' Chananel and Rabbeinu Tam and the Riva [cited in the Or Zarua, and it is implied that this is his view as well; and the Rashba also cites this in the name of R' Chananel, and so it appears in R' Chananel as we have it] and likewise the view of the Ittour and the Smag and the Ra'az and R' Shimon [cited in the Eshkol] and the Ramban in Sefer Torat HaAdam, and the Ra'ah in his chiddushim, and the Rashba, and the Ritva, and the Meiri, and Maharam Chalawa [see there, in the chiddushim of the Ritva and of Maharam in Pesachim, first chapter]. And furthermore, the Rosh explains even the words of the Rif according to his own position, see there, contrary to the explanation of the Ran. And also the Peri Chadash expresses uncertainty regarding the Rambam's position and explains it per the view of the other poskim; see siman 495 [and see in Nehar Shalom and in Chemdat Moshe there and in the Penei Yehoshua and in the Korban Netanel who were greatly troubled to reconcile the sugyos according to this position]. And it is possible that this is also the view of the Rama, and so it appears somewhat from the Vilna Gaon's explanation of his words. And if so, there remains no one whose view is to be lenient except Rashi, R' Yeshaya, and the Riaz — against all these poskim. And certainly one should be stringent and adopt the view of those who are stringent. And so ruled in the Yam Shel Shlomo.
