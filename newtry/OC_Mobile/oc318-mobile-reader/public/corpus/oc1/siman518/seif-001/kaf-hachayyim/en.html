1) [Se'if 1] Since carrying was permitted for a need, etc. Kindling likewise, since it was permitted for food preparation, was also permitted for a non-food need, as stated above at the beginning of siman 511. The same applies to the other labors involved in food preparation, such as slaughtering, baking, and cooking. If one performs them for no need at all or for a weekday, some say that he nevertheless does not incur lashes. Others say that they were permitted for a non-food purpose only when there is some need for that day, such as carrying a child or lulav, or a mitzvah need. When there is neither a mitzvah need nor any ordinary need that day, as when carrying stones, he incurs lashes, as Maggid Mishneh writes in chapter 1, halachah 1. See also below, paragraph 12, and the beginning of siman 527.
2) There: Since carrying was permitted for a need, etc. This applies even when carrying through a public domain, as explained in the Mishnah, Beitzah 12a. The Rosh there, Yam Shel Shlomo, first chapter of Beitzah, siman 34, and the other later authorities write likewise.
3) There: Such as a child, etc. Rabbeinu Chananel explains: a child to be circumcised, a lulav with which to fulfill the mitzvah, and a Torah scroll from which to read. Tosafos there writes that the child need not specifically be carried for circumcision; carrying him for an outing is likewise permitted. The Rosh writes that if one needs to go to the synagogue or take a Yom Tov pleasure walk and cannot leave the child alone at home, he may carry the child with him. Tur and Levush write likewise. Ran, however, explains it as a child carried either for circumcision or for an outing, because that entails Yom Tov enjoyment. Yam Shel Shlomo there similarly writes that the "child" mentioned may be carried even merely for an outing. Magen Avraham, paragraph 1; Chemed Moshe, paragraph 1; Shulchan Aruch HaRav, paragraph 1; Chayei Adam, rule 96, paragraph 3; Mikra'ei Kodesh, siman 12, paragraph 1; Mishnah Berurah, paragraph 3.
4) There: Such as a child and a lulav, etc. This implies that carrying for another mitzvah that is not needed that day is forbidden. Bach; Knesses HaGedolah, Hagahos on the Tur, paragraph 1; Eliyah Rabbah, paragraph 1; Mishbetzos Zahav, paragraph 1.
5) There: And a Torah scroll. The same applies to bringing books along to the synagogue and even returning them, for the Sages permitted the return so that people would bring them initially. The Rosh there, cited by Beis Yosef; Olas Shabbos, paragraph 1; Eliyah Rabbah, paragraph 2; Chemed Moshe there; Shulchan Aruch HaRav there; Mikra'ei Kodesh there. This means that if one fears they will be stolen, he may return them; if there is no concern of theft, returning them is forbidden. Even when theft is a concern, if they had already been left there for many days, returning them is forbidden. Yam Shel Shlomo, first chapter of Beitzah, siman 34; Olas Shabbos there; Magen Avraham, paragraph 2; Shulchan Aruch HaRav there; Chayei Adam, rule 96, paragraph 1; Mikra'ei Kodesh there. This applies when one does not intend to study from them again that day. Olas Shabbos there. If he wishes to recite songs and praises from machzorim on Yom Tov, however, he may always bring them. Chayei Adam and Mikra'ei Kodesh there. The same applies to other books: if he wishes to study them, he may carry them in every circumstance. This follows from the poskim, and Mishbetzos Zahav, paragraph 1, explicitly writes that books from which to study a fixed lesson are a need of that day.
5) Any carrying permitted for a Yom Tov need because it parallels food preparation is likewise permitted for jewelry because of the honor of Yom Tov. The Rosh, first chapter of Beitzah, citing a responsum of the Geonim; Tur; Levush; Taz, paragraph 1; Olas Shabbos there; Chayei Adam there; Mishnah Berurah, paragraph 5. See above, siman 516, paragraph 21, and siman 303, paragraph 112.
6) There: And utensils. Gloss: That he needs somewhat. This implies that according to Shulchan Aruch it is permitted even if he has no need for them at all. Although Beis Yosef appears uncertain whether some need is required, Shulchan Aruch here rules without qualification to permit, in accordance with the implication of Rif and Rambam cited in Beis Yosef. Vilna Gaon likewise explains that Rif, Rambam, and Shulchan Aruch permit even when there is no need at all, following the plain Gemara: since carrying was permitted for a need, etc. Maamar Mordechai, paragraph 1, writes similarly, though he subsequently says that Shulchan Aruch remained uncertain, as in Beis Yosef, and therefore decided neither to prohibit nor permit but left his wording unqualified. Shulchan Gavoah, paragraph 2, writes that Rema's gloss explains Shulchan Aruch rather than disagrees with it. Yad Aharon, Hagahos Beis Yosef, writes likewise. Shulchan Aruch's words can thus be understood either way. Rema in his gloss definitively prohibits carrying unless there is some need, etc. Levush; Yam Shel Shlomo, first chapter of Beitzah, siman 34; Taz, paragraph 1; Chemed Moshe, paragraph 1; Shulchan Aruch HaRav, paragraph 1; Chayei Adam, rule 96, paragraph 1; Mikra'ei Kodesh, siman 12, paragraph 1; Ben Ish Chai, Parashas Bamidbar, paragraph 9; Mishnah Berurah, paragraph 5, rule likewise.
7) There: And utensils. On Yom Tov one may transport utensils within the techum from a city to a village for a mitzvah meal when the host did not prepare all the necessary utensils. Responsa Chasam Sofer, siman 147; Pischei Teshuvah.
8) There, gloss: That he needs somewhat. Regarding carrying knives, Yam Shel Shlomo writes: In my youth I saw people careful not to carry knives when going for a walk outside the city on Yom Tov, because after the meal the knives were no longer needed that day. Within the city but outside the eruv, however, they would carry them. Taz, paragraph 2, disagrees, writing that knives are certainly considered necessary for food throughout the day, because if one encounters a fruit he can eat it with them. Eliyah Rabbah, paragraph 5, agrees, as does Shulchan Aruch HaRav, paragraph 1. Magen Avraham, paragraph 2, however, cites Yam Shel Shlomo without disagreement, implying that he accepts his view. Chayei Adam, rule 96, paragraph 2, likewise rules so. Nevertheless, even according to Taz, if one knows with certainty that he will not need a knife on this outing (for example, when going to the synagogue or the like) it is forbidden to carry it. Shulchan Aruch HaRav there and Mishnah Berurah, paragraph 10, write likewise.
9) A timepiece, however, may be carried according to all opinions, because it is needed at all times. These laws apply to women as well as men. Ben Ish Chai, Parashas Bamidbar, paragraph 9.
