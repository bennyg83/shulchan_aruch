Lest [they be stolen]. For example, the key of his chest (Sefer Mitzvos Katan; Beis Yosef). And Yam Shel Shlomo forbids unless it is specifically a key needed for ochel nefesh or an ornament. And so the Taz agreed, and he wrote that every conscientious person should be stringent for himself where there is no eruv, not to take with him anything but what is needed somewhat for ochel nefesh on that very day; but not on account of fear of theft, especially where he can give the key to someone trustworthy in his house, for then it is forbidden according to everyone; end quote. And machzorim: it is forbidden to return them to his house unless he fears lest they be stolen, for then they permitted their end for the sake of their beginning; but if they had been lying there from many days past, it is forbidden to return them to his house on Yom Tov. And I saw that when people go out to stroll outside the city, they remove the knives that are no longer much needed that day, since they are no longer accustomed to eat; but in the city outside the eruv there is not so much concern, for the Sages instituted eruv chatzeros only for Shabbos, when it is forbidden to carry out any thing; but on Yom Tov, when it is permitted to carry out any thing except what has no need of the day at all, for this reason they did not institute eruv chatzeros (Maharshal; see there). And the Taz wrote about him that certainly knives throughout the day are called a need of ochel nefesh, for if some fruit presents itself to him he will eat with them; see there.
