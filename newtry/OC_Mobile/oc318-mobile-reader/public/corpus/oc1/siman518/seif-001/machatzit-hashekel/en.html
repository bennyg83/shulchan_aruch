Magen Avraham wrote. Beis Yosef wrote in siman 495, etc. Since Magen Avraham's words here are for people of my level difficult to understand, I must therefore elaborate and explain my discourse according to the weakness of my understanding. What Magen Avraham wrote in the name of Beis Yosef, siman 495: this is not Beis Yosef's language, and Magen Avraham added explanation to his words. This is the language of the Rambam, Hilchos Yom Tov 1:4: Any melacha for which one is liable on Shabbos, if he did it on Yom Tov not for the need of eating, he is liable to lashes, except for carrying out and kindling, for since they were permitted, etc.; and other melachos: whatever has a need of eating is permitted, such as slaughtering, baking, and kneading and the like; and whatever does not have a need of eating, such as writing and weaving and building and the like, is forbidden; end quote. And this is the language of the Maggid Mishneh: the intent from our teacher's view is that any melacha, etc., and that melacha is not for the need of eating: if he did it on Yom Tov he is liable to lashes, except carrying out and kindling, etc., even if he did them not for the need of eating. And the meaning of a melacha not for the need of eating is explained in our teacher's words, and it includes any melacha that is not done with eating and drinking. And if so, by law carrying out and kindling should have been forbidden like writing and weaving; rather, since they were permitted, etc. And it appears there is a reason for these two: carrying out, because it too is done with food and drink; and even though it is [also] with other things we say "mitoch," etc. And kindling, even though it is not with foods and drinks, Scripture wrote "You shall not kindle a fire in any of your dwellings on the day of Shabbos": on Shabbos it is forbidden, but on Yom Tov it is permitted. And so they expounded, because "on the day of Shabbos" is superfluous, for the verse above it already deals with Shabbos, etc. But other melachos that are not for the need of eating, such as writing and demolishing and weaving and building: even if he did them for eating he is liable to lashes. And other melachos that are with eating, such as slaughtering and baking: even if he did them not to eat them he is not liable to lashes, etc. And if he cooked for a non-Jew or for a weekday he is not liable to lashes, etc.; end quote of Maggid Mishneh. And so it is brought in Beis Yosef above at the beginning of siman 495; end quote of Maggid Mishneh. And I learned from their words and from the words of Magen Avraham and Peri Chadash at the beginning of siman 495 that there are three distinctions. (1) A melacha whose essence is for the need of eating and also prepares the food, such as baking and cooking: we say "mitoch," and even if it is not for the need of eating it is permitted. (2) A melacha whose essence is not for the need of eating: even if he did them for eating he is liable; that is, building, for one can find it with eating, such as one who makes cheese, of which we say in Shabbos folio 95 that it is a toladah of building; nevertheless, since the main melacha of building is not for the need of eating, even cheese-making which is for the need of eating, the Rambam holds he is liable, like the Sages there in Shabbos; see there. (3) We require that that melacha be a preparation for eating and drinking; therefore by law carrying out should have been forbidden, for it does not prepare the food through the carrying out; even so it is permitted and we say regarding it "mitoch," since at least it is done with the food itself, for he carries out the food itself; it has a law as if he prepares the food. And kindling, which is not done with the food itself: granted that cooking is with eating and prepares it; but kindling is only with wood, which are preparations for food, and preparations are permitted according to everyone, for they are preparations that cannot be done from the eve of Yom Tov, and even according to the Rabbis who dispute R' Yehuda they are permitted, as written above at the beginning of siman 495.
Nevertheless it is permitted, and we say regarding it "mitoch" from the superfluity of "on the day of Shabbos." And even though from the superfluity of "on the day of Shabbos" kindling was permitted on Yom Tov entirely, even if there is no slight need in it; and from the Maggid Mishneh's words who wrote that kindling is permitted because of mitoch; and yet regarding mitoch we hold that we require some slight need: there is no difficulty, for the Maggid Mishneh follows his view that he holds the Rambam holds like Rashi's position, that what we require regarding mitoch some slight need is only by rabbinic law, and according to the Torah it is permitted even if there is no need at all. And if so, kindling and slaughtering and baking and the like are equal: according to the Torah they are permitted even if there is no need at all; but by rabbinic law we require some slight need.
And see what I wrote in siman 501 in the name of the Ran, etc. Like ochel nefesh itself, for the Ran holds that a fire to warm oneself is ochel nefesh, because it is enjoyment of the whole body. If so, we find kindling that is done with ochel nefesh, for a fire to warm oneself: the wood are like the food. And according to this one might think that he necessarily disagrees with the Ran, for according to the Ran we do not need the superfluity of "on the day of Shabbos" to permit kindling on Yom Tov, for kindling is like other melachos done with the food itself. Rather, Magen Avraham in siman 501, s.k. 3, explained the Ran's words: that the Ran also holds that even a fire to warm oneself is not ochel nefesh, and was only permitted because of mitoch; see there. And if so one can say there is no dispute between the Ran and the Maggid Mishneh.
And it is astonishing, for in Pesachim, etc. And so it is stated there on folio 5a: that which Scripture wrote, "But on the first day you shall make chametz cease from your houses": necessarily it means from the eve of the first day of Yom Tov, meaning from its essence; for if you think [it means] on Yom Tov itself, behold it is written "You shall not do any melacha," and we find that kindling is an av melacha. Rava said: we learn from R' Akiva three things, etc.; and we learn that kindling went out to divide (for in this R' Yehuda and R' Nosson disagree). For the verse "You shall not kindle" is superfluous, since it is included in "You shall not do any melacha"; and R' Nosson holds that it went out to divide, so that you should not say one is only liable if he did all thirty-nine melachos (and in Pesachim Rashi explains this on folio 5a); therefore kindling went out from the general rule; and it did not go out for itself alone but for the general rule; and just as one is liable for kindling alone, so too all thirty-nine melachos: if he did one of them he is liable; but in any case it is an av like all the other thirty-eight melachos. And R' Yehuda holds that it went out as a negative commandment: that all thirty-eight melachos are avos and one is liable for them without warning to kares, and with warning to stoning; but kindling is only with a negative-commandment prohibition. And R' Akiva necessarily holds like R' Nosson: for if like R' Yehuda that it went out as a negative commandment, why does Scripture call kindling an av melacha? But Tosafos wrote in the name of the Riva: if it went out as a negative commandment, Scripture removed kindling from the general rule of "You shall not do any melacha"; if so, on Yom Tov where only "You shall not do any melacha" is written, kindling is not included and is permitted on Yom Tov; and we learn that we do not say "since it was permitted," etc.; thus far the substance of the Gemara. And upon this Magen Avraham wrote: we learn that even though the negative commandment "You shall not kindle" was not said regarding Yom Tov, etc.; and his words are puzzling, for Maggid Mishneh only wrote his words that from the word "on the day of Shabbos" we exclude kindling on Yom Tov according to Beis Hillel who say mitoch; but Beis Shammai who do not say mitoch also regarding kindling, as stated in the Gemara, therefore did not expound "on the day of Shabbos" to exclude Yom Tov and establish it for another exposition. And if so, what difficulty is there for Magen Avraham from R' Akiva? For R' Akiva also holds like Beis Shammai, who do not say mitoch, and he too did not expound "on the day of Shabbos" to exclude Yom Tov; and if so kindly kindling is included in "You shall not do any melacha." And perhaps one can say that it appeared to Magen Avraham forced to say: that by external reasoning one would have said that in "You shall not do any melacha" on Yom Tov kindling is also included; and according to Beis Hillel the verse "on the day of Shabbos" comes to take kindling out of "You shall not do any melacha" written regarding Yom Tov; and Beis Shammai who did not expound "on the day of Shabbos" leave kindling included in "You shall not do any melacha" on Yom Tov. This is not the way of Scripture: to write a superfluous verse regarding Shabbos to explain a verse written regarding Yom Tov; and how can we say that it was written in the Shabbos portion "on the day of Shabbos" to explain the verse "You shall not do any melacha" written regarding Yom Tov, that kindling is not included? Rather Maggid Mishneh necessarily holds that by external reasoning kindling is not included in "You shall not do any melacha" on Yom Tov. For on Shabbos the Torah took kindling out of "You shall not do any melacha" written for Shabbos, for whatever reason it may be; if so regarding Yom Tov where "You shall not do any melacha" is written and it did not detail kindling, by reasoning kindling should be permitted on Yom Tov; rather, since the Torah forbade kindling on Shabbos and we do not find explicitly that the Torah permitted more on Yom Tov than on Shabbos except melacha of ochel nefesh, if so kindling too is forbidden on Yom Tov from the verse written regarding Shabbos "You shall not kindle." And therefore it works well for Beis Hillel who expounded "on the day of Shabbos" to exclude Yom Tov: that this verse "You shall not kindle" does not apply to Yom Tov. And if so for Beis Shammai, granted that they did not expound "on the day of Shabbos" to exclude Yom Tov and kindling is forbidden on Yom Tov: its prohibition is not because "You shall not do any melacha" is written regarding Yom Tov, for kindling is not included; rather from what is written on Shabbos "You shall not kindle," Beis Shammai hold that the same applies to Yom Tov as above, since they did not expound "on the day of Shabbos" to exclude Yom Tov. If so R' Akiva's words are difficult who said "and behold it is written 'You shall not do any melacha'": granted that he holds like Beis Shammai, nevertheless kindling is not forbidden on Yom Tov from the verse "You shall not do any melacha" but from the verse "You shall not kindle" written on Shabbos; and he should have said "and behold it is written 'You shall not kindle.'"
And one cannot say that this is specifically, etc. Meaning: perhaps Beis Hillel and Beis Shammai and R' Akiva disagree here: that according to Beis Hillel kindling is not included in "You shall not do any melacha," and it works well that "on the day of Shabbos" excludes Yom Tov as above; but Beis Shammai and R' Akiva hold that kindling is included in "You shall not do any melacha," and there is no room to exclude from "on the day of Shabbos" as above. And if you object, on what reasoning do they disagree? One can say that their dispute is whether kindling went out to divide or went out as a negative commandment: Beis Shammai and R' Akiva hold it went out to divide, and if so it is included in "You shall not do any melacha"; but Beis Hillel hold it went out as a negative commandment and is not included in "You shall not do any melacha," as Tosafos wrote above: that all other thirty-eight melachos forbidden from the verse "You shall not do any melacha" are avos and one is liable for them to kares and stoning; and kindling its prohibition is only a negative commandment. And if so R' Akiva according to his view that it went out to divide: it works well that he said "and behold it is written 'You shall not do any melacha,'" for according to him kindling is included in "You shall not do any melacha." And this can be used to reject; if so the Rambam's words are difficult, for Maggid Mishneh wrote his words to explain the Rambam's words; and if so according to Maggid Mishneh the Rambam holds it went out as a negative commandment, therefore we say mitoch regarding kindling; and this is not so, for the Rambam in Hilchos Shabbos counts kindling among the other avos, and therefore one must say it went out to divide; rather Magen Avraham wished to reject from it; and regarding this from the Gemara's words with which he is presently occupied.
For this depends on that, etc. Meaning: since he holds that it went out to divide, the same applies that it is included in "You shall not do any melacha," and there is no room to exclude from "on the day of Shabbos" as above; therefore we do not say mitoch regarding kindling. And likewise the reverse: if he holds that we do not say mitoch, necessarily one must say it went out to divide, for otherwise it was excluded from "on the day of Shabbos."
And it is stated in the Gemara (Beitzah folio 12) that regarding cooking as well we say mitoch. And the same applies to baking and slaughtering and the like of things done to the food itself to prepare it, as above.
And nevertheless one who cooks from Yom Tov for a weekday is liable to lashes. Meaning since there is no need of the day at all in it. And this is Tosafos's position who hold that according to the Torah we require a need of the day. But according to Rashi who holds that according to the Torah even if there is no need of the day at all, nevertheless for us who say mitoch it is permitted: if so, one who bakes for a weekday, granted he does a rabbinic prohibition, for by rabbinic law we require some need of the day, but there are no lashes. And that which we say in Pesachim folio 47b, one who bakes from Yom Tov for a weekday is liable to lashes: that is because they do not say mitoch; they hold that Beis Shammai and Beis Hillel do not disagree about mitoch but about something else, as Peri Chadash wrote at the beginning of siman 495; see there. But according to the Rambam who says [mitoch] like Rashi's position, as Maggid Mishneh wrote above: if so one who bakes from Yom Tov for a weekday is not liable to lashes, as Maggid Mishneh himself wrote at the beginning of Hilchos Yom Tov. And the Rambam himself ruled that one who bakes from Yom Tov for a weekday is liable to lashes: Peri Chadash already raised this at the beginning of siman 495.
See what I wrote at the beginning of siman 527 on what was written that he is liable to lashes when baking from Yom Tov for a weekday because there is no need of the day at all in it. Nevertheless he should be exempt from lashes because of "since" (ho'il), for we hold like Rabbah there in Pesachim that one is not liable to lashes when baking from Yom Tov for a weekday because of "since if guests happen upon him it is fit for him." And at the beginning of siman 527 Magen Avraham wrote that one can find a case where "since" does not apply, such as when he baked or cooked on Yom Tov near nightfall, when it is impossible that the melacha finish today and guests arrive while it is still Yom Tov.
