(א) (seif katan 8) "A dangerously ill animal, and per the one who permits, etc." — In tractate Beitza, daf 27b, we learned in the Mishnah: an incident where they asked R' Shimon regarding an animal that died on Yom Tov and regarding the challah that became impure; and he asked in the study hall, and they answered him: they should not be moved from their place. And the Talmud derives: the animal is analogous to the challah — just as the challah has sanctity, so too the animal has sanctity, and therefore it is forbidden to move it, for it is not fit even for dogs, since consecrated items are not redeemed to feed to dogs. But a non-consecrated animal is permitted, since it is fit for dogs, even though it became a neveilah on Yom Tov — all of this follows R' Shimon, who does not hold by muktzeh. And [the Talmud] asks: this works well according to Mar the son of R' Yosef in the name of Rava, who said that R' Shimon disagrees with R' Yehuda and permits even a healthy animal that died — if so, the Mishnah accords with R' Shimon. But according to Mar bar Ashi in the name of Rava, who said that R' Shimon concedes that a healthy animal that was well before Yom Tov and died on Yom Tov is forbidden — if so, the Mishnah follows no one's view. And [the Talmud] answers: [the Mishnah speaks] of a dangerously ill animal, and [this is the view of] all. This is the version of the Rif. And the explanation is: there are three distinctions. A dangerously ill animal before Yom Tov that died on Yom Tov — even R' Yehuda concedes that it is permitted. A healthy animal that died — R' Shimon concedes that it is forbidden. And an animal that was ill without life-threatening danger before Yom Tov — in this R' Yehuda and R' Shimon disagree. Therefore [the Talmud] establishes the Mishnah as speaking of a dangerously ill animal, and it follows the view of all, for even R' Yehuda concedes that it is permitted.
