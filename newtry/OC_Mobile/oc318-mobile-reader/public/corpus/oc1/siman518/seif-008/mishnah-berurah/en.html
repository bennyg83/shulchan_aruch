{38} One may take, etc. This se'if deals with an ordinary sukkah made for shade; but the same applies to the festival sukkah, as explained below in siman 638, se'if 1 in the gloss.
{39} Those adjacent, etc. Meaning that the wood of the sukkah itself or its schach is certainly forbidden to take for the need of his pot, for the reason of demolishing a structure; and only reeds that he leaned around the walls: since they were not woven with the wall, they are not nullified to it, and there is also no muktzeh prohibition regarding them, for his mind was on them from yesterday if he should need them, for he does not demolish his tent by this. And it deals with when they are not fixed and attached to the ground. And it is explained in the poskim that all this is when he did not lean the reeds in order to thicken the walls of the sukkah so that they be strong; but when he leaned them expressly to thicken, even though they were not interwoven with the wall and stand by themselves, even so they are nullified to the wall and it is forbidden to take from them; and even if he set them up after the wall already stood, they are also nullified on account of the wall.
{40} If he threw bundles. That is, reeds bound together.
{41} For they are not nullified. For since he did not untie their binding at the time he placed them on the schach, he did not nullify them to the schach, but rather intended to store them; but if he placed unbound reeds on the schach, even though he had already covered all that was needed, the reeds are nullified on account of the main schach. And it is not similar to wood adjacent to the walls of the sukkah, which are not nullified since they are not interwoven with the walls; which is not so regarding schach, which are not interwoven at all; and therefore when additional reeds join them they mix and become one schach. And it is explained in the poskim that even regarding bound reeds, if he intended expressly to thicken the schach with them, they are nullified to the schach even though he did not untie their binding, and it is forbidden to take from them because of demolishing; and see Biur Halacha.
{42} If his intent is to kindle them. Meaning if he now wishes to kindle them; but we do not require that his mind was on them from yesterday, for they are not muktzeh, as we wrote above regarding wood adjacent to the walls. And know that all wood that it is forbidden to take from the sukkah, whether wood of the schach and the walls themselves, or those that were added to them and nullified with them: even if the sukkah fell on Yom Tov, it is also forbidden to move them and take them; and likewise to burn them in their place, because of muktzeh. And even if the sukkah was rickety from the eve of Yom Tov and standing to fall: nevertheless at the entrance of Yom Tov it was impossible for him to take them because of demolishing a structure, and since they were forbidden then they were forbidden for the whole day. And whether a stipulation helps: see Biur Halacha.
