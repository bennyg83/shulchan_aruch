Those adjacent to the walls of the sukkah. And specifically when he leaned them after the wall was finished with its schach, even though he leaned them to thicken it; but if he leaned them at the beginning of its making in order to thicken it or strengthen it, it is forbidden to take them (Or Zarua; Hagahos Asheri; Yam Shel Shlomo; Beis Yosef).
Bundles. But wood lying on the schach is nullified to the schach, and it is forbidden to take them, for it is like demolishing.
His intent is to kindle them. Meaning he wishes to kindle them now; but we do not require that his mind was on them from yesterday, for they are not muktzeh; see siman 638. A rickety sukkah that fell: according to R' Shimon it is permitted to kindle with its wood, and according to R' Yehuda it is forbidden; and if he stipulated to take from it on Yom Tov when it falls, even a sound [sukkah] is permitted even according to R' Yehuda (Yam Shel Shlomo). And Beis Yosef ruled that according to R' Yehuda a stipulation does not help. And so it appears to me: for it is well regarding R' Shimon, who permits a rickety one since he sits and hopes that it will fall; if so, if he stipulated explicitly, even a sound one is permitted; but according to R' Yehuda, who forbids a rickety one, for he holds that whatever was designated away from Bein HaShemashos is designated away for the whole day, a stipulation does not help even on a rickety one. And therefore Beis Yosef was silent here and did not write the laws of a stipulation, for he ruled in siman 495 like R' Yehuda. And this requires further study: for regarding muktzeh on account of a prohibition, even on Shabbos we hold like R' Yehuda, and even so he ruled in siman 279, se'if 4, that a stipulation helps for a lamp that went out; and it is possible that he holds that it is more made to go out than a rickety sukkah. But the Me'or wrote, and this is his language: a rickety sukkah, a stipulation helps regarding it even according to R' Yehuda, and it is not muktzeh on account of a prohibition, for even if he demolishes it before it falls there is no issue of demolishing a tent, since it is rickety; rather l'chatchila it is forbidden because it appears like demolishing a tent; and regarding a sound one a stipulation does not help even according to R' Shimon; and if it was rickety and fell and he did not stipulate, in this they disagree, and we hold like R' Yehuda even on Shabbos regarding muktzeh on account of a prohibition of this sort, like the leftover oil in the lamp; end quote. But the view of the Ran and Tosafos in chapter "Kirah" is that a stipulation helps for muktzeh on account of a prohibition, as written in siman 279, se'if 4. And nevertheless it requires further study: for what practical purpose did the Tur write that a rickety sukkah that fell is permitted according to R' Shimon, and so Yam Shel Shlomo wrote, for even on Shabbos we do not hold like R' Shimon regarding muktzeh on account of a prohibition, all the more so on Yom Tov, as the Me'or wrote? And one cannot say that he holds that since there is no Torah prohibition in its demolition it is not considered muktzeh on account of a prohibition, for regarding coins on the pillow we also say that since they were designated away from Bein HaShemashos they are designated away for the whole day, even though there is only a rabbinic prohibition. Therefore it appears to me that even according to one who permits muktzeh, nevertheless he concedes in this that it is forbidden; see what I wrote in se'if 9.
