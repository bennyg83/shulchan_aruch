One may take wood, etc. In the mishnah we say: one may not take wood from the sukkah except from what is adjacent to it (Rashi explained: for example on Pesach or Shavuos when one sits in it for shade; rather from what is adjacent now; implying that if the schach is thick it is permitted to take from them). And this demolishes a tent (meaning: since he nullified it to the schach, any little bit he takes demolishes a tent). And Shmuel answered: what is "adjacent"? Adjacent to the walls (reeds stood up adjacent to the wall and not woven with them are not nullified, and it is not similar to schach that is not woven; therefore all are equal). Rav Menashe said: you may even say when it is not adjacent to the walls; Rashi explained: rather adjacent to the schach; for it was taught regarding Issurayasa, bundles of reeds: since he did not untie their binding and placed them on the schach, he did not nullify them but [intended] to store them there. And Hagahos Asheri wrote: and he did not nullify them to the wall; and when he did not intend to make the wall thick; for if he intended to thicken it, it is forbidden; and so we say in the Yerushalmi; end quote. And this is the language of the Tur: one who makes a sukkah on Shavuos for shade may not take wood from it on Yom Tov (it appears the explanation is: certainly even if he makes it before Shavuos even for the festival of Sukkos its law is thus; rather if he wrote this law regarding one made for the festival, one would say the prohibition is because of the festival sanctity that rests on the sukkah; it teaches that even a shade sukkah is forbidden; and Beis Yosef strained over this for nothing); even if it was rickety and fell; and R' Shimon permits a rickety one that fell (for if it was not rickety before Yom Tov even R' Shimon concedes it is forbidden, for he certainly removed his mind from it; rather regarding a rickety one, for he puts in mind that it will fall: according to R' Shimon there is no muktzeh, for we say his mind was on it from yesterday; and according to the first Tanna we do not say his mind [applies] to anything not fit from Bein HaShemashos); and if after he finished it with its walls and its schach he leaned additional bundles to it and did not attach them there, or threw still more on the schach, it is permitted to take according to everyone; end quote of the Tur. And Maharshal wrote: that is why the Tur wrote "after he finished," etc.: that then it is permitted even if he intended to thicken; for Hagahos Asheri only forbade intending to thicken before finishing in the process of making it. And this does not appear correct to my humble opinion: for once he intends to thicken, what does the finishing that already was help, since he intends to thicken the wall and it will remain there, of itself it is nullified to the wall, and certainly forbidden to take from there; rather on the contrary we must say the Tur's intent is specifically when he does not intend to thicken at all, that is when everything needed was already finished and there is no need to thicken, then specifically it is permitted; and therefore he said "after he finished it with its walls," meaning it needs no repair for schach or wall; this appears to me simple and clear. Beis Yosef further challenged the Tur regarding what he wrote "he leaned additional bundles to it," etc., implying that also regarding walls there is no permit by leaning except with bundles; and this is not so, for it is explained in Rashi that by the wall even if they are not bundles it is permitted with reeds, that is stood up adjacent to them. And my master and father-in-law of blessed memory wrote that according to Rav Menashe who said regarding Issurayasa he holds that also regarding walls specifically bundles are required, and the Tur ruled like him; and the Gemara's language does not imply thus, for Rav Menashe said "you may even say when not adjacent to the walls," etc., implying that regarding the schach he said we can find a permit through bundles, but regarding walls bundles are not needed, but as Shmuel; and so is explained by the language "you may even say," meaning one need not force and explain "adjacent" as specifically to the walls; but in the law he does not disagree with Shmuel, for otherwise he should have said the mishnah whether walls or schach [requires] bundles; and both are law, as Beis Yosef wrote in the name of the Rif and the Rosh. And Maharshal also wrote that the Tur holds Rav Menashe disagrees with Shmuel, for he did not write a permit above except that he threw after making the sukkah, but did not write that if he placed tied bundles it is kosher in every manner; rather he was not concerned about it because he ruled like Shmuel who only permits adjacent to the walls, as he wrote "he leaned after making the sukkah," and the same applies above; but I do not know why we should not say they do not disagree and both are true; end quote. And his words are very puzzling in his understanding of the Tur as permitting to throw there above without a bundle since he already made the schach; and this is certainly forbidden according to everyone, for otherwise one would explain the mishnah as permitting adjacent to the schach because the schach was already finished; rather certainly regarding schach it is forbidden in every manner except with tied bundles; and the Tur who wrote "or that he threw still more on the schach" refers to bundles mentioned before this. And the explanation of his words: certainly Shmuel does not disagree with Rav Menashe, and the Tur rules like both: that is, regarding schach there is a side of elevation to permit if he places there a tied bundle, since he did not untie it, for anyone who wishes to cover scatters the bundle; rather certainly he intends to take them later from there; and this is Rav Menashe's answer. But regarding walls there is more of a permit without bundles, for one who wishes to strengthen the wall places there a tied bundle so that it strengthen the wall by its weight; and if so the Tur well mentioned bundles, that is tied ones, in the opening clause regarding walls for a novelty: that even with bundles that strengthen the wall it is permitted since they are not attached to them; and all the more so when they are not bundles it is permitted regarding walls since they are no strengthening to the wall at all. And in the concluding clause regarding walls (schach) it is specifically with bundles that it is permitted, since he did not untie them as is people's way; but when they are not bundles it is forbidden above. And all this is after he finished it, when his intent is not for benefit by adding: that is when everything belonging to schach and walls was already finished, then there are these permits; but if he intended an addition that it remain there, this is forbidden both regarding walls even without a bundle and regarding schach even with a bundle; all this appears clear in the Tur's explanation with Heaven's help. And Beis Yosef wrote: and it appears to me where he leaned to the wall or on the schach tied bundles, since they are not nullified to them it is permitted to take from there; the same applies that on the festival Sukkos it is permitted to take, for the name of the festival did not rest upon them; however it is possible that by rabbinic law they are still forbidden because of muktzeh, as they forbade what is beyond the measure needed for a sukkah for this reason, as written in siman 638; end quote. And to my humble opinion one should distinguish: adjacent to the wall, where we have no proof to say he intended not to add, one should forbid on the festival Sukkos; but regarding schach with a tied bundle where there is proof he did not intend to add, and if so he should have permitted this, one should permit even regarding a mitzvah sukkah, as appears to my humble opinion.
