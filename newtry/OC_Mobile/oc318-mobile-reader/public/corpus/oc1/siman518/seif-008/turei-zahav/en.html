One may take wood from the sukkah, etc. In the Mishnah we learn: one may not take wood from the sukkah except from what is adjacent to it (Rashi: e.g. on Pesach or Shavuot when one sits in it for shade — from what is adjacent now; implying if the skhakh is thick one may take from it). This contradicts the sukkah rule (since nullified to skhakh, any taking undermines the ohel). Shmuel answers: what is "adjacent"? adjacent to the walls — reeds standing by the wall not woven with them are not nullified and unlike skhakh that is not woven, therefore all are equal. Rav Menashe said: even say when not adjacent to walls — Rashi: only adjacent to skhakh, for it is taught in Issurayasa: bundles of reeds, since he did not untie them and placed them on the skhakh, they are not nullified but stored there; Hagahot Ashri wrote they are not nullified to the wall; and if he did not intend to thicken the wall — if he intended thickening it is forbidden; so in Yerushalmi — end. The Tur: one who makes a sukkah on Shavuot for shade may not take wood from it on Yom Tov (appears even if made before Shavuot even for Sukkot the law is the same, unless this law is stated for the festival purpose, then the prohibition is due to festival sanctity on the sukkah — teaching even a shade sukkah is forbidden; the Beit Yosef strained this needlessly). Even if it was rickety and fell — R' Shimon permits rickety and fallen (if it was not rickety before Yom Tov even R' Shimon concedes forbidden, for he certainly removed his mind from it; only rickety where he expects it to fall — for R' Shimon there is no muktzeh, we say his mind was on it from yesterday; the Tanna Kamma does not say mind applies to what is not fit for Beit Hillel). If after he finished the walls and skhakh he leaned more bundles not tied there, or threw more on the skhakh — permitted for all — end Tur. The Maharshal wrote: why did the Tur write after he finished, etc., then permitted even if he intended to thicken — for Hagahot Ashri forbade intending to thicken only before finishing construction, not in my opinion: once he intends to thicken, what does finishing help — he intended to thicken the wall and it remains nullified to the wall, certainly forbidden to take from there; rather we say the Tur's intent: specifically when he does not intend to thicken at all — all need is finished and no thickening needed — then permitted; therefore "after he finished the walls" means no further repair to skhakh or wall needed — so it appears clear. The Beit Yosef further challenged the Tur's "leaned more bundles" implying only bundles permit leaning in walls — not in Rashi, for by the wall even non-bundles are permitted with reeds standing adjacent; the Magen Avraham wrote Rav Menashe requires bundles in walls and the Tur ruled accordingly — the Gemara language does not imply this, for Rav Menashe said "even say when not adjacent to walls" implies on skhakh one can find permission via bundles, but walls need no bundles per Shmuel — as "even say" means no need to force "adjacent" to mean walls only. The Maharshal wrote the Tur holds Rav Menashe disputes Shmuel, therefore only wrote permission for throwing after building, not that tied bundles placed anytime are permitted — not concerned because he ruled like Shmuel only adjacent to walls as "leaned after building" and likewise above; but I do not know why not say they do not dispute and both are correct — end; their words are very puzzling in understanding the Tur as throwing above without bundles after skhakh is done — certainly forbidden for all, else the Mishnah should explain "adjacent to skhakh" because skhakh was finished — certainly on skhakh forbidden in all cases except tied bundles; Tosafos "or threw more on skhakh" refers to bundles mentioned before; clearly Shmuel and Rav Menashe do not dispute; Tur rules both: on skhakh there is room to permit tied bundles since he did not untie them — everyone scatters bundles when skhaching but intends to take them later — Rav Menashe's explanation; walls have more leniency without bundles, for one strengthening a wall places a tied bundle as weight — therefore Tur mentioned bundles first by walls to include even bundles strengthening the wall, permitted since not attached; certainly non-bundles permitted by walls since not strengthening; in the ending regarding walls (skhakh) only bundles are permitted since he did not untie them like people do; non-bundles forbidden above; all after finishing when he does not intend benefit from adding — skhakh and walls complete then these are permitted; if he intends an addition to remain, forbidden in walls even without bundles and on skhakh even with bundles — clear in Tur's explanation. The Beit Yosef wrote: where one leans tied bundles on wall or skhakh, since not nullified, permitted to take — likewise on the festival sukkah; however possibly rabbinically forbidden due to muktzeh as they forbade placing for sukkah preparation, siman 638 — end; in my humble opinion distinguish: by the wall, without proof he did not intend to add, forbid on festival sukkah; on skhakh with tied bundle proving no intent to add, one could permit even festival sukkah — so it appears to me.
