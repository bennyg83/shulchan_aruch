Se'if 8: Those adjacent, etc. The Gemara there. And the Rambam and Shulchan Aruch omitted the case of one who stipulated, which they said there. And the Ran establishes it even regarding a sound [sukkah], even according to R' Yehuda. And in Tosafos in chapter "Kirah" of Shabbos, folio 45a, s.v. "and if," they explain it as rickety; and so he wrote there on folio 44a, s.v. "that is in the lamp." But the Rambam explains that it is according to R' Shimon, and regarding a sound [sukkah], that without a stipulation it is forbidden; but according to R' Yehuda, a stipulation does not help (Beis Yosef). And Tosafos in Beitzah there, s.v. "he said," etc., wrote that we do not hold so in the conclusion, but rather regarding a mitzvah sukkah; and they wrote that on account of a prohibition a stipulation does not help; and all the more so according to the version of the books that do not have in the Gemara in the concluding clause "we come to a sukkah," etc. And the Ran wrote that this is the approach of the Rif, and this is the view of the Rambam, who likewise omitted here a stipulation that helps, and he likewise omitted the distinction between sukkah wood and its decorations. But according to our version, per Rashi's explanation, a stipulation does not help for sukkah wood; and so he wrote in siman 638, se'if 1 in the gloss. And see above, siman 279, se'if 4. And Tosafos in Shabbos there distinguished between a sukkah and a lamp; see there. And the Ramban cited a Tosefta in Beitzah there: one may not take wood from the sukkah, and even on the last day of Yom Tov of the festival; if he said, "When I wish I will take," this is permitted. And it appears that it refers to the festival sukkah, unlike Rashi; and even so he holds that a stipulation helps for a Shabbos lamp, as he wrote there from the Yerushalmi. See what I wrote.
