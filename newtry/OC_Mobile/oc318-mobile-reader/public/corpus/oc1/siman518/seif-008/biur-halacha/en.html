One may take wood adjacent - See Mishnah Berurah: from the sukkah itself it is forbidden, and even if it fell on Yom Tov it is also forbidden because of muktzeh. And whether a stipulation helps for a sukkah that fell was not clarified in the words of the Mechaber and the Rama. And see in Magen Avraham who strained over this; and Mateh Yehudah challenged him and wrote that they relied on what they wrote above in siman 279, se'if 4, and the same applies here, for both are muktzeh on account of a prohibition; and if so, according to the Mechaber who ruled there that a stipulation helps, the same applies here. However, there are further views among the Rishonim: some say specifically regarding a rickety one that at least stands for falling and for kindling, except that Yom Tov holds it back, and in such a case a stipulation helps; however regarding a sound one a stipulation does not help according to R' Yehuda for muktzeh on account of a prohibition; and this is the view of Tosafos in chapter "Kirah" (Shabbos folio 44a) s.v. "that is in the lamp" and on folio 45 s.v. "and if"; and so the Rashba wrote (in his novellae and in Avodat HaKodesh) and the Ra'ah and the Meiri; and so too is the view of the Me'or that regarding a sound one a stipulation does not help. And the view of the Ramban and the Ran is that a stipulation helps even regarding a sound one, as Beis Yosef wrote; and so too is the view of the Ittur. And it is reasonable for the law that a stipulation does not help regarding a sound one, for they are many. And especially since there are views that hold that even regarding a rickety one a stipulation does not help [Tosafos in Beitzah folio 30b s.v. "he said," and also the Rambam according to Beis Yosef]. And even though from Beis Yosef's words it is implied that he holds for the law that a stipulation helps even regarding a sound one, that is because he did not see all the above Rishonim. And in Kuntres Acharon of the Graz he innovated that a rickety sukkah that fell is nolad: originally a sukkah and now wood; and regarding nolad a stipulation does not apply, for nevertheless it is nolad; and if so, according to the one who forbids nolad on Yom Tov a stipulation does not help even regarding a sound one; see there where he elaborated. And according to the Rama in siman 279 who wrote that our custom is like the poskim that a stipulation does not help for a lamp, there is no practical difference in all this that we wrote, only regarding the essence of the law.
And the same law applies if, etc. - See in Beis Yosef who wrote that since the Rambam omitted this law, which stems from Rav Menashe's answer, it implies that he holds that he disagrees with Shmuel and ruled like Shmuel; and he concluded: but the Rif and the Rosh who copied this imply that they hold that they do not disagree with each other; and it is implied that his intent is to rule thus for the law, as is his way in every place to rule like both against one. And I saw in Biur HaGra who wrote that since the Shulchan Aruch omitted this, it implies that he holds for the law unlike Rav Menashe; but in Beis Yosef this is not so implied (and it is possible that he holds that this is a greater novelty, and since he copied that this is permitted, all the more so in this). And however it may be, for the practical law one should certainly rule like the Rama, for so too is the view of the Rashba in Avodat HaKodesh and the Meiri; see there.
He threw bundles - See Mishnah Berurah: in this case, if they were not bundles they would be nullified on account of the schach; and this is explained explicitly in our sugya. And it is explained in the novellae of the Rashba that if he expressly intended not for the sake of schach but only to store them, they are not nullified and it is permitted to take from them; and he explains thus the intent of the Yerushalmi in what it wrote "and that is when he intended to thicken"; see there; and according to him the Yerushalmi refers to the schach. However, Or Zarua did not explain thus, but rather that it refers to what is adjacent to the walls; see there where he proved that it does not refer to the schach. And at first glance Or Zarua disagrees with that of the Rashba, and regarding schach that is not in bundles they are necessarily nullified and mix with the rest of the schach even if he placed them to store. And it is possible that they do not disagree: according to everyone, if he intends expressly to thicken, even adjacent to the wall they are nullified on account of the wall; and conversely, also regarding schach, if he intends expressly not to thicken, even when unbound they are also not nullified. And the Gemara's distinction is only in an unspecified case, or for another person who does not know what the placer's intent was: and in this, regarding reeds adjacent to the walls, since he did not weave them with the wall, presumably he did not nullify them to the wall; and regarding reeds on the schach, since the schach too is not woven and thick, they are presumably nullified. And see the Meiri who wrote that also regarding Issurayasa that is not nullified, it is only when he places them to dry them and the like; and at first glance this implies that if they were not Issurayasa they would be nullified even when he places them to dry, which is unlike the Rashba's view; and nevertheless it is possible that he too deals with an unspecified case. And in Avodat HaKodesh he did not copy the matters in the language of his novellae, but rather wrote that even though we say that from what is adjacent he also demolishes a tent, nevertheless that is specifically when he did not stipulate; but if he stipulated it is permitted, for he already revealed with his mouth that he is not placing it for covering. And see in Shulchan Aruch HaRav who wrote as an obvious matter that without Issurayasa, even if he placed them to store, they are nullified and it is forbidden to take from them because of demolishing a tent; and this requires further study for the law.
