And the Ra'az wrote that it deals with a rickety partition, whose demolition has no Torah prohibition. And the Ramban elaborated to reply to him, that we find much muktzeh on account of a rabbinic prohibition. And it appears to me that even the Ra'az concedes this, as I wrote above regarding a rickety sukkah that fell; rather he holds that here there are two [factors]: a rabbinic prohibition, and also the produce themselves are not muktzeh, and therefore it is permitted; but with a complete partition he holds it is forbidden. And according to what is written in the Shulchan Aruch, one may learn that if someone has turnips buried in a pit, and a non-Jew opened them on Shabbos or on Yom Tov, there is no muktzeh concern regarding them. Nevertheless, if the non-Jew did it for the sake of a Jew, it is forbidden in any case, for it is a Torah melacha, as written in siman 394, se'if 2, and see the beginning of siman 515. And during Pesach one must be careful about this if he knows for certain that the pit was opened today.
