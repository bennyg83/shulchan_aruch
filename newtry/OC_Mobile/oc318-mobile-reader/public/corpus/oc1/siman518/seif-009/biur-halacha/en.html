A house that is full of produce, etc. — See the Mishna Berurah, that even if the house was a sound complete structure, so it is clarified in Magen Avraham and in other later authorities, since the Mechaber stated his ruling without qualification in the Shulchan Aruch, from which we infer that he holds per the view of the Rif and the Rambam that in all cases it is permitted, and in accordance with the reasoning of the Ramban that muktzeh does not apply in this matter, as we have cited in the Mishna Berurah. And even though the Rosh and the Tur cited the practical ruling per the view of Rashi — that [the Sages] only permitted [demolishing] a house that is not a complete structure, only one where the bricks were arranged without mortar, since there is only a d'rabbanan prohibition in such demolition, as the Gemara explains per the view of R' Meir, but in a complete structure there is [the concern of] muktzeh machmat issur which is forbidden even on Yom Tov — and this is likewise the view of the Rashba as well [except that he permits it in a rickety structure that one expects will fall and from which he has not removed his attention from the produce] — nevertheless, [the Mechaber] ruled per the view of the Rif and Rambam and the reasoning of the Ramban, because the Ran also cited it, and the Maggid Mishneh wrote that this is the primary view, and so I found in the Chidushei HaRa'ah who also cited the reasoning of the Ramban as the practical ruling. Nevertheless, l'chatchila it appears to me that one should not be lenient, since many of the Rishonim are stringent regarding a house that is a complete structure — in addition to Rashi, the Rashba, and the Rosh mentioned above, this is also found in the Razah [whom Magen Avraham cites], and in Or Zarua, and in the Meiri, all of whom followed the view of Rashi. And Rabbeinu Yeruham also wrote that this view is the primary one. And see in Tosafos who were even more stringent than this, that even in the airspace between bricks it is only permitted according to R' Yehuda. And also the view of the Ramban which was cited to permit — one who examines the Milchamos will see that he cited two resolutions there, and per the first resolution, the Mishnah speaks specifically when [the house] had been breached before Yom Tov — see there. Therefore, it appears to me that one should not be lenient in this matter, and likewise in the law of turnips that Magen Avraham derived from this seif — except in a time of pressing need.
