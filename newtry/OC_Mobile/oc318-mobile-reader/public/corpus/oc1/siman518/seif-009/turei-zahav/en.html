A house that is full, etc. In the mishnah we say: a house that is full of produce and was breached: he takes from the place of the breach. The Rama: he may even demolish l'chatchila and take. Why? Does he not demolish a tent? Shmuel said: with a course of bricks (Rashi explained: an arrangement of stones without mortar). And the reason it is permitted to take from the place of the breach: we do not say they are muktzeh on account of a prohibition, that one cannot breach on Yom Tov, since breaching is only forbidden by rabbinic law, for there is only a course of bricks there: they are not muktzeh. And therefore the Rosh and the Tur wrote: but if it was a complete building, even R' Shimon concedes that it is forbidden to take from the place of the breach. And the Ran wrote that the Ramban challenged this according to the first Tanna who does not permit to breach: why were the produce not forbidden because of muktzeh, even though there is only a rabbinic prohibition? For a rickety sukkah is muktzeh on account of a rabbinic prohibition; even regarding a sound one one is not liable for demolishing except when demolishing in order to build; and likewise regarding coins on the bed we say that by placing he becomes a base for something forbidden, and even after the coins were taken they are forbidden, even though there is only a rabbinic prohibition. Therefore the Ramban explained that even though it is forbidden to breach, the produce are not muktzeh on account of the prohibition of demolishing, for we only forbid when the prohibition is in their body or they became a base for a prohibition, similar to a lamp and a bed; but here the produce are fit and did not become a base, except that something else causes that he cannot benefit from them, and it is only as if his produce were in a place he cannot go; until here. Therefore the Rambam ruled without specification to permit, as written here in the Shulchan Aruch, implying even if it was a building wall it is permitted even if it was breached of itself on Yom Tov. And Beis Yosef wrote: and it appears that his reason is that we only established it as a course of bricks because of the difficulty according to R' Meir why he permits to breach; we must say the first Tanna deals in every manner; therefore since we hold like the first Tanna, even regarding a complete building it is permitted; end quote. The explanation of his words: certainly the first Tanna also deals with a course of bricks, for he certainly deals in the manner of which R' Meir deals and disagrees with him that it is forbidden to breach l'chatchila even with a course of bricks, and there is no permit except when it was breached of itself; and then it is permitted even regarding a complete building. And Maharshal also rules like the Rambam and Shulchan Aruch. And according to this it appears to me that one who has produce outside the techum and a non-Jew brought them on Shabbos or on Yom Tov: they are permitted, for there is no muktzeh here, for the produce themselves today are not muktzeh, except that he cannot take them because of the techum prohibition. And since here it is permitted when breached of itself even with a Torah prohibition, all the more so here regarding what a non-Jew brought from outside the techum even for a Jew: nevertheless afterward it is like coming of itself, permitted to another Jew. And even according to Rashi and the Rosh and Tur who did not permit here regarding a complete building, nevertheless they permit with a course of bricks since it is only a rabbinic prohibition; the same applies regarding outside the techum, for techumim are rabbinic. And if so there is a great astonishment at the Rashba in a responsum that Beis Yosef brought in siman 515, and the Rama ruled it as law there at the end of that siman, regarding a Jew's produce that were outside the techum: that they are forbidden to everyone for the reason of muktzeh, since they were outside the techum the owners designated them away; and here it is proven according to everyone that it is permitted, for in any case muktzeh there is no more stringent than from a course of bricks here. Therefore it appears to my humble opinion that we do not hold like that responsum of the Rashba that the Rama mentioned at the end of siman 515. The Mordechai wrote in chapter "One may make an eruv": and if you say, even though we established it as a course of bricks where there is no demolishing and building, nevertheless how is it permitted to open it (meaning according to R' Meir's words)? Does he not move earth and stones that are muktzeh? And the Riva says that moving of muktzeh that is for the need of ochel nefesh is permitted to move in order to take the food; and a proof from our custom that we kindle upon ashes that were kindled on Yom Tov which are muktzeh, and we move them for the need of ochel nefesh to cover the pastry; end quote. And Beis Yosef wrote: one should be astonished, for if so why did they not permit to slaughter l'chatchila in order to cover with ashes that were kindled on Yom Tov? And this requires further study; end quote. And his astonishment is itself astonishing: for Tosafos in the first chapter of Beitzah folio 8 bring this of the Riva that moving muktzeh for the sake of ochel nefesh is permitted, and there itself they bring the Yerushalmi that it is forbidden to slaughter l'chatchila in order to cover with ashes that were kindled on Yom Tov. And it is simple that the reason is that covering the blood is not considered ochel nefesh, for the meat is not forbidden if he did not cover; rather covering is a mitzvah by itself upon him, and he should not cause the mitzvah to be nullified l'chatchila.
