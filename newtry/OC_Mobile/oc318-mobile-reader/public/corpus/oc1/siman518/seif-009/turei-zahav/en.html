A house that is full, etc. In the mishna they said: a house full of fruit and it cracked — one takes from the place of the crack; Rama: one may even dismantle l'chatchila to take — why? And behold dismantling a house Shmuel said in air of bricks (Rashi: arrangement of stones without mortar); and that which it is permitted to take from the place of the crack — the reason is we do not say they are muktzeh because of prohibition, for he cannot diminish on Yom Tov; since diminishing is forbidden only d'rabbanan, for there is only air of bricks — they are not muktzeh; therefore Rosh and Tosafos wrote; but if it was complete building even R' Shimon agrees it is forbidden to take from the place of the crack; and Ran wrote Ramban challenged this on the first tanna who does not permit diminishing — why are the fruits not forbidden because of muktzeh, even though there is only a d'rabbanan prohibition; and a shaky sukkah is muktzeh because of their prohibition, even for a healthy person one is not liable for dismantling except dismantling in order to build; and likewise money on the bed — they say by placing it becomes a base for a prohibited matter, and even after removing the money it is forbidden, even though there is only a d'rabbanan prohibition; therefore Ramban explained that even though diminishing is forbidden, the fruits are not muktzeh because of prohibition of dismantling, for they only forbade when the prohibition is in them or it became a base for prohibition like candle and bed; but here the fruits are fit and did not become a base, only that fear causes he cannot enjoy them — it is only as if his fruits were in a place he cannot go; therefore Rambam ruled stam leniently, and as written here in Shulchan Aruch implying even if the wall was of building it is permitted even if it cracked by itself on Yom Tov; and Beit Yosef wrote it appears his reason is we do not establish it as air of bricks, but because it is difficult for R' Meir why he permits diminishing — we can say the first tanna deals with every case; therefore since we hold like the first tanna, even in complete building it is permitted — end quote. Explanation of his words: certainly the first tanna also deals with air of bricks, for certainly it deals in the manner R' Meir deals with and disagrees, forbidding diminishing l'chatchila even in air of bricks, and the leniency is only when cracked by itself; and then it is permitted even in complete building; and Rashal rules like Rambam and Shulchan Aruch; therefore it appears to me one who has fruit from outside the techum and a non-Jew brought it on Shabbat or Yom Tov — it is permitted, for there is no muktzeh here, for the fruits today are not muktzeh, only he cannot take them because of techum prohibition; and since here it is permitted when cracked by itself, even with a d'oraisa prohibition, all the more here where a non-Jew brought from outside the techum even for this Israelite's need — nevertheless afterward it is as if it came by itself, permitted for another Israelite; and even per Rashi, Rosh, and Tur who did not permit here in complete building, nevertheless they permit in air of bricks since it is only d'rabbanan prohibition; the same applies to outside the techum which is d'rabbanan; and if so there is great wonder on Rashba in responsum brought in Beit Yosef siman 496 and Rama ruled there halachically at end of that siman regarding fruits of an Israelite that were outside the techum, that they are forbidden to all because of muktzeh since they were outside the techum the owners designated them — and here it is proven for all it is permitted, for in any case muktzeh is not stricter than air of bricks there; so it appears to me we do not hold like that responsum of Rashba that Rama cited s.s. siman 496: Mordechai wrote in chapter Kol HaMearvin: you asked — even though we establish air of bricks where there is only prohibition of dismantling and building, nevertheless how is it permitted to open it (meaning per R' Meir), behold one moves dirt and stones which are muktzeh; and Riva says moving muktzeh for ochel nefesh need is permitted to move it in order to take the food, and proof from our custom that we kindle on ashes kindled on Yom Tov which are muktzeh, and we move them for ochel nefesh need to cover the pot roast — end quote; and Beit Yosef wrote it is astounding: if so why did they not permit slaughtering l'chatchila to cover with ashes kindled on Yom Tov — and this requires study — end quote; and his wonder is astounding, for in Tosafos first chapter Beitzah 8 they bring this Riva called moving muktzeh for ochel nefesh, and there itself they bring Yerushalmi that it is forbidden to slaughter l'chatchila to cover with ashes kindled on Yom Tov; and it is obvious the reason blood covering is not considered ochel nefesh, for the meat is not forbidden if he did not cover, but covering is a mitzvah in itself upon it and one should not cause nullification of the mitzvah l'chatchila:
