(s.k. 7) Of a dovecote, etc. Of a house is forbidden. For in Beitzah folio 9 we say: that which Beis Hillel permitted there to move a ladder is specifically a dovecote ladder, for he does not need to plaster his roof with it; but an attic ladder that he needs to plaster his roof with is forbidden in a public domain because of marit ayin. Therefore even in the house it is forbidden to move it, for whatever they forbade because of marit ayin is forbidden even in innermost rooms. And Tosafos challenged: behold in Eruvin we say that even a ladder that is not of a dovecote it is permitted to move on Shabbos. And Rabbeinu Tam answered that in Eruvin it deals with a house ladder, which is smaller than an attic ladder, and we are not concerned that they will say he needs it to plaster his roof. And R' Eliezer of Metz answered that in Eruvin it deals with Shabbos, when he need not take them out to a public domain; therefore in his house it is permitted even an attic one, for in the house he does not need to plaster his roof; but on Yom Tov, since it is permitted to take it out to a public domain and there he needs to plaster his roof, therefore even inside the house it is forbidden, for whatever is forbidden in a public domain because of marit ayin is also forbidden in innermost rooms. And if so, according to R' Eliezer of Metz on Yom Tov even a house ladder is forbidden; for Rabbeinu Tam only distinguished between house and attic from the necessity of their question from tractate Eruvin; and since according to R' Eliezer of Metz in any case there is no difficulty from tractate Eruvin, why from the Torah should we distinguish between house and attic? And since the Tur in siman 308 ruled like R' Eliezer of Metz and permits on Shabbos even an attic ladder, automatically on Yom Tov even a house ladder is forbidden. And one who is lenient in the house has not lost out. So Yam Shel Shlomo wrote, and he explained the reason: even though according to R' Eliezer of Metz there is no necessity from the Eruvin sugya to distinguish between house and attic, nevertheless it is possible by reasoning without necessity to be lenient regarding a house ladder according to R' Eliezer of Metz.
And so it appears in the rulings of Tosafos; therefore one who relies on this has not lost out. And according to this, what Magen Avraham wrote "and one who is lenient in the house": it is possible he means a house ladder, and even taking it out to a public domain is permitted. However, I did not understand: for all this is according to the Tur's view in siman 308 who rules like R' Eliezer of Metz. But the Mechaber who ruled without specification in siman 308 to forbid regarding an attic ladder even on Shabbos, and it is the Rambam's language, necessarily ruled like Rabbeinu Tam and not like R' Eliezer of Metz.
If so, why did Magen Avraham copy Maharshal's words onto the Mechaber's words that of a house is forbidden? That is, according to the view of R' Eliezer of Metz (and so Yam Shel Shlomo challenged the Rambam that he should have explained that of a house is permitted; and this is not so great a difficulty, for it is not the Rambam's way to bring a new law that emerges from give-and-take and is not stated explicitly). And the implication of Beis Yosef's language in the Shulchan Aruch has no decisive ruling: in the opening clause he only wrote to permit a dovecote one, implying that even of a house is forbidden; and in the concluding clause he mentioned to forbid an attic one, implying that of a house is permitted; and therefore one of the inferences is not precise. And it is possible that Magen Avraham's intent is not to load Maharshal's words into the Mechaber's words, but rather he wrote a reasoning of his own to be stringent on Shabbos like Rabbeinu Tam's stringency, that an attic ladder even in his house is forbidden, and to be stringent on Yom Tov like R' Eliezer of Metz to forbid a house ladder even in the house.
