(s.k. 2) Lest, etc. Unless it is for the need of eating: for example, that he has in his chest foods needed for the Yom Tov meal, or matters of adornment needed to adorn with them on Yom Tov, and he fears lest they be stolen and through this he will refrain from Yom Tov joy.
For the sake of their beginning. For if we do not permit him the return, he will not bring them at first to the synagogue out of fear of theft; and he needs them in the synagogue to pray from them.
But, etc. It is forbidden, etc., even if he fears they will be stolen from there. For they only permitted the return for the sake of their beginning, so that he not fail to bring them on Yom Tov there, as above. And since they already lie in the synagogue and he did not bring them on Yom Tov to the synagogue, we have no "their end for the sake of their beginning."
And I saw, etc. That they are no longer accustomed to eat. And the Taz disagrees and wrote that knives throughout the day are called a need of ochel nefesh, for if some fruit presents itself to him, what will he need a knife for if not to eat them?
It is permitted according to Tosafos in Kesubos folio 57a s.v. "mitoch," etc. Who wrote there that the Rashba wished to find a permit for the custom of carrying out for the need of a non-Jew on Yom Tov, because he holds like the Rif that they did not institute eruv chatzeros on Yom Tov, since something with some slight need is permitted to carry out even to a public domain, and what need is there for eruv chatzeros; and for something with no slight need at all, which are few, for their sake they did not wish to institute eruv chatzeros. If so, since for us we have no public domain, as above in siman 345, se'if 7, and its law is like karmelit: if so, just as it is permitted to move in a courtyard that is not joined by an eruv on Yom Tov, the same applies that it is permitted to move in a karmelit. And the custom works well. And afterward he retracted and wrote, and this is his language: that from a courtyard that is not joined by an eruv one may not learn a leniency for karmelit, for it is more stringent; end quote of Tosafos. If so, granted that we do not hold in this like the Rif and hold that on Yom Tov eruv chatzeros is required, as written in s.k. 5: nevertheless we hear from Tosafos's words that outside the eruv is lighter than karmelit. And therefore even though knives were permitted in the city outside the eruv, but outside the city which is karmelit, which is more stringent, we do not permit. And the Taz disagrees and wrote that our alleyways have the law of karmelit. If so, what stringency is there outside the city more than in an alleyway outside the eruv? And see above in siman 363, se'if 26 in the Rama's gloss, where it is explained that our alleyways have the law of courtyards. However, at the beginning of siman 365 in Magen Avraham s.k. 4, Magen Avraham was in doubt whether also for a leniency we say that our alleyways have the law of courtyards; see there.
