(ג) But not all of his body — see Mishna Berurah that except for face, hands, and feet he considers it is not for all souls [tzorech kol nefesh]; so is explained in Tosafos and Mordechai and Chinuch mitzvah 298 that only face, hands, and feet are for all souls, but the whole body is only for pleasure and it is a Torah-level melacha in heating for them, as the later authorities copied here. And it appears that therefore even one limb besides face, hands, and feet is forbidden; and so is proven in Meiri who wrote the reason is that this is the usual practice, as they said: a person washes face, hands, and feet for the honor of his Maker; and so is implied somewhat in that Chinuch — therefore only those are permitted. And in Chemdas Moshe I saw that he copied that all who do not wash most of the body are permitted, and he copied this from siman 326 regarding Shabbat in water heated from erev Shabbat, where it is also written that face, hands, and feet are permitted but not the whole body; and Rama wrote there that other limbs are also permitted if one does not wash most of the body. And it appears this is not comparable to there, for there the water was already heated from erev Shabbat and only the rabbinic decree of bathing applies; and Rama holds that all who do not wash the whole body there is no bathing there, unlike here where one heats hot water on Yom Tov, where melacha on Yom Tov is not permitted except for something for all souls, and we say that for face, hands, and feet it is permitted because this matter is for all souls but not the whole body. And what is the difference if one heats for the need of the whole body itself or many limbs of the body — all that one washes besides face, hands, and feet appears not to be for all souls. And even for Rambam and Semag and Geonim who hold there is no Torah prohibition only because of a decree — nevertheless, since the Gemara did not mention leniency except for face, hands, and feet [which is the usual matter], it appears that other limbs are forbidden, for otherwise they should have included that all who do not wash the whole body or most of it are permitted, all included [and also the law of siman 326 is not so simple for this reason]. Rather I saw that the source of Rama's law in siman 326 derives from Rosh, Laws of Mikvaot at the end — Beit Yosef brought it there, and from his words it is proven that likewise in our matter he holds that for feet it is not precise and likewise for other limbs; but nevertheless for practical law in our matter Rama did not emend this, and it appears he did not see fit to be lenient as his view, only there regarding bathing which is rabbinic for all, but not to heat hot water which is Torah-level for many poskim; and in Meiri it is clearly proven forbidden, and he explicitly disagrees also with the law of 326 where he wrote explicitly on Shabbat that even one limb besides face, hands, and feet is forbidden; and so too Rashi's view there regarding heating a bucket in its simple sense; and Beit Yosef sensed this and strains to explain it — see there. And from all this it appears that in our matter, where there is concern of a Torah prohibition, at least one should not be lenient; but in Biur Halacha siman 199 the Mechaber copied Rosh's law as halacha, only he copied it with a slight change — he did not write that it is permitted to heat a bucket to wash the private parts as Rosh wrote [per his view that feet are not precise], rather he wrote that it is permitted to wash the private parts in a small amount of water that was heated even on Yom Tov, and perhaps he intentionally changed the wording. Yet for practical law Shach there brought in the name of Mishna Berurah that he disagrees with Rosh, and his view too is perhaps they permitted only face, hands, and feet — see there [and I do not have the book itself]. And even for the Mechaber one should not compare optional bathing to bathing the private parts, for perhaps the Mechaber relied on Rosh only there where it is for the need of a mitzvah immersion; however afterward I found in Rashba's novellae on Shabbos that his view too is that face, hands, and feet are not precise and likewise other limbs — any part of the body only is permitted, like Rosh; and see Shaarei Teshuvah in what he wrote in the name of Machazik Berachah to be lenient, and so is implied the view of Noda B'Yehuda in Even HaEzer siman 25 at the end — see there.
