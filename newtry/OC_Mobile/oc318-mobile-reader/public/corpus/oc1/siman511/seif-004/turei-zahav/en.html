(א) The facility for eating. Deza is an epic mental food for the poor, but they don't have and imagine to be treated as a burgeoning figure

(ב) Even if one scatters them etc. For there is no concern of extinguishing here, for in food for souls it is permitted since it is impossible from erev Yom Tov, unlike mustard in siman 510 where it is possible from erev Yom Tov.

(ג) A cup of spiced [wine] on garments. In the Gemara: pressing a cup on silk is forbidden because it creates odor. Rashi explained: to press a cup of spiced [wine] on the silk of a garment to put good smell in them is forbidden, for he creates odor in the silk — it implies he intends that the smell enter the garment. And in Maharil it is written forbidden to place an etrog on a coat on the first day of Yom Tov because it creates odor, similar to a cup on silk in chapter 2 of Beitza; and Rashal wrote on this and I wonder, for he did not forbid placing the cup except in order to finish as Ateret Zekenim wrote, and this is proof that he mentioned silk and not a garment in general, only that the way they finish them; but where one does not intend to finish it appears permitted, for it is not relevant here to say psik reisha except in actual melacha, but finishing itself is not melacha, only extinguishing and kindling — end. And his words are correct, and do not argue with what is written siman 302 that one whose hands are stained from fruit water should beware not to touch a cloth because of dyeing, and although he does not intend to dye the cloth, dyeing is a primary melacha and therefore it would be psik reisha. And I saw in some communities that they pour fragrant oil called spikenard into water on Yom Tov for the need of priestly hand washing for the platform, and they do a prohibition, for they create odor in the water; and even if they pour on erev Yom Tov, nevertheless they create odor on Yom Tov through the priests; and this is pleasant to them and they are accustomed to it — therefore one should prevent doing so.
