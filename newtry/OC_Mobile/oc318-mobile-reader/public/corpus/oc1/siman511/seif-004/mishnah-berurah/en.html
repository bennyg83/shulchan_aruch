(21) "Mugmar." Fumigation with incense is not something equally enjoyed by all people, but principally by those accustomed to luxuries, and was therefore not permitted on Yom Tov. Regarding smoking tobacco on Yom Tov, see Biur Halacha. (22) "Upon the coals." With wood coals this is forbidden even if they were originally kindled for food preparation, because placing the spices on them initially somewhat extinguishes the coals and afterward kindles the spices. All the more so is it forbidden if he kindled the coals specifically for this purpose. The Gemara implies that even a pottery coal, meaning a piece of pottery heated for roasting meat upon it, is forbidden. (23) "To fumigate the house." This is forbidden specifically when one performs the fumigation on Yom Tov. Before Yom Tov, however, one may take an iron vessel full of holes, fumigate it with spices, and plug the holes; on the following day he may open the holes and the house will then be perfumed by itself. This may be done even on Shabbos. [Gemara] (24) "To improve them for eating." Even if the foods are already good as they are and he merely wishes to improve their flavor further, it is permitted. (25) "Of wood." Although, as above, this involves both extinguishing and kindling, it is permitted because this is something equally desired by all people. Even poor people desire to improve the taste of their food, although such spices may not be readily available to them. Mustard in siman 510 is different because its preparation can ordinarily be done before Yom Tov. Know that some authorities hold that this permission applies only when the coals had already been kindled for food preparation, such as for a pot. According to them, one may not initially kindle wood solely in order to scatter aromatic spices beneath fruit over the coals. (26) "And it is forbidden," etc., "upon garments." Even a garment that already has a fragrance may not have additional fragrance placed upon it in order to make it smell more strongly. Nevertheless, if an etrog had been resting on a garment before Yom Tov and was removed, it may be returned, because it does not thereby acquire any additional fragrance. (27) "Because he creates," etc. This is rabbinically forbidden, because creating a new quality resembles performing a new melacha. (28) "Fragrance." The same applies to placing an aromatic substance into water on Yom Tov in order to give the water a fragrance; this is forbidden.
