Mugmar. For it is not equal for every person.
To sweeten them for eating. It is implied from his language that even if they are already good to eat, but he wants to sweeten them further, it is permitted; for there is no concern of extinguishing, since its end is that it kindles; and there is no concern of generating fragrance, for his intent is not to generate fragrance, only to prepare foods, as with meat that they roast on coals; see siman 507, se'if 4.
And it is forbidden to press. And even though it is permitted to crush herbs, as is written in siman 322, se'if 5, for there fragrance is already present, and it is only that he adds more (Gemara); and one should not bring a proof from here that a garment that already smells may have spices placed upon it so that it smell more, for this case is different: the fragrance is already there, only that by crushing he increases it so that the fragrance emerges; which is not so with a garment, for nevertheless he adds a new fragrance. And nevertheless, an esrog that had been lying from erev Yom Tov on the garment, and he took it away, it is permitted to return it on Yom Tov, for it does not smell more on that account (see siman 638). And it is plain that it is forbidden to put something fragrant into the water so that it smell, not like the practice of some who do so for washing the hands of the kohanim.
