1) [Se'if 1] It is permitted to make a fire, etc. Although the Torah states, 'Only that which may be eaten by every person, that alone, etc.,' the Sages permitted making a fire because once kindling was permitted for food preparation, it was also permitted when not needed for food preparation. Beis Yosef in the name of Ran; Taz, subsection 1; Magen Avraham, subsection 1. Bach, however, writes that a fire, which provides benefit to the entire body, resembles food preparation and is permitted without invoking the principle of 'since.' See there. Olas Shabbos, subsection 1, writes likewise. Rambam also writes in his Commentary on the Mishnah that Beis Hillel permits a fire because the phrase 'only that which may be eaten by every person' includes all bodily benefits. See there. Avodas HaKodesh, gate 3, subsection 5, likewise implies that the reason is that anything needed by every person is included in the verse, 'That which may be eaten by every person may be done for you.' See there. See below, subsection 10.
2) There, in the gloss. Some say that it is forbidden to heat a winter room on Yom Tov. The wording of Mahari Weil implies that only Jews may not heat it, but having a non-Jew do so is permitted. Magen Avraham, subsection 2; Eliyah Rabbah, subsection 4. The later authorities write likewise. See below, subsection 7.
3) There, in the gloss. For sometimes the cold is not severe, etc. This means that when the cold is not severe, the act is forbidden. It appears to me that if the cold is so severe that fatty foods congeal, one may light the fire, because this is needed for food preparation. Magen Avraham, subsection 3; Eliyah Rabbah there. The later authorities write likewise. Even according to those who forbid heating, it is permitted in this case.
4) There, in the gloss. For sometimes the cold is not severe, etc. According to this opinion, the same applies to making a fire in order to warm oneself before it. This is permitted only when the cold is so severe that most people need to warm themselves before a fire. Shulchan Aruch HaRav, subsection 2.
