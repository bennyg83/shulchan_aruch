No letter should adhere to another. If one tag is attached to another, there is a practical difference. If the tags of the letter itself are attached to one another, the opinion of the Rema MiPano is to invalidate, and his words are brought below in Magen Avraham and Eliyah Rabbah. But if the bodies of the letters are each recognizable on their own, and only the tag of one letter touches a tag in the letter next to it, or from one word to another word, the Meiri is lenient in this. The Magen Avraham and the Pri Chadash in Even HaEzer, siman 125, s.k. 11, are also stringent in this. However, the Eliyah Rabbah in this siman wrote that one should be stringent only where possible, and his words are brought in Pri Megadim, Eshel Avraham. What appears from Shaarei Teshuvah in s.k. 7, that one should be stringent even according to the Meiri after the fact if they were not corrected, I examined the Pri Chadash there and it is explicitly implied that according to the Meiri one should validate after the fact even without scraping. Nevertheless, ab initio one should certainly be stringent and scrape, in accordance with the opinion of the Ralbach brought by the Magen Avraham in s.k. 3, that correction helps even according to him. Even tags that touch one another within the letter itself seemingly can be corrected, even in tefillin or mezuzah, and it is not called out of order, since we see that the form of the letter was not changed previously through this. So wrote the book Keset HaSofer.
Each letter must be surrounded by parchment. The term gevil that he used is not precise, for tefillin are not written on it, as below in seif 7. Rather, he used the language that applies to a Sefer Torah, but it is not precise. Olas Tamid.
