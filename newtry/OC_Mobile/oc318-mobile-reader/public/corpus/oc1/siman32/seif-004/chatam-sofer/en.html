There, in Magen Avraham, se'if katan 33: 'Knesset HaGedolah writes.' Note: See Responsa Chatam Sofer, Yoreh De'ah, siman 263, concerning a Sefer Torah in which Elokeinu was written by extending the bottom of the nun and placing the vav beside it. According to Magen Avraham it is invalid; the question was how to correct it. Chatam Sofer first challenges Responsa Avodat HaGershuni, siman 95, who assumes that the prohibition against erasing a letter appended after a Divine Name is only rabbinic, and who writes that adding a letter that thereby invalidates another letter is merely indirect erasure. Chatam Sofer disputes both points. He concludes that if the writing clearly appears divided into two words, so that it is undoubtedly invalid, one may without hesitation erase the vav first and write another within the nun. Since it is erased precisely because it appears as two words, the vav is not appended to the Divine Name. If it does not appear as two words, however, the vav cannot be erased, because perhaps the writing is valid and the vav is appended to the Divine Name. Nor may the Divine Name be cut from the parchment, etc. Nevertheless, to avoid consigning an entire sheet to genizah, one may cut out the vav and place it in genizah. The Torah does not explicitly prohibit cutting out a letter, so a doubt is resolved leniently. He should then write another vav within the nun in the manner explained above. Examine the responsum itself carefully.
