Processing for a Torah scroll is effective for all, and this is not considered lowering sanctity, for designation is not legally substantive, and in any case it is permitted from a more severe sanctity to a lighter one, though not to mundane use; see siman 42 and Magen Avraham here, letter 10. Processing for tefillin is effective for a mezuzah, and processing for a mezuzah is effective only for a mezuzah. Now, one may be uncertain: if he processed hide for the leather of the head tefillin compartment, upon which the shin is written, which has the actual sanctity of the tefillin themselves, does this help for writing the parshiyos upon it? See below, seif 37, where some say that processing for its required purpose is not needed. If so, even if it was processed for the head tefillin compartment, it is as though nothing was done. See Beit Yosef here, that according to the view that a mezuzah does not require processing, it is as though nothing was done. If he processed them for straps, they are invalid for parshiyos, for the parshiyos have greater sanctity. Regarding leather for compartments, one can also say that it is invalid if he later made parchment from it, since their sanctity is more severe than that of a strap. With the help of Heaven, this will be further explained in siman 33:3.
What is written. The Bach, on the Gemara’s question in HaKometz, “Worn tefillin are not made into a mezuzah,” asks why not answer that the case is where he scored lines, and the scoring for tefillin does not help because tefillin do not require scoring while a mezuzah does. This implies that scoring must be for its required purpose. But in Yoreh Deah 271 the Tur rules that a Torah scroll does not require scoring for its required purpose, as the Shach there, seif katan 2, cites. One should not say that for a mezuzah everyone agrees scoring must be for its required purpose, unlike a Torah scroll, over which there is a dispute, as explained in Yoreh Deah 271 and 288. For in the Beit Yosef there in 271 it is stated that we rule like Rava, that designation is not legally substantive, meaning in such a case, where he does not perform a significant act but only scoring, and this is sound even according to Rabbeinu Tam, who holds that processing must be for its required purpose, since processing is a significant act and is stronger than designation, whereas scoring is only designation and does not require this. If so, even a mezuzah does not require scoring for its required purpose. We have learned that there are three types of scoring: tefillin do not require scoring; if one processed hide for a Torah scroll, then changed his mind and scored it for tefillin, and afterward changed his mind back to a Torah scroll, it is valid, because scoring does not require its own purpose. But if he scored even for a Torah scroll and afterward changed his mind to use it for a mezuzah, according to the Bach it is invalid, for he holds that a mezuzah requires scoring for its required purpose; according to us, it is valid. Perhaps “it does not require scoring for its required purpose” means when done neutrally, but if it was done explicitly not for its purpose, that is worse. See Magen Avraham, letter 10, regarding what is written about Beer Hagolah, letter reish, with the help of Heaven. There is a fourth scoring, in a get, Even HaEzer 125, Beit Shmuel letter 10 in the name of Rashi on Menachos, that scoring in a get is according to the Torah. See Levush, Yoreh Deah 288, regarding scoring tefillin.
