Se'if 4, one must ensure that no letter adheres, etc. If the letter is large and it adhered at its end in such a way that, if the adhered part were scraped away, the form of the letter would remain, some validate it, meaning even without scraping away the adhesion: GraZ; Machatzis HaShekel, letter 1; Knesses HaGedolah, Hagahos Tur, in the name of Radach; Olas Tamid, letter 6; Emes LeYaakov, Laws of Touching Letters, letter 31; Derech HaChaim, siman 12, letter 31; Beis Menuchah, letter 56. But Magen Avraham, s.k. 3, wrote that one should be stringent, meaning to scrape away the adhesion; see Machatzis HaShekel. So too wrote Halachah Berurah, letter 3; Eliyah Rabbah, letter 8; Yad Aharon, Hagahos Tur; GraZ, letter 5. This is also our custom here in Baghdad, may the Supreme One establish it: in every case where a letter adhered, we scrape away the adhesion, provided that the letter's form was not changed, as written below in se'if 18 and se'if 25. But if such a case is found during Torah reading, one does not remove the Torah scroll. Although our custom is that even when one letter adheres to another we take out another Torah scroll, as below, letter 28, since all the above authorities permit it, including the authorities who wrote that custom, this is how we rule.
And Knesses HaGedolah further wrote. There, in the name of Meiri, that if the letter itself is not joined, but one tag is joined to another, it is valid. Some explain this as the tag of this letter being joined to the tag of another letter: GraZ, letter 5; Shaarei Teshuvah, letter 7. Others explain that the two letters are joined by one tag: Machatzis HaShekel; Yosef Ometz, siman 41. In the name of Ralnach he wrote that it appears Rambam disagrees; see there. Olas Tamid, letter 7, likewise wrote that one should be stringent. Magen Avraham, s.k. 3; Halachah Berurah, letter 3; Eliyah Rabbah, letter 7; and Pri Chadash in Even HaEzer, siman 125, letter 18, wrote likewise, as did Yad Aharon, Hagahos Tur; GraZ, letter 5; Emes LeYaakov, Laws of Touching Letters, letter 31; Derech HaChaim, siman 12, letter 31; Beis Menuchah, letter 57; Meleches Shamayim, rule 6, letter 4, in Chochmah; and Keset HaSofer, siman 7, letter 1. As a matter of law, since all the authorities disagreed with Meiri, one should be stringent and scrape away the adhesion, whether the tag of one letter adhered to the tag of another letter, or whether one letter adhered to another by means of one tag.
