And parashat Shema. Av Bet Hillulim — and regarding the form of the letters and the tagin explained there, it is fitting that God-fearing scribes learn them, each person from each person — that is, each should choose for himself an expert, proficient scribe who will teach him mouth to ear, and also show him with a finger, "thus see and sanctify" — not like those who rely on themselves to write with holy intentions yet have not entered the secret of God, only what they see in Sefer Mitzvot Shemurim and similar books compiled on this topic; and they imagine in themselves that they produce the matter correctly, and they have erred from the path of reason, for their understanding is too small to contain the depth of the matters. And whoever did not receive the matters clearly would do better not to involve himself in this at all and write without intentions. Nevertheless one may be careful to act in the forms of letters written by famous scribes, men of reputation and men of deed, as Maharam Zacuto wrote and in Birkei Yosef end of siman 25, that one should be exacting in the act of mitzvot that it be in order and as prescribed in their law, even though we do not know their hints and secrets, for the mitzvah acts by segulah and makes an impression above even when done without particular intent — only that one must intend the general intent that one do this act per the commandment of his God, in the name of what his elder, the author of Chesed le-Avraham, may his rest be in Eden, wrote in his glosses on manuscript as above; and see below seif 64.
