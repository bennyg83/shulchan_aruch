(1) And the parasha of Shema—and one must write four [threads of the] dalet of echad as large as four small daletin (Magen Avraham); and it is possible that they do not measure by that script but any in which there are very small daletin suffices; therefore the custom is to write only the [dalet] larger than the other daletin in that script.

(2) To write them—all the parshiyot, whether of the head and whether of the hand.

(3) In this order—as it is written "and these words shall be," etc., in their being. All the more so one must be careful for this reason that each parasha itself be written in its order without missing even one letter, for afterward he cannot complete it, as below in seif 23.

(4) Invalid—the tefillin made from those parshiyot [are invalid], but the parshiyot themselves are not disqualified; e.g. if he began writing from the parasha "And it shall be when He brings you," he may attach to it the parasha Kadesh from other tefillin if he knows certainly they were written before these, for otherwise it is a Torah doubt le-chumra; and likewise if at the time of writing it was in order but afterward one earlier parasha was invalidated, all parshiyot after it are automatically invalidated; and regarding combining other parshiyot the law is as above.

(5) Before—because it is earlier in the verse; and some say the opposite because the holiness of the head is more stringent than the hand; we practice as the Rav wrote in the Hagahah, and be-di'eved for all opinions there is no insistence. It is written in Sefer HaKavanot that lechatchilah he should write all parshiyot of head and hand consecutively without interrupting between them with any speech at all; see in Sha'arei Teshuvah.
