Se'if 25 — And likewise if there were not some yodin on the alephs, shins, and ayins, etc. See note: this apparently contradicts what Shulchan Aruch wrote above [se'if 1] in the Hagahah — "however, if we see that the form of the letter does not remain as corrected, it is invalid even if a child reads it according to its law." If so, here too, if the yodin of the ayins and alephs do not touch, it is plain that it is not the form of a letter according to its law — so why would a child's reading help here? Therefore Taz wrote explicitly, in the name of Rivash: therefore one may not validate an aleph whose left leg does not touch the middle line just because a child calls it aleph, etc. — how did Shulchan Aruch write here the opposite? It is true one could say Shulchan Aruch's intent is specifically for correction it is permitted and it would not be out of order; but in any case, if they did not correct it, it is invalid, and a child's reading does not help except to qualify it to correct afterward and it would not be out of order — but whatever was not corrected is invalid. This is dealt with in se'if 1. But in truth there is no reason for the matter — either way, if through a child's reading it has the name and form of a letter, then without correction too it should be validated; and if it does not have the form of a letter on it, then again why should it not be considered out of order if he writes afterward, since until now it did not have the name of a letter — why should it not be considered out of order? Furthermore, if even though it has no letter form and is not considered out of order, why does it depend on whether the child reads it as a letter or not — since it has no letter form and is not considered out of order, why should there be a distinction whether the child recognizes it or not? Therefore we say the main distinction is whether their separation is evident — if their separation is evident, what the child reads does not help and it is invalid even if corrected afterward, and it is out of order; this is dealt with in se'if 1. Here it deals with a case where their separation is not evident — whatever the child calls it, it has the form of a letter and is not out of order; automatically, even if they did not correct it yet, it is also kosher. So teaches Shulchan Aruch's language itself above in se'if 1, which wrote "likewise these protrusions of the zayins that do not touch and their separation is not evident," etc. — it implies this is his main reason: since their separation is not evident, the same applies to yodin on alephs and ayins, as is clear to me and correct. I again saw in Peri Megadim in Eshel Avraham that he wrote thus, and I mean his view in this; see there briefly, and understand well.
