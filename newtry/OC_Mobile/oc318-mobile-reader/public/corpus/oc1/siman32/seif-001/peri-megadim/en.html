He should write. See Taz. This is in the Beit Yosef regarding the rule that if he omitted something there is no remedy, in the name of Rabbi Yitzchak Aksandari. However, the Bach wrote at the beginning of the siman that from the Tur it implies that the head tefillin, whose sanctity is more severe, should be written first. The Perishah wrote that the Tur’s statement that each one is on its own parchment refers to the hand tefillin, and “on its own” means that he should not mix them, but should write each parsha in one column. If so, no one disagrees with Rabbi Yitzchak Aksandari. See the Taz, whose words follow the Bach. The Eliyah Rabbah writes that Rabbi Yitzchak Luria would write the head tefillin first, but we rule as the Rav writes in the gloss; after the fact there is no invalidation. Although writing out of order invalidates even after the fact, even for one letter, as explained in seif 23, because “they shall be” teaches that they must remain as written, nevertheless the precedence of hand tefillin before head tefillin is not derived from “they shall be,” but from the verse placing it first, and we therefore put them on in that order. Accordingly, after the fact it is valid; see Atzei Shittim, letter 3. If the parsha of Kadesh became invalid, the other three became invalid as well. If he has a parsha of Kadesh from other tefillin and knows that it was written before these, he may combine it with them; in a case of doubt it is invalid, as in Eliyah Rabbah, letter 1, and Beer Hagolah. This is correct, for a doubt in a Torah matter is ruled stringently, and it seems that the Baer Heitev did not write this way. Regarding doubt between hand tefillin and head tefillin, however, there is certainly no concern, for after the fact it is valid if the head tefillin were written first, as I wrote in letter 2 concerning this.
