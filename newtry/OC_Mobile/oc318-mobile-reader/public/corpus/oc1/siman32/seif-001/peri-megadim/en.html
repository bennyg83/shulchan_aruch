He shall write. Taz. And this is in Beit Yosef regarding "if one is missing there is no repair," in the name of R. Yitzchak of Alexandria; but Bach wrote at the beginning of the siman about mezuzah — it implies that shem reshut, whose sanctity is weightier, is written first. Perishah wrote what the Tur said: only on klaf alone does "asher yishkon" refer, and alone — that he not mix them but write each parasha on one column; if so there is no dispute with R"Y; see Taz — these are Bach's words. And in Ashkenaz responsa it says that Mahari of Luria practiced shem reshut first; and we rule as Rabbenu Yerucham wrote in the gloss: b'dieved it is not invalid even though lo k'sidran — b'dieved invalid even one letter, as explained in seif 23; and "they were in their being" — yud-hei; but shem Yisrael before shem reshut does not come out from "they were in their being" but from that he preceded Scripture (and we leave it thus) — shem Yisrael first is therefore kosher b'dieved. Peri Megadim, note 3: If parashat Kadesh was invalidated, three others are invalidated; and if he has parashat Kadesh from other tefillin and knows it was written before these, he may combine them; and from doubt — invalid. Eliyah Rabbah, note 1 — Erev HaGolah siman 60 and HaKein: surely doubt of Torah is strict; and as it seems in Be'er Heitev he did not write thus. Shem Yisrael and shem reshut from doubt — surely there is no concern, for b'dieved kosher shem reshut first; as cited in note 2 of this.
