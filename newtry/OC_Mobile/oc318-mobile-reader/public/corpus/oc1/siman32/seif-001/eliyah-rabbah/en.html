And if he changed, etc. For it is written, “and they shall be,” meaning that they must remain in their proper state. Malbushei Yom Tov wrote in the name of Derashos Maharash as follows: Therefore, if an invalidity is found in the fourth passage, he may write another one, but not so with the others; rather, he must write that passage and also the ones after it, end quote. If the first passage became invalid and he has an old one that he knows was written before these last three, he may join it to them, even though initially the last passages were not written for this joining. But in a case of doubt it is prohibited. So too wrote Responsa Avodas HaGershuni, siman 60. See the beginning of siman 34. I found written: one who writes tefillin, his ink, from which he earns a tropaik, is an istira, which is one-eighth of a Tyrian sela, from the margin of chapter HaSholeiach, end quote.
The hand tefillin first, etc. See this siman, seif 23, and siman 25, seif 6. The Bach wrote that the Tur’s view is that initially one should write the head tefillin first, and so ruled Sefer Mitzvos Shemurim, because the head tefillin has greater sanctity, and this was the practice of Mahari Luria. The Levush wrote: he should write in a straight script, as it is written, “This is my God and I will beautify Him,” meaning, beautify yourself before Him with mitzvos, end quote. That is to say, he should be careful that when he writes without scoring, the lines should be straight and not crooked. However, after the fact, if he wrote them crooked, the Bach wrote that we have no objection. Radbaz, siman 120, wrote that he should write Ashuris script, not Provençal script. The Perishah wrote that he should write a straight script, meaning that he should not write one word large and another small; “straight” means beautiful.
