And they must be written, etc. This gloss discusses priority in time. The matter of priority in placement is explained below in siman 34 in the Tur and Beis Yosef; see there.
In this order. See the Mishnah Berurah. This is from the Eliyah Rabbah, the Pri Megadim, and the other later authorities. The Taz in Yoreh Deah, siman 290, holds that according to Tosafos, Menachos 32a, s.v. dilma, it is valid if there is no order in the passages of Shema and Vehayah im shamoa, since they are not adjacent in the Torah. See there in the Nekudos HaKesef, who disagrees with him. I also saw in the book Kedushas Yom Tov that he likewise writes not like the Taz, and that there is no distinction regarding order between passages and letters. He writes that Tosafos holds that the intent of the Mechilta concerns the order of placement, that Vehayah ki yevi’acha should not be arranged on the parchment before Kadesh; but if it is arranged after Kadesh, even if it was written first, it is valid. We, however, do not rule leniently even in that manner, since it was written out of order, as is evident below in seif 23 and in Yoreh Deah, siman 288. From the words of the Gra in all these places it is also evident that he holds like the ruling of the Shulchan Aruch and not like Tosafos. See below in siman 64 in Biur Halacha, where with Hashem’s help we answered the Taz’s difficulty well.
And if he changed. Meaning, he wrote the later passage first, even if its writing was in its proper place, such as where he left part of one sheet blank and wrote the passage Vehayah ki yevi’acha on the second sheet, and afterward wrote the passage Kadesh on the first sheet; it is invalid.
