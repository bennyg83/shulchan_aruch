Sefer Kol Yaakov
Seif 1, The mitzvah of tefillin, etc., until “to its appointed time.” The Tur wrote until “to its appointed time, from days to days,” and so wrote the Levush, and Rabbeinu Zalman, letter 1, and that is the truth. And as to the two words “from days to days” not being written in the books of the Shulchan Aruch, Ruach Chaim, letter 1, wrote that it is a scribal error that fell into the books.
There, and the passage of Shema, etc. In Sefer HaKavvanos, in the discourses on the recitation of Shema, discourse 6, it is written in the name of the Arizal that in tefillin and mezuzah one must write this dalet of the word Echad large and thick, the size of four small dalets. Magen Avraham, s.k. 1, cites this. If one did not write this dalet so large as four dalets of that same script, but only wrote it larger than the other letters, it is sufficient so long as it contains the measure of four very small dalets. Pri Megadim, Eshel Avraham, letter 1. See our words in Yoreh De'ah, siman 274, letter 17, and siman 275, letter 24.
There in the gloss, and he must write them in this order, etc. There is no distinction between the hand tefillin and the head tefillin. So Maran ruled as halachah in Beis Yosef, unlike Hagahos Semak, who wrote in the name of Rabbeinu Yechiel that regarding head tefillin, since they are four parchments, one need not be particular about the order of their writing. Although Rashbash, siman 632, upheld the words of Hagahos Semak, the halachah is not so. Machazik Berachah, letter 2; Chikrei Lev, siman 7; Zecher LeAvraham, letter tav; Shaarei Teshuvah, letter 3; and so wrote Meleches Shamayim, rule 8, letter 1, in Chochmah; Keset HaSofer, siman 9, letter 1; and Imrei Shefer, rule 20, letter 1, in plain terms, without distinguishing between hand tefillin and head tefillin.
A scribe. If he wrote many passages for pairs of head tefillin at one time, and was careful to write them in order so that all four passages of each head-tefillin set would be in order, but afterward all the passages became mixed together, and it is not recognizable in any group of four that he takes from them whether those he took were written in order, for it may be that the first passage he took was written last relative to the three joined with it, and the like, they require burial. Machazik Berachah, letter 3; Shaarei Teshuvah, letter 2; Zecher LeAvraham in Imrei Shefer, rule 20, letter 1. One may not permit this by force of a double doubt, namely perhaps all four passages came to his hand in order, and even if not all four came in order, perhaps the halachah follows the view that we do not require the passages of head tefillin to be written in order. Since the passages became mixed, it is impossible that four passages written in order should come together, and the prohibition is more established than the permissibility. In such a case we do not make a double doubt, as the Shach writes in siman 110, in the laws of double doubt, letter 33.
If. If the last passage of head tefillin became spoiled, he writes another in its place. This is unlike where one of the earlier passages became spoiled, in which case he must write that passage and all the passages after it. Machazik Berachah, letter 4; Shaarei Teshuvah, letter 2; Zecher LeAvraham.
Tefillin. Tefillin that were torn are governed by the law of a Torah scroll that was torn. Darchei Moshe in the name of Kol Bo. See Yoreh De'ah, siman 280, se'if 1, and our words there, letter 1.
There, in this order, etc. If after he wrote them in order one or two of the first passages became invalid, and he knows clearly that he has other passages corresponding to those that became invalid, which were written before these latter passages, he may join them. This is so even though at the outset those passages were not written in order to be joined to these passages, but to other passages. But if there is doubt whether they were written earlier or not, it is invalid. Avodas HaGershuni, siman 60; Eliyah Rabbah, letter 1; Yad Aharon, Hagahos Tur; Pri Megadim, Mishbetzos Zahav, letter 1; Yeshuos Yaakov, letter 8; Shaarei Teshuvah, letter 2; Maamar Mordechai, letter 1; Meleches Shamayim, rule 8, letter 2, in Chochmah; Keset HaSofer, siman 9, letter 2, in Lishkas HaSofer; Imrei Shefer, rule 20, letter 1. Although Chikrei Lev in Orach Chaim, siman 7, wrote that there is room to challenge Avodas HaGershuni, nevertheless, since all the later authorities mentioned agree with him, this is how we rule.
There, and if he changed it, it is invalid. If he wrote a passage that appears earlier in the Torah last, for example he wrote the passage of Vehayah Ki Yeviacha before the passage of Kadesh, he may write additional passages that are later than it and join them to it, that is, he writes Vehayah Ki Yeviacha, Shema, and Vehayah Im Shamoa and joins them to Kadesh. Likewise, if he wrote later passages first, for example he wrote Vehayah Ki Yeviacha, Shema, and Vehayah Im Shamoa and afterward wrote Kadesh, he may join them to an earlier passage from other tefillin whose later passages became invalid. If he cannot join them, they require burial. GraZ, letter 1. Some say that if he reversed the passages, writing Vehayah Ki Yeviacha before Kadesh intending to place them in tefillin that way, even the passage of Kadesh is invalidated, and similarly with the second and third or third and fourth passages: from the reversal downward, all the passages are invalid and he can no longer join other passages to them. What was written before the invalidating act, however, such as where he wrote Kadesh and Vehayah Ki Yeviacha and then wrote Vehayah Im Shamoa before Shema, remains valid, and only from the reversal downward is invalidated. Chikrei Lev, Orach Chaim, siman 7; Zecher LeAvraham wrote this briefly. It follows from the rabbi's words that the same applies if an error was found in the first or second passage of tefillin: from the error downward is invalid, and one cannot join other earlier-written passages to the final passages, since the latter passages were written in invalidity; there is no distinction between reversing one passage with another and writing after the error, for in both cases it was written in invalidity. This is our practice here in Baghdad, may the Supreme One establish it, that if an error or invalidity is found in the first passage or in the first two, even if he has matching passages that were written earlier, we do not join them to the later passages because those later passages were written in invalidity. But if all four passages were written validly and afterward invalidity occurred in the first passage or in the first two, and he has corresponding passages that were written before the latter ones, they may be joined, as stated in the preceding letter. If one of the earlier passages of head tefillin became invalid through adhesion or separation, and we do not know whether this occurred at the time of writing or after all four passages had been written validly, one may permit joining earlier-written replacement passages to the later passages on account of a double doubt: perhaps the adhesion or separation occurred after all four passages had been written validly, and even if it occurred at the time of writing and the remaining passages were written in invalidity, perhaps the halachah follows the view that head-tefillin passages need not be written in order, as above, letter 3. This double doubt is reversible. In addition, perhaps the halachah follows GraZ. It appears that for hand tefillin as well one may permit them in such a case, for there is an opinion in Mordechai that even with hand tefillin, if they were not written in order, one need not be particular, and Beis Yosef copied it in siman 34, as did the later authorities there. Thus one can make a double doubt in such a case: perhaps the adhesion or separation occurred after all four passages were written validly, and even if it occurred at the time of writing and the remaining passages were written in invalidity, perhaps the halachah follows the view that hand-tefillin passages need not be written in order; furthermore, perhaps the halachah follows GraZ, who does not accept the concept that they were written in invalidity, as he wrote that where earlier passages in the Torah were written last, one may write other later passages and join them to it, and where later passages were written first, he may join them to an earlier passage from other tefillin whose later passages became invalid. This is specifically according to our custom. But in a place that has no custom, one may permit in all cases: whether all four passages were written validly and afterward invalidity occurred in them, whether an error was found in the earlier passages such that the later passages were written in invalidity, or whether the passages were reversed, for example he wrote Vehayah Ki Yeviacha before Kadesh and has a Kadesh passage written before these passages, he may join it to Vehayah Ki Yeviacha; and if Kadesh was written last, he may write the later passages and join them to it. When an error is found in the earlier passages and he has corresponding passages written before these tefillin, he may join them to the later passages by force of a double doubt: perhaps the halachah follows the view that writing in order is not required, and even if the halachah follows the view that it is required, perhaps the halachah follows GraZ, who permits joining in all cases. This double doubt is reversible. Furthermore, perhaps Avodas HaGershuni permits joining in all cases, and the later authorities cited above brought him, leaving Chikrei Lev alone in the matter. Although we do not establish the halachah according to those views, nevertheless for a double doubt we act as Rav HaGaon wrote in Zivchei Tzedek, part 2, siman 110, in the laws of double doubt, letter 158, that we make a double doubt even from a rejected opinion and even against Maran and Rama. However, where there is a custom one should not be lenient, for it is difficult to annul a custom. See Baer Heitev, siman 551, s.k. 7, siman 690, s.k. 15, and Shulchan Aruch, siman 114.
And if. If he wrote the passage of Vehayah Ki Yeviacha before the passage of Kadesh, then even according to our custom, by which we do not join where something was written in invalidity, nevertheless one may permit using the passage of Kadesh by writing the three later passages and joining them to it, since there are those who permit this. Pesach HaDevir, part 2, in Kuntres Acharon, siman 34, in the name of Adnei Paz in responsum, siman 46, was asked such a case and concluded to permit. Likewise, Torah Lishmah, manuscript, cited by the great gaon Rabbi Yosef Chaim in Rav Pealim, siman 4, considered this question and concluded to validate it from the words of Maran in Shulchan Aruch, siman 108, se'if 1, and from what Rabbi Shimshon wrote in Negaim, chapter 14, mishnah 10, and from Mahari Kurkus there; see there. The reason is that they consider this passage of Vehayah Ki Yeviacha as though it does not exist, and the passage of Kadesh remains valid, and therefore he may write the three final passages and join them to it. But the passage of Vehayah Ki Yeviacha may not have a previously written Kadesh passage joined to it, since it was written in invalidity; it is no different from where an error was found in the passage of Kadesh and afterward he wrote Vehayah Ki Yeviacha, in which case according to our custom we do not join to Vehayah Ki Yeviacha a Kadesh passage that had been written earlier, because when an error is found in the Kadesh passage, Vehayah Ki Yeviacha is considered as though it had been written earlier. Torah Lishmah there also wrote that the passage of Vehayah Ki Yeviacha is invalid because it was written in invalidity.
And if. If a scribe has a passage of Kadesh, or the passages of Kadesh and Vehayah Ki Yeviacha, and he wants to write four new tefillin passages, and before he begins writing he has in mind that if Kadesh, or Kadesh and Vehayah Ki Yeviacha, become invalid during writing and he notices only after completing all four passages, he will join those passages that he already has, which were written earlier, in their place, our custom is to be lenient. Since he stipulated concerning them from the beginning, the later passages are not considered written in invalidity.
There, ideally he should write the hand tefillin first, etc. Maran wrote likewise in Beis Yosef in the name of Maharia. But Bach wrote that the Tur's opinion is that he should first write the head tefillin and afterward the hand tefillin. Eliyah Rabbah, letter 2; Ateres Zekeinim on Levush, letter 1; and GraZ, letter 1, wrote likewise. So too, according to the kabbalah of the Arizal in Sefer HaKavvanos, in the discourses on tefillin, discourse 2: first one must write the head tefillin, complete their preparation, sew them, and blacken them; afterward he writes the hand tefillin, prepares them, and blackens them. Also. One must be careful to write all four passages of the head tefillin, or all four passages of the hand tefillin, consecutively, without any interruption between them whatsoever, and all the more so not in the middle of them. If he is elderly or ill and cannot write all four passages together, he should write the first three passages together and then write the fourth by itself. If this too is difficult for him, he should at least write the first two passages together and not interrupt between them in any way, and afterward write the other two passages together. If he wrote only the first two passages together, interrupted, and afterward comes to write the passage of Shema, he should first pass the quill over and write over the word Vayedaber in the passage of Kadesh and over the words Vehayah Ki Yeviacha in the second passage, and afterward write from the passage of Shema onward; through this it is considered somewhat joined. If at first he already wrote three passages, he should also pass the quill over the word Shema in the third passage, and afterward write the entire fourth passage together. Also. Before beginning to write, he should pray and say: May it be Your will, Hashem my God and God of my fathers, that Your Shechinah rest upon the work of my hands and that You grant me success in this writing, for I am writing these tefillin for the sake of the unification of the Holy One, blessed be He, and His Shechinah, for the sanctity of the mitzvah of tefillin which You, Hashem our God, commanded us; and save me from error in writing and from error in intention. And so may it be Your will. Then he should begin writing, end quote. See further below, siman 34, where we wrote a longer Yehi Ratzon in the name of Matza Shmurah; see there.
And Meleches Shamayim wrote. In rule 17, letter 13, he wrote: Nevertheless, it is obvious that this dispute concerns only enhancement at the outset, but all agree that whether he wrote the head tefillin first or the hand tefillin first, the tefillin are valid without any doubt, end quote. He further wrote there, letter 10, in Binah: Know that what Baer Heitev wrote in s.k. 3 in the name of Sefer HaKavvanos, that one should write all four passages of the head tefillin or of the hand tefillin consecutively and not interrupt with any speech at all, see there, is not a matter of law, but of piety; these are good and precious practices for a scribe fit for such matters, such as one who is careful, diligent, expert, strong, broadly knowledgeable in the laws of writing Torah scrolls, tefillin, and mezuzos, and complete in his fear of Heaven. But one who does not possess all these qualities, in my humble opinion, should be greatly concerned that by writing continuously without resting in between, he will become weary and fatigued and will ruin more than he improves. Therefore, in my humble opinion, it is better to pause during the writing to rest, so that he may gather strength to repair the work of Heaven according to halachah, and he will bear blessing from Hashem, end quote. Keset HaSofer, siman 20, letter 6, in Lishkas HaSofer, cited him and agreed with his words.
And what is written. Regarding what Sefer HaKavvanos wrote, that if he interrupted between passages he should pass the quill over the first word of each passage already written, Imrei Shefer, rule 20, letter 3, in Shulei HaYeri'ah, wrote in the name of Bigdei Yesha that one should not do so; see there for the reason. It appears that this is how one should rule. The reason is that even from the rabbi's own words it appears that there is not such an obligation to pass the quill again over the earlier passages, for he wrote that it is considered somewhat joined, implying that it is not a complete joining. In addition, there is concern lest by passing the quill over, a letter change into another letter, for example a reish become a dalet, and he then fix it, so that he will have written out of order; he will come to repair and will end up ruining, Heaven forbid. Passing the quill over tefillin requires great craftsmanship, for the letters are fine, and a person can easily stumble, Heaven forbid. Therefore it is preferable to sit and not act. Rather, the correction is that if an event occurred and he could not complete all four passages together, then if he knows how to direct the intentions in writing tefillin, he should return and direct the general intentions of the passages that were written earlier. For example, if he wrote two passages of the head tefillin and two remained, when he returns to write the two, he should again have in mind that the 31 verses in the four tefillin passages correspond to etc., and the 21 mentions of the Divine Name correspond to etc.; the first passage has ten verses corresponding to etc., the second passage corresponds to etc.; and he should again have in mind that the four passages correspond to the four letters of the Name Yud-Hei-Vav-Hei, the first passage corresponding to the yud of Yud-Hei-Vav-Hei and to 72 and 161, the second passage to the hei and to 63 and 143, etc. Through this all four passages will be joined together. See below, siman 34, the order of the intentions.
