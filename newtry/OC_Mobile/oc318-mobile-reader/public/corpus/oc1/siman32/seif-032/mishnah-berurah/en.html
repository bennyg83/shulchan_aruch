Above. Above the lines, the height of the roof of a lamed. It seems to me that this means the measure of medium writing, even if he writes only small script, for Menachos 31 says the measure is like the fullness of the atba of scribes, and the early authorities explained that this means enough to write the roof of the above-mentioned letter. If there were a distinction in this, where did they give its measure? One could, with difficulty, reject this and say that the Gemara speaks only of one writing medium script.
A final letter. And a bit more for surrounding blank parchment. The reason for all this is so that, when such letters occur, he will have room for them. Some say that above the roof of a lamed and below a final kaf or final nun, one must leave an additional half-fingernail. This is only ab initio.
At all. Only a small amount for their surrounding blank parchment.
A little. Meaning, slightly more than the measure needed for surrounding blank parchment. Some are stringent that ab initio there must be enough parchment at the beginning to roll the entire passage, as with a mezuzah.
A letter. A small letter, namely a yud. After the fact, one should not invalidate unless it appears to an average child as one continuous piece of writing.
A line. Some say this need be left only in a Torah scroll, and the scribes have so practiced, not being particular about this.
A hairbreadth. This is only ab initio. After the fact, one should not invalidate unless the word appears divided into two, as all this was explained in Yoreh Deah, siman 277, regarding a Torah scroll.
A little blank space. But Magen Avraham, in the name of several later authorities, and the Gra in his Biur, ruled that one need not leave more blank space than the space normally left between words in the middle of a verse, unlike what the Shach wrote in Yoreh Deah 274, seif katan 6; see there.
