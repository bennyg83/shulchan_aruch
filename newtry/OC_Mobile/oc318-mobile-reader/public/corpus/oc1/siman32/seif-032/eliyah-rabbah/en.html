The lamed, etc. It is surprising that he ruled leniently, not like Rabbeinu Yonah, who requires leaving the measure of a full itba above the height of the lamed. Every conscientious person should be stringent for himself and do so, as the Bach writes; the Taz likewise wondered about this. In Sefer Baruch She'amar I found the following: To me, Avraham, the common explanation does not appear correct, for they explain a full itba as the grip of a fingernail. Rather, I interpreted their explanation to mean that certainly one needs at least half a fingernail above for the neck of the lamed and below for the leg of the kaf and the like. Their explanation of the grip of a fingernail means above the tags of the lamed and below the legs of the kaf, so that it will be surrounded by blank parchment, end quote. So too Responsa Maharil, siman 149, wrote briefly in his name, and concluded that the work Baruch She'amar is authoritative.
And one must leave, etc. Olas Tamid wrote that it appears this does not invalidate after the fact. This is the language of Maharil, siman 179: since in a Torah scroll the reason one must leave between one word and another the measure of a full letter is only for beauty, and tefillin are covered, there is no concern. So too wrote Shiltei Giborim and Knesses HaGedolah, end quote. This is surprising, for I examined Maharil there, and also Shiltei Giborim and Knesses HaGedolah, and this law of spacing between one word and another is not mentioned there. Rather, they speak of the law of spacing between one line and another; see there. In any event, it appears as a matter of law that after the fact it is valid even if he did not leave between one letter and another the breadth of a hair, and between one word and another the measure of a full letter. However, if because of their closeness it appears as one word to the point that a child errs in it, it is invalid, as explained in Yoreh De'ah siman 274.
A little blank space, etc. Knesses HaGedolah wrote that it appears the rabbi, of blessed memory, learned this from Responsa Rivash, siman 286. This is surprising, for the Rivash, of blessed memory, wrote that after the fact it is not invalid, but not that one should leave a blank space ab initio. So too Sefer Zichronos wondered about the Rama, end quote. So ruled Magen Avraham. I also saw in Lechem Chamudos as follows: I do not know where he got this from. Also in Hilchos Sefer Torah, siman 274, the Rama did not write so, only that if he left such space, it is valid, end quote. But I merited to obtain Sefer Baruch She'amar and found that he wrote explicitly like the Rama, and there is no doubt that the Rama took it from him. From Hilchos Sefer Torah there is no difficulty, for there he says that if he left much space, as long as he did not leave the measure of a parashah it is valid; but he certainly must leave a small space between one verse and another, as the Shach wrote there. Also regarding the law of spacing between line and line, Knesses HaGedolah wrote there: I have not seen poskim who mentioned this. But I saw it there in Sefer Baruch She'amar, and there is no doubt that this book was not in the hands of the great authorities mentioned above. Although I wrote above in the name of Maharil that one need not leave space between line and line, he wrote there only regarding those who have that custom. For this is his language: if they do not customarily leave space between line and line, perhaps in a Torah scroll it is for beauty, etc., end quote. But as a matter of law it implies that he also holds that one must leave space. However, after the fact, one should not invalidate in all this.
