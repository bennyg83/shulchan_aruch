And none of them should extend out at all. The Beis Shmuel wrote that since the wording is doubled, it implies that even after the fact it is invalid, even with regard to the other Names that may not be erased; even a suffix attached to a Name from behind is invalid if it extends beyond the line. So it appears to me as halachah, though not for practice. This also appears from the rulings of Mahara Isserlein, siman 93, who wrote that since the wording is “it must,” this implies even after the fact; see there. Nevertheless, this requires study, for we find many places where “must” means only initially, as the Bach wrote, and see siman 219 and the first chapter of Gittin. Furthermore, the Shach in Yoreh Deah, siman 276, seif 7, wrote that some poskim validate where part of the Name was suspended above the line; if so, all the more so one might say this where it extended outside the line, since the prohibition of suspending part of the Name is mentioned in the Gemara, whereas this prohibition is not mentioned explicitly in the Gemara but only derives from Rabbeinu Tam. From where, then, do we know to invalidate after the fact? See what I wrote in seif 18 in the name of Rabbi Moshe Galanti. In Mishnas Binyamin, siman 57, he wrote that if one wrote “Elokeinu” without the yud, it is permitted to suspend the yud, since it is not part of the Name itself. If so, all the more should an attached suffix to the Name that extends outside the line be valid. This also appears from Maharik, shoresh 130. The gaon, wonder of our generation, our master Rabbi Yitzchak, Av Beis Din of the holy community of Pozna, ruled to erase all the upper lines and draw them out so that they be even with the Name, since where it can be corrected it is not called after the fact. This also appears from what is written in siman 39, seif 2, in the name of Rabbi Meir Lublin. But if there is a sacred Name in the upper lines which may not be erased, it is valid after the fact.
