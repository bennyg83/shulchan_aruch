On the Taz: these are the words of Beit Yosef in the name of the Nakdan. Orchot Chayyim (I found it written, apparently an error, for in Beit Yosef it is thus in the name of Orchot Chayyim, see there). And here is the reason for a shin of four heads: on the Tablets the letters were engraved through from one side to the other (mem and samekh by miracle), and in hollow letters one finds four creases — they are the four sides of the shin that were on the Tablets — and the three hollow spaces between the four creases are the form of a hollow shin that was on the Tablets. Therefore the Nakdan wrote well, as cited in Semag: one should make within it two yods — meaning the creases are yods and touch the edges of the shin below; then the hollow space, i.e. the three gaps within it that resemble the writing of the Tablets, does not contain the form of a shin unless the two middle creases do not touch the edges of the shin — then there is no break below and the three hollow spaces form a shin as is known. Therefore one need not make the two middle creases with yod-form at their heads, only simple lines, since the hollow space and gap between them is the shin-form like the Tablets. But there are other reasons for a shin of four heads; if so, the creases must contain an actual shin-form with yods and must reach precisely to the edge of the shin, for the creases are the shin-form, not hollow space. So too one should act; see Magen Avraham note 59 on this. And what he wrote about shin as days in the year — see Levush on this; and Eliyah Rabbah note 65: the year has 365 days and 52 Sabbaths and 4 days of Passover, 2 of Shavuot, 12 of Rosh Chodesh and Yom Kippur and Sukkot — 365 days, and shin days remain (meaning 300 also remain; and for the Mechaber in another place the reason is in Orchot Chayyim, Beit Shammai: m-tz-p-tz gematria "with mercy" — see Eliyah Rabbah siman 10).
