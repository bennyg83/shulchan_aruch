The Orchot Chaim, and I found written that at first glance this is a textual error, for in the Beit Yosef it appears this way in the name of the Orchot Chaim; see there. The reason for the shin with four heads is that on the Tablets the letters were engraved from side to side, with the mem and samech standing miraculously, and the letters were formed from empty space. It follows that the four folds are the four walls of the shin that were on the Tablets, and the three spaces between the four folds are the form of the shin made of air that was on the Tablets. According to this, the Nakdan properly wrote regarding what the Semag wrote, that one makes within it two yuds, which implies that the folds are yuds and touch the bottom of the shin below. It follows that the air, namely the three spaces within it, which corresponds to the writing of the Tablets, does not have the form of a shin unless the two middle folds do not touch the bottom of the shin. Then they do not interrupt below, and the three spaces have the known form of a shin. According to this, the two middle folds do not need to have the form of yuds at their heads, only simple lines, since the air and space between them is the form of the shin, like the Tablets. But there are other reasons for the four-headed shin, and according to them the folds themselves must have the form of an actual shin with yuds, and they must specifically reach the bottom place of the shin, for the folds are the form of the shin, not the air. This is the proper way to make it. See Magen Avraham, letter 59, concerning this. Regarding what is written about the days of the year, see Levush.
