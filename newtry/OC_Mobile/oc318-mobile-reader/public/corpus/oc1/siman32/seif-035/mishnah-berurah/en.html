Must, etc. Magen Avraham, in the name of the gaon Rabbi Yitzchak of Pozna, and likewise Olas Tamid, Eliyah Rabbah, and several later authorities, conclude that this is specifically ab initio; after the fact one should not be stringent. Nevertheless, if this happened in a Torah scroll, he ruled that one should erase all the upper lines, if they contain no sacred Name that may not be erased, and extend them so that they are even with the Name, for since it can be repaired it is not considered after the fact. But if there was above it some sacred Name, or in tefillin and mezuzos where one cannot erase because of writing out of order, it is valid as it is without correction. Some are stringent even after the fact, and Rabbi Akiva Eiger and Derech HaChaim agreed with them. However, if in the upper lines the last letter is a beis, dalet, reish, or the like, which can be extended, all agree that whether in a Torah scroll or in tefillin and mezuzos, one should extend those letters so that they are even with the letter of the Name that protruded outside the line, and there is no issue of writing out of order. Similarly, if he has written only one line so far, and in the second line the Name extended outside the line, he should make other scoring lines and extend them so that they reach the end of the Name, and there is no concern because of the first line. Know further that regarding letters attached to the Divine Name, the later authorities agree that one should not be stringent after the fact if they protrude outside the line.

This is not comparable to one letter going outside the line, because what goes outside is considered like an insertion, and we rule in Yoreh Deah 276 that one may not insert part of the Name. This case is different. So wrote Bnei Yonah. Rabbi Akiva Eiger, in his novellae to this siman, is stringent even in this case; see there. But the Biur of the Gra explicitly proves like Bnei Yonah, to be lenient.
