(152) The letters of the Name — even the other Names that are not erased.

(153) One must, etc. — The Magen Avraham in the name of the Gaon Maharar Yitzchak of Pozna [and likewise Or Torah and Eliyah Rabbah and several Acharonim] concludes that this applies strictly l'chatchila, but b'dieved one need not be strict. Nevertheless, if it occurred in a sefer Torah, he ruled to erase all the upper rows if they contain no Name from among the Names that are not erased, and to extend them so they are even with the Name — for since it is possible to fix, it is not called b'dieved. But if there was some Name above, or in tefillin and mezuzot where it is impossible to erase because of "not in order," they are kosher as-is without repair. And there are those who are strict even b'dieved, and the Gra and the Chida agreed with them. However, if in the upper rows the final letter is bet, dalet, resh, or the like, which can be extended — all agree that whether in a sefer Torah or in tefillin and mezuzot, one should extend them so they are even with the letter of the Name that protruded outside the line, and this is not an issue of "not in order." And likewise, if he wrote only one more row and in the second row extended the Name outside the line, he should make other ruling lines and extend them until the end of the Name; and for the first row there is no concern. And know further that regarding letters attached to the Name, the Acharonim agree that one need not be strict b'dieved regarding what protrudes outside the line.

(154) From them at all — even one letter; and it is possible that even most of it is as the whole, but regarding part of a letter we have no concern. And if the entire Name came out outside the margin of the column, it is kosher b'dieved, and it is not comparable to one letter protruding outside the line — for what protrudes outward is considered as hanging, and we rule in Yoreh De'ah siman 276 that part of a Name cannot be hung, unlike here. So wrote Bnei Yona, and the Gra in his chiddushim in this siman is strict in this as well — see there; but in Biur HaGra it is proven explicitly like Bnei Yona to be lenient.
