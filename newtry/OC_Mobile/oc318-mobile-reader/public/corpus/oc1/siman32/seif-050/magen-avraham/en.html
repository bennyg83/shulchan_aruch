One should not buy, etc. This is the wording of the Beis Yosef in the name of the Agur: one should not buy sinews from non-Jews, for they also take them from non-kosher animals, and this is a Torah-level doubt, and anything fixed is treated as half and half. Regarding what the Olas Tamid wrote, that there is no need for the rule of fixed, because most animals are non-kosher, one can answer that, in any event, most animals that are eaten are kosher, and from those that are not eaten one does not take sinews, for a non-Jew certainly does not slaughter his animal merely for the sinews; carcasses and tereifos are a minority. It seems to me that this applies specifically when it is known that some make them from non-kosher animals, in which case it is fixed; but ordinarily we follow the majority, as written in Yoreh Deah at the beginning of siman 86, that one may buy eggs from a non-Jew and need not be concerned that they came from a carcass or tereifah, and this is an explicit Talmudic ruling in Chullin. Even where it is known that some make them from non-kosher animals, it is only forbidden to buy in their homes, but in the marketplace it is permitted to buy, for anything separated is assumed to have separated from the majority, as written in Yoreh Deah siman 114; see there. This is also in Kesubos at the end of the first chapter, and see above siman 513 in the Beis Yosef. It seems to me there is another reason why it is forbidden to buy from a non-Jew: since these are a halachah transmitted to Moshe from Sinai, they require spinning for the sake of the mitzvah, as written in siman 33, seif 4, and non-Jews are not fit for acting for the sake of the mitzvah.
