And one must leave, etc. Olas Tamid wrote that it appears this does not invalidate after the fact. This is the language of Maharil, siman 179: since in a Torah scroll the reason one must leave between one word and another the measure of a full letter is only for beauty, and tefillin are covered, there is no concern. So too wrote Shiltei Giborim and Knesses HaGedolah, end quote. This is surprising, for I examined Maharil there, and also Shiltei Giborim and Knesses HaGedolah, and this law of spacing between one word and another is not mentioned there. Rather, they speak of the law of spacing between one line and another; see there. In any event, it appears as a matter of law that after the fact it is valid even if he did not leave between one letter and another the breadth of a hair, and between one word and another the measure of a full letter. However, if because of their closeness it appears as one word to the point that a child errs in it, it is invalid, as explained in Yoreh De'ah siman 274.
