There. It emerges that the first three passages, according to the Rambam. From what he writes here, 'according to the Rambam,' and from what he writes afterward, that it is like Bereishis, which is open according to the Rambam, and afterward, regarding the two following passages, he writes, 'and this is so both according to the Rosh,' etc., it implies that the first passage is open only according to the Rambam. For according to the Rambam, there is no closed passage except in the middle of a line, and no open passage except at the beginning of a line. But according to the Rosh, there is no sign of openness in the first passage. On the contrary, if we wished to satisfy the opinion of the Rosh, of blessed memory, it would be fitting to begin it in the middle of a line, for that is the sign of an open passage according to the Rosh. Yet it is impossible to do so, because according to the Rambam that would make it completely closed. Therefore we begin at the beginning of a line, making it open according to the Rambam but not according to the Rosh. However, the Beis Yosef in the name of Mahari Abuhav writes that even according to the Rosh it is open; see there. One must say that when the Taz writes here 'according to the Rambam,' he means that this is found explicitly in the Rambam: that the first passage, like Bereishis, has no concern even though there is no blank space before it, as Mahari Abuhav proved from his words. This law is not found in the Rosh, but nevertheless we can say that the Rosh also agrees. Therefore we begin at the start of a line and satisfy both opinions, whereas if we began in the middle of a line we would not satisfy the Rambam. What he writes afterward, 'and this is so both according to the Rambam and according to the Rosh,' means that this point is explicit in the words of both of them, that it constitutes an opening. By contrast, regarding the first passage it is not explicit that it is open except in the Rambam. Examine this carefully.
