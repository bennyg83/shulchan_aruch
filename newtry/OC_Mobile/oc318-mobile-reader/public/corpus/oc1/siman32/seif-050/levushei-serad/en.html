It is found that the first three parashiyot, per the Rambam from what is stated here in the Rambam's view and likewise afterward as in Bereishit — it is open per the Rambam — and afterward in the two parashiyot that follow he wrote "and this is" both for the Rambam and for the Rosh; it implies that the first parashah is not open except for the Rambam, because for him there is no closed except in the middle of a line and no open except at the head of a line, unlike the Rosh — in the first there is no sign of open; on the contrary, if we came to satisfy the view of the Rosh, may the memory of the righteous be a blessing, it would have been fitting to begin in the middle of a line, which is a sign of open for the Rosh; but the Rama did so because for the Rambam it would be entirely closed, therefore they begin at the head of a line and it is open for the Rambam and not for the Rosh. However, in Beit Yosef in the name of Mahari Abohav he wrote that also for the Rosh it is open — see there. And it should be that what the Taz stated here per the Rambam means that thus it is explained in the Rambam that the first parashah, as in Bereishit, has no stringency even though no part precedes it, as Mahari Abohav proved from his words; and in the Rosh we do not find this law — nevertheless we can say the Rosh also agrees, therefore they begin at the head of a line and satisfy both; for if they began in the middle of a line they would not satisfy the Rambam. And what he stated afterward, "and this is" both for the Rambam and for the Rosh, means that this is explained in the words of both of them — that it is an opening; unlike in the first parashah, it is not explained explicitly that it is open except for the Rambam — examine this:
