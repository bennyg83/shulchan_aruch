[33] If the leg of the heh touched, etc. Even by a hair's breadth very thin, even though the child knows it is heh — thus wrote Radbaz. Olat Tamid wrote: if he wrote ayin instead of vav — if he made the vav initially and afterward the circle around it, he drags the circle and the vav remains; but in the reverse case he must drag all of it — so far. And it seems to me this compares to what the Levush wrote in this seif: initially he wrote the roof and leg, etc., and afterward erred, etc. Magen Avraham wrote: it seems to me specifically when the yod itself touched the aleph; but if the left tip of the yod touches the aleph it is kosher, as written in Even HaEzer siman 125 seif 16, for even without the tip it is called yod — so far. And from what the Levush wrote "he drags the entire yod," this requires investigation on what Magen Avraham wrote that he drags all he wrote in that letter after making the touch. I was asked regarding a Torah scroll where the upper yod of Elohim was found touching the aleph of Elohim — may one drag the yod? Answer: one may say it compares to what the Rama wrote in Yoreh De'ah siman 276 regarding a heh of a divine name whose leg touches its roof slightly — there is room to doubt in the matter. The reason in Beit Yosef and Shach: although invalid in any event, since people call it heh it seems like erasing the Name — if so, the same law here with aleph, even though the yod literally touches; everywhere people call it aleph. But I found another correction: add ink above on the yod until below is considered the yod's leg — scribes say sometimes the yod becomes so thick; proof from Taz s.k. 16 that there is correction by adding ink; see there.
