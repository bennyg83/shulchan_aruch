Their square was ruined — See Mishnah Berurah. Know that regarding the definition of ruining the square of the housings, it is clear to me that we do not require that they be ruined over the majority of their height — even in a minority suffices. Even if he made them very large, we do not say that from here is a leniency and the measure of tefillin will remain without this — unlike those who are lenient regarding wrapping gevil if there is letter measure without the wrap; I would say this from what we hold in Zevachim 62a, that the altar's square is required, and we also hold that even a defect in the altar disqualifies, and this is because of square, as in Tosefta Sukkah 49a, s.v. shekhol — we do not require specifically that the majority of the altar be damaged, but even by a k'zayit or a tefach, each according to his view in Chullin 18a; see Chullin 18a in the Gemara. One could say that in that case it is because the altar has a measure, which is not so here, for tefillin height has no required measure — as the Gemara states regarding the altar height. If so, if he had not made the height except up to above the place of the surround and there made the horns too, he would fulfill bedi'eved; nevertheless, since now he built at this full height, we require that above be square, as proven in Zevachim 54d, in the passage beginning "Rav said"; if so, the same applies in our matter regarding tefillin. It is also clear to me that it is not specifically if it was ruined at the tip of the corner above — the same applies if it was ruined and damaged elsewhere on the outer face of the housing until its square was lost thereby; also from that altar, it is plain that disqualifying a defect in the altar applies anywhere on its sides, not specifically at its tip. Therefore, if because of the great wear of the tefillin it is evident to all that one of its sides has sunk inward and does not stand in its proper place, it appears they must be repaired. It is also clear to me that through a small defect in the housings, of the measure of a fingernail groove — and possibly even slightly more than that — the square is not lost thereby; even if we say that at the time of making the scribe must be careful even regarding the slightest lack in any place, for in any case it is not perfectly square — nevertheless, bedi'eved, when it was ruined, the square is not lost through a slight lack. Proof from what is answered in Chullin 18 — "this with stone, this with lime": with stone, for it is written "whole stones," it disqualifies even with a fingernail groove; but with lime, for "whole" is not written regarding it, therefore we require that a significant measure be lacking, like a tefach or a k'zayit — see there. Behold, even regarding the altar, where we hold square is required and there too complete square is needed so that it be equal even on its diagonal, as proven there in Zevachim 62b from "around," that all around be equal, as Rashi explains there — nevertheless it is kosher with lime; rather, necessarily, through a defect like a fingernail groove the square is not lost thereby. Even if the small defect in the tefillin was at the tip of the corner above — otherwise the Tosefta should have taught "this and this with lime," and here it deals with its tip. Even though some disagree that regarding the altar, which is large in its measure, a slight defect is not considered to remove the square thereby, unlike in our matter — nevertheless it seems to me the law is with me; otherwise Rosh would still consider there a defect that disqualifies with a fingernail groove, such as in the square of tefillin, and similarly Taz wrote in siman 285, se'if 4; see there. For I require study as to the very measure of ruining the square — how much is called ruining in our matter; this is clear, that we cannot say regarding tefillin specifically that only if the lack of square was a tefach, as Rambam ruled there regarding the altar, for all tefillin do not hold that much; and perhaps if the square of the tefillin was pressed in slightly more than a fingernail groove, in our matter too it is called ruining the square — requires study.

That it is required — See Mishnah Berurah regarding warping of the titura; I copied from Lishkat HaSofer, and he brought proof for this — Rashi explained in Menachot 35a, "tefillin shall be square in their stitching," meaning that the sewing thread should not be drawn much so that it not shrink and diminish the square; see there. What difference is there whether it warped because of the stitching or because of the titura itself? Even though there is some room to disagree on this — so far.
