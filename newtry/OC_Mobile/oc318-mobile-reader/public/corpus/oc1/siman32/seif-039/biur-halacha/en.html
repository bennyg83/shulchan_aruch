That they should be square. See the Mishnah Berurah, which is from Yeshuos Yaakov. In my humble opinion, however, this ruling requires great study. He first copied the words of Olas Tamid and Eliyah Rabbah, who wrote that when no other tefillin are available, after the fact one should be stringent only regarding the stitching if it is not square, but not regarding the compartments. He wondered about them, saying that even regarding stitching that is not square, it is better to put them on than to completely forgo the mitzvah of tefillin. In truth, it is obvious that they too mean that he should put them on and thereby fulfill, at least according to Rashi; see the Rosh in Hilchos Ketanos. Rather, one who studies Olas Tamid will see explicitly that if the compartments are not properly square and there are no other tefillin, one may recite a blessing on them; but regarding the stitching, he should put them on without a blessing. See Hagahos Baruch SheAmar, who also wrote that if the compartments are not perfectly square, including a perfect diagonal, nevertheless they should not be invalidated; his language implies that one may recite a blessing on them. Practically, however, it appears to me that one should not rely on this regarding a blessing, and the law is with Yeshuos Yaakov, since many Rishonim hold that the halachah was stated also regarding the compartments: Shimusha Rabba, the Rif, though the Rif said this only regarding head tefillin according to Mordechai, while Sefer HaTerumah explains the Rif differently and agrees that even according to the Rif there is no distinction between hand tefillin and head tefillin; the Rambam, the Rosh, Sefer HaTerumah, and several other poskim. See what we will write nearby regarding a slight spoiling of the square.
In their stitching. See the Pri Megadim, who remains in requires study because of Rashi's opinion, and he also joins the Rambam's opinion to this. In truth, one who studies chapter 3, halachah 1 and halachah 10, will see explicitly that the Rambam's opinion is that square stitching is a halachah leMoshe miSinai. Perhaps it refers only to the squareness of the titura, but that is very forced. In Biur HaGra he also wrote explicitly that according to all the Geonim, the squareness of the titura and of the stitching is a halachah leMoshe miSinai. Therefore one should not depart from what Yeshuos Yaakov wrote, that in pressing circumstances he should put them on without a blessing.
One need not be particular. For squareness was stated only regarding length and width.
Their squareness was spoiled. See the Mishnah Berurah. Know that the boundary for spoiling the squareness of the compartments is obvious to me: we do not require that most of their height be spoiled; even a minority suffices, even if he made them very large. We do not say: remove the excess from here and the required measure of tefillin remains without it, similar to those who are lenient regarding surrounding blank parchment when there is enough of the letter without it. I say this from what we rule in Zevachim 62a, that the squareness of the altar is indispensable, and we also rule that even a nick in the altar invalidates it, because of squareness, as Tosafos in Sukkah 49a, under "for all," writes. We do not require specifically that most of the altar be nicked, but even an olive-bulk or a handbreadth, each master according to his view, as in Chullin 18a; see the Gemara there. One should not say that there the reason is because the altar has a fixed measure, unlike tefillin whose height has no measure, for this is not so. There too we rule that the measure of its height is not indispensable, as stated in that Gemara. If so, if he had made its height only up to above the place of the surrounding ledge and made the horns there as well, he would have fulfilled after the fact; nevertheless, once he built it to this height, its top must be square, as is proven in Zevachim 54a, Tosafos under "Rav said." The same applies in our matter regarding tefillin. It is also obvious to me that this is not limited to a spoilage at the upper corner-point; the same applies if it was spoiled or nicked elsewhere on the outer walls of the compartment, so that its squareness was lost thereby. This too follows from the above matter of the altar, for it is obvious that an invalidating nick in the altar applies anywhere in its walls, not only at a corner-point. Accordingly, if because of the tefillin's great age it is evident to all that one of its walls has sunk inward and does not stand in its proper place, it appears that they must be repaired. It is also obvious to me that a small nick formed in the compartments, the size of a fingernail-catching nick, and perhaps even somewhat more, does not thereby remove the name of square from them. Even if we say that at the time of making, the scribe must be careful about even the slightest deficiency anywhere, for at any rate it is not perfectly square, nevertheless after the fact, once it was spoiled, the name of square is not lost because of the slightest deficiency. The proof is from Chullin 18, where the Gemara answers: this is with stone, that is with lime. With stone, since the verse says "whole stones," even a fingernail-catching nick invalidates; but with lime, where "whole" is not written, a significant deficiency such as a handbreadth or olive-bulk is required; see there. Thus even regarding the altar, where we rule that squareness is indispensable and where it must be fully square, equal even in its diagonal, as proven in Zevachim 62b from "around," meaning all its surroundings must be equal, as Rashi explains there, nevertheless it is valid with lime. Therefore, a fingernail-catching nick does not cause it to lose the name square. This is so even if the small nick in the tefillin occurred at the upper corner-point; otherwise the Gemara could have answered that both cases were with lime, and here it speaks at the corner-point. Although one could distinguish that regarding the large altar a minor nick is not considered sufficient to remove its status as square, unlike in our matter, nevertheless in my humble opinion the law is as I have stated. Otherwise, Rav Chisda there should have listed another nick that invalidates at the size of a fingernail-catching nick, namely the square of tefillin, similar to what the Taz wrote in siman 645, se'if 4; see there. What remains uncertain to me is the actual measure of spoilage of the square, how much is called spoilage in our matter. It is clear that regarding tefillin we cannot require that the deficiency of the square be a handbreadth, as the Rambam ruled there regarding the altar, for the entire tefillin do not have that size. Perhaps if the square of the tefillin became crushed slightly more than a fingernail-catching nick, in our matter this is also called spoilage of the square; this requires study.
