(173) That they be square — and if he did not make them square it prevents b'dieved [Rambam chapter 3 of Laws of Tefillin, see there]. Nevertheless if he has no other tefillin he puts them on for the interim without a berachah; and when an opportunity arises for other tefillin he puts those on; see Biur Halachah:

(174) That their squaring — of the stitching; and it seems simple that the stitching must be square both above and below; and even though for me it is not clear regarding b'dieved, nevertheless l'chatchila certainly one must be careful about this:

(175) So that it is, etc. — meaning a perfect square whose length is exactly equal to its width; the Sages measured that every measure that an object has in length, it has on the diagonal an additional two-fifths of that measure; here too it must be an actual square, but if it is not an actual square it will not have on its diagonal the measure of two-fifths additional but a different measure. Therefore we measure it with four lines and know whether it is square — namely, first one measures one line in length and one line in width that they be equal; but lest it be equal in its middle and taper on its sides, therefore one measures it further with two more lines on its diagonals that the two diagonal lines too be equal:

(176) And one must square, etc. — and this too is from halacha leMoshe miSinai per the majority of poskim. And this too must be a perfect square as in the stitching; and even though an actual square in the craft of the batim — namely that it be in the utmost reduction — is nearly impossible, nevertheless as much as one can do in this matter one is certainly obligated to do; see Aruch HaShulchan:

(177) Their base — and that is the titura, namely that he cut the ma'avarta from both its sides so that the square of the titura is recognizable, as below in s.k. 294. One must be very careful about this, for all this prevents even b'dieved; and one must also be careful about the stitching, for because scribes make holes somewhat large, the thread is pulled to the sides and thereby the stitches are not equal — one goes in and one comes out. The titura must be square both from above and from below; and now, alas, many do not watch over their tefillin that they be square according to law, even those exacting in mitzvot — there are those who watch only that the tops of the batim be square and do not watch over the titura and the stitching, which is also fundamental to the law and is something easy to correct:

(178) And also the batim — not as some practice, making shel yad rounded at the top, only that they square the titura below; rather both shel yad and shel rosh must be square. The intent regarding shel rosh is when the four batim together are square and not each one by itself. Squaring of the batim must be throughout their entire height; and the squaring must be by the batim themselves and not by something else hammered onto them; see below s.k. 285:

(179) If their squaredness was ruined — such as when the batim became bent, one facing east and one west, or the corners of the batim were completely ruined until they became round, or the titura or stitching was ruined until their squaredness was lost:

(180) Some say — there is no dispute about this; and the author's way is thus in many places, as we wrote above in s.k. 123:

(181) One must square them — for since their squaring is halacha leMoshe miSinai, at all times we require that they be square; nevertheless one need not measure them every day if their square remains — we leave them in their presumption unless he sees they were ruined; and if the titura became bent — even though in essence it is square, only because of the bending of the titura it appears as though it is not square — he returns and fixes it:
