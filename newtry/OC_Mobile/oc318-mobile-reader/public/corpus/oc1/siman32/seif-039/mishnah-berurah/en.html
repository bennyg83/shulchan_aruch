(173) That they be square - If he did not make them square, it invalidates even after the fact, as Rambam writes in chapter 3 of Hilchot Tefillin, see there. Nevertheless, if he has no other tefillin, he should put them on for the time being without a blessing; when other tefillin become available, he should put them on. See Biur Halachah.
(174) That their squareness - This refers to the stitches. It appears simple to me that the stitches must be square both above and below. Although I am not certain regarding after the fact, initially one must certainly be careful about this.
(175) So that it be, etc. - Meaning, with a complete square, whose length is exactly like its width, the Sages estimated that whatever measure it has, its diagonal has two fifths more than that measure. Here too it must be an actual square; if it is not an actual square, its diagonal will not have the measure of an additional two fifths, but another measure. Therefore one should measure it with four lines and know whether it is square: first measure one line lengthwise and one widthwise to be equal; but perhaps it is equal in the middle and narrows at the sides, so he should measure also the two diagonal lines, so that the two diagonal lines are also equal.
(176) And one must square, etc. - This too is a halachah leMoshe miSinai according to most poskim. This too requires a complete square, as with the stitching. Although an exact square in the making of the boxes, namely with ultimate precision, is almost impossible, nevertheless whatever a person can do in this matter he is certainly obligated to do. See Eshel Avraham.
(177) Their base - This is the titura; that is, he should cut the ma'avarta on its two sides so the squareness of the titura will be recognizable, as below in seif 44. One must pay great attention to this, for all of this invalidates even after the fact. One must also pay attention to the stitching, for because scribes make the holes somewhat large, the thread is drawn to the sides, and thereby the stitches are not equal in a square: this one enters and that one exits. The titura must be square both above and below. Now, because of our many sins, many do not watch their tefillin to ensure they are square according to law; even among those meticulous in mitzvot, some watch only the top of the boxes to be square and do not watch the titura and the stitches, which are also of the essence of the law, and it is an easy thing to fix.
(178) And also the boxes - Not like some who practice making the hand-tefillin round at the top while squaring only the titura below; rather, both the hand-tefillin and head-tefillin must be square. With the head-tefillin, the intent is that the four boxes together be square, not each one by itself. The squareness of the boxes must be along their full height, and the squareness must be through the boxes themselves, not through something else plastered over them. See below seif katan 185.
(179) Their squareness was damaged - Such as if the boxes became bent, one facing east and one west, or the corners of the boxes were entirely damaged until they became round, or the titura or stitching was damaged until they lost their squareness.
(180) There is one who says - No one disagrees with this; this is the Mechaber's way in many places, as we wrote above in seif katan 123.
(181) That one must square them - Since their squareness is a halachah leMoshe miSinai, at all times we require them to be square. Nevertheless, one need not measure them every day to see whether their squareness remains; we maintain them on their established status unless he sees they became damaged. If the titura became bent, even though in itself it is square, but because of the bending of the titura it appears not square, he should go back and fix it.
(182) And he should make - After the fact, even if one is wider than the other, it is kosher.
