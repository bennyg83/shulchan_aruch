All of them. See Taz. See below, seif 38, and Taz 32 and seif 47. The essential rule for hand tefillin is that one letter, one compartment, even after the fact, is indispensable. I do not know whether, if he placed them in four compartments, this is valid; see siman 42. Regarding the parshiyos, the mitzvah initially is that they be on one parchment, just as it is one letter on the outside so it is one unit within. After the fact it is valid, and the practice is to paste them together as in seif 47. For head tefillin, four compartments are required because of the word “totafos,” and this will be explained below with the help of Heaven.
And what is written. The Shulchan Aruch writes in seif 3 that if he cast gold over it, he may pass ink over it. Although so long as he has not passed ink over it, it is invalid, this is not considered out of order, since he is not writing but only passing over it, and the lower writing remains automatically. In Eliyah Rabbah 5 he wrote that if one wrote with ink, cast gold over it, and then covered it with ink, it is nevertheless invalid, for otherwise there would be a remedy for Divine Names; see there. One can say that when he passes ink over part of a letter and has not yet completed it, this too is like erasing. What the Nimukei Yosef, cited by the Beit Yosef, wrote regarding casting gold over Divine Names that were written over ink, and the Beit Yosef wrote that this is why he specifically mentioned Divine Names, should not be challenged by asking how he knew this, for he mentioned Divine Names because with other letters one may pass ink over them. One can answer that the Nimukei Yosef did not write this because of the difficulty, for it can be said that the statement refers to a Torah scroll, as Rashi explains in Shabbos 103b, s.v. “if he wrote it, it is valid,” and there other letters can be corrected by scraping and rewriting, whereas for Divine Names this is forbidden. See the Bach here. See Levush, Yoreh Deah 277:5, and the Shach there, seif katan 6. It can already be resolved that there is an omission in the wording there, for with other letters scraping helps. Also, what is written there that if he scraped and wrote in ink, this refers to writing in gold, unlike where he cast gold over it and passed ink over it, where the ink remains, as stated here. I later saw that Eliyah Rabbah here, letters 6 and 7, sensed this; see there. According to this, in tefillin, if he cast gold there is a remedy, not writing. Now, what is written here that it is like erasing the Divine Name, it is not actual erasure, since the lower writing remains. It implies from here that even if he cast gold not with the sanctity of the Divine Name, it is forbidden to erase, unlike the Shach.
