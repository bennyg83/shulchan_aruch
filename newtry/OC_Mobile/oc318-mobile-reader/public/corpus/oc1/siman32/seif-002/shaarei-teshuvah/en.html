In this order. See Baer Heitev. What he wrote in the name of Olas HaGolah means that he had two parshiyos that he knew were written before the later ones that remained. One might have thought that since they were not written in order to be joined with these, they are invalid to join; therefore he teaches that we are not concerned for this. Even one witness is believed to say that he knows these were written earlier. But if he is uncertain whether they were written earlier or later, Heaven forbid that such a thing be done in Israel to join them together; see there and Eliyah Rabbah. What Baer Heitev wrote to see Responsa Devar Shmuel, siman 302, is not as Leket HaKemach understood, for Devar Shmuel there is stringent only where he found one of the earlier parshiyos invalid and wrote another after it. According to the later authorities, if a scribe wrote several parshiyos for tefillin shel rosh together and they became mixed so that he may take an earlier parsha written later than three others, they require genizah. If the last parsha was ruined, he writes another in its place; if an earlier parsha was ruined, he must rewrite it and all the parshiyos written after it.
