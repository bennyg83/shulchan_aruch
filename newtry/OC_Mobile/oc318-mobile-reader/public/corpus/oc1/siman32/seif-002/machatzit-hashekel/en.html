(Seif katan 2) From soot, etc. And in Yoreh De'ah he also wrote that initially, etc., from something that comes from a tree. Meaning, Beis Yosef wrote there in siman 271: A Torah scroll must be written with ink made from the soot of substances soaked in gallnut water. On this the Rama wrote there: Initially, it is proper to be careful not to make ink except from things that come from a tree. But surely soot from oils and gallnuts are things that come from a tree. It must be that the Rama meant to exclude the notion that one must specifically use soot from oils; the main concern is only that the ingredients come from a tree. This contradicts what the Rama wrote here. And in chapter 2 of Shabbos it implies that they made ink from the soot of oils. The Talmud there, 23a, states: Rabbi Shimon bar Zutri taught: All oils are suitable for ink, and olive oil is the choicest. Rabbi Shmuel bar Zutri taught it this way: All soots are suitable for ink, and the soot of olive oil is the choicest. In any case, it appears that oils and their soot are equivalent regarding ink.
