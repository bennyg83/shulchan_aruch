Except. See Taz. He added the words “what is the reason” and that it is invalid if he made Ve-hayah im shamoa open. In the Beit Yosef it says only, “what is the reason, if he changed it, it is invalid.” For Shema being closed, everyone agrees that it is invalid, even though it is not adjacent to Ve-hayah ki yeviacha, since the parsha before it, according to beis din, does not make Shema open, but rather its beginning at the head of the line does, according to the Rambam’s method, which is the primary one, as I will write in letter 26 with the help of Heaven. Accordingly, in tefillin as well it is proper that it be open. A mezuzah too, where closed parshiyos are valid, refers only to Ve-hayah im shamoa; see Maggid Mishneh, laws of tefillin 5:2, and Yoreh Deah 288:13. Regarding what he wrote to answer from Menachos 32a, you will see that the master’s words are founded on pillars of fine gold, as I will explain. There, on 32b, the Gemara asks: are we allowed to lower sanctity and make them into a mezuzah? But a mezuzah requires scoring. It would be forced to answer that this is not according to halacha. Yet a Torah scroll also requires scoring, as the Rambam writes in laws of Torah scroll 1:12, even if this is a halacha given to Moshe at Sinai. Let it say that the reason it mentioned “one may not lower sanctity” is because that reason applies equally to both; see Tosafot, s.v. “but.” Rather, the questioner is necessarily asking from a Torah scroll and tefillin to Ve-hayah im shamoa, that here it is closed and here it is open. Rashi explains, s.v. “here,” that this is in a Torah scroll, and there in a mezuzah, to remove the Taz’s question from a Torah scroll. How was the questioner satisfied, since Shema and Ve-hayah im shamoa are far apart, one in Vaeschanan and one in Eikev? See Tosafot, Shabbos 79b, s.v. “but,” and Menachos 32a, s.v. “perhaps.” One must say that he thought Ve-hayah im shamoa was at the beginning of a column, and that one writes Shema; necessarily writing parshiyos out of order is valid, but words are invalid. See Taz, Yoreh Deah 288:3 and 290:1. The answerer replies only “to complete,” meaning that it refers only to Shema at the end of a column and completing it. Or, as Tosafot wrote, according to Rashi’s explanation, that one sews them together and it is valid. According to the Rambam, however, both of these do not apply, for in chapter 8, halacha 1, he writes that if he sewed them or advanced one parsha before another, it is invalid. Necessarily, the questioner held that regarding a Torah scroll there is an independent reason why one may not make it into a mezuzah, and the Gemara mentioned the reason that applies to both. If so, according to his view, the essential question must be from tefillin, and tefillin also require specifically that Ve-hayah im shamoa be closed. There too on 32b, where it asks from scoring regarding tefillin, a Torah scroll cannot be made in any case, since the passages are distant, sewing them is invalid, and “to complete” is forced in his eyes, since in truth even open ones are valid; see there. Therefore the Gemara asks and answers that it is a tannaitic dispute, which is not as forced as “to complete.” See Yoreh Deah 288 in the Shach, letter 1, and understand.
A question. A scribe wrote in Ve-hayah ki yeviacha, “with a strong hand Hashem took us out,” and saw that if he would write “from Egypt” there would not be space for nine letters and Shema must specifically be open at the head of the line. It seems that he should suspend the word “from Egypt” above, and there will be the space of nine letters. For the reason one may not suspend insertions is because of out-of-order writing, as the Kesef Mishneh writes regarding tefillin; see Taz. But ordinary words written in order may be suspended. See the commentator in the Yerushalmi, chapter 24 of Megillah, page 6a, that ordinary words written in order may be suspended. The essential ruling is like the Rambam, that an open parsha must be at the head of the line, as I wrote in letter 26. This requires study.
