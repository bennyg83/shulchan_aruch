(114) Every letter, etc. — this section explains the law of writing out of order, and its topics are long; I will include briefly: in this law there are three details: [a] If there is a defect in some letter, whether at the time of writing or afterward [Pri Megadim in introduction] — if to the eye it does not have its form on it, such as a yod missing its right leg, or a shin missing one yod, and all such where something is missing from the letter until its image is not upon it — even if it is not mistaken for another letter and he wrote before it, no correction helps; and even if a child happened to read it as a letter, it is invalid as out of order; and all the more if through its defect it resembles another letter, such as dalet resembling reish, or bet resembling kaf, or chet where on the left leg there is a break between leg and roof and it looks like heh, or tzadi where there is a break where the yod touches its body and it looks to the eye like yod and nun — we do not rely on children and correction does not help. [b] If through its defect it did not entirely lose its form, such as a thin break in the middle of some letter while the main form remains, or the yods on alefs and shins and legs of tavs that do not touch the body of the letter — if a child who is neither wise nor foolish recognizes them, even though the letter is not qualified thereby because its form is not complete, nevertheless this helps that the main form remains on it so it may be corrected and is not called out of order. [c] A third manner: even though the main form is on it and a child reads it as a letter, nevertheless no correction helps, such as a letter invalidated in a matter whose correction is to scrape part and rewrite [such as the leg of heh and kuf that touched above — even a touch thin as a hair whose correction is to scrape the whole leg and rewrite, for mere separation does not help because of hollowing of interiors; and likewise the foot of alef on the roof of alef, and similar, as in seif 18] — and it happened that before correction he wrote before it: in this no correction is conceivable, for he must scrape all that was written invalid, and what he corrects afterward is out of order. And now we begin to explain the section, with God's help: Any letter that is written, etc.; and the same if it was corrupted afterward.

(115) And its form is not on it — meaning: anything visible to the eye that has no form on it at all; and even though when the foot of alef touched the roof of alef, or the face of alef on its roof, or the leg of heh and kuf touching above — the main form of the letter is still recognizable and a child would call it a letter — nevertheless since this invalidation's correction is only by scraping all that was made invalid, as in seif 18, its form was entirely nullified; and if he fixes them afterward it is out of order.

(116) The leg of the alef — and the same for all those the Rema wrote in his hagahah at the end of seif 18 cited above.

(117) Touching — even a touch thin as a hair.

(118) Tzadi that he wrote — meaning: that he separated the yod from the nun so much that it appears as two letters; and likewise in all similar cases — therefore even if a child read it properly, he cannot correct afterward, since our eyes see they have the form of other letters; when he corrects afterward it is new writing out of order. And the same even if they do not have the form of other letters, only it is visible to all that the main form is missing, such as a yod without its right leg, or alef missing its upper yod — he cannot correct afterward. But if he did not make the left leg of the yod — although if it remains so it is invalid, for it is not a proper yod per most poskim as below siman 36 — nevertheless since it has its main form, for even without a tip it is called yod, he may correct it and this is not out of order.

(119) Ayin-yod — and the same: open mem that he wrote like samekh without drawing the tag between them.

(120) Or chet as two zayins — even if he made a roof over them, only that they do not touch each other above and their separation is evident — this letter lost its main form, appearing as two zayins.

(121) Yods on the alefs — and the same if there was a break in the middle of some letter [Pri Megadim in another place in introduction].

(122) And a child, etc. — meaning: therefore even if their separation is evident [i.e., only when one sees them] he may correct them, for the letter has not lost its main form since the child called it a letter [as Gra wrote in his chiddushim, not like Pri Megadim who was strict — see BHL]; but if their separation is not evident until one looks closely, one need not show the child. Nevertheless if it happened he showed the child and the child did not read it as a letter, we follow him to be strict and one cannot correct [as Pri Megadim wrote].

(123) And there are those who say — there is no dispute here, only a novel law.

(124) Evident — for if it was evident, it lost its main form, as we wrote in s.k. 120.

(125) It is permitted to join them — for the child is not accustomed to such a chet; even one who is qualified, if corrected, will call it two zayins.
