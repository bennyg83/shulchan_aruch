[25] And one need not cover, etc. But in Keneset HaGedolah he explained the words of Beit Yosef that specifically what is after it need not be covered, but what is before must be covered — thus is the custom — and even what is after must cover the lower line — so far; and he did not mention from the words of the Levush, and thus it seems in the Shulchan Aruch. The Taz wrote: "a child who is not clever" means he does not understand the matter, but he is accustomed to reading the words. The Taz wrote — his wording: "Correct and clear that this 'if severed' deals with when part of the letter's length was severed below entirely and nothing remains of it except the part before the break — then it depends on the child's reading whether we doubt if enough of that letter remains; but if part of the leg also remains below the break, a child does not help, for the child will combine what is below with the upper part, and in truth there is no valid combination — and therefore one should say one must cover before the child the part remaining after the break" — so far. And this requires investigation, for Beit Yosef wrote in the name of the Meiri — his wording: when a hole is pierced in the middle of the leg until the upper part of the leg is severed from the lower, and this correction depends on the hole being so thin that the break does not prevent correct reading for an average child — so far his wording. It is explained that perhaps it deals even when something remains below — kosher through a child; however specifically when the break is thin — for if it is large perhaps a child does not help — and this is included in what the Shulchan Aruch and Levush wrote "but if nothing remains," etc., "for our eyes see the letter is not as required" — so far. All this when done after writing; but at the time of writing it seems combination does not apply. The Taz further wrote: I saw my teacher, of blessed memory, who wrote that they do not validate through a child except when it was severed after writing; but if it was written thus from the start a child does not help, even though there is no hole — so far his wording. His words are puzzling, for certainly there is no distinction — if the scribe happened to write some simple letter and was unsure if it is long enough, and he cannot correct it without lo kelachar yad, he surely shows the letter to a child — so far his wording. In my humble opinion it is plain the Bach deals with needing to combine also part of the letter written after the break, as I explained — and it applies even to letters that are not simple; then he holds it is invalid at the time of writing. But if we need only part of the letter above it, as with simple letters, he concedes it is kosher even when done at the time of writing. A complete proof: the Bach himself concludes — his wording: "However, if the parchment break and the part are in simple letters such as vav, nun, and mem — the scribe would not have continued further than where it broke and no more; it would have been vav and nun as required, but he extended the letter after the break too much — one may validate. But if until the break from the start of writing there was only as much as his own small letter, one may not validate, since it was not written correctly from the start" — so far his wording. It implies it deals at the start even with letters that are not simple. Yet it is difficult why he does not validate when there is until the break as much as his own small letter — meaning a full vav or small nun — do we require large letters? One must say he holds that in any case at the time of writing this does not suffice — and this requires investigation. And it seems in any case when there is a hole it is invalid at the time of writing, for it is not surrounded by parchment.
