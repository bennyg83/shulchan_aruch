Two zayins, etc. For all these are invalid, since this is broken writing and not whole writing. Malbushei Yom Tov wrote that the same applies to an open mem whose vav has been detached from the kaf; and so I heard from an expert who had received such a tradition, end quote. It appears that in all these cases, even if a child can read it, it is invalid. See siman 36, se'if 2, regarding this. Magen Avraham wrote that the same applies if he did not make the left leg; they can be corrected.
And after he wrote before it, etc. For if he had not written before it, it would be a correction by scraping, made while in a state of invalidity, as below, siman 36, se'if 2.
Even though the child, etc. This is because children are not accustomed to ches letters formed with a staff, as below, siman 36, at the end of se'if 2.
