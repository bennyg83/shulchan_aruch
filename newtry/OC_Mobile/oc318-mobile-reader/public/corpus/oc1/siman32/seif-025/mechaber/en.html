Any letter that is written improperly and does not have its form, such as where the leg of the Alef touched the roof of the Alef, or the inner face of the Alef touched the roof beneath it; or the leg of the Hei or the leg of the Kuf touched; or one letter was divided into two letters, such as a Tzadi that he wrote as Yud Nun, or a Shin that he wrote as Ayin Yud, or a Ches as two Zayins, and after he wrote further he went back and corrected them, this is considered not in order and they are invalid. But to separate letters that are attached after he wrote further is proper, for since the letter has its form, when he separates it from its fellow it is not considered writing. The same applies if some of the Yuds on the Alefs, Shins, and Ayins, or the legs of the Tavs, were not touching the body of the letter, and a child who is neither wise nor foolish recognizes them; even though he wrote further, he may go back and correct them, for since the form of the letter was recognizable, there is no issue of having written them not in order. There is one who says that the same applies if the upper bridge of a Ches does not touch together, but their separation is not plainly recognizable, even though the child reads it as two Zayins, it is permitted to attach them.
