Any letter written not in its proper form and lacking its shape — for example, the leg of an alef touched the roof of the alef, or the inner face of the alef touched the lower roof beneath it, or the leg of a heh or the leg of a kuf touched, or one letter was divided into two letters — for example a tzadi written as yod-nun, or a shin written as ayin-yod, or a chet as two zayins — and after he continued writing he went back and corrected them: this is considered written out of order and they are invalid. But to separate attached letters after he continued writing is acceptable, for since the letter had its shape, when he separates it from its neighbor it is not as if he is writing anew. The same applies if the small yods on alef, shin, and ayin, or the legs of tav, were not touching the body of the letter, and a child who is neither wise nor foolish recognizes them: even though he continued writing, he may go back and correct them, for since the letter's shape was recognizable there is no concern of writing out. of order. Some say the same if the upper branch of a chet does not touch — but the separation is not clearly recognizable — that even though a child reads it as two zayins, it is permitted to attach them.
