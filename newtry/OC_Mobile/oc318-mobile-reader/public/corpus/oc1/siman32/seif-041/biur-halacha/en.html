The length and width of the compartments, etc. See Eruvin 93b in the Gemara, and study carefully the Rosh in Hilchos Ketanos, in the brief order for preparing tefillin, what he wrote regarding the measure of tefillin, and the Biur HaGra in this se'if. In any event, in my humble opinion, even according to the Shulchan Aruch, who ruled anonymously as law that they have no measure, nevertheless since it is proven there in the Gemara that on the head, and likewise on the arm, there is room only to place two tefillin and not three, one must at least be careful not to wear very small tefillin at one time and tefillin three times as large as the first ones at another time. For example, if the first time they were one and a half fingerbreadths by one and a half fingerbreadths, and the second time they were four and a half fingerbreadths by four and a half fingerbreadths, and the like, then either way there is a problem: either the first tefillin were smaller than the required measure, or the second tefillin were placed outside the place of tefillin. See below, siman 233, se'if 1, that one should not create two contradictory practices even not on one day. Although one could reject this by saying that in the time of the Gemara their tefillin were medium-sized, about two fingerbreadths or more, unlike this case, if so the Rosh's proof there falls away, and regarding the Shulchan Aruch's ruling not like the Rosh, the later authorities did not mention that it was for this reason, but rather for another reason; see Beis Yosef and Biur HaGra. The proper practice is that a person's tefillin should always be medium-sized, that is, not less than two fingerbreadths by two fingerbreadths and not more than four by four fingerbreadths, including the titura and ma'avarta. In this way, at least as a matter of law, he fulfills all opinions.
