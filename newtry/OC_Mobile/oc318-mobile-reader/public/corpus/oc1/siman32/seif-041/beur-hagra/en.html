See also Tosafot, Sukkah 5a, s.v. and upon, etc., and Yoma 12b and 25a. Their proof that the forehead plate was in the place of the tefillin is from the fact that the Torah calls that of the Kohen Gadol a mitznefes, and that of an ordinary kohen migbaos. Rather, that of the Kohen Gadol was shorter because of the forehead plate and tefillin, unlike that of an ordinary kohen, where it was only because of tefillin. See the above Tosafot in Sukkah 5a. But the Rambam in chapter 8 of Hilchos Klei HaMikdash wrote: the mitznefes stated regarding Aharon is the migbaos stated regarding his sons, except that the Kohen Gadol was wrapped in it like one who wraps a bandage, and his sons were wrapped in it like a cap; therefore it is called migbaos. So too wrote the Ramban in Parashas Tetzaveh, and he brought proof from what is stated at the end of chapter 7 of Yoma: the ordinary kohen serves with four garments, etc., he adds, etc. And there at the end of the chapter he wrote: the mitznefes of the Kohen Gadol or of an ordinary kohen was sixteen cubits long, etc.
