41) Seif 12. The parchment should be from the hide of an animal, etc. In Responsa Rema MiPano, siman 37, he wrote that there are levels: the hide of a fetus of an undomesticated kosher animal is more fitting for the mitzvah than other hides; the hide of a fetal domesticated animal is preferable to bird hide; bird hide is preferable to the hide of an undomesticated animal; and the hide of an undomesticated animal is preferable to the hide of a domesticated animal. These ranks apply whether all are carcasses or all were slaughtered. However, a fetal domesticated animal from a slaughtered mother takes precedence over a fetal undomesticated animal that is a carcass; and similarly, the hide of a slaughtered domesticated animal takes precedence over the hide of an undomesticated animal that is a carcass. This was cited by Shiyarei Knesses HaGedolah, Hagahos Tur, letter 3; Olas Tamid, letter 20; Eliyah Rabbah, letter 19, who wrote there that the case of a slaughtered domesticated animal versus a fetal carcass requires study; Pri Megadim, Eshel Avraham, letter 14; Kerem Shlomo on this seif, who wrote that regarding a non-kosher animal born from a kosher one, or a kosher animal born from a non-kosher one, Responsa Yad Eliyahu, siman 2, rules stringently; and in siman 35 he rules that the hide of an animal that copulated with a human or with which a human copulated may not be used for writing tefillin or mezuzos; Baer Heitev, seif katan 15. See also Heichal Shlomo, siman 2, letter 1, and Lishkas HaSofer, letter 4, who cites Responsa Rema MiPano. Regarding what he wrote that a slaughtered animal is superior to a carcass, he wrote in the name of Chasam Sofer that this does not appear so from Shabbos 108a at the end; see there for his resolution. See Meleches Shamayim, klal 2, letter 2, in Binah, who wrote concerning the Chasam Sofer's difficulty that the Rema MiPano himself certainly noticed this, for he concluded in that responsum that from the law of the Gemara every kosher species is valid, even its carcasses and tereifos. His intent is simple: although according to strict law there is no concern, nevertheless the optimal mitzvah has the distinctions he stated. End quote.
