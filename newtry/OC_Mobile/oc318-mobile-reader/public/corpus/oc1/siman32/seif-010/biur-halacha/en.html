Like letters. So that the non-Jew will not exchange them for other hides. Specifically something like letters is needed; holes made merely as a sign are not effective. Pri Megadim.
To forge. For he can place the hide on top of another hide and likewise make, with an awl, something like those letters. The Machatzis HaShekel wrote that great care is needed when processing is done through a non-Jewish craftsman, for he places patches over the holes found in the parchment, and those patches were almost certainly not processed for the sake of the mitzvah, since they are from pieces of hides that he trims and takes from whatever comes to hand to make a patch. After processing they are hardly recognizable except when held against the sun.
Because he is afraid. Otherwise, the rule that a rabbinic doubt is treated leniently would not apply here, even according to his view that processing for its sake is rabbinic, because wherever the non-Jew benefits from it, it is usual and nearly certain that he will exchange it. But in a place where the non-Jew does not benefit from the exchange, we rule that we are not concerned for exchange. Pri Megadim.
