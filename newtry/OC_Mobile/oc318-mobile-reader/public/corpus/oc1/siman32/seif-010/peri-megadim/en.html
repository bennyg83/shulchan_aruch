If. Taz: here the word nifsek — one may explain in two aspects: nikav (pierced), and this has no explanation except nikav afterward; but if the ink broke off, or a thin scratch, or the scribe at first wrote a broken vav — a child helps. Taz wrote that what is below does not combine, and what is above alone requires a measure of doubt worth being uncertain about, and a child helps; but if what is above is evident to us that it does not have a vav's measure, what will combining below save us? It is similar to the yods of alef and the like in seif 25 — one must in any case repair. I concede that likewise in vav: whenever their separation is not evident clearly, a child helps to combine and repair them, and it is not lo k'sidran, as we must say below in seif 25, there — except that here it discusses when they are far and evident; then without repair the lower does not combine. And here the spring in Beit Yosef and Dagul Meirevavah, note 4, depends on the dispute: Rabbi Meiri wrote that likewise in heh, and all bent letters — a child helps to combine what is below; and Mordechai wrote plainly that only a doubt helps if there is the measure; ah, bent letters — not automatically; heh that are straight — below they do not combine. Dagul Meirevavah wrote that the Tur holds like Rabbi Meiri (in Dagul Meirevavah he wrote that the Tur distinguishes between nikav and nifsek and not between straight and bent — his intention is that he distinguishes in nikav: we require after it was written, for otherwise there is no surrounding gevil; unlike nifsek without a hole, which from the outset is kosher with a child). And here in Shulchan Aruch Rabbenu Yerucham emended: straight, but bent — no, for they do not combine, like Mordechai. And Bach, in the Tur's method, said that he wrote stam nifsek and it implies even bent, like Rabbi Meiri — and specifically after it was written; for if at first it broke without a hole, a child does not help, for it adjoins this; and if after it broke — see there; and Eliyah Rabbah, note 25; understand. But per Rabbenu Yerucham's ruling that he emended straight letters — there is no distinction even before writing: if it broke and was not pierced, a child helps; and the proof — in note 9 they disputed alef and the like that do not help because there is no letter form; ah, a straight letter even from the outset helps without a hole, and a bent one even afterward does not help combining below, for us — and this is when the separation is evident clearly, or without repair, as I wrote; Magen Avraham, note 21.
