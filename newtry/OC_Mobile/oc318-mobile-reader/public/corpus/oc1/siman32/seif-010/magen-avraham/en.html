It is easy for an idolater to forge — he places the hide on top of another hide and also makes holes like letters.

At a glance (betevi'at ayin). That he placed identifying marks [Beit Yosef, Hagahot Maimoniyot], or because these perforations were made more recently than his own [Hagahot Maimoniyot ch. 1]. It is unresolved: in Yoreh De'ah siman 118 seif 3 he wrote that where apostates who know how to write are common, the writing is not a valid sign except for one who recognizes the writing, and we do not say we fear the non-Jew lest he recognize the writing. It is possible to say that there too they were strict only when sending something to one's fellow when the non-Jew knows he will not recognize the writing. Alternatively, one may say that for food matters they were stricter. In Yoreh De'ah siman 130, in Beit Yosef — Hagahot Maimoniyot wrote: if one gives it to him at the outset for tanning for the sake of holiness, the non-Jew may remove it for its repair and one need not assist him further. In Beit Shmuel he wrote that one should write at the head before him and not mark with an awl.
