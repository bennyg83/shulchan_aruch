Even if there is no space between them. Many were troubled by this matter, as explained in the Beit Yosef. In the Gemara in chapter HaKometz it says: If he wrote them on one skin and placed them in four batim, he has fulfilled the obligation; but he must place space between them. These are the words of Rabbi. The Sages say: He need not. And they agree that he places a thread or cord between each one. Rashi explained: There must be space between the parshiyos when he writes them, so that after he writes them on one skin, he cuts the skin from above like four fingers, and places them in four batim; the skin remains joined below as one skin, so that each parsha is in one bayis, and between them, inside the four batim of the one skin, he places a thread or cord between each bayis. End quote. Rabbeinu Yerucham wrote: If he wrote them on one skin, they are valid even if there is no space between them, provided he placed them in four batim. According to this, one must say that when Rabbi said that he must put space between them, it does not refer to the writing, for even according to the Sages one must put space between parsha and parsha so that he can insert them into four batim. Rather, it means that one must place a large space between one bayis and another, and the Sages say this is not necessary to that extent, but a thread or cord between each one suffices. The Beit Yosef wrote: So ruled the Nimukei Yosef. But what Rashi explained, that there must be space between the parshiyos when he writes them, is difficult, for it implies that this is according to Rabbi, but according to the Sages it is not necessary; and if so, it is impossible to insert them into four batim, etc. End quote. He pressed himself greatly to explain Rashi’s words. See there. After this he brought in the name of Mahari Abuhav and Mahari Chaviv, who asked that if they are on one parchment it is impossible for them to stand upright in the batim, and if they are not upright, they are invalid. The Beit Yosef answered that the requirement that they stand upright applies only initially. It appears to me that this is not difficult, for he can cut between each parsha from above until close to the bottom, leaving only a little attached below, and then each parsha can be stood upright in its own bayis, while all remain one skin. The requirement of space between them is only enough for this cutting and insertion, and where one made them in a way that each stands in its bayis, it is valid even if there was no additional space between the written parshiyos.
Provided that he covers it with hide, etc. In the Tur it is written: “and he placed them in one bayis, he has fulfilled the obligation.” The Beit Yosef wrote that the Tur was imprecise, but in my humble opinion it is he who was imprecise in this. For so it says in the Gemara: The Sages taught: If he wrote them on four skins and placed them in one bayis, he has fulfilled the obligation, and he must paste them together; these are the words of Rabbi Yehudah. Rabbi Yosei says: He need not. Rabbi Yehudah said: Rabbi Yosei concedes to me that if one has no hand tefillin and has two head tefillin, he covers one hide over them and places it. This means that he covers one hide over all the batim, and it appears like one bayis. The Gemara asks: Is this not their dispute? Rava answers that Rabbi Yehudah in truth retracted and conceded to Rabbi Yosei. The Beit Yosef was troubled why the Tur did not write the law that he covers it with hide, which would be a greater novelty, even though they are placed inside four batim. This is astonishing, for if there were any advantage in the law of covering it with hide over what is written in the baraisa, “and he places them in one bayis,” why did Rabbi Yehudah not initially state the greater novelty? In the place where he said “he need not,” he should have said even if he covers it, etc. Rather, necessarily they are plainly equivalent. If so, the Tur needed to write only the law of the baraisa, that he places them in one bayis, and the law of covering it with hide follows automatically. The main novelty is that one need not paste them, etc.
