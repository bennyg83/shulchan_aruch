Even if there is no space between them. Many struggled with this, as explained in Beit Yosef. In the Gemara, chapter HaKometz: if he wrote them on one hide and placed them in four compartments — valid; must leave space between them — Rabbi's view; Sages say not required. Both agree one puts thread or mashicha between each. Rashi explains: there must be space between each parshah when writing them — after writing on one hide he cuts from above the hide like four finger-widths [Beit Yosef copied: like four parshiyot] and placed them in four compartments, glued at the bottom on one hide so one parshah is in the first bay; between them, inside the four bays of the first hide, he puts thread or mashicha between each bay — thus Ramban; Rivash: if on one hide they are kosher even without space if placed in four compartments. Therefore what Rabbi said — must give space between them — does not refer to the writing; for the Sages too one must leave space between parshah and parshah to fit them in four compartments, but rather to say one must leave a large space between bay and bay, and Sages say not so much — thread or mashicha between each suffices — Beit Yosef and Nekudat Yosef. But Rashi's explanation that there must be space between the parshiyot when writing them is difficult, for it implies this is for Rabbi alone but not Sages — then it is impossible to place them in four compartments, etc. He strained greatly explaining Rashi's words — see there. Afterward he brought in the name of Mahari Abo and Mahari Chabiv who asked: if they are on one klaf, how can they stand upright in their bays — how can he take the parshah from the first bay and move it to the adjacent bay if it is upright? But if they are not upright but flat he can place the first parshah well in the first bay; at the end of the parshah there will be a slight fold, and from there he takes it out to the third bay, and likewise for the third and fourth, etc. They conclude that in truth upright position is not required; this tanna disputes those who require upright position. Then the Tur's words remain difficult, for he ruled both views within the time of speech. In my humble opinion there is another way here: Rashi did not intend that he cut between the parshiyot themselves, between each parshah and its fellow, inserting through the name of the bays — that is certainly like writing each on a special klaf; whence would it be invalid that they are pasted below the writing together? Rather Rashi's intent is that he takes a large hide — meaning above the writing there is a large portion — and in that portion he cuts; each cut is aligned opposite the break between parshah and its fellow, and he puts that portion above the writing into a bay — meaning the measure that separates between bay and bay is intended to separate between each parshah. In truth the parshah itself is not inside the cavity of the bay but aligned directly against it, and the separation holds itself between the cut of hide — meaning above the writing. Thus all the parshiyot are within four bays, since between parshah and parshah there is a break in the hide separating bay from bay, and the break reaches the writing of the parshiyot. In this Rabbi and Sages dispute: Rabbi holds that since all the parshiyot are connected, there must be space between them; Sages say not required — thread or mashicha suffices to make separation between them. Through this Rashi's language is explained: he wrote "cut above the hide" — meaning above the writing — and did not write "cut between the parshiyot"; the rest of his wording accords with this. "Is it not impossible to fold the parshiyot from their end to their head since they are on one klaf?" — this is not critical for the law; therefore one may say so in all the ways here. Likewise "does the break not reach below?" — we hold like Rav Dimi: since the groove between bay and bay is recognizable, one need not more; not like Abaye who said the groove must reach the place of sewing — as explained here.

And one spreads a hide over them, etc. In the Tur it is written "and he places them in one bay — valid," and Beit Yosef wrote that the Tur was not precise. In my humble opinion he was not precise here, for thus it is in the Gemara: Tannaim taught — if he wrote them on four hides and placed them in one bay — valid, and must glue — Rabbi Yehuda; R. Yishmael says not required. R. Yehuda and R. Yishmael dispute. R. Yehuda concedes that if he lacks a single-parshah tefillin set and has two single-bay sets, he spreads one hide from above and places it — meaning he covers one hide over all the bays so it appears as one bay. The Gemara challenges — this is their dispute — and answers Rava that R. Yishmael in truth retracted and conceded to R. Yehuda. Beit Yosef found it difficult: why did the Tur not write the law of spreading a hide over them, which is a greater novelty — even though they rest inside in four bays. One wonders: if spreading a hide over them were superior to what the baraita says "and places them in one bay," why did R. Yehuda not state the greater novelty first where he said "not required" — he should have said "even if he spreads..." Rather it must be they agree; therefore the Tur need only write the baraita's law of placing in one bay, and the law of spreading is implicit. The main novelty is that gluing is not required, etc.
