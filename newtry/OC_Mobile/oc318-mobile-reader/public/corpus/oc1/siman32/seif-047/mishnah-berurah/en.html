Even if there is no, etc. Meaning, all the more so when there is space between the passages, so that he can cut them and leave enough surrounding blank parchment for each one, that is certainly acceptable, even though at the time of writing he wrote them on one piece of parchment. The later authorities wrote that even when there is no space between them, and he will be forced to place each passage in its housing not upright, nevertheless it is valid, because placing them upright is only for the mitzvah. This is the explanation according to what the Beis Yosef wrote in the name of Maharia and Ri ben Chaviv, and so the Bach agreed; see Magen Avraham in the name of Darchei Moshe.
Or a cord. Meaning that since he wrote the passages on one piece of leather, he must separate them with a thread, cord, or sinew, as we practice between one housing and another, in order to create a recognizable sign that the housings are separated. But if he wrote the passages on four pieces of leather, he need not place anything between one housing and another. Some authorities hold that in every case one must place a separation between the housings, and this is the practice to follow, as the Mechaber writes below in seif 51, “and he shall pass a thread,” etc. Nevertheless, regarding after the fact, the Taz wrote there that it does not invalidate, for the main view follows the first opinion.
In four housings. All the more so if he placed them in one housing, for that is preferable.
And the custom, etc. Meaning that if he placed them in one housing, although he certainly fulfilled the obligation, nevertheless initially it is a mitzvah to glue them together. The later authorities wrote that although initially it is a mitzvah to write the four passages of the arm tefillin on one piece of parchment, nevertheless, if he already wrote them on four parchments, it is considered an after-the-fact case, and he may place them initially if he first glues them together. If an error was found in the fourth passage, it is permitted even initially to write it on a separate parchment and glue it, for this is considered an after-the-fact situation.
Valid glue. Meaning from a kosher animal. Pri Megadim wrote that this is only for the mitzvah, but not indispensable, since the gluing itself does not invalidate; automatically there is no concern with what he glues it.
