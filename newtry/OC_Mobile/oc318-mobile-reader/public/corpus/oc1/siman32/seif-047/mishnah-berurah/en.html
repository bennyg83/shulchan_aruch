(216) Even if there is not, etc. — that is, all the more so when there is space between the parshiyot that he can cut them and leave enough wrap of scroll for each, which is fine, even though at the time of writing he wrote them on one klaf. And the acharonim wrote that even though when there is no space between them one will be compelled to place each parasha in its bayit not upright, nevertheless it is valid, for upright placement is only a mitzvah [thus is the explanation according to what Beit Yosef wrote in the name of Mahari and Mahari ben Chabiv, and Bach agreed; see Magen Avraham in the name of Darkei Moshe]:

(217) Or glue — that is, since he wrote the parshiyot on one hide, he must separate with a thread, or glue, or sinew, as we practice between bayit and bayit, to show that the batim are distinct; but when writing the parshiyot on four hides, one need not place anything between bayit and bayit. Some poskim hold that in every case one must make a separation between batim, and so one should practice, as the Mechaber wrote below in seif 51, "and pass a thread," etc. Nevertheless, for b'dieved Taz wrote it does not invalidate there, since the primary view is the first view:

(218) In four batim — and all the more so if he placed them in one bayit, for then it is more preferable:

(219) And the custom, etc. — that is, if he placed them in one bayit, even though he certainly fulfills [the mitzvah], nevertheless lechatchilah it is a mitzvah to paste them [separately in four]. The acharonim wrote that although lechatchilah one should write the four hand parshiyot on one klaf, nevertheless if he already wrote on four klafim it is considered b'dieved, and one may place them lechatchilah if one pastes them beforehand; and if an error is found in the fourth parasha, one may write it on a klaf alone even lechatchilah and paste it, for this is like b'dieved:

(220) Valid glue — that is, from a pure animal. Peri Megadim wrote this is only for the mitzvah, not invalidation; since pasting does not invalidate, there is no insistence on what one uses to paste:
