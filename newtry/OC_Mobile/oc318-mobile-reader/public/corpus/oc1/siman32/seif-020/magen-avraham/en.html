Missing or extra one letter, etc. There is room to question: if he wrote "mitzvot" in full and erased the vav, whether extending the tzadi downward helps, or perhaps as long as he does not extend the letter upward it is considered two words even though they stick together below — and likewise the word "natan." Nevertheless, whatever appears as two words is invalid (Beit Yosef). Levush wrote one should be strict about this; it implies that if at the beginning of writing he extends the lower leg of the nun and tzadi and writes the adjacent letter within them — as in the source example — it is permitted, for it is truly one word; so appears in Beit Yosef siman 37, letter 3, who wrote to write "artzenu" with the vav inside the nun; see there. And thus he wrote in the name of Ra'avyah vol. 2 siman 78: if one letter is swallowed in its neighbor — e.g., the letter alef enters the gimel and the nun — all agree it is invalid; it is unresolved; see at end of ch. 25. He also wrote there: Radbaz vol. 1 ch. 2 found an extra vav or yud — he may drag it and thicken slightly the letter before or after it, for this is not considered changing the letter; if he wrote one extra word he may erase it, for we need not worry about Rabbeinu Tam who holds the measure of a parashah is three letters. But one may not extend the letter before it, such as heh or kuf, for that is out of order; see there. It seems to me it is not out of order since the letter itself is written properly [thus]. It seems to me this is the case: if he extends the roof of the heh and leaves the leg in place — as in the source example — it is not the form of a heh, for we require the leg of the heh at its end, as Bach wrote siman 35 in the name of Beit Shmuel; if he erases the leg and writes it at the end, it is certainly out of order. Nevertheless it seems to me that if it were a section of about nine letters, when he drags it there would be a parashah break where there should be none and the tefillin would be invalid; therefore it seems permissible to extend the heh even though there will be no leg at the end. He also wrote: Ra'avad validated tefillin found with doubled words, and my heart wavers but I am not able to dispute; see there siman 120. Our ancestors wrote "malei" — one may drag and extend the bet.
