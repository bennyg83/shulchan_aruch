Missing or extra by one letter, etc. One may question whether, if he wrote the word mitzvos in full spelling and erased the vav, it helps to extend the tzadi downward, or perhaps, as long as he does not extend the letter upward, it is considered like two words even though it is attached below. The same applies to the word nasan. In any case, wherever it appears like two words it is invalid; Beis Yosef. The Levush wrote that one should be stringent about this. It implies that if, at the beginning of writing, he extends the lower leg of the nun or the tzadi and writes the adjacent letter inside it in this manner, it is permitted, for it is truly one word. This is also implied in Beis Yosef, siman 37, letter 3, where he wrote that one should write the vav of artzeinu within the nun; see there. In Knesses HaGedolah he wrote in the name of Rabbi Avraham ben Chaviv, part 2, siman 78, that if one letter is swallowed into another, for example if one letter enters into the gimel or the nun, it is invalid according to all; this requires study, see se'if 28. He wrote further there that the Radbaz, part 1, siman 2, wrote: if he found an extra vav or yud, he should scrape it away and thicken slightly the letter before it or after it, for this does not involve changing the letter. If he wrote one extra word, he should erase it, for we need not be concerned for Rabbeinu Tam, who holds that the measure of a parashah is three letters. But he should not extend the letter before it, such as a hei or kuf, because that would be out of order, end quote. It seems to me that this is not out of order, since the letter itself was written properly; Knesses HaGedolah. It seems to me that his meaning is as follows: if he extends the roof of a hei and leaves the leg in its place in this manner, it does not have the form of a hei, for we require the leg of the hei to be at its end, as the Bach wrote in siman 35 in the name of Sefer Baruch She'amar. If he erases the leg and writes it at the end, that would certainly be out of order. Nevertheless it seems to me that if there is a blank space of about nine letters, so that when he scrapes it away there will be a parashah in a place where there is no parashah, and the tefillin will be invalidated, then it seems to me that it is permitted to extend the hei even though its leg will not be at its end. He wrote further: the Raavad validated tefillin in which doubled words were found, but my heart is uneasy, except that I am not worthy to disagree; see there, siman 120. If one wrote la'avoseinu in full spelling, he may scrape away and extend the beis.
