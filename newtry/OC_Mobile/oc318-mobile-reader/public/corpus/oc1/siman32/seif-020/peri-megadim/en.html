They were. Written out of order. See Taz. The “some say” does not disagree with what preceded, for there the case is two zayins without a bridge, whereas here it has its bridge. He writes “some say” even when most decisors did not write this way and even where no one disagrees, as is his good way consistently. In siman 36:2, where if they do not touch they are invalid, this means as long as he has not corrected them; but once he corrected them, it helps whenever a child reads it that way. It seems that this is only when their separation is not plainly recognizable. But if there is a large separation such that the letter changed, a child’s reading does not help. This resembles two zayins with a bridge: even if a child reads it as a ches, if their separation is plainly recognizable, a child’s reading does not help, and it is considered writing out of order, so it seems to me. The point of a yud can be corrected afterward, as in Magen Avraham, letter 36; see Taz 36.
An incident. There was a case in which in tefillin the word “hishamru” was written twice. He erased one vav from the second “hishamru” and extended the reish to the second vav, and I validated it; see Magen Avraham 33. Even if the second word had been an entirely different word unrelated to tefillin, and it had a vav at the end, and he scraped as above, it would also be valid, for what concern is there? Although it was initially invalid because the vav was very distant, this is like “to your fathers,” where as long as he has not corrected and extended the beis it appears as two words. The general rule is that whenever he is not newly writing the form of the letter, even though it was invalid, extension afterward helps.
And know. If the face of the alef touched excessively, there is no remedy by extending and thickening the yud, as the Orach Chaim writes in letter 33 regarding an alef of a Divine Name in a Torah scroll; see what I wrote in Magen Avraham, letter 28. In any case it is considered out of order. This is unlike the yuds of the shins, where their separation is not plainly recognizable. Here everyone knows that this is not the form of an alef, and wherever writing is needed to correct the form of the letter, it is considered out of order. The notation really belongs more in seif 25 than here, and it is not a textual error, for the notation in letter 21 refers to seif 23, as is clear. See letter 21.
