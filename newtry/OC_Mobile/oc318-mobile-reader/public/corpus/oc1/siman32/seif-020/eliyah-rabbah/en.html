[20] It appears divided, etc. But if the hole is so small that when the quill passes over it the hole is sealed with ink, they write on it — Levush — even though the hole is visible against the sun, since the quill does not feel it, it is kosher. Thus wrote the Bach, Keneset HaGedolah, Nachalat Tzvi, and Olat Tamid. But the Taz was silent and wrote that if it is visible against the sun it is invalid — and this is puzzling, for he did not mention that the Bach permits it, which is the main view. The Bach also wrote that if it was pierced so much that the letter appears divided in two it is invalid and cannot be fixed before writing — even if a child can read it — since at the time of writing it was not whole. But if after it was written it was pierced, even if divided in two it is kosher, provided we require that a child can read it.
