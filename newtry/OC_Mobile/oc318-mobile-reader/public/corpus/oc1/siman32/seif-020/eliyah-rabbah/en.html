Regarding deficient and plene spellings, etc. Olas Tamid wrote that according to the Rashba above, who says that anything fit for mixing, etc., the same applies here with plene spellings: since it is fit to be corrected, there is no blessing in vain. However, as soon as he knows that there are extra letters, he must correct it, end quote. This requires study. The Levush elaborated here about the practice of allowing young boys to write tefillin in order to train them in writing, and he spoke at length about the punishment. He also mocked those who tell a scribe, 'Write tefillin for my sake.' In Sefer Baruch She'amar it is written: one should write good and whole letters, not broken ones, with deliberation and great intent, and one should not hurry in writing them in order to earn much money, for that profit will go to loss and disgrace, and he will lose his soul, for he causes the public to sin. Whoever writes good and valid tefillin according to his ability has a reward doubled and redoubled, and is saved from the judgment of Gehinnom, end quote. Sefer Chasidim, siman 85, writes: 'and his righteousness endures forever' refers to one who brings merit to the public, such as one who teaches those who fear Hashem how to correct tefillin in order to repair them for others.
