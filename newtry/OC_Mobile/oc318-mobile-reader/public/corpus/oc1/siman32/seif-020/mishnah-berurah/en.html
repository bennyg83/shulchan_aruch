(100) Missing or extra - Even if the word's reading is not changed by this, such as with plene and defective spelling, as below.
(101) One letter - Even if the point of a yod is missing, it invalidates, as stated in Menachot 29a.
(102) Is great - Aside from the severe sin of theft.
(103) And trembles at the word of Hashem - I saw fit to copy here the language of Levush, which is very necessary for our matter. This is his language: It should not be like what some scribes do now, leaving boys who are learning to write tefillin in order to train them in writing, and afterward the scribe sees whether they were written according to halachah regarding missing and extra letters, and considers that sufficient. Afterward they place them in the boxes and sell them, and the scribes credit those monies toward the boy's tuition. They show themselves a permissive face, saying: we are doing kindness with poor boys by teaching them the writing profession for free, and it is the work of Hashem. But I say: their gain is lost through their loss; on the contrary, they are not doing good with their people, for a boy is a boy and does not know his right from his left. He has no intention in the world; they are like people merely occupied with writing to beautify the script, not for any sanctity or mitzvah intention in the world. The scribe's punishment is very great, for he causes people to stumble by wearing those invalid tefillin, as above in seif 19. Furthermore, to improve his merchandise, the scribe will tell everyone, "I wrote them, and I wrote them with intention," etc. All who do this will certainly have to give judgment and receive many punishments, and about them it is said, "Cursed is one who does the work of Hashem deceitfully." Therefore every scribe should be careful and distance himself from this, and it will be good for him. The lishmah stated regarding tefillin does not mean for the sake of the owner of the tefillin, as with a get, which must be for the sake of the man and woman, but for the sanctity of tefillin; see there. He concluded about this: it is proper for one who has authority to appoint proper tefillin writers, people of truth, haters of gain, masters of Torah, God-fearing, and trembling at His word in every city, just as they appoint shochtim and inspectors, and not to trust every scribe whose intent is only to profit money through writing and attractive preparation of tefillin. Even though this intention too would have been good, to beautify the mitzvah in its construction, that is only if they also had holy intention; but in this they are not careful. This is enough, end of his pure language. The book Beit Shmuel wrote: he should write good and complete letters, not broken ones, slowly and with great intention. A person should not hurry in writing them in order to profit much money, for that profit will go to loss and disgrace, and he will lose his soul, for he causes the public to sin. Whoever writes good and kosher tefillin according to his ability, his reward is doubled and redoubled, and he is saved from the judgment of Gehinnom. Sefer Chasidim siman 85 wrote: "his righteousness endures forever" refers to one who brings merit to the many, such as one who teaches those who fear Hashem how to repair tefillin so they can repair for others. See siman 39 regarding when a person is fit to write tefillin.
