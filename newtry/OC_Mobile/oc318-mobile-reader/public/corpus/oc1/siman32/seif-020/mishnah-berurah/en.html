(103) And zealous for the word of God — I saw fit to copy here the language of the Levush, which is very necessary for our matter, as follows: And not as is done now — many scribes place young students learning to write tefillin in order to train them in script, and afterward the scribe checks whether they were written according to halachah in their deficiencies and excesses, and that suffices for him; and afterward they place them in housings and sell them, and the scribes count those monies as tuition for the boy and show them a permitted face, saying: behold, through this we are like those who bestow kindness on poor boys to teach them the craft of writing for free, and it is God's work. But I say: their reward is lost in their loss, and rather they are not doing good to their people, for the boy is a boy and does not know his right from his left and has no intent in the world — only they are like those occupied with writing to beautify the script, not for any sanctity and no mitzvah intent in the world. And behold the scribe's punishment is very great for causing the masses to stumble who place these invalid tefillin [as above in se'if 19]. And moreover, to beautify his merchandise the scribe will tell everyone "I wrote them and wrote them with intent," etc. — and all who do so will surely give account and receive great punishment; regarding them it is said "Cursed is one who does the work of the Lord deceitfully." Therefore every scribe should beware and distance himself from this — and it is good for him. For the "for its sake" stated regarding tefillin does not mean for the owner of the tefillin, as with a get that requires the man's and woman's names, but only for the sanctity of tefillin — see there; and he concluded thus. And it is fitting for one who has power to appoint tefillin writers — respectable men of truth, haters of profit, Torah scholars, God-fearers, zealous for His word — in every city, as slaughterers and inspectors are appointed, so that one should not trust all scribes whose intent is only to profit money by writing and fine repair in making tefillin; even though this intent too would be good to beautify the mitzvah when built — that is only if they also had holy intent, but they are not careful in this — enough on this, end quote the pure one. And he wrote in Beit Shmuel: one should write good and whole letters, not broken, with moderation and great intent, and one should not hurry in writing to profit much money, for that profit will go to loss and ruin and he will lose his soul, for he causes the masses to stumble; and everyone who writes good valid tefillin according to his ability — his reward is doubled and redoubled and he is saved from the judgment of Gehinnom. And he wrote in Shaar HaTziyun siman 85: and his righteousness stands forever — this one who benefits many, such as one who teaches God-fearers to repair tefillin for others.
