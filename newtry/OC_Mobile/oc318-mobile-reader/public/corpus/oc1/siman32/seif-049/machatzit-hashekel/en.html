(Note 49) Closed, etc., per the Rosh, etc., and afterward in the second line he should begin at the head of the line "V'haya," etc., as should read; and also in the Rabbi there is a scribal error, see there.

And one should not do because of, etc., like tefillin of Rashi and Rabbeinu Tam, as explained siman 34 — for it is different in that siman 34 of tefillin of one master are invalid for the other master and tefillin of the other master are invalid for the first master; not so here, if he does as the Rabbi wrote, certainly he fulfills according to all — even though per the Rosh "V'haya im shamo'a" is open and per the Rambam if he made it open it is invalid; behold, per the Rambam in such a case it is closed, and per the Rosh, which is called closed, one may rely on the view of those who validate if he made parsha "V'haya im shamo'a" open; and also according to us, who make them all open — even though per the Rambam it is invalid if he made parsha four open — nevertheless the reason of the Rambam in this is strained, as written above in the name of the Rabbi, the reason of the matter. Therefore the primary [ruling] in this is like those who validate as open.

The Semak wrote, etc., unlike the Beit Yosef, etc., which refers to lo k'sidran — for in the Semak it is written that in the head [tefillin], which are on four parchments, one need not be careful about the order of their writing; and the Magen Tzedek understood from this word that he means one need not be careful even if the head [tefillin] were written lo k'sidran. And at this the Rabbi wondered: regarding lo k'sidran there is no distinction between head and arm. But Magen Avraham wrote that the intent of the Semak is regarding open and closed [parshiyot], since they are on four parchments they have no connection to each other; but the Semak did not speak at all of lo k'sidran.
