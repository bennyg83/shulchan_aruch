(s.k. 49) Closed, etc. According to the Rosh, etc., and afterward, on the second line, he should begin at the head of the line, “Vehayah,” etc. This is the correct reading. Also in Beis Yosef there is a scribal error; see there.
And one should not do so because of, etc. Like Rashi tefillin and Rabbeinu Tam tefillin, as explained in siman 34. That case in siman 34 is different, for the tefillin of one master are invalid according to the other master, and the tefillin of the other master are invalid according to the first. This is not so here, for if he does as Beis Yosef wrote, he certainly fulfills the obligation according to everyone. Although according to the Rosh “Vehayah im shamoa” is open, and according to Rambam if he made it open it is invalid, nevertheless according to Rambam in such a case it is closed; and according to the Rosh, although it is called closed, one may rely on the view of those who validate if he made the parashah of “Vehayah im shamoa” open. Also according to us, who make all of them open, although according to Rambam it is invalid if he made the fourth parashah open, nevertheless, since Rambam’s reason in this matter is strained, as written above in the name of Beis Yosef explaining the reason, the primary ruling here follows those who validate an open parashah.
Semak wrote, etc. Not like Beis Yosef, etc., who understood it as referring to writing out of order. For in Semak it is written that regarding head-tefillin, since they are on four parchments, one need not be particular about the order in which they are written. For this reason Beis Yosef understood from this language that it means one need not be particular even if the head-tefillin were written out of order. About this Beis Yosef wondered, for regarding the requirement that they be written in order, there is no difference between head-tefillin and hand-tefillin. But Magen Avraham wrote that Semak’s intent concerns open and closed parshiyos: since they are on four parchments, they have no connection to each other. Semak, however, did not speak at all about writing out of order.
