Seif 16, the straight letters, etc. So wrote the Mordechai, to distinguish between the case here, which concerns the straight letters, as stated there regarding the vav of vayaharog, and that case in seif 15.
And there is no need, etc. This appears from Rashi there, s.v. who is not wise, etc. This is what I found written.
Nevertheless, etc. For the benefit of a child's reading is that it reveals the matter, namely, that the letter there is in its proper form.
That which we validate, etc. This is from what is stated there: any letter that is not surrounded, etc. This is what is written in seif 15, if after it was written, etc.; and this is what is meant by without a surrounding of parchment from its beginning, etc.
Or if, etc. Although in the Yerushalmi, chapter 2 of Berachos, we say: If he mixed the letters, one baraisa teaches it is valid and one baraisa teaches it is invalid. The one who says valid refers to below; the one who says invalid refers to above, such as 'our land' and 'our glory.' But with 'your land' and 'your glory' it is a question. That is to say, above means that before the letter was formed it became invalid, unlike below, where after the form of the letter was completed it became attached; and regarding the middle they raised the question. So wrote the Mordechai and other poskim. However, in our Talmud they said: any letter that is not surrounded by gevil, etc., and it does not distinguish between below and above, for it always requires a surrounding. See there.
