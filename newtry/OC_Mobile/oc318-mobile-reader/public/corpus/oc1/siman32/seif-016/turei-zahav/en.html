Or the leg of the alef was severed. This matter cannot be understood as written, and I will set out before you the approach of the Talmud and the decisors. This is the wording: Agra had the leg of the hei in the word “ha’am” severed by a hole, meaning that a hole came in the hei of “ha’am” in his tefillin. Rabbi Abba said: If there remains the size of a small letter, it is valid; if not, it is invalid. Rami had the leg of the vav of “vayaharog” severed. Rabbi Zeira said: Go bring a child who is neither wise nor foolish; if he reads it as “vayaharog,” it is valid, and if not, it is invalid. The Mordechai explained that where the leg of the vav of “vayaharog” was severed, it does not help that there remains the size of a small letter. On the contrary, if only the measure of a yud remained, it would certainly be invalid, for we certainly require a vav. The same applies to all simple letters, such as zayins and final nuns. But in the case where the leg of the hei was severed, it is valid if there remains the measure of a yud. Maharik, root 69, wrote regarding alefs whose yud does not touch the body of the letter, and regarding the point of the peh that does not touch its roof, that it is invalid, and this is his wording: It is obvious that one cannot validate it through the reading of a child, for these matters were stated only where the letter was severed and its measure was diminished, such as a shortened vav, where we are uncertain whether it reached the length of a vav or only the measure of a yud. Therefore the reading of a child helps, for it is merely revealing the matter that it has the length of a vav, since he did not read it as a yud. But where it is obvious that the letter has not remained in its form, what can the child’s seeing help, when our eyes see that the letter is not in its form? End quote. The Rivash, siman 120, wrote similarly, that a child’s reading helps only where the invalidation is because this letter resembles another letter. But where the invalidation is because its form has been broken, it does not help. This is his wording: Similarly, one cannot validate an alef whose left leg does not touch the middle line because the child reads it as an alef, for due to the separation he will not err to say that it is another letter, namely an inverted ayin, etc. End quote. If so, the Rama’s statement that where the leg of the alef is severed the child’s reading helps requires study against the decisors. One should not force an answer and say that once it had already been written validly and then was severed, a child’s reading helps in all matters. This is not so, for the reason I mentioned in the name of the decisors, and further, Maharik deals with a case that was written invalidly from the outset, and nevertheless he does not distinguish between his law and the severed leg of the vav in the Talmud, which deals with a later severance. Rather, certainly there is no distinction in this. This also appears from seif 25 below, where the Shulchan Aruch wrote that if the leg of the alef does not touch, he may repair it and this is not considered out of order, implying that without repair it is certainly invalid. Maharik wrote this reasoning there as well. The Levush also did not mention this phrase, “or the leg of the alef was severed,” which the Rama mentioned. Nor should one emend the Rama’s words to read hei instead of alef, for regarding the letter hei he said that if its leg was severed, a child helps; but even with the letter hei, a child does not help, as the Mordechai wrote explicitly, cited by the Beit Yosef, for there the measure is specifically the size of a small letter. Therefore it appears that there is a textual error here, and the phrase “or the leg of the alef was severed” mentioned by the Rama belongs afterward. It should read: However, if we see that the form of the letter has not remained properly, or the leg of the alef was severed, it is invalid, etc. This appears to me without doubt or any other spirit.
If a child, etc. It appears to me that this severance refers to where part of the length of the letter downward was completely cut off, and only the part before the break remains. Then it depends on the child’s reading if we are uncertain whether the measure of that letter remains. But if something also remains below the break, namely a gap was made across the width of the leg of the letter and below the gap part of the leg remains, a child does not help, for the child will combine what is below with the upper part, whereas in truth it cannot be combined. This is similar to the severed leg of the alef that I mentioned in the name of the Rivash, where a child does not help. In this case it appears to me that one must cover the part that remains after the break, so that it is not before the child, except for what is above it. This is not comparable to what the Shulchan Aruch wrote here, that one need not cover the other letters, for in this case he certainly agrees that one must cover it. This appears correct and clear. And I saw that my teacher and father-in-law, of blessed memory, wrote: We validate through a child only if the letter was initially written according to law and afterward became invalid; but if it was initially written this way, a child does not help, even where there is no hole. End quote. His words are surprising, for certainly there is no distinction in this. If it happened that the scribe wrote a simple letter and was uncertain whether it is as long as it should be, and he cannot correct it because it would be out of order, certainly he shows the letter to a child. A proof is from the words of Maharik and Rivash that I brought above regarding the yud of an alef being separated, and they speak of the scribe having made it so from the outset, as is clear in their words there, and nevertheless they discussed whether showing it to a child applies, for other reasons. Rather, there is certainly no distinction in this. What the decisors wrote in this matter, “if it was severed,” follows the language of the Talmud, and there the incident happened that way.
And not wise. In the Gemara this remedy was stated regarding the severed vav of “vayaharog Hashem.” Rashi explained that if he is wise, he understands that it would be blasphemous to read it as “yeihareg” toward Heaven, and therefore says “vayaharog.” End quote. This implies that what we require, that he not be wise, means not so wise that he understands the matter and because of that says the severed letter so that it will be properly explained. But if he is wise in recognizing letters well and does not understand what is written before him, this is certainly valid.
And not foolish. Meaning that he does not know how to read.
And one need not cover, etc. Meaning that although he does not understand the matter, one might have thought that because he is accustomed to reading words, he will say on his own what seems to him fitting for that word. Therefore it teaches us that we are not concerned for this. See what I wrote nearby above, that sometimes one specifically must cover.
That which we validate, etc. In the Beit Yosef he proved this, for he was troubled by why we validate a severed letter by means of a hole, since we say in HaKometz that any letter not surrounded by parchment on all four sides is invalid. He answered that this is said only where a letter adhered to another letter, but where it was severed by a hole, it is not; and the wording of Rashi also indicates this, etc. One may further answer that what we validate when it was severed by a hole applies only when it was severed after it had been written, as written here in the Shulchan Aruch. Now from the words of the Tur it implies that he does not hold like the second answer, for he wrote: In the Yerushalmi it implies that it must also be surrounded by parchment inside; therefore, if its entire inside was pierced, it is invalid. According to the second answer, why is it invalid when pierced? Even if it was pierced from the outside it is valid, since it was initially written validly. Rather, necessarily he holds like the first answer. However, this requires study, for the words of the Shulchan Aruch contradict one another. In seif 15 he wrote that according to the Yerushalmi, if its entire inside was pierced it is invalid, like the words of the Tur; but according to his words here, this is valid even from the outside. What the view of the Shulchan Aruch is in this requires study, and it is surprising that the Rama wrote nothing about it.
