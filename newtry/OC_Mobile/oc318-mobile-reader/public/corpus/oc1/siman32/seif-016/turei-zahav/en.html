Or the alef's leg was severed. This cannot be reconciled — I will set before you Talmud and poskim method: Agra — severed for him the heh's leg of "the people" in the hole [explanation: a hole came in the people's heh in his tefillin]; Rabbi Abba: if full small letter remains — kosher; if not — invalid. They asked: severed for him the vav's leg of "and kill"; Rabbi Zeira said: bring a child neither wise nor foolish — if he reads "and kill" — kosher; if not — invalid. Mordechai explained: in severed vav leg of "and kill" — what remains as full small letter does not help; on the contrary if yod measure remained — certainly invalid, for we require vav; and so all simple letters like zayins, simple nuns; but in that case of severed heh leg — kosher if yod measure remains. Maharik root 69 on alefs whose yod does not touch itself, and peh whose point does not touch roof — invalid; and this is his language: plainly one does not validate by child's reading — these matters were said only where the letter was severed and lacks measure, like severed vav we doubt if vav length or yod measure — therefore child helps, mere revelation; vav has measure since he did not read yod; but where plainly the letter does not remain in form — what helps child's sight when our eyes see no letter form — end quote. And Rivash siman 120 wrote: child's reading helps only where invalid because this letter resembles another; but where invalid because form severed — does not help; and so one does not validate alef whose left leg does not touch middle line because child reads alef — for due to separation he will not err to say another letter, i.e. inverted ayin, etc. — end quote. If so, uncertain in Rama's words that when alef leg severed child's reading helps — against poskim; and do not strain to say for already written valid and afterward severed child's reading helps in all — this is not from poskim' reason; and Maharik discusses written initially invalid and still does not distinguish his law from severed vav leg in Talmud discussing severed afterward — surely no difference. And so from Shakh below who wrote Shulchan Arukh: if alef leg does not touch — may fix and no out-of-order — implies without fix certainly invalid; and this reasoning Maharik wrote; also Levush did not mention "or alef leg severed" Rama mentioned; also do not emend heh in Rama's words in place of alef — on heh he said if her leg severed; also in heh child's reading does not help as Mordechai wrote Beit Yosef brought — there measure is specifically small letter — end quote; therefore scribal error seems here — "or alef leg severed" Rama mentioned belongs later; and so must be; however if we see letter form does not remain as corrected, or alef leg severed — invalid, etc., as above without doubt — different matter.

If a child, etc. It seems to me this "severed" refers to severed somewhat from the letter's length below completely, and only the part before the break remains — then depends on child's reading if we doubt whether that letter's measure remains; but if something remains below the break — meaning break in width of the letter leg and after the break part of leg remains below — child does not help, for child combines below with upper part but truly no combination — similar to severed alef leg I mentioned in name of Rivash that child does not help. And therefore it seems one must cover the part remaining after the break and not have before the child except what is above — and this is not like Shulchan Arukh here that other letters need not be covered — in this he certainly admits must cover, as above — correct and clear: And I saw our teacher, may he rest in peace, wrote we do not validate by child unless written initially proper and afterward disqualified; but if written initially thus — child does not help even without hole — end quote. His words are puzzling — surely no difference: if scribe wrote some simple letter and doubted if long enough and cannot fix — out of order — surely show letter to child; proof from Maharik and Rivash I brought above on severed yod from alef — discussing scribe doing so initially as their words explain, and still they debated child's viewing for other reasons — surely no difference. And what poskim wrote "if severed" — they followed the Talmud language and there the incident was so.

And not wise. In Gemara this fix is cited on severed vav of "and kill"; Rashi: if wise he understands it is blasphemy to say "let Him be killed" upward — he says "and kill" — end quote. Meaning we require "not wise" — so wise he understands the matter and therefore says the severed letter and it will be explained well; but not wise in recognizing letters well and does not understand what is before him — this is certainly kosher.

And not a fool—one who does not know how to read.

And one need not cover [the letter], etc. Explanation: even though he does not understand the matter, nevertheless it happens that because he is accustomed to reading the words, he will say by reasoning whatever seems to him to belong to that word; he thereby teaches us that we are not concerned about this—and as I will explain shortly, sometimes one must specifically cover it.

That which permits [a letter split by a hole], etc. In the Beit Yosef he proved this, for it was asked of him: why do we permit any letter that was split through a hole, yet in Hakometz we say that any letter that does not have parchment surrounding it on all four sides is invalid? And one may answer that we say this only where the letter was stuck to its neighbor, but where it was split by a hole, no—and thus Rashi's language is precisely expounded. And one may explain that permitting a split through a hole applies only where it was split after it was written, as stated here in the Shulchan Aruch. From the Tur's words it appears that he did not hold like the second answer, for he wrote: the Yerushalmi implies that the inside too must be surrounded by parchment; therefore if the entire interior was pierced it is invalid. per the second answer, why is it invalid if pierced by a hole—would it not be kosher even if pierced from outside, since it was first written in a kosher state? Rather, perforce he holds like the first answer. But the Shulchan Aruch's words contradict each other: in seif 15 he wrote that for the Yerushalmi, piercing the entire interior is invalid, as the Tur says, yet here, le-filosofia, it is kosher in this case even from outside—what is the Shulchan Aruch's view here? And it is astonishing that Rama wrote nothing about it.
