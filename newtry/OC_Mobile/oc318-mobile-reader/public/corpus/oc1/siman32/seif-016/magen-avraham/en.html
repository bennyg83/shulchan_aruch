The simple letters — such as vav, etc.: if it was severed but enough remains for a full yud, then it appears as a yud.

The leg of the ayin and the like [thus is the correct reading], and so in manuscripts; in another manuscript it reads "the leg of the alef" — a printing error, as stated above.

One need not cover. Maharit, Yoreh De'ah siman 32, wrote that one must cover what is before it, but what is after it one need not cover.

That no form remains — for example, the yud of the alef does not touch, for a child's reading is effective only in the length of the letter [Beit Yosef siman 36 in the name of Maharik, Terumat HaDeshen siman 41, responsum of Maharil siman 127, and at end of ch. 25].

And it was severed in it — even without a hole [Bach]. R' Yehuda HaLevi wrote in a responsum: if there is a thin scratch inside the letter and it does not pass beyond the outline, it is kosher since it does not appear as a letter split in two.

Without the parchment outline — even though it is properly long; see above, seif 4.
