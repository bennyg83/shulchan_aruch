[16] For the sake of a Torah scroll, etc. And there is no reduction of holiness here when writing tefillin or a mezuzah on it, for as long as nothing has been written on it there is no sanctity in the parchment itself — Levush. You may wonder at the responsa of Avodat HaGershuni siman 65, who extensively discussed whether it is permitted to write tefillin on parchment processed for a Torah scroll, and he did not see the words of the Shulchan Aruch, Levush, and Beit Yosef; see siman 42. And it implies from the Levush mentioned above that whatever was written on a sheet for a Torah scroll — any lines — is also disqualified for the name; it is forbidden to write on the remainder tefillin and mezuzot, for it is like one who contracted for it and invited him. Also it compares somewhat to Yoreh De'ah siman 288 that one does not write a mezuzah on a Torah scroll's margin; and in responsa I expanded.
