A vav, zayin, or final nun, etc. This is the text in the Shulchan Aruch editions printed with Maharam Ravkash, in Beer HaGolah and Olas Tamid, and so it is explained in the Levush. But before the Taz the version was that the leg of the alef was severed, instead of nun, and he wrote at length in astonishment from what the Rivash wrote, that one cannot validate an alef whose left leg does not touch the middle line merely because a child reads it as an alef; see there at length. But this is for nothing. Magen Avraham wrote that one must say the correct reading is “the one.” It also seems to me that even according to that version, there is no difficulty, for one can say that the Rama is speaking where the leg of the alef was severed below, while above it touches the middle line, and in that case perhaps the child’s reading helps. However, perhaps the measure of a small letter suffices here, as in seif 15 above regarding the leg of a hei; this requires study.
And it is not necessary to cover, etc. However, Knesses HaGedolah explained the words of Beis Yosef that specifically what is after it need not be covered, but what precedes it must be covered, for such is the custom; and even what is after it, one must cover the lower line, end quote. He did not mention the words of the Levush, and so it also implies in the Shulchan Aruch. The Taz wrote that a child who is not wise means one who does not understand the matter, but is accustomed to reading the words. The Taz wrote as follows: it is correct and clear that this case where it was severed refers to where part of the length of the letter was severed and below it entirely, leaving only the part before the break. Then it depends on a child's reading, if we are uncertain whether enough of that letter remains. But if part of the leg also remains below the break, a child does not help, for the child will join what is below to the upper part, whereas in truth it is not joined. In such a case one can say that the part remaining after the break must be covered before the child, end quote. This requires study, for Beis Yosef wrote in the name of the Meiri as follows: when a hole was made in the middle of the leg until the upper side of the leg was severed from the lower side, this correction depends on the hole being so thin that the break does not prevent a properly corrected reading by an average child, end quote. It is clear that the case may speak even where something remained below, and it is valid through a child; however, that is only where the break is thin, for if it is large, perhaps a child does not help. This is included in what the Shulchan Aruch and Levush wrote, but if nothing remained, etc., for our eyes see that the letter is not halachically proper, end quote. All this applies where it happened after the writing, but where it happened at the time of writing, it appears that the parts are not joined. The Taz further wrote: I saw my teacher and father-in-law, of blessed memory, write that we validate through a child only where it was severed after the writing, but if it was written that way from the outset, a child does not help, even though there is no hole, end quote. His words are puzzling, for certainly there is no distinction in this. If it happened that the scribe wrote some simple letter and became doubtful whether it was long enough, and he cannot correct it because that would be out of order, he certainly shows the letter to a child, end quote. In my humble opinion, it is clear that the Bach speaks of a case where one must also join some of the letter that was written after the break, as I explained, and even in letters that are not simple. In that case he holds it invalid when it occurs during the writing. But if one needs only the part of the letter above the break, as in simple letters, he agrees that it is valid even if it occurred during writing. A complete proof is that the Bach himself concluded as follows: however, if the break in the parchment and blank area is in simple letters, such as vav, nun, or final mem, and the scribe would not have continued any further than the place where it was severed, and it would still have been a proper vav or nun, but he extended the letter beyond the break, it should be validated. But if, from the beginning of the writing, there was only the measure of a small letter of its own until the break, it should not be validated, since it was not written correctly from the start, end quote. This implies that he is speaking initially even of letters that are not simple. Yet it is difficult: why does he not validate where there is, until the break, the measure of its own small letter, meaning the full measure of a small vav or nun? Do we require large letters? One must say that he holds that, in any event, at the time of writing this is not sufficient; this requires study. It nevertheless appears that where there is a hole, it is invalid at the time of writing, since it is not surrounded by blank parchment.
We see, etc. The same law applies where the leg or roof of an alef, or of a peh, or the like, did not touch; because of the separation we would not mistakenly say that it is another letter. So wrote the Taz and Knesses HaGedolah in the name of the Rivash.
