Was broken. This may be explained in two ways: either because of a hole, and this is valid only if it was pierced afterward, as will be explained nearby inside; or because the ink was lacking in that place, and in this way the ruling of the Shulchan Aruch applies even if it happened at the beginning of the writing.
The straight ones. Therefore it does not help in its case if the measure of a yud remained; on the contrary, it appears like a yud and is invalid. Therefore, in this case we require a child.
Or the leg of the final nun was broken. This is how it should read. It means a final nun, and the like, such as a final kaf whose leg is short and resembles somewhat a dalet or a reish. This is from Pri Megadim and Derech HaChaim.
If a child. See the Taz, who wrote that this case of a break refers to where part of the letter's length broke off completely downward, and nothing remains of it except the part before the break. Then it depends on the child's reading, if we are uncertain whether there remains the measure of that letter. But if part also remained below the break, meaning that the break occurred across the width of the letter's leg and after the break a further portion of the leg remained below, the child does not help in this case, because the child will combine what is below with the upper part, but in truth it cannot be joined. This is similar to the broken leg of an alef. Rather, one must cover the part that remains after the break. This is not similar to what the Shulchan Aruch wrote here, that one need not cover the other letters, for in this case he certainly agrees that one must cover it. So is the consensus of the later authorities, unlike what is written in the book Yeshuos Yaakov to be lenient like the view of the Meiri. In Biur HaGra in siman 36, it also implies like the Taz, that we do not join to the letter what is below.
Not wise. This means not so wise that he understands the matter and, because of that, says the broken letter and it is thereby interpreted well. But he is not called wise if he is skilled in recognizing letters well but does not understand what is written before him, for that too is certainly valid.
And not foolish. Foolish means that he does not know how to read the letters. Anyone who knows how to read the letters, even though he is not so expert in the forms of the letters, if he says that it does not have the form of a letter, it is invalid.
And there is no need to cover. So wrote the Levush. But Magen Avraham, in the name of Maharit, wrote that one must cover what precedes it, for if he begins from the start of the verse he will follow the familiar flow. Even with a child who has not learned that passage, one must still cover for him what precedes it up to this word. But this word, and also what follows it, need not be covered at all, in any case.
The letter. For example, alefs whose yud does not touch their body, and the point of the pei that does not touch its roof, and likewise all non-straight letters that have a break in their middle, which is invalid whether this occurred at the time of writing or after the writing. The child's reading helps only where the letter was broken and its measure was reduced, and because of this the letter resembles another letter somewhat, such as a severed vav where we are uncertain whether it has reached the length of a vav or only the measure of a yud; therefore the child's reading helps, for it is merely a disclosure that it has the length of a vav since he did not read it as a yud. The same applies to a final nun and final kaf where there is doubt whether they have their proper length, or to a bent kaf that somewhat resembles a beis, and the like, where there is room to doubt whether its appearance resembles another letter. But where we see that the letter has not remained in its form, what does the child's viewing help, when our eyes see that the letter is not in its form? Therefore, one should not validate an alef whose left leg does not touch the middle line because the child reads it as an alef, for because of the separation he will not err and call it an inverted ayin. The same applies to straight letters: if we see with certainty that it does not have the form of a letter, it does not help that a child reads it that way; only where we are uncertain whether it has the proper measure does it help. All this is without correction. Regarding correction, see Biur Halacha.
When a letter was broken. The same applies to what was validated above in se'if 15 regarding a pierced leg of a hei. But where its hollow was pierced, which the Mechaber validates there, that is even if it was pierced before the writing; he mentioned there, 'if after it was written,' only because of the latter clause, the pierced leg.
And then it was broken. The reason is that we require surrounding blank parchment only at the time of writing. According to this, even if the hole reaches from one letter to the adjacent letter, it is also valid. However, Derech HaChaim in the laws of Torah reading, law 26, is stringent in this; see Biur Halacha. Know further that regarding letters touching each other, even if one knows clearly that it occurred after the writing, it is also invalid, as stated by Derech HaChaim there; and so it also implies from Beis Shmuel in Even HaEzer, siman 125, s.k. 29, see there, for he also holds that one must be concerned for the first answer in Beis Yosef, in the passage beginning, 'And if you will say,' etc., which does not distinguish regarding touching between whether it was not surrounded at the time of writing or only afterward.
From the beginning. When there is doubt as to when the hole was made, we assume that it was made after the writing, for presumably, had it been present at the time of writing, the scribe would have seen it, unless the hole is very small. This is from Derech HaChaim.
A hole. Specifically a hole or tear, even if the break is not visible at all, because it was not surrounded by blank parchment at the time of writing. This is from the first Levush. But a mere break is valid and requires no correction, since there is the measure of a letter up to that place, as above in se'if 15 and se'if 16. If fine scratches are found within the measure of a letter, see Biur Halacha in se'if 25.
A leg. All the more so the roofs and middles of letters that do not have surrounding blank parchment at the time of writing. He mentioned a leg as a novelty: even though by the time he writes the end of the leg the form of the letter has already been properly completed, nevertheless, as long as he has not removed his hand from the letter, this is called at the time of writing, and we require the entire letter to be surrounded by blank parchment.
Without surrounding. Even if it is long enough, meaning that it has such a measure that even if some of it were scraped below, the form of the letter would remain, nevertheless it is invalid. This is from Magen Avraham in s.k. 21; see Magen Avraham, s.k. 3.
From its beginning. But if the parchment was cut afterward and because of this it is not surrounded by blank parchment, it is valid.
Invalid. If he wants to correct it, he should scrape a little so that it will be surrounded by blank parchment. But attaching a patch does not help for surrounding blank parchment, because we require the letter to be surrounded by the body of the sacred writing material.
