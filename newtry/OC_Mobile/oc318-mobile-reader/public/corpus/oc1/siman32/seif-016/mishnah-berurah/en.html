(45) Broken — it can be in two ways: through a hole — then it is valid only if pierced afterward, as explained below regarding inside; or because ink was lacking in that place — in this way Shulchan Aruch applies even if it was so from the beginning of writing.

(46) The simple letters — therefore it does not help if the measure of a yod remains; on the contrary it looks like a yod and is invalid; therefore we require a child here.

(47) Or the leg of nun broke — as should be read. He explains: nun peshutah and the like, such as kaf peshutah whose leg is short and somewhat resembles dalet or reish [Pri Megadim and Derech HaChaim].

(48) If a child — see Taz who wrote "broken" means when part of the letter's length below was entirely severed and nothing remains except the part before the break; then it depends on the child's reading if we doubt whether enough of that letter remains. But if something remains also below the break — the break is in the width of the leg and after the break part of the leg remains below — a child does not help, for the child will combine what is below with the upper part when in truth they cannot be combined. This is like a severed leg of aleph; one must cover the part remaining after the break. This is not like what Shulchan Aruch wrote here that other letters need not be covered — here certainly he agrees one must cover, as later authorities agree (not like Yeshuot Yaakov to be lenient like Meiri; Biur HaGra siman 36 seems likewise like Taz that we do not combine to the letter what is below).

(49) Neither wise — meaning so wise that he understands the matter and from that says the broken letter and it is interpreted properly; but one is not called wise if he is wise in recognizing letters well yet does not understand what is written before him — this too is valid.

(50) Nor foolish — foolish means he does not know how to read letters; whoever knows how to read letters, even if not expert in letter shapes, as long as he says there is no letter-shape, it is invalid.

(51) One need not cover — and so wrote the Levush; but the Magen Avraham in the name of the Maharit wrote that one must cover what is before it, for if one begins from the beginning of the reading the child will take and go; and even a child who did not learn that section — even so one must cover for him what is before it up to this word, but this word and also what is after it one need not cover at all in any manner:

(52) The letter — such as an alef whose yod does not touch itself, and likewise the dot of a peh that does not touch its roof, and likewise any non-simple letters that have a break in the middle — it is invalid whether this was done at the time of writing or after writing; a child's reading does not help except where the letter was severed and lacks its full measure and because of this the letter is somewhat similar to another letter, such as a truncated vav where we are uncertain whether it reached the length of a vav or the measure of a yod — and therefore a child's reading helps, for it is merely revealing an obvious matter: a vav has its length because he did not read it as a yod; and the same applies to a simple nun and a simple kaf where there is doubt whether they have the measure of their length or a bent kaf that is somewhat similar to a bet — and the like where one may be uncertain whether its form resembles another letter; but where we see that the letter does not remain in its shape, of what use is a child's reading when our eyes see that the letter is not in its shape? Therefore one should not validate an alef whose left leg does not touch the middle line so that the child will read it as an alef — for because of the separation he will not err to say it is an upside-down ayin. And the same applies to simple letters: if we see with certainty that it has no letter-form, what a child reads as such does not help; only where we doubt whether it has proper measure does it help — and all this without correction; for correction see Biur Halachah:

(53) When a letter is severed — and the same applies to what he validated above in se'if 15 regarding piercing the leg of a heh; but regarding piercing its hollow, where the author validates — even if pierced before writing and he did not state there whether only after it was written, etc. — rather because of the end of the matter regarding piercing the leg:

(54) And afterward it was severed — and the reason is that we do not require surrounding by parchment except at the time of writing; and accordingly even if the hole extends from one letter to the adjacent letter it is also valid; indeed Darkhei Chayyim in Laws of a Torah scroll siman 26 is strict about this — see Biur Halachah. Know further that regarding touching of letters, even if one knows clearly that it was done after writing, it is likewise invalid [Darkhei Chayyim there, and so it is implied in Beit Shmuel in Even HaEzer siman 125 s.k. 89 — see there, for he holds one must also be concerned for a single word in Beit Yosef in the words "and you shall not," etc., that he does not distinguish regarding touch whether it was not surrounded at the time of writing or afterward]:

(55) At the beginning — and in doubt when the hole was made we are lenient that it was made after writing, for presumably if it were at the time of writing the scribe would have seen it — unless the hole is very small [Darkhei Chayyim]:

(56) A hole — specifically a hole or tear, even though the break is not visible at all, and because parchment was not surrounded at the time of writing [Levush] — but an ordinary break is valid and no correction is needed since there is letter-measure up to that place, as above in se'if 15 and 16. If thin scratches are found within letter-measure see Biur Halachah in se'if 25:

(57) Leg — and all the more so the roofs of letters and their middles, which do not have surrounding by parchment at the time of writing; he mentioned leg to include even though at the time of writing the end of the leg the letter's form is already finished properly — nevertheless as long as he has not lifted his hand from the letter it is called "at the time of writing," and we require surrounding by parchment for the entire letter:

(58) Without surrounding — and even if it is long as required, that is, it has measure such that even if dragged down a little the letter-form would remain — nevertheless it is invalid [Magen Avraham in s.k. 21; see Magen Avraham s.k. 3]:

(59) From its beginning — but if the parchment was cut afterward and thereby there is no surrounding by parchment, it is valid:

(60) Invalid — and if he wishes to correct he drags a little so that it will be surrounded by parchment; but attaching cloth does not help for surrounding by parchment, for we require surrounding of the letter from the body of the STaM:
