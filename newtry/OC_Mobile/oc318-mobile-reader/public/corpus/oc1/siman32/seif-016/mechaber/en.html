If one of the letters was severed —' the simple ones such as vav, zayin, or if the leg was severed [of the alef] (of the nun) and the like [Mordechai Hilchot Ketanot 192]} — if a child who is neither wise nor foolish can read it, it is kosher; and if not, invalid. And one need not cover the other letters for it as is the custom. {Rama: However, if we see that the form of the letter in its proper shape did not remain, it is invalid even if the child reads it properly [Mordechai, Maharik root 69, and Rivash].} This that we validate when a letter was severed is specifically when it was written in kosher fashion and afterward was severed; but if from the beginning when it was written there was a hole and it was severed in it, or if the leg of a kaf peshutah or the like reaches to the end of the parchment without being surrounded by parchment from its beginning — invalid.
