The simple letters. Such as vav or zayin, or the leg of nun was severed — so it should read. So too in Orach Tzedek; so in Mordechai; so the author Yad Aharon; in some texts: or the leg of alef was severed. The Taz raised many difficulties and concluded the severed leg of alef is in its place afterward; however, if we see no letter form remains as required, or the leg of alef was severed, it is invalid; see there. Magen Avraham's text reads "leg of the one" — see there; his words are not understood. See Sefer Torat Chaim, Kuntras Olelot HaKerem.

Not a wise child. The Taz wrote: it seems this severance refers when part of the letter's length below was entirely severed and only the part before the break remains — then it depends on the child's reading whether we doubt if enough of that letter remains; but if something remained below the break — the break in the width of the leg and part of the leg remains below — a child does not help, for the child joins what is below to the upper part though in truth there is no joining; this resembles severed leg of alef, but one must cover the part remaining after the break; this is unlike what Shulchan Aruch says here that other letters need not be covered — in this case Beit Chadash certainly agrees one must cover; we do not validate by a child unless the letter was written properly and then severed; if written thus from the start a child does not help even without a hole; see there. The Taz disagrees and writes there is no difference — if the scribe wrote a simple letter and doubted if it is long enough and cannot correct because it would be out of order, he surely shows the child the letter; see there. See responsum Mateh Yosef chelek 1 siman 1 who defended Beit Chadash; see Kuntras Olelot HaKerem.

Letters. Implying that before and after it need not be covered — so Beit Chadash in Orach Chaim siman 125; see there. But Mahari Weil chelek 2, part 14, siman 32 wrote: it refers to what follows; what precedes must be covered, for if he begins from the start of the verse he continues straight through — so they practiced; see there. Yad Aharon wrote: even if the child did not learn that portion, one must cover what precedes — unlike Get Pashut siman 255, seif kaf-gimel 83; see there.

The letter. Thus Maharik, shoresh peh-tet [siman 9], on the law of alefs whose yod does not touch itself, and the point of peh that does not touch its roof — invalid and cannot be fixed by a child's reading; these words were said only where the letter was severed and lacks its measure, such as a severed vav where we doubt if it reached the length of vav or of yod — then a child's reading helps, for it is mere revelation; if its length is vav and he did not read yod, but where it is clear the letter does not remain in form, what help is the child's sight when our eyes see the letter is not in form? So too Rivash siman 122 — a child's reading does not help except where invalidity is because this letter resembles another; but where invalid because its form was severed, a child does not help. Therefore one cannot validate an alef whose left leg does not touch the middle line so a child reads it as alef — because of the separation he will not mistake it for another letter, namely an inverted ayin; see there. See seif 25.

And severed in it. For Beit Chadash, as I wrote in seif kaf-alef 21, there is no difference between severance by hole and without hole except when the parchment layer in the middle of the letter is visible — we do not validate by a child unless written properly first and then severed; if written thus from the start a child does not help even without a hole. The Taz must say here it refers specifically to severance by hole, then there is this distinction since it is not surrounded by parchment. For this is Beit Yosef's language — but if validated by severance of any letter in a perforation, we say in HaKometz every letter not surrounded by parchment on four sides is invalid; one could say we validate only when severed after writing, for when written it was valid and surrounded on four sides; but if at the start of writing there was a hole and it severed, even if it has letter measure it is invalid because it was never surrounded — see there; Taz — examine. Responsum Rabbi Halevi: if a thin scratch is inside the letter and does not pass beyond the parchment it is valid since no letter appears divided in two — see Mateh Yosef chelek 1 siman 1.

Parchment. Even if it is long enough — Magen Avraham.
