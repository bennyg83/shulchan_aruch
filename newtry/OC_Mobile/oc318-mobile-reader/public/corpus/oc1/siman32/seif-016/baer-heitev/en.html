The simple letters. Such as vav or zayin, or if the leg of the nun was severed; this is the correct text. So wrote Olas Tamid, and so appears in Mordechai, and so wrote the author of Yad Aharon. In some texts it says, “or if the leg of the alef was severed,” and the Taz raised many objections to it and concluded that this phrase about the severed leg of the alef belongs later. Nevertheless, if we see that the proper form of a letter did not remain, or that the leg of the alef was severed, it is invalid; see there. The Magen Avraham reads “the leg of one,” see there, but his words are not understandable. See Sefer Toras Chaim, in Kuntres Olelos HaKerem.
Not wise. The Taz writes: It appears to me that this case of being severed refers to where part of the length of the letter was severed and entirely below, and only the portion before the break remains; then it depends on the child’s reading, if we are in doubt whether the measure of that letter remains. But if something remains below the break as well, namely the break runs across the width of the letter’s leg and another piece of the leg remains below the break, a child is not effective, for the child will combine the lower part with the upper part, while in truth they are not joined. This is like the case of the severed leg of an alef. Rather, one must cover the part that remains after the break. This is not comparable to what the Shulchan Aruch wrote here, that one need not cover the other letters, for here he would certainly agree that one must cover it. The Bach ruled that we validate through a child only where the letter was initially written properly and was then severed, but if it was initially written this way, a child is not effective even though there is no hole; see there. The Taz disagrees and writes that there is no distinction in this matter, for if it happened that the scribe wrote some simple letter and was unsure whether it was as long as it should be, and he cannot correct it because that would be out of order, he certainly shows the letter to a child; see there. See Responsa Mateh Yosef, part 1, siman 1, who defends the Bach, and see Kuntres Olelos HaKerem.
Letters. This implies that neither the letters before nor after it need be covered. So wrote the Bach in Even HaEzer, siman 125; see there. But Maharit, part 2, Yoreh Deah section, siman 32, writes that this refers to what is before the child, but what precedes it must be covered, for if the child begins from the start of the verse, he will continue by rote; and so is the custom, end quote. Yad Aharon writes that even though the child has not learned that passage, nevertheless one must cover what is before it, unlike Sefer Get Pashut, siman 155, s.k. 83; see there.
The letter. These are the words of Maharik, shoresh 69, regarding alefs whose yud does not touch the body of the letter, and similarly the point of a peh that does not touch its roof: it is invalid, and it cannot be validated through a child’s reading. These rulings were stated only where the letter was severed and its measure was diminished, such as a cut vav where we are in doubt whether it reaches the length of a vav or only the measure of a yud. There the child’s reading helps, for it merely reveals the matter, that it has the length of a vav since the child did not read it as a yud. But where it is obvious that the letter has not remained in its form, what can the child’s seeing help, when our eyes see that the letter is not in its form? End quote. So too the Rivash wrote in siman 120 that a child’s reading helps only where the invalidity is because the letter resembles another letter. But where the invalidity is because its form has been interrupted, it does not help. Therefore one should not validate an alef whose left leg does not touch the middle line because the child reads it as an alef, since because of the separation the child will not err and call it another letter, namely a reversed ayin. End quote. See seif 25.
And it was severed in it. According to the Bach that I cited in s.k. 21, there is no distinction whether it was severed by a hole or not by a hole. Rather, when the blank parchment is visible in the middle of the letter, we do not validate it through a child unless it was initially written properly and afterward the letter was severed. But if it was initially written this way, a child does not help, even though there is no hole. According to the Taz, one must say that here it speaks specifically where it was severed by a hole, for then this distinction applies, since it is not surrounded by parchment. For this is the language of the Beis Yosef: If you will ask, where do we validate when some letter was severed by a hole, but do we not say in HaKometz that any letter not surrounded by parchment on all four sides is invalid? One may answer that we validate specifically when it was severed after it was written, for when it was written it was valid, since parchment surrounded it on all four sides. But when at the beginning of the writing there was a hole there and it was severed by it, even if the measure of a letter remains, it is invalid, because it was never surrounded by parchment; see there and in the Taz, and analyze carefully. Responsa Rabbi Yehudah HaLevi writes that if there is a thin scratch inside the letter and it does not pass through to the parchment, it is valid, since it does not appear as a letter divided into two. See Mateh Yosef, part 1, siman 1.
