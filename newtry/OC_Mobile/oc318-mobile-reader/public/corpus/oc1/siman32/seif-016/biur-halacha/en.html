The simple ones. The same applies if the doubt concerns a dalet whose roof is short, and we are uncertain whether it has its required measure; he shows the letter to a child. Acharonim.
Such as a vav or zayin. See the Pri Megadim, who is uncertain whether to say that with a zayin that was severed, the measure of a small letter suffices, since the fact that its roof extends on both sides proves that it is not a yud. This is brought in Chiddushei Rabbi Akiva Eiger. See Derech HaChaim in Hilchos Kerias HaTorah and Shaarei Ephraim, who equated a zayin with a vav as a matter of law. In any event, if the child read it as a yud, it is certainly invalid. See what we wrote in seif 15 in the name of Knesses HaGedolah. See also Beis Yosef in siman 36, under the letter vav.

Knows how to read it. Then, even if it is a large hole or a large break whose separation is plainly recognizable, it is valid and needs no correction, for it should not be better than if he had initially written such a short letter, which is valid.
And if not. Even if the break occurred afterward, such as where the ink jumped off the parchment and only a reddish trace from the rust of the ink remained, and even if the break was so small that its separation was not plainly recognizable, and he showed the child also the part below the break and the child read it as the letter, it is nevertheless invalid, for we do not combine with it what is below, as the Taz wrote. All this concerns leaving it as is without correction. But regarding correction, if the child knows how to read the letter through combining what is below, this helps even for tefillin and mezuzos, where we specifically require writing in order, for the name of a letter was not entirely lost from it, since the child knows how to read it through combination, even if this break occurred at the time of writing. The Pri Megadim wrote that all this is where their separation is not plainly recognizable; but if their separation is plainly recognizable, no correction helps it even if it occurred after the writing. See below, seif 25, in Biur Halacha, what we wrote about this.

When it was written validly. Nevertheless, this is specifically where it was severed by a hole, for the Beis Yosef, in his second resolution, added that even by a hole we require that it was severed afterward. But regarding a letter touching another letter, even if it was written validly and was separated afterward, it is invalid, as implied in seif 18. The Devar Moshe, Orach Chaim, siman 13, brought by Rabbi Akiva Eiger in his novellae, says this. The Derech HaChaim, Hilchos Kerias HaTorah, law 25, holds that this too depends on the two resolutions in the Beis Yosef, and according to the second resolution it is valid, but one must be concerned for the stringency of both resolutions; according to him, this is a legal doubt. There is no proof at all from seif 18, for there it speaks at the time of the writing itself, where after the form of the letter was completed one letter touched another, unlike here where he had already removed his hand. This distinction is necessarily implied from what the Shulchan Aruch wrote about reaching the end of the parchment. Perforce, he does not hold like the Yerushalmi, which validates a letter touching another letter after its form was completed, as the Perishah and the Biur HaGra wrote; and nevertheless, he wrote at the end that only if it lacked surrounding parchment from the beginning is it invalid. This implies that if the parchment was cut afterward and thereby it is not surrounded by blank parchment, it is valid. Perforce, the reason is as we wrote. From what the Gra cited in siman 18, s.k. 50, see there, it also implies that he explains the Shulchan Aruch as speaking at the time of writing, and it does not at all contradict the reasoning of the second resolution mentioned by the Shulchan Aruch in seif 16. What emerges from our words is that there is no proof to be more stringent in this than the view of the Derech HaChaim there, see there, that it is a legal doubt, with a practical difference regarding whether to take out another Torah scroll; see there.
And afterward it was severed. See the Beis Yosef, and see Derech HaChaim, Hilchos Kerias HaTorah, law 26, who wrote there that even according to the first resolution of the Beis Yosef, lack of surrounding blank parchment caused by a hole invalidates, and the Mechaber was concerned for the second resolution only stringently. According to his words, the Mechaber does not distinguish between a puncture before or after writing except where there is at least surrounding blank parchment around the hole near the letter, for the hole does not annul the surrounding parchment around it. Thus there is some surrounding blank parchment at least. But if the hole reaches from one letter to the adjacent letter, it is invalid in every case, for this is considered like one letter touching another, in accordance with the first resolution, which we too follow stringently. Therefore the Mechaber invalidates above, according to the Yerushalmi, even if it was punctured after being written, for puncturing its entire cavity is like one letter touching another; see there. All this is according to his view. But the Pri Megadim, in Mishbetzos Zahav s.k. 8, holds that the essential law of the Mechaber applies only before it was written; see there. The words of the Derech HaChaim are astonishing. If so, why did the Mechaber write, or if the leg, etc., was without surrounding parchment from the beginning? Even if the parchment adjacent to the letter was cut afterward, it should be invalid according to him, for perhaps the halachah follows the first resolution and we do not distinguish between before and afterward; only by a hole would we be lenient because there is at least surrounding blank parchment adjacent to the hole near the letter, unlike where it was completely cut. Rather, it is certain that the Beis Yosef holds that if the hole was made after the writing it is valid in every case, in accordance with the second resolution, that at that point we do not require surrounding blank parchment. Furthermore, it appears to me that all the more so according to the first resolution of the Beis Yosef, lack of surrounding blank parchment caused by a hole does not invalidate. For the Beis Yosef derived this law written in the Shulchan Aruch because he found difficult how we validate any letter severed by a hole, since we say that any letter not surrounded by blank parchment on all four sides is invalid. He answered that we say this only where it occurred by one letter touching another, but not by a hole; or alternatively, that we invalidate due to surrounding blank parchment only if at the time of writing it was not surrounded, unlike where it was written validly. The Beis Yosef here copied the second resolution as law. According to the Derech HaChaim’s new position, that even according to the first resolution one requires surrounding blank parchment and the reason it is valid with a hole is that there is parchment beyond the hole adjacent to the other letter and this counts as surrounding parchment, I am astonished. If so, when the leg of a final kaf reaches the end without surrounding parchment, it should be invalid even according to the first resolution. This is not so, as is proven in the Beis Yosef, that the responsum of the Ralbach, which invalidates this, fits only according to the second resolution. Even if one would force this into the Beis Yosef and say that he wrote the law of the Ralbach according to all views, nevertheless the long Darchei Moshe explicitly proves that according to the first resolution it is valid in this case; see there. Afterward, with Hashem’s help, I found that the Levushei Serad also challenges the Noda BiYehuda, who likewise wrote like the Derech HaChaim. The Pri Megadim too, in Mishbetzos Zahav s.k. 14, wrote explicitly that according to the first resolution of the Beis Yosef we do not require surrounding blank parchment regarding a hole, and even if from the beginning of the writing the leg of the final kaf was without surrounding parchment, it is valid. If so, according to this, even if the hole reaches from one letter to the adjacent letter it is valid for us when it occurred after the writing. Although all this is clear with Hashem’s help, nevertheless practical ruling requires study, since the Derech HaChaim and the Noda BiYehuda are stringent in the matter. At least where another lenient factor joins, such as where the letter is larger than its required measure and the hole was made at its end in such a way that even if one deducts the place of the hole and the measure of surrounding blank parchment the proper measure for that letter remains, there is room to be lenient and to add the view of the Radach cited in this siman in Magen Avraham s.k. 3. For even according to the Derech HaChaim and Noda BiYehuda, it is only a legal doubt, perhaps the halachah follows the first resolution. It is better if he can scrape away a little from the thickness of the line of the letter so that it will be surrounded by blank parchment, for this is effective in every case, even in tefillin and mezuzos, where we require writing in order, as the Magen Avraham permitted scraping the edge of the leg of a final kaf that touches the end without surrounding parchment.
