Dalet. See Taz. The general rule is that scraping does not help to create writing, and this is called engraving from within; but writing does help, as when one thickens the letter from a dalet into a reish or from a reish into a dalet by writing the tag. Regarding out-of-order writing, however, the reverse is true: scraping is valid, while writing is invalid. In our novellae to Shabbos 75a we raised a difficulty from Shabbos 104b to here.
It is difficult. I have a difficulty with what the Shulchan Aruch wrote plainly, that if one wrote a dalet in place of a reish, erasing the tag does not help. In Shabbos 104b Rava said that one who removed the roof of a dalet and made it into a reish, and Rashi explains that he thereby corrected the scroll; see there. In the Chidushei HaRashba he wrote that from here we learn that this is not called engraving from within, even though the text there says “removed its roof,” which is imprecise and means its tag. So too in the Yerushalmi, one who erases one point is liable for erasing and writing, where he made a dalet into a reish. It is clear that this is not engraving from within. See our novellae there. This requires study. I later saw that there is no difficulty here, for that case in Shabbos, regarding a scroll, is not speaking with respect to its validity as a Torah scroll, but regarding the prohibition of keeping an uncorrected scroll in one’s house, not to render it valid for reading and the like. See Beit Shmuel, Even HaEzer 155, letter 3, in the name of the Semag and Mordechai, that on Shabbos one is liable even for marking; see there. His intent is that indeed it is not valid for a Torah scroll, tefillin, or mezuzos, but it is relevant to the prohibition of keeping an uncorrected scroll, lest one learn from it or copy an error from it. But it is not valid. The same applies to Rav Ashi’s explanation there, that one removed the bridge of a ches and wrote two zayins; this is engraving from within, and on Shabbos a marking suffices; see there. According to this, for tefillin and mezuzos, if he made the bridge of two zayins and scraped away the bridge, it is invalid because of engraving from within. The Mechaber follows his own view in Yoreh Deah 279:1 and the gloss there, while the Chidushei HaRashba follows the Rif as cited in Hagahos Maimoniyos, laws of Torah scroll 7:12, letter 10; see there.
