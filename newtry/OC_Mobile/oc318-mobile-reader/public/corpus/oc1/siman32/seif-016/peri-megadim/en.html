Dalet. Taz: the rule is — dragging does not help to be considered writing thus, and it is called hollowing of interiors; but writing helps, for it thickens the letter — from dalet to resh, or from resh to dalet that the scribe writes the tag. And regarding lo k'sidran — the opposite: dragging is kosher and writing invalid. And in our novellae, Shabbat 77a, we challenged from Shabbat 104b to here.

A difficulty for me. What Shulchan Aruch wrote plainly: he wrote dalet instead of resh — erasing the tag does not help; and on Shabbat 104b Rava said: he took the roof of the dalet and made it a resh; and Rashi explained the scribe repaired thus — see there; and in Chiddushei HaRan he wrote: from this we hear it is not hollowing of interiors in such a case; and although he wrote there "he took its roof" — it is an error, but for us; and likewise Yerushalmi: one erases one point and is liable for erasing and writing — he writes dalet and makes it resh — it is clear this is not hollowing of interiors; and see our novellae there; uncertain. Again I saw there is no difficulty here — that case in Shabbat in a book is not for a Torah scroll, but it is forbidden to leave a book not proofread in one's house and not to be kosher to read from it and the like; and Av Bet Hillulim, Even HaEzer siman 155, note 3, in the name of Semag and Mordechai on Shabbat — a list too is liable; see there; and his intention: likewise it does not validate for Torah scroll, tefillin, and mezuzot, but for the matter that it is forbidden to leave (see Ran, chapter HaBoneh, likewise) a book not proofread lest one learn from it and copy an error from it — but it is not kosher. And likewise the resolution of Rav Ashi there: he took the crossbar of chet and wrote two zayins — it is hollowing of interiors, and Shabbat 77 in the list; see there. And therefore in tefillin and mezuzot, if one made a crossbar into two zayins and engraved the crossbar — invalid because of hollowing of interiors. And the author, for his method, in Yoreh De'ah responsum 49, siman 61, and in the gloss, and Chiddushei HaRan like Rif in Hagahot Maimoniyot, chapter 7 of Laws of Torah scroll, halachah 12, note 10; see there.
