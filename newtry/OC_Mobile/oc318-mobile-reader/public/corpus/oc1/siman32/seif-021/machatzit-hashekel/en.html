(Seif katan 21) And it was split, etc. If there is a scratch, etc., and it does not pass through, etc. Meaning, the ink surrounds it. Gloss: Since it is known to all who enter the gates of our city that my revered father-in-law, of blessed memory, did not have before him the responsa of Mahari Beit HaLevi, and I, the young one, was granted by God to see the actual responsum of Mahari Beit HaLevi, I have seen fit to copy his language letter for letter, so that the intent of Magen Avraham's words will be well understood. In siman 81 he was asked, among other matters, about a Torah scroll in which a crack was found in the parchment separating the letters of a divine name. The crack does not pass through to the outside, but is only on the writing side; it is very tight and is not noticeable unless one lifts the letter with his finger from behind the parchment. He responded that in his humble opinion, regarding the letter whose form they sent, he had written that in a Torah scroll it is not so much a concern, and there is no doubt that if it is less than the form they sent, the scroll is valid. The matter therefore depends on the scholars of that place to examine the scroll itself carefully. Since those who read these words have not seen the form of this crack, they cannot understand all that is written, so he explains the case: the scroll is made of gevil, a whole hide, and across the face of the word there is a very thin crack, visible only by very close inspection and almost not describable as a crack, but rather as a scratch. The body of the gevil is intact and good, and its appearance is like the rest of the sheet, for the scratch is only slightly on the surface of the gevil and is not sunken at all. Accordingly, where the ink surrounds it and the split does not pass through, it remains valid. The detailed responsum should be consulted for the full reasoning.
