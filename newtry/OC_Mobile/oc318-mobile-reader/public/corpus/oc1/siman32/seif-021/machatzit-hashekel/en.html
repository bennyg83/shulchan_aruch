(s"k 21) And if severed… if there is a scratch… and it does not pass… meaning ink surrounds it (hagahah: for it is known to all who enter our city gate that our teacher, may the memory of the righteous be a blessing, did not have before him the responsa of Mahari of Brisk. And I, the younger, God favored me to see the responsum of Mahari of Brisk in the original; therefore I saw fit to copy his language letter for letter, whereby the intent of Magen Avraham's words will be well understood, and so on: siman 81, question… also found a Sefer Torah in which there is a crack in the gevil separating the letters of your God, and the crack does not pass from outside, only from the writing side, and it is very tight and not visible unless one raises the letter with a finger behind the gevil. Answer: in my humble opinion it appears… and regarding the letter you sent, its form — I wrote that in a Sefer Torah it is not so — he says there is no doubt if it will be less than the form you sent that the scroll is valid. And this matter depends on the sages of the place to see well in the scroll itself; and since all who see these words of ours have not seen an image of this crack they cannot understand all written — I will set forth the matter as it is. For the scroll is made of gevil, which is whole hide, and across the word passes one very thin crack not visible except by careful looking to examine it well, and it can hardly be called a crack, only a scratch; but the body of the gevil is intact and good and appears like the rest of the sheet, for this scratch is only on the surface of the gevil slightly and does not sink at all — we find no decisor to invalidate, for they always said "torn." And Mahari Kolon in root 122 wrote, and so on: in my humble opinion it seems to distinguish between where the body of the letter was torn and where the tear passes between letter and letter — certainly where the body of the letter was torn and glue holds it, it is found there is no writing except through glue; and although the torn lips touch one another and no parchment is missing, only torn and cracked — nevertheless the issue is that the letter should be surrounded by gevil; and even where the letter's thigh is whole and only parchment inside the letter's hollow is missing, it disqualifies in the Jerusalem Talmud — all the more here where the letter's body is divided in two, only glue holds it; and now, if we do not glue with glue where the letter's body was torn and disqualified, for it is not surrounded by gevil, and it is worse than not surrounded — we do not glue it to consider it a letter through gluing — end quote; behold clear proof from the Rambam that it is not disqualified by this scratch. And this is the precision of his words in general and in particular: it implies specifically where the tear or crack passes from side to side entirely — then we do not glue it, because the letter was already disqualified by not being surrounded by gevil, and it now comes to validate through glue, and it is found there is no writing except through glue. But whenever the sheet's body was not torn or cracked, only a mere scratch on the surface of the gevil while the gevil remains intact, there is no lack of surrounding by gevil — the gevil stands as it was; nor is there reason to say he connects it with glue, since it is whole by itself without gevil — so that if in a Sefer Torah it is less than the image you sent, in my humble opinion there is no concern. So wrote the gaon R' Yaakov — end quote, responsum of Mahari of Brisk, may the memory of the righteous be a blessing; and from now you will understand what is before you in Magen Avraham's words):
