With gallnuts. See Baer Heitev. Panim Meiros, part 2, siman 32, discusses parchment that after processing is split and smeared with white color to make it very smooth and white. Some scribes wanted to prohibit it because one writes not on the parchment but on the color, which separates between parchment and writing. He answered that this is jealousy of scribes and empty speech; it is a preferred mitzvah to write on such parchment because of beautifying the mitzvah, and this color, being attached to the parchment, is not an interruption. It seems that initially one should rub it strongly with a white cloth and clean it well, so whatever is not attached will be removed, while what remains attached becomes one body with the parchment and does not interpose. Still, this should be permitted only for a thin coating, not a thick layer smeared over it.
