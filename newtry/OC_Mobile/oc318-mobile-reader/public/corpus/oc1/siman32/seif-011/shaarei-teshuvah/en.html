In grooves. See Be'er Heitev; and see Penim Me'urot part 2 siman 32: on parchment after processing they divide it and smear white paint so it be smooth and exceedingly white. Some scribes wanted to forbid it because one does not write on the parchment but only on that paint, and it interposes between the parchment and the writing. He answered that scribes' jealousy is this, and vanity will break out their mouths; and he wrote that it is a mitzvah min ha-muvchar to write on such parchment "thus [did] Eli [speak] and I will beautify Him." And this paint affixed to the parchment is not an interposition; also all for its adornment is not an interposition, see there. And it seems l'chatchila one should pass over its surface forcefully with a white cloth and wipe well; then what of the paint is not stuck to the parchment — good adhesion will be removed by this wiping, and what remains stuck becomes one mass with the parchment and is not an interposition. Nevertheless it seems one should permit only a thin smearing, but not if it is plastered on its surface and somewhat thick.
