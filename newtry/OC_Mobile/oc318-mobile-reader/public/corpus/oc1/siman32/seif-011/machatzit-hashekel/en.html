(Seif katan 11) And this is, etc. It does not help to blacken, etc.; Taz in Yoreh De'ah, etc. He wrote so here as well, but he wrote that even if the Israelite assisted only at the end of the processing, it suffices. For Tur wrote there in the name of Rav Natronai Gaon, and the Shulchan Aruch here alluded to it in seif 11, that if it was processed not lishmah, there is a remedy to return the parchment to lime and again bring it to stretching, and that is acceptable. If so, all the more when he assists while the first lime is still on it, it is effective. This is the substance of his words.
Nevertheless, all this follows Beit Yosef's view that this is so. In Yoreh De'ah siman 271, regarding what Rosh wrote in a responsum that in a place where Jewish processors are unavailable one may rely on the Rav who permits if idolaters process it while a Jew stands over them and assists somewhat, Beit Yosef wondered: what does it matter that Jewish processors are unavailable? In any case, a Jew can put the hides into the lime lishmah and idolaters can process them, and it would be processing lishmah even according to Rambam, who holds that a Jew standing over him does not help, for even if a Jew tells him to process lishmah, as we say regarding a get, idolaters do not listen and act with their own intent. Rosh disagrees, for specifically a get, which requires much writing lishmah, the idolater does not do; whereas here, where only the beginning of the processing needs to be lishmah, this is easy and the idolater listens. Since it suffices for the Jew to say at the start of the processing that he is doing it lishmah, Beit Yosef answered: perhaps the idolaters would not allow the Jew to put the hides into the lime, end quote. But Bach wrote there that the effectiveness of saying at the start of processing that he does it lishmah is specifically when the Jew completes and performs the whole processing; then it follows the beginning where he said he was doing it lishmah. But when idolaters complete it, we do not say that it follows the beginning of the Jew's act. Therefore, even if a Jew puts the hides into the lime, Rosh permitted only where Jewish processors are unavailable, and according to Rambam it is invalid; see there. Therefore one who fears Heaven should be careful to take parchment processed by a Jew. Great care is also needed when an idolatrous craftsman processes it, for he places a patch over holes in the hide, and those patches almost certainly were not processed lishmah, since they are from pieces of hides he trimmed off and he takes from whatever comes to hand to place as patches. They are barely recognizable after processing except against the sun.
