(s"k 11) And this is… is not effective to blacken… Taz in Yoreh De'ah… likewise here, except that he wrote that even if the Israelite did not help except at the end of the processing it suffices, for the Tur wrote in the name of Ran Gaon, and Shulchan Aruch hinted here to it, seif 11, that if it was processed not leshmah there is a correction to return the parchment to lime and again bring it to stretching; if so, all the more when he helps while the first lime is still on it, it helps at present:

Nevertheless all this is per the view of the Rabbenu Yerucham, who holds thus, as he wrote in Tur Yoreh De'ah siman 271; see the Rosh in responsum in a place where there are no Israelite workers — one may rely on the Rambam who permits if non-Jews process and an Israelite stands over them and helps a little; and the Rabbenu Yerucham wondered: what of it that Israelite workers are not found — nevertheless cannot an Israelite put the hides into the lime leshmah and have non-Jews process, and it is processing leshmah even for the Rambam (who holds that an Israelite standing over them does not help, for even if an Israelite told him to process leshmah, as we say regarding a get, the non-Jew does not heed and acts per his own mind; and the Rosh disagrees, for a get requires writing much leshmah, which non-Jews do not do, unlike here where only the refinement of processing leshmah is a light matter and non-Jews heed) — for it suffices when the Israelite says at the beginning of processing that he does it leshmah; and so on. But perhaps non-Jews would not allow Israelites to put hides into lime — end quote. But the Bach wrote that what helps when he says at the beginning of processing that he will do leshmah applies specifically when the Israelite finishes and did all the processing — then we trace back to the beginning that he said he does leshmah; unlike when non-Jews finish, we do not say we trace back to the beginning of the Israelite's act; therefore even if an Israelite puts hides into lime, the Rosh did not permit except where there are no Israelite workers; and likewise for the Rambam it is invalid — see there; therefore a God-fearing person should be careful to take parchment processed by an Israelite. One also needs great care when non-Jews process: the craftsman places a cloth over holes in the hide, and those patches are almost certainly not processed leshmah, being pieces of hide he cut; he takes from whatever comes to hand to cast patches, and they are barely recognizable after processing except against the sun:
