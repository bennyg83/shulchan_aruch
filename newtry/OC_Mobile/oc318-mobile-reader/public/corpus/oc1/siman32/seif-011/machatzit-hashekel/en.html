(Seif katan 11) And this is, etc. It does not help to blacken, etc.; Taz in Yoreh De'ah, etc. He wrote so here as well, but wrote that even if the Jew assisted only at the end of the processing it suffices. For the Tur wrote there in the name of Rav Natronai Gaon, and the Shulchan Aruch alluded to it here in seif 11, that if the parchment was processed not for its sake, it can be corrected by returning it to the lime and then bringing it again to a state of stretching and the like. If so, all the more so where the Jew assists while the first lime is still on it, this is effective. These are his words.
Nevertheless, all this follows the view of Beis Yosef, who holds this way. In Tur Yoreh De'ah siman 271, on the responsum of the Rosh that in a place where Jewish processors are unavailable one may rely on the Rav who permits processing by a non-Jew while a Jew stands over him and assists somewhat, Beis Yosef wondered: What difference does it make that Jewish processors are unavailable? A Jew could still place the hides into the lime for the sake of the mitzvah and the non-Jews could process them, and it would be processing for its sake even according to Rambam. Rambam holds that a Jew standing over him does not help, for even if the Jew tells him to process it for its sake, as we say regarding a get, a non-Jew does not obey and acts with his own intent. The Rosh disagrees, because only with a get, where much writing must be done for its sake, does the non-Jew not do so; here, however, only the beginning of the processing must be for its sake, which is an easy matter and the non-Jew obeys. In any case, according to Beis Yosef, it suffices that the Jew say at the beginning of the processing that he is doing it for its sake. He answered: perhaps the non-Jews would not allow the Jew to place the hides into the lime. But the Bach wrote there that when saying at the beginning of processing that it is being done for its sake helps, this is specifically when the Jew completes it and performs all the processing, for then the later work follows the beginning, when he said that he was doing it for its sake. But when a non-Jew completes it, we do not say that it follows the beginning of the Jew's act. Therefore, even if a Jew places the hides into the lime, the Rosh permitted this only where Jewish processors are unavailable; according to Rambam it is invalid. See there. Therefore, one who fears Heaven should be careful to obtain parchment processed by a Jew.
