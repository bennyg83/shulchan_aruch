[12] And if he does not know, etc. Thus wrote the Levush, and it implies that if he knows how to straighten the line he may not rule lines, for it is the work of an amateur; and thus wrote the Bach, except that he wrote that the Rama's view is not so, since the Shulchan Aruch writes one may not rule lines except an upper line, and the Rama added the word "need" — his wording is "one need not rule lines," etc. In my humble opinion it is possible he added this because of the precision that an upper line requires ruling, but in truth one may not rule every line because of amateur work — understand this well. It may also be said to teach that even for the Shulchan Aruch one may rule all four sides, but not between line and line. Sefer Baruch wrote that he said: it seems to me good to rule between the lines on the hair side, but above and below and on the sides one should rule on the flesh side.
