Animal, undomesticated animal, and bird, etc. It appears that the later-mentioned item is more beloved. So too Responsa Menachem Azaryah, siman 37, wrote that bird hide is superior for tefillin to the hide of a domesticated animal, because holy spirits blow within a kosher bird; therefore its blood requires covering. For this reason, the hide of an undomesticated animal is superior to that of a domesticated animal, although bird is superior, as the lightness of the quill testifies. He wrote further that the hide of an unborn fetus is superior to all of them, because it did not benefit from this world. No creature emerges into the air of the world without receiving the judgment with which this world was created, and the fetus is exempt from that judgment because it did not emerge into the air of the world. There is no doubt that the fetus of an undomesticated animal is preferable to that of a domesticated animal, and in both cases one from a slaughtered mother is preferable, for its mother’s slaughter renders it permitted and it has no judgment regarding the fetus. But with a carcass, whether killed by a person or that died on its own, it implies that there is a severe judgment even for the fetus; and the fetus of a slaughtered domesticated animal is preferable to the fetus of an undomesticated animal that is a carcass, end quote. There is room to doubt whether the hide of a slaughtered domesticated animal takes precedence over the hide of an undomesticated animal that is a carcass, for perhaps only regarding a fetus, where there is no judgment in the case of slaughter, is there a distinction between slaughtered and carcass, and so it appears in Baruch SheAmar. This is unlike a domesticated animal that emerged into the air of the world, where perhaps there is no distinction. However, in Olas Tamid it seems that Menachem Azaryah’s intent is that a slaughtered domesticated animal comes first. It also requires study which comes first: the hide of a slaughtered domesticated animal or a fetus whose mother became a carcass. Magen Avraham wrote in the name of Tosafos that even other books that contain Divine Names may not be written on the hide of an impure animal.
