And if he does not know, etc. So wrote the Levush. It implies that if he knows how to keep the line straight, he is not permitted to score, for it is an unskilled act. So too wrote the Bach, except that he wrote that the Rama’s view is not so, since the Shulchan Aruch writes, “one should not score except the upper line,” and the Rama added the word “need,” as follows: “one need not score, etc.” In my humble opinion, it is possible that he added it because of the inference that the upper line does require scoring; but in truth, each and every line should not be scored because it is an unskilled act. Examine this carefully. One may also say that he came to teach that even according to the Shulchan Aruch, one may score all four sides, but not between line and line. Baruch SheAmar wrote: It seems to me that it is good to score between the lines on the hair side, but above, below, and on the sides he should score on the flesh side.
