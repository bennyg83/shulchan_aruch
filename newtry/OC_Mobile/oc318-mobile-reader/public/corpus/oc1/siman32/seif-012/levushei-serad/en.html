Seif katan 7. Before it was written. Meaning, he has still not mentioned writing until afterward, where he wrote, 'and one may write over it.' At first he wrote only that if one passes the quill with ink over it, which implies that he passes the quill only over the hole, meaning a thin stroke no thicker than the size of the hole. Regarding this he wrote that one should not distinguish, and he called that thin stroke a letter. If so, one may not write over it unless that thin stroke is not split in two, and that is impossible unless the hole itself is closed; it does not help that the thickness of the letter surrounds the hole. This is clear.
