(s.k. 7) Until it was not yet written. Meaning: he had not yet mentioned writing until afterward when he wrote "and they write upon it." But at first he wrote only that if the quill passes over it with ink — implying one passes the quill over the hole alone, i.e. a thin scratch not thick, only as full as a hole — and on this he wrote one may not divide; a thin scratch called a letter — if so one does not write upon it unless that thin scratch cannot be divided into two, which is impossible unless the hole itself was sealed; it does not help that the letter's thickness surrounds the hole — clear.
