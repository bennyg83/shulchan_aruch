(Note 52) From one hide. Not as it seems from the baraita, etc., and likewise those made from one hide except that strips are cut from it and nevertheless in the middle they remain connected, and afterward the strips are joined together again — in this way the batim are made; per the Bach they are invalid, since in any case they are torn on all sides of the batim, and according to him sewing and likewise glue do not create connection.

And likewise Tosafos in Menachos — for the Talmud sought to prove there that in a mezuzah, if he made its parshiyot closed, it is valid — from what we learned: a worn sefer Torah and worn tefillin — one does not make from them a mezuzah, for one does not downgrade from a severe sanctity to a lighter sanctity; and it answered that in a sefer Torah these parshiyot — that is, "V'haya im shamo'a" — are closed and in a mezuzah open; or that also in a mezuzah if he made it closed it is valid; and it rejected: perhaps to complete; and Rashi explained that the baraita needed to state the reason because one may not downgrade — even to complete it is forbidden — that is, the beginning of the parsha is written as required for a mezuzah, except that at the end of the parsha one line is missing, which is forbidden: to take that line and attach it to the mezuzah and complete it. And Tosafos wrote: and it answered that it is forbidden thus, for we say in the Yerushalmi that tefillin and mezuzot require that they be written on one hide; and one answers: since they are sewn together, its law is like one hide, see there; and if so, the same law applies to batim — and all this is according to Rashi; and the view of those who disagree must hold like the explanation of Tosafos there — such as if parsha Shema is written in a sefer Torah at the bottom of the column and there is a margin beneath it, and he cuts parsha Shema from the sefer Torah and writes in the margin beneath Shema parsha "V'haya im shamo'a" as required for a mezuzah; or the reverse — parsha "V'haya im shamo'a" is written at the top of the column and there is a margin above, and he now writes in the margin above parsha Shema, see there (and even though in such a case it is lo k'sidran — see on this the Taz in Yoreh De'ah siman 290); and according to this there is no proof because sewing is called connection.
