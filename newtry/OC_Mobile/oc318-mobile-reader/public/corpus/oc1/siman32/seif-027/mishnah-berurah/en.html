That were somewhat erased. Meaning, some of the appearance of their ink has left the letters. If at the beginning of the writing the ink was not black, but appeared like whitened ink whose color had faded, or like red ink, and one must pass a pen over it, this is considered writing out of order. But in the case of the Shulchan Aruch it is not called writing out of order, since even now the writing is valid, and what he adds merely preserves it from being erased further. This is only where some of the ink color remains. But if all the ink has sprung away from the parchment and only a reddish trace of the ink rust remains, when he passes a pen over it he is writing out of order. Therefore, if some ink peeled from part of the length of a vav, and only the rust-trace remained at that spot, and it must be shown to a child, then if the upper part has the measure of a vav, one must cover the lower part so that the child not combine it with the upper part, similar to what we wrote above in seif katan 48. Know further that the Pri Megadim holds that even if actual ink remains, but the upper blackness peeled away and red remained, this is writing out of order, since red is invalid for tefillin. The Chasam Sofer, in Yoreh Deah, siman 256, disagrees and holds that if the change to reddishness came from age, it is valid, for it was written with ink. The halachah requires writing with ink to teach only that it must be written with ink, and it is the way of most ink, as it ages, for its appearance to fade and turn somewhat reddish, becoming like the color called brown. Nevertheless, it is proper to pass a pen over it. Even over the sacred Names it is almost certainly permitted, like ink over ink, for there is no erasure here. But if it changed to actual red, which is not the ordinary way of ink, or even only to reddishness but it changed quickly right after writing, that Torah scroll has no remedy, for it appears that this is not from age. Perforce there is a defect in the essence of the ink, since it was made from other ingredients, and it was invalid from the outset because it was not written with ink; regarding the Divine Names, it is forbidden to pass ink over them.
