Perhaps the law follows the second answer, that one must distinguish between whether it was written in validity or not; therefore here he validates only when it was written in validity, and likewise in seif 15 he wrote, 'if after it was written,' meaning specifically after it was written. He was also concerned that perhaps the law follows the first answer, that there is no basis at all to distinguish between whether it was written in validity or not, but that the Bavli holds that surrounding blank parchment is needed only between one letter and another. Consequently, the Yerushalmi, which requires surrounding blank parchment inside the letter, invalidates even after it was written. Therefore he wrote there, in seif 15, to be concerned for the Yerushalmi where its entire hollow was pierced, even after it was written. Yet this is certainly against the Bavli, for according to both answers, even a hole on the outside is valid after it was written. How much more so inside the letter, which is more lenient, as explained in the responsum of Maharlanch cited in the Beis Yosef. If so, he should not have been concerned for the Yerushalmi at all in this matter. This is clear. The Noda BiYehuda, siman 5, answers this difficulty of the Taz by saying that according to the Beis Yosef's first answer, a hole inside the letter is more severe than a hole outside it; see there. In my humble opinion he erred, and the responsum of Maharlanch in the Beis Yosef escaped him. There Maharlanch refutes the Beis Yosef's first answer from the Yerushalmi, which invalidates a hole inside the letter, and all the more so outside it; see there. Also, his underlying reasoning there, that a hole between letters is comparable to one letter's touching another, is refuted by the end of Maharlanch's words; see there. Thus all the words of the Taz are correct. One who examines the Beis Yosef will see that for this reason the Shulchan Aruch wrote the second answer here, for in the Beis Yosef, after writing the two answers, he brought the words of Maharlanch, who refuted the first answer from the Yerushalmi and proved the second answer. According to that, the Yerushalmi, which invalidates a hole inside the letter, is speaking specifically before writing. Therefore the Taz wondered about seif 15, where he wrote that the Yerushalmi invalidates even after it was written. There is no need to elaborate further here; examine it carefully.
