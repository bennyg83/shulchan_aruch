Apparently one can say: the author feared the stringency of both answers — namely, perhaps answer 2, that one must distinguish whether it was written in validity or not — therefore he validates here only when written in validity; and in seif 15 he wrote "if after it was written," etc. — meaning specifically after writing. He also feared perhaps answer 1, that there is no reason to distinguish written in validity or not, but the Bavli holds one needs surrounding gvil only between letter and letter — hence Yerushalmi, which requires surrounding gvil from within, disqualifies even after writing; therefore he wrote there in seif 15 to fear Yerushalmi when the entire inside was pierced even after writing. But this is certainly against the Bavli, for even pierced outside is valid after writing for both answers — unlike inside, which is more lenient, as explained in the responsum of Maharalnach brought in Beit Yosef; if so, there was no need to fear Yerushalmi here — clear. Rabbi Noda BiYehudah siman 5 answers this difficulty of Taz: Beit Yosef's answer 1 makes a hole inside stricter than outside (see there); in my humble opinion he erred and omitted this responsum of Maharalnach in Beit Yosef, for it contradicts Beit Yosef's answer 1 from Yerushalmi disqualifying inside — and all the more outside (see there). Also the core of his view there — a hole between letters is like touching letter to letter — is hidden from Maharalnach at the end of his words (see there). Thus all Taz's words are correct; one who examines Beit Yosef will see why he wrote answer 2 here in Shulchan Aruch as in Beit Yosef: after writing both answers he brought Maharalnach, who contradicts answer 1 from Yerushalmi and proves answer 2 — then Yerushalmi, which disqualifies piercing inside before writing specifically; therefore Taz wondered on seif 15 that Yerushalmi disqualifies even after writing. One need not extend further — examine.
