That were erased somewhat. But if at the beginning of writing the ink was not black but resembled white that was darkened in appearance, or red, and he had to pass a quill over them — that is out of order [Sefer HaZichronot, thus].

And it is not out of order. Since now the writing is kosher, what he adds is only to preserve it so it not erase further — permitted [Terumat HaDeshen siman 48]; and so Maharil in responsum siman 127 wrote: since it is only to brighten and preserve them, it is permitted. If so, it implies specifically when some ink color remains; but if all the ink flaked off the parchment and only a reddish trace from the rust of the ink remains, it is out of order as above.
