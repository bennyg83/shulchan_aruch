That were somewhat erased. But if at the beginning of writing the ink was not black, but resembled white whose appearance had faded, or red, and one must pass a quill over them, it is considered out of order; Sefer HaZichronos, Knesses HaGedolah.
And it is not considered out of order. Since the writing is now valid, and what he adds to it is only to preserve it so that it not be further erased, it is permitted; Terumas HaDeshen, siman 48. Maharil likewise wrote in Responsa, siman 127, that since it is only in order to brighten and preserve them, it is permitted, end quote. If so, it implies specifically when some of the ink color remains. But if all the ink jumped off the parchment and only a reddish trace from the rust of the ink remains, it is considered out of order. So it seems to me.
