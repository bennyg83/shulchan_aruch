Leather. See Taz. The leather of the compartments is derived from the verse “from what is permitted,” even though the shin is a halacha given to Moshe at Sinai; nevertheless it is derived from the verse “from what is permitted in your mouth.” The same applies to the hand tefillin, since this is true of the head tefillin, and all the more to the hand tefillin. Regarding the straps, which form a dalet and yud, some say this is not a halacha given to Moshe at Sinai, and even according to the view that it is, nevertheless there is no actual writing in them, unlike the shin, which is made by folds; see Tosafot, Shabbos 62a, s.v. “shin.” Now, in Pri Megadim to Yoreh Deah 110 we discussed whether a halacha given to Moshe at Sinai in a case of doubt is permitted according to the Torah or not, and there are many practical consequences here. If he is uncertain whether the leather for the compartments came from a kosher or non-kosher animal, it is invalid; regarding straps, it is a halacha given to Moshe at Sinai. Nevertheless we rule that a doubt in a halacha given to Moshe at Sinai is forbidden; see Yoreh Deah 294 regarding doubtful orlah. This requires study, for in Shabbos 28b, Rashi s.v. “halacha given to Moshe at Sinai,” explains that straps are derived by comparison throughout the Torah.
I was uncertain. What is the law of leather from an item forbidden for benefit, such as an ox sentenced to stoning or idolatry, for compartments and for parshiyos? See Even HaEzer 124, Chelkas Mechokek and Beit Shmuel there. The same applies to tefillin, that the letters fly in the air if the parchment comes from something requiring burning. Nevertheless, aside from that, it is repulsive for a mitzvah, like a shofar of idolatry. Whether one who recites a blessing over it has recited a blessing in vain may depend on what is written regarding a get. Items forbidden for benefit may even initially be used, since mitzvos were not given for benefit. Even one who disagrees regarding a get and forbids it initially may agree here, as we have written.
And ink. Ink made from something forbidden for benefit, such as barley beer that passed over Pesach and afterward was cooked into ink, became unfit even for a dog’s consumption. One can say that chometz is among items that must be buried and whose ashes are forbidden; see below, siman 442, Magen Avraham 15, and Pri Megadim there. But ink made from smoke and charcoal, which is the essential ink as written at the beginning of the siman, if it was made from smoke and charcoal of forbidden wood, nevertheless it comes from items that are burned, and their ashes are permitted even to ordinary use, even for the scribe to earn money from it and take payment. Regarding ink, one cannot say that in any case everything destined to be burned is as though burned and that only black color is needed, which exists. We learn that “its measure is crushed” is one principle, and “anything destined for burning is as though burned” is another principle, even for something that does not require a measure. In this there is a dispute between Rabbi Shimon and the Sages. See Chelkas Mechokek and Beit Shmuel in Even HaEzer there, and with the help of Heaven in the laws of lulav and esrog this will be explained regarding the law of orlah. There are no gallnuts of orlah, for orlah is written regarding a food tree. See what I wrote in letter 28; one can explain the Taz’s words regarding a halacha given to Moshe at Sinai; see there.
