Hide. Taz: here, housing hide — Scripture derives from "that which is permitted in your mouth"; even though shin [of tefillin] is halachah leMoshe miSinai, nevertheless Scripture goes out from "permitted in your mouth" — and the same for shin, since Scripture said so for shin; and straps — dalet and vav — some say it is not halachah leMoshe miSinai; and also for mem-dalet halachah leMoshe miSinai — nevertheless there is no actual writing, unlike shin from creases — see Tosafot Shabbat 62a, s.v. shin. And here in Peri [Megadim], Yoreh De'ah, kuf-yod — we sided that halachah leMoshe miSinai: if doubt — permitted or not? — and practical difference aplenty; here in doubt whether hide is pure or impure — for housings invalid, and for straps halachah leMoshe miSinai; however we hold halachah leMoshe miSinai — doubt is forbidden; see Rashbaz, doubt of orlah; see there. Uncertain — Shabbat 28b, Rashi, s.v. halachah leMoshe miSinai — it is clear straps are from a hekesh in the whole Torah.

I was uncertain. Hide of issur hana'ah — idolatry or [other] — what is its law for housings and parashiyot? See Even HaEzer, siman 124, Choshen Mishpat, and Beit Shmuel there; and the same for tefillin — its letters "fly in the air" if the parchment is matter requiring burning; however in any case it is repugnant for a mitzvah, like a shofar of idolatry; and if he blesses — one may say whether it is a blessing in vain depends on what he wrote regarding a get and issurei hana'ah: even lekhatchilah mitzvot were not given for benefit; and even one who disputes regarding a get and forbids lekhatchilah — here he agrees, as I wrote.

And ink. Of issur hana'ah — barley beer that passed Passover and was cooked afterward — ink is disqualified from dog food; one may say chametz of those buried — its ashes forbidden; see below, siman 442, Magen Avraham 15, and Peri [Megadim] there. Ink made from smoke and charcoal — and that is the main ink, as written at the beginning of the siman — if made from smoke and charcoal, all its wood is forbidden; nevertheless from what is burned — its ashes are permitted even for a commoner; even the scribe may earn from it and take money. With ink it is not appropriate to say "in any case, all that stands to be burned" — one needs only black color; and here too we find we learn from crushed tablets — its measure is separate; and all that stands to be burned is like burnt separately, even in a matter that needs no measure; and in this is the dispute of Rabbi Shimon and the Rabbis — end of commentary; and Beit Shmuel in Even HaEzer there; and there in Laws of Lulav and Etrog the law of orlah will be explained. Wood of orlah — there is none, for it is written "food tree"; see in letter 28 one may say Taz's words on halachah leMoshe miSinai; see there.
