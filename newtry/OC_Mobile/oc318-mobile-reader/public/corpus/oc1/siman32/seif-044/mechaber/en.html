The titura of tefillin is a halacha given to Moshe at Sinai, meaning that he places leather below to cover the opening of the batim, and it appears like the plank of a bridge, which is called a titura. The ma'avarta of tefillin is a halacha given to Moshe at Sinai, meaning that the measure of the titura should be long on one side, and he makes the ma'avarta in it. How so? He cuts it on two sides so that it is not as wide as the width of the titura, so that the squareness of the titura is recognizable. The strap passes through that ma'avarta, and for this reason it is called the ma'avarta. Also for the hand tefillin he should make a titura and ma'avarta. He should roll each parsha from its end toward its beginning, and wrap them in a small parchment. Some are particular to wrap them only in valid parchment. It is a halacha given to Moshe at Sinai that he should wrap around it hair of a kosher domestic or wild animal. Rama: The practice is to wrap hair around the parsha, then wrap valid parchment over it, and then wrap hair over it again. Agur, siman 45. The practice is that this hair should be from a calf. If he cannot find calf hair, he wraps with cow or ox hair, and he first washes the hair well until it is clean. Some of this hair must be visible outside the batim.
