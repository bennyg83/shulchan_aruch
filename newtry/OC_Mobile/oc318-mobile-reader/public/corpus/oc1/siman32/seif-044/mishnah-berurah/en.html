That he should place, etc. Even according to those who hold that the housings must all be from an actual single piece of leather, nevertheless we do not require the titura and the maavarta to be of one piece of leather with the housings. Rather, even if it is a separate piece of leather it is valid, and this is what the Mechaber wrote, “that he should place,” etc. Nevertheless, if possible, it is good to be meticulous even in this, in order to satisfy the view of those who are stringent in this. See Biur Halacha.
(Rav) should be long - and if it is interrupted, it is permissible to sew:
That it be recognizable. For the titura too must by law be square, as we wrote above in seif 39.
From its end to its beginning. When he places it into its housing, like a mezuzah, which is rolled from “echad” toward “Shema,” and not the reverse, for the reason explained in Yoreh Deah, siman 288.
And they wrap them, etc. Meaning initially, but if he did not wrap them, it is valid after the fact if he has no others. See Machatzis HaShekel and Biur Halacha.
In a small piece of parchment. The same applies to a piece of cloth if he has no parchment, as Pri Megadim writes. See Biur HaGra.
In valid parchment. Meaning, but not from a non-kosher domesticated or wild animal. With a cloth, however, even according to this opinion it is entirely acceptable. See Beis Yosef, who wrote the reason: since he is using parchment, it must specifically be from what is permitted to your mouth, as with the housings and straps. Biur HaGra questions this law.
And a halachah given to Moshe at Sinai. Therefore, if he did not wrap the hair around it, it is invalid, even though he wrapped parchment around it.
And the custom is to wrap, etc. For some say that he should wrap the hair around the body of the passage and afterward wrap the parchment over it, and some say the reverse. Therefore the custom is to fulfill both views. See Biur HaGra, who wrote that by strict law there is no concern which comes first.
Of a calf. So that he remember the incident of the calf and not sin, and also in order to atone for that sin. Eliyah Rabbah wrote that for this reason it is good to make every part of the tefillin’s preparation from calf leather, excluding those who make the straps from goatskin.
A little hair. See Magen Avraham, and see Chiddushei Rabbi Akiva Eiger, who agreed that the protruding hair should be less than the length of a barleycorn.
Outside the housings. Some say it should be next to the housing in which the passage of Kadesh is placed, and some say next to Ve-hayah Im Shamoa. It is good that it protrude from the passage of Ve-hayah Im Shamoa on the side facing the passage of Kadesh.
