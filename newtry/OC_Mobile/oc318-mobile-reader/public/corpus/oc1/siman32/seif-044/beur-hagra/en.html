Seif 44, and this is, etc. According to the explanation of the Geonim. One should not ask: if so, in Shabbos 28b, why did they not establish it as referring to the titura and the passageway for the strap? One can say that since it is sewn to it, it is like it, unlike the straps. Mordechai.
He should roll, etc. As stated there on 31b, and as above in seif 32. See below, siman 691, seif 2, regarding what is stated there; and see Tosafot 35a, s.v. passageway for the strap: it does not seem to invalidate, etc., first, etc.
And he wraps them, etc. From what is stated in the Yerushalmi, first chapter of Megillah: it is a halacha given to Moshe at Sinai that they are written on skins, etc., wrapped with hair, and covered with a cloth. Therefore the Rambam wrote that one wraps a cloth around them. But in other Rishonim it is written: with parchment and cloth, meaning with parchment. The Sefer HaTerumah and other poskim wrote that there is no proof from the Yerushalmi, for there it discusses a tear, as written in Maseches Sofrim. The Rambam invalidates even after the fact, following his view that it is a halacha given to Moshe at Sinai; they disagreed with him. Nevertheless, the Mordechai wrote: go out and see, etc., that the practice follows the other Rishonim. This is what is stated in Shabbos 79b, regarding the mezuzah itself, for the word itself is apparently extra.
And there are, etc. As stated there in Shabbos: they were made fit only, etc. This requires study, why it was not established there as referring to the cloth.
And the practice is, etc. For the Rambam wrote the cloth before the wrapping with hair, as written in the Shulchan Aruch. The Sefer Mitzvos Gadol asked that in the above Yerushalmi it is the reverse, and so too in the other Rishonim. Therefore the Agur wrote: and the practice is, etc. But as a matter of law there is no concern which one comes first.
And they practiced, etc. As it is written: and with His mighty arm, etc.; and so it is in the Zohar, volume 2, 237b.
And if, etc. This too is because of the above reason, except that calf hair is preferable, as it is written: and the sole of their feet, etc. See Etz Chaim 32:4.
