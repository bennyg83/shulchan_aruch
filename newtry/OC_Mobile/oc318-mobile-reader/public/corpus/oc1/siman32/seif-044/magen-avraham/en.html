A bit of hair. It is written in Sefer Yesh Sachar, in the name of the Zohar, that the hair should not protrude outside the housings by the measure of a barley-grain. This is the wording of Zohar Pekudei, page 425: within the mystery of tefillin, one calf hair must be hidden so that it protrude outward and be visible, for this strand of hair does not impart impurity outside unless that hair is joined and becomes the measure of a barley-grain; but less than that does not impart impurity. This requires study, for hairs do not impart impurity even if they are many; moreover, less than a barley-grain, even of bone, does not impart impurity, all the more so flesh or hair. It seems to me that the intent is to what we learned in the third chapter of Ohalos: everything from a corpse is impure except hair, etc.; but while attached, all is impure, meaning when the hair is attached to it. If so, this is what it means: this strand of hair does not impart impurity outside unless it is attached, meaning when the hair is attached to a bone the size of a barley-grain, and it “becomes a barley-grain,” meaning that the hair combines with the bone to make the measure of a barley-grain. If so, one can say that it does not matter if the hair protrudes outside the housings more than a barley-grain. The Shelah, page 116, wrote: some say that the hair must protrude from the passage of “Kadesh,” but I received that it is from the passage of “Vehayah im Shamoa.” It seems to me that it is good to place it near the passage of “Vehayah im Shamoa,” on the side facing the passage of “Kadesh.”
