But. See Taz. What he wrote as the Beit Yosef’s first answer, like Rashi on Menachos 29a, s.v. “not surrounded,” I explained well in letter 8; see there, that the words of the Shulchan Aruch can be reconciled. However, what he wrote that the Tur follows the first answer, I did not merit to understand, for the difficulty is obvious: even a hole on the outside does not invalidate unless one letter is actually attached to another, all the more so a hole on the inside. At first glance one can reconcile the first answer by saying that when one letter is attached to another it no longer has the name of a letter, but surrounding parchment is not required. If a final kaf reaches the end or a heh is severed and the like, whenever the form of the letter is recognizable, it remains valid. The Yerushalmi says that the same applies inside, meaning that whenever the entire inner hollow is perforated, it is not considered a proper form, and it resembles a letter attached to another, where its form is lacking; inside is worse when it fills the entire hollow. But according to this, I do not know what I wrote in letter 8, for the Tur concludes that the entire hollow was perforated, and indeed according to the Tur and the first answer the entire hollow is certainly required. This differs from our ruling according to the second answer and the Shulchan Aruch, where if the hollow was perforated initially, even at one side, it is invalid. This requires study. Perhaps indeed this is so according to us; understand.
