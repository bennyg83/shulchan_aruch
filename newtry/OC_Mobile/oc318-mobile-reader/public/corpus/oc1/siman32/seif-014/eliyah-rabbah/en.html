[14] And if he changed [the placement] it is invalid. It implies even on our parchment, since he concluded that it is like their parchment. Even though in Yoreh De'ah siman 271 the Rama wrote that some are lenient in place of hair, and the Bach ruled there to validate ex post facto when there is no other Torah scroll — one must say that a Torah scroll is different, for the Rosh, Tur, Sefer HaTerumot, and Mordechai validate; which is not so with tefillin, where only the Mordechai validates. And further, in Darkei Moshe there it is explained that he ruled even for a Torah scroll that it is invalid. Also it does not compare to a get in Even HaEzer siman 125, that in a place of agunah one should not be stringent. I heard a sign to know the place of hair: when one moistens the parchment it shrinks toward the flesh side; likewise when one cuts a small strap and hangs it and moistens it, it tends toward the flesh side. In any case it seems that one should not invalidate old tefillin because of this sign, for we establish the scribe in a presumption of validity, and we also rely on the Mordechai — whenever we do not know with certainty that it was written in the place of hair.
