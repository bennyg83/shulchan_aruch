And if he changed it, it is invalid. This implies even with our parchment, for he concluded that it is like their parchment. Although in Yoreh Deah, siman 271, the Rama wrote that some are lenient on the hair side, and so too the Bach ruled there to validate after the fact when there is no other Torah scroll, one must say that a Torah scroll is different, because the Rosh, the Tur, Sefer HaTerumos, and Mordechai validate it, whereas with tefillin only the Mordechai validates it. Furthermore, in Darchei Moshe there it is clear that he ruled even with a Torah scroll that it is invalid. It also is not comparable to a get in Even HaEzer, siman 125, where in a case of agunah one should not be stringent. I heard a sign for identifying the hair side: when one moistens the parchment, it contracts toward the flesh side; likewise, when one cuts a small strip, hangs it, and moistens it, it bends toward the flesh side. Nevertheless, it seems that one should not invalidate old tefillin because of this sign, for we leave the scribe in his presumption of validity, and we also rely on the Mordechai as long as we do not know with certainty that it was written on the hair side.
