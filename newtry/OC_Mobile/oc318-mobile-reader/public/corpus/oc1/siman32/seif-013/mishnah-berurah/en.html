Into two. The hole is so small that when he passes the quill over it, the hole is closed with ink and the hole is not felt by the quill, so one may write over it. Even though a little ink fell in that place and a fine hole is visible against the sun, it is valid. But if it was pierced so much that the ink does not pass over it, it is invalid, for because of it the letter appears divided into two. Even if the hole is in the middle of the thickness of the letter, in its roof or leg, and ink surrounds it on every side, it is invalid; and even if up to the place of the hole there is the form of a letter. All this is before writing. But if after writing the letter was divided into two by a hole, we look: if it has the form of the letter up to the place of the hole, it is valid, as will be explained nearby in se'if 16.
