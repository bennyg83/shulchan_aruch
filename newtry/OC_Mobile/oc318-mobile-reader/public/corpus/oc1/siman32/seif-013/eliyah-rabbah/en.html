It appears divided, etc. But if the hole is small, so that when one passes the quill over it the hole is filled with ink, one may write on it, according to the Levush; even though the hole is visible against the sun, since it is not felt by the quill it is valid. So wrote the Bach, Knesses HaGedolah, Nachalas Tzvi, and Olas Tamid. But the Taz wrote simply that if it is visible against the sun it is invalid. This is surprising, for he did not mention that the Bach permits it, and that is the essential ruling. The Bach wrote further that if it is perforated so much that the letter appears divided in two, it is invalid, and before writing it does not help even if a child can read it, since at the time of writing it was not complete. But if it was perforated after it was written, even if it was divided in two it is valid, except that we require that a child be able to read it.
