And he should not score, etc. It is invalid even if the scoring is black, for it is extra writing, according to the Levush. Also in Yoreh Deah, siman 271, the Levush did not conclude that it is invalid. It seems to me that he holds there is a distinction between tefillin, which do not require scoring, and a Torah scroll, which does require scoring. However, Lechem Chamudos, folio 78, wondered about the Levush cited above, since his view is that it is valid; this requires study. If the scoring was done with ink, it appears from Ateres Tzvi that it is valid after the fact. I later found in Sefer Baruch SheAmar that he wrote as follows: It appears that one may score tefillin with tin or lead between line and line even according to Rabbeinu Simcha, who invalidates scoring in tefillin, since it is not called scoring unless one makes an indentation and groove, end quote. Thus he permits it even initially. If so, it is enough for us to prohibit it initially.
