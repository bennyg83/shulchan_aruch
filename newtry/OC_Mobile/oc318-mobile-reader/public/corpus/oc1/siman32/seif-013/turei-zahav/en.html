Divided in two. Ostensibly if there is a hole in the letter's roof or thigh — even if the hole's cavity appears — kosher, since the hole is in the middle and ink surrounds it — then not divided in two; but really not so — for Beit Yosef wrote in name of Riaz: a small hole like a needle point until if one passes the quill in ink it is sealed — that the letter is not divided in two is not called a hole, and one writes over it, etc. — behold he calls the letter divided in two until not yet written; and furthermore requires specifically sealed — for even though ink surrounds, nevertheless called divided in two at the hole place — but requires a hole so small ink blocks it and it is not visible against the sun — at time of writing; but if after writing it was pierced inside the letter — kosher as Tur and Shulchan Arukh wrote nearby on piercing its interior.
