Divided in two. At first glance it implies that if there is a hole within the roof of a letter or its leg, although the empty space of the hole is visible, nevertheless it is valid, since the hole is in the middle and ink surrounds it, so that it is not divided in two. But in truth this is not so, for the Beit Yosef wrote in the name of Rabbi Yitzchak Aksandari: The meaning of a small hole is one as fine as the point of a needle, such that if one passes the inked quill over it, it becomes closed; since the letter is not divided in two, it is not called a hole, and one may write upon it, etc. End quote. Thus he calls the letter divided in two even before it was written. Further, he specifically requires that it become closed. Rather, one must say that even if the ink surrounds it, nevertheless the place of the hole is called divided in two; therefore the hole must be so small that the ink closes it and it is not visible at all against the sun. This is at the time of writing. But if after it was written a hole was made inside the letter, it is valid, as the Tur and Shulchan Aruch wrote nearby regarding the inside being pierced.
