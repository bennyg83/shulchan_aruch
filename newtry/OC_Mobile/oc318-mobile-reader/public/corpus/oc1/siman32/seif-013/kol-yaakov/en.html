43) Seif 13. The parchment should be whole, etc. A small hole like the point of a needle, which closes when ink is passed over it so that the letter is not split in two, is not called a hole, and one may write over it. Beis Yosef; Emes LeYaakov in the laws of holes and tears; Ledevid Emes, siman 14, letter 1; Beis Menuchah in the laws of holes, letter 1.
44) There. Meaning that the letter should not appear, etc. If the hole is small and, when the quill passes over it, the hole is closed by ink, even if the hole is visible against the sun after the writing, it is valid, since it is not felt by the quill. Bach; Shiyarei Knesses HaGedolah, Hagahos Beis Yosef, letter 6; Olas Tamid, letter 21; Magen Avraham, letter 15; Eliyah Rabbah, letter 20; Maamar Mordechai, letter 9; GraZ, letter 17; Meleches Shamayim, klal 7, letter 3, in Chochmah. Keset HaSofer, siman 7, letter 2, wrote that this is, for example, where the letter is thick and the ink surrounds the hole on every side; see there. But the Taz, seif katan 7, wrote that if the hole is visible against the sun it is invalid. Emes LeYaakov, in the laws of holes and tears, wrote that after the fact it is valid. It appears to me that his reference to Taz, seif katan 7, is a scribal error and should read Magen Avraham, seif katan 15; he understood it like the Levush, that the Magen Avraham permits after the fact. So wrote Ledevid Emes, siman 14, letter 1, and Beis Menuchah, letter 1. This is how one should practice: initially one should not write over any hole if it is such that after writing it will be visible against the sun; after the fact, one may permit it if the ink surrounds it on every side. Keset HaSofer there wrote: It appears to me that one should glue a small piece of parchment from the outside and fill the hole with ink. End quote. But if the hollow of the hole is visible to the eye, so that the letter appears split in two at the place of the hole, even if the ink surrounds it on every side, it is invalid. GraZ, letters 16 and 17.
45) And if the quill did not feel it, but we see that the ink came out through the hole to the other side of the parchment, it is invalid. Meleches Shamayim there.
46) There. That the letter should not appear divided into two. But if the hole is large enough that the letter appears divided into two, it is invalid, and it does not help even if a child can read it. Bach; Eliyah Rabbah, letter 20; Atzei Shittim.
47) The requirement that the hole not be so large that the letter appears divided into two applies specifically when it was punctured before the writing. But if it was punctured after it was written, even if it was split in two, it is valid, provided that a child is able to read it. Bach; Shiyarei Knesses HaGedolah, Hagahos Beis Yosef, letter 7; Olas Tamid, letter 21; Eliyah Rabbah, letter 18. This applies if the part extending until the hole has the valid measure of that letter without joining what is below the hole, or if the hole does not interrupt the full thickness of the letter, but a thin complete thread of its thickness remains at the place of the hole; see there.
