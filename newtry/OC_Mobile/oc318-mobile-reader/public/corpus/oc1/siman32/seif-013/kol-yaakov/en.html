Ink. Ink made with an admixture of ordinary gentile wine: some permit writing Torah scrolls, tefillin, and mezuzos with it, Be'er Esek, siman 109; Devar Shmuel, siman 162. Others prohibit it: Maaseh Rokeach on Rambam, chapter 1 of Hilchos Tefillin, halachah 4; Meleches Shamayim, rule 4, letter 3, in Chochmah. The author of Devar Shmuel himself, in siman 164, leans toward stringency, for he wrote as follows: Nevertheless, a sacred matter, and the writing of many mentions of the Name, is more severe; it is proper to sanctify ourselves with what is permitted to us and distance ourselves from ugliness and from anything resembling it, end quote. Keset HaSofer cited him in siman 3, letter 3. See Beis Yosef at the end of Yoreh De'ah, siman 123, Shulchan Aruch there in siman 134, and Yad Eliyahu, siman 35. See Pachad Yitzchak, letter dalet, entry Ink, who wrote in the name of the glosses of Maharar Gael on Shulchan Aruch, Orach Chaim, siman 32, letter 1: I heard that some are careful not to write Torah scrolls, tefillin, and mezuzos with ink bought from gentiles, but only with kosher ink made by a Jew, end quote; see there. Meleches Shamayim wrote likewise there.
