Kadesh. I will explain before you the source of the dispute for clarity. We already said three portions are required — Kadesh, VeHayah ki yevi'akha, Shema open, and the fourth VeHayah im shamo'a closed — only dispute in open and closed form. For the Rosh, explained Yoreh Deah siman 275: whatever has part of a line and begins writing in the middle of a line, and likewise writing and leaving part at line end and beginning the next portion at the next line head — that is open; the measure of the part is enough to write nine letters. Closed is leaving part in the middle of a line as above and beginning at the end of that same line; likewise if he finished the portion at line end he leaves one line as part and begins on a third line. Rambam wrote: an open portion has two forms — if he finished in the middle of a line he leaves the rest of the line empty as above and begins the open portion at the second line start (like the Rosh); if no empty measure remains or he finished at line end he leaves the line after empty and begins the open at the third line start (this is closed for the Rosh). A closed portion has three forms — if he finished in the middle he leaves space as measure and begins at that line's end one word (like the Rosh); if not enough for nine letters and one word at the end he leaves all empty, leaves slight space at second line head and begins the closed portion from the middle of the second line (possibly also the Rosh); if he finished at line end he leaves at the second line start as the space measure and begins the closed portion from the middle of the line (this is open for the Rosh). We find an open portion always begins at line start; closed always begins from line middle. Beit Yosef wrote: how do we make tefillin to satisfy everyone? If we make the three open — leave measure at start and begin portion in line middle — we do not satisfy Rambam, who holds beginning in line middle is called closed. If we leave part at each portion's end, the last is also open, for whatever part is at a portion's end makes the following portion open; therefore in Eretz Yisrael they rule like Rambam: first two portions begin at page top and end page middle; third Shema begins page top and ends bottom; fourth begins line middle — three first portions are open per Rambam, for the first though no part precedes is open like Bereishit, simply open per Rambam; two after the first become open by the measure of the part before — the part at the prior portion's end makes the next open beginning at line head — both Rambam and Rosh agree; only starting portion four in line middle is open per Rosh and we require closed. For Rambam this is closed — thus the author's words are explained. Taz wrote we have another way satisfying Rambam and Rosh; first on Rambam's second closed form: if not enough line remains for space and one word at line end, leave all empty, slight space at second line head, etc.; there I wrote possibly also the Rosh — if so, one who wishes should do thus: portions one and two begin page top, leave at end part for nine letters; portion three also begins page top, leave less than nine; portion four also page start with slight space less than nine — portion four is closed for Rambam and Rosh, for Rambam this is middle start, and for Rosh no open here since no one place has full measure; one could leave nothing at end of portion three and at start of portion four leave less than measure — in any case we require space between portions of nine letters whether closed or open; here impossible in one place lest it be open — therefore we split the measure in two; see there, Taz. So Rama from Pano siman (56) [106]; see Sheyurei Kenesset HaGedolah in Beit Yosef gloss seif 26; do not make two pairs of tefillin for doubt — Beit Yosef; Beit Yosef wrote a great one invalidated all tefillin in his land made per the Rosh because their Torah scroll portions followed Rambam — Torah in his head and tefillin on his head contradict. See Magen Avraham; I said afterward: to know what to do with Rabbenu Tam tefillin in open/closed matters, since portion four precedes portion three — it is simple per Rabbi Beit Yosef: Kadesh and VeHayah ki yevi'akha begin line head; at end of Kadesh leave nine letters; at end of VeHayah ki yevi'akha leave none or less than nine; VeHayah im shamo'a begins upper line middle with nine letters before; at its end leave nine; Shema begins line head — Kadesh, VeHayah ki yevi'akha, and Shema are open for Rambam and Rosh; VeHayah im shamo'a closed per Rambam. Per Taz: Kadesh and VeHayah ki yevi'akha begin line head; at end of Kadesh leave nine; at end of VeHayah ki yevi'akha leave less than nine; VeHayah im shamo'a leaves space at head less than nine and at end nine; Shema begins line head — examine. Yet this is difficult per Magen Avraham siman 34, seif 2; nevertheless they must be written in order as siman 32, seif 1 — therefore he must leave part of VeHayah im shamo'a and write Shema at parchment edge, then write VeHayah im shamo'a in the middle; see there. If leaving at end of VeHayah ki yevi'akha less than nine per Taz, or none per Beit Yosef, then writing Shema at parchment edge at line head is open for Rambam but closed for Rosh; see Rambam's second form of open; after study I saw this is nothing, for after VeHayah im shamo'a above it is fixed and becomes open — this helps as explained Yoreh Deah siman 275, seif 1 in the gloss; see Rama from Pano siman 37; Orach Tzedek; in hesitation Rama siman 25.
