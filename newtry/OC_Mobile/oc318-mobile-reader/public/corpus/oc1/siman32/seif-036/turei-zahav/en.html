Except the last parashah, etc. Explanation: in the Torah it is setuma (closed), therefore one must make it setuma in tefillin too. Even though in the Torah it is setuma because it has affinity with what precedes it—unlike in tefillin—nevertheless write as in the Torah. And as the Beit Yosef in the name of Rambam: if one made the setuma open or vice versa they are invalid. The first three parashiyot in tefillin are all petuḥot (open) and the last setuma. He challenged this: in Hakometz folio 32, on the mezuzah parashah they make them setumot and if made petuḥot they are kosher because in the Torah they are not adjacent—so here in tefillin, granted Kadesh and Vehaya ki yeviakha, which are written in Torah order, must be petuḥot here too; but Shema and Vehaya im shamo'a, which are not adjacent in the Torah—why would it be invalid if one made Vehaya im shamo'a petuḥa? And the Beit Yosef answered: since in some tefillin parashiyot a change invalidates, we do not split the rulings—and it is forced to Rambam. And Rashal in responsum 37 explained that "invalid" that Rambam says means disqualify from the outset of fitness le-khatḥilah. It seems to me to reconcile Rambam, who proved thus from the Talmud, that perforce there is a difference between tefillin and mezuzot in Vehaya im shamo'a: in tefillin it requires setuma even bedi'eved, since in Hakometz Rabbi Natan said: one mitzvah in a mezuzah with setumot—and if made petuḥot, shall we say it supports him? Similarly a worn sefer Torah and tefillin—one does not make from them a mezuzah because one does not lower from a severe sanctity to a lighter one; here one does lower and makes—why here setumot and here in mezuzah petuḥot? Rather perforce even in mezuzah with setumot. And Rambam inferred that perforce this inference of "one does not lower" applies also to tefillin—for if sefer Torah alone, let him state it; for if from sefer Torah one does not make a mezuzah because in sefer Torah they are setumot and in mezuzah we require petuḥot, and the reason stated is because one does not lower—that applies to tefillin too, that one should not make from them a mezuzah even though it would also be kosher petuḥot; nevertheless he gave one reason for both, namely one does not lower—perforce that in tefillin too we require setumot as in sefer Torah and in mezuzah; and if petuḥot are kosher bedi'eved, why did he not say "let us derive support from tefillin" for the reason of not lowering—here one lowers and makes, and why are tefillin setumot and mezuzah petuḥot? He should have supported that bedi'eved kosher petuḥa, since he did not answer thus but answered there a forced change—learn that in tefillin invalid petuḥot even bedi'eved, as above; according to Rambam this is correct.

And some permit, etc. I will explain before you the source of the dispute so that practical halakha may be clarified be-siyata di-Shemaya. We already noted that one must make three parashiyot petuḥot—namely Kadesh, Vehaya ki yeviakha, Shema—and the fourth, Vehaya im shamo'a, setuma; only there is dispute in the form of petuḥa and setuma. For Ra'ash it is explained in Yoreh De'ah siman 275: whoever leaves a portion in the line and begins to write in the middle of the line—and likewise one who writes and leaves at the end of the line a portion and begins the parashah after it at the head of the second line—this is petuḥa; and the measure of the portion is enough to write nine letters. Setuma is whoever leaves a portion in the middle of a line in that measure and begins to write at the end of that same line; and likewise if he finished the parashah at the end of the line he leaves one line as a portion and begins on the second line. Rambam wrote: a petuḥa parashah has two forms—if he finished in the middle of the line he leaves the rest of the line blank in that measure and begins the petuḥa parashah from the beginning of the second line [this is like Ra'ash]; and if nothing remains blank in that measure, or he finished at the end of the line, he leaves the line after it blank and begins the petuḥa from the beginning of the third line [and this is Ra'ash's setuma]. A setuma parashah has three forms—if he finished in the middle he leaves spacing in that measure and begins at the end of the line with one word [like Ra'ash]; and if nothing remains to write nine letters and one word at the end, he leaves all blank and leaves a little space from the head of the second line and begins the setuma parashah from the middle of the second line (and possibly Ra'ash too); and if he finished at the end of the line he leaves at the beginning of the second line spacing in that measure and begins to write from the middle of the line a setuma parashah [and this is Ra'ash's petuḥa]. It follows: a petuḥa parashah always begins at the beginning of a line, and a setuma always begins from the middle of a line. The Beit Yosef wrote: if tefillin are made as the Tur said—leaving at the end of the line a portion for three words of three letters, or at their beginnings, except the last parashah which shall be setuma—in this we fulfill Ra'ash, who holds that beginning in the middle of a line is called petuḥa; but not Rambam, who holds beginning in the middle of a line is called setuma—then all would be setumot if one leaves a portion at their beginnings; and how did our master the Tur write "or at their beginnings"? Perforce there is fitness only if one leaves a portion at the end of each parashah—then even the last is petuḥa, for whoever has a portion at the end of a parashah makes the parashah after it petuḥa. Therefore they practice throughout the land of Ishmael in all their ways like Rambam: the first two parashiyot begin at the top of the page and end at the bottom in the middle of a line; parashah 3, Shema, begins at the top and ends at the bottom; and parashah 4 begins in the middle of the line. Thus the first three parashiyot are petuḥot per Rambam, for the first—even though there is no portion before it—is called petuḥa like Bereishit, which is simply petuḥa per Rambam, as poskim say from his words. The two parashiyot after the first become petuḥot by force of the portion measure before them—the portion that the prior parashah leaves at its end makes the following parashah petuḥa and it begins from the head of the line; this is the same for Rambam and Ra'ash, only that beginning parashah 4 in the middle of a line is petuḥa per Ra'ash and we require it setuma. For Rambam it works that in this manner it is called setuma. In these countries the custom is that all three parashiyot begin at the top of the column and end in the middle of the page, and parashah 4 also begins at the top and ends at the end of the last line—and this is difficult, for per Rambam parashah 4 would not be setuma since it begins at the top; and even per Ra'ash, though we find setuma from the top of the page by leaving the first line blank and beginning from the second line—nevertheless in this case, where one begins from the top and already left a portion at the end of parashah 3, surely parashah 4 is also petuḥa. And one might say the portion at the end of parashah 3 helps only that the parashah itself is petuḥa thereby, but for the parashah after it it does not help at all. This reasoning Maharam of Padua wrote in responsum 77 in the name of Orḥot Ḥayyim, but he did not agree except to validate tefillin already written and spread, for Rambam's words do not prove this. Rashal in responsum 37 greatly challenged this reasoning and there corrected at least our layout because Baal HaItur held all four parashiyot must be petuḥot in tefillin—in this we also fulfill Ra'ash, for bedi'eved he admits one validates petuḥa even parashah 4; and Rambam who wrote invalid if one made the fourth petuḥa—le-khatḥilah he disqualifies it. But per what I wrote above from the Talmud's words, that even bedi'eved invalid petuḥa parashah 4 per Rambam, it is not fitting to do so le-khatḥilah and bless daily upon them. Rashal also challenged further that Baal HaItur's words do not fit at all with saying parashah 4 should be petuḥa—he is against the Talmud and the custom we practice in mezuzah; and this view is void against all earlier and later poskim. And here we have another way to fulfill Rambam and Ra'ash. We begin with Rambam's second form of setuma: if nothing remains of the line to leave spacing and write one word at the end, he leaves all blank, etc.—likewise if done at the end of the page leaving a portion that is not the measure of spacing between parashah and parashah—namely nine letters and room for one word—and begins at the top of the page, giving there a portion less than that measure—this too is called setuma per Rambam; the reason of setuma is thus: since there is not a full measure in one place at the end of the line, likewise if you add to it the spacing before parashah 2, we say it is as if the beginning of the second parashah were written at the end of the line that has a portion at the end of the first parashah—and if so it is fully setuma. Not so if at the end there is spacing in measure—then the following parashah is a separate matter and is petuḥa, all that begins at the head of a line; and likewise at the end of the page with a portion at the top of the page after it, for it is one reason. Whoever wishes to do most correctly should do thus: parashot 1 and 2 begin at the top and end with a portion for nine letters; only parashah 3 begins at the top and ends with less than measure—less than nine letters; and parashah 4 also at the top with at least a little space less than measure—then parashah 4 is setuma per all, both Rambam—for this too is called beginning in the middle—and Ra'ash, for here there is no petuḥa since in one place there is not a full measure. But one should not say to leave a portion at the end of parashah 3 in measure and nevertheless not count as measure for setuma of the following parashah, since he did not leave further spacing for writing the word at the head of the parashah after it—as Rambam explicitly states in setuma that there must be spacing at the end of the parashah to write nine letters and also to write one word; if we did so we would not fulfill Ra'ash, who holds whoever leaves nine letters alone is petuḥa the parashah after it even if it begins at the very top—and then parashah 4 would be petuḥa. Nor should one say not to leave at the end of parashah 3 at all and at the beginning of parashah 4 leave less than measure at the head of the line—for in any case we require spacing between parashah and parashah of nine letters, whether setuma or petuḥa; only here it is impossible in one place, for then it would be petuḥa—therefore we split the measure in two as noted, and fulfill Rambam and Ra'ash. All the above I wrote as correct in my humble opinion; though one should not reflect on any custom, heaven forbid, nevertheless Rashal already wrote that it is not fitting to practice thus from today onward. He wrote layout 1 as seemed to his eyes to fulfill also Mahari Segal and early siddurim that Beit Yosef brings in Yoreh De'ah siman 275. In my humble opinion one need only be concerned to fulfill Rambam and Ra'ash, who saw all the baraitot and explained per their view—and that suffices to fulfill all; and it is exactly like our custom, only we had to widen slightly at the head of parashah 4—unlike the layout Rashal wrote, which is an entirely different matter; and the layout I mentioned is also in Maharam of Padua's responsum there, and he praised it. He wrote that even though our Ashkenazi custom mentioned above may be reconciled, nevertheless henceforth they should not continue. In the Levush he wrote: if my strength avails I will abolish it and conduct writing like the custom of the land of Ishmael. I say: since here the mitzvah is to change, why should we act like the land of Ishmael in which we would not fulfill Ra'ash? Therefore make a slight change, and as said one fulfills all, as above.
