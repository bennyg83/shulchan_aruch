(155) All its parshiyot — meaning, from the arm tefillin, since they are written on one klaf and open and closed parshiyot apply to them — that is, when one leaves an empty space at the end of the last line of the first parasha enough for nine letters, and begins the following parasha at the start of the line on the second sheet, then the following parasha is called open, just as in a sefer Torah a parasha that begins at the start of a line is called an open parasha according to all, when there is a space of nine letters before it in the preceding line or at the end of the last line on the preceding sheet. But in head tefillin, where the parshiyot are written on four klafim, one need not be careful about open and closed parshiyot; nevertheless, l'chatchila the Acharonim were careful about this; and see in Be'er Heitev what he wrote in the name of Peri Megadim on this.

(156) The open ones except — because the first three, which are Kadesh, V'hayah ki y'vi'acha, and Shema, are open in the Torah, and the parasha V'hayah im shamo'a is closed; therefore one must write in tefillin likewise. That is, one should leave an empty space of nine letters at the end of the last line in parashat Kadesh, so that in parashat V'hayah ki y'vi'acha it begins at the start of the first line on the second sheet — the parasha will be open as in the Torah; and likewise after parashat V'hayah ki y'vi'acha, so that parashat Shema will be open; but parashat V'hayah im shamo'a one makes closed, and the manner of closing will be explained below. And parashat Kadesh — although in the Torah it is open because it follows a break of nine letters in the preceding line — here that does not apply, since it is the first parasha; nevertheless, since it begins at the start of a line and nothing is written before it, it is called open, as no writing closes it.

(157) And if he changed — whether he made a closed one open or an open one closed.

(158) And there are those who validate — meaning, b'dieved; and their reason is that although in the Torah parashat V'hayah im shamo'a is closed, nevertheless since in the Torah it is not at all adjacent to parashat Shema, this is not considered a change — for the space between it and parashat Shema is open and not closed, unlike the closed space before it in the Torah; since also in the Torah there is between it and parashat Shema in the Torah a very large open space. And likewise the Acharonim agreed.

(159) In all of them open — and conversely, if he made any of the three parshiyot closed, it is invalid according to all — so wrote the Acharonim. They further wrote that specifically if he made parashat V'hayah im shamo'a an open parasha it is kosher according to this view; but if there is no space at all before it between it and parashat Shema — neither on its sheet nor on the sheet of parashat Shema — meaning that he began it at the start of the top line on the sheet and left no space before it on that line and not at the end of a line of parashat Shema, or he left space but it does not contain the measure of space between parasha and parasha — that is, enough to write nine letters — this is considered a complete change from how it is written in the Torah and is invalid according to all, even regarding this parasha alone. And at the end of parashat V'hayah im shamo'a one need not leave any space, and likewise the practice is to conclude on "ha'aretz" at the end of the bottom line.

(160) And in these countries, etc. — and although l'chatchila it is a mitzvah to make it closed, also for those who validate all open — and so this custom is not proper; nevertheless Rama cited it, since the main point is that b'dieved it is kosher in any case, and it is impossible to make it closed according to his words — one should uphold this custom so as not to cast blame on the early authorities; and likewise Magen Avraham in the name of Levush Chadash. But from the words of the Levush and the Gra it appears that it is better to do as written in the Shulchan Aruch.

(161) The practice also, etc. — and likewise in Rabbeinu Tam tefillin one should make all of them open; and even though he writes V'hayah im shamo'a after Shema, as below in siman 34 in Mishna Berurah seif katan 3, he should leave the measure of nine letters after "ha'aretz," and when writing parashat Shema he begins at the start of the column at the head of the line.

(162) One begins at the start, etc. — for this makes it open according to all when he left space for nine letters at the end of the bottom line on the preceding column. And if he did not begin at the start of the line but left a small space — even less than nine letters — this parasha is called closed per the Rambam and is invalid even b'dieved; and in Peri Megadim [letter 26] he wrote that even if he did not continue inward at the start of Kadesh or V'hayah ki y'vi'acha or Shema — only one or two letters — one could also say it is invalid, and this requires further study; and in Sefer Ma'amar Mordechai it appears that one need not be strict about this b'dieved; and in any case l'chatchila one must be very careful about this [and if he left the measure of the word "asher" — it seems one should be strict even b'dieved, for at this measure there are certainly about six small letters and more, as Machatzit HaShekel wrote; and per the Taz, by law it joins according to all to be called closed even if the space above and below was only the measure of nine small letters by combination, as Peri Megadim wrote — thus what the Rambam wrote "and leave a small space," etc., is a very small measure]. Therefore, if one sees that he began the parasha even one letter inward from where he initially ruled, he should continue the remaining lines likewise.

(163) Nine letters — the Acharonim wrote that l'chatchila one must leave space for three words "asher," and therefore one must likewise leave a full two small letters in addition to the nine letters — for between word and word one must leave a full small letter; and b'dieved one may be lenient when one leaves only the full measure of nine small letters [that is, yudin], as the implication of the Shulchan Aruch here; and Peri Megadim wrote that one measures the space of the nine letters according to that script, and this entire measure applies whether for open or closed.

(164) Setumah per the Rambam — and even though per the Rosh it is petuchah, nevertheless we practice thus because it is impossible to make in tefillin the form of setumah that will satisfy all opinions; for the setumah we practice making in a Torah scroll — namely, that one finishes the preceding parashah in the middle of a line and leaves space for nine letters, and afterward begins the following parashah also on that same line — is called setumah according to all, setumah from before it and after it. It is impossible to make this in tefillin, for each parashah is on a separate column. So too if one wishes to leave one blank line partly at the beginning of a parashah, it would be so — and to begin it at the head of the second line — this too is not setumah according to all, for it is only for the Rosh and not for the Rambam, for according to him it is petuchah. Since it is impossible to make it setumah according to his view, they practiced per the Rambam, because such is the primary ruling even in a Torah scroll if one cannot act according to his view, as written in Yoreh De'ah siman 275. The Taz found a stratagem to make it setumah that will satisfy all opinions — namely, that in parashah one or two one does everything as written in the Shulchan Aruch, only in parashat Shema at its end he leaves a space of less than nine small letters, and likewise he leaves a space of less than nine small letters at the beginning of parashat VeHayah — and it would be so that thereby it does not have nine letters in one place but only by combination it is called setumah according to all; and so too in the responsum of the Rama miFano and in Biur HaGra, and so it is implied from the Pri Megadim that he too practiced thus. Know that b'dieved even if one left a space like nine large letters on every side — also valid, for per the Rambam it is setumah and per the Rosh it is petuchah; and we wrote above in s.k. 258 in the name of the later authorities that they agreed the halachah follows whichever view validates.
