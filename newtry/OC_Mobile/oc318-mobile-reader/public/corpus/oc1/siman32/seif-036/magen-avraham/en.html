And in these countries, etc. It is difficult why they adopted this as the initial custom, for even those who validate it agree that it is preferable to make “Vehayah im Shamoa” closed. It seems to me that the reason is that with closed parshiyos it is impossible to make them according to all opinions; therefore they adopted the custom of making them open. The Bach wrote that they also finish the passage of “Vehayah im Shamoa” at the end of a line, and this is the custom according to Sefer Baruch She’amar. After the fact, if he made it closed it is valid, as stated in Darchei Moshe. The Levush wrote: according to our custom, we do not leave blank space at the end of “Vehayah”; rather, we finish “al ha’aretz” at the end of the bottom line. If he did not leave blank space the length of nine letters at the end of “Kadesh” and at the end of “Vehayah ki Yevi’acha,” they are invalid. Even between “Shema” and “Vehayah im Shamoa,” if he did not leave blank space the length of nine letters at the end of “Shema” and began “Vehayah im Shamoa” at the beginning of a line, they are invalid, for this form is neither open nor closed according to any opinion, unless he began “Vehayah” in the middle of a line and left before it blank space sufficient to write nine letters, which is closed according to the Rambam. That is the custom in Muslim lands, and even according to the Rosh this form is also open; since we are accustomed to make all of them open, if I had the power I would institute this practice initially in these countries, for then they are valid according to all opinions. These are the words of the Levush. It does not appear so to me, for our custom is an ancient custom, and one should not change it, so as not to cast aspersions on the earlier authorities, as stated in Lechem Chamudos.
Closed according to the Rambam. But according to the Rosh, one must leave one blank line at the beginning of the column, and then on the second line begin “Vehayah im Shamoa” at the beginning of the line; then it is closed. One should not, because of this, make two pairs of tefillin, as stated by the Beis Yosef. See what I wrote in siman 34, seif 2. The Beis Yosef wrote that one great authority invalidated all the tefillin in his country that had been made according to the Rosh, because their Torah scrolls had the parshiyos according to the Rambam; if so, one who is called to the Torah while wearing tefillin on his head would be contradicting himself. The Semak wrote, and in tefillin shel rosh, which are four pieces of parchment, one need not be particular about open and closed parshiyos, though the custom is to be particular. So too wrote Kol Bo and Mahari Abuhav, unlike the Beis Yosef, who understood it in another way, that it refers to being written out of order; his words require study.
