And in these countries, etc. It is difficult: why do they practice thus l'chatchila? For those who validate admit it is preferable to make VeHayah Im Shamo'a setumah. It seems to me the reason is that in setumot one cannot make them according to all opinions; therefore they practice making them petuchot. Bach wrote: they also end parashat VeHayah Im Shamo'a at the end of a line, and so they practice according to Beit Shmuel [Bach]. B'dieved if one made it setumah it is valid [Darkhei Moshe]: Levush wrote for our custom: one does not leave space at the end of VeHayah — only "upon the land" at the end of the bottom line. If one did not leave space at the end of Kadesh and at the end of VeHayah Ki Yevi'acha — nine letters' space — they are invalid; even between Shema and VeHayah Im Shamo'a: if one did not leave space at the end of Shema for nine letters and began VeHayah Im Shamo'a at the head of a line — they are invalid, for this form is neither petuchah nor setumah according to his view, unless he began VeHayah in the middle of a line and left space before it for nine letters — that is setumah for the Rambam, and so they practice in the Land of Ishmael. Even for the Rosh this form is also petuchah, yet we practice all as petuchot; if my strength were sufficient I would rule to practice thus l'chatchila in these lands that are valid according to his view, end quote Levush. It seems to me our custom is an established custom and one should not change it, lest one cast aspersion on the early authorities [Lev Chayyim].

Setumah per the Rambam. But per the Rosh one must leave one line blank at the beginning of the column, and then on the second line begin at the head of the line VeHayah Im Shamo'a — then it is setumah. One should not make two pairs of tefillin because of this [Beit Yosef]; see siman 34 se'if 2. Rabbi Yosef Karo wrote that a great one invalidated all the tefillin in his country that were made per the Rosh, for their Torah scrolls had the parshiyot per the Rambam — if so, what arises in the Torah and tefillin on his head contradict each other, end quote. Semak and Tashbetz wrote they are four compartments — one need not be particular about petuchot and setumot, but they practiced being particular, end quote. So too Kol Bo; Mahari Weil disagrees with Beit Yosef, who understood differently — that it refers to not in order — and his words are difficult.
