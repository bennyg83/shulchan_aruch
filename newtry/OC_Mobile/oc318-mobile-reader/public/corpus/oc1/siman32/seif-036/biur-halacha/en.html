He shall make all its parashiyot — See Mishnah Berurah, acharonim on "head" — even if each parashah head starts mid-line kosher though generally closed for Rambam; Peri Megadim letter 49 — possibly strict bedi'eved if evident closed on same parchment; if did not leave nine letters at end (Shitah Mekubetzet) — lenient as other acharonim though Peri Megadim doubts — each parashah separate parchment, Kadesh in Ten Commandments considered open (nothing written before in tefillin).

They leave space for nine letters — Forgot full nine at end of Kadesh/VeHayah — Levush invalid; Eliyahu Rabbah: past tense, started first line top of page — invalid, no open parashah; lekhatchilah if occurs — start second line (also open per Rambam); missing line count (siman 35) — if changed, not disqualified. Mishnat Avraham: two blank lines between parashiyot — even Rosh considers open — leave two lines at parashah start; one line only — Rosh invalid closed, three parashiyot need open for validity; bedi'eved one line — Rambam, do not disqualify; lekhatchilah fulfill all. Peri Megadim: mice ate space — uncertain; Shitah Mekubetzet — lenient.

And if they leave less, etc. — Not for validity; lekhatchilah improper — hints next parashah open; bedi'eved even three lines (JP) — not canceled closed status of VeHayah if not at line head; Rambam rule: closed begins mid-line (Ma'amar Mordechai, acharonim, Beit Yosef Yoreh De'ah 275, Ri ibn Ezra, Gra). Now: left nine at end Shema — majority hold even without space at VeHayah start nine letters — still closed per Rambam.
