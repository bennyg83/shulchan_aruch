According to their words, even if he began the start of each passage in the middle of a line it is valid, although generally, according to the Rambam, that makes it a closed passage. But according to the words of the Pri Megadim in Eshel Avraham, letter 49, see there, perhaps one should be stringent in this even after the fact, since on that very parchment it is recognizable as a closed passage, and what difference does the parchment of the previous passage make to us? However, if he did not leave blank space of nine letters at the end of each passage in head tefillin, it appears to me that one may be lenient, like the plain view of the other later authorities, although the Pri Megadim questions this too there, because each passage is on a separate parchment and is considered like the passage of "Kadesh" in hand tefillin, which is considered open because nothing is written before it in the tefillin.
They leave blank space enough to write nine letters. If he forgot to leave a full nine-letter space at the end of the passage "Kadesh" and the passage "Vehayah ki yeviacha," the Levush explains that it is invalid. Eliyah Rabbah wrote that here he speaks where he already went on and began the first line at the top of the page; therefore it is invalid, for there is no open passage here anymore. But initially, when this happens, his solution is to begin writing at the start of the second line, which is also open according to the Rambam. Even though the number of lines mentioned in siman 35 will not remain, we rule there that if he changed it, he has not invalidated it. However, from what I have now seen in Sefer Mishnas Avraham, who wrote that where one leaves a space of two lines between one passage and another it is open even according to the Rosh, it appears to me in this case that he should leave a space of two lines at the beginning of the passage. If he leaves only one line, according to the Rosh his tefillin are invalid, for it is a closed passage, and the three passages must be open indispensably according to all opinions, as the later authorities wrote. Although after the fact, if he left only a one-line space, the tefillin should not be invalidated, for in the main law we rule here like the Rambam, nevertheless initially one who fears Heaven should certainly fulfill all opinions. The Pri Megadim wrote that one may ask: if he left a nine-letter space and mice or worms ate the blank area, so that no blank parchment remains there at all, is it valid in such a case or not? See there, where he is uncertain about this even regarding head tefillin. According to what I wrote above at the beginning of the se'if in the name of the later authorities regarding head tefillin, one should certainly be lenient.
And if they leave it less, etc. He does not say this as indispensable, but rather that initially he holds it is not proper to do so, because this somewhat indicates that the following passage will be open. But after the fact, even if he left in it a blank space enough for three words of "asher," nevertheless the status of closed passage has not been removed from "Vehayah im shamo'a," since it did not begin at the head of the line. The general rule, according to the Rambam, whom we follow as the main law, is that a closed passage is any passage that begins in the middle of a line. So it is written in Sefer Maamar Mordechai and the other later authorities, and so is proven in the Beis Yosef, Yoreh Deah, siman 275, from what he wrote there in the name of Rabbi Yitzchak Aksandari, and likewise in the Gra's commentary here. Know that in the case now under discussion, where he left at the end of Shema enough space for nine letters, the opinion of most later authorities, almost all of them, even those who disagree with the Taz and hold that combining spaces does not help, is that even if he did not leave a nine-letter space at the beginning of "Vehayah im shamo'a," it is nevertheless called closed according to the Rambam.
According to the Rambam. See the Mishnah Berurah, where we wrote in the name of the Taz about this. The GraZ wrote that initially one should avoid following the Taz's advice. He gives the reason that it is clear from many poskim that they do not accept the concept of combining spaces at all, meaning from the fact that they wrote that there is no way to make a closed passage according to all opinions. Also, the difficulty that the Beis Yosef raised in Hilchos Mezuzah, siman 288, against the Rosh and Tur there would otherwise be a proof from the Rosh and Tur to the Taz. Therefore his intent must be from the later authorities in this siman, who found no solution that satisfies all opinions. If so, there is here neither an open nor a closed passage, and it is invalid; it is better to follow the Shulchan Aruch's advice, so that at least it is certainly valid after the fact. Sefer Maamar Mordechai also questions the Taz's ruling because he is uncertain whether combining helps. Nevertheless, one should certainly not prevent someone who follows the Taz, for the Gra wrote that there is proof to his ruling from the Rosh and Tur, and so too the Gaon Rabbi Menachem Azariah of Fano wrote, as cited in Rabbi Akiva Eiger's novellae, and other later authorities, as I wrote in the Mishnah Berurah. All that is written in the Shulchan Aruch concerns Rashi tefillin. Regarding Rabbeinu Tam tefillin, with respect to open and closed passages, since according to him the fourth passage precedes the third, one should likewise follow one of these three methods. Either he should make them all open, as written in the Mishnah Berurah, s.k. 161, which is our practice in our country for Rashi tefillin and as written in the gloss. One who follows the Mechaber should do as follows: the passages "Kadesh" and "Vehayah ki yeviacha" begin at the head of a line; at the end of "Kadesh li" one leaves a nine-letter space; at the end of "Vehayah ki yeviacha" one leaves no space, and if he leaves any, it is less than nine letters. Then he begins the passage "Shema" at the edge of the parchment, which is the fourth column, since we require the writing to be in order; when he writes it, he begins at the head of a line. Afterward he returns to the third column to write the passage "Vehayah im shamo'a," beginning in the middle of the upper line, leaving before it a nine-letter space, and at its end leaving a nine-letter space so that the following passage, "Shema," will thereby be open. It turns out that "Kadesh li," "Vehayah ki yeviacha," and "Shema" are open both according to the Rambam and according to the Rosh, while "Vehayah im shamo'a" is closed according to the Rambam. According to the Taz as well everything is as above, except that according to him one should leave at the end of "Vehayah ki yeviacha" less than a nine-letter space, and likewise at the beginning of "Vehayah im shamo'a" leave less than a nine-letter space, through which "Vehayah im shamo'a" is called closed according to all opinions.
