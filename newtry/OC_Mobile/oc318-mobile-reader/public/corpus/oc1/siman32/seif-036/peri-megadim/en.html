Even. See Taz. He elaborated, and the content of his intent is that below it is truly one compartment, only above it separates into four compartments, and after the fact this is sufficient. He wrote them on one actual parchment, only above he left a large margin and cut there, inserting each margin somewhat into its compartment; the four parshiyos are on one parchment, and below they stand upright in one compartment. Below there is only one compartment, and they are not rolled, but stand this way, and in any case they are upright. What he wrote that we rule like Rav Dimi but like Abaye is apparently a textual error, and it should read “not like Abaye.” He said it according to the Bach’s method, that we rule like Rav Dimi, that initially it is forbidden but after the fact it is permitted, and not like Abaye, who is stringent even after the fact, and not like the Beit Yosef; see there. “He wrote them on one parchment” is speaking after the fact. According to this, what the Semag wrote, that the shin must extend until the seam, is also not indispensable but only initial practice, for this is the explanation of “some say” in Rav Dimi’s statement in Menachos 35a, as in Beer Hagolah, letter samech. If so, after the fact it is not indispensable. The Rav’s statement, “and likewise the yud must be,” is not so exact, for one is indispensable and the other only initial. Darkei Moshe answered in another way, and in Magen Avraham, letter 62, he explained his interpretation. I did not understand the Darkei Moshe before us, which the Derishah also cites, and I therefore did not want to elaborate on it.
