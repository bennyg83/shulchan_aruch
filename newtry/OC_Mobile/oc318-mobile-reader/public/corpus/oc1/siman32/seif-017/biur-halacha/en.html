If a drop fell, etc. Know that in every case where the Shulchan Aruch writes that scraping alone is ineffective because of the invalidity of engraving the interior, it is clear from the plain words of the poskim that there is no practical difference whether it occurred at the time of writing or after the writing. This differs from an invalidity because it is not surrounded by blank parchment, which depends on the two resolutions in the Beis Yosef, s.v. and if you ask, how do we validate, as the Shulchan Aruch rules in seif 16. So too it is stated in the Pri Megadim, copied below in seif 25. See Sefer Get Pashut, Even HaEzer, siman 125.
A drop of ink. See Mishnah Berurah s.k. 1. This is the language of the Derech HaChaim, laws of Kerias HaTorah, law 35: If wax dripped on a letter on a weekday, he should remove it. If the drip is on letters of the Names that may not be erased, even on a weekday it is forbidden, for one must be concerned that some of the letter will peel off with it. What should he do? He should heat the parchment well from the outside opposite the place of the drip, and through this the wax will come off easily and none of the writing will adhere to it at all. Shaarei Ephraim, gate 5, law 7. If this occurred on Shabbos, then if the letter is visible through it and can be read that way, it is valid. But if it is covered and is not visible through the wax, if this is found between one aliyah and another, since one may not read by heart what is covered by the wax, and one may not read from printed chumashim because of the honor of the congregation, and there is no defect to the scroll since two readers reading from two scrolls is not considered a defect, another scroll should be taken out. If it is found in the middle of the reading, one should read what is covered by heart and afterward complete with that scroll the required number of readers. Since what was covered by wax has already been read, one may continue reading further in this Torah scroll, for the fact that it is covered by wax does not make it invalid. End quote. See the aforementioned Shaarei Ephraim and Chiddushei Rabbi Akiva Eiger on this siman. Rabbi Akiva Eiger wrote further there that when a Jewish craftsman repairs a worn siddur and must place paper and glue it with paste onto the nice area, and he comes upon sacred Names that may not be erased, there is concern for the prohibition of erasing, since it is impossible to peel it off while all the letters remain intact, and this is worse than wax; see there, where he discusses it at length. He concludes that the craftsman should do as follows: he should place paper over each Name, a paper without glue, or glue it slightly with wax, which is not erasing, as above, for the prohibition on Shabbos to scrape wax from a Torah scroll is for another reason; see there. Afterward he should glue the paper with paste, so that the second paper is glued onto the first paper that lies over the Name. This is not erasing, for if the second paper is peeled off, the Name will remain whole.
And it is not recognizable. See Mishnah Berurah s.k. 62 regarding where it fell directly on the lines of the letter and the letter is recognizable; we wrote there to be lenient in this, although the Pri Megadim in Mishbetzos Zahav s.k. 15 inclines to be stringent because of writing over writing, unless the letter had already dried when the drop fell; see there. This has already been challenged because of several difficulties; see Pischei Teshuvah, Even HaEzer, Seder Gittin. Although he somewhat resolves it there, aside from its being very forced, one should not be stringent in this after most poskim are lenient even regarding actual writing over writing, and all the more so with this drop of ink that fell on its own, where the term writing does not apply to it. It further appears to me that there is another remedy even in actual writing over writing where the second writing was not for the sake of the mitzvah: he should pass the pen over it again for the sake of the mitzvah, and in either possibility it is effective. It seemingly is not considered not in order, since from the time of the first writing its form was upon it, as Magen Avraham wrote in s.k. 39.
And through this the letter will be recognizable. See the Mishnah Berurah, where he wrote that by strict law even correction is not required, etc. If the drop fell outside the letter, near the letter, at the time of writing, then even if the letter is still recognizable and it did not thereby touch another letter, by strict law he must scrape the drop at the place where it touches the letter; otherwise the letter is not considered surrounded by blank parchment. So it implies from Noda BiYehuda, siman 75; see there.
And it is invalid because we require “and he shall write,” etc. See Sefer Beis Meir on Even HaEzer, siman 125, who upholds in this matter the view of the Rashba, who holds that this is not considered engraving the interior, since at first a letter was made through writing. The same applies where one erred and wrote a dalet instead of a reish, that there is only a rabbinic concern related to engraving the interior; see there. But Rabbi Akiva Eiger in his novellae here disagrees with this; see there. So too it is proven from Yam Shel Shlomo, Gittin, folio 20, that it is biblical.
And he wrote a dalet instead of a reish. The same applies if it became a dalet after the writing of the reish was completed; it too cannot be validated by carving, according to what the Mechaber ruled regarding a drop of ink and regarding letters adhering to each other, even after the writing was completed, unlike the Semak. Afterward I found that Chiddushei Rabbi Akiva Eiger noted this.
