(61) Ink — and the same applies to another color if thereby the letter's form is changed; but wax that dripped on the letter, even though it covers the letter and is not visible — nevertheless it is permitted to remove it, for wax does not invalidate the writing; see Biur Halachah:

(62) Into — whether the drop touched the body of the letter or not. And the same if the drop fell on the very lines of the letter and the drop spread also outside the letter until the letter is not visible in its image thereby:

(63) And it is not visible — whether thereby its image was changed to another letter, such as a drop fell into the space of a bet and it appears as a peh, or its image was lost and its name is not upon it. And the same applies to other letters; and even if by the drop only the tip of a yod is missing, whether on the right or left, it is invalid. And if one is uncertain about the letter whether its form is upon it or not — one shows it to a child who is neither wise nor foolish:

(64) To drag — and even passing a quill over the letter after scraping its interior does not help. Magen Avraham:

(65) The ink — and even if the drop is still wet and the letter has already dried, we do not say it is merely covered over the letter, since in any case the letter's form was invalidated before correction:

(66) Scraping the interior — meaning he scrapes the inside of the letter and around it, and thereby what is not erased remains in letter-form:

(67) And not scraping — and this too is called scraping since he does not act upon the body of the letter but rather drags a little of the letter until its form is no longer upon it, and afterward he corrects it if he has not yet written after this letter — for otherwise it is invalid in a Torah scroll, mezuzah, and tefillin, for we require in order. And all this law that the Shulchan Aruch states is because the letter is not visible and the first writing is thereby nullified; but if the letter's form was not changed one may erase the ink drop, and there is no distinction whether the ink fell only into the letter's hollow or also on the letter itself and the roof of the letter was thickened or the thigh thereby — nevertheless he may correct and it is not called scraping the interior, since the letter is still visible. By law he does not even require correction in this, only it is better that he correct; accordingly he may make this correction even in a Torah scroll, mezuzah, and tefillin where we require in order — see Biur Halachah. Know further that the Shulchan Aruch deals even when the drop fell after the letter was finished, and all the more if it fell before the letter was finished — that he legally finished the letter first and it is not yet visible; in this all agree there is no remedy by dragging the drop, for there was never a letter-name upon it, only now by dragging — and this is actual scraping of the interior; but if he dragged the drop at the outset and now wishes to finish the letter, there are opinions among the poskim: the Raavad holds it does not help, for he holds that since the drop fell on part of the letter, writing is canceled from it and it is like ordinary scraping — and what would help even if he finishes the letter by writing? And all the more if an actual drop of ink fell on the parchment and it became a kind of half-letter — one may not complete the letter by writing; but Beit Yosef and Rama [in Even HaEzer siman 125 se'if 4] are lenient even in this last law and hold it is not called scraping the interior except when one finishes the letter by scraping. But the latter authorities lean to be strict in this last law unless he draws the wet ink drop and moves it from place to place until he makes a whole letter of it — for this is writing:

(68) There is no remedy — meaning by erasure there is no remedy, but it is permitted to add ink and make it round. And likewise if he erred and wrote a reish instead of a dalet and a kaf instead of a bet, and they were thick letters — there is no remedy that he drag them and set them in the image of dalet and bet, but he may add ink to them and make them in the image of dalet and bet. And likewise if he erred and wrote a simple nun instead of a zayin — there is no remedy that he drag and set it as zayin, for this is actual scraping of the interior; rather he must erase it completely. And likewise if he erred and wrote a heh instead of a dalet — there is no remedy to drag the leg of the heh and leave a dalet that resembles a samekh and bet where a drop of ink fell there and its image was spoiled thereby, for dragging the drop does not help since one does not act upon the body of the letter; and the same in similar cases — rather he must also drag a little of the roof until it remains like a vav, or drag the thigh until no dalet-form remains upon it, and afterward he corrects it. The general rule: dragging does not help to constitute writing and is called scraping the interior; but writing helps, for he thickens the letter — from lamed to reish, or from reish to dalet, when he writes the tag. And regarding not in order, the reverse — dragging is valid, such as in adhesion of letter to letter as below, for this is not called acting upon the body of the letter; but writing is invalid. And what we permitted to add ink and set them in their image — that is when he had not yet written the letters after this:
