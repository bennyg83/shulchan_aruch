Ink. The same applies to another color if through it the form of the letter is changed. But if wax dripped onto a letter, even though it covers the letter and it is not recognizable, nevertheless it is permitted to remove it, for wax does not nullify the writing. See Biur Halacha.
Into. Whether the drop touched the body of the letter or not. The same applies if the drop fell directly on the strokes of the letter, and the drop also spread outside the letter until the letter is no longer recognizable in its form because of it.
And it is not recognizable. Whether through this its form changed into another letter, such as when a drop fell inside the hollow of a beis and it appears like a pei, or its form was lost and the name of the letter is not upon it. The same applies to other letters. Even if through the drop only the point of the yud was lacking, whether on the right or on the left, it is invalid. If one is uncertain whether the letter still has its form, he shows it to a child who is neither wise nor foolish.
To scrape. Even passing a quill over the letter after he has hollowed out its inside does not help. Magen Avraham.
The ink. Even if the drop is still wet and the letter has already dried, we do not say that it is merely like a covering over the letter, since in any event the form of the letter was invalidated before the correction.
Chak tochos. This means that one hollows out the inside of the letter and around it, so that automatically what he does not erase remains in the form of a letter.
And not by hollowing. This too is called engraving, since he is not performing an act on the body of the letter. Rather, he must also scrape away some of the letter until its form no longer remains upon it, and afterward correct it, provided that he has not yet written after this letter; otherwise it is invalid for tefillin and mezuzos, where we require writing in order. All this law of the Shulchan Aruch is because the letter is not recognizable and the original writing is thereby nullified. But if the form of the letter was not changed, he may erase the drop of ink. There is no distinction whether the ink fell only into the hollow of the letter or also upon the body of the letter and thereby thickened the roof or even the leg; nevertheless he may correct it, and it is not called chak tochos, since the letter is still recognizable. Strictly speaking, in such a case no correction is even required, only it is better that he correct it. Therefore he may make this correction even in tefillin and mezuzos, where writing in order is required. See Biur Halacha. Know further that the Shulchan Aruch speaks even where the drop fell after the letter was completed; all the more so if it fell before the letter was completed properly, and he completed the letter while it was still unrecognizable. In that case everyone agrees that scraping the drop does not repair it, for it never had the name of a letter upon it, and only now, through scraping, it becomes a letter, which is actual chak tochos. But if he first scraped away the drop and now wishes to complete the letter, there are differing opinions among the poskim. The Radach holds that it does not help, for since the drop fell upon part of the letter, the status of writing was removed from it and it is mere engraving; what then is gained even if he completes the letter by writing? All the more so, if a drop of ink actually fell on the parchment and became like half a letter, one may not complete the letter by writing. The Beis Yosef and Rama, in Even HaEzer siman 125, seif 4, are lenient even in this latter case, and hold that it is called chak tochos only when one completes the letter by engraving. The later authorities, however, incline to be stringent in this last case, unless he draws the wet drop of ink and moves it from place to place until he makes from it a complete letter, for that is considered writing.
There is no remedy. Meaning, there is no remedy by erasing, but one may add ink and make it round. Similarly, if one erred and wrote a reish instead of a dalet, or a kaf instead of a beis, and the letters are thick, there is no remedy by scraping them and setting them in the form of a dalet and beis; but one may add ink to them and make them into the form of a dalet and beis. Likewise, if he erred and wrote a final nun in place of a zayin, there is no remedy by scraping it and setting it as a zayin, for this is actual chak tochos; rather, he must erase it completely. So too, if he erred and wrote a hei instead of a dalet, there is no remedy by scraping off the leg of the hei so that a dalet remains, for it is like a samech or beis into which a drop of ink fell and whose form was thereby ruined, where scraping the drop does not help because he is not performing an act on the body of the letter. Here too it is the same. Rather, he must also scrape some of the roof until it remains like a vav, or scrape the thigh until the form of a dalet no longer remains upon it, and afterward correct it. The general rule is: scraping does not help to make that an act of writing, and it is called chak tochos; but writing helps, as when he thickens a letter from a dalet into a reish, or from a reish into a dalet by writing the tag. Regarding writing out of order, the reverse is true: scraping is valid, as in the case of a letter adhering to another letter, as below, for this is not called an act on the body of the letter; but writing is invalid. What we permitted, to add ink and restore the letters to their form, is only where he has not written the subsequent letters.
To erase the tag. Even if afterward he will also extend the roof and bottom of the kaf, it does not help, for even without this its form is already upon it.
