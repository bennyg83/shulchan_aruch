If it removes a point of the letter, or if it appears as though it is part of the body of the letter, even though the letter is plainly recognizable, it is invalid; Radach, Bayis Alef, chambers 31, 32, 33, and 34; Knesses HaGedolah. Even if he erased the drop completely and completed the letter through writing, it is invalid, because we require the whole letter to be made through writing; Radach. See Rabbi Moshe Alashkar, siman 5. In Knesses HaGedolah he wrote: if a drop of ink fell and it is shaped like half a letter, we have not found anyone who permits completing the letter through writing; but Beis Yosef in Even HaEzer, siman 125, validates it, and Rama ruled likewise there in se'if 4. See there for several laws. If a drop fell into the hollow of a beis and it appears like a pei, or the name beis is no longer upon it, or it fell into another letter and its name is no longer upon it, there is no remedy by erasing; Beis Yosef in the name of Sefer HaTerumah. It seems to me that if wax dripped on it and he removed it, it is permitted; see what I wrote in siman 340, se'if 3.
A beis in place of a kaf. The same applies in the reverse case; Rabbi Moshe Alashkar and Knesses HaGedolah. If he wrote a dalet that resembles a reish, he may correct it with a quill as long as he has not written further; Beis Yosef in the name of Ri'a, and so the Rivash wrote, siman 7. The same applies from a beis to a kaf.
