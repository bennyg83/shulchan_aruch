And afterward it became invalid, etc. This is difficult, in light of what the Tur and Shulchan Aruch wrote, that the Yerushalmi invalidates when the entire inside is perforated, even after the writing. So too the Levush wrote in se'if 13. If so, all the more should we require a large surrounding blank area on the outside, and if so, even if it happened after the writing it should invalidate. A similar difficulty also arises for me on the Beis Yosef, s.v. 'and if you say, etc.,' regarding the explanations he gave there, and also regarding the responsum of Rabbi Levi that the Beis Yosef cited. This requires study, and I have been brief.
To scrape away the ink, etc. This is so even though the ink is still wet, as the Taz writes. In Even HaEzer siman 125, se'if 9, it is clear that there is no distinction between letters that are wet and letters that are dry.
If he erred and wrote, etc. It appears that the same applies if the letter was first written properly and afterward became damaged and took the form of a dalet; it is likewise invalid, and it has no remedy by scraping away all of it, because that would be out of order, unless nothing was written afterward. The Taz wrote that specifically through erasing the tag there is no remedy, but if he wants to add ink to it and make it round, that is proper. Similarly, if he made a reish instead of a dalet, it can be corrected by adding ink and making it square, and this is when nothing was written afterward, end quote. It seems to me that the same applies to a beis and kaf, and it seems to me that this is the meaning of the Rivash in siman 7, not like Maharam Alashkar. Olas Tamid wrote that it implies that if the letter was somewhat recognizable, even though it changed, such as where ink fell on a samech and thereby the hollow became small and the area around the letter thickened, it is valid, provided that it is recognizable to a child. But Knesses HaGedolah wrote as follows: the Radach concluded that if a drop of ink fell on a letter and it appears as if it is part of the body of the letter, even if the letter is recognizable to a child, it is invalid, end quote. So too the Levush wrote there in Even HaEzer. The Chelkas Mechokek wrote that after the fact it is valid, and this requires study. The body of the responsum of the Radach also requires study, for it is not in my possession at present. Also what the Magen Avraham wrote in the name of the responsum of the Radach is surprising; see there. He further wrote that if a drop fell into the hollow of a beis and it appears like a peh, or it no longer bears the name of a beis, or if it fell into another letter and it is not recognizable to a child, it is invalid.
