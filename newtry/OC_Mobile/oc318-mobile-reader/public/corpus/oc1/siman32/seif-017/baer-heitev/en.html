The letter. If it lacks the thorn of a letter, or it appears as if the blot is part of the body of the letter, then even though the letter is clearly recognizable, it is invalid: Radach and Knesses HaGedolah. The Pri Chadash, in Even HaEzer, siman 125, s.k. 15, disagrees with him and writes: I see no reason for this ruling, for if the roof or leg became thickened by that drop of ink that fell on the body of the letter, what of it? He can repair it and go back and make it so with the quill, and this is not carving out the inside. Similarly, where any small thorn extends outside the letter in a way that does not change the form of the letter at all, and the letter is also recognizable to one who is neither wise nor foolish, here too it appears to me that it is valid, end quote. This also appears to be the view of Olas Tamid; see there. If a drop of ink fell, even if he erased the drop completely and finished the letter by writing, it is invalid, for the entire letter must be made by writing: Radach. If a drop of ink fell and formed something like half a letter, we have not found anyone who permits completing the letter by writing: Rabbi Moshe Alashkar, siman 5. But the Beis Yosef in Even HaEzer, siman 125, validates it, and so rules the Rama there, seif 4. Maharikash wrote in Even HaEzer, siman 125, that if one made a word divided so that it appeared like two words, and at the side of the divided parts there was a shin of three heads, and he made it four-headed and thereby lengthened it to fill the space in the middle of the divided parts of the word, and afterward scraped away from the middle heads of the shin so that a three-headed shin remained by itself, it is valid and does not appear to be carving out the inside. Nevertheless, it is better to erase the first head before writing the head by which he lengthened the shin. Maharam ben Chaviv there, s.k. 35, and the Pri Chadash there, s.k. 15, rejected his words and wrote that in such a case it is called carving out the inside and is invalid; see there. The second correction is primary. If he wrote two words as one word through an extra letter between them, for example, he needed to write “with a strong hand” and added a kuf between the words “strong” and “hand,” or wrote the word “with strength” by adding an extra beis at the beginning of that word, it is permitted to erase the beis or kuf between them, and there is no invalidation because of carving out the inside: Get Pashut, siman 125, s.k. 36. If the leg of the hei became attached by a very thin hairline, and a child who is neither wise nor foolish knows that it is a hei, I would not protest against one who is lenient to separate only the leg: Ralnach, simanim 1 and 97. But Radach, Bayis 1, Cheder 31, and Pri Chadash, Even HaEzer, siman 125, s.k. 10, disagreed with him, that one should not rule leniently in the matter, and it is proper to rebuke one who does so. So too wrote Olas Tamid: since it is visible to the eye that the form of the letter did not remain, even though it is thin, is the matter dependent on thinness or thickness? If it is attached, what difference is there whether it is thick or thin? If a drop of ink fell into the hollow of a beis and it appears like a peh, or it no longer has the name beis, or it fell into another letter and that letter no longer has its name, there is no remedy by erasure: Beis Yosef. Two adjacent beises in one word that became attached at their bottoms, and the visible form of the letter was spoiled so that it is not recognizable whether it is a beis or a kaf, cannot be repaired by scraping in order to separate the attachment, for that is called carving out the inside. Rather, one must erase the second leg and write it again: Devar Shmuel, siman 89. If wax dripped onto a letter and he removed it, it is permitted; even though the letter is not recognizable, he may scrape off and remove the wax, and this is not carving out the inside: Pri Chadash, and so wrote the Magen Avraham.
To scrape. Even if he passes the quill over it after he carved out its inside, it does not help. Olas Tamid.
Kaf. The same applies in the reverse case.
The inside. Only carving invalidates in this matter. But if one wrote a reish resembling a dalet and wants to add ink to make it round so that it will be a reish, or likewise if he made a reish instead of a dalet, there is a remedy by adding ink to it and making it square, and this is permitted. The same applies from a beis to a kaf or the reverse; the law is also so. All this, that there is a remedy to correct it, applies before he wrote another letter after it. But not afterward, for then it is out of order and invalid in tefillin. Regarding tefillin where, in a plain passage at the end, the scribe needed to complete the width of the line and extended the roof of a kaf until it reached the end of the line, so that the width of the roof resembled a large dalet or a large reish, what is the law?
