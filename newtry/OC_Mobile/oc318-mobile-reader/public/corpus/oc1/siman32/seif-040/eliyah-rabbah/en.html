[40] In the middle of a word, etc. E.g., "the rest of the clay for the sacrifice" — their law is to be missing and he wrote full — there is no correction except with a new quill and it is lo kelachar yad. The same applies to "you shall bind them" mezuzah in Shema, and in the parasha "and it shall be if hearing" he gave them a benefit that they are missing vav — if he wrote full it is invalid; but to extend the letter when correction depends on bet or kaf or dalet or reish that one may extend slightly — e.g., "to your fathers who worked" — kosher — Beit Yosef. But in Keneset HaGedolah he wrote in the name of the Radbaz that full and missing vav or yud that are small and thin — whatever thickens the letter before or after so it does not appear as two words — there is in this neither change of letter nor lo kelachar yad — so far. He further wrote in the name of Radbaz: if "in strength" was written twice, one should not drag the latter strength but extend the roof of the kuf of the first "in strength" until it reaches near the word "hand" — whether one leaves the kuf's leg in place with the leg detached from the roof, or drags the kuf's leg and pulls it to the end of the roof — it is lo kelachar yad; rather he leaves it partial, for whenever between word and word there is not the measure of one peh, it is kosher — so far. And he, of blessed memory, wondered on him: if one leaves the kuf's leg in place it is not lo kelachar yad. He further extended to justify that even if one wishes to drag the kuf's leg and pull it near the end of the roof, there is no lo kelachar yad here, since this scraping is not called lo kelachar yad except when there is need for that writing and without that writing the tefillin are invalid. But when there is no need for that writing it is not called lo kelachar yad. And when there is need for that writing we also only said thus when the need is because of deficiency of the letter or word itself; but when the need is only so it should not appear as a closed parasha, it is not called lo kelachar yad. It is explained that Magen Avraham's attack on Keneset HaGedolah here is not so. And in any case it seems one should extend the kuf and heh even if there will be no leg at its end. Remnants of Keneset HaGedolah wrote in the name of Mahara and Mahara Sasson: tefillin where chet is like dalet and zayin combined — do not invalidate them; drag from the dalet until one makes a zayin and it is not lo kelachar yad — so far. Olat Tamid wrote: in my humble opinion no correction is needed at all — however in practice this requires investigation. And what the Levush wrote "one may question," etc. — thus wrote Beit Yosef; therefore I wonder at Magen Avraham who wrote on Beit Yosef's words — his wording — and the Levush wrote one should be stringent.
