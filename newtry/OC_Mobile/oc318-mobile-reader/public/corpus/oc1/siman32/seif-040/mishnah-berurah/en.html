(183) The batim — and all the more one should be careful that the shin be black; and sometimes because of much use the blackness peels off it:

(184) A mitzvah, etc. — it is implied b'dieved it is not preventive; and there are poskim who hold it is halacha leMoshe miSinai like the straps, and preventive even b'dieved; and in Biur HaGra it is implied that he sides thus for practice; and so in Yeshuot Yaakov; and so it is implied in Eliyah Rabbah and in Knesset HaGedolah that one should be strict; if not in a place where it is not possible, one may rely on those who are lenient so as not to be prevented from the mitzvah of tefillin:

(185) Black — and the blackening is better if it is a black color that has no substance at all, and the tefillin are black only in appearance superficially; nevertheless those who blacken with a kind of glaze that cannot be peeled — even though they can remove it from the parchment by fine crumbs — one need not be strict b'dieved; and see what he wrote in se'if 48 in Biur Halachah in the name of the Pri Megadim that his view too is not to be strict in this. Indeed what scribes of our time innovated, that they blacken with plaster that can be peeled completely from every side of the tefillin and its form is like black paper — this is invalid [Sefer Nishmat Adam, and the genius author of Beit Me'ir agreed with him — not like Noda B'Yehuda, see there]:

(186) Sign 33 — in the Rama's hagahah there; and see there in Mishna Berurah that the Rama goes per the author's view here that it is only an ordinary mitzvah; and according to what is written in sign 184 one should review this:

(187) And that it be, etc. — but if it is not recognizable from outside, even though inside there are four batim and each parashah is placed in a separate bayit — it does not help:

(188) The groove recognizable — but a scratch or mere mark does not help at all, for in any case one needs a slight distinction between the batim that the grooves be actually recognizable; and Chayei Adam rejected the practice of scribes who paste the batim and coat the entire bayit with plaster or glaze, only making an impression in the glaze — all the more l'chatchila we require that the groove reach all the way down; see Biur Halachah.
