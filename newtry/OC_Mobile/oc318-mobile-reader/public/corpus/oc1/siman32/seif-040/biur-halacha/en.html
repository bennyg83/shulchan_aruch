The groove between housing and housing, etc., and if it did not reach it is kosher — See Chayei Adam, from whom it appears that even those accustomed to make tefillin by gluing the four hides — nevertheless it helps bedi'eved if he glued the four housings together at the end and left only a little above so that the grooves are visible; but Chayei Adam requires being careful that the grooves be at least in the actual separation of the housings and not only in the glaze alone. In responsum Chatam Sofer, siman 5, it appears that those accustomed to make their tefillin by gluing — it does not help even if the grooves are visible, unless they are fully separate; then he should be careful that their square remain, for it is common that through separation the square is lost. The custom of the world in our country is like Chayei Adam, and so Yeshuot Ya'akov implies. Nevertheless, every God-fearing person should be careful lekhatchilah to make the housings separate, as Chayei Adam himself wrote — lekhatchilah it is plainly correct to do so. If they are not glued at all, it sometimes happens that the square is ruined over time; therefore it may be better to glue a little at the lower part of the housing at the titura between housing and housing. There is another method: glue the hides a little between housing and housing, and if the hides do not reach each other, place inside them a thin piece of parchment with glue to join them, and the sides of the housings; likewise at the tops of the housings there should be no gluing at all — in this way all the grooves will still be well visible and their square will remain; but in this method one must be very careful always not to pull one housing forcefully from its fellow, lest the hide between housing and housing tear.
