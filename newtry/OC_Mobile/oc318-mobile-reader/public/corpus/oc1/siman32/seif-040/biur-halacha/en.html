See Chayei Adam, which implies that even those who customarily make tefillin by gluing the four pieces of leather, nevertheless after the fact it helps if one finally glues the four compartments together and leaves only a little at the top so that the grooves are recognizable. However, Chayei Adam requires care that the grooves at least be in the actual separation of the compartments, not in the glaze alone. In Responsa Chasam Sofer, siman 5, it implies that for those who make their tefillin by gluing, recognizable grooves do not help; rather, they must be completely separated, and then one must be careful that their square remains intact, for it is common that through separation the square is lost. The practice of the world in our country follows Chayei Adam, and so it also implies in Sefer Yeshuos Yaakov. Nevertheless, every person who fears Heaven should initially be careful to make the compartments separate, as Chayei Adam himself wrote, that initially this is certainly proper. If they are not glued at all, sometimes the square is spoiled over time because of this; therefore perhaps it is better to glue a little at the lower edge of the compartment near the titura, between one compartment and another. There is also another method: to glue slightly the leathers that are between one compartment and another, and if the leathers do not reach each other, to place between them a thin piece of parchment with glue that connects them together, while the sides of the compartments and the tops of the compartments are not glued at all. In this way all the grooves are still clearly recognizable and their square remains intact. But in this method one must always be very careful not to pull one compartment forcefully away from another, so that the leather between the compartments not tear thereby.
