(Note 23) even if he erased the drop a little. There it refers when he wrote part of the letter and a drop of ink fell on it and was spoiled by this fall. Then even though he erased the drop and part of the letter he wrote remained in its first form and he finished the rest through writing as is proper, nevertheless it is invalid — for the part of the letter on which the drop fell did not return to its first form except through hollowing the interior, and this does not help. And at first glance it requires further study — for in a mem that stuck together they validate by scraping the quill stroke, etc., and this is because what he wrote as the form of a nun was already there and now he acts on the body of the letter, which is not hollowing the interior; and if so here too similarly. And it is possible that Radbaz according to his view, who holds that in a mem too one must scrape — and it is further possible that Shulchan Aruch too concedes this, for here it is worse, since at the time of the drop's fall there was not yet a letter on it; if so, through the drop's fall it was entirely spoiled and we consider everything as if it is a drop of ink. Unlike in a mem that was not spoiled until he continued the form of vav next to the bent nun form, and when he reached its end it stuck properly — one can say that the nun form written in valid fashion need not be erased. And it requires further study in Radbaz's own responsa. And what was stated "if a drop fell in the hollow of the bet," etc. — the reason is that although made valid at first, nevertheless since its form was lost through the drop and it returns to its original state through scraping, scraping the sides is considered invalid repair unless he scrapes part of it until the letter's form is nullified and he returns and writes it — it is kosher according to Shulchan Aruch below; and according to Radbaz and Maharam Alshekar, in this too one must scrape everything specifically, for since its form was lost, he must perform an act of writing on the entire letter:
