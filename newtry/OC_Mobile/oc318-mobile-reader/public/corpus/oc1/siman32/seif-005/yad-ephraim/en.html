Shulchan Aruch, seif 5. “If possible.” See Beit Shmuel, Even HaEzer, siman 123, seif katan 4, who rules that even when no other tefillin can be found, one should put these on without a blessing, unlike Levush.
Seif katan 23: Even if he erased part of the drop. The case there is one where he wrote part of a letter, a drop of ink fell upon it, and that part was spoiled by the drop. Even if he then erased the drop, leaving intact the portion of the letter that had originally been written in its proper form, and completed the remainder through ordinary writing, it is nevertheless invalid. The portion upon which the drop fell did not return to its original form except through carving out the interior, and that does not help. At first glance this requires study. In the case of a mem whose parts became attached, we validate it by scraping away the projecting stroke. The reason is that the form of a nun had already been written within it, and now he performs an act upon the body of the letter, which is not considered carving out the interior. Why should the same not apply here? Perhaps Radach follows his own view that even regarding a mem one must scrape away the entire letter. Alternatively, Shulchan Aruch may also agree here, because this case is worse. When the drop fell, no complete letter yet existed. The drop therefore ruined the writing entirely, and the whole mass is treated as a mere drop of ink. By contrast, with the mem, the writing was not spoiled until he extended the form of a vav alongside a bent nun and they joined at the end. It is therefore reasonable to say that the form of the nun, which was written validly, need not be erased. The matter requires study in Responsa Radach itself. When a drop fell into the hollow of a bet, the reason for invalidation is that although the letter was initially formed validly, its form was destroyed by the drop and it returns to its former shape only through scraping. This is called carving out the legs, unless he scrapes away enough of the letter to nullify its form and then rewrites it. According to Shulchan Aruch below, that repair is valid. According to Radach and Maharam Alashkar, however, even then the entire letter must specifically be erased, because once its form was destroyed, a new act of writing must be performed throughout the whole letter.
