Upright. It implies the mitzvah is preferably thus; but if impossible, under duress we do not require upright, as Semag wrote: if there is no space between them it is valid even though one cannot stand them upright [Beit Yosef, Rabbi Chaviv, see there]. In Darkei Moshe he explained that in Semag it deals where they were written on one parchment one under the other — then one can stand them upright; one must say Kadesh and VeHayah were written the end of one beside the end of the other, and Shema written the beginning of VeHayah at the end of Shema at the end of VeHayah Im Shamo'a — but Rashi did not explain thus, for he wrote one must cut the hide below, implying we require upright, see there.
