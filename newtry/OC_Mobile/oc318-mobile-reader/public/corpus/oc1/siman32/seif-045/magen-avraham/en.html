Upright. This implies that this is the optimum manner of the mitzvah, but if it is impossible otherwise we do not require them to be upright. As is written in seif 47, if there is no space between them it is valid, even though it is impossible to stand them upright, as stated by the Beis Yosef in the name of Rabbi Yosef Chaviv; see there. In Darchei Moshe he answered that seif 47 discusses where they were written on one parchment, one beneath the other, and then it is possible to stand them upright. One must say that “Kadesh” and “Vehayah” were written with the end of one beside the end of the other, and “Shema” was written with its beginning at the end of “Vehayah,” and the end of “Shema” at the end of “Vehayah im Shamoa.” But Rashi did not explain so, for he wrote that one must cut the leather down to the bottom, implying that we require them to be upright; see there.
