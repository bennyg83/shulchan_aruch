(s.k. 48) And in these countries, etc. And it is difficult, etc. Know that although what I write here is explained in the Taz, nevertheless he discusses it at length, and I will come briefly with some added explanation. An open parashah is one that is open for itself and does not continue what is above; a closed parashah is one that continues the matter above, except that a space is given between them so that Moshe could reflect between one parashah and another, as Rashi wrote. We rule that between one parashah and another, whether open or closed, one must leave a blank space large enough to write nine letters. However, the Tur here wrote a space large enough for three words of three letters each, and Rambam, chapter 7 of Hilchos Tefillin, wrote nine letters, like “asher asher asher.” The Bach in this siman, s.k. 4, wrote as follows: one should not ask why our teacher wrote that one should leave a blank space of three words, etc., and did not write briefly that one should leave a space of nine letters. For one must also leave a space the size of two small letters in addition to the nine letters, since when one writes three words of three letters each, one must leave the space of a small letter between word and word, as above in seif 32; and this amount of blank space must be left, end quote. According to his words, one must say that they did not write the measure as eleven letters either because those nine letters are the measure of large letters, like “asher asher asher,” while the two letters between the words that the Bach mentioned need only be small letters, as explained in seif 32. Also, it is explained in seif 32 that between one letter and another one must leave a tiny space like a hairbreadth. If so, besides the nine letters one must also leave the spaces between the letters; but those two letters that the Bach added according to his explanation do not need space. If it had taught a measure of eleven letters, I might have thought that one also needs the measure of ten hairbreadth spaces between the letters, but this is not so. All this follows the Bach. But in Shulchan Aruch here, and likewise in Shulchan Aruch Yoreh Deah and in the Tur Yoreh Deah, three words are not mentioned, only nine letters, implying that he holds that nine letters alone suffice, unlike the Bach.
The matter of the form of an open or closed parashah is a dispute between Rambam and the Rosh. According to Rambam, every open parashah must begin at the beginning of the line. Therefore, if he finished the parashah in the middle of a line and there still remains empty space until the end of the line the measure of nine letters, he leaves it blank and begins the next parashah at the start of the line below it. That parashah, which begins at the start of the line, is called open, and in this the Rosh also agrees that it is called open. But if there is blank space from the end of the parashah he finished until the end of the line more than nine letters, enough for one word, and he left a blank space of nine letters and afterward wrote the first word of the following parashah, and the same applies to more than one word depending on the available space, this is called closed according to Rambam, since he did not begin at the start of the line; and in this the Rosh also agrees that it is called closed. However, there are two other forms in which Rambam and the Rosh disagree, and their positions are opposite. One is where he finished at the end of a line, left the entire line below it blank, and on the line below that blank line began another parashah. According to Rambam this is called open, since it begins at the start of a line; but according to the Rosh it is called closed, for we view all three lines, the line where the first parashah ended, the blank line, and the line below it where the parashah begins, as if they are one line, and the blank space is in the middle of the line, which is called closed, as above. The second form is where he finished the parashah at the end of a line, and on the line below it he left at its beginning a blank space the measure of nine letters, and afterward began another parashah. According to Rambam this is called closed, since it does not begin at the start of the line; but according to the Rosh, in such a case it is called open. Beis Yosef wrote in Yoreh Deah, siman 275, that initially one who fears Heaven should satisfy everyone and write in a form that is open according to all where it must be open, and likewise where it must be closed he should make a form that according to all is closed. Rama wrote: however, if he cannot align it in this way, he should not depart from the words of Rambam, whose words are primary in this matter. In tefillin one must make the parshiyos as they are in the Torah: the first three parshiyos are open in the Torah, and the fourth parashah, “Vehayah im shamoa,” is closed in the Torah. Therefore, the parashah of “Kadesh,” which is first, must be open. Although it is not recognizable at its beginning whether it is open or closed, since there is no blank space before it at all, nevertheless this is sufficient for it to be called open, like the beginnings of the five books of the Torah, which Rambam did not count among the open and closed parshiyos, for Bereishis and the other four books have a sufficient indication that they are the beginning of a book; and the same applies to the beginning of “Kadesh,” which is the beginning of the tefillin. However, to indicate that the parashah of “Vehayah ki yeviacha” is open, one must leave at the end of “Kadesh” a blank space of nine letters. When he then begins “Vehayah ki yeviacha” at the top of the parchment, with no blank space before it, it is as if he began on the line below the end of “Kadesh,” which is open according to all, if he began at the start of the line as above. The same should be done at the end of “Vehayah ki yeviacha,” so that there will be an indication that the parashah of “Shema” is open according to all. But to make “Vehayah im shamoa” in a form that is closed according to all is impossible, for then on the last line of “Shema” he would have to leave a blank space of nine letters and begin “Vehayah im shamoa” on that same line, as above; and this is impossible, not only with the head-tefillin, where each parashah is on its own parchment, but even with the hand-tefillin, since in any event each parashah must begin at the top of the parchment. Since Rambam’s words are primary in this matter, Beis Yosef wrote that he should not leave at the end of “Shema” a blank space of eight letters, but at the top of the parchment where he begins “Vehayah im shamoa” he should leave a blank space at the beginning of the line of nine letters, and it is closed according to Rambam but open according to the Rosh, as above. However, regarding this, Beis Yosef in the name of Rambam disagrees with those who validate. Rambam’s view is that if he made “Vehayah im shamoa” open, it is invalid, since in the Torah it is closed. Although in the Torah the reason it is closed is because it relates to the parashah before it, whereas in tefillin “Vehayah im shamoa” does not relate to the preceding parashah of “Shema,” since “Shema” is written in Parashas Vaeschanan and “Vehayah im shamoa” is written in Parashas Eikev, and if so, although initially it is proper to write it in the form in which it is written in the Torah, why should it be invalid if he did not do so? Beis Yosef answered that Rambam holds that just as with the three preceding parshiyos, if he changed from the way they are written in the Torah they are invalid, the same law applies to “Vehayah im shamoa.” Those who validate do not accept this reasoning, and therefore they validate it at least after the fact if he made “Vehayah im shamoa” open.
And the Bach wrote: and they also finish the parashah of “Vehayah im shamoa” at the end, etc. This is the correct reading. It appears to my humble mind that he needed to teach this because one might have thought that it is proper to finish in the middle of a line, as the Mordechai wrote, comparable to a Sefer Torah, where we rule that one must finish “before the eyes of all Israel” in the middle of a line. Therefore he teaches that it is not comparable, for specifically with a Sefer Torah, this shows that the Torah ends here and nothing may be added to it; therefore the conclusion is in the middle of the line, to show that it is not because of a lack of parchment that no more was written, since there is still empty space before it, the remaining half line, and nevertheless nothing is written there, but rather because here is the conclusion of the Torah from the mouth of Hashem and nothing more may be added. This reason does not apply to tefillin. See Responsa Maharshal, siman 37, at the end, where something of this is explained.
If he made it closed, meaning according to the view and custom of Beis Yosef, unlike our custom.
Even according to our custom. Meaning: certainly according to the custom of Beis Yosef, to make the fourth parashah closed, one certainly should not leave blank space at the end of “Vehayah im shamoa,” because by finishing at the end of the line it appears more closed. Although the mark of an open or closed parashah is at the beginning of the parashah, nevertheless this is preferable, that the end of the parashah also finishes at the end of a line, for there is more indication. So too wrote Maharshal in responsum 57, at the end of the responsum; see there. Rather, even according to our custom, in which we make the fourth parashah open as well, nevertheless one should finish “Vehayah im shamoa” at the end of the line, and this is the statement of the Bach above.
And if my strength were straight, etc. That they are valid according to all opinions. Meaning: according to our custom, in which we also make the fourth parashah open, if so they are invalid according to Rambam, who holds that if one changed and made the fourth parashah open, it is invalid.
But it does not appear to me that our custom, etc. The Taz wrote a form by which to satisfy everyone, based on what Rambam wrote as the third form for a closed parashah. That is: if he finished the parashah in the middle of a line and there is not enough blank space to leave the space of three words and write the first word of the other parashah, by which it would be closed according to all, and he needs to write the following parashah so that it will be closed, what should he do? If he begins at the start of the second line, it will be open according to Rambam. Therefore he must leave a small blank space at the beginning of the second line and afterward write the following parashah; it is closed according to Rambam, since it does not begin at the start of a line. According to this, even if one left blank space at the end of “Shema” the measure of nine letters and at the beginning of “Vehayah im shamoa” left a small blank space, then according to Rambam “Vehayah im shamoa” would be called closed, as above, since it does not begin at the head of the line. But according to the Rosh, such a case would be open, since the end of “Shema” has, in any event, a blank space of nine letters; for according to the Rosh, even if he does not leave blank space at the end of “Shema” but leaves blank space at the beginning of “Vehayah im shamoa” the measure of nine letters and begins “Vehayah im shamoa” in the middle of the line, nevertheless it is called open. Rather, at the end of “Shema” he should leave a little blank space, but not the measure of nine letters, and likewise before “Vehayah im shamoa” he should leave a little blank space, but not the measure of nine letters. In any event, when the two empty places, the end of “Shema” and the beginning of “Vehayah im shamoa,” are combined, there should be the measure of nine letters, for in any case we require a space between one parashah and another of nine letters. Such a case is closed according to all: not only according to Rambam is it closed, for according to him even if there were blank space at the end of “Shema” of nine letters, nevertheless, since he leaves space at the beginning of “Vehayah im shamoa” and does not begin at the head of the line, it is called closed according to Rambam, all the more so in such a case, where the end of “Shema” does not have blank space of nine letters. Rather, even according to the Rosh, he agrees that such a case is closed, since there is not in any one place the measure of nine letters, but only by combining them. These are his words. But in my poverty I am perplexed how it is possible to do this and satisfy everyone, for Beis Yosef wrote in Yoreh Deah, siman 275, and the Shach there, s.k. 4, wrote likewise in his name, that although initially one needs between one parashah and another the measure of nine large letters, nevertheless after the fact it suffices if there is blank space the measure of nine small letters, namely nine yuds. In my humble opinion, three times “asher” is more than eighteen yuds. If so, how can he act? If he divides the measure of nine yuds and leaves some of it blank at the end of “Shema” and some at the beginning of “Vehayah im shamoa,” initially one needs blank space the measure of three times “asher.” And if he leaves part of the three times “asher” blank at the end of “Shema,” and the remainder of the three times “asher” at the beginning of “Vehayah im shamoa,” in any event there is the measure of nine yuds in one place, and that is at least after the fact the measure of a parashah break, so according to the Rosh it is open.
