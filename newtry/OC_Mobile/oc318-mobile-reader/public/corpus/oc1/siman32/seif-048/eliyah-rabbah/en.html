[48] Unless he returns, etc. It implies that if one writes from the text he need not express with his mouth; and in Shulchan Aruch Yoreh De'ah 32 it implies that even when writing from the text he must express with his mouth — and they contradict each other; thus wondered Olat Tamid. In my humble opinion one may reconcile: here it deals with tefillin that are fluent in his mouth — then if he expressed them with his mouth we do not require from the text; therefore when writing them from the text and they are also fluent in his mouth he need not take them from the text. But there in a Torah scroll it deals, for presumably they are not fluent in his mouth as explained in Beit Yosef there — therefore he must express them with his mouth. And this seems the Levush's intent, and likewise Lechem Chamudot at the end of the laws of Torah scroll and Magen Avraham. But the Bach wrote the reason for expressing with his mouth is to draw all holiness onto the letter — if so, always required. In any case the Shulchan Aruch, Levush, and poskim do not accept this reason, but rather the reason lest he err. And it implies if he did not err it is kosher ex post facto in all this; and likewise in Beit Yosef and poskim in Yoreh De'ah.
