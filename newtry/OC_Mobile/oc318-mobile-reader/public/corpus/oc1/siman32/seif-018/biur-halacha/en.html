An open mem that became attached. See the Mishnah Berurah, who wrote regarding the law where it became attached like a hairbreadth that it depends on the law of a heh. It seems more correct to me to say that even those who are lenient in the law of a heh, as explained in Beis Yosef below in siman 36, agree that here the matter is different. There, its form has not entirely changed to a ches, since it is recognizable that the right leg is not made as it should be if it is to be a ches. But here, by an open mem whose opening was closed, it actually became a closed mem. Yet perhaps, since when one looks at the place of adhesion one sees that it is thin and not according to the form of the writing, its first form is still upon it, as with a heh. This requires study.
And it was closed. See the Taz, Even HaEzer, siman 125, seif 4, who wrote that this is specifically where the mem was closed after it was completed. But if he closed the mem before its completion, in this case he may scrape away the closing, since he still must add the slanted strip like a yud. Therefore he should first scrape away the adhesion and afterward complete it; see there. It appears that according to the view of the Radach that we wrote in the Mishnah Berurah, s.k. 68, one should be stringent even in this.
The entire snout. It appears obvious to me that this is specifically where it became attached while writing the snout, so that the snout was made invalidly. But if it became attached after the writing, he does not specifically need to scrape the snout; the same law is that he may scrape from the nun until he annuls its form as a letter. It requires study whether, where it was written validly, scraping half the snout is also effective. This depends on whether in general the whole snout is indispensable in a mem, or perhaps it is only required initially; this requires study.
And a reish that he made like a dalet, etc. If he made a wide roof for a final kaf until it appears like a reish, the Magen Avraham wrote that he should extend the leg until it is twice the length of the roof. Whether that double length is indispensable or only initial practice, see below, siman 36, in the laws of the forms of the letters. If he has no room to extend it, the Magen Avraham wrote in the name of the Maharil, and the Pri Megadim likewise ruled simply in siman 36 in the forms of the letters, that its correction is to erase the entire roof, for scraping away the excess certainly does not help here, as it is actual engraving of the interior. Even in the word “your God” he may erase it, but he must be careful not to erase the leg. This requires study, for is it not comparable to a reish that he made like a dalet, where the Shulchan Aruch ruled that one must scrape all of it because it was written at one time? Here too it is similar. See Yad Ephraim, who distinguishes this somewhat, but it is forced. Afterward I found in Chiddushei Rabbi Akiva Eiger that he wrote simply that it is comparable to a reish that he made like a dalet. See Shaarei Teshuvah, s.k. 22, in what he copied on this from Responsa Ginas Veradim; it requires study whether one may rely on this after all the Acharonim are stringent that he must at least erase the roof. However, if such a case occurred in the final kaf of “your God,” where he made it wide like a dalet or a reish, it appears that one may rely leniently, that erasing the roof suffices if he has no room to extend the leg. This is because even with a reish that he made like a dalet, the Pri Chadash is lenient, and the Machatzis HaShekel brought him, that it is enough to annul its form as a letter. Rabbi Akiva Eiger too, in his novellae, left the words of the Shulchan Aruch here requiring study because of that question of the Pri Chadash, and it is surprising that they did not mention him.
One should be stringent and say, etc. See Chiddushei Rabbi Akiva Eiger; similarly the Machatzis HaShekel wrote in the name of the Pri Chadash.
Because both the roof and the thigh, etc. For all of it was written at one time in the normal manner of writers. According to this, if he first wrote a reish and afterward erred and made a tag on it like a dalet, it is enough that he scrape the roof or the thigh together with the tag and then write it again like a reish. Levush and Pri Megadim.
And if he scraped and separated it, etc. This applies even if the adhesion occurred before the letter was completed. See Biur HaGra and Sefer Shaarei Ephraim by Rabbi Efraim Zalman Margoliyos, who write that this law of the Shulchan Aruch can be questioned. It is evident there from the view of Shaarei Ephraim in his Pischei Shearim that he rules leniently in it only regarding tefillin and mezuzos, which are like an after-the-fact case, for if erasure were required it would be invalid because it was not written in order. But regarding a Torah scroll, and likewise tefillin and mezuzos if he had not yet written the words after it, scraping the adhesion alone is not effective if it became attached before the letter was completed and he completed the letter invalidly.
It is valid and is not called, etc. See the Mishnah Berurah, in what he wrote: and if the leg, etc. This is clear from what we rule, that scraping is effective even if the letter was attached at its beginning.
The legs of the heh, etc. See the Mishnah Berurah, where he wrote that whenever the form of the letter is not present, etc.; this is the language of the Chelkas Mechokek in Even HaEzer, see there. Regarding what I wrote, that even if it became attached in such a way that the child knows it is a heh, etc., so it appears clear from the fact that the Beis Yosef brought in this siman and in siman 36 the source for this from the words of Maharik, shoresh 95, and not as he wrote in shoresh 78; there he is explicitly discussing such a case, as explained there at the end of the shoresh. He also brought this from Terumas HaDeshen, and it is likewise proven there, for otherwise there would be no question at all from the Ram against the Rosh; see there. So too it is explained explicitly in Even HaEzer, siman 125, seif 16, in the gloss, that although the child reads it as a heh, nevertheless it should be invalidated except in a case of agunah; see there. The Pri Chadash is stringent there even in a case of agunah for the reason we wrote. So too the Magen Avraham wrote in the name of the Radach, and so too it is proven in Yoreh Deah, siman 276, in the Beis Yosef; see there. However, if this was found in a Torah scroll, where the leg of a heh or kuf touched above and a child who is neither wise nor foolish reads it properly, it appears to me that another scroll should not be taken out because of this. In truth, this law is subject to a dispute among great authorities, as is evident to one who examines Beis Yosef, Even HaEzer, siman 125. The proof is that they validate it in a case of agunah; see there. If so, regarding taking out another scroll, one may join to this the view of the Rambam, who validates reading from an invalid Torah scroll, making it a double doubt, as the Pri Megadim ruled in a similar case in siman 143, and the Derech HaChaim in Hilchos Kerias HaTorah. But regarding tefillin it is at least a doubt concerning Torah law, and a doubt concerning Torah law is treated stringently. Know further that the Gra in his commentary agrees with the view of the Mordechai, that suspending the heh, and all the more so the kuf as is proven to one who examines it, is only for the mitzvah in a place where the scribal custom is to make the ches with humps, so there is a clear distinction between ches and heh. It seems obvious to me that what he wrote to be lenient is specifically where it touches only like a hairbreadth and from a distance it has the form of a heh, since the child also reads it as a heh. In truth, in such a manner it has the form of a heh and not a ches; it is only because it should not appear like a ches, which is included in the case of making chesin into hehin taught in the baraisa, about which it says it must be put away. Therefore, if they make the ches with humps, there is again a distinction, and one should not invalidate after the fact. But if the leg of the heh adhered to the roof with a thick adhesion until it actually appears like a ches, certainly the name ches is not changed to heh by the scribes. I later found, with Hashem’s help, in the explanation of the Gaon Rabbi Mordechai Banet on the Mordechai that he too wrote this in the view of the Mordechai. The Tashbetz also holds so, and he is brought in the Beis Yosef, Even HaEzer, siman 126, and in Chiddushei Rabbi Akiva Eiger below in siman 36; see there. Nevertheless, it requires study whether one may rely on the Mordechai’s view to be lenient here even by separating it, since the Shulchan Aruch is stringent in this. One should not reject this by saying that the Shulchan Aruch speaks in a place where the scribal custom is to make a ches without humps, which is valid in that case as the Magen Avraham wrote below in siman 36 and the Acharonim agreed, and therefore he is also stringent there in Even HaEzer regarding a get, for there the custom is not to make a ches with humps, so there is no distinction between heh and ches, and therefore it is invalid. This is not so, for the Shulchan Aruch is stringent even with a kuf, where through the leg touching the roof its form is not changed at all, and all the more so here. In a pressing situation where there is no remedy by erasing the letter, such as where he wrote the sacred Name after it, and it is known that this thin touch occurred after the letter was validly completed, perhaps one can combine the view of the Semak, brought in the Tur in the law of a drop of ink that fell, see there, who holds leniently here by scraping. This requires study.
He should scrape the leg. See the Mishnah Berurah, in what he wrote: all of it, etc. This applies only where the touch occurred at the time of writing. But if it occurred after the writing, the law of heh and kuf is not the same. With a heh, he must erase the entire leg, for if any amount remains, the name of the first heh is still upon it according to the Rosh, as stated in seif 16. By contrast, with a kuf it is enough that he annul it from the form of a letter. Know further that what the Pri Megadim wrote in siman 36, Eshel Avraham, letter alef, as another remedy, namely that he should scrape a little from the roof until the leg is outside the roof and thereby its form will be annulled, and afterward extend the roof of the kuf above the leg, meaning that he should make a small separation, and this is proper whenever there is no issue of not in order, end quote, speaks of where the touch occurred afterward. Otherwise the leg was made invalidly and he must scrape it.
The leg of the alef touched, etc. Know that there is no distinction whether it touched at the time of writing or afterward, like the case of a drop of ink that fell in seif 17. However, if it touched at the time of writing, he must erase the entire leg; and if it touched after he completed the letter properly, he need not erase the whole leg, but only annul it from the form of a letter, like what the Levush wrote and the Magen Avraham brought at the end of s.k. 25; see there.
Or the face of the alef, etc. See the Magen Avraham, that this is specifically if the body of the yud adhered to the alef. But if only the left point of the yud touched the alef, it is valid, for even without the point it is called a yud. That is, although because of the touching it is considered as though the yud has no point, this is not indispensable regarding correction. The Pri Megadim wrote about this that in any event scraping is required, and that suffices; it is not necessary to erase the entire yud.
He should scrape all that was made invalidly, etc. See the Magen Avraham, who wrote that this means everything he wrote in that letter after he made the touch. See the Pri Megadim, who explained his words regarding the upper yud, that if the upper yud touched the alef and he completed the letter afterward, he must scrape the entire alef, and scraping the yud does not help. The Magen Avraham inferred this from the word “all” written in the Shulchan Aruch. It is very astonishing to derive such a new law from such a slight inference. Moreover, his essential law is refutable, for the Beis Yosef wrote as follows: If the leg of the alef touched the roof of the alef, or the face of the alef touched the face of the roof beneath it, the Agur wrote that all of them are invalid and cannot be corrected by erasing or scraping, for this is called engraving the interior. However, by scraping what was made invalidly it is enough, as I wrote nearby regarding the leg of a heh or kuf that touches above. End quote. The plain language of “however” proves that it refers to both cases, and that scraping the yud and the leg suffices, similar to the leg of a heh or kuf. This is also proven more strongly from his language in siman 36, where he wrote that the Agur says that if the upper yud of the alef touched, etc.; and so he wrote in the name of Rabbi Yitzchak Aksandari. He wrote further there that if they adhered and thereby became invalid, it is not enough to erase the adhesion, for that is engraving the interior; rather, one must scrape the entire yud that adhered and correct it initially. This too was copied in the long Darchei Moshe. It is clear that even with the adhesion of the upper yud, scraping the yud is enough. So too, from what he concluded there in Beis Yosef, siman 36, with this very law of scraping the yud when the yud of a shin or ayin adhered, etc., it is also proven as we wrote. According to the Pri Megadim, one would need in those cases too to scrape the entire letter, as with the adhesion of the yud on the alef. One cannot say that the Beis Yosef is speaking in all of this where it adhered after he had completed the letter properly, for if so, why must he erase the whole yud? It would be enough to scrape part of it until he annuls the alef from the form of a letter, as above, and likewise with yuds that touched other letters. The Levush too wrote explicitly that the law of the upper and lower yud is the same, as the Pri Megadim himself brought, unless we push aside the Pri Megadim’s words and say that the Beis Yosef, Darchei Moshe, and Levush speak where he first wrote the body of the letter and afterward the upper yud, and upon completing it it adhered to the bent line of the alef. Were it not for the words of the Pri Megadim, it would seem to me that the Magen Avraham refers to the lower leg of the alef and comes to explain the Shulchan Aruch’s intent in writing “all,” namely that he must scrape the entire leg, since it was written in one act of writing after the touch was made, and it is not enough to scrape part of it until he annuls the alef from the form of a letter. The same applies where the upper yud adhered, but in any event it suffices to scrape the entire yud, and he need not scrape what was written afterward, since it was written in two acts of writing. The Magen Avraham did not concern himself to explain this, for the Mechaber had already explained it at the beginning of the seif. In particular, what the Pri Megadim wrote in the laws of the forms of the letters, letter alef, that the Taz in Even HaEzer, siman 125, also holds like the Magen Avraham, that there is a distinction between the upper yud adhering and the lower yud, meaning the leg of the alef. His intent is to the Taz there, s.k. 10, who disagrees with the Beis Yosef and Rama in the law of a drop of ink that fell and one completed the letter through writing, and holds that the entire letter must be made through writing and not made on its own. From this the Pri Megadim infers that the same applies, that according to the Taz one must be stringent in this law too. In my humble opinion this is no proof at all, for there his reason is that the entire letter must be formed through writing and not on its own. But here, where it was not made on its own, it may be comparable to a case where one wrote the roof and leg and afterward wrote the yud. It is not comparable to a dalet in place of a reish, where one must scrape all of it, because there he made everything in one act of writing, as the Taz wrote in s.k. 17; unlike our case, which appears to be two acts of writing. Also in Sefer LeDavid Emes by the author of Birkei Yosef, he does not distinguish between the adhesion of the upper yud and the lower yud. However, afterward I saw in some later Acharonim after the Pri Megadim that their view also seems like the Pri Megadim, that the Magen Avraham’s view distinguishes between the upper and lower yud. Therefore initially one certainly must be stringent and scrape everything made invalidly after the touch occurred, meaning even the lower leg of the alef and the like in the other letters listed here in the gloss. After the fact, it requires examination whether to be stringent in this, since the plain language of the Beis Yosef and Darchei Moshe in siman 36 and the Levush does not imply so, as above. Afterward I found in Chiddushei Rabbi Akiva Eiger that he hinted to examine the responsum of Rabbi Yehoshua HaLevi, brother of the Taz. I examined it and found that he too takes it as obvious that when the yuds on the alefs touch, one need not scrape the lower leg beneath the line; see there, regarding what he wrote about the upper yud. But regarding the bent line, which the Shulchan Aruch calls the roof of the alef, he holds there that one must scrape it, for it is called something made invalidly through the adhesion to the yud; see there. This is unless he first wrote the bent line and the lower leg and afterward completed the upper yud and it adhered to it, in which case everyone agrees that he need only scrape the yud, for the line was written validly. Returning to our matter: initially it is proper to be concerned for the stringent view and scrape even the lower leg; after the fact, if he did not scrape the lower leg, it appears that one should not be stringent. Regarding the bent line, one must examine the matter, for from the plain language of the Beis Yosef, Darchei Moshe, and Levush cited above it implies that the yud must be erased, but not the line. The practical ruling requires study.
