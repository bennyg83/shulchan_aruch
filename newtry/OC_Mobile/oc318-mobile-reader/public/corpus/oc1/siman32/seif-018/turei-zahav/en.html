Like the form of a bent nun. This means that the writing of an open mem is done in two acts of writing: first one writes it like a bent nun, and afterward one suspends from its side something like a vav. Therefore, wherever the invalidation occurred in it, namely in the vav, he must scrape that entire part. But the nun, which was written validly, need not be scraped. This excludes the case of a reish that he made like a dalet, where everything was written invalidly in one act, and therefore he must scrape it all. According to this, the same applies to any letter written in two acts, such as a gimel and the like: if the invalidation occurred in the first act of writing, he need scrape only that part.
Whether after it was completed, etc. Nevertheless, if this was found in a sefer Torah, one should not take out another, for the Beit Yosef wrote in the name of the Rashba that even if he does not scrape it, as long as he is able to scrape it, it is valid, following Rabbi Zeira’s rule that anything fit for mixing, etc. My teacher and father-in-law, of blessed memory, wrote that even if this was found on Shabbos, when it is impossible to scrape it, nevertheless one need not take out another, since according to the essential law of a sefer Torah it has a remedy; it is only that the prohibition of Shabbos rests upon it.
