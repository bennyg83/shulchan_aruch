Whether. See Taz. At first glance, if a final nun was found attached at its end, then according to the Rashba’s explanation of the Yerushalmi, if it is attached below it is valid without scraping, as in the rule of Rabbi Zeira. Even according to us, who require scraping, the Rashba is worthy to rely on so as not to take out another scroll. This differs from attachment above, where according to the Rashba it is invalid and scraping does not help, for he rules like the Yerushalmi. If so, upon whom can we rely? With the help of Heaven, this will be explained in siman 143. Know that regarding adhesion of one letter to another, the Mechaber wrote whether it occurred before the letter was completed or afterward. But regarding a drop of ink falling in seif 17, he did not write “before it was completed,” because before it was completed it is sometimes valid; see what I wrote in Magen Avraham, letter 23.
And know. If the letters of the Divine Name were completed and became attached below, one may scrape them apart. Do not say that since we rely on the Rashba leniently, all the more should we be stringent and perhaps scraping is unnecessary and he is erasing for nothing, for Rabbi Yitzchak Aksandari in Yoreh Deah permitted only because it is invalid. This is mistaken, for if it were forbidden to scrape, it would not be fit for separation and would be invalid, and he therefore may properly separate it. This is not comparable to a Shabbos prohibition, where after time passes it becomes fit for separation.
