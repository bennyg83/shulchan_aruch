Since he does not perform an act in the body of the remaining letter, but merely scrapes away the closure, and passing the quill over the letter does not help; Sefer HaTerumah and Semag, cited in Knesses HaGedolah. Rabbi Moshe Galanti wrote in his responsa, siman 124, that a final kaf whose upper part he made like a dalet and not rounded is valid. Although the Beis Yosef in siman 36 wrote in the name of the Riaz that it is invalid and correction is not effective, nevertheless, since many communities and expert scribes write it like a dalet, each river follows its own course, and anything that has no root in the Gemara is valid after the fact, end quote; see there. But in Responsa Maharil, siman 123, it implies that it is invalid, and he wrote there, siman 104, that the leg of the kaf must be twice the length of the roof so that it not resemble a reish. Rabbi Yehoshua Levi wrote, siman 81, that if one made a wide roof, it requires correction by erasing the entire roof; even in the word Elokecha he may erase it, but he must be careful not to erase the leg; Knesses HaGedolah. It seems to me that if he can extend the leg so that it is twice the length of the roof, that is proper. Rabbi Yehoshua Levi wrote there that even according to the Rosh, who forbids cutting out Divine Names, it is permitted to peel it off and write in its place, or to erase the word before it and write the Name in its place, end quote. But Tosafos in Arachin, folio 6, wrote that when one scraped away the Name, it is forbidden to write something else in its place, end quote.

It seems to me that the same applies if the leg of the kaf reaches the end without surrounding blank parchment: he may scrape away a little, for it is no worse than one letter stuck to another, and this is also implied in Beis Yosef; see there. The Bach wrote in Even HaEzer, siman 125, that if the entire length of the letter is attached to another, scraping does not help; and if the form of the letter changed through the attachment, it is like a mem that became closed; Radach in Orach Chaim, Knesses HaGedolah. If the leg of the hei became attached like a hairsbreadth in such a way that a child knows it is a hei, I would not protest against one who is lenient to separate it; Ralbach, siman 57.
