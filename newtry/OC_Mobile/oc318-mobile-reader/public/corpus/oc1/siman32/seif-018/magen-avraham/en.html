It is not effective. R' Eliyahu Mizrachi vol. 1 siman 96 wrote to drag the adhesion; R' Moshe Alshakar wrote to drag the entire mem; therefore one should act as stated here. See Rama siman 36 (thus): if at first he wrote a resh and afterward erred and made a tag like a dalet, it suffices when he drags the roof or the side with the tag and writes it again like a resh as stated above for the law; one who is strict — let blessing come upon him (Levush and Lechem Chamudot on Hilchot Sefer Torah, beginning of chapter 2).

Like scratching out what is inside — since he does not act upon the body of the remaining letter but only drags what closed it, and passing a quill over the letter is not effective (Sefer HaTerumah and Semag, thus). R' Moshe Galanti wrote in his responsum siman 124: a plain kaf that he made above like a dalet and not round like a resh — kosher; even though Rav Binyamin siman 36 wrote in the name of the Rosh that it is invalid and correction is not effective, nevertheless since many communities and established scribes write like a dalet — follow the majority practice, and the widespread custom is that anything without root in the Gemara is kosher bedieved; see there. But in responsum of Maharil siman 123 it implies it is invalid; and he wrote there siman 104: the foot of the kaf must be doubled like a roof so it not resemble a resh. R' Yehuda Levi siman 81 wrote: if he made a wide roof he needs correction to erase the entire roof, even in the word "Elohekha" he may erase it but must take care not to erase the leg (thus). It seems to me that if he can extend the leg to be doubled like the roof — so it appears — and Maharil wrote there that even for the Rosh who forbids digging into the Divine names, one may scrape and rewrite in its place, or erase the word before it and write the Name in its place. But Tosafot in Erchin page 6 wrote: when one drags out a Name it is forbidden to write anything else in its place.

And the separation is kosher. It seems to me the same applies if the leg of the kaf reaches the end without a parchment outline — he may drag a little, for it is no worse than adhesive from letter to letter; so appears in Beit Yosef, see there. Bach wrote in Orach Chayyim siman 125: if the entire length of the letter is stuck to its neighbor, dragging is not effective; if the form of the letter changed through sticking it is like a closed mem (Radbaz in Acharei Mos, thus). If the leg of the heh stuck like a hair's breadth such that a child knows it is a heh, whoever finds it easy to separate — I would not restrain his hand (Ralbag siman 57, and Radbaz disagrees on this, thus).
