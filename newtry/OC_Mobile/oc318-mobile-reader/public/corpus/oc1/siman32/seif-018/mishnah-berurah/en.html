(70) That adhered — and if he invalidates by this a minute touch like a hair's breadth, see what we wrote below regarding heh; see Biur Halachah:

(71) And it closed — and it is like a closed mem whose place is only at the end of a word and not in its middle; and likewise all letters of the double forms in the alef-beit — one writes the first ones at the beginning and middle of the word and the latter ones at the end; if one changed, it is invalid:

(72) And to open it — and the same applies to other letters whose image was damaged at the time of writing or afterward, and by dragging they will stand in their image — such as a dalet whose leg he extended and it resembles a simple kaf, and all the like: dragging does not help them since one does not act upon the body of the remaining letter but erases its leg, as above in s.k. 68, and afterward he corrects it. And the same applies here: if he extended the roof of the letter in width until it appears to everyone as a dalet — but above regarding writing heh instead of dalet, where we wrote there that one must also erase the leg of the dalet or its roof apart from the leg of the heh — this advice does not help, for extending the roof of the dalet — behold in any case its form is upon it; therefore this is not called any correction:

(73) As scraping the interior — for on the body of the letter one does nothing but drags the closure; and the same if he erred and wrote a chet instead of a bet — zayinin: there is no use in dragging only the roof apart from that reason; rather he must drag them until they are eliminated from their image. And likewise if he erred and made a simple final kaf above like a dalet, which according to many poskim is invalid as will be explained below in siman 36 — likewise there is no use in dragging the tag and making it round, for it is scraping the interior; rather he adds ink to it and makes it round. It appears that not being in order does not hinder this correction, for in any case its form is upon it:

(74) And what is its remedy — everything written in this section that there is a remedy means before he wrote the letter after it, for otherwise it is invalid in a Torah scroll, mezuzah, and tefillin, for we require them specifically in order — except where we explain explicitly that not being in order does not hinder:

(75) In the form of nun-kaf — for writing an open mem is in two writings, that is, at first one writes like a bent nun and afterward one hangs at its side like a vav; if so, whatever became invalid in it — that is, the vav — must be dragged entirely; but the nun that was written validly need not be dragged. And the same applies to every letter written in two writings, such as gimmel and the like: if it became invalid in one writing one need only drag that one — unlike reish that he made like a kind of dalet, where everything was written at once invalid and everything must be dragged:

(76) And reish — or vav that they made like a kind of reish, and all the like that was done in one writing:

(77) Like a kind of dalet — and the same applies to the roof of a simple kaf that they made wide until it looks like a dalet or reish; see Biur Halachah. Scribes stumble in this, alas:

(78) That was finished — the letter in its proper length, such as if he wrote the word "lo" and the vav adhered at its end to the lamed, and everything like this; and even in touch of any amount where the letter's form is not changed thereby — nevertheless it is invalid, for we require that the letter be surrounded by parchment. And if the letters spread from the moisture of the ink called giplasin until they appear stuck together — nevertheless if it appears the letters are not touching it is valid; but if the letters touch, even if this was done after writing, nevertheless it is invalid — Even HaEzer siman 125 s.k. 96 in the hagahah and in Beit Shmuel, see there; see above in Biur Halachah at the end of s.k. 96. If adhesion is found between letter and letter when they took out a Torah scroll for reading, see below siman 143 in Biur Halachah:

(79) Valid — and the same if the legs of letters or their roof and middle reach the end without parchment surrounding — one may drag a little, for it is no worse than adhesion of letter to letter where dragging helps. Know that in this not being in order does not hinder, as explained below in s.k. 105, since one does not add to the body of the letter. And even in letters of the Name that were finished and adhere below, one may drag:

(80) Scraping the interior — and it deals where the letter's form was not changed by the adhesion; but if the letter's form changed and the child cannot read it — and all the more if it changed to the form of another letter, such as a vav that adhered at its end to a bent nun with thick adhesion until it appears in the form of a tet, until a child who is neither wise nor foolish will read it as tet — dragging the adhesion does not help here, for it is like a mem that closed and is called scraping the interior; therefore one must also drag the nun, which too changed from its form and was invalidated by the adhesion; and likewise everything like this:

(81) As its correction — and regarding an ink drop falling in se'if 17, even though it was written at first as its correction, it was damaged by the drop and is not visible — unlike here where even before dragging each letter is clearly visible on its own; accordingly if the entire length of the letter is stuck to its fellow, dragging does not help. And Peri Chadash is lenient in this; see Sha'arei Efraim and Netiv Chayyim:

(82) If they touched — but if there is a thin gap, even only like a hair's breadth, it is valid.

(83) The heh — even if attached only like a hair's breadth such that a child knows it is heh, nevertheless he must scrape the entire leg, for wherever there is no letter-shape as transmitted to Moses at Sinai there is no letter on it; the same for kuf — a child's reading does not help.

(84) He shall scrape — mere separation does not help because it is like hollowing out.

(85) The leg — meaning the entire leg, whether in heh or kuf, since it was made in invalid fashion; see Biur Halachah.

(86) On the roof of the alef — the upper and lower yods should not touch the roof except at their thin parts, as explained below in Mishnah Berurah siman 36 on letter shapes.

(87) The face of the alef — meaning the upper yod; specifically when it was entirely attached and its shape was lost; but if it spread slightly and is not thin enough for proper writing, if a gap remains there is no concern and no correction is needed.

(88) To separate it — meaning to separate only the adhesion, and the letter remains proper by itself.

(89) That he shall scrape, etc. — meaning he must scrape the entire leg, and it does not suffice to separate the leg from the alef until its letter-shape alone is nullified, since the entire leg was written in invalid fashion. Likewise for a yod attached to an alef on the lower roof — he must scrape the entire yod and rewrite it; some are strict when the upper yod adheres at the time of writing to scrape the entire alef for this, for the rest of the letter is thereby written invalidly; but bedieved it is uncertain whether to be strict, as I wrote in Biur Halachah.

(90) On the yod — that is, the yod was made a straight line and there is no form of a yod; but it does no harm if the line is thickened a little.

(91) And similarly — and the same applies if the left leg of the tav was made straight and does not extend downward outward. See Levushei Serad, who left it in doubt why dragging is needed at all: he may continue afterward and thicken a little the yod of the shin until it is proper; and similarly in such a case with the leg of the tav — see there; and so it implies in Pri Megadim in Mishbetzot Zahav letter 20 that this advice helps even for alef; and see at the end of the words of Levushei Serad. All this is when he had not yet written the words after it, for otherwise certainly correcting the tav does not help; and similarly because it is out of order, since it no longer has the form of a tav.
