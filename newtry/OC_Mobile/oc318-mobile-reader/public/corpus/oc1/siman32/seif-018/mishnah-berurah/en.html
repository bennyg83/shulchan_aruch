That adhered. Whether a very thin contact, like a hairbreadth, invalidates in this case, see what we write below regarding the letter hei, and see Biur Halacha.
And it was closed. It becomes like a final mem, whose place is only at the end of a word and not in the middle. So too, regarding all the doubled letters mem, nun, tzadi, pei, and kaf in the alphabet, one writes the first forms at the beginning and middle of a word, and the final forms at the end; if he changed this, it is invalid.
And to open it. The same applies to other letters whose form was damaged at the time of writing or afterward, and which would be restored to their form through scraping, such as a dalet whose leg he extended so that it resembles a final kaf. In all such cases scraping does not help, since he is not performing an act on the body of the letter that remains; rather, he must erase its leg, as stated above in s.k. 68, and then correct it. It likewise helps in this case if he extends the roof of the letter widthwise until it is clear to all that it is a dalet. But above, where one wrote a hei in place of a dalet, where we wrote that he must also erase the leg of the dalet or its roof in addition to the leg of the hei, this solution of extending the roof of the dalet does not help, for even without it its form is already upon it, and therefore this is not called any correction.
Like chak tochos. For he performs no act on the body of the letter, but only scrapes away the closure. The same applies if he erred and wrote a ches instead of two zayins; it does not help to scrape only the roof, for the same reason, but he must scrape them until he nullifies their form. Likewise, if he erred and made a final kaf above like a dalet, which according to many poskim is invalid, as will be explained below in siman 36, it likewise does not help to scrape the tag and make it round, for that is chak tochos; rather, he should add ink to it and make it round. It appears that writing out of order does not invalidate in this correction, for even without this its form is upon it.
And what is its remedy. Wherever this seif says there is a remedy, that means before he has written the next letter; otherwise it is invalid in tefillin and mezuzos, where we specifically require writing in order, except where we explicitly explain that writing in order does not prevent the correction.
Like the form of nun and kaf. For writing an open mem is done through two writings: first one writes it like a bent nun, and afterward one hangs at its side something like a vav. Therefore, whatever part became invalid, namely the vav, must be scraped entirely, but the nun, which was written validly, need not be scraped. The same applies to any letter written in two acts of writing, such as a gimel and the like: if invalidity occurred in one act of writing, only that act must be scraped. This excludes a reish that he made like a dalet, where everything was written invalidly in one act and therefore everything must be scraped.
And a reish. Or a vav that he made like a reish, and all similar cases where it was made in one act of writing.
Like a dalet. The same applies to the roof of a final kaf that he made so wide that it appears like a dalet or a reish. See Biur Halacha. Scribes stumble in this, due to our many sins.
That was completed. The letter was completed in its proper length, such as if he wrote the word lo and the vav adhered at its end to the lamed, and all similar cases. Even with the slightest contact, where the letter did not thereby change from its form, it is nevertheless invalid, for we require the letter to be surrounded by parchment. If the letters spread because of the moisture of the ink, called giplasin, until they appear as if joined, but nevertheless it is evident that the letters do not touch, it is valid. If the letters do touch, even if this occurred after the writing, it is nevertheless invalid, as in Even HaEzer siman 125, seif 16 in the gloss and in Beis Shmuel there; see there. See above in Biur Halacha at the end of seif 16. If an adhesion between letters was found when a Sefer Torah was taken out for reading, see below in siman 143 in Biur Halacha.
It is valid. The same applies if the legs of letters, or their roofs or middle parts, reach the edge without surrounding parchment; he may scrape away a little, for this is no worse than letters adhering to one another, where scraping helps. Know that in this case writing out of order does not prevent the correction, as explained below in seif 25, since he does not add to the body of the letter. Even with the letters of the Divine Name that were completed and adhere at the bottom, he may scrape.
Chak tochos. This refers to a case where the letter did not change from its form because of the adhesion. But if the letter changed from its form and the child cannot read it, and all the more so if it changed into the form of another letter, such as a vav whose end adhered to a bent nun with a thick adhesion so that it appears like a tes, to the point that a child who is neither wise nor foolish would read it as a tes, scraping the adhesion does not help, for it is like a mem that became closed, and is called chak tochos. Therefore, he must also scrape the nun, which also changed from its form and was invalidated by the adhesion, and the same applies to all similar cases.
Properly. Regarding the ink drop in seif 17, although the letter was originally written properly, it was ruined by the drop and is no longer recognizable. This is not so here, where even before scraping each letter is clearly recognizable by itself. According to this, if the whole length of a letter is attached to another letter, scraping does not help. The Pri Chadash is lenient in this. See Shaarei Ephraim and Nesiv Chaim.
If they touched. But if there is a thin separation, even only like a hairbreadth, it is valid.
The hei. Even if it adhered only like a hairbreadth, in a way that a child knows it is a hei, nevertheless one must scrape the entire leg, for wherever the form of the letter is not as transmitted to Moshe at Sinai, the name of a letter is not upon it. Likewise with a kuf, a child’s reading does not help.
He should scrape. Mere separation does not help, because it is like chak tochos.
The leg. Meaning all of it, both in a hei and in a kuf, since it was made invalidly. See Biur Halacha.
In the roof of the alef. For the upper and lower yuds of the alef should touch the roof only with their thin points, as will be explained below in Mishnah Berurah, siman 36, in the laws of the forms of the letters.
The face of the alef. This means the upper yud, and specifically where all of it adhered, so that its form was ruined. But if it spread slightly and is not as thin as it ought to be for the beauty of the writing, if a separation remains, there is nothing wrong and no correction is needed.
To separate it. Meaning to separate the contact alone, and the letter will then remain proper by itself.
All. This means that he must scrape the entire leg; it is not enough to separate the leg from the alef until he nullifies it only from the form of a letter, since the whole leg was written invalidly. Likewise, if the yud on the alef adhered to the roof beneath it, one must scrape the entire yud and write it anew. Some are stringent that if the upper yud adhered at the time of writing, one must scrape the entire alef because of this, for afterward the rest of the letter was automatically written in invalidity through this. But after the fact it requires study whether one must be stringent in this, as I wrote in Biur Halacha.
With the yuds. That is, the yud became a straight line and does not have the form of a yud. But it does no harm if the line became slightly thick.
And the pei. The same applies if the left leg of the tav became straight and does not project downward outward. See Levushei Serad, who remains in difficulty as to why any scraping at all is needed; let him afterward extend and thicken the yud of the shin slightly until it is proper, and similarly in the leg of the tav; see there. So too it appears from Pri Megadim, Mishbetzos Zahav, letter 20, that this solution helps even with an alef. See the end of the words of Levushei Serad. All this is only where he has not written the following words; otherwise such a correction certainly does not help for the tav and similar cases, because it is out of order, since it presently does not have the form of a tav.
