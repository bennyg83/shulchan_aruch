In invalid form. Meaning mem patach is written in two writings — first like a bent nun, then a tag hangs from its side like vav; whatever invalidity is in the vav, drag it all; but the nun written properly need not be dragged — unlike resh made like dalet where all was written invalidly at once and must all be dragged. Accordingly in any letter written in two writings like gimmel where invalidity is in one writing — drag only that part; if he first wrote resh then mistakenly tagged it like dalet, dragging the tag or thigh with the tag and rewriting as resh suffices; the strict also here — ta'av. Levush and Lechem Chamudot: kaf leg must be double the roof lest it resemble resh. Responsum Mahari Halevi siman 81: if he made kaf roof wide, correction requires erasing the whole roof, even in Elohekha; he may erase but beware not to erase the leg — Magen Avraham wrote, and it seems to me if he can extend the leg to be double the roof, that is fine; see the note before this.

And the separation is valid. It seems the same if kaf leg reaches the parchment end without wrapping — one may drag somewhat, no worse than letter stuck to letter — Beit Chadash in Orach Chaim siman 125: if the whole letter is glued to its neighbor, dragging does not help; if the form changed by adhesion it is like sealed mem — Radak, on Magen Avraham. See Beit Chadash who raised: if adhesion is found in a Torah scroll during reading, need not take out another even on Shabbat and Yom Tov when dragging is impossible — nevertheless need not take another, since fundamentally a Torah scroll may be fixed by dragging, only Shabbat prohibition overrides; whatever is fit for mixing is not mixed, etc.; see there. In Sheyurei Kenesset HaGedolah: our custom is even letter stuck to letter found in a Torah scroll on Shabbat or Yom Tov we take out another — on Lechem Chamudot siman 143; in Sefer Even HaEzer he is strict about letters stuck at the start of writing that have no remedy by dragging — see there at length in Maginei Eretz, Profes edition.

On the roof. This whole section refers before writing the following letter, when there is remedy; afterward not, for it is out of order; specifically when no letter form remains without correction — forbidden to fix by writing before it because out of order; but if letter form remains, even without correction, he may fix even later, since the form was evident — no out-of-order concern. See seif 25 and seif kaf-vav 26, as explained.

Invalid. Specifically when the yod itself touched the alef; but if the left tip of yod touches alef it is valid, for even without the tip it is called yod — on Magen Avraham.
