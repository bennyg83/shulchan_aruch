In invalidity. That is to say, because the writing of an open mem is done in two writings: first one writes something like a bent nun, and afterward one hangs at its side something like a vav. Therefore, anything in which the invalidity was made, namely the vav, must be scraped away in its entirety, but the nun that was written validly need not be scraped away. This excludes a reish that one made like a dalet, where everything was written at once in invalidity, and the whole thing must be scraped away. According to this, the same applies to any letter written in two writings, such as a gimmel, where invalidity occurred in one writing; one need scrape away only that part. And if he first wrote a reish and afterward erred and made a tag on it like a dalet, it is sufficient to scrape away the tag, or the leg together with the tag, and then write it again like a reish; one who is stringent even in this is praiseworthy. Levush and Lechem Chamudos. The leg of the kaf must be twice the roof, so that it not resemble a reish. In Responsa Mahari HaLevi, siman 81, he wrote that if one made the roof of the kaf wide, it requires correction by erasing the entire roof, even in the word “your God” he may erase it, but he must be careful not to erase the leg. The Magen Avraham writes: It appears to me that if he can extend the leg so that it is twice the roof, that is proper. See the preceding s.k.
And he separated it, it is valid. It appears to me that the same applies if the leg of a final kaf reaches the end of the parchment without surrounding parchment; he may scrape away a little, for it is no worse than one letter attached to another. The Bach wrote in Even HaEzer, siman 125, that if the entire length of a letter is attached to another, scraping does not help. And if the form of the letter changed through the attachment, it is like a mem that became closed: Radach; see Magen Avraham. See the Bach, who concluded that if an attachment is found in a Sefer Torah during the reading, another scroll need not be brought out, even if it was found on Shabbos or Yom Tov, when it cannot be scraped. Nevertheless, another scroll is not brought out, since according to the essential law a Sefer Torah has a remedy through scraping, only the prohibition of Shabbos rests upon it; and anything fit for mixing, lack of actual mixing does not prevent it, etc.; see there. In Shiyarei Knesses HaGedolah he wrote: Our custom is that even where one letter attached to another is found in a Sefer Torah on Shabbos or Yom Tov, another Sefer Torah is brought out. See above, siman 143. In Sefer Even HaOzer he is stringent regarding letters attached at the beginning of the writing, that they have no remedy through scraping; see there at length in Sefer Maginei Eretz, Proops edition.
At the roof. This entire seif deals with a case before he wrote the following letter, for then there is a remedy. But afterward there is not, because it is out of order. Specifically, where the form of the letter did not remain without correction, it is forbidden to correct it after he wrote before it, because it is out of order. But if the form of the letter remained even without correction, he may correct it even afterward, since the form of the letter was recognizable and there is no problem of being out of order. See seif 25 and s.k. 26, what is written there.
Invalid. This is specifically where the yud itself touched the alef. But if the left thorn of the yud touched the alef, it is valid, for even without the thorn it is called a yud. See Magen Avraham.
