(243) On every side — the reason for twelve stitches is corresponding to the twelve tribes of Israel. We do not require stitching inside the bayit, only adjacent to the bayit; and one does not sew as one sews garments at the edge of a garment without leaving a margin, but leaves part of the titura outside the stitching. The hide of the batim below must extend from all four of its sides until it reaches beneath the stitch holes, so that the hide of the batim is sewn on every side together with the titura — and not like scribes who shorten the hide of the batim so it does not extend further beneath the stitch holes and is not sewn at all with the titura, only tightening the bayit inside the titura's groove; and Baruch She'amar wrote that he invalidated many tefillin for this reason. Rather, one must sew the hide of the batim together with the titura:

(244) From two sides — meaning that all the stitching should go around from both sides, front and back; thus one sews with two needles, one exiting toward the back and one entering toward the front:

(245) However, if, etc. — and likewise if he did not pass the stitching thread through, it does not invalidate post factum; see above in se'if kaf zayin:

(246) With one thread — and if it breaks, some say he may tie it, and some say he should remove it entirely from there and sew anew with another thread, since when it breaks during sewing it is evident the thread was weak and about to break and counts for nothing; but when the thread was short from the start and did not break, all agree he may tie another thread to it to finish the stitching.
