(Note 51) Where it is possible, etc., without a blessing — because the Rosh holds that the hide of the batim also requires processing lishmah. But the Rambam holds that the hide of the batim does not require processing at all; if so, the same law applies when they were processed not lishmah, for it is not inferior to not having processed them at all; therefore where it is not possible to find [hides] processed lishmah, he may leave batim that were processed not lishmah or were not processed at all, for one may rely on the Rambam in this so as not to nullify the mitzvah of tefillin; but nevertheless he should not recite a blessing, for blessings are not indispensable. And since the primary [view] is like the Rosh, who requires processing lishmah, therefore one should not recite a blessing upon them. For Rashi also holds thus: in Sanhedrin 48b, Abaye and Rava disagree — Abaye holds that designation is significant and Rava holds designation is not significant; and they conclude it is a dispute of tannaim: the Tana Kama holds that batim of tefillin do not require processing lishmah, and Rabban Shimon ben Gamliel holds that processing lishmah is required; and Rashi explained that processing is not called an act and is only like designation; and the Tana Kama holds that designation is not significant, therefore he holds processing lishmah is not required, since processing does not effect a change, since it is only designation and designation is not significant; and Rabban Shimon ben Gamliel holds designation is significant, therefore he requires processing lishmah, even though processing is only designation — nevertheless processing is effective since he holds designation is significant. And Tosafos challenged: if so, since we rule like Rava against Abaye that designation is not significant, then according to Rashi processing lishmah is not required. And in Gittin he invalidated a sefer Torah if the parchment is not processed lishmah; and he strained to distinguish between the hide of the batim and the parchment — for on the hide of the batim is written shin, which is a halacha l'Moshe miSinai, and it is fitting that they have the law of parchment. Therefore Tosafos explained the reverse: the Tana Kama holds processing lishmah is not required because he holds designation is significant, therefore he does not require processing lishmah and designation suffices. But Rabban Shimon ben Gamliel holds designation is not significant and designation does not help, therefore one must do an act lishmah — that is, processing. And so holds the Rosh; if so, since we rule like Rava that designation is not significant, therefore even the hide of the batim requires processing lishmah. And the Rambam explains like Rashi; and in order to resolve the difficulty of Tosafos he explained thus, as Tosafos wrote, to distinguish between the hide of the batim, which does not require processing lishmah, and the parchment, which requires processing lishmah; and even though Tosafos wrote that this is strained — Rashi and the Rambam hold it is not strained; therefore the Rambam holds that the hide of the batim does not require processing lishmah, since we rule designation is not significant like Rava (for even if not processed at all it is valid according to him). And see siman 42 and see there what is written in the Magen Avraham.
