(s.k. 51) Where possible, etc. Without a blessing. This is because the Rosh holds that the leather of the housings also requires processing for the sake of the mitzvah. But Rambam holds that the leather of the housings does not require processing at all. If so, the same law applies when they were processed not for their sake, for that is no worse than if they had not been processed at all. Therefore, where it is not possible to obtain housings processed for their sake, he should put on housings that were processed not for their sake, or not processed at all, for in this matter one may rely on Rambam’s view so as not to be nullified from the mitzvah of tefillin. Nevertheless he should not recite a blessing, since blessings do not prevent fulfillment. And since the primary ruling follows the Rosh, who requires processing for its sake, one should not bless over them. Rashi also holds this way. For in Sanhedrin 46, Abaye and Rava disagree: Abaye holds that designation is significant, and Rava holds that designation is not significant. The Gemara concludes that this is a dispute of Tannaim: the first Tanna holds that tefillin housings do not require processing for their sake, while Rabban Shimon ben Gamliel holds that they do require processing for their sake. Rashi explained that processing is not called an act, and is only like designation. The first Tanna holds that designation is not significant; therefore he holds that processing for its sake is unnecessary, for the processing neither raises nor lowers the status, since it is only designation, and designation is not significant. Rabban Shimon ben Gamliel holds that designation is significant; therefore he requires processing for its sake, for although the processing is only designation, nevertheless the processing is effective because he holds designation is significant. Tosafos asked: if so, since we rule like Rava against Abaye, that designation is not significant, then according to Rashi’s explanation processing for its sake should not be required. Yet in Gittin, a Sefer Torah is invalid if the parchment was not processed for its sake; and it is difficult to distinguish between the leather of the housings and parchment, since on the leather of the housings the shin is written, and it is a halachah transmitted to Moshe from Sinai, so it is fitting that they have the law of parchment. Therefore Tosafos explained the opposite: the first Tanna holds that processing for its sake is unnecessary because he holds designation is significant, and therefore processing for its sake is not needed, for designation suffices. But Rabban Shimon ben Gamliel holds that designation is not significant, and designation is ineffective; therefore one must perform an act for its sake, namely the processing. The Rosh likewise holds this way. If so, since we rule like Rava, that designation is not significant, even the leather of the housings requires processing for its sake. Rambam explains like Rashi. To resolve Tosafos’ difficulty, one must necessarily say as Tosafos wrote, that there is a distinction between the leather of the housings, which does not require processing for its sake, and the parchment, which does require processing for its sake. Although Tosafos wrote that this is forced, Rashi and Rambam hold that it is not forced. Therefore Rambam holds that the leather of the housings does not require processing for its sake, since we rule that designation is not significant, like Rava, for according to him even if it was not processed at all it is valid.
