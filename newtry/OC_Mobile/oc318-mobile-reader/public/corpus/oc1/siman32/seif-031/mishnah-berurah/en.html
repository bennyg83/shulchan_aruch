From writing. This implies that when one writes from written text he need not articulate the words with his mouth. It speaks specifically where the passages are also fluent to him, and then we are not concerned that he may err. So Magen Avraham and Eliyah Rabbah wrote to resolve the apparent contradiction with Yoreh Deah, siman 274, seif 2, and see Pri Megadim, who explains that this is Magen Avraham’s intent. But many later authorities disagree and rule that in every case he must articulate the word with his mouth before writing it. The Bach explains the reason: this is the proper mitzvah of writing Torah scrolls, tefillin, and mezuzos, so that the sanctity of the breath of reading each word as it leaves the reader’s mouth is drawn onto the letters when he writes them on the parchment. All this is ab initio, but after the fact it is not invalid in any case, provided he did not err.
By heart from one dictating, etc. Even if it is also fluent in his mouth.
And read it with his mouth. Each and every word before he writes it, so that he not err; all the more so if he is writing by heart without someone dictating.
