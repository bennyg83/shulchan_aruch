He is not permitted, etc. Even that portion which is fluent in his mouth, since the whole is not fluent in his mouth. The Bach is lenient regarding that portion, and so ruled Magen Avraham.
Unless he repeats, etc. This implies that if one writes from a written text, he need not utter it with his mouth. But in Shulchan Aruch, Yoreh De'ah 62, it implies that even one who writes from a written text must utter it with his mouth, and these rulings contradict one another. Olas Tamid likewise wondered about this. In my humble opinion, one can resolve it as follows: here it speaks of tefillin that are fluent in his mouth, in which case, if he utters them with his mouth, we do not require that he write from a written text. Therefore, when he writes them from a written text and they are also fluent in his mouth, he need not utter them from the text. But there, regarding a Torah scroll, presumably they are not fluent in his mouth, as explained in the Beis Yosef there, and therefore he must utter them with his mouth. This appears to be the intent of the Levush, and so also appears to be the view of Lechem Chamudos at the end of Hilchos Sefer Torah and of Sefer Magen Avraham. However, the Bach wrote that the reason he must utter them with his mouth is in order to draw all the sanctity onto the letter; if so, he must always utter them. In any event, the Shulchan Aruch, Levush, and the poskim do not hold of this reason, but rather the reason is lest he err. It implies that if he did not err, it is valid after the fact in all this, and so it also implies in Beis Yosef and the poskim in Yoreh De'ah.
