From the folds of the leather. This is the wording of Darchei Moshe in the name of the gloss in Baruch She’amar: one should not change the custom of the elders, who made it from a fold of leather. However, if he made them with a mold, or wrote them in ink on the white housing, we do not have grounds to invalidate them. In Gittin 20 it appears that when one presses from the other side with a mold, this is called engraving the strokes of the letters and is valid. So too in Even HaEzer, siman 125, seif 4. Thus the custom is to make the shins with a mold. However, one can say that this applies specifically to a metal plate, but leather, which is soft, is pushed away also around the letter, and this is considered carving out the interior spaces. Menahem Azaryah wrote in responsum, siman 93, that in a Torah scroll, tefillin, and mezuzos even engraving the strokes of the letters is invalid. It appears from his words that stamping with ink is valid, for this is called writing, only he did not want to rule so in practice; see there. It seems to me that in tefillin and mezuzos it is invalid, because it is impossible to be exact, and sometimes the second letter is stamped before the first, making it written out of order. The Taz in Yoreh Deah, siman 271, wrote that even a get should not be made with a mold, for it resembles engraving. In Gittin, Tosafos implies that we learn from the tablets that engraving is valid for a Torah scroll, as we find with the tablets; nevertheless, initially one should be careful, and so is the custom.
On the wearer’s right. It seems to me that with a left-handed person we follow ordinary right and left. The Nakdan in the Semag wrote that the shin with four heads is the more important one; therefore they make it on the right of the reader. If so, we are not concerned that the wearer is left-handed.
Four heads. The Nakdan explained the reason: so that in the recessed writing there should be a shin of three heads, as there was on the tablets. Therefore the yuds below should not touch the shin, unlike most scribes, who are not careful about this. These are the words of the Bach. It seems to me that one should not move from the custom of our fathers, for the Nakdan devised this reason on his own; in the Zohar, parashas Pinchas, page 580, other reasons are found for there being four heads. If so, just as with the three-headed shin the yud must touch, so too with the four-headed shin both must touch below. In Semag and Shimusha Rabbah it is written: he should make within it two yuds, etc.; see there. This also appears from the wording of the Rama in seif 43.
