Of four heads. This alludes to the script of the Tablets, which was engraved, as it says, “engraved on the Tablets,” so that all the letters were formed from the air. It follows that the shin had four walls for three spaces; this is the shin of four creases, where three spaces are made. We also have the script of a sefer Torah, whose letters protrude, and corresponding to that we make a shin of three heads. The reason this allusion is made specifically with the letter shin is to allude to the days on which a person lays tefillin during the year, which are shin in number. Some say that the Divine Name in the cipher of Atbash equals the letters mem, tzadi, peh, tzadi, and this includes the shin; therefore it says, “and all the peoples of the earth shall see that the Name of Hashem is called upon you,” whose initial letters allude to shin, for nothing is seen outwardly except the two shins. I found that it is better to make these shins with creases and not by a mold, for that is like engraving, and one should not make engraving as in the writing of tefillin.
