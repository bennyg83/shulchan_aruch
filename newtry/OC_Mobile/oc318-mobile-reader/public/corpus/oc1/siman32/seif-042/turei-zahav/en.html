Of four heads — this alludes to the script of the Tablets that was sunken writing, as it says "engraved on the Tablets" — behold all the letters were from the air. Thus the shin has four faces for three air-spaces, from four creases made there creating three spaces. We also have twenty letters in a sefer Torah that were protruding, and opposite them one makes a shin of three heads. The reason for this allusion specifically in the letter shin is to hint at the days a person puts on in a year, which are three hundred and sixty-five — shin. Some say the Name HaVaYaH in at-bash adds up to matzafatz — meaning shin. Therefore it says "and all the peoples of the earth shall see that the Name of Hashem is called upon you" — initial letters shin — which is not seen from outside but the two shins. I found it better to make these shins in creases and not by stamp, for that is like engraving, and one does not engrave as in tefillin script.
