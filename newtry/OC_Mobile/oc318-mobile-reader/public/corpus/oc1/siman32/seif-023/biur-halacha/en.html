It has no remedy. See the Mishnah Berurah, which says that otherwise he should scrape away, etc. This means that first he should scrape away, and afterward write what was missing from it; for otherwise, below, this requires study, since at the time of writing it was not written in order.
And if there is extra, etc. See the Mishnah Berurah, where he wrote in the name of the Pri Megadim regarding a doubled word that sometimes it is better to erase the first one, in order to account even for Rabbeinu Tam's opinion; see there. See also there in Mishbetzos Zahav, s.k. 20, where he records an incident with a scribe who wrote "beware, beware" twice, erased one vav and the second word "beware," and extended the reish until the second vav; he ruled it valid, see there. According to the above Pri Megadim, initially one should also do this. He further wrote there that even if the second word were another word that has no relevance at all to tefillin, and it had a vav at the end, and he scraped as above, it is also valid, for what of it? Even though at first it was invalid because the vav was very far from "to your fathers," nevertheless, so long as he had not yet repaired it and extended the beis, it appeared as two words. To me this requires study, for we require that he write them for their sake, meaning for the sanctity of tefillin, and this is indispensable even after the fact, as above in se'if 19. Even if you will say that he intended this word for their sake, who says that this helps, since that word has no relevance at all to tefillin? This requires study.
