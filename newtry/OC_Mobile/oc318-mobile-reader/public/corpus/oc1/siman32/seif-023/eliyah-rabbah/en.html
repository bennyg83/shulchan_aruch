In the middle of a word, etc. For example, the words chomer, se'or, and lizboach, whose law is to be written deficiently, and he wrote them plene, have no remedy except with a new reish, and that is out of order. The same applies to me'odecha, totaphot, and mezuzot in Shema, and in the parashah of Vehayah Im Shamoa, if he wrote the word hatovah or otam lacking a vav where it should be plene, it is invalid. But to extend a letter, such as where the correction depends on a beis, final kaf, dalet, or reish, which can be extended slightly, for example in le'avotecha, avdah, or yevi'acha, it is valid, as stated by Beis Yosef. But Knesses HaGedolah wrote in the name of the Radbaz that with plene and deficient spellings involving a vav or yud, when they are small and thin, any thickening of the letter before or after them will prevent it from appearing as two words, and there is no change of a letter and no writing out of order, end quote. He further wrote in the name of the Radbaz that if the word bechozek was written twice, he should not scrape away the second bechozek and extend the roof of the kuf of the first bechozek until it reaches near the word yad; whether he leaves the leg of the kuf in its place or scrapes the leg of the kuf and draws it to the end of the roof, this is out of order. Rather, he should leave it blank, for whenever there is not between one word and another a space the size of one peh, it is valid, end quote. He, of blessed memory, wondered at this, for if he leaves the leg of the kuf in its place, it is not out of order. He further elaborated to argue that even if one wants to scrape the leg of the kuf and draw it near the end of the roof, there is no writing out of order here, since this erasure is not necessary. It is called out of order only when that writing is needed and without that writing the tefillin would be invalid. But when that writing is not needed, it is not called out of order. Even when that writing is needed, we say this only when it is needed because of a deficiency in the letter or the word itself, but when it is needed only so that it not appear like a closed parashah, it is not called out of order. It is clear that the Magen Avraham's objection to Knesses HaGedolah on this matter is not valid. In any event, it appears that the kuf should be extended, even if there will not be a leg at its end. Shiyarei Knesses HaGedolah wrote in the name of Mahara and Mahara Sasson that tefillin in which the ches is shaped like a dalet and zayin should not be invalidated, and he should scrape from the dalet until he makes it into a zayin, and this is not out of order, end quote. Olas Tamid wrote: in my humble opinion, it needs no correction at all; however, in practice this requires study.
