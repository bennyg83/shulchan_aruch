(Seif katan 23) And it is not, etc. And if it makes it deficient, etc., or if it appears, etc., even though the letter is recognizable, it is invalid, says Radach. In Even HaEzer siman 125, Pri Chadash seif katan 15 wrote concerning these words of Radach: I do not see a reason for this ruling. If the roof or leg of the letter became thick because of an ink drop that fell on it, what of it? He can correct it and make it proper with the pen, and there is no carving out of the interior, since if he left it as it was without scraping at all it would be valid. Similarly, when a small thorn protrudes from the letter in a way that does not change the form of the letter at all, and the letter is recognizable to a child who is neither wise nor foolish, here too it appears to me to be valid. The words of the Rav mentioned in this law are far from reason. However, I do not understand why Magen Avraham and Pri Chadash brought this law in the name of Mahardach, for in my humble opinion Rama in his gloss there, siman 125 seif 8, brought it in the name of Maharik, that some are stringent when an ink drop fell even if the letter is recognizable. There too the Pri Chadash disagrees, saying that this does not appear from the responsum of Maharik; see there.
Even if he erased the drop, etc. In my humble opinion this is a scribal error, and it should read: if he did not erase the drop completely, etc. This is the law that he afterward brings in the name of Knesses HaGedolah.
If, etc. And it looks like a peh, then the name of a beis is not upon it, etc. This should be the reading, and so it is in Beis Yosef. But I do not know why Magen Avraham copied it, since it is the very law explained in Shulchan Aruch. Beis Yosef needed to bring it in his book only to exclude the opinion of Semak, who disagrees with the law written here in Shulchan Aruch.
And it appears to me that if wax dripped, etc. It is permitted, for this is not considered carving out the interior. When the wax is removed the letters remain on their own. This differs from an ink drop, where if he scrapes away the drop it is invalid for that reason. Here, with wax, even before the wax is removed, the letters beneath the wax were not nullified; they were only covered by wax.
See what I wrote in siman 340 seif katan 3. Meaning, there Magen Avraham brought in seif katan 4 in the name of the Bach that if wax dripped on its own onto a book, and in that place there is room to write two letters, and he erased it on Shabbos, he is liable. This refers to a case where something is truly written beneath the wax, as appears from the language of the Bach brought in the Taz there; see there. So too Responsa Me'il Tzedakah siman 25 wrote. Therefore, the reason for liability is writing, for as long as the wax covered the letters it is as if he had erased them. This is also explained in that responsum. At first glance, this contradicts Magen Avraham's words here, where he wrote first that if wax dripped, etc., it is permitted; for if so, according to the Bach this should be considered carving out the interior. However, the above responsum of Me'il Tzedakah disagreed with the Bach, and explained the Tosefta from which the Bach derived his ruling in another way. He wrote by reasoning that removing wax is not considered writing, but only revealing covered writing. Otherwise, if sheets were tied with a permanent knot on Shabbos, the scroll would be invalid to read from, and most of our Torah scrolls are full of wax drips. Nevertheless, reading in the place of the wax is forbidden, because written matters may not be recited by heart, etc. According to this, Magen Avraham's ruling here is understandable: where wax dripped, as above, it is not carving out the interior. But since in siman 340 Magen Avraham brought the Bach's words and did not disagree with him, his words here require explanation, and one must distinguish between the cases.
