(s"k 23) And it is not… and if it lacks… or appears… although the letter is recognizable it is invalid — Radbaz; and in Even HaEzer siman 125 part 8 s"k 15 he wrote on Radbaz's words, and so on: and I do not see reason for this ruling, for if the roof or thigh thickened from an ink drop that fell — what of it, he can fix it and make it only with a quill, and this is not hollowing out, for if left thus without any dragging it is valid; and likewise when a small thorn protrudes outside the letter without changing the letter's form at all — also the letter is recognizable without a neither-wise-nor-foolish child; in this too it seems to me to validate. The Rambam's words cited in this law are far from reason — end quote. Except I did not understand that Magen Avraham and Peri Chadash brought this law in the name of Maharadach; and in my humble opinion Rama in hagahah there siman 125 seif 8 brought in the name of Maharik that some are strict when a drop fell even if the letter is recognizable — see there; and there Peri Chadash disagrees, for it does not seem so in Maharik's responsum — see there:

Even if he erased the drop… in my humble opinion it is a textual error and should read: if he did not completely erase the drop… and this is the law he brought afterward in the name of 25:

If… and it appears like a pe, then there is no bet… and so it should read, and so in Beit Yosef. But I did not know for what purpose Magen Avraham copied it, for it is the law explained in Shulchan Aruch, and the Rabbenu Yerucham had to bring it in his book to exclude the view of Semak who disagrees with the law written here in Shulchan Aruch:

And it seems to me that if wax dripped… it is permitted, for it is not like hollowing out, for through removing the wax the letters remain anyway, as with an ink drop — unlike the Taz, for if he drags the drop the Taz here with wax; also before removing wax the letters beneath were not nullified, only covered with wax:

See there siman 340 seif 3. Meaning that Magen Avraham brought in s"k 4 in the name of Bach that if wax dripped by itself onto the scroll and in its place there is room to write two letters and he erased it on Shabbat he is liable — that is, when it is really written beneath the wax; and as appears from Bach's words brought in Taz there — see there; and likewise in responsum Ma'alot Tzedakah siman 25; therefore the reason for liability is because of writing, for all the while wax covered them it is as if he erased them. And likewise explained in responsum Mahatz cited above, and this contradicts Magen Avraham's words at first, "if dripped… permitted," for per Bach it is hollowing out. However, in responsum Mahatz cited above he disagreed with Bach, and what Bach brought from the addendum — another explanation in it, different from what Bach understood; and it is reasonable as he wrote, that it is not like writing in erasing wax, but only like revealing signed writing; but from now, if sheets are tied in a permanent knot on Shabbat, it will be invalid to read from it, and most of our scrolls are full of wax drips. However, to read in the place of wax is forbidden because matters of writing may not be recited by heart… end quote.
