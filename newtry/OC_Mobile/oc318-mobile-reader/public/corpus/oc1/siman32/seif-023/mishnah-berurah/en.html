(108) Correction — through completion; and it deals with that from here until the end of the parshah there are Divine Names that may not be scraped, for otherwise he would scrape to the end.

(109) In their being — meaning: in the order they are written in the Torah they should be written.

(110) One letter — and if he wrote an extra word he should scrape it and may leave the space blank [if he has no letters from the words before it that he could extend into that place]; and the blank space does not invalidate as long as there is not in its place the measure of spacing between parshah and parshah, which is the space of nine letters. And sometimes there is correction even in this, such as when he can extend a letter from the word before it to reduce the space of nine letters. And more than this: in a way that tefillin would be invalidated because of the space — then even if at the end of the word before it there is a heh or kuf he may extend its roof to reduce the space, and although through the extension the leg of the heh and kuf will not be at their end, there is no concern b'dieved. And Pri Megadim wrote that in a doubled word it is better to erase the second, for the first word is considered properly written. And if before the first there is a letter he could extend, it is better to erase the first word in order to consider also the view of Rabbeinu Tam that the measure of a parshah is three letters.

(111) That he scrape — and the parshah is not invalidated because of hollowing of interiors, since he does not act on the body of the words and letters.

(112) It appears as two words — and sometimes correction helps by scraping it and extending the letter before it to fill its place, such as in le-avoteikha where he wrote it full with a vav after the bet: he scrapes it and extends the bet before it to fill its place. And likewise if the correction depends on kaf or dalet or reish that can be extended a little to fill the space of the extra letter that was scraped; but if the letters that can be extended are after the extra letter, he cannot extend them backward until he first scrapes from them, such as in she'ar written in the Torah where he wrote it full with a vav after the alef — he cannot extend the reish after it until he first scrapes its leg, and it is found that he canceled its form; and when he corrects afterward it is writing out of order and there is no correction unless he can fill its place by thickening the letter before and after it a little, which is not considered changing the letter; unlike when the letters that can be extended are before the extra letter, then he can extend them much without subtracting anything from them. But there is room to doubt regarding the first mitzvah whose law is to be written deficient and he wrote it full and erased the vav afterward — whether extending the tzadi downward afterward helps, or perhaps as long as he does not extend the letter upward it is considered two words even though he joined it from below; and likewise in hotsi'akha if he wrote it full with a yod between tzadi and alef — in all these there is room to doubt whether correction helps by extending the letter before it downward; and nevertheless anything that appears as two words is invalid. But if at the very beginning of writing he extends the lower leg of the nun and tzadi and writes the adjacent letter within it, such as panai or artzi, it is kosher since they were written in one word; nevertheless lekhatchilah it is not proper to do so, to hide a letter inside a letter, for some are strict about this.
