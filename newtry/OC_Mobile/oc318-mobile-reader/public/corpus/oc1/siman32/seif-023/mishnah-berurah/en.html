A remedy. By completing it. This speaks of a case where from here until the end of the passage there are Divine Names that may not be erased; otherwise he should scrape until the end of the passage.
They shall be as they are. Meaning that they must be written in the order in which they are written in the Torah.
One letter. If he wrote an extra word, he should scrape it away and may leave the place blank, if he has no letters from the preceding words that he can extend into that place. The blank space does not invalidate so long as in its place there is not the measure of the space between one passage and another, which is nine letters. Sometimes there is a remedy even for this, such as if he can extend a letter from the preceding word in order to reduce the space of nine letters. More than this: in a case where the tefillin would be invalidated because of the space, even if at the end of the preceding word there is a hei or kuf, he may extend their roofs to reduce the space, and although through the extension the leg of the hei or kuf will not be at its end, this is not a concern after the fact. Pri Megadim wrote that with a doubled word it is better to erase the second one, for the first word was written properly. If before the first word there is a letter that can be extended, it is better to erase the first word, in order to be concerned even for Rabbeinu Tam’s view that the measure of a passage break is three letters.
That he should scrape. The passage is not invalidated because of chak tochos, since he performs no act on the body of the words and letters.
It appears as two words. Sometimes correction helps by scraping it away and extending the preceding letter so that it fills its place. For example, if one wrote la’avosecha plene, with a vav after the beis, he should scrape it away and extend the preceding beis so that it fills its place. So too if the correction depends on a kaf, dalet, or reish, which can be extended slightly to fill the place of the extra letter that was scraped away. But if the letters that can be extended are after the extra letter, and they cannot be extended backward until one first scrapes from them, such as the word she’er written in the Torah, if he wrote it plene with a vav after the alef, he cannot draw the reish backward until he first scrapes its leg. It follows that he has nullified its form, and when he afterward corrects it he is writing out of order. There is no remedy unless he can fill its place by thickening slightly the letter before it and the letter after it, in a way that does not constitute a change in the letter. This is not so where the letters that can be extended are before the extra letter; then he can extend them greatly without detracting from them at all. However, one should be uncertain regarding the first occurrence of mitzvos, whose law is to be defective, if he wrote it plene and afterward erased the vav: does lengthening the tzadi below afterward help, or perhaps as long as he does not lengthen the letter above, it is considered like two words even though he joined it below? So too with nasan, whose law is to be defective, and with hotzi’acha, if he wrote it plene with a yud between the tzadi and the alef. In all these cases one should be uncertain whether they have a remedy by lengthening the preceding letter below. In any event, whenever it appears as two words, it is invalid. But if at the beginning of the writing he extends the lower leg of the nun or tzadi and writes the adjacent letter into it, such as in the words penei or artzi, it is valid, since they were written as one word. Nevertheless, initially it is not proper to do so, to swallow one letter into another, because some are stringent in this.
