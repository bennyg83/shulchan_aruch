They were written out of order. For all of these cases where the invalidation requires repair through scraping, repair does not help, since it is engraving from within; for he must scrape the entire leg that was written invalidly. Therefore, even though he repairs it afterward, it is out of order. But with yuds that do not touch the bodies of the letters, he can repair them by attaching them, and it is not considered out of order. Regarding a ches that he wrote as two zayins, since the form of a ches is not there at all, subsequent repair does not help. According to this, if he also wrote the roof-piece over the two zayins, only they were not attached to one another, repair afterward certainly helps as well. What the Mechaber wrote afterward in the name of “there is one who says” is not a disagreement, but only a novel law. See what I wrote further regarding yuds that do not touch in siman 36.
It appears like two words, etc. The Beit Yosef wrote in the name of Rabbi Yitzchak Aksandari that sometimes repair helps for an extra letter, such as if “la’avosecha” was written plene with an extra vav; after he scrapes away the vav, he extends the beis before it in a manner that fills the space of the erasure. Regarding a tzadi, he was uncertain, such as if “hotzi’acha” was written with a yud, and if he erases the yud he can afterward extend the lower part but not the upper part, whether this helps or not. This uncertainty also applies to a bent nun whose lower part was extended much more than the proper measure to fill the line, whether it is valid or not.
