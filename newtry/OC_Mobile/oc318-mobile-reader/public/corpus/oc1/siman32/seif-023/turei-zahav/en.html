They were written out of order. For all those whose invalidity requires correction by scraping, correction does not help, because it is ḥak tukhot—for one must scrape the entire stroke written invalid, and thus even if corrected afterward it remains out of order. But with yods that do not touch the body of the letters, one may correct by pasting them, and it is not considered out of order. Regarding ḥet that he wrote as two zayins, where there is no form of ḥet at all—therefore correction afterward does not help. Accordingly, if he also wrote the cross-stroke over the two zayins, only that it did not adhere together, that is certainly fine and correction afterward also helps. And what the author wrote later in the name of "some say"—there is no dispute here, only a novel ruling; and as I wrote further below in siman 36 regarding yods that do not touch.

It looks like two words, etc. As the Beit Yosef in the name of Riaz: sometimes correction helps to leave an extra letter, such as "le-avoteikha" full with vav—that after scraping the vav, the bet before it will extend so that the portion of the scraped place will be filled. In ḥet they were uncertain, such as "hotziakha" that they wrote with yod: if he erases the yod he may afterward extend the lower part and not the upper—whether it helps or not, perforce. This doubt exists in a bent nun where the lower part extends much more than the proportion fit to fill the line—is it kosher or not?
