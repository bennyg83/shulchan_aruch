Se'if 6, it is not necessary to score lines, etc. Bach wrote that Shulchan Aruch ruled like Tosafos and the Rosh in the name of Rabbeinu Tam, that if one knows how to write straight without scoring, he has no permission to score all the lines, for he is considered an ordinary person acting unnecessarily. Moreover, since Baal HaItur and Rabbeinu Simchah hold that scoring lines in tefillin invalidates them even after the fact, therefore one should score only the top line, which is required in any event because it is prohibited to write three words without scoring. But he should not score all four sides, lest according to them he has done an extra act and invalidated the tefillin. The rabbi in the gloss to Shulchan Aruch, however, followed the stringent authorities and wrote that one must score the four sides, and he inserted the word must, end quote of Bach; see there. This is also the explicit reading in Olas Tamid, that one should not score; see there, letters 12 and 13. Such was the reading before Magen Avraham; see s.k. 6, Machatzis HaShekel there, Levushei Serad there, Halachah Berurah, letter 7, Maamar Mordechai, letter 7, and Birkei Yosef, letter 1.
There, only the top line. If he did not score even the top line, although he committed a prohibition, the tefillin are valid. So it appears from the words of GraZ, letter 8, who wrote: Tefillin do not require scoring except for the top line, because rabbinically it is prohibited at the outset to write three words from a verse of sacred Scripture without scoring, end quote. This implies that after the fact, if he already wrote, one should not invalidate. Peschei Olam and Matamei HaShulchan, letter 19, wrote in the name of Gra that if he did not score even the top line according to the Mechaber, or from every side according to Rama, according to Rabbeinu Tam it is invalid even after the fact; but according to what we maintain, that in a Torah scroll one scores all of it, we have no proof to invalidate. The reason is that the halachah was decided in the Talmud, Megillah, folio 18b, and Menachos, folio 32b, that tefillin do not require scoring. The need to score the top line is only because it is prohibited to write three words without scoring, as appears in Yoreh De'ah, siman 284, and as the authorities wrote. If so, this prohibition does not relate to the mitzvah of tefillin, but to the person, so that he write straight, as Machatzis HaShekel, s.k. 7, and the Shach there wrote. Therefore one should not invalidate after the fact. So too appears from the responsum of Rabbi Akiva Eiger, siman 50, cited by Pis'chei Teshuvah, siman 288, Ohel HaEdus, who wrote: Since the halachah transmitted to Moshe from Sinai requires scoring in a mezuzah, if he wrote without scoring it is not called writing, end quote. This implies that with tefillin, where it is not a halachah transmitted to Moshe from Sinai, even if he did not score, it is called writing and is valid. Such an incident occurred in our city Baghdad, may the Supreme One establish it, with one scribe who wrote many tefillin without scoring even the top line, and the gaon Rabbi Yosef Chaim Tov, may his light shine, permitted them.
There, and if he does not know how to keep the writing straight, etc. Even if he knows how to keep the writing straight without scoring, if he scores in order to write more beautifully and more straight, since he is not doing it as a stringency but in order to write more straight, he is not called an ordinary person acting unnecessarily. Beis Yosef; GraZ, letter 8. Pri Megadim, Eshel Avraham, letter 6, wrote that there is in this neither ordinary-person behavior nor an extra act.
There, and he should not score with lead, etc. The same applies to other types of colors, such as black and red. Beis Yosef. So Shulchan Aruch wrote in Yoreh De'ah, siman 271, se'if 5, lead or the like, and so wrote the later authorities.
There, because the place of the scoring remains colored. If after the fact he scored with other kinds of colors, some validate: Lechem Chamudos, Laws of Torah Scrolls, letter 25; Eliyah Rabbah, letter 13; GraZ, letter 8. But Levush, letter 6, invalidated. Pri Megadim, Eshel Avraham, letter 9, wrote that according to Levush, even if he scored with ink it is invalid, and it is forbidden to score between the lines as well. Keset HaSofer, siman 3, letter 3, in Lishkas HaSofer, wrote about Lechem Chamudos that it is not compelling. Regarding what Lechem Chamudos wrote about Levush, that in the laws of tefillin, siman 32, se'if 6, he wrote that if one scored with lead it is invalid, while in the laws of a Torah scroll, siman 271, se'if 5, he did not mention any invalidation, Keset HaSofer wrote in Lishkas HaSofer there: One can say that Levush relied on what he wrote in the laws of tefillin, that it is invalid; see Beis Yosef, Even HaEzer, siman 125, end quote. Also Meleches Shamayim, rule 5, letter 5, in Binah, challenged Eliyah Rabbah and Bnei Yonah, cited in Keset HaSofer there, from Beis Yosef, Even HaEzer, siman 125, and wrote: From there we learn to here that, beyond the lack of the required scoring, there is also a concern of writing over writing, end quote. See Pis'chei Teshuvah, siman 271, letter 15.
