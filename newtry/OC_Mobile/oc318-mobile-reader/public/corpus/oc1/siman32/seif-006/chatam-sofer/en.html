See note: see well responsa Chatam Sofer Yoreh De'ah, siman 256 [there he extended regarding a Torah scroll whose ink changed after writing and turned reddish for several reasons — whether it is kosher or not. He concludes there, and these are his words: what emerges from our words — the above Torah scroll, if the change to redness is from aging and the way of ink, behold it is kosher, for it was written in ink; it is good to pass over it with a quill to preserve it many more days. It is nearly certain that even in the holy Names it is permitted — ink on ink, for there is no erasure here. But if it changed to actual red, unlike the nature of plain ink, or even only to redness that changed immediately and quickly, which appears not to be from aging but that the ink was made by a new invention from other markers — behold it is invalid from the outset, and it is forbidden to pass over the holy Names, for it is like ink on top of sikra that erases the lower layer. Regarding sanctity of the Name there is no distinction — even if written in any color it is forbidden to erase, for there it is not written "and you shall write," from which we derive writing in ink, etc.].
