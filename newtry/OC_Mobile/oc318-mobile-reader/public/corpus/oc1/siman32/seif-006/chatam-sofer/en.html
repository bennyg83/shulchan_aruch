Note: Examine Responsa Chatam Sofer, Yoreh De'ah, siman 256, concerning a Sefer Torah whose ink changed after writing and became reddish for various possible reasons, and whether it remained valid. He concludes: 'It follows from our discussion that if the aforementioned Sefer Torah's reddish change resulted from age, in the ordinary manner of ink, it is valid, for it was written in ink. It is proper to retrace it with a quill so that it will endure longer. It is almost certain that this is permitted even over the sacred Names, because ink over ink involves no erasure. If, however, it changed to actual red unlike ordinary ink, or even merely became reddish but changed immediately, demonstrating that age was not the cause and that the ink was manufactured through a new process from other substances, then it was invalid from the outset. One may not retrace the sacred Names, because this resembles placing ink over red pigment, thereby erasing the lower writing. Regarding the sanctity of a Divine Name there is no distinction: even if written in any color, it may not be erased, because the verse “you shall write them,” from which we derive writing in ink, is not stated regarding that prohibition, etc.'
