Seif 6, one should not, etc. The wording of the Shulchan Aruch is that one should not score lines, as Tosafos wrote in Menachos there, s.v. ha, etc., and one can say, etc. The Rama emended the word to 'must,' as written in the Tur, that one need not, etc.; and if he wishes to score lines, permission is in his hand. As written in the Sefer HaTerumah, he is not called a commoner unless he does so as a stringency, but in this case, where his intent is to write more straightly, it is permitted.
Except, etc. Tosafos there, from that which is stated in Gittin 6b, and Tosafos there, s.v. Rabbi Yitzchak says, etc.; and see Yoreh De'ah siman 284, seif 2, in the gloss.
And if he is not, etc. Tosafos in Gittin there, because of 'This is my God and I will beautify Him,' etc.
And not, etc. For this is what is stated in Moed Katan 7, that one may score with a reed, not to exclude a knife but to exclude lead and the like, etc. Sefer HaTerumah and Hagahos Maimoniyos.
And some say, etc. That is to say, it requires four scored lines, unlike the Shulchan Aruch, which requires only one above. So too Tosafos wrote in Gittin there, in the above s.v., that when it says that a sheet that is not scored is invalid, this means four scored lines, above, below, and on the sides. The same applies to tefillin, for according to them a Sefer Torah and tefillin are equal, as stated there. So too wrote the Semag, the Semak, Mordechai, and the Rosh. But according to us, since we hold that a Sefer Torah requires scoring, as written in Yoreh De'ah siman 271, seif 5, this is not necessary.
