16 — One may not, etc. Shulchan Arukh's language: one may not rule lines; as Tosafot Menachot there, s.v. ha, etc.; one may say, etc.; Rama emended "requires" as Tur — "not required," etc.; if he wishes to rule, he may; as in Sefer HaTerumah — not called a layman except when he does so for stringency; but here his intent is to write straighter — doubtful.

Except, etc. Tosafot there from Gittin 6b; Tosafot there, s.v. amar Rabbi Yehudah, etc.; see Yoreh De'ah, siman 284, seif 2, in Hagahah.

And if he is not, etc. Tosafot Gittin there — for this reason "Eli," etc.

And not, etc. As Semag in Mezuzah — from sargelin with a reed: not to exclude a knife but to exclude lead and the like; Sefer HaTerumah and Hagahot Maimoniyot.

And some say, etc. Meaning he requires four rulings — not like Shulchan Arukh, which requires only one above; so Tosafot Gittin there, s.v. han"l: "a sheet that is not ruled is invalid" means four rulings above, below, and on the sides; the same for tefillin — for them Torah scroll and tefillin are equal, as written; so Semag, Semak, Mordechai, and Or Zarua; but for us, who rule a Torah scroll requires ruling as Yoreh De'ah, siman 271, seif 5 — we do not need this.
