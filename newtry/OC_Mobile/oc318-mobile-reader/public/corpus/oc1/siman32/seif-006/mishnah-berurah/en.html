The upper line. This is because tefillin do not have a halachah transmitted to Moshe at Sinai requiring scoring, as a mezuzah does. Rather, it is only because one may not write three words from a verse without scoring. Therefore, when he scores the upper line it is sufficient, for ordinary people know how to train their hand to write all the lines straight after they have written the upper line. It says that he need not do so because, if he wishes to score all the lines in order to write more straightly and beautify the lines, he has permission to do so. If he did not score even the upper line, see Biur Halacha.
He should score. This means initially, because of, 'This is my God and I will beautify Him.' But after the fact, even if he did not score and he wrote crooked lines, the tefillin should not be invalidated.
With lead. The same applies with ink, red pigment, and anything similar; even between the lines it is forbidden to score with it. All this is initially, but after the fact one should not be stringent; so wrote Lechem Chamudos and Eliyah Rabbah. In Responsa Devar Shmuel, siman 362, he is very doubtful about this if it was scored with some black color, because of a letter touching another letter through the black scoring, unless he wrote beneath the scoring; see there.
