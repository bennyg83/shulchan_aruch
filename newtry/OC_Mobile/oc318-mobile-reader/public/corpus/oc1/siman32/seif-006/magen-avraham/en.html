One should not rule lines. Meaning: because tefillin do not require ruling and one exempt from the matter who does it is called a layman; in some books it is written here in hagahah "one need not rule," and so Bach — Rama emended the word "must" and disagrees with him, and wrote one should not rule.

An upper line. Because it is forbidden to write a verse without ruling, and when ruling an upper line suffices. He challenged in Berakhat HaZevach in Menachot folio 32: if so why did Mar Zutra rule all the parshah, as in the end of chapter 2 of Yevamot, end quote. In my view the Tur and Rosh were careful about this, for they wrote that ordinary people know how to steady their hand after writing an upper line; Mar Zutra was not expert to steady his hand after an upper line — therefore he ruled everything. Also the Talmud's language is "Mar Zutra rules" and writes the whole parshah — one may say he did not rule only an upper line but wrote the whole parshah, to exclude the view that one need not write it all. He also challenged what the Rosh wrote in Yevamot there — that Abbaye wrote a get of chalitzah and we read it to ourselves: "until she is taken I did not know why" — for a kal vachomer, three write, end quote. He challenged: the Rosh himself wrote in Megillah that two write without ruling, three do not write, etc., see there. In my view the Rosh holds that Abbaye did not write everything because he holds like Mar bar Rav Ashi that it cannot be written even with ruling, as explained in the Gemara — therefore the Rosh challenged: a kal vachomer, three words they write with ruling in any case; if so why did Abbaye not write "I did not wish to take"?

And if he does not know, etc. They did not say in Yerushalmi that one exempt from the matter who does it is called a layman except when he does it as a stringency; but here he does not intend that, for it is not a matter of prohibition to which stringency applies — he does not rule only to write more straight — he is not a layman (Beit Yosef). See Yoreh De'ah siman 239 se'if 7. In truth one must settle this law, for we find many stringencies people adopt on themselves and we do not say they are called laymen; also in Beit Shmuel he wondered at this — see Yeshuot Yaakov chapter 8 of Chullin on eating meat after cheese, who wrote in the name of Maharam that he seems to disagree with the Talmud unless he does it for a fence and separation; see siman 63 se'if 2 and siman 232.
