It need not be scored, etc. Because they are covered and are not read. This is somewhat difficult: if so, why do we require scoring for a mezuzah in Yoreh Deah, siman 281, even though a mezuzah is a law given to Moshe at Sinai? In any event, the reason is difficult. However, in Sefer HaTerumos, siman 196, I found another reason: because tefillin contain only a few lines. Still, this reason too is difficult. My father, the rabbi, may his lamp shine, answered that mezuzah is different, because whenever he wants he can easily take it from its tube and read it; moreover, a mezuzah must be checked twice every seven years, end quote. Afterward I found in Kesef Mishneh, chapter 1 of Hilchos Tefillin, in accordance with his words.
And if he does not know, etc. So wrote the Levush. It implies that if he knows how to keep the line straight, he is not permitted to score, for it is an unskilled act. So too wrote the Bach, except that he wrote that the Rama’s view is not so, since the Shulchan Aruch writes, “one should not score except the upper line,” and the Rama added the word “need,” as follows: “one need not score, etc.” In my humble opinion, it is possible that he added it because of the inference that the upper line does require scoring; but in truth, each and every line should not be scored because it is an unskilled act. Examine this carefully. One may also say that he came to teach that even according to the Shulchan Aruch, one may score all four sides, but not between line and line. Baruch SheAmar wrote: It seems to me that it is good to score between the lines on the hair side, but above, below, and on the sides he should score on the flesh side.
And he should not score, etc. It is invalid even if the scoring is black, for it is extra writing, according to the Levush. Also in Yoreh Deah, siman 271, the Levush did not conclude that it is invalid. It seems to me that he holds there is a distinction between tefillin, which do not require scoring, and a Torah scroll, which does require scoring. However, Lechem Chamudos, folio 78, wondered about the Levush cited above, since his view is that it is valid; this requires study. If the scoring was done with ink, it appears from Ateres Tzvi that it is valid after the fact. I later found in Sefer Baruch SheAmar that he wrote as follows: It appears that one may score tefillin with tin or lead between line and line even according to Rabbeinu Simcha, who invalidates scoring in tefillin, since it is not called scoring unless one makes an indentation and groove, end quote. Thus he permits it even initially. If so, it is enough for us to prohibit it initially.
