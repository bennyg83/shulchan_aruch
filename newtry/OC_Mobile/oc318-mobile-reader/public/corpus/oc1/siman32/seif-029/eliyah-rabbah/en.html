[29] He erred and wrote, etc. And it seems the same law applies if initially the letter was written correctly and afterward was spoiled and became like the form of a dalet — likewise invalid, and it has no correction by dragging all of it because it is lo kelachar yad, unless nothing was written after it. The Taz wrote specifically through erasing the tag there is no correction; but if he wishes to add ink to it and make it a circle, it is fine; likewise if he made reish instead of dalet there is correction to add ink and make it square — and this is when nothing was written after — so far. And it seems to me the same applies to bet and kaf; and it seems to me this is the explanation of Rivash siman 7 — not like Maharam Alashkar. Olat Tamid wrote it implies that if the letter was recognizable in part even though changed — e.g., something fell on a samekh and thereby the surrounding hollow became small and thickened — it is kosher provided it is recognizable to a child; but in Keneset HaGedolah the late authority wrote and raised Radbaz that if a drop of ink fell on a letter and it looks as if from the body of the letter, even if the letter was recognizable to a child it is invalid — so far his wording. And thus wrote the Levush in Even HaEzer there; and in Chelkat Mechokek he wrote that ex post facto it is kosher — and this requires investigation. Also requires investigation in the body of Radbaz's responsum, for I do not have it at present; also what Magen Avraham wrote in the name of Radbaz's responsum — you may wonder; see there. He also wrote: if a drop fell inside the hollow of bet and it looks like peh, or there is no bet on it; or it fell into another and its name is not on it — there is no correction by erasing; and if wax dripped and one removed it, it is permitted. Keneset HaGedolah wrote in the name of Maharam Alashkar's responsum: a drop of ink fell and it is made like half a letter — we do not find anyone who permits completing the letter by writing — so far. Magen Avraham wrote that Beit Yosef and Rama in Even HaEzer there validate. Olat Tamid wrote that if most of the letter is in original writing one may validate when there are no other tefillin; however if a drop of ink fell and a complete letter was made without erasing one may question it, since the letter was not written in the manner of writing. See in Even HaEzer there.
