It is halachah le-Moshe mi-Sinai that tefillin [are written] on klaf and not on duchsustos and not on gevil. One writes on klaf on the flesh side, and if one changed this it is invalid. What is klaf and what is duchsustos? When the hide is processed they split it in two: the outer part toward the hair is called klaf and the inner part attached to the flesh is called duchsustos. Thus when we say "one writes on klaf on the flesh side" we mean the side closer to the flesh—that is, where it adjoins the duchsustos. Our parchments that are not split have the law of klaf and one writes on them on the flesh side, for what is scraped from the upper skin at the hair side is only as much as needed to prepare and smooth it; and even if one were to split the hide in two one would need to scrape thus. From the flesh side one scrapes much until only klaf remains.
