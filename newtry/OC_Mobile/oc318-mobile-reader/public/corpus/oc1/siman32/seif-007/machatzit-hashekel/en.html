(Note 7) Line, etc., is forbidden — for we hold that specifically a mezuzah (and likewise a sefer Torah according to Rashi, and so we hold in Yoreh De'ah) requires ruling lines; but tefillin do not require ruling lines — that is, specifically that one need not rule every line and line as in a mezuzah (and sefer Torah according to us) from a decree of Scripture. But in any case the upper line requires ruling, for it is forbidden to write a verse from the Torah without ruling lines in order to straighten the writing; and for this it suffices with an upper line — since an upper line is already written before him in straightness, he knows to straighten beneath it a second line even without ruling, for the upper line serves as ruling for the second line, and the second for the third, and so all of them.

(ב) And he raised a difficulty in the book Beit HaZevach, etc., end of chapter 2 of Yevamos, that Abaye said: a court that writes a bill of chalitzah for a yevamah who performed chalitzah before them should write in it "We showed him from 'When brothers dwell' until 'the name of his brother in Israel,' not 'the name of his father's brother'" (that is, they should write the entire verse that is "When brothers dwell together" to establish his brother's name in Israel, not "his father's brother," and they should write the beginning and end of the verse) — and "We showed him from 'until she be taken' (because the yavam says 'I did not wish to take her')" and "We showed him from 'So shall it be done' until 'the house of him whose shoe was removed'" (and not the entire verse that is "So shall it be done to the man who will not build his brother's house, and his name shall be called in Israel the house of him whose shoe was removed") — and the Talmud concludes on this: Mar Zutra rules lines, etc., for the entire parsha; and Beit HaZevach understood that he ruled all the lines and wrote for all the above verses as written in the Torah, because it is forbidden to write without ruling lines; and on this Beit HaZevach raised a difficulty. However, the wording of the difficulty from Magen Avraham in the name of Beit HaZevach is not precise, for it implies there is no difficulty in Beit HaZevach except on what he ruled the entire parsha, and it would suffice for him with an upper line alone; and on this one answers: the words of Magen Avraham. But in truth Beit HaZevach raises a greater difficulty than this, and therefore the words of Magen Avraham are short and garbled, and this is his language: "And furthermore the Rosh wrote in Megillah chapter 2 and Yevamos 32b: 'Where he wrote an upper line without ruling (such as if he wrote on the upper line secular words and a letter of peace — from here onward the upper line, etc., serves as ruling, for we say here that if he ruled the upper one he need not rule again; and it is not reasonable to distinguish, for here it is different since the first is ruled, written straight, etc., because ordinary people know how to train their hands [to write] the entire line straight without ruling' — end of the Rosh; and on this Beit HaZevach wrote, and this is his language: 'It requires study: if so, why did Mar Zutra need to rule in a chalitzah document? For behold, before they reached "We showed him," etc., they wrote several lines before the mentioned verses of the halitzah performer' — end; behold, he raised a difficulty that he should not have had to rule at all, even an upper line, since in the chalitzah document several lines were written before they read the above verses; and according to this it must be said that what Magen Avraham wrote in the second answer — that in truth Mar Zutra did not rule only an upper line — this does not suffice to resolve the difficulty of Beit HaZevach, who raised a difficulty that he should not have had to rule even an upper line, as above; and it must be said that the intent of Magen Avraham is that the reason is explained in the Rosh: that even if the upper line is not ruled, nevertheless one need not rule a second line, for ordinary people know how to train their hands to write all lines straight without ruling; and Mar Zutra did not know how to write the upper line straight without ruling — but if the first line were ruled, then he knew how to aim to write the lines beneath it straight, and therefore he ruled only the first line. And afterward he wrote: in truth in Beit Shmuel — but for the first answer, even if the first line were ruled, Mar Zutra would not know how to aim to write straight beneath it, and therefore he had to rule the entire parsha that they wrote, that ordinary people, etc., in the Rosh I did not find this language except regarding an upper line — that ordinary people know how to train their hands, etc., as I brought above; and perhaps Magen Avraham derives this from logic — that this too is the reason for the lines beneath the upper one, to exclude the one who says he did not write the whole thing — that is, Abaye, as above; and also afterward there Mar bar Idi challenges him: "But it is not possible to write" (and Rashi explained: one does not write one parsha alone); and to this Mar Zutra said to write the entire parsha, to exclude Mar bar Idi.

And furthermore he raised a difficulty, etc. — for the Rosh raised a difficulty, etc., and Beit HaZevach understood that the Magen Tzedek did not write for Abaye all the verses because he did not want to rule from concern for trouble; and on this the Rosh raised a difficulty: nevertheless "We showed him 'I did not wish to take her'" — it is possible to write the verse as written even without ruling, for the verse is only three words, and three words one may write without ruling; and on this Beit HaZevach raised a difficulty. Behold, a baraita teaches: three [words] one writes without ruling, four require ruling; but R. Yitzchak disagrees and holds that specifically two one may write without ruling and not three; and the Rosh ruled like R. Yitzchak — if so, Abaye acted properly.

And it appears to us, etc., that he holds like Mar bar Idi, as should read — and that is, because of slight trouble to rule a little, Abaye did not act thus; however he holds like Mar bar Idi that even by ruling it is forbidden, for one may not write, as above. "Three words one writes with ruling" — for according to R. Yitzchak, two one writes, three one does not write; and the similarity of "two one writes" — that is, without ruling — likewise what it said "three one does not write" means without ruling, but by ruling it is certainly permitted. And likewise the baraita "four words" — and specifically one parsha it is forbidden to write alone. But which words are permitted? If so, why did Abaye not write at least "I did not wish to take her" — all three words — and that is by ruling? But on the two verses "We showed him," which are long verses, the Rosh did not find proof that it is permitted for Mar bar Idi; therefore he did not raise a difficulty on them, only on "I did not wish to take her."
