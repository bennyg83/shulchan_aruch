And not on gevil. Gevil is the hide that was not split, and from which only the hair was removed and it was prepared there, while from the flesh side nothing was removed.
And if he changed, it is invalid. This refers to all of them. Even if he wrote the tip of a yud not on the parchment, in a place of flesh, such as where in one place he did not remove the duchsustus, it is invalid. See below.
And one writes on them on the flesh side. He should be very careful not to err and write on the hair side, for this prevents validity after the fact. The sign for recognizing after processing which side is near the flesh is that one sees toward which side the parchment contracts when it is moistened; that is the place near the flesh. Nevertheless, old tefillin should not be invalidated because of this sign, for although after testing it appears that they were written toward the hair side, we maintain the scribe who wrote them in his presumption, the presumption that he was knowledgeable and expert, and that he certainly wrote them according to the law. We also rely on the Mordechai, who validates them if they were written on the hair side. Eliyah Rabbah.
To prepare and smooth it. If one scraped more than this, see Nishmas Adam, klal 13, whose opinion is that it is no longer called parchment. In Chasam Sofer, Orach Chaim, responsum 3, it appears that he is lenient in this.
And on the flesh side they scrape extensively. The sign by which to know that all the duchsustus has been scraped away is a tradition in the mouths of scribes: wherever it is found in the parchment, it is recognizable to everyone, for it is smooth, while the rest of the parchment is not so smooth. That smooth place can be peeled and separated with a knife or needle. After the fact, if he did not scrape so much, see Machatzis HaShekel, whose opinion is to be stringent. In Responsa Mishkenos Yaakov and in Nishmas Adam he wrote that one should not invalidate the tefillin for this reason after the fact, since at least the thin layer of the hide close to the flesh, upon which the name duchsustus certainly rests, was scraped away from the hide. So too in the above-mentioned responsum of Chasam Sofer and in Responsa Chesed LeAvraham he is lenient for this reason. Although one need not protest against one who is lenient after the fact, since he has authorities upon whom to rely, it is highly proper to warn the scribes to be careful about this at least ab initio, not to leave on the parchment before they write on it any membrane or thin layer at the place of writing, even a thin layer like a hairbreadth, for all of this involves the concern of duchsustus.
