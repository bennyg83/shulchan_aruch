In Derishah I found that he wrote about this as follows: See Yoreh Deah, siman 276, where I wrote in the name of the Rivash that one must be concerned because of speckled writing, end quote. So too wrote Lechem Chamudos as follows: But in Beis Yosef to Yoreh Deah, siman 276, on the words of Nimukei Yosef, he wrote that the Rivash wrote in a responsum that if one transgressed, scraped them, and wrote them in ink, it is valid. However, with all the Divine Names in the scroll, even after the fact it is invalid because it appears speckled, end quote. According to this reasoning, it seems to me that if he did so with the other letters of the Torah scroll, if it is in many places and appears speckled, it is invalid. This is the language of Lechem Chamudos. In my humble opinion, it seems that this Rivash does not refer at all to the words of Nimukei Yosef in a case where one sprinkled gold over ink; for if so, why did he write that if one transgressed, scraped them, and wrote them in ink, etc.? Scraping off the gold alone should suffice, since underneath it was already written in ink, as is implied by Beis Yosef and Levush here, who wrote that what is beneath remains intact. Nor does there appear to be any prohibition here because of speckling, since the writing beneath remains intact. Rather, he certainly refers to the basic law of one who wrote with gold or with the colors themselves: if one transgressed, scraped them, and wrote them in ink, it is valid, and in that case the law of speckling applies. Now it is understandable that Beis Yosef did not write the words of the Rivash in this siman, because here, with tefillin, if he wrote with gold or the color itself, scraped it, and then rewrote it in ink, it is always invalid because it is out of order. This is unlike Yoreh Deah, which deals with a Torah scroll, where writing out of order is valid. If so, there is no disagreement at all in the law here. Examine this carefully.
