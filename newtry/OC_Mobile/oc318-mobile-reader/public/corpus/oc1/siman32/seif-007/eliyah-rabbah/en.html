[7] He scrapes, etc. So wrote Shulchan Aruch and Beit Yosef. In Derisha I found he wrote on this — this is his wording — "and see Yoreh Deah siman 276; I wrote in the name of Rivash that one should be concerned for mottled writing," until here his wording; and similarly Lechem Chamudot wrote, and this is his wording: "But in Beit Yosef on Yoreh Deah siman 276 he wrote on the words of Nemukei Yosef that Rivash wrote in a responsum that if he passed and scraped them and wrote them in ink it is kosher; however for all the Divine Names the scroll is invalid even after the fact because it looks mottled," until here his wording. According to this reasoning it seems to me if he did so on the other letters of the Torah, if in many places it looks mottled it is invalid — until here Lechem Chamudot's wording. In my humble opinion it seems this Rivash does not refer at all to Nemukei Yosef's words when one threw gold on ink; for if so, why did he write that if he passed and scraped and wrote in ink, etc. — is not scraping the gold alone sufficient, since below it was already written in ink, as implied in Beit Yosef and Levush here that below remains intact; also there does not seem to be any prohibition for mottled writing at all since the writing below remains intact. Rather certainly it refers to the main ruling that he wrote in gold or in colors themselves — that if he passed and scraped and wrote in ink it is kosher, and on this the law of mottled writing applies. And now it is settled that Beit Yosef did not write Rivash's words in this siman because here in tefillin it is always invalid when he wrote in gold and color themselves, scraped, and rewrote in ink because it was not in order — which is not the case in Yoreh Deah, which deals with a Torah scroll that is kosher when not in order; and if so there is no dispute at all in the law here. Examine this.
