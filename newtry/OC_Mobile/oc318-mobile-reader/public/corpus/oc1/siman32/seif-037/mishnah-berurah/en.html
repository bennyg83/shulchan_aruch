The leather of the housings. The same applies to the leather of the titura and the maavarta, since they are attached and sewn to the housings.
Kosher species. For it is written, “so that the Torah of Hashem shall be in your mouth,” meaning from what is permitted to your mouth. Since the letter shin, which alludes to the Name Shaddai, is formed by creasing the leather of the tefillin, it is included in the Torah of Hashem. There is no distinction between the head tefillin and the arm tefillin.
From parchment. Even though it is thin, it is still properly called leather.
A fetus. For its hide too is called leather. Even though regarding impurity it is called flesh, nevertheless it is no worse than bird skin.
To make them from parchment. Only he must blacken them, for black straps are a halachah given to Moshe at Sinai.
Processed for their sake. If the processing was only for the straps, it does not help for the housings, whose sanctity is more severe.
Where possible. For the Rambam maintains that the leather of the housings does not require processing at all. On the contrary, it is stronger when it has not been processed; therefore even leather that was not processed at all is valid, and all the more so he does not require processing for its sake. Most authorities disagree with him and require processing, and also that it be for its sake. The Shulchan Aruch ruled that where possible it requires processing for its sake, but where it is not possible to find covering for the housings from leather processed for its sake, one may rely on the Rambam rather than be idle from the mitzvah of tefillin. When he later finds leather processed for its sake, he should put these housings away in genizah and make housings from leather processed for its sake. The later authorities disagree whether, for the time being, he should wear them with a blessing or without a blessing. The Birkei Yosef agreed with the Magen Avraham that he may recite the blessing.
