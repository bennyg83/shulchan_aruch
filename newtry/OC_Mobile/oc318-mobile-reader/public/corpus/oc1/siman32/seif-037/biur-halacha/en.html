The leather of the compartments. See the Mishnah Berurah, where he wrote: and the same applies to the leather of the titura, etc. Therefore scribes who place between the compartment and the titura old pieces of leather that they have lifted from refuse piles or from torn shoes should know that they are committing a transgression. Since this leather is attached to the compartment, it too must specifically be from valid leather exactly like it. So wrote Sefer Lishkas HaSofer, and initially the law is certainly with him. But after the fact this requires study, according to what the Pri Megadim wrote below in se'if 47, that the Rama's requirement that the glue be valid is for the mitzvah but not indispensable, since the gluing itself is not indispensable, and therefore there is no concern with what he glues with. If so, seemingly the same applies in our case; it is not comparable to the leather of the titura and ma'avarta, whose obligation is by law and therefore they must be from a kosher species. This requires study.
From the hide of an animal, etc. See the Darchei Moshe, who wrote that one does not make them from fish skin because the question in Shabbos 108 was not resolved. At first glance this requires study, for the Rambam wrote that the reason one may not write on fish skin is that its foulness does not cease through processing. This implies that the foulness of other skins does cease through processing. If so, according to the Rambam, who rules that the leather of the compartments is permitted even when not processed, it should be permitted even from fish skin. This may be rejected.
Kosher species. See the Mishnah Berurah. Therefore, if any doubt arises regarding the leather of the compartments, whether it is from a kosher species, one should be stringent, for it is a biblical doubt. Even regarding the straps one should be stringent, as the Pri Megadim wrote in s.k. 27, and it appears that he retracted what he wrote in his introduction to Hilchos Tefillin on this matter; see there. Also what he wrote there in the introduction, that the leather of the hand tefillin compartment must be from a kosher species only rabbinically, is not correct in my humble opinion. According to that, the Gemara should have answered that it refers to hand tefillin, which do not have a shin. What the Pri Megadim wrote about this is very forced. Rather, it seems obvious that the Gemara holds that since it is a halachah leMoshe miSinai even regarding the stitching, that the compartments must specifically be sewn with sinews from kosher animals, all the more so the compartments themselves must be from the leather of a kosher animal. For this we do not need Rav Yosef's statement. Rashi wrote similarly in Menachos 42b, under "invalid"; see there. This is not included in the rule that we do not derive an a fortiori inference from a halachah leMoshe miSinai, for it is merely revealing the matter. After the Gemara challenges, "but did Abaye not say: the shin of tefillin, etc.," it could have answered that the case concerns hand tefillin, and then challenged from the baraisa as it does now; it simply did not wish to lengthen the discussion, as similar points are written in the rules of the Talmud. See Gilyon HaShas of Rabbi Akiva Eiger. I have written what appears to me.
Processed for its sake. This processing is, according to all opinions, not a halachah leMoshe miSinai. The reason processing is required is because it must be for its sake, for every sacred item requires being made for its sake; Pri Megadim in Eshel Avraham, s.k. 12.
Where possible. See Magen Avraham, who wrote that if he has no other parchment, he may recite a blessing over this, for the Rambam is worthy of being relied upon for a blessing, and Rashi also holds so; see there. At first glance, Magen Avraham is lenient about the blessing only where the leather was processed but not for its sake, but not where it was not processed at all, for this we do not find in Rashi. In truth, from what the Gra revealed to us, that the Rambam's source is from Eruvin 96, where the leather of the compartments is like the leather of an amulet, which is entirely unprocessed leather, and we see from this that our sugya follows the first Tanna of Rabban Shimon ben Gamliel and not Rabban Shimon ben Gamliel, see there, it follows that according to Rashi, who rules like the first Tanna of Rabban Shimon ben Gamliel, even entirely unprocessed leather is permitted. Otherwise, to whom would that sugya in Eruvin apply? Nevertheless, in my humble opinion, the main law requires study: whether one may rely on the Magen Avraham who is lenient regarding a blessing, although the Birkei Yosef agreed with him and not like the Bach and Eliyah Rabbah. The Gra wrote in his commentary at the end of siman 33, se'if 3, that the Rambam's leniency regarding lishmah seemingly requires study, for the Rambam himself wrote that if a non-Jew covered the compartments or sewed them, it is invalid, because their making is like their writing on account of the shin. That is to say, if so, here too the leather should require processing for its sake on account of the shin. He answers that the Rambam follows his own position, that he does not require processing for every sacred writing, for regarding mezuzah he does not require processing, only regarding a Sefer Torah and tefillin. The Gra's intent is that he does not require processing for its sake for mezuzah, although he does require processing, as the Rambam explicitly wrote in Hilchos Tefillin, chapter 1. If so, according to what we rule below in siman 39, se'if 2, that making the compartments is like writing because of the shin, as stated in the Tur, and we also rule in Yoreh Deah 288 regarding mezuzah not like the Rambam, for although the Shulchan Aruch there wrote only that it requires processing for its sake initially, all the later authorities disagreed, see the Taz there; and even according to the Shach there, at least it would be without a blessing. The Gra also wrote there that all the poskim disagree with the Rambam. If so, why should we be lenient here to allow a blessing over leather not processed for its sake? This requires study in practice. If some doubt arises regarding the processing of the leather of the compartments, one may be lenient, for it is like a double doubt. So it implies from the Pri Megadim in Mishbetzos Zahav, s.k. 31.
