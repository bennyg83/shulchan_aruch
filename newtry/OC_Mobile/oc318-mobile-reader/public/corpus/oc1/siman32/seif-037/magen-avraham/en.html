And one may make it from parchment. In Sefer Baruch She’amar he wrote that one should not make the housings from parchment unless he places the parchment in water of tree bark, called loah in the vernacular, with which hides are processed. In the gloss there he validates parchment. It is good to be stringent initially. Menahem Azaryah, siman 37, wrote: the hide of a fetus is the choicest, then bird hide, then hide of a wild animal, then hide of a domesticated animal, and then hide of a carcass.
Where possible. If he has no other parchment, he should put them on without a blessing, as stated by the Bach. It does not appear so to me; the Rambam is worthy to rely upon even to bless, for Rashi also holds like him. Rashi holds that Rava, who said that designation is not legally significant, follows the first Tanna, who does not require processing for its sake. Tosafos objected that we say in Gittin that a Torah scroll requires processing for its sake, and they wrote that it is forced to distinguish between the leather of the housings and the parchment on which one writes. But Rashi did not regard this as forced, and neither did the Rambam; therefore he wrote that it does not require processing for its sake. So too wrote in Nachalas Tzvi. See what I wrote in siman 42.
