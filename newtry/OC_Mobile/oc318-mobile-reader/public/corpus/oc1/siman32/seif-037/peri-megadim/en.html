And this. See Taz. See Menachos 34b. One can say that their dispute is about placing the four parshiyos in one compartment: according to Rabbi Yehudah, after the fact one must paste them together, while according to Rabbi Yosei it is not necessary, even initially. But where one has four compartments of head tefillin and no hand tefillin, Rabbi Yosei said only after the fact that he fulfills the obligation. See the Levush, who wrote that if he wrote the four parshiyos in one compartment, he has fulfilled the obligation, and the choicest mitzvah is to paste them. If he has no hand tefillin but has two head tefillin, he covers, etc. He holds that Rabbi Yosei’s statement “it is not necessary” implies only after the fact, but initially even in one compartment they must be pasted. This is the approach of the Bach and Taz in seif 36, that Rav Dimi’s statement that it need not reach the place of the seam refers only after the fact, and we rule like Rav Dimi, not like Abaye; see there. But according to the Beit Yosef in his long work, who explains that we rule like Abaye and that according to Rav Dimi it is not necessary initially, the same applies to Rabbi Yosei that it is not necessary initially, whereas four compartments covered over applies only after the fact. Now there is no difficulty: he teaches the greater novelty, that this is initial practice and this is after the fact. The Rav’s gloss, “and the custom,” means that even in one compartment he fulfills the obligation only after the fact, but initially Rabbi Yosei agrees that they must be pasted; understand. It seems logical that when covering with leather, this is also so that the shin should not be visible, for hand tefillin have no shin, and an additional feature would be an issue of adding, so it seems to me.
