According to the Rambam, etc., and he assisted. To explain the law of assistance one must elaborate somewhat. The Taz brought here and in Yoreh Deah, siman 271, that assistance is effective even at the end, and he brought proof from returning the parchment to the lime; see there. The Perach Mateh Aharon, siman 45, challenged him: first, how can we say here that the end proves the beginning, since the beginning was done by a non-Jew, and how can we say that because the Jew came at the end and assisted for the sake of the mitzvah, this proves that the beginning was for the sake of the mitzvah. Furthermore, even regarding a Jew himself he brought proof that, as a matter of law, there is doubt whether we say that the end proves the beginning; see there carefully. In truth, according to his understanding of the Taz, there is an even greater difficulty with the Taz, for how did the Taz bring proof from returning the lime into the parchment? Perforce, that case is inferior, for with returning the lime, even if there was no instruction to the non-Jew, it is effective, as appears from the words of the Tur in Yoreh Deah there; whereas here instruction to the non-Jew is certainly required, since assistance alone has no substance. Rather, it is clear that when the Taz wrote that he assists him, he did not mean that he performs some act together with the non-Jew, for such assistance certainly has no substance. Rather, he means that he too performs some act of processing without the non-Jew. The reasoning of the Taz is that this certainly counts as a significant designation for sanctity, since without the Jew finishing it the hide would not yet have been processed. The Tosafos were stringent only because preparing the parchment and scoring it do not count as designation for sanctity, since the work of processing had already been completed. But here, where he performs a complete act in the processing, without which the hide would not have been completed in its processing, it is effective. For this he brought proof from returning the thin layer, which is parchment, as stated in the Tur. That is to say, regarding Shabbos there is certainly no processing after processing, as is explained in the laws of Shabbos concerning other labors; yet here it is effective. Perforce, here we require only that he perform an act demonstrating that it is a significant designation for sanctity. If so, all the more so when he performs the completion of the processing itself; this is certainly called processing even regarding Shabbos, as is proven in Shabbos, folio 75, where it asks: this is salting, this is processing. Thus, even though he did only salting, while it still has the status of hide and not parchment, as stated in Shabbos, folio 79, he is liable for processing. If so, all the more so where he did the tanning alone, through which the processing is completed so that it is called parchment and not diftera, that he is liable. All the more so it is called processing regarding doing it for its sake. If so, we do not need to say that the end proves the beginning, for the end alone is sufficient, and consequently we also do not need to say that he must instruct the non-Jew at the beginning to do it for its sake. With this all the difficulties in the words of the Taz are removed. What emerges from our words is that there are two kinds of assistance. If he performed an action in partnership with the non-Jew, even at the beginning, this certainly is not effective except when he told the non-Jew to put it into the lime for the sake of the mitzvah, and even that is effective only according to the Rosh. This is the case discussed by the Shulchan Aruch, which wrote that he assisted him somewhat in its processing. But certainly, if he performed an action by himself in the work of processing, even though the non-Jew performed the beginning of the processing, since without the Jew it would not have been completed, the Taz holds that it is effective even if he did not tell the non-Jew at the beginning to do it for its sake. Even the Rambam and Tosafos, who hold that instructing the non-Jew does not help at all, would agree according to the Taz that this is effective. Afterward, with Hashem’s help, I found in the explanation of the Gaon Rabbi Mordechai Banet on the Mordechai, at the end of Hilchos Sefer Torah, s.v. or he assists, that his words there explicitly indicate that he too holds as we have written, that even if he did not tell the non-Jew anything about doing it for its sake, it is effective, since the Jew performed an action himself in the work of processing for the sake of the mitzvah.
