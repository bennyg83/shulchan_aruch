And so is the practice, etc. The Bach and later authorities validate this after the fact even without assistance. The Taz wrote that even if a Jew assists at the end of the processing, it helps, but it does not help that a Jew assists in blackening the hides, for that is not one of the labors of processing, and whoever is lenient in this causes many to sin, end quote. So too wrote Magen Avraham. But I found in Responsa Be’er Esek, siman 10, as practical halachah, that it is permitted, for this is the practice in Salonika, and so ruled the rabbi Manoach Corcos from Rome; this requires study. In the Rosh at the end of Hilchos Tzitzis, correction, scraping, and smoothing done for the sake of the mitzvah do not help. See the Tur in Yoreh Deah there, that if they are able to return the parchment to a stretched state and pass lime over it for its sake, it helps. In Responsa Be’er Esek there it sounds as though he disagrees with the Rosh, but in my humble opinion they do not disagree. Examine this carefully. Hagahos Maimoniyos wrote that if a Jew initially gives it into the processing for the sake of sanctity, he need not assist. Be’er Esek wrote further that he should leave the hide in lime until the hair falls off by itself, and if he removed it before then, he should not write on it. In Shulchan Aruch Mahari Luria wrote: One must process two hides, one for the strap of the head tefillin and one for the hand tefillin; therefore, when placing the hide in lime and water for the head straps, he should have intent with the Name Yud-Hei-Vav-Hei, and for the hand strap with the Name Alef-Dalet-Nun-Yud, and this is considered for their sake, end quote. Although I have not seen people practice so, I wrote it for those who are meticulous.
