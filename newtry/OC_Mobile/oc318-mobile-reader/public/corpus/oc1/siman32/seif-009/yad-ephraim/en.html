(Note 26) And Rabbi Yosef Halevi wrote, etc., that one should not erase the leg — meaning that there he wrote that to erase the protrusion and leave the remainder is not called a repair; therefore either he erases everything and writes anew to fulfill Radbaz and his supporters, or he erases only the roof as other decisors — but this applies specifically to profane writing, whereas in the dalet of Elokekha one may not erase everything, for it is a stringency that leads to a leniency, lest erasing only the roof suffice. And at first glance it requires further study, for here it is comparable to a resh like a dalet, since the way is to write the roof first; and if so, automatically the leg was made invalid and one must erase it. And one can say: for example, his intent at the time of writing was to extend the leg of the kaf so it would be double like the leg of the roof — if so, at the time he extended the leg it was kosher, only now that he left it thus and cannot extend it further because he wrote other lines beneath it, it is invalid; and nevertheless, at the time of the act it was written kosher and need not be erased. And according to this, it is possible that if it was at the end of the page such that if he extended further it would not be mukaf gvil, or there was no room at all to extend further, then he would erase the leg as well — it requires further study. Again, the responsum of Mahari Halevi came to hand, and I saw there siman 81 that he wrote the reason one must scrape the entire roof — even according to Mahari ben Habib, who holds that when one performs writing in the body of the letter it is not called hollowing the interior. Unlike scraping part of the roof, which would be repair without writing — that is called hollowing the interior. And if the dalet is not part of the name but in another word, when he wishes to erase the whole dalet and return and write it as its repair, it is repair according to all. Only in the name of the Omnipresent, such as Elokekha, one must erase the entire roof and take care not to erase the leg — and this is to fulfill the concern of Maharam Isserles, who wrote that what he scrapes of the leg of the heh written without precision is preferable, since in the body of the letter — namely the heh that is the dalet — written properly, he does not touch it; likewise here, since in the leg of the kaf written properly, presumably the leg of the kaf he extended downward until the second line, where it has no more room to extend further, he does not touch it (it seems he cited both versions of the language and means it was not written invalidly), only the roof was made invalid — that is preferable, end quote. And I rejoiced, for the matters are somewhat close to what I wrote and I explained somewhat more. And although one may distinguish between that case of Mahari and our case, nevertheless since it is comparable, out of stringency one should scrape the letters of the Divine Name. And what is written in Magen Avraham, "and Rabbi Yosef Halevi wrote that even for Rosh," etc., "and write the name in its place" — see what is written in Tosafot's glosses on Shabbat and in other glosses on what is stated here, and it is not found; and thus Mahari of Beit Halevi: "However, in the simple kaf of Elokekha it would seem this repair does not apply," etc. And therefore it would seem one must remove the sheet, etc. Or if the book is made on gvil, as most of our books are, to peel the word Elokekha with a sharp knife and bury it and return and write the word Elokekha on the parchment with the kaf as its repair; or if he wishes to scrape also the word before it and hang it, and in its place write the word Elokekha — and as Rambam, of blessed memory, wrote in chapter 1 of Hilchot Sefer Torah, one may write the name on a place one scraped and on the erasure. Also Tur siman 276 wrote: one does not hang the name unless he erred and skipped — he scrapes one word before it and writes the name on the scrape and hangs the word he erased, end quote. And although Rosh wrote it is better to remove the sheet from the binding, etc. — there it refers to cutting the parchment entirely, but to peel the name, etc., is fine. And it would seem that also in the kaf of Elokekha our stated repair of erasing the entire roof, etc., helps — see there. And his intent seems to be that certainly one must peel the entire word Elokekha from the name; only that if it is on gvil he can peel and still some thickness remains on the parchment to write on; and if it is impossible to write on the place where the name was peeled from, where only very thin parchment remains, he can scrape the word before it and write the name in that scraped place, and the word he scraped he hangs between the lines before the word Elokekha — this is clear in his intent. And what Magen Avraham wrote, "but Tosafot in Eruvin," etc. — it seems he wrote thus because he did not have before him the body of Mahari of Beit Halevi's responsum, only what he brought in his name in Keneset HaGedolah. And in some places it seems Keneset HaGedolah too was not in Magen Avraham's hands, only what he copied in Beit Yosef from Keneset HaGedolah's words in the name of a responsum; and therefore he explained Mahari's words as referring to a case where one writes some word in places one peeled, and it does not refer above to the matter of Elokekha, where plainly it does not refer thus — why would he write on this place and leave part? Rather it refers to skipping a word and writing the name in its place — then he peels the name and writes in its place the word; and therefore Magen Avraham brought on this Tosafot's words. And if he had had the body of the responsum before him, he would not have written that Mahari Halevi addresses a different matter and is not relevant to Tosafot in Eruvin.
