Seif katan 26. Rabbi Yosef HaLevi writes, etc., that he should not erase the leg. His meaning is that merely erasing the excess and leaving the remainder is not considered a valid repair. Therefore, either he must erase the entire letter and rewrite it, thereby satisfying Radach and those who follow him, or he should erase only the roof, in accordance with the other poskim. This applies specifically to an ordinary word. In the final kaf of the Divine Name “Elokecha,” however, he may not erase the whole letter, because a stringency that leads to leniency would result, since perhaps erasing only the roof is sufficient.
At first glance this requires study, because the case resembles a resh written like a dalet. Since the usual manner is to write the roof first, the leg was necessarily written in an invalid state and should also require erasure. Perhaps the case is one where, at the time of writing, he could still have extended the leg of the kaf until it was twice the length of the roof. When he drew the leg, it was therefore written validly. It became invalid only afterward, when he left it as it was and could no longer extend it because other lines had already been written beneath it. Since the actual act of writing was valid, the leg need not be erased. According to this, if it was at the bottom of the page, so that extending it would have deprived it of surrounding parchment, or if there was never room to extend it further, he may have to erase the leg as well. This requires study.
I later obtained Responsa Mahari HaLevi and saw in siman 81 that he explains why the entire roof must be scraped away even according to Mahari ben Chaviv, who holds that when one performs an act of writing upon the body of the letter it is not considered carving out the interior. Scraping away only part of the roof would repair the letter without any act of writing and is therefore carving out the interior. If the dalet is not part of a Divine Name but appears in another word, erasing the entire dalet and rewriting it properly constitutes a valid repair according to all opinions. Only in the final kaf of a Divine Name, such as “Elokecha,” must the entire roof be erased while taking care not to erase the leg. This also satisfies the concern of Maharam Isserlein, who writes that scraping the leg of a hei that was written imprecisely is preferable because one does not touch the body of the letter, namely the dalet portion of the hei, which was written correctly. Here too, since the leg of the kaf was written correctly, for presumably he extended it down to the second line and had no additional space in which to lengthen it, one does not touch that leg. It appears that his wording is merely idiomatic, and his intent is that the leg was not written invalidly. Only the roof was made improperly, so erasing the roof is preferable. I was pleased to find that his reasoning is close to what I wrote, though I have explained it somewhat further.
Although one can distinguish between Maharam Isserlein’s case and ours, Mahari HaLevi compares them because of the severity of erasing letters of the Divine Name.
Regarding the statement in Hagahot Ashri, that Rabbi Yosef HaLevi writes that even according to Rosh, etc., one should write the Divine Name in its place, see the comments printed in Hagahot Tosafos Shabbos and other glosses. Their interpretation is incorrect. Mahari of the house of Levi writes as follows: “Regarding the final kaf of ‘Elokecha,’ it would initially appear that this repair is inapplicable. It would therefore seem necessary to remove the sheet. Alternatively, if the scroll is written on gevil, as most of our scrolls are, one should peel away the word ‘Elokecha’ with a sharp knife, place it in genizah, and rewrite the word ‘Elokecha’ upon the remaining parchment with a properly formed kaf. Or he may also scrape away the preceding word, suspend that word between the lines, and write ‘Elokecha’ in its place. Rambam writes in the first chapter of Hilchos Sefer Torah that it is permitted to write a Divine Name upon an erased or scraped place. Tur, siman 276, likewise writes that a Divine Name may not be suspended. If it was mistakenly omitted, one should scrape away the preceding word, write the Divine Name upon the scraped place, and suspend the erased word.”
Although Rosh writes that it is preferable to remove the sheet rather than cut out the Name, there he discusses cutting completely through the parchment. Peeling away the Name is permitted. Mahari HaLevi then concludes that even the kaf of “Elokecha” can apparently be repaired by erasing the entire roof, as described there.
His intent is clear. Ordinarily one must peel away the entire word “Elokecha.” If it is written on gevil, one can peel it and still leave enough thickness to write upon. If the place from which the Name was peeled is too thin to write upon, he may scrape away the preceding word, write the Divine Name in that scraped place, and suspend the erased word between the lines before “Elokecha.”
Magen Avraham’s reference to Tosafos in Eruvin appears to have arisen because he did not possess the full text of Responsa Mahari of the house of Levi, but only the excerpt cited in Knesses HaGedolah. In several places it appears that even Knesses HaGedolah itself was not before Magen Avraham, and that he had only the citation copied by Olas Tamid in the name of that responsum. He therefore understood Mahari HaLevi to be discussing a case where another word would be written in the place that had been peeled, not the preceding discussion of “Elokecha,” where one would simply leave the place blank. Rather, he thought the case involved an omitted word where the Divine Name had been written in its place, so that one would peel away the Name and write the omitted word there. For that reason Magen Avraham cited Tosafos. Had he seen the full responsum, he would not have written this, because Mahari HaLevi addresses a different matter and his ruling is unrelated to Tosafos in Eruvin. I wrote all this after seeing the responsum itself. Before seeing it, I had written differently in the margin, but this now appears correct.
