Even. For the Rambam holds that a non-Jew acts for his own purpose, and although the non-Jew says that he listens to the Jew, his mouth and heart are not aligned in this matter. But the Rosh holds that since the Jew stands over him at the beginning, when he places the hides into the lime, and tells him to put them then into the lime for their sake, and we do not require the non-Jew's intent for this except at the moment of placing them into the lime, he obeys him for such a brief time and places them with that intent. But if the Jew standing over him merely thought in his heart that it should be for their sake, it does not help. Even if he tells him explicitly, as long as he stands at a distance and does not instruct him, it does not help even according to the Rosh.
A little. Even if he assisted only at the end of the processing, and even if that assistance was only in partnership with the non-Jew, it is valid. All this is only for the mitzvah in the best manner; but after the fact, according to the Rosh it is valid even if he did not assist at all, for a non-Jew acts according to the intent of the Jew who commands him to do it for its sake.
And so is the practice. According to the later authorities, the practice initially should be that the Jew himself places the hides into the lime for their sake, and tells the non-Jew that all the remaining work of processing that he does should also be done for their sake. The non-Jew may afterward remove it by himself and prepare it, and the Jew need not stand over him further or assist him further. If the Jew himself placed the hides into the lime for their sake and said nothing to the non-Jew, Pri Megadim wrote that this requires study, for according to the Beis Yosef in Yoreh Deah, siman 271, it is valid even according to the Rambam, while the Bach forbids it. However, according to the words of the Noda BiYehuda in siman 175, see there, and the commentary of the Gaon Rabbi Mordechai Banet on the Mordechai at the end of Hilchos Sefer Torah, one may be lenient after the fact. If the Jew assisted the non-Jew somewhat at the end, and with this the work of processing was completed, but he did not tell the non-Jew at all to do it for its sake, this certainly does not help, for assistance has no substance. This applies where he did not himself complete the processing, but only assisted together with the non-Jew. But if the Jew completed it himself without the non-Jew's assistance, such as where the Jew removed the hides from the lime before their processing was complete and then placed them back into the lime for their sake, the Taz in Yoreh Deah and the above-mentioned Gaon Rabbi Mordechai are lenient in this. One should not protest against one who follows their lenient view, as we clarified in Biur Halacha.
