(28) Even — for Rambam holds a non-Jew acts on his own accord, and even though the non-Jew says he listens to the Jew, his mouth and heart are not aligned in this. But Rosh holds that since the Jew stands over him at the beginning when placing hides in lime and tells him to place them in lime for its sake, we do not require the non-Jew's intent for this, only at the moment of placing in lime he obeyed him briefly and placed them with that intent. But if the Jew standing over him thought in his heart alone for its sake, it does not help; and even if he tells him explicitly — wherever he stands from afar and does not instruct him, it does not help, even for Rosh:

(29) A bit — even if he only assisted at the end of processing, and even if that assistance was only in partnership with the non-Jew — it is valid, and all this is for the mitzvah in general; but post factum for Rosh it is valid even if he did not assist at all, for a non-Jew on the intent of the Jew commanded to do for its sake acts:

(30) And so they practice — and the custom per the latter authorities: lechatchilah it must be so that the Jew himself places the hides in lime for its sake and tells the non-Jew that all the rest of the processing work he will do, he will also do for its sake; the non-Jew may afterward remove and finish it himself without needing to stand over and assist further. If the Jew placed the hides himself in lime for its sake and said nothing to the non-Jew, Peri Megadim wrote there is doubt [for Beit Yosef Yoreh De'ah siman 271 holds it valid even for Rambam, and Bach forbids]; indeed Noda BiYehudah siman 175 see there, and the comments of the Gaon Maharam on Mordechai at the end of Hilchot Sefer Torah one may be lenient post factum. If the Jew assisted the non-Jew a little at the end and thereby finished the processing work and told the non-Jew nothing to do for its sake — this certainly does not help, for assistance has no substance. This refers when finishing processing he did not do alone but only with assistance of the non-Jew; but if the Jews finished alone without the non-Jew's assistance, e.g. the Jew removed hides from lime before processing finished and returned and placed them in lime for its sake — Taz in Yoreh De'ah and the Gaon Maharam mentioned are lenient, and one should not protest those who are lenient per their words, as we clarified in Biur Halachah:
