Even. See Taz. Shabbos 108a, Tosafot s.v. “which,” says that nevelos are also permitted, for the Torah was particular only that it be from what is permitted in your mouth. One who bows to an animal, see siman 11:8, Magen Avraham 12, and siman 586, letter 6: initially it is forbidden for a mitzvah, but after the fact it is acceptable, and it is not forbidden for benefit, for living creatures do not become forbidden to ordinary use. Now, from tefillin the entire law and every mitzvah are derived that they must be from what is permitted in your mouth; see siman 586, Magen Avraham 3. This is difficult to me: if so, a garment made from rabbit or camel wool and the like should be invalid for tzitzis, and we have not heard such a thing. Apparently silk from worms proves the contrary, for it appears to be from the body itself; see seif 19. And if you say that only items fit for the work of Heaven were validated, meaning sanctity, excluding tzitzis which is only a mitzvah, this is wrong, for shofar proves it is also a mitzvah and nevertheless a non-kosher animal is invalid. See Megillah 26 and above, siman 21, in the Beit Yosef. However, one who examines the Ran at the beginning of chapter 2 of Rosh Hashanah, on the mishnah of shofar, sees that shofar is considered work of Heaven because remembrance is like something inside the Sanctuary. And the case of the tachash in Shabbos 28b implies that this is not a full hekesh. If so, tzitzis is understandable, for a mitzvah is not derived from sanctity. The Taz’s words in letter 27 are also understandable, that straps are a halacha given to Moshe at Sinai, because the comparison is not a full comparison. See Eliyah Rabbah 586, letter 5. The Levush also wrote that straps are a halacha given to Moshe at Sinai.
