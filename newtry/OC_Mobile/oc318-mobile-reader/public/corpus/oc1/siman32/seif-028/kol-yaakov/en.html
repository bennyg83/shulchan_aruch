So it appears from the words of GraZ, letter 8, who wrote: Tefillin do not require scoring except for the top line, because rabbinically it is prohibited at the outset to write three words from a verse of sacred Scripture without scoring, end quote. This implies that after the fact, if he already wrote, one should not invalidate. Peschei Olam and Matamei HaShulchan, letter 19, wrote in the name of Gra that if he did not score even the top line according to the Mechaber, or from every side according to Rama, according to Rabbeinu Tam it is invalid even after the fact; but according to what we maintain, that in a Torah scroll one scores all of it, we have no proof to invalidate. The reason is that the halachah was decided in the Talmud, Megillah, folio 18b, and Menachos, folio 32b, that tefillin do not require scoring. The need to score the top line is only because it is prohibited to write three words without scoring, as appears in Yoreh De'ah, siman 284, and as the authorities wrote. If so, this prohibition does not relate to the mitzvah of tefillin, but to the person, so that he write straight, as Machatzis HaShekel, s.k. 7, and the Shach there wrote. Therefore one should not invalidate after the fact. So too appears from the responsum of Rabbi Akiva Eiger, siman 50, cited by Pis'chei Teshuvah, siman 288, Ohel HaEdus, who wrote: Since the halachah transmitted to Moshe from Sinai requires scoring in a mezuzah, if he wrote without scoring it is not called writing, end quote. This implies that with tefillin, where it is not a halachah transmitted to Moshe from Sinai, even if he did not score, it is called writing and is valid. Such an incident occurred in our city Baghdad, may the Supreme One establish it, with one scribe who wrote many tefillin without scoring even the top line, and the gaon Rabbi Yosef Chaim Tov, may his light shine, permitted them.
