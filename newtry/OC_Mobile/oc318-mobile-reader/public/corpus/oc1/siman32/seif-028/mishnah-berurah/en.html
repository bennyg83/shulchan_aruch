That it not enter. Even the slightest amount of the head of a lamed may not enter the line above it. The same applies to a final kaf and the like, that they should not enter the line below into the space of a tet, ayin, and the like.
Into the space of the heh. The same applies to the space of an alef, tav, and the like. All the more so, he must be careful not to enter the space of a dalet or reish, lest it appear like a heh, as will be explained below.
Or the ches. The same applies if the head of a lamed enters the space of a final kaf, as below in seif katan 132. See Responsa Mahari M. of Brisk, siman 8; Rabbi Akiva Eiger also ruled leniently about this after the fact in his novellae.
Without touching. If it touched, it is invalid in any case because it lacks surrounding blank parchment. The wording of the Shulchan Aruch, which says that one must be careful, implies that this is only ab initio and not indispensable after the fact. This is provided that it did not enter in a way that changes the form of the letter, such as where it entered only slightly. But if it entered so much that the form of the letter changed, so that if one covered the lower line and only the head of the lamed were visible, and not the body of the lamed, an average child would not recognize what letter it is, this is a change in the form of the letter and is invalid. It also cannot be repaired because of writing out of order. All the more so, if the head of the lamed entered the hollow of a dalet or reish and made it appear like a heh, it is invalid, and the child reading it correctly does not help, since we see with our own eyes that it has thereby changed into another letter.
