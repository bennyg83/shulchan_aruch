Ibid. “And also enough space to write one word.” This requires study, because Rambam does not mean that the space needed for one word is included in the prescribed blank interval. Rather, he means that if the end of the first line contains space for nine letters together with the following word, it is preferable to leave the nine-letter space and write that next word on the same line, thereby making the section closed. Since there is then no room for the first word of the following section, one leaves a small blank at the beginning of the second line. The essential interval between one section and the next is never more than nine letters, even according to Rambam.
Even where the end of the first section contains the nine-letter measure of a section break but there is no room to write a word from the second section and thereby make it closed, Rambam holds that leaving space at the beginning of the line where the second section begins and starting in the middle of that line is also considered a closed section. This is not because neither blank space independently contains the full measure and they are joined together. Rather, even if together they do contain the full measure, we view the two spaces as though they were joined and as though the beginning of the second section had been written on the same line upon which the first section ended.
Taz writes, “as Rambam writes, that there should not be a space,” etc. In Nezirus Shimshon, the gaon Rabbi Shimshon Chasid emends this to read, “there must be a space.” In truth, that emendation is incorrect. Taz refers to Rambam’s second arrangement, where he writes that if the required measure did not remain, one leaves the rest of the first line blank and leaves a small space at the beginning of the second line. Taz understands that specifically when the first blank does not contain the full measure does the space at the beginning of the second line help, because the two spaces combine. Taz brings proof from this that if the first blank lacks the measure of nine letters plus the writing of one word, the blank on the second line can help. Accordingly, one might leave the measure of nine letters but not enough additional room to write a word. Taz responds that this would not satisfy Rosh. This is the straightforward meaning of Taz.
In my humble opinion, however, Rambam does not mean that the blank on the second line helps only in that specific case. Rather, as I explained, if there is space for nine letters, which is the measure of a section break, together with room to write a word, it is preferable to make the section closed in that manner. Only where there is no room to write a word would beginning at the head of the next line make the section open. One must therefore leave a small blank at the beginning of that line.
