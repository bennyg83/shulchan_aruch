It requires further study — for truly this is not Rambam's intent to say that the measure of one word is included in the measure of spacing; only Rambam means that if there were the measure of nine letters and writing the word after it, it is better to leave nine letters and write the word on the same line so that it be setuma; and since there is no place to write the word at the beginning of the next parasha, therefore one must leave a little space at the beginning of the second line; but nevertheless the main measure of spacing between parasha and parasha is no more than nine letters even according to Rambam; and nevertheless, even though at the end of the parasha there is the measure of nine letters, which is the measure of a parasha, and there is no place to write a word from the second parasha to make it setuma — Rambam holds that when one leaves at the beginning of the line at the head of the second parasha and begins from the middle of the line, this too is called setuma, and not because in each section there is not the measure except by combination, but even though there is combination as the measure, they are viewed as if they are joined and as if written at the beginning of the second parasha on the same line that closed the first parasha. And what Taz wrote "and as Rambam stated that there should not be spacing," etc. — I saw in the book Neziret Shimshon by the gaon Rav Shimshon Chassid who emended "there should be spacing," etc. And truly this is not so — that Taz's intent is that Rambam in the second version wrote "and if there did not remain the measure, etc., he leaves everything blank and leaves a little space from the second line"; and Taz holds that there must specifically be no spacing, etc., then what he leaves as space from the second line helps, for then they combine together; and Taz brings from this that in any case if there were not there the measure of nine letters, writing the word helps the spacing on the second line; and if so, here too one can do that he leave the measure of nine letters, only that there not also be space for writing the word; and on this he wrote that otherwise we have not fulfilled the view of Rosh, etc.; thus it is straightforward in Taz's intent. However, in my humble opinion, this is not Rambam's intent to say that the spacing on the second line does not help except in this specific case; rather, as stated — certainly if there is spacing as the measure of nine letters, which is the measure of a parasha, and he has place to write a word, it is better to make it setuma thereby; only when there is no place to write a word, then if he begins at the head of the line it is petucha, and therefore one must leave a little space at the beginning of the line:
