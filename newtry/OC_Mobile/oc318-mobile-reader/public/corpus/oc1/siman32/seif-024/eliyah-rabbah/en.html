A vav, zayin, or final nun, etc. This is the text in the Shulchan Aruch editions printed with Maharam Ravkash, in Beer HaGolah and Olas Tamid, and so it is explained in the Levush. But before the Taz the version was that the leg of the alef was severed, instead of nun, and he wrote at length in astonishment from what the Rivash wrote, that one cannot validate an alef whose left leg does not touch the middle line merely because a child reads it as an alef; see there at length. But this is for nothing. Magen Avraham wrote that one must say the correct reading is “the one.” It also seems to me that even according to that version, there is no difficulty, for one can say that the Rama is speaking where the leg of the alef was severed below, while above it touches the middle line, and in that case perhaps the child’s reading helps. However, perhaps the measure of a small letter suffices here, as in seif 15 above regarding the leg of a hei; this requires study.
