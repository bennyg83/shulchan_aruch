[24] A vav, zayin, or straight nun, etc. Thus is in the printed Shulchan Aruch with Maharam of Lublin (Be'er HaGolah) and Olat Tamid; and thus explained in the Levush. But before the Taz the version was that the leg of the aleph was severed in place of the nun, and he extended puzzlement at what the Rivash wrote — that one should not validate the aleph whose left leg does not touch the middle line so a child would read it aleph. See there at length — and this is for naught. Magen Avraham wrote one must say "the one" [first interpretation]. And further it seems to me that even for this version it is not difficult — one may say the Rama deals with when the leg of the aleph was severed below, and above it touches the middle line, and thereby the child's reading may help. However, perhaps as a full small letter suffices for this, as in seif [15] above regarding the leg of the heh — and this requires investigation.
