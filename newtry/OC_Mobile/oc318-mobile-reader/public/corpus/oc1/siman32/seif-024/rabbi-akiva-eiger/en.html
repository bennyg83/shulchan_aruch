Magen Avraham, seif katan 26. To peel it off and write in its place. I wrote in a responsum to the esteemed Rabbi Moshe, son of Rabbi Lozi, of blessed memory, dayan of the holy community of Hamburg, regarding a Divine Name written in a place not fit for it, where his honor ruled to peel it off and write another word in its place, based on the proof from Shabbos 116a, and because he found similarly in Responsa Noda BiYehudah, who ruled this as halacha on the basis of that proof. In truth, that responsum does not decide so as practical halacha except in his own case, where another word had already been written, that after the fact it is not invalid. But to write there initially he did not rule anything; he only wrote that the prohibition was not clear to him, for it is explicit only in the matter in tractate Arachin, and several decisors permit. Further, it is explicit in Shabbos 116 that when the writing is gone, its sanctity is gone. See there. Possibly in practice he would not have decided to be lenient against the explicit words of Tosafot. One may also explain the view of that gaon, who seems more inclined to be lenient, because he wrote that no decisor explicitly forbade it except Tosafot. But in my poverty I saw that the gaon Rabbi Eliezer of Metz, in his Sefer Yereim, siman 9, wrote that only when one writes on the legs of a bed, which is not fit for writing the Divine Name, may he use the remaining part. But when it was written on parchment, it sanctifies the entire sheet. See there. This is Rashi’s second explanation in Arachin 6, and the Semag also derives this from the words of the Baal Halachos Gedolos. Because of the difficulty from the sugya in Shabbos, one cannot reject all the words of these geonim. What his honor wrote, in his wording, to prove from this like the view of Maharil cited by the Magen Avraham in siman 32, seif katan 26, my friend, I have already paused over the Magen Avraham’s statement, “but in Tosafot in Arachin,” etc., which seems to imply that he understood Maharil and Tosafot to disagree. In my poverty I do not know the place for this, for Tosafot spoke only of writing a weekday word, whereas Maharil deals with writing a holy Name in its place. I saw in Tosefes Shabbos, in the beginning of his book in his glosses on the Magen Avraham, that he wrote: “to write the Name in its place” means the word, as the Shach wrote in Yoreh Deah, siman 276, seif katan 4. The Machatzit HaShekel cited this and wrote that it is correct. It seems that his intent is that from this the words of the Magen Avraham, “but in Tosafot in Arachin,” are understood. Yet even so this does not prove the point, for although the Shach wrote that we sometimes find a word called “the Name,” without intending specifically the holy Name, nevertheless here it is possible to explain that “the Name” is exact and refers specifically to a holy Name. Even more, in the source of the law in Maharil, this wording does not appear; rather, it says there that “your G-d” was written with a kaf properly. Thus it deals only with writing a holy Name in its place. Not only have I shown that Tosafot would not permit this, I have also not found anyone who explicitly disagrees with Tosafot on this point. Regarding the main difficulty from Shabbos, upon which his honor and the gaon Noda BiYehudah focused, and also Responsa Nachalah LiYehoshua, one may say that there in Shabbos, regarding saving from a fire, a severe sanctity is required; and since the writing is gone, its severe sanctity is gone, so one does not save it from the fire. Nevertheless, its sanctity remains upon it insofar as one may not write a weekday word in its place. But as a matter of practical halacha this requires study.
