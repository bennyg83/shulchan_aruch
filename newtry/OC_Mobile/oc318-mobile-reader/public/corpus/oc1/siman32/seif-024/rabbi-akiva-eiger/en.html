Magen Avraham comment 26 — to scrape it off and write in its place. Here I wrote in a responsum to Rabbi Moshe, may his light shine, son of Rabbi Meir haLevi, may he rest in peace, dayan of the holy community Hamburg, about a Name written not in its proper place, for which the rabbi ruled from the outset to scrape and write in its place another word, from the proof of the passage in Shabbat 17a, and that he found thus in responsum Noda BiYehudah, who ruled so for halachah, and from this proof. In truth, in the responsum he does not decide halachah thus except in his own case, where another word was already written that is not invalid bedieved; but to write lekhatchilah thus he ruled nothing, only that the prohibition is not clear to him, for it is not explicit except in a matter of tractate Arakhin, and several teachers of rulings permit. And further, it is explicit in Shabbat 17 that when it was written, its sanctity was written — see there. And it is possible that in practice he would not decide to be lenient against the explicit words of Tosafot. Also one may resolve the gaon's view, which seems more lenient, from his having written that no posek explicitly forbids except in Tosafot. But I myself saw that the gaon Rabbi Eliezer of Metz in his Sefer Yereim siman 9 wrote that specifically when writing on the knees of the bed, which is not fit for writing the Name, one may use what is permitted; but when written on parchment, the whole sheet is sanctified — see there. And this is in Rashi's second explanation in Arakhin 6a, but in Semag he is precise thus from the words of Beit HaGadol, and because of the difficulty from the passage in Shabbat one cannot reject all these words of the geonim. What the rabbi wrote in his wording to prove from this the view of the Rosh in Magen Avraham siman 32 comment 26 — my friend, I examined the Magen Avraham on what he brought, but in Tosafot in Arakhin etc. it appears that Tosafot and the Rosh disagree, and I myself did not know a place for this, for Tosafot spoke only of writing a mundane word, but the Rosh deals with writing a sacred Name in its place. And I saw in Tosafot Shabbat at the beginning of his book in his glosses on the Magen Avraham, who wrote: to write in its place the Name — that is, the word, as Shakh wrote, Yoreh Deah siman 276 comment 4 — end quote; and Machatzit haShekel brought this and wrote on it that it is correct. It seems his intent is that from this the words of the Magen Avraham are understood, who wrote on what he brought in Tosafot Arakhin. However, even so one cannot derive that although Shakh wrote that sometimes we find a word called by the Name's name without intending exactly the sacred Name, nevertheless here one may explain that here he used precisely the Name, and deals specifically with a sacred Name. And especially in the Rosh's source there is no such wording, only that God's Name was written in kaf as proper — end quote; thus he deals only with writing a sacred Name in its place, and I showed not only that Tosafot would not permit this, but also that I found no one who explicitly disagrees with Tosafot on this. And mainly the difficulty from that passage in Shabbat on which the rabbi stood — answer for the gaon: in responsum Nachalat Yehoshua one may say that there on Shabbat, for removing it because of fire, stricter sanctity applies, and since when it was written its sanctity was written more strictly, one does not save it from fire; but nevertheless its sanctity remains that one should not write in its place a mundane word, only a sacred one. But for practical ruling — requires further study.
