At the beginning. For if even one letter was not written for the sake of tefillin, it is invalid, and passing a quill over it for that purpose does not help, as in Yoreh Deah 274. “At the beginning” means that at the beginning of all the passages he should say, “I am writing these passages for the sake of etc.” Strictly speaking, this helps even if he pauses between the passages; nevertheless it is better that he say this at the beginning of each passage, etc.
The writing. It appears that the same applies when correcting separated letters: it must be done for their sake, since without this the tefillin are invalid, for they do not have the proper form of letters.
He should say. If another person writes, he too must say this, even in the middle, when he begins.
With his mouth. Thought alone does not suffice, even after the fact, as Eliyah Rabbah and Pri Megadim write. See Chiddushei Rabbi Akiva Eiger.
I am writing, etc.. Some wrote that it is proper then also to say, “and all Divine Names in it are for the sanctity of the Name,” lest he later forget to sanctify the Name in its place. See Biur Halacha.
Each time. If he writes two Divine Names without interruption, one sanctification is sufficient.
At the beginning of the writing. That he is writing for the sanctity of tefillin. Although at the beginning he did not mention the sanctity of the Divine Names, it is sufficient, for in any event he mentioned that it is for sanctity. But if at the beginning he also did not mention it explicitly, only in thought alone, or if now he did not sanctify the Divine Names even in thought, he has not fulfilled the requirement even after the fact.
After the fact. But initially he must explicitly say every time he writes a Divine Name that it is for the sanctity of the Name, even if at the beginning of the writing he said, “and all Divine Names in it”; and so the later authorities ruled.
