(92) At the beginning — for if even one letter was not written for the sake of tefillin it is invalid, and passing the quill for its sake does not help [Yod Ra'avad]; and "at the beginning" means at the beginning of all the parshiyot — he should say: I am writing these parshiyot for the sake of tefillin, etc., and this helps by law even if he paused between parshiyot; nevertheless it is better that he say at the beginning of each parshah, etc.

(93) The writing — it appears the same applies to correcting separate letters, that he needs intent for their sake, since otherwise the tefillin are invalid, for they do not have the proper form of letters.

(94) He should say — and if another writes, he too must say likewise, even in the middle when he begins.

(95) With his mouth — and thought does not suffice, even b'dieved [Achronim and Pri Megadim]; and see in the novellae of Ra'avad.

(96) I am writing, etc. — some wrote it is proper that he also then say all Divine Names in it are for the sanctity of the Name, lest he later forget to sanctify the Name in its place; see Biur Halachah:

(97) Each time — and if he writes two Divine Names without interruption, one sanctification suffices:

(98) At the beginning of writing — that he writes for the sanctity of tefillin; even though he did not mention at the beginning sanctity of the Divine Names it suffices, for nevertheless he mentioned for sanctity; but if even at the beginning he did not mention explicitly, only in thought in general, or now he did not sanctify the Divine Names even in thought — even post factum it does not fulfill:
