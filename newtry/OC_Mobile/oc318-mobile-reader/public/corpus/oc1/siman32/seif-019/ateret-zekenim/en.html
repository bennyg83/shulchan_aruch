At the beginning of writing he should say aloud (as above); some say thought suffices — that he writes the divine names for their sake. Further: this is the intention for the divine names in Sefer HaChasidim of Rabbi David Oppenheim, author of Sefer Daled-Chet, which he transmitted in manuscript to the worthy student Rabbi Tavel, of blessed memory, a scribe of Torah, tefillin, and mezuzot; I copied it from the manuscript of the aforementioned pious man, and thus it reads: At the tip of a yod he should intend the crown with the name Ehyeh and intend toward the cause of all causes bound to it, and from there light is drawn forth and clothed in the body of the yod; likewise when he comes to write heh he should intend the drawing forth of light and its clothing in the heh, and likewise from heh to vav and from vav to the final heh; at the final heh he completes the intention toward the supernal Emanator. One must also intend with every letter the highest inclusion of ten — how so? In yod: intend in its three tips crown, wisdom, understanding within wisdom; the upper tip alludes to Ein Sof and also to crown within wisdom, and the body of the yod to wisdom; the lower tip to understanding. Further: intend the filling of the yod which is vav-dalet, that it includes six extremities in wisdom; dalet is a tenth of 65, ten, and all ten are closed within it and hidden. When he comes to write heh he should make yod first and intend that Abba comes to unite with Imma to place in her what was hidden within him — zayin-dalet — and make the upper line and draw it from yod slightly to indicate that yod receives; this is the secret "a river went out of Eden"; he draws the right leg from heh and intends that it is vav emerging from between Abba and Imma, and vav includes six extremities, and dalet likewise in concealment, for the upper roof which is the river serves here and there. The middle leg within heh is tiferet and yesod including six extremities; one must make it slightly thick to indicate two vavs, each of which is tiferet and malchut; when the sons emerge from Imma in her concealed aspect, pregnant from them and going out to the birth opening, he intends that they emerge from Imma and intends in the head the three first — chochmah, binah, daat — these are the brains in the head (see Pardes shaar 3 sections 67 and 68). In drawing vav before reaching the middle he intends chesed, gevurah, tiferet — "El Elohim YHVH" — these names are the arms and body. In the name YHVH he intends that yod-heh are found in it from Abba and Imma, vav is its own aspect, heh is its feminine counterpart — two partzufim that came to the world; in drawing vav he further intends netzach, hod, yesod — the two legs and Imma, including her shechinah — "the heh is hers" as explained in Tikunim 5 and 6. When he comes to write the final heh he writes the right leg first and intends that it is tiferet coming to give the shechinah what was hidden within it; afterward he writes the upper roof and intends the attribute of Adonai nourished from the right leg which is the name YHVH; on the small middle leg he intends the unification of the two names as one — namely Y-A-H-D-V-N-H-Y. At the beginning of writing he should say, etc.; but one need not say this at the start of every paragraph, unlike a divine name with an extra elevation, which requires it at every name; some say one must say so at the start of writing every paragraph and section (Beit Yosef in the name of the Ran). The first view is primary; but if he did not say so at the start of writing, it is invalid, and likewise if he did not say so at every divine name in every name it is invalid (Rabbi Yehudah in Sefer HaRosh and Orahot Chaim).
