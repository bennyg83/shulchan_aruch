The groove of the shin, etc., reaches until the place of the seam — Bedi'eved see Mishnah Berurah that one may be lenient; so too Darkhei Moshe in the name of Or Zarua, that there is no concern. What Darkhei Moshe wrote from Baruch Shemar — that it implies he is strict to disqualify in Beit Shmuel before us — is not so; rather, in the Hagahah there he brought to be lenient bedi'eved.

And likewise the yods in the shin — In the long Darkhei Moshe it states "the yodin in the shin," and it appears this was also the text before Magen Avraham in his Hagahah on Shulchan Aruch here; therefore he brought proof from it for his view that one should not distinguish between the right shin and the left. Know that from Chayei Adam it appears he is in doubt in the law whether like Bach and his supporters or like Magen Avraham; therefore it seems to me that even though we decide in Mishnah Berurah that lekhatchilah it is better that the yodin in the left shin also reach its lower edge — nevertheless bedi'eved one should not be strict with them to disqualify, since according to many great authorities even lekhatchilah they did not have them touch; so too Peri Megadim wrote only that it is proper to do like Magen Avraham — unless in a case where one yod touches and one does not, for then either way it is invalid, as Chayei Adam wrote. Know further that per the words of the great authorities who hold that on the left shin its yodin should not touch its edge — likewise the yodin themselves should not have the form of a yod as in the shin of three heads, but should be like simple lines; nevertheless according to Magen Avraham's ruling that one should not distinguish between right and left, one should not distinguish in this either. If he made on the shin of four heads [and all the more so of three heads] simple lines and they reach their edges — it is two contradictory matters [Peri Megadim] and invalid.

It must touch below, etc. — See the reason in Mishnah Berurah — it is from Chayei Adam, that it must be written like a Torah scroll, since it is halachah leMoshe miSinai; therefore it appears the same law applies if the yod does not touch its line — possibly it is an impediment; all the more if there was no yod at all but only a simple line. Even if the other lines were made properly as yodin on their heads, only one line was not proper — and all the more if all of them alone on the shin of four heads: there, if the lines were simple without heads, one should not be strict bedi'eved, for one may rely on the great authorities who are lenient — as above.

That the edges of the shin too — See Mishnah Berurah regarding bedi'eved, and in Avodat HaYom it appears that bedi'eved one should not be strict; I do not know the source for this — perhaps it deals with a case where its form was not lost thereby and only part of its tip entered inside; but if a little of the shin itself entered the ma'avarta, he too would concede — requires study.
