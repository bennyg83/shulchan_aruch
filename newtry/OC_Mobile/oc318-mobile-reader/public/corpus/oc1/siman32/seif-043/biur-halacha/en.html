The groove of the shin, etc., should reach the place of the seam. Regarding after the fact, see the Mishnah Berurah, where there is room to be lenient. So too wrote Darchei Moshe in the name of Or Zarua, that there is no concern. What Darchei Moshe wrote, that from Baruch SheAmar it implies that he is stringent to invalidate it, is not found in the Baruch SheAmar before us. On the contrary, in the gloss there he brought that one may be lenient after the fact.
And likewise the yud in the shin. In the long Darchei Moshe the text reads: the yuds in the shin. It appears that this version was also before the Magen Avraham in his gloss to this Shulchan Aruch, and therefore he brought proof from it for his view that one should not distinguish between the right shin and the left shin. Know that from Chayei Adam it appears that he is uncertain as a matter of law whether to rule like the Bach and those with him or like the Magen Avraham. Therefore it seems to me that although in the Mishnah Berurah we decide that initially it is better that the yuds of the left shin also touch its lower edge, nevertheless after the fact one should not be stringent to invalidate them, since according to many great authorities even initially they should not touch. The Pri Megadim also wrote only that it is proper to act like the Magen Avraham, except where one yud touches and one yud does not touch; then either way it is invalid, as the Chayei Adam wrote. Know further that according to the great authorities who hold that the yuds of the left shin should not touch its lower edge, the same applies that those yuds themselves should not have the form of a yud as in the three-headed shin, but should be like simple lines. However, according to the ruling of the Magen Avraham that one should not distinguish between right and left, there is no distinction in this either. If one made simple lines in the four-headed shin, and all the more so in the three-headed shin, and they reach to their lower edges, this is a self-contradiction, as the Pri Megadim writes, and it is invalid.
It must touch below, etc. See the reason in the Mishnah Berurah. It is from Chayei Adam, that it must be written as in a Sefer Torah, since it is a halachah leMoshe miSinai. According to this, it appears that the same applies if a yud does not touch its own line, that perhaps this too is indispensable; all the more so if there was no yud at all, only a simple line. This is true even if the other lines were made properly, with yuds on their heads, but one line was not according to law, and all the more so if all of them were not, except in the four-headed shin; there, if the lines were simple without heads, one should not be stringent after the fact, for one may rely on the great authorities who are lenient, as above.
That also the lower edges of the shin. See the Mishnah Berurah regarding after the fact. In Sefer Avodas HaYom it implies that after the fact one should not be stringent, and I do not know a source for this. Perhaps he speaks of a case where its form was not lost thereby, and only part of its point entered into it. But if a little of the body of the shin itself entered into the ma'avarta, he too would agree. This requires study.
