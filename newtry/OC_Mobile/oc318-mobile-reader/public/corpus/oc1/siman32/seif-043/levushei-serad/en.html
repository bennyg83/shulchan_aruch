Taz, seif katan 21. To fill the line. At first glance this means that the lines must be equal, as stated in Shulchan Aruch, seif 33, and therefore he extended the nun. But this is difficult: what relevance does a bent nun at the end of a line have? Furthermore, what does he mean by writing that there is doubt whether it is valid? Even if the lines are not equal, there is no invalidation after the fact. Perhaps this is what he means: for example, the word 'for our fathers' was at the end of the line, and he wished to align the lines, so he extended the nun in order to write the vav next to it and thereby make the lines equal. He was uncertain whether, since he does not extend it upward, it is considered two words. Nevertheless, this is not precise. Why did he not state this very doubt simply, for example where there was an extra vav written, as appears in the Beis Yosef and is cited in Magen Avraham, seif katan 36, such as where he wrote 'he gives'? Why did he need to invent that it was written that way in order to align the lines? Therefore it appears more likely that this is a scribal error, and it should read: to fill the empty space. This is in accordance with the words of the Beis Yosef, and the word 'line' is a scribal error.
