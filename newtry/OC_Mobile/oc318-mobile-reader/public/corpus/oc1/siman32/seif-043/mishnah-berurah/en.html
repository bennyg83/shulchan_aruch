Of the shin. Whether the right one or the left one.
Shall reach, etc. Meaning all the way to the titura itself. After the fact it is valid even if it did not reach, provided it has the form of a shin upon it.
The yud in the shin. Some of the great authorities distinguish between the right shin and the left shin, saying that with the left shin, on the contrary, one should be careful that the yuds not touch the lower edge of the shin below. But the Magen Avraham ruled that one should not distinguish between the right and left shin, and in the left shin we require that the two yuds touch below at its edge. Pri Megadim likewise wrote that this is proper to do, and Birkei Yosef wrote similarly in the name of Mahari; see there. So ruled the other later authorities. Pri Megadim further wrote that one should see to it that the shins have the form of yuds upon them, not mere simple lines.
Touch below. For otherwise it does not have the name of a shin, and this invalidates even after the fact. In the left shin, if one touches and one does not touch, it is invalid. See Biur Halacha.
That also the lower edges of the shin. Meaning the points of the shins. The reason is that the whole shin must be visible, to fulfill what is written, “and all the peoples of the earth shall see that the Name of Hashem is called upon you,” and Rabbi Elazar said: these are the tefillin of the head. “The Name of Hashem is called” has the initials shin. After the fact, if it entered slightly into the titura, so that because of this it does not have the proper form of a shin, this requires study. See Biur Halacha.
