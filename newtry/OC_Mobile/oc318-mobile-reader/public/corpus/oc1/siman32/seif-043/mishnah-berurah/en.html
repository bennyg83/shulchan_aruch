(196) Of the shin — whether of the right or of the left:

(197) Reaches, etc. — that is, until the titura itself; and post factum it is valid even if it did not reach, as long as the form of the shin is on it:

(198) The yods in the shin — some of the great authorities distinguish between the right shin and the left shin: on the left, rather, one is careful that the yods not touch the lower edge of the shin; but Magen Avraham ruled that one should not distinguish between right and left, and on the left we require that both yods touch below on its edge; and so too Peri Megadim, that thus it is proper to do; and so too in Birkei Yosef in the name of Mahari — see there; and so ruled other acharonim. Peri Megadim also wrote that it should appear that the shins have the form of yods, not mere plain lines:

(199) Should touch below — for otherwise there is no shin on it, and this invalidates even b'dieved. On the left shin, if one [yud] touches and one does not, it is invalid; see Biur Halachah:

(200) That the edges of the shin too — that is, the points of the shins — and the reason is that the entire shin must be visible, to fulfill what is written: "All the peoples of the earth shall see that the Name of Hashem is proclaimed over you"; and R' Elazar said: these are the head tefillin — "the Name of Hashem is proclaimed" is an acronym for shin.
