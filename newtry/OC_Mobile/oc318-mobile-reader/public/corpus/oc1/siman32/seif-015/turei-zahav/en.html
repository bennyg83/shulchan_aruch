Even if its entire inside was pierced. In chapter HaKometz: If the inside of a hei was pierced, it is valid; if its leg was pierced, it is invalid. Rashi explained: “its inside” means the inner leg, and “its leg” means the right leg. Another version: “its inside” means the parchment space within it. Thus each explanation has both a leniency and a stringency. According to the first explanation, there is a leniency regarding the inner leg of the hei, that even if the smallest amount remains it is valid; and there is a stringency, that it does not address the inner parchment space at all, so by implication we rely on the Yerushalmi cited by the Tur, that even inside it must be surrounded by parchment. According to the second explanation, there is a stringency regarding the inner leg, namely that it is like the right leg and is invalid if there did not remain the size of a small letter; and there is a leniency, that our Talmud contradicts the Yerushalmi and holds that inside it need not be surrounded by parchment. The Rosh agreed with the first explanation, as written here in the Shulchan Aruch. The other decisors cited by the Rama hold like the latter explanation, and therefore according to the Rama’s ruling we do not take account of the Yerushalmi that requires surrounding parchment inside. It is possible that the Rama’s view is to rule according to the stringencies of both explanations that we mentioned, and this is proper. However, the Tur concludes: Therefore, if its entire inside was pierced, it is invalid. This implies specifically its entire inside, but not part of it. The reason appears to be that all the parchment within the letter is considered one unit. Therefore, although the parchment inside the letter was severed on one side, what remains on the other side protects it. This is similar to where part was severed on one side outside, but what remains on that same side is intact, where we have not found any invalidation.
