50) Seif 15. If after it was written it was punctured, etc.; but in the Yerushalmi it implies, etc. The Shulchan Gavoah, letter 34, wrote: Our master cited this Yerushalmi only as an initial concern, for where there is an anonymous ruling followed by a dispute, the halachah follows the anonymous ruling. Therefore, if there are no other tefillin except these, he puts them on with a blessing. End quote. So wrote Tosefes Shabbos in Sefer Pelaim; Keset HaSofer, siman 7, letter 7; Emes LeYaakov, klal 10, letter 2. See Levush on Taz, seif katan 14, and our words below, letter 69.
51) There. That also on the inside it must be surrounded by blank parchment. If the hole does not fill the entire hollow, even though the hole is immediately adjacent to the letter and no blank parchment at all remains between the letter and the hole inside it, nevertheless it is considered surrounded by blank parchment even at the place of the hole, by means of the blank parchment inside it on the other side of the hole. Taz, end of seif katan 8; Eliyah Rabbah, letter 22; GraZ, letter 19; Noda BiYehuda, first edition, Yoreh De'ah, siman 75. Some say that blank parchment on one side does not help for the other side, and we require the letter to be surrounded by blank parchment on all its sides; whenever the hole reaches the thickness of the letter, it is invalid according to this view. Maamar Mordechai, letter 13. So wrote Nahar Shalom, letter 15; Keset HaSofer, inquiry 4, s.v. hen. See below, letter 69.
52) If the entire hollow of the letter was punctured, and it is possible to scrape away a little of its thickness and make it surrounded by blank parchment while its form remains, it is permitted. Meleches Shamayim, klal 7, letter 1, in Chochmah; Keset HaSofer, siman 7, letter 7.
