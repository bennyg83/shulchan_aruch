But in Yerushalmi — See Mishnah Berurah: know, etc.; see Taz's novel ruling that even for Yerushalmi there is disqualification only if its whole interior is pierced, not if pierced on one side near the letter. See Peri Megadim and Darkhei Chaim — this refers only to Beit Yosef's first answer; see there. For his second answer, which the author ruled like, near Taz — the Yerushalmi law: if pierced inside before writing near the letter, even partially — invalid. So Yerushalmi states explicitly, chapter 1 of Megillah: a stone was pierced in the middle of bet — if gevil surrounds it on every side, kosher; if not — invalid. It seems plain Tur's intent is the same, but he minimized "its whole interior" to exclude where only a hair's breadth of gevil surrounds the letter on all sides — kosher. From this Yerushalmi, Darkhei Chaim's view to reconcile Taz is hidden — he wrote Tur holds like Beit Yosef's first answer, requiring whole interior; but from this Yerushalmi it is proven we do not require whole interior. Nevertheless, in pressing need, if no scribe can peel well near the hole so gevil surrounds it, one may rely on poskim who validate even whole interior pierced — though Taz wrote possibly Rama's view is strict like both explanations; one who examines Darkhei Moshe at length will see plainly he leans to validate whole interior piercing.

And so is the halachah — See Mishnah Berurah, se'if kuf-mem, from his words, etc.; so too Beit Yosef implies. See Darkhei Chaim in the laws of Kodesh, law 26 — from his words it appears Rama was strict because of a doubt in the law; according to his words there, the entire interior was pierced and the left leg was pierced until less than a full aleph-vav-kuf remained — they have one law together. But one who examines Darkhei Moshe at length will see clearly from his words that he agrees with Mordechai, who is lenient when the entire interior is pierced; if so, automatically the left leg is equal to the right leg for disqualification, as is known to one who studies the poskim — one matter depends on the other — except Bach wrote that Tur is lenient regarding both.

**If the right leg was pierced and something remained, etc.** — See Mishnah Berurah in se'if kuf-mem-alef, where he writes the ruling of Magen Avraham brought in the name of Rambam: if there remains in the letter's thickness like a vav or a thin yud it is valid; we explained that there is no required measure for letter thickness. We wrote thus according to Peri Megadim in this siman se'if yod-vav, that if a thin black scratch remains it suffices. It would seem that even less would be valid; yet this cannot be reconciled with the Gemara's wording for Rambam — if one's thigh was pierced, if k'malei aleph-vav-kuf remained it is valid, if not, invalid — implying that less is invalid. But this is not proven except on the Meiri's view; for us the Gemara does not deal with splitting a letter through its thickness (see Peri Megadim there), and the remainder need not be k'malei aleph-vav-kuf; still, it requires study after Magen Avraham cited it.

**K'malei aleph-vav-kuf** — If this script is medium-sized, it would seem ineffective if k'malei aleph-vav-kuf is small script, for otherwise there is no measure to the matter; but if this script is large, it seems certainly effective if k'malei aleph-vav-kuf is medium script.

**Valid** — See Mishnah Berurah: that one need not show it to a child — so imply all the later authorities: likewise Perishah, Taz, Peri Megadim, Machatzit HaShekel, and Dagul Merevavah; we do not abandon the plain sense of these great ones because of a doubt in Yad Ephraim. It is astonishing how he could say Magen Avraham's view in Rama is thus — is it not clear from Magen David that Mordechai holds we do not require a hei, only k'malei aleph-vav-kuf, otherwise he would not question there (see there); and here Rama recorded in Hagah in Mordechai's name, and Peri Megadim in Mishbetzot Zahav wrote explicitly that although Magen David inclines to Tur like Meiri, in Shulchan Aruch it is unstated like Mordechai; Perishah challenged Magen David and concluded Tur too is like Mordechai — we do not combine a split letter; thus the Gemara proves that for hei and the like, a remainder of k'malei aleph-vav-kuf alone suffices. The reason seems plain: the Sages knew hei's measure intrinsically, so no child-test is needed — a child is not accustomed to this form, for each scribe initially writes a long letter, like chet (explained below se'if 25) — no child-proof because chet of chatotrot is unfamiliar. Nevertheless bedieved, if shown to a child who does not err and cannot read it, one should be strict — I saw Sheyarei Knesset HaGedolah incline that even where we do not heed a child to be lenient, we heed him to be strict (see there). Though for chet of chatotrot (below end se'if 25) a child's reading does not detract, since chatotrot chet is wholly unfamiliar — even written properly he would read it with two zayins, as Beit Yosef explains end siman 36 — unlike our case.

And if not — invalid — We do not combine what is below the hole or break even if separation not evident; child neither wise nor foolish reads it as a letter — all without fixing; if he fixed the break, law as explained next.
