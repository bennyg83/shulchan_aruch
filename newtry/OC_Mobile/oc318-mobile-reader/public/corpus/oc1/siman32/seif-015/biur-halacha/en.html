But in the Yerushalmi. See the Mishnah Berurah, who wrote: Know, etc. See the Taz, who wrote a novel law, that even according to the Yerushalmi there is invalidity only when the entire interior is punctured, but not when its interior is punctured on one side near the letter. See the Pri Megadim and Derech HaChaim, that this applies only according to the first resolution of the Beis Yosef; see there. But according to his second resolution, which the Mechaber rules like just below in seif 16, the law according to the Yerushalmi would be that if its interior was punctured before the writing near the letter, even partially, it is invalid. In truth, this is stated explicitly in the Yerushalmi, chapter 1 of Megillah: if the middle of the beis was punctured, if parchment surrounds it on every side it is valid, and if not, it is invalid. It seems clear to me that this is also the intent of the Tur, except that when he wrote the entire interior he meant to exclude a case where the actual interior of the letter was punctured and there remained only a hairbreadth surrounded by blank parchment on all sides, which is valid. From this Yerushalmi the reasoning of the Derech HaChaim is refuted, for he sought to justify the words of the Taz, s.k. 8, by writing that the Tur holds like the first resolution of the Beis Yosef and therefore the entire cavity is specifically required. From this Yerushalmi it is explicit that we do not require the entire cavity. Nevertheless, as a matter of law, in a pressing situation where no scribe is available who can properly peel away a little near the hole so that it will be surrounded by parchment, it seems to me that one may rely on the poskim who validate even when the entire interior was punctured. For although the Taz wrote that it is possible that the Rama’s view follows the stringency of both explanations, one who examines the long Darchei Moshe will see explicitly that his view is to be lenient when the entire interior was punctured.
And such is the halachah. See Mishnah Berurah s.k. 40, where he wrote: from his words, etc.; and so it implies in the Beis Yosef. See Derech HaChaim, Hilchos Kerias HaTorah, law 26, where his words imply that the Rama was stringent because of a legal doubt. According to his words there, if the entire cavity was punctured and if the left leg was punctured until there was not left the measure of a small letter, they share one law. But one who examines the long Darchei Moshe will see explicitly from his words that he agrees with the view of the Mordechai, who is lenient where the entire cavity was punctured. If so, the left leg is automatically equal to the right leg for invalidation, as is known to one who examines the poskim, for these matters depend on each other, aside from the Bach who wrote that the Tur is lenient in both.
If the right leg was punctured, if there remains, etc. See Mishnah Berurah s.k. 41, where he wrote the law of the Magen Avraham, who brought in the name of the Ram that if there remains in the thickness of the letter the equivalent of a vav or a thin yud it is valid. We wrote there the reason, that there is no fixed measure for the thickness of letters. All this we wrote according to what the Pri Megadim wrote in this siman, s.k. 16, that if a thin black scratch remains it is sufficient. It would seem from this that even the smallest amount is valid. But according to this, the language of the Gemara according to the Ram is not fitting, for this is what is stated there: if its thigh was punctured, if there remains from it the measure of a small letter it is valid, and if not it is invalid. This explicitly implies that even if any amount remains it is invalid. Yet in truth this is proven only according to the view of the Meiri, whereas according to us the Gemara is not discussing at all a case where the letter was severed in its thickness; see the Pri Megadim there. Therefore it is not necessary that the letter retain the measure of a small letter. Nevertheless, this requires study, since the Magen Avraham cited him.
The measure of a small letter. If this script is of medium size, it would seem that it does not help if the measure of a small letter is from small script, for otherwise there is no fixed measure. But if this script is large, it appears certain that it helps if the measure of a small letter is from medium script.
It is valid. See the Mishnah Berurah, where he wrote that it need not be shown to a child. So it implies from all the Acharonim, namely the Perishah, Taz, Pri Megadim, Machatzis HaShekel, and Derech HaChaim. We do not abandon the clear ruling of these great authorities because of the doubt of Yad Ephraim. In truth, his words are astonishing, for how did he want to say that this is the view of the Magen Avraham in the Rama? It is explicit from the Darchei Moshe, and likewise in the Perishah, that the view of the Mordechai is that regarding a heh and similar letters we require only the measure of a small letter; otherwise his question there would have no force at all, see there. Here, the Rama in the gloss is marked as citing the Mordechai. So too the Pri Megadim writes explicitly in Mishbetzos Zahav that although in the Darchei Moshe he inclines to say that the Tur’s view follows the Meiri, nevertheless in the Shulchan Aruch he wrote simply like the view of the Mordechai. So too, in the Perishah he challenges the Darchei Moshe and concludes that the Tur’s view too is like the Mordechai, that we do not say combination in a severed letter. If so, it is automatically proven from the Gemara that in a letter heh and the like, leaving the measure of a small letter alone suffices. The reason for this appears simple: the Sages had a tradition regarding the letter heh and the like that this is their essential measure. Therefore one need not bring proof from a child, for a child is not accustomed to this form, since everyone initially writes a longer letter. It is similar to the ches that will be explained below in seif 25, where there is also no proof from a child because he is not accustomed to a ches with humps. Nevertheless, after the fact, if they showed it to a child who is neither wise nor foolish and he could not read it, one should be stringent, for I saw that the Shiyurei Knesses HaGedolah inclines to say that even in places where we do not rely on a child to be lenient, we rely on him to be stringent; see there. Although regarding a ches with humps it is explained below at the end of seif 25 that a child’s reading does not detract from it, that is because there he is not at all accustomed to a ches with humps, for even if it were written properly he would read it as two zayins, as explained in the Beis Yosef at the end of siman 36. This is not so in our case.
