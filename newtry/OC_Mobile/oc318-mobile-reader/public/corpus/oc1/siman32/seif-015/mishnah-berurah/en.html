After it was written, etc. We will first present two introductions and then explain, with Hashem's help. First: every letter must have the form proper to that letter; if not, it is invalid, and in this there is no difference whether it was not written according to halachah or whether it was later damaged. Second: every letter that is not surrounded by blank parchment on all four sides is invalid; in this law there are two details. First, this applies specifically if it was not surrounded by blank parchment before the writing, but if after the writing a hole or tear developed near the letter on the outside and because of this it is not surrounded by blank parchment, it is valid, as will be explained in se'if 16 in Shulchan Aruch. Second, most poskim hold that blank parchment is required only around the outside of the letters, and not inside them, except for the Yerushalmi, which is stringent about this. Now we will explain the Shulchan Aruch. If after it was written it was pierced, etc., the reason is that inside the letter there is no requirement of surrounding blank parchment. According to this, even if the hole was there before the writing it is valid. The reason it says, 'If after it was written it was pierced,' is because initially one should not write even if the hole is in the middle of its hollow and does not fill its interior. But in truth, if he transgressed and wrote, even if the hole was there before it was written, it is valid and requires no correction.
Inside the hei. The same applies to other letters that have three walls and a hollow inside them. But a final kaf, and certainly a vav and the like, are not called an inside.
The whole hollow. Even if the hole touched the letter itself, as long as a thin scratch remains outside, it is valid, for there is no required measure for the thickness of the letters.
That also inside, etc. If so, its law is like the outside: if the hole was inside it before the writing, it is invalid. See the Taz and Pri Megadim in the name of the Levush, who say that one should be stringent like the Yerushalmi. Know that according to the Yerushalmi, surrounding blank parchment is required on all sides inside just as outside. Therefore, if it was pierced inside before the writing next to the letter, and because of this it is not surrounded by blank parchment, one should scrape slightly from the inside thickness of the letter's line so that it will be surrounded by blank parchment. See Biur Halacha at the end of the passage beginning, 'But in the Yerushalmi,' etc.
It was pierced. The same applies if some of the ink was erased and it was not pierced. Specifically with a hei the Rosh disagrees and validates it if any amount remains, both above the hole and below, for he holds that there is no minimum measure for the suspended point of the hei; even if one initially wrote such a short leg, it is valid according to the Rosh. But with other letters, such as a ches and the like, even the Rosh agrees that the left leg has the same law as the right leg.
Require the measure of, etc. This refers to a case where there is no deficiency because of being surrounded by blank parchment, such as where it was pierced after the writing, as stated nearby at the end of se'if 16. Otherwise it is invalid, even if more than the measure of a small letter remained, even according to the Rosh.
And so is the halachah. From these words it implies that he did not rule like the other poskim only as a stringency, but that it is obvious to him that the halachah follows them. If so, the scribes who stumble in this must be warned very strongly. In tefillin and mezuzos, where we require writing in order, at first glance no correction helps, even if a child reads it as the letter, since we see that it does not have the form of a letter; without this it is not called a hei according to these poskim, as below in se'if 25, see there. Nevertheless, it appears that one may be lenient through correction, similar to the leniency of Pri Megadim regarding a final kaf that he made square through correction; see there.
It was pierced. The same applies if the letter was broken without a hole, or if the edge of the letter was erased. The Shulchan Aruch is speaking about its length, and the same applies if part of its thickness was interrupted, whether by a hole or by erasure: if a thin black scratch remains like a thin vav or yud, it is valid, for there is no measure for the thickness of the letters. But with a hole, whether in its thickness or in its length, we require that it was pierced after the writing, as below in se'if 16. This is from Magen Avraham and Derech HaChaim.

If there remained. This means from the hole upward; we do not combine with it what is below the hole, even if a child who is neither wise nor foolish reads it as the letter, since we see that the form of the letter did not remain properly, as was explained nearby in the name of the Taz. But if from the hole upward, or from the break upward if the letter was broken in the middle, there remains the measure of a small letter, it is valid and need not be shown to a child. The same applies if one initially wrote such a short small leg: it is valid. See Yad Ephraim and Biur Halacha.
The full measure of a small letter. This means a yud, together with its lower point, according to what is ruled below in siman 36 in Beis Yosef, that it is not called a yud without its lower point.
