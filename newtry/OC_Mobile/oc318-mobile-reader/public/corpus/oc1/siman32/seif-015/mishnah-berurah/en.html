(34) After it was written, etc. — We precede with two introductions and then explain, with God's help. First: every letter must have the proper shape for that letter; lacking it, it is invalid, whether it was not written properly initially or later became defective. Second: any letter not surrounded by parchment on all four sides is invalid; this law has two details: (a) specifically if it was not surrounded before writing — but if after writing a hole or tear occurred near the letter from outside and thereby it is not surrounded, it is valid, as explained in Shulchan Aruch seif 16. (b) Most poskim hold one need only be surrounded on the outside of the letters, not inside, except Yerushalmi which is strict. Now we explain Shulchan Aruch: if after writing it was pierced inside, etc. — the reason is that inside does not require surrounding; therefore even if the hole preceded writing it is valid. He mentions "after writing" only lekhatchilah one should not write even if the hole is in the hollow and he does not fill it; but bedieved, if he wrote, even if the hole preceded writing, it is valid and needs no correction.

(35) Inside the heh — and the same for other letters that have three sides with a hollow inside; but a straight kaf, and all the more so vav and the like, are not called "inside."

(36) The entire hollow — even if the piercing touched the letter itself, as long as a thin line remains on the outside it is valid, for there is no measure for letter thickness.

(37) Also inside, etc. — therefore its law is like outside: if the hole inside preceded writing it is invalid; see Taz and Pri Megadim in the name of Levush to be strict like Yerushalmi. For Yerushalmi one requires surrounding on all sides inside as outside; therefore if pierced inside before writing at the letter and thereby not surrounded, one should scrape a little from inside the thickness of the letter's stroke so it becomes surrounded; see Biur Halachah at the end of "but in Yerushalmi."

(38) Pierced — and likewise if some ink was erased without piercing. Specifically regarding heh the Rosh disagrees and validates if anything remains above the hole or below, for he holds there is no measure for the suspended dot; and even if he wrote from the outset a short leg like this it is valid per the Rosh; but for other letters such as chet and the like, the Rosh also admits that the left leg is judged like the right leg.

(39) Require the full measure, etc. — this refers when there is no deficiency from lack of surrounding, such as piercing after writing as below in seif 16 at the end; otherwise it is invalid even if more than a full small letter remains, even per the Rosh.

(40) And this is the halachah — from his words it implies not only stringency like the many poskim, but that the halachah is certainly like them; therefore one must warn scribes greatly, for they err in this. In tefillin and mezuzot that require kesidran, apparently no correction helps even if a child reads it as a letter, since we see it lacks letter-shape — without this it is not called a heh per these poskim, as below in seif 25. Nevertheless it seems one may be lenient through correction, similar to Pri Megadim's leniency regarding a simple kaf made square through correction — see there.

(41) Pierced — and likewise if the letter broke without a hole, or the end of the letter was erased; Shulchan Aruch speaks of length, and the same if part of its thickness broke, whether by piercing or erasure: if a thin black line remains like a vav or thin yod it is valid, for there is no measure for thickness; but for piercing, whether in thickness or length, we require piercing after writing, as below in seif 16 [Magen Avraham and Derech HaChaim].

(42) The right — of the heh. Pri Megadim wrote: it seems to me the same applies to other letters such as dalet, lamed, peh peshutah, tzadi peshutah, kuf, reish, tav, or chet [apparently in chet, whether the right or left leg]; if enough of the right stroke remains like a yod, that suffices — see below siman 36 on letter shapes for lamed, peh peshutah, tzadi peshutah, kuf, and tav.

(43) If remains — meaning from the hole upward; we do not combine what is below the hole even if a neither-wise-nor-foolish child reads it as a letter, since we see the letter's shape did not remain properly, as explained below in the name of Taz. But if enough remains from the hole upward [or from the break upward if the letter broke in the middle] like a small letter, it is valid and need not be shown to a child; the same if he wrote from the outset a short leg like this — it is valid; see Yad Ephraim and Biur Halachah.

(44) Full aleph — meaning yod, and in its lower thorn, per what is ruled below siman 36 in Beit Yosef that yod without the lower thorn is not called yod.
