Engraving. See Taz. Even if a drop fell on a dry letter, the Taz invalidates in Even HaEzer 124, letter 9, and the Beit Shmuel there, letter 11, explains the Levush in this way: if ink fell on a letter and did not invalidate the letter at all, meaning the drop fell precisely on its strokes and it is like the original, then if it dried first it is like casting gold dust in seif 3 here, where scraping helps; see there. It seems that his intent is that while wet, the letter remains, but we rule that writing cancels writing, and the matter does not leave the realm of doubt, as we will say below with the help of Heaven. We require writing for its required purpose, both for a get and certainly for tefillin, where one letter is indispensable, unlike a get, where one can distinguish between the form text and the essential text; see there in Darkei Moshe and Beit Yosef. According to this, in tefillin in such a case, the Mechaber’s wording implies that the letter is not recognizable, meaning that a drop fell inside a beis, samech, or the like, and it no longer has the name of that letter; this too is called not recognizable. See Magen Avraham, letter 23. What is written in Even HaEzer 125:8, that some are stringent even if the letter is recognizable, means that some ink fell on it but its form is still clearly recognizable, and there the reason is because of scraping. In tefillin, however, whenever the form of the letter is clearly recognizable and has not changed, one may scrape, for one may write in a scraped place and upon scraping; see Beit Shmuel and Chelkas Mechokek there. It seems that even if ink fell exactly on the strokes of the letter, one can say that writing cancels writing, and the upper writing was not for the sake of tefillin, for it fell unintentionally, and it is invalid even after the fact, as we have said. See Magen Avraham, letter 23, for another explanation of what is written in Even HaEzer there. Now I have some reflections on this matter, and I will set them out. This also belongs to seif 3.
Know. In Even HaEzer 131:5 and Beit Shmuel, letter 5, ink over ink is not considered upper writing at all, and it does not invalidate her from marrying a kohen, for ink over ink is nothing; the same applies to red dye over red dye. But there are those who say there that we are concerned; see there. Accordingly, if tefillin were written and afterward ink fell unintentionally on the letter while its form remained, or he passed ink not for its purpose over them, we have entered this dispute: according to the Rambam it is valid, and according to the dissenting view it is invalid. Also, if one wrote tefillin in red dye and afterward passed ink over them, at first glance this helps, for in Gittin 19a the Gemara asks about witnesses who do not know how to sign, that one marks with lead and they sign, but writing with lead itself is valid. If so, it is writing over writing, yet the upper writing is not valid writing. For tefillin, ink specifically is required, so red dye is nothing, and afterward ink helps when passed over it for its required purpose. See Beit Shmuel, Even HaEzer 125, letter 2. According to this, in seif 3, if he wrote in gold and passed ink over it, one can say that this helps. See Eliyah Rabbah, letter 5, which I cited in seif 3; see there. See Terumas HaDeshen 228. This requires study.
