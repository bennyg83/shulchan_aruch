Engraving. Taz: and even if a drop fell on a dry letter — invalid. Taz in Even HaEzer siman 124, note 9; and Beit Shmuel there, note 11, explained Levush's words thus: ink fell on a letter and the letter was not invalidated at all — meaning the drop fell on the vav's actual outline and behold it is as at first; then it dried beforehand — it is like throwing gold dust in seif 3 here, where dragging helps; see there. And it appears his intention: while still wet the letter stands — "writing cancels writing" does not emerge from any doubt, as we must say below, there; and we require writing lishmah — both in a get and all the more in tefillin, where one letter prevents; unlike a get, where one may distinguish between tofes and toraf — see there in Dagul Meirevavah and Beit Yosef. And therefore in tefillin in such a case — from the author's language it implies the letter is not recognizable, meaning inside bet and samekh and the like; a drop fell where there is no letter upon it — also called "not recognizable"; and Magen Avraham, note 23 (what he wrote in Even HaEzer 125:8 — some are strict even when the letter is recognizable, meaning some ink fell on it but nevertheless the letter form is recognizable well; and there the reason is because of dragging; unlike tefillin: whenever the letter form is recognizable well and unchanged, one may drag, for they write on the place of the drag and upon the drag; see Av Bet Hillulim and Chayei Adam there). And it appears: even if ink fell on the letter's actual outline — one may say writing cancels writing, and the upper writing not for tefillin, because it fell unintentionally — invalid even b'dieved, as we say. And Magen Avraham, note 23 — another explanation on what he wrote in Even HaEzer there. And here I have reflections on matters I spoke of — I now set them forth (and this belongs also to seif 3):

Know. In Even HaEzer siman 131, seif 5, and Beit Shmuel, note 5: ink upon ink — there is no upper writing at all and it does not disqualify for kehunah at all, for ink upon ink is nothing (likewise sikra upon sikra is nothing); but some say there one is concerned — see there; and if so — he wrote tefillin and afterward ink fell on the letter unintentionally and its form is upon it, or he passed ink not lishmah over them — we come to the dispute: for Rambam — kosher; and for R"Y — invalid. Also he wrote tefillin in sikra and afterward passed ink over them — apparently it helps, for in Gittin 19a it challenges: witnesses who do not know how to sign — they mark with a stylus and sign; behold they wrote with a stylus — kosher; if so it is writing upon writing — no, the upper writing is not kosher writing, and tefillin require ink specifically; if so sikra is nothing and ink afterward helps when he passed lishmah; see Beit Shmuel, Even HaEzer siman 125, note 2. And therefore in seif 3: he wrote in gold and passed ink over it — one may say it helps; and Eliyah Rabbah, note 5, brought what I brought in seif 3; see there; and see Turei Zahav, responsum 288; uncertain.
