If it was perforated inside, the law is, etc. From the fact that the Levush wrote that initially one should not write this way over a hole, it implies that if one transgressed and did so, it is valid. But from the end, where he says, “but if it was perforated after it was written, etc.,” it implies that if it was perforated before it was written and he transgressed and wrote it, it is invalid. The reason may also be as above, because at the time of writing it was not complete. It also resembles the end of seif 16, and so it is implied in Bach, seif 12.
It is valid, etc. It is implied from the Taz that the same law applies to a hole inside the roof of the letter or in the middle of its leg, with ink surrounding it.
But in the Yerushalmi, etc. So ruled the Bach. The Taz wrote that this is specifically when the entire inside was perforated and not only part of it. His reasoning appears to be that all the parchment inside the letter is considered one unit; therefore, even if the parchment inside the letter was interrupted on one side, what remains on the other side protects it.
