Of hand. Be'er Heitev wrote in name of Sefer HaKavanot, and this is his language: one must also beware to write all parashiyot of head or hand consecutively and not interrupt with any speech at all, and all the more in their midst; and if he is old or sick and cannot write all parashiyot together — he writes three first parashiyot and then the fourth; and if that too is hard — he writes the first two parashiyot consecutively at least, and afterward writes the other two together; but between first and second parashah he does not stop for any matter. And if he wrote the first two and stopped before beginning parashat Shema — the quill passes over words "and spoke" of Kadesh and words "and it was" of the second, and afterward writes from Shema onward — and thereby there is some connection. If he already wrote all three at first and stopped — he passes the quill over "and spoke" of the first parashah and "and it was" of the second and "Shema" of the third, then "and it was if listening" — and thereby connection also before he begins to write. He prays and says: "May it be Your will, the Omnipresent our the Omnipresent, that Your Shekhinah rest on the work of my hands and You make me succeed in writing this that I write these tefillin for the sake of the sanctity of the mitzvah of tefillin You commanded us, and save me from mistakes of writing and mistakes of intent, amen, so may it be Your will" — end quote. Pa'aneach — called by poskim Sefer HaKavanot; and it is better to say "may it be Your will" above; and in Matzat Shmurim printed YHR at length, see there; and for brevity the abbreviated form is preferable.
