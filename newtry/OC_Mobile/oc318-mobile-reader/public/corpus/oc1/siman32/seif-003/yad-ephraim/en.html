However, in my opinion it is superfluous, and also the figure of a leg that he cites somewhat does not imply thus. And see Taz, who wrote that one should not emend the heh, for in the heh the law is as a full small letter — see there; and in my humble opinion one can say that the requirement of a full small letter is because otherwise a child does not help, as the decisors state. But nevertheless, even though there is a full small letter, specifically if it is read to a child who is not wise — for otherwise, since he wrote that it is not read to a child, etc., one should invalidate it, for this is not complete writing, as Torah said that the writing should be recognizable to all because of the writing itself. And if it is not read to a child who is not wise, only a wise person senses it in his heart's understanding, it does not help since there is no recognition from the writing itself. One can say that we require both — a full small letter and also a child's reading. And in the Gemara in the case where the heh was broken in the lower part — it seems this was straightforward that the heh would be read to a child, for there was only a thin break in a hole after writing, and the thin hole that joins also what is below, as Beit Yosef stated in the name of Meiri and as Eliyah Rabbah stated. Unlike when the break is a complete break — one must distinguish by means of a child also in the letter heh. And behold, if the leg of the dalet broke and above remained like a full letter, nevertheless one requires distinction by means of a child, as in the letter heh; and likewise if the leg of the chet broke — the law is thus, for even though in the chet a full small letter remained on each side, namely a yod, it does not help merely that there be a leg on it; but nevertheless for it to have the status of valid writing, a child's reading is required. And now that we come to this, it would seem in my humble opinion that there is a scribal error in Magen Avraham "heh, chet, dalet." And he means that Rama came to explain what was stated "one of the letters broke" — namely that in simple letters it broke in the body of the letter, such as vav and zayin, and the word "leg" is not so applicable to them. And again he states that even in letters that are not simple, such as heh, chet, dalet, if the leg in them broke, a child is required. And what is "heh and chet before dalet" is like a sign for the two words of echad, which is a notarikon thus; and what was stated "and similar to it" means kaf peshuta. And in letters that are not simple one also finds it, such as bet or kaf whose sides broke above and below — one must check whether the shape of a house or the shape of a kaf remains on it. However, since Magen Avraham abbreviated this in the essential law, therefore I do not decide this gloss. And if my intent is true in this, it is a wondrous matter.
