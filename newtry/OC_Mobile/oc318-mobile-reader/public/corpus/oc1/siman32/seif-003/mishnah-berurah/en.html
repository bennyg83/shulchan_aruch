(7) In black ink — and ink whose appearance resembles blue; see in Biur Halachah.

(8) From wood smoke — meaning: he should not put gum or vitriol [called in foreign tongue copper water] into it, for lekhatchilah we require writing that can be erased; and through gum or vitriol the writing stands and is not erased — and all this is only for the mitzvah of doing optimally; but by law the Rema also agrees it is permitted to make from nut-gall water and gum and vitriol, and so is the custom today through a mixture of all three and through cooking, for this is better [and as written in responsum Mishkenot Yaakov siman 37]; and see in Magen Avraham who wrote that in his day he did not see even one of the great ones who practiced making ink from wood smoke and oils [but perhaps they made ink from thorns as he concluded in the name of Maharil]; and so ruled in Sefer Get Mekushar and in Berkei Yosef to do as our custom now, for ink made from wood smoke and oils deteriorates and is erased easily — therefore it is not practiced now. Ink made from nut-gall water alone without gum [called in foreign tongue gum arabic] or from vitriol alone is invalid even b'dieved, as the Gra wrote in his Biur below siman 491 — see there; and see in Birkei HaLev. It is permitted to write a Torah scroll with ink made from ordinary wine; and see in Shut, seder katan 4. Ink does not need to be made lishmah.

(9) One letter — our teacher holds the same applies to part of a letter, such as the tip of a yod.

(10) In other kinds of colors — that are not black, such as red and green and the like.

(11) One who scrapes gold lettering — and even though as long as he does not scrape it is invalid, for the upper writing cancels the lower writing, it is not considered that through the scraping the writing is out of order, since the scribe only writes by scraping and the lower writing remains automatically.
