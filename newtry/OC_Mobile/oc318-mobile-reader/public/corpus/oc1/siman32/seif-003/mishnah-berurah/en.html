(7) In black ink. Regarding ink whose appearance resembles blue, see Biur Halacha.
(8) From the smoke of wood. Meaning, he should not put into it gum or copper sulfate, called kuper vasser in the vernacular, because initially we require writing that can be erased; through gum or copper sulfate the writing remains and is not erased. All this is only for the optimum performance of the mitzvah, but as a matter of law even the Rama agrees that it is permitted to make ink from gallnut-water, gum, and copper sulfate, and this is the custom today, to make it from a mixture of all three and by cooking, for this is preferable, as written in Responsa Mishkenos Yaakov, siman 37. See Magen Avraham, who wrote that in his days as well he did not see any of the great authorities accustomed to write with ink made from the smoke of wood and oils, though perhaps they made it from ink of thorns, as he concluded in the name of Maharil. So ruled Sefer Get Mekushar and Sefer Birkei Yosef, to follow our present custom, because ink made from the smoke of wood and oils spoils and is erased easily; therefore we do not use it now. Ink made only from gallnut-water without gum, called gumi in the vernacular, or only from copper sulfate, is invalid even after the fact. So wrote the Gra in his commentary below, siman 691; see there, and see Biur Halacha. It is permitted to write tefillin and mezuzos with ink made from ordinary non-Jewish wine; see Shaarei Teshuvah, seif katan 4. Ink need not be made for the sake of the mitzvah.
(9) One letter. This is not precise, for the same applies to part of a letter, such as the tip of a yud.
(10) With other kinds of colors. Meaning colors that are not black, such as red, green, and the like.
(11) He removes the gold. Even though as long as he has not removed it, it is invalid because the upper writing nullifies the lower writing, the removal is not called writing out of order, since he is not writing, but only removing, and the lower writing remains by itself.
