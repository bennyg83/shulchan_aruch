Writing. See Taz. In the Tur it is written that perfect writing means that each letter must be surrounded by parchment, properly crowned, and not missing the point of a yud; all of this is included in perfect writing. See the Bach. Regarding what was written against the Bach, certainly whenever one does not write but only scrapes, it is not called out of order, and even if it is invalid in that state, it is still not out of order. As I wrote in letter 2, where gold was cast, scraping the gold helps. The matter of the point of a yud is discussed in siman 36 in the Tur and Levush, and its form is as follows: the right leg is somewhat bent, and on the left face there is a tag above, on the face itself, not in the middle, with a small point descending below, shorter than the tag, as in the Levush. It is not necessary to bend the left point, as is clear from Beit Yosef 36. For that reason he bends the right side and keeps the left short, so that it should not resemble a ches. “Bent” means that the tag descends downward as a point. It appears from Beit Yosef 36 that even according to Rashi it is invalid without the left point. In Taz 36 he validates correcting it afterward, and it is not considered out of order, unlike the Levush, who is stringent. Eliyah Rabbah in siman 36, letter 23, agrees that it may be corrected afterward, and similarly the letters that require crowns may be corrected afterward, as in Magen Avraham 36:3. The reason is that whenever the letters are recognizable, it is no longer considered out of order. If so, I wonder why all the yuds of the tefillin should not be corrected with left points; one should be careful about this.
What is written in seif 7, that it is a halacha given to Moshe at Sinai, etc. There are four names: hide, which is not processed; gevil, which is processed and nothing has been scraped from it, but only the required amount has fallen from it; parchment; and duchsustus. See Taz, Yoreh Deah 271:4. There the Rav validates, after the fact, parchment written on the hair side. See Eliyah Rabbah here, 14, that in tefillin it is invalid even with our parchments; see there. A Torah scroll is different. There, in siman 288:6, a mezuzah is initially written on duchsustus, and our parchments are valid for all. One must examine whether a mezuzah written on our parchment on the hair side is valid according to those who are lenient regarding a Torah scroll in 271. I later saw in Beit Yosef, Yoreh Deah 271, that a mezuzah is also valid according to those who are lenient, as with a Torah scroll, for these are valid whether on gevil, parchment, or duchsustus. According to this, gevil written on the flesh side is also valid, and so it appears from Levush, Yoreh Deah 271:10; see there. There is also a fifth type, diftera, which was processed but not with lime and gallnuts; it is invalid for Torah scrolls, tefillin, and mezuzos. See what I wrote in letter 4 regarding processing and scoring lines.
