Writing. Taz: in the Tur he wrote "proper writing" — that every letter be surrounded by gevil and tagged properly, and that the tip of the yod not be missing; all this is included in "proper writing." There is Bach, and he wrote on Bach: certainly whoever writes only by dragging alone is not called "out of order," and even if invalid — nevertheless it is not "out of order," as written in letter 2 — throwing gold helps to drag the gold. The matter of the tip of the yod — see seif 36 in Tur and Levush; its form is like this: [] — meaning, the right leg slightly bent; and on the left of its face a tag above, on its face itself (not in the middle), and a small point descending below, shorter than the tag — Levush. One need not bend its left tip, as shown in Beit Yosef 36; and therefore he bends the right and left short so it should not resemble chet; "bent" means the tag descends with a point, etc. It implies in Beit Yosef 36 that even for Rashi it is invalid without a left point. Taz 36 permits fixing afterward and it is not "out of order," unlike Levush who is strict. Eliyah Rabbah in 36, letter 23, agreed to fix afterward; and so a scribe of the Great Court may fix afterward — Magen Avraham 36:3. The reason: whenever the letters are recognizable, it is no longer "out of order." If so, I wonder about myself — why do they not fix all the yods in tefillin with left points? One should be careful about this.

He wrote in seif 7, halachah leMoshe miSinai, etc. — here are four names: there is hide that is not processed, gevil that is processed, and one does not scrape anything from it — only the measure fell from it — parchment and duchsustos; Taz, Yoreh De'ah, responsum 4. There the Rabbenu Yerucham permits bedi'eved parchment in place of hair; and Eliyah Rabbah here, Yoreh De'ah — that in tefillin it is invalid on our parchments too; see there. And a Torah scroll is different; there, responsum 88, siman 66 — mezuzah lekhatchilah on duchsustos and our parchments are kosher for all. One should examine mezuzah: if he wrote on our parchment in place of hair — is it kosher? There are those lenient in a Torah scroll — responsum 4. Again I saw in Yoreh De'ah responsum 4 in Beit Yosef that mezuzah too is kosher; there are those lenient like a Torah scroll, for these are kosher — whether gevil, parchment, or duchsustos; and therefore gevil in place of flesh is kosher, as Levush writes — Yoreh De'ah responsum 4, siman 66; see there. There is also a fifth — processed papyrus and not with lime on wood — invalid for Torah scroll, tefillin, and mezuzot. And see in letter 4 the law of processing and ruling lines.
