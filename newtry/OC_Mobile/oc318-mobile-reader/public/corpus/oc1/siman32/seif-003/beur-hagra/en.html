Se'if 3, "one shall write them," etc. Yerushalmi, first chapter of Megillah, halachah 4: they should write on hides with ink — Avodat HaGolah.

Se'if 3, "black among," etc. For it says "in ink" — to exclude other colors, such as red and green and the like. Rambam, chapter 1; and as we wrote: even a letter, etc.; and he said "among," etc. — not like Rabbeinu Tam, who forbids gallnut water, as Tosafos write: Shabbos 23a, Megillah 19a, Gittin 19a, and Niddah 20a. But Rosh contradicts himself: gallnut water alone — no; but with gum and the like, even on parchment treated with gallnuts — valid. This is what he meant by "that has in it," etc. He also permits gallnut water, as they proved in the aforementioned Tosafos and Yerushalmi, chapter 2 of Gittin: these sons of Medinach are naked — enough, etc. — they pour on them ink that has no gallnut water; evidently either way it is called ink.

Se'if 3, "and lekhatchilah," etc. Shabbos 23a: "all smoke-producing," etc.; and see Rashi there.

Se'if 3, "he wrote even," etc. Rambam; and Ralbag: that which he said there regarding the Divine Names — the same applies elsewhere, since he wrote not in ink but in passing.

Se'if 3, "if he threw," etc. So explained Nekudot Yosef: what is stated, "the Divine Names," etc. — that he threw gold upon them; and Beit Yosef writes that therefore he singled out Divine Names — for regarding other [writing], one passes gold over, etc., as stated there.
