Seif 3: They must be written, etc. The Jerusalem Talmud, first chapter of Megillah, states that it is a halachah transmitted to Moshe at Sinai that they be written upon hides and with ink. See Be’er HaGolah.
Black, whether, etc. The requirement that they be written with ink excludes other colors, such as red, green, and the like. Rambam writes this in chapter 1, and as the Mechaber continues, even if one letter, etc. The words “whether, etc.” reject Rabbeinu Tam, who disqualifies gall water, as stated in Tosafos, Shabbos 23a, Megillah 19a, Gittin 19a, and Niddah 20a. Rosh, however, refutes his view. Gall water alone is not valid, but when mixed with gum or the like it is valid even upon parchment treated with gall, and is preferable. This is the meaning of “that contains, etc.” The law is also not limited specifically to gall water, as Tosafos prove in the cited passages and as stated in the Jerusalem Talmud, chapter 2 of Gittin: “Those eastern people are exceedingly shrewd, etc.; they pour upon them ink that contains no gall water.” Thus, whether it contains gall water or not, it is called ink.
Initially, etc. Shabbos 23a states, “All types of soot, etc.” See Rashi there.
If he wrote even, etc. This is Rambam. The Gemara’s reference to Divine Names is not exclusive. The same law applies to all letters, since they were written with something other than ink. The Gemara merely discussed the ordinary case.
If he cast, etc. This follows the explanation of Nimukei Yosef, that the statement concerning Divine Names means that he cast gold upon them. Beit Yosef writes that Divine Names were specified because in the case of other words he may remove the gold, as explained there.
