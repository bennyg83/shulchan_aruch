(s.k. 3) No letter shall stick — if the letter is large, etc., it is valid. In Orach Tzedek he wrote this is in Yerushalmi; the reason adhesion disqualifies is because the place of adhesion is as if removed from the letter — therefore if the letter is large so that even if what stuck were dragged away the letter's form would remain, adhesion does not disqualify.

Only one tag stuck to its fellow — this does not mean tags inside a letter stick to each other (that law is Magen Avraham siman 36 s.k. 3); rather two letters are stuck by one tag of one of them. So Peri Chadash, Hilchot Gittin siman 128 s.k. 11 (see there) — it seems Rambam disagrees on the second law in the name of Meiri; Peri Chadash wrote: it does not appear so from Rambam, for they wrote simply adhesion letter-to-letter is invalid, end quote. See Beit Yosef on their discussion of Rambam.

And it seems also in the first law… meaning he only doubted the second law in Meiri's name; Magen Avraham added doubt on the first law from Radach — Orach Tzedek may mean as explained in Yerushalmi, distinguishing adhesion above or below. Orach Tzedek understood he followed Radach in Beit Yosef… Also below seif 18: stuck letters are invalid only by scraping between them; until scraped — invalid; we do not apply "what is fit for soaking." Magen Avraham on kaf peshutah to parchment end — seif 16 invalid; yet Magen Avraham s.k. 27 permits scraping kaf end until mukaf gvil — all this shows not like Radach.
