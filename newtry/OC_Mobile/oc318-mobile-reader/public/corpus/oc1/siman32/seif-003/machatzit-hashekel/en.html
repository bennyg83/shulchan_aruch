(Seif katan 3) That it should not adhere; if the letter is large, etc. It is valid. In Olas Tamid he wrote that this is stated in the Yerushalmi, and he wrote that the reason is that the invalidity of adhesion is because the place of attachment is considered as if removed and missing from the letter. Therefore, if the letter is large, so that even after scraping away the attached part the form of the letter will remain, the adhesion does not invalidate it.
Only when one tag is attached to another. This does not mean that the tags within a single letter are attached to one another, for that law is brought in Magen Avraham siman 36 seif katan 3. Rather, it means that two letters are joined by the tag of one of them. So too it is brought in Pri Chadash to Hilchos Gittin, siman 128 seif katan 11; see there, where it appears that Rambam disagrees. Meaning, he disagrees with the second law brought in the name of Rabbi Menachem Meiri. Pri Chadash wrote there likewise: This does not appear from the words of Rambam and the other decisors, who wrote simply that a letter attached to another letter is invalid. See Beis Yosef, etc., in his discussion of these words of Rambam.
And it appears to me that even regarding the first law, etc. Meaning, Knesses HaGedolah objected only to the second law written in the name of Rabbi Menachem Meiri. Magen Avraham added that even regarding the first law, which he brought in the name of Mahardach, one may say as Olas Tamid wrote above, that this is explained in the Yerushalmi, which distinguishes between attachment above and attachment below. Olas Tamid understood that it referred to the words of Mahardach. See Beis Yosef, s.v. 'Semak wrote, if it fell,' where he brings the Yerushalmi and the responsum of Rashba to explain the Yerushalmi another way. He also wrote that we rule like that Yerushalmi. Also below, in seif 18, it is explained that attached letters become valid only by scraping between them and separating them; as long as the adhesion was not scraped, they are invalid, and we do not say that whatever is fit for mixing is not impeded by lack of actual mixing. This is also explained there in Magen Avraham concerning a final kaf that reaches the end of the parchment, which in seif 16 is written to be invalid; nevertheless Magen Avraham wrote in seif katan 27 that one may scrape a little at the end of the final kaf until it is surrounded by blank parchment. From all this it is clear that the law does not follow Mahardach.
