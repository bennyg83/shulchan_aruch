Se'if 3, he should write them with black ink, etc. This is not specifically tefillin; the same applies to Torah scrolls and mezuzos. So it is in Beis Yosef. This requires study, for here the Mechaber writes without qualification that any kind of ink is valid, provided it is black, which implies even at the outset, whereas in Yoreh De'ah, siman 271, se'if 6, he wrote that ideally one should make it from the smoke of oils, and after the fact other ink is valid. Likewise, Rama here wrote that at the outset one should write with ink made from the smoke of oils, while in Yoreh De'ah he wrote that at the outset one should make it from things that come from a tree. See Pri Megadim, Eshel Avraham, letter 2, who asked this question and left it requiring study; Eliyah Rabbah, letter 4, likewise wrote that it requires study. See Olas Tamid, letter 3.
There in the gloss, and at the outset one should be stringent to write with ink made from smoke, etc. Nowadays scribes do not write with this ink, because it spoils and is easily erased. Rather, at the outset they write with ink made from gallnut water and vitriol, as Maran wrote here without qualification, that any ink is valid provided it is black. See Shach in Yoreh De'ah, siman 271, letter 11, and our words there, letters 15 and 17, where we wrote that the custom is to make ink from gallnut water and vitriol alone, and one should not change, for the custom of our fathers is Torah.
And now. Recently new people have come who are clever about boiling the gallnuts in spirits together with water so that the ink will shine more. It is known that this spirit causes the ink, after a few days, to jump off the parchment, creating separations in the letters and invalidating Torah scrolls, tefillin, and mezuzos, Heaven forbid. If so, one who does this causes the public to sin, and there is no end to his punishment, and he will have to give judgment. Whoever changes has the lower hand, for they permitted only boiling the gallnuts with water, not with something that causes the ink to jump off and invalidate the letters. One who buys Torah scrolls, tefillin, and mezuzos must supervise this, so that he not be in possible suspension from the mitzvah and recite a blessing in vain, and he should warn the scribe lest he be among the few who are deficient in fear of Heaven and deal deceptively with the work of Heaven, placing this bad substance into the gallnut water so that it produces shine, while its end is to produce dust. Therefore one should not buy Torah scrolls, tefillin, or mezuzos from one who does this, and should not pay attention to the shine. To those who listen it will be pleasant, and upon them will come the blessing of good.
Ink. Ink made with an admixture of ordinary gentile wine: some permit writing Torah scrolls, tefillin, and mezuzos with it, Be'er Esek, siman 109; Devar Shmuel, siman 162. Others prohibit it: Maaseh Rokeach on Rambam, chapter 1 of Hilchos Tefillin, halachah 4; Meleches Shamayim, rule 4, letter 3, in Chochmah. The author of Devar Shmuel himself, in siman 164, leans toward stringency, for he wrote as follows: Nevertheless, a sacred matter, and the writing of many mentions of the Name, is more severe; it is proper to sanctify ourselves with what is permitted to us and distance ourselves from ugliness and from anything resembling it, end quote. Keset HaSofer cited him in siman 3, letter 3. See Beis Yosef at the end of Yoreh De'ah, siman 123, Shulchan Aruch there in siman 134, and Yad Eliyahu, siman 35. See Pachad Yitzchak, letter dalet, entry Ink, who wrote in the name of the glosses of Maharar Gael on Shulchan Aruch, Orach Chaim, siman 32, letter 1: I heard that some are careful not to write Torah scrolls, tefillin, and mezuzos with ink bought from gentiles, but only with kosher ink made by a Jew, end quote; see there. Meleches Shamayim wrote likewise there.
