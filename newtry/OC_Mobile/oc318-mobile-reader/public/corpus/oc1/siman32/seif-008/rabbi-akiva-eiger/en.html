Seif 8: and it is good to utter with his lips. It implies l'chatchila one should be concerned for Ravenu Baruch's doubt — perhaps one must say with the mouth that one is doing so for its sake; but b'dieved no. And I have a question: in seif 19 in the hagahah "and some say it suffices," etc. — one may be lenient b'dieved, for it implies b'dieved such intent when writing the divine names considers writing them for their sake, since at the beginning of writing he said he writes for the sanctity of tefillin; but otherwise intent does not help. Also in Yoreh De'ah regarding a Torah scroll the author cited "and he shall say with his mouth" and did not cite the language "and it is good" — it implies by law he must utter with his lips, and it is unresolved.
