Even. See Taz. There is much to consider in the words of the Mechaber here and in the Taz here and letter 14, and I, the poor one, will explain according to my ability with the help of Heaven. See Menachos, HaKometz, 29a, and Rashi, s.v. “that it is not,” s.v. “its leg,” and s.v. “a vav.” Rabbi Akiva Eiger, and see Beit Yosef, who gives two answers. The first says that we do not validate a letter severed by a hole through a small letter or a child’s reading, and if so, why is it not lacking surrounding parchment? The first answer is like Rashi, s.v. “that it is not,” that only adhesion of one letter to another is a problem, but surrounding parchment is not required. According to this answer, there is no place at all for the Yerushalmi’s rule that a hole inside the letter invalidates, for even on the outside it causes no harm except where one letter is attached to another. The second explanation in Rashi regarding the inside of the letter is also difficult, for why mention the hollow; it should have taught the greater novelty that even outside it does no harm. Therefore the Tur follows the Beit Yosef’s second answer, that initially one needs surrounding parchment, but after it was written the writing is not invalidated if a hole developed. There is support for this, for “you shall write them” teaches complete writing and derives surrounding parchment; once it was written validly, it is no longer invalidated. But if we say this is from a halacha given to Moshe at Sinai, as the Bach writes in seif 4, and the proof is that a Torah scroll also requires perfect writing, one can say that it is invalidated even afterward. Nevertheless, one can say it is not a halacha given to Moshe at Sinai, and a Torah scroll derives it from tefillin; see there. Now Rashi’s first explanation of “inside” is the hollow, and regarding tefillin, see the Beit Yosef. For a Torah scroll, one can say it does not require surrounding parchment, or if it is invalid, it has a remedy by suspending above, and this requires study.
And now. Let us come to the Shulchan Aruch. The Mechaber wrote that if, after it was written, it became perforated, then certainly initially, from what he wrote “one may not write,” it follows that if its inside is perforated, even if the hole does not fill the entire hollow, it is forbidden initially. This fits Shabbos 108a, that any hole over which the ink passes is acceptable, implying that otherwise one may not write initially, even if it is inside the letter or perhaps within the thickness of the stroke, which is like inside; see the Taz and understand. But if it became perforated after it was written, this is after the fact and valid, and no correction is needed at all by scraping the thickness of the line to make it valid, as with a final kaf in Magen Avraham 27, for on the inside surrounding parchment does not matter at all, as the second version of the Gemara says, that if its inside was perforated it is valid and needs no scraping at all, even if it was that way initially and he transgressed and wrote, it is valid. But the Yerushalmi requires inside the letter as outside, namely initially, when it was written in invalidity. If so, even after it was written, wherever it is possible to correct it by scraping the thickness of the line so that it is thin and surrounded inside, one should do so. As it appears from the Taz, the Mechaber ruled exactly like the first version in Rashi: lenient regarding the left leg and stringent regarding the inside of the letter initially, even though he wrote this plainly, since he wrote “but” and not “some say.” The Rav, by not adding that the inside does not invalidate even initially and needs no correction, means that initially, if it was written that way, after the fact when he transgressed it is valid. This shows that he rules stringently like both versions. The Taz wrote a new law from the wording of the Tur.
