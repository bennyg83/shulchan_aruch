With gallnuts or with lime. This is indispensable, for without this it is not called parchment but only diftera. He should leave the hide in the lime until the hair falls off by itself, and not by scraping; if he removed it before then, he should not write on it, for it is still diftera and invalid. There is one who disagrees and wrote that if a scribe removed the hides after four days and the hairs had not yet come off them, it appears that after the fact there is no concern, for it does not depend on the hair at all; since it was already placed in lime and properly prepared, it is not called diftera; see there.
With his lips. After the fact, thought is sufficient.
At the beginning. Nothing more is needed, neither to articulate with his lips nor to think that they are being processed for their sake, but only at the beginning. This is so even if the processing continues for several days, for everything one does is done on the basis of the original intent. The beginning of processing is called the moment when he places the hides into the lime, not when he first places them into water, because that is not yet called processing.
For the sake of a Sefer Torah. Its sanctity is more severe than that of tefillin, and the lesser is included in the greater; nevertheless, it is permitted to change it for tefillin or mezuzos, even though their sanctity is lighter than its sanctity. But it is forbidden to change it for a mundane use, unless he explicitly stipulated at the beginning of their processing that it would be permitted to change it even for a mundane use, in which case it is permitted to change it. The proper practice is that scribes should act this way initially when they place the hides into the lime, saying: 'I place these hides into the lime for the sanctity of a Sefer Torah, and I stipulate that when I wish I may change it for any purpose.' They should not follow the common practice of scribes who say, 'for the sake of a Sefer Torah, or tefillin, or mezuzos, or another optional matter,' for then the later authorities are uncertain whether it helps. See Biur Halacha.
Invalid. It helps only for a mezuzah, whose sanctity is lighter than that of tefillin. If he processed it for tefillin, it helps even for a mezuzah, but not for a Sefer Torah. If he processed the hide for the sake of straps, it does not help to write passages on it if he later made parchment from it, for their sanctity is more severe. Even to make hide for the housings from them, one may also say that it is invalid, for their sanctity is more severe than that of a strap.
