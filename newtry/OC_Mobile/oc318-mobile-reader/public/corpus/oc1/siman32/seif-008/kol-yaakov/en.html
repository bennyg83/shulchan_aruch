If he wrote a passage that appears earlier in the Torah last, for example he wrote the passage of Vehayah Ki Yeviacha before the passage of Kadesh, he may write additional passages that are later than it and join them to it, that is, he writes Vehayah Ki Yeviacha, Shema, and Vehayah Im Shamoa and joins them to Kadesh. Likewise, if he wrote later passages first, for example he wrote Vehayah Ki Yeviacha, Shema, and Vehayah Im Shamoa and afterward wrote Kadesh, he may join them to an earlier passage from other tefillin whose later passages became invalid. If he cannot join them, they require burial. GraZ, letter 1. Some say that if he reversed the passages, writing Vehayah Ki Yeviacha before Kadesh intending to place them in tefillin that way, even the passage of Kadesh is invalidated, and similarly with the second and third or third and fourth passages: from the reversal downward, all the passages are invalid and he can no longer join other passages to them. What was written before the invalidating act, however, such as where he wrote Kadesh and Vehayah Ki Yeviacha and then wrote Vehayah Im Shamoa before Shema, remains valid, and only from the reversal downward is invalidated. Chikrei Lev, Orach Chaim, siman 7; Zecher LeAvraham wrote this briefly. It follows from the rabbi's words that the same applies if an error was found in the first or second passage of tefillin: from the error downward is invalid, and one cannot join other earlier-written passages to the final passages, since the latter passages were written in invalidity; there is no distinction between reversing one passage with another and writing after the error, for in both cases it was written in invalidity. This is our practice here in Baghdad, may the Supreme One establish it, that if an error or invalidity is found in the first passage or in the first two, even if he has matching passages that were written earlier, we do not join them to the later passages because those later passages were written in invalidity. But if all four passages were written validly and afterward invalidity occurred in the first passage or in the first two, and he has corresponding passages that were written before the latter ones, they may be joined, as stated in the preceding letter. If one of the earlier passages of head tefillin became invalid through adhesion or separation, and we do not know whether this occurred at the time of writing or after all four passages had been written validly, one may permit joining earlier-written replacement passages to the later passages on account of a double doubt: perhaps the adhesion or separation occurred after all four passages had been written validly, and even if it occurred at the time of writing and the remaining passages were written in invalidity, perhaps the halachah follows the view that head-tefillin passages need not be written in order, as above, letter 3. This double doubt is reversible. In addition, perhaps the halachah follows GraZ. It appears that for hand tefillin as well one may permit them in such a case, for there is an opinion in Mordechai that even with hand tefillin, if they were not written in order, one need not be particular, and Beis Yosef copied it in siman 34, as did the later authorities there. Thus one can make a double doubt in such a case: perhaps the adhesion or separation occurred after all four passages were written validly, and even if it occurred at the time of writing and the remaining passages were written in invalidity, perhaps the halachah follows the view that hand-tefillin passages need not be written in order; furthermore, perhaps the halachah follows GraZ, who does not accept the concept that they were written in invalidity, as he wrote that where earlier passages in the Torah were written last, one may write other later passages and join them to it, and where later passages were written first, he may join them to an earlier passage from other tefillin whose later passages became invalid. This is specifically according to our custom. But in a place that has no custom, one may permit in all cases: whether all four passages were written validly and afterward invalidity occurred in them, whether an error was found in the earlier passages such that the later passages were written in invalidity, or whether the passages were reversed, for example he wrote Vehayah Ki Yeviacha before Kadesh and has a Kadesh passage written before these passages, he may join it to Vehayah Ki Yeviacha; and if Kadesh was written last, he may write the later passages and join them to it. When an error is found in the earlier passages and he has corresponding passages written before these tefillin, he may join them to the later passages by force of a double doubt: perhaps the halachah follows the view that writing in order is not required, and even if the halachah follows the view that it is required, perhaps the halachah follows GraZ, who permits joining in all cases. This double doubt is reversible. Furthermore, perhaps Avodas HaGershuni permits joining in all cases, and the later authorities cited above brought him, leaving Chikrei Lev alone in the matter. Although we do not establish the halachah according to those views, nevertheless for a double doubt we act as Rav HaGaon wrote in Zivchei Tzedek, part 2, siman 110, in the laws of double doubt, letter 158, that we make a double doubt even from a rejected opinion and even against Maran and Rama. However, where there is a custom one should not be lenient, for it is difficult to annul a custom. See Baer Heitev, siman 551, s.k. 7, siman 690, s.k. 15, and Shulchan Aruch, siman 114.
And if. If he wrote the passage of Vehayah Ki Yeviacha before the passage of Kadesh, then even according to our custom, by which we do not join where something was written in invalidity, nevertheless one may permit using the passage of Kadesh by writing the three later passages and joining them to it, since there are those who permit this. Pesach HaDevir, part 2, in Kuntres Acharon, siman 34, in the name of Adnei Paz in responsum, siman 46, was asked such a case and concluded to permit. Likewise, Torah Lishmah, manuscript, cited by the great gaon Rabbi Yosef Chaim in Rav Pealim, siman 4, considered this question and concluded to validate it from the words of Maran in Shulchan Aruch, siman 108, se'if 1, and from what Rabbi Shimshon wrote in Negaim, chapter 14, mishnah 10, and from Mahari Kurkus there; see there. The reason is that they consider this passage of Vehayah Ki Yeviacha as though it does not exist, and the passage of Kadesh remains valid, and therefore he may write the three final passages and join them to it. But the passage of Vehayah Ki Yeviacha may not have a previously written Kadesh passage joined to it, since it was written in invalidity; it is no different from where an error was found in the passage of Kadesh and afterward he wrote Vehayah Ki Yeviacha, in which case according to our custom we do not join to Vehayah Ki Yeviacha a Kadesh passage that had been written earlier, because when an error is found in the Kadesh passage, Vehayah Ki Yeviacha is considered as though it had been written earlier. Torah Lishmah there also wrote that the passage of Vehayah Ki Yeviacha is invalid because it was written in invalidity.
And if. If a scribe has a passage of Kadesh, or the passages of Kadesh and Vehayah Ki Yeviacha, and he wants to write four new tefillin passages, and before he begins writing he has in mind that if Kadesh, or Kadesh and Vehayah Ki Yeviacha, become invalid during writing and he notices only after completing all four passages, he will join those passages that he already has, which were written earlier, in their place, our custom is to be lenient. Since he stipulated concerning them from the beginning, the later passages are not considered written in invalidity.
