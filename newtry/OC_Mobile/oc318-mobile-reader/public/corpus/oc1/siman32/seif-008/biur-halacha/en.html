The parchment must, etc. Processing is a halachah transmitted to Moshe at Sinai, for without this it has only the name hide, and not gevil, parchment, or duchsustus. Pri Megadim. All of the sections stated here regarding processing are only for the passages of the tefillin, and likewise for writing Torah scrolls and mezuzos, aside from the Rambam’s opinion regarding mezuzah, as appears in Yoreh Deah, siman 288, seif 5. Regarding the hide of the boxes, this will be explained below in seif 37.
And it must, etc., be for their sake. Without this they are invalid. If he is in a place where he cannot obtain other tefillin except these, which were written on hide processed not for their sake, although in a Sefer Torah in such a case it appears that he may recite a blessing over it in public, with tefillin he should don them without a blessing; see there in the Pri Megadim. Know further that the Pri Megadim decided simply that processing for their sake is rabbinic, and he brought proof for this from a responsum of the Rambam that is brought in Kesef Mishneh, chapter 1 of Hilchos Tefillin, halachah 11. A practical difference is where one is uncertain whether hide was switched for him with hide not processed for its sake; one could say that a rabbinic doubt is treated leniently. However, from Responsa Rabbi Akiva Eiger, siman 2, it implies that he holds it is biblical. I also saw someone who wrote in the name of the Magen Giborim that all poskim who are stringent regarding processing for its sake hold that it is biblical. Nevertheless, it appears that in pressing circumstances one may be lenient in their doubt, for aside from this there are many early authorities who hold that processing for their sake is not required; see Responsa Mishkenos Yaakov, Orach Chaim, siman 42. This is similar to what was written in Responsa Perach Mateh Aharon, volume 2, siman 101, brought in Birkei Yosef, Yoreh Deah, siman 271. Know further that unspecified processing is like processing not for its sake, Responsa Perach Mateh Aharon, siman 45, for so it appears from Tosafos, Gittin 45, s.v. processing for its sake. In the book Beis Aharon he introduced a new argument, that nowadays, when mostly no other books are written on parchment, only a Sefer Torah, the unspecified case should be like for its sake. This requires examination.
At the beginning of the processing. If only the end of the processing was for their sake, see below in seif 9, where we clarified that the Taz in Yoreh Deah 271, seif 3, holds that it helps, while Perach Mateh Aharon, volume 1, siman 45, disagrees and holds that in practice there is a doubt whether we say that the end proves the beginning. In the Gra’s commentary above in siman 11 he likewise wrote that it is doubtful whether we say that the end proves the beginning; see there. Nevertheless, it seems that one should not be stringent after the fact, since there are many who are lenient. The Gaon Rabbi Mordechai Banet, in his commentary on the Mordechai, is also lenient in this; see there carefully at the end of Hilchos Sefer Torah, s.v. or he assists. In particular, the proof the Taz brings for his words there in Yoreh Deah 271, from the geonim who validate returning even parchment that had been processed entirely not for its sake to a stretched state and applying lime to it for its sake, is certainly surprising in this case. Gloss: all the more according to what I found in the name of Responsa Chavot Yair, who explains that responsum of the geonim, which permits returning processed hide to a stretched state and applying lime to it for its sake, as referring to hide not fully processed. If so, it is explicitly clarified like our words. See the Kuntres Acharon to the Shulchan Aruch of the GraZ. In my humble opinion, it seems that the dak mentioned there in the responsum of the geonim means actual parchment, and so is evident there from the words of the Tur and the Shach at the beginning of that siman. We cannot push it aside by saying that the Tur too is discussing parchment that was not fully prepared, with salting alone, or with salting and flouring but not gall treatment, for the Tur would not have permitted this when there is no possibility, etc., even in pressing circumstances. Know that the Tur and also the Shulchan Aruch omitted there that first law of the responsum of the geonim, who permit even with salting alone in pressing circumstances; it follows that they do not accept it. The reason is that they hold that since this is a halachah transmitted to Moshe at Sinai, as Rambam wrote in chapter 1 of Hilchos Tefillin, one may not be lenient about it. End gloss. Perhaps the Gra’s opinion is also so, as with the doubt whether one must specifically articulate it with his mouth. There too the Gra brings that it is doubtful, and nevertheless they were stringent only ab initio, as is evident from the Tur and Shulchan Aruch, which wrote that it is good to articulate it with his lips. As the Pri Megadim wrote there, so may be said here, and all the more according to the Pri Megadim’s opinion that processing for its sake is rabbinic. What emerges from our words is that if one forgot to put the hides into the lime for their sake, and he remembered during the processing while they still have the status of diftera, he should take them from the lime and put them back into the lime for their sake. This seems clear to me with Hashem’s help. All this is if he thinks for their sake during the act of processing itself. But if he thinks for their sake during the later preparation of the parchment, such as when smoothing it with pumice stone and the like, or during scoring of the sacred texts, or with straps only when blackening them, and he then thinks for their sake, this certainly does not help, as stated by the Taz there in Yoreh Deah, the Pri Megadim, and the consensus of the later authorities. Know further that regarding them we rule that it is not at all necessary that it be for its sake; even regarding mezuzah, where scoring itself is a halachah transmitted to Moshe at Sinai, nevertheless it need not be for its sake. But some later authorities are stringent about the matter regarding scoring, namely the Bach in mezuzah and the Bnei Yonah even in a Sefer Torah, even after the fact. It is proper to take their words into account at least ab initio. See Pri Megadim, Mishbetzos Zahav, s.k. 4, and you will understand all of it. See also Pri Megadim, who wrote further that according to the Bach, even scoring of a Sefer Torah will not help for a mezuzah; but according to what we rule, that a Sefer Torah and mezuzah are equal, it helps. See there further, that scoring of tefillin and mezuzah certainly does not help according to the Bach, and even if one scored them for their sake, it is as if it does not exist. However, we have already concluded that one should not be stringent after the fact. See there in Pri Megadim, who wrote that all this is in an unspecified case, but if one explicitly thinks during the scoring not for its sake, perhaps it is worse according to all opinions.
Or for the sake of a Sefer Torah. See the Mishnah Berurah regarding what I wrote, and not like the practice of the scribes. See there for the reason, that in a biblical matter we rule that bereirah does not apply. According to what the Pri Megadim wrote, that processing for its sake is rabbinic, one may be lenient after the fact. This requires study in practice.
