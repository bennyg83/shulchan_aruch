And it is good to express this with his lips, etc. This implies that by strict law thought alone suffices, and so wrote Responsa Menachem Azaryah, siman 94. However, when non-Jews process it with the intent of a Jew, it does not suffice unless the Jew expresses it with his lips. The Bach, siman 271, wrote that preparing the parchment and scoring do not require being done for their sake.
For the sake of a Torah scroll, etc. There is no lowering of sanctity when one writes tefillin or a mezuzah on it, for as long as nothing has been written on it, there is no sanctity in the parchment itself. This is the Levush. It is astonishing regarding Responsa Avodas HaGershuni, siman 65, who dealt at length with the question of whether it is permitted to write tefillin on parchment processed for the sake of a Torah scroll, and did not see the words of the Shulchan Aruch, Levush, and Beis Yosef. See siman 42. It is implied from the Levush cited above that once some lines have been written on a sheet for a Torah scroll, even if it became invalid there, it is forbidden to write tefillin or mezuzos on what remains, for it is like one who wrapped something in it and designated it. It is also somewhat similar to Yoreh Deah, siman 290, that one may not write a mezuzah on the margin of a Torah scroll. I have written about this at length in the responsa.
