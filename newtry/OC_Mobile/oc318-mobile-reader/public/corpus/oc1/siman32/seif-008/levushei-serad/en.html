(There) He wrote in Hagahos Maimoniyos. These words have no relevance here at all, and one must say that this is a scribal error and that their proper place is in seif katan 11, after what he wrote: 'and after the fact, even if he did not assist at all, it is valid.' About this he cited Hagahos Maimoniyos, that if the Jew himself placed it into the processing, that is sufficient even initially, and he need not assist him again, because placing it into the lime for its sake is the essential processing, and the rest are merely preparations. About this he concluded there in seif katan 11: 'In any event, he must say to the non-Jew, etc.' Meaning, regarding after-the-fact validity, even if he did not assist him at all, it is valid because we rely on the Rosh; nevertheless, we do require the Jew's instruction. Therefore he must tell the non-Jew to place it into the lime for its sake, since this is the essential processing and the other acts are merely preparations. Accordingly, if he did not tell the non-Jew to place it into the lime for its sake, even though he told him regarding his other acts, perhaps it is invalid even after the fact. This is the flow of his words in seif katan 11.
