If the letters of a Divine Name, etc. See the Mishnah Berurah, which wrote that this applies only when they became attached, etc. This is from Noda BiYehuda, Yoreh Deah, siman 169, and as he wrote there, the reason is that perhaps the law follows the Beis Yosef's second answer, that surrounding blank parchment is required only at the time of writing. It appears obvious to me that if one is uncertain when the attachment occurred, he may separate it based on a double doubt: perhaps it happened at the time of writing; and even if you say that it happened afterward, perhaps the law follows the Beis Yosef's first answer above in se'if 16, that with touching there is no distinction. Even according to those who are stringent about a double doubt in the prohibition of erasing, one may be lenient here according to what the Noda BiYehuda wrote there, that one who erases part of a letter while its form still remains is not liable for erasing, and the prohibition is only rabbinic; see there.
