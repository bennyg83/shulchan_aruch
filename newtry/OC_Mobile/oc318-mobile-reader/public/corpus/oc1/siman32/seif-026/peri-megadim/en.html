Taz (and see Tosafot Menachot 32a, s.v. vehaidna — they brought ancient arrangement; and he wrote; and Yerushalmi); and the rule is: between parashah and parashah one requires a measure of nine [letters], and less than that is invalid — whether open or closed. In the form of open and closed there is much dispute; we copy only the dispute of Rambam and Rosh, may he rest in peace; and for both of them the form of open is two, and the form of closed is three. For Rambam, chapter 8 of Laws of Torah scroll, halachah 1: open must begin at the start of the line, and closed always in the middle of the line — and this is the siman. One says: open — two forms: (1) he leaves a space of nine letters at the end of the previous one and begins the second at the start of the second line; or (2) he finishes the previous parashah at the end of a line and leaves a line — space (nine) — and begins open at the start of the third line. The form of closed — three: (1) space in the middle of a line — nine — and he writes that line of the closed [parashah] one word; (2) if there is no space of nine to write one word, he begins to write the closed [parashah] a little in the middle of the line; (3) he leaves space of nine and begins in the middle of the line — he holds "not only this but also that." For Rosh, the form of open is two: (1) he leaves space at the end — nine — and begins open at the start of the second line; (2) he leaves nine and begins in the middle of the line. Closed — three: (2) like Rambam's drawing; (3) if he leaves a whole line as space, he even begins afterward on the third line — at its head it is closed. If so, in arm tefillin (in Shulchan Arukh, Magen Avraham, 19) that we require writing specifically in four columns, not two in one column — how is it done? (see seif 47) To fulfill Rambam and Rosh he suggested making the second form in closed — for Rosh agrees to it whenever there is not a space of nine letters in one place, only by combination; Rosh agrees it is closed; and when Rosh said "he begins in the middle of the line" for open that has before it the space of nine letters in one place — not like this by combination. And he wrote for Rambam: closed — the reason is that there is no space of the [required] measure in one place — difficult for Rambam: whatever does not begin at the head of the line, even if in one place there is more than nine letters — is closed. Also what he wrote for Rambam: the reason in closed — when he placed a space of nine alone and not enough to write one word, which is the measure of closing — therefore when he places in the second a little space, it is by combination — I do not understand him; for it appears Rambam's reason is: whatever does not begin at the start of the line is closed. And whatever the custom our master wrote is correct — that now Kadesh is recognizable that it is at the head, nothing before it like Bereishit (however one who examines Rambam did not count Bereishit — neither open nor closed); VeHayah Ki Yevi'acha and Shema are recognizable from the space of nine letters and they begin at the head of the line; and at the end of Shema less than nine; and VeHayah Im Shamoah too less than nine — and one fulfills all. And it is possible for Rosh too: even though they place space below — nine — whatever does not have nine at its beginning in one place is closed; only one should not do so, as Taz wrote; and at the end of VeHayah Im Shamoah one should not leave nine letters as space, for some say this is a sign of open before it — see Maharam of Padua, 17, who extended. See Eliyah Rabbah in Taz 5; uncertain.

And here. In mezuzah — Yoreh De'ah responsum 88, and in Shach, letter yod — see there; also one may say like Taz: at the end of Shema he leaves less than nine, and VeHayah Im Shamoah begins from the seventh line a little far from the sheet, and by combining together — nine — he fulfills both; and necessarily in mezuzah he must begin the seventh line in the middle to be closed lekhatchilah; if so, it will be by combination and he fulfills all, as stated. Again I looked in Beit Yosef, Yoreh De'ah responsum 88, and found so for Rosh — that he holds three letters is the measure of a parashah; for Maharshal the measure of nine will be divided; and the same for us — nine will be divided. In Menachot 32 it says: "he makes his parashiyot open," etc.; he concludes afterward "even open," and one may say it is a dispute there — some write "on the earth" at the start of a line and some at the end of a line; see there. And Maharam of Padua (the Tur also brought) teaches: nine letters' space before the parashah — properly open specifically, etc.; and we rule "on the earth" at the start of a line, and nevertheless closed — for the space indicates what follows. Also one may see: for Magen David "on the earth" at the end of a line — sometimes there will be free space of nine where there is no parashah — and invalid; Magen Avraham, letter 33.

And in tefillin. Of Rabbenu Tam, that one must write in order (and see seif 34, and Magen Avraham, letter 2) — if so, he writes Kadesh and VeHayah Ki Yevi'acha as required so they be open for all, and leaves space (in Shulchan Arukh) at the end of VeHayah Ki Yevi'acha less than nine, and leaves space for parashah VeHayah Im Shamoah, and writes Shema at the head of the column, at the head of the line; and afterward writes VeHayah Im Shamoah a little in the middle of the line, less than nine; and at the end of VeHayah Im Shamoah he leaves a full space of nine letters — and fulfills all. And Maharam of Padua, Rabbenu Tam — no VeHayah Im Shamoah; and we hold that spaces indicate the parashah that follows.

And know. There is a picture: he leaves a whole line free at the head, and VeHayah Im Shamoah a little in the middle of the line beneath it — and necessarily for Rambam it is closed that begins in the middle, for open is only at the head; and for Rosh a whole line indicates its closing. With this one may say somewhat Taz's words above — only one should not do opposites; and this picture one may say is neither open form nor closed form upon it, as I wrote; understand. And Magen Avraham, letter 48, there — more will be explained. And in letter 49 there — I return to what is written above for Rosh: whoever leaves nine letters' space at the end of a parashah, even if he begins the second parashah in the middle with less than nine — it is open, as proven in Yoreh De'ah, Eliyah Rabbah, in Yerushalmi — open from its end; see there in Beit Yosef and Tur there — it implies that by combination it does not help Rosh to be closed, for he wrote "and he said to him" (who did not leave the measure of space to write words there): leave a line's space and begin on line 3 — and why would he not begin on a line with less than nine by combination? Uncertain; see there.

I found. They explained this that is written in Shulchan Arukh, seif 33: "he shall make the lines equal and beware that there not be three letters" — and bedi'eved not invalid. And sometimes even in one letter and two one may say invalid — at the head of Kadesh, VeHayah Ki Yevi'acha, Shema — for it appears to Rambam, may he rest in peace, this form is closed: whatever does not begin from the head of the line; uncertain in this. Also one may ask: if he left a space of nine and mice or worms ate the place of the space and there is no parchment space at all — is it kosher in such a case or not? And in Shulchan Arukh, such a case warrants much doubt. Now I have not found a clear ruling on this law.
