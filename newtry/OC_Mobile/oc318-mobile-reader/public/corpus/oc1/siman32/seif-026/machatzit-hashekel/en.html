(s.k. 26) Like engraving, etc. Rabbi Moshe wrote, etc. And although Beis Yosef wrote in siman 36 in the name of Rabbi Yitzchak Aksandari that it is invalid, etc., the language of Beis Yosef regarding a final kaf is as follows: if its roof is short, etc. Rabbi Eliyahu Mizrachi wrote that all final letters must be long enough that, if bent, they would become bent letters, end quote. For this reason one should not make corners above on this final kaf, but it should be rounded like a reish, so that if one were to bend it, it would become a bent kaf, for the only difference between them is that this one is final and this one is bent, end quote.
And Rabbi Yehudah Leib wrote there that even according to the Rosh, who forbids cutting out, etc., as stated in Yoreh Deah, siman 275, seif 8, in the gloss.
And he should write the Name in its place. In Sefer Tosefes Shabbos he wrote that “the Name” means the word, as the Shach wrote in Yoreh Deah, siman 276, s.k. 4; and he wrote well.
But Tosafos in Eruvin, etc. And Sifsei Daas in Yoreh Deah, siman 275, s.k. 3, wrote in the name of Maharlbach as follows: all the more so it is forbidden to remove the thin peel from the parchment, for then it appears more disgraceful and is close to erasure, etc., end quote. And Beis Yosef, in his book Bedek HaBayis, wrote at the end of siman 276 in the name of the Rashbatz, who was asked whether one may peel off the Name like duchsustus and bury it. He answered that it appears that initially this was ruled against, lest the peel not be whole and one come to erasure, etc. Furthermore, the ink is absorbed into the parchment and a mark remains, so that the upper Name would be severed, with half above and half below, etc. Even not in the place of Divine Names, it might be prohibited because of writing on the place of the parchment, for we have not found, etc., with a peel, only scraping and erasure, etc. He wrote at the end that he did not protest against scribes who peel, meaning not in the place of a Divine Name, etc. See there.
