There. It must be that he holds like the first answer. Meaning, the Tur holds like the first answer, that there is no basis at all to distinguish between whether it was written in validity or not. Rather, according to the Bavli, we are particular only about one letter adhering to another, but a hole does not matter. Therefore the Tur properly formulated this as a dispute: according to the Bavli, the only invalidation is adhesion of letters, but not a hole, even if the hole existed before the writing, since there is no basis at all to distinguish between whether it was written in validity or not. According to the Yerushalmi, which requires surrounding blank parchment even within the letter, it is invalid even after it was written. The Pri Megadim erred here, and the Noda BiYehuda, first edition, siman 75, also erred, for he understood the Taz's statement that he holds like the first answer to mean that the Yerushalmi holds like the first answer. Therefore he wondered, for according to the first answer there is invalidation only in adhesion of one letter to another, and there is no room for the Yerushalmi's law; see there. This is a mistake. The Taz means that the Tur holds like the Beis Yosef's first answer, namely according to the Bavli, which validates when it was severed. Therefore the Tur made it a dispute, because the Yerushalmi does not distinguish between adhesion of letters and a hole, since it requires surrounding blank parchment even inside the letter. This is clear.
