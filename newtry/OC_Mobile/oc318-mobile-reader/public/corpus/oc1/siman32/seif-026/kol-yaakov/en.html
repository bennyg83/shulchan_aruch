Rema MiFano wrote. In siman 38, he reports an incident involving one whose hand was amputated, who held the quill with his lips and wrote Torah scrolls, tefillin, and mezuzos, and he invalidated them because this is not the normal way of writing. Olas Tamid, letter 17, cites this and writes that the same applies to anything similar, for it is not the way of writing; even if there are no others, one should not recite a blessing over tefillin written in this manner. This implies specifically that one does not recite a blessing over them, but one may lay them without a blessing if it is impossible to find others. But Magen Avraham, s.k. 5, wrote in the name of Rema MiFano that he invalidated them even if it is impossible to find others. So too wrote Halachah Berurah, letter 5; GraZ, letter 7; and Keset HaSofer, siman 3, letter 7. Their words imply that if it is impossible to find others, he should not lay them even without a blessing. See Chiddushei Rabbi Akiva Eiger, who wrote concerning Magen Avraham's words: I did not find this there, but rather Rema MiFano learned this law from the law of writing with the left hand; since we maintain that if one wrote with the left hand and there are no other tefillin, they are laid without a blessing, one can say that the same applies to writing with the mouth; see there. See Eliyah Rabbah, letter 10.
