From one piece of leather. Regarding pieces of leather sewn together, the Magen Avraham tends to say that they are considered one piece of leather. So too, Responsa Chasam Sofer, Orach Chaim, siman 5, writes that one may be lenient with pieces that are sewn together; if they were only joined with glue, he is stringent there. However, Chayei Adam implies that the same applies when they are joined with glue, and so it also appears from the Or Zarua, and this is now the widespread custom in our countries. Nevertheless, initially it is proper and correct to make it from an actual single piece of leather, for some are stringent and hold that sewn-together pieces, or pieces glued together, do not help. See Chayei Adam, who writes that in any case the four housings must be separated from one another and not glued together. It should not be like what scribes do, gluing them with glue while merely making marks above like a groove, for it requires study whether this may be validated even after the fact. For whichever way you look at it, if by gluing we consider it one piece of leather, then we have only one housing containing four partitions, which is invalid; and if the glue does not make it one piece of leather, and there are here four housings with four grooves because the glued part is deemed separate, then the tefillin themselves were made from two pieces of leather and are invalid. In truth, it is good that even those who make them from an actual single piece of leather have their tefillin separated, lest the gluing be considered a connection; and the tefillin of the Gra were separated. See Biur Halacha.
