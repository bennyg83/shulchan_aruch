He should make four compartments, etc. See Magen Avraham in the name of the Bach, that through sewing it is not considered one piece of leather. See Machatzis HaShekel, that the same applies to gluing according to the Bach. In the glosses of Rabbi Chaim Sanzer, he wrote that gluing is effective according to all opinions, while in Chasam Sofer, which I cited in the Mishnah Berurah, he wrote the opposite. According to what is found in the Or Zarua it appears that the two are equal, as will be explained later. Machatzis HaShekel wrote that the same applies to compartments made from one piece of leather, but strips are cut from it while they remain attached in the middle, and then the strips are joined together again, thereby forming the compartments: according to the Bach, they are invalid, since at any rate they are cut on every side of the compartment, and in his view sewing, and likewise gluing, does not create a connection. However, the Pri Megadim wrote in s.k. 52: It is proper to make everything from literally one piece of leather; after the fact, the scribes cut above and below while leaving it attached. It appears to me to bring proof for the scribes that this is called one piece of leather and is not included in "two or three memorials." The entire force of those who are stringent and hold that one piece of leather is indispensable is because perhaps tefillin are comparable to a get. Regarding a get, Even HaEzer, siman 130, se'if 7, in the gloss, rules that gluing is a possible invalidation because the Torah said "one scroll," not two or three scrolls; the same would apply in our matter. Yet regarding gittin themselves, the Gemara on folio 20b concludes that where it remains attached it is permitted, because it is still called one scroll. Therefore it appears that here, according to all opinions, sewing or gluing helps, and it is not like the case Machatzis HaShekel cited at the beginning of siman 33 in the name of Mordechai, for there it remained that way without repair. I found in Pischei Teshuvah on Orach Chaim at the beginning of siman 33 that he too inclines this way, though he did not bring our proof. Accordingly it is obvious that even if a small hole was made in the leather at the place of the square, as commonly happens, nevertheless the name of one piece of leather is not lost through this, and even if the square was thereby spoiled, later repair helps.
From one piece of leather. See the Mishnah Berurah, where I wrote that there are those who are stringent that it is not considered a connection. This refers to the Bach, and likewise Sefer Biur Mordechai and Responsa Rabbi Akiva Eiger, siman 11, incline to say that it is uncertain whether gluing is a connection. Their proof is from the Rama's ruling in Even HaEzer, siman 130, in the gloss, in the name of Or Zarua, regarding a get, that it is a doubtful divorce; presumably the same applies in our case. But now that we have merited the light of the great Or Zarua printed in our days, I found in siman 176 that if one wrote tefillin on two pages on one piece of leather, and even on two pieces of leather and joined them together either by sewing or by glue, it is valid. There too it is in the name of Or Zarua. It must be that he distinguishes between a get and tefillin. Afterward I searched further and found in this siman in Darchei Moshe that he cites Or Zarua as follows: Or Zarua wrote that if one wrote tefillin on two pages on one piece of leather, and even on two pieces of leather and glued them together, it is valid. Yet there in Even HaEzer 130, se'if 7, the Rama in his Darchei Moshe and in the Shulchan Aruch cites Or Zarua regarding a get, that it is uncertain. It must be that there is a distinction between a get and tefillin, or that there, because of the stringency of a married woman, Or Zarua did not want to rely on the proofs he brought in siman 176. Rashi in Megillah 8b also seems, at first glance, to imply that sewing with sinews helps for tefillin and mezuzos, and this must concern the passages, for if it concerns sewing the tefillin themselves after the passages have been placed inside, what relevance would mezuzah have? Perhaps, however, Rashi mentioned mezuzah not precisely. But in Ritva there in Megillah I found explicitly that sewing two skins helps both for tefillin and for mezuzah. Certainly in our matter, making the compartments is no worse than the passages themselves, for which joining helps, as is proven in Biur HaGra. Therefore, although in Yoreh Deah, siman 288, se'if 4, the Mechaber ruled anonymously like the Rambam that sewing does not help for mezuzah, nevertheless one who makes the compartments by sewing or gluing has authorities upon whom to rely: Sefer HaTerumah, who concludes that it is only good and proper to make them from one piece, the great Or Zarua cited by the Darchei Moshe as law in this siman, the Ritva in Megillah, and also according to one answer of Tosafos in Menachos. It may be that even the Rambam, who holds that sewing does not help for the passages of a mezuzah, nevertheless agrees regarding making the compartments, that joining them by sewing is not considered two or three memorials. In my humble opinion, one could further say that when the Torah excluded "one memorial" and not two or three memorials, it excluded only making four compartments from four skins, each skin entirely by itself, meaning the compartment with its titura for one passage, and so for each passage, so that each compartment is a separate memorial for the person. That is what the Torah excluded. The intent of Chazal in saying "one leather" is that all the compartments be fixed in one leather, and that each compartment not be an independent pouch. This is somewhat implied by the wording of the Mechilta. But this does not seem to follow from the Rishonim; for if so, even without sewing one compartment to another, merely fixing them in one titura would be considered one memorial, and no posek mentions such a leniency. On the contrary, Rashi and the other poskim each depict making it from literally one piece of leather, which indicates that once the Gemara mentions one leather, they hold it must be literally one leather, or at least joined by sewing according to the other poskim. The Gra in his commentary to Kelim, chapter 18, also wrote that sewing can make a connection, although in his commentary in this siman he seems somewhat inclined to stringency. See Kovetz on the Rambam, who discusses this from a baraisa in Gittin, where it is a dispute between Rabbi Shimon ben Elazar and the Rabbis, and the law follows the Rabbis that it is not considered a connection. See Yeshuos Yaakov in the gloss of his grandson, who wrote about this. One may also reject that proof, for it is an anonymous mishnah against a dispute in a baraisa, and the law follows an anonymous mishnah; this requires study in the rules of halachah. Nevertheless, initially, one who can do so should certainly be stringent and make it from one piece of leather in order to satisfy all concerns in a biblical positive commandment.
