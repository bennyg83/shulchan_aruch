There, seif 6. The minimum amount of food that requires Bircas HaMazon is a kezayis. The Gemara records a dispute whether this obligation is Torah-level or Rabbinic. See my work on Megillas Rus, in the additions from the year 5611, on the verse, “She ate, was satisfied, and left over,” where I explained the basis of their dispute and connected it to the uncertainty raised by Mishneh LaMelech, Hilchos Chametz chapter 1, regarding the principle that eating is measured by a kezayis. Examine it carefully. Regarding the ruling that the minimum amount requiring Bircas HaMazon is a kezayis, were I not hesitant I would suggest a novel explanation. Berachos 20b states that Rabbi Avira expounded: Hashem said, “I wrote in the Torah, ‘You shall eat, be satisfied, and bless,’ yet Israel is stringent with itself to recite Bircas HaMazon even for a kezayis or a kebeitzah.” Rashi explains that a kezayis follows Rabbi Meir and a kebeitzah follows Rabbi Yehudah. This is difficult, for we rule that a kezayis suffices, so why mention the rejected view? Based on what I wrote in Sefer HaChaim, siman 486, that food lodged between the teeth does not combine for a prohibition but does combine for a mitzvah, one might say as follows. Rabbi Meir’s measure of a kezayis applies to mitzvah eating, such as matzah, because even what remains between the teeth combines to complete the mitzvah measure and is therefore also sufficient for Bircas HaMazon. Rabbi Yehudah would require a kebeitzah even there. Rabbi Meir, however, may agree that with ordinary, non-mitzvah eating, food between the teeth does not combine, and one therefore needs a full kezayis apart from what remains between the teeth. Since more than a kezayis is then required, the measure is set at a kebeitzah, which certainly leaves a kezayis actually swallowed. The Gemara could thus mean that Israel is stringent to recite Bircas HaMazon for a kezayis when eating matzah and for a kebeitzah when eating voluntarily. Yet the poskim do not rule this way. It therefore appears that the Gemara means something else. Elsewhere the Gemara says regarding the response Baruch Hashem HaMevorach that one need not place himself in a dispute. This shows that even where the halachah follows one view, it is proper, where possible, to satisfy all opinions. See Taz, siman 167. Accordingly, regarding the wording of the blessing HaMotzi, the halachic novelty is evident from the blessing itself, and it is appropriate to demonstrate that novelty. But regarding the amount eaten, the blessing does not reveal how much one consumed, so there is no gain in displaying a novelty. It is therefore preferable to satisfy all opinions. Thus, although a kezayis technically requires Bircas HaMazon, the scrupulous eat a kebeitzah so as to satisfy Rabbi Yehudah as well. The Gemara describes two forms of scrupulousness. First, although the Torah says, “You shall eat, be satisfied, and bless,” they recite Bircas HaMazon even for a kezayis. Second, even though a kezayis would suffice, they are stringent to eat a kebeitzah so as to satisfy all views. This is correct. The matter may be explained further by noting the precise wording, “May Hashem lift His face toward you.” If it meant simply that Hashem should show favor, it should have said, “May Hashem lift your face,” as it says regarding Lot, “I have lifted your face,” and regarding Iyov, “Hashem lifted the face of Iyov.” Why does it say “His face”? Even when one shows favor, the degree of favor shown to a poor person dressed in torn clothing is not the same as that shown to a wealthy person dressed in fine garments. The favor corresponds to the dignity of the petitioner. Likewise, when a person merits, he bears the Divine image, and when he does not merit, he lacks it. The blessing therefore asks for two favors. First, “May Hashem lift His face toward you,” meaning that He should grant you a dignified countenance and the Divine image. Then He should lift your own face and show you favor fitting for one whose countenance is honored. The ministering angels therefore asked: Hashem wrote in the Torah that He does not show favor, implying that He grants not even one form of favor, yet the verse asks that He perform two forms of favor. Hashem answered that Israel is stringent to the level of both a kezayis and a kebeitzah, thus performing two forms of self-imposed favor, and therefore He too performs two forms for them. This also reconciles the passage with Niddah 70b, which answers differently, distinguishing between Israel and the nations. Both answers are needed: one form of favor derives from the distinction between Israel and the nations, and the second from their stringency regarding a kezayis and a kebeitzah. According to this, the phrase “a kezayis and a kebeitzah” may indeed be understood as Rashi explains, a kezayis according to Rabbi Meir and a kebeitzah according to Rabbi Yehudah. Examine this carefully.
