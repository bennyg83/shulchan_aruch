(16) Until when, etc. This refers to the case mentioned above, where one forgot and did not recite Bircas HaMazon.
(17) As long as he is not hungry. After that point, the original eating is considered to have ended and he has lost the opportunity to recite Bircas HaMazon for it. If he now wishes to eat again, Magen Avraham holds that he must recite HaMotzi again even if he never diverted his attention, because once the food was digested he also lost the original blessing. Many Acharonim disagree and hold that the original blessing remains valid so long as he did not divert his attention in the interim.
(18) Because of that eating. This appears to exclude a case where he estimates that the first food alone had already had sufficient time to be digested and he would have become hungry again, but in the meantime he ate other foods and therefore is presently not hungry. If he then remembers that he did not recite Bircas HaMazon after the first eating, he may no longer do so, because that eating has already ended. This applies where, after finishing the first eating, he diverted his attention from eating more. If, however, everything occurred during one continuous meal, as is common at large meals where people sit for many hours and the original bread may already have had time to digest, they may nevertheless recite Bircas HaMazon at the end. Since they continued eating side dishes, pastries, and drinking throughout that period, it is all considered one meal, and the original food is treated as though it had not yet been digested.
(19) Those fruits. This is not literal. Rather, if he has no appetite for any fruit, that is a sign that the food has not yet been digested in his stomach.
(20) If he does not know, etc. All this applies when he ate as much bread or fruit as he desired. If he ate only a small amount and wished to eat more but had no more food, it is very difficult to determine the measure of “as long as he is not hungry,” since he still had an appetite immediately after eating. Many Acharonim therefore write that in such a case the limit is the time needed to walk four mil, namely seventy-two minutes. Until that time he is obligated to recite Bircas HaMazon, since even a small amount certainly has not been digested within so short a period. Some Acharonim incline to the view that if he ate only a small amount, delayed before reciting Bircas HaMazon, and cannot determine whether the food has been digested, then even within four mil he should eat at least another kezayis of bread and afterward recite Bircas HaMazon. He need not recite HaMotzi again over that bread if he did not divert his attention from eating more. Nevertheless, if he has no bread, he may rely on the first view and recite Bircas HaMazon until four mil have elapsed. See Biur Halachah.
