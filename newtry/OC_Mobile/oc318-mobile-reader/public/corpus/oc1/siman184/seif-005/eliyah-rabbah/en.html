Its measure is as long as, etc. I found that Abudarham, folio 130, concludes that after a small eating the time is the amount needed to walk four mil. Sefer Zikaron by Rabbi Yishmael HaKohen writes likewise. This is explicit in Berachot 53 as explained by Rashi and Tosafot. Although the Gemara states this according to Reish Lakish, we can infer that the dispute between Rabbi Yochanan and Reish Lakish concerns only a substantial meal. If Rabbi Yochanan's standard of 'so long as he is not hungry' also applied to a small eating, Reish Lakish should have framed his four mil measure as a disagreement with Rabbi Yochanan. Evidently Rabbi Yochanan agrees in the small eating case. Yet this creates a difficulty: why did the Rif, Rambam, Rosh, and all the poskim omit Reish Lakish's rule? This requires study. I later saw that the Taz rules that if one ate only a kezayit of bread and remains hungry, or ate another food and still desires more, the time limit is four mil.
