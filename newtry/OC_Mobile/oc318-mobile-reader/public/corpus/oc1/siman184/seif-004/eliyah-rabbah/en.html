He must repeat, etc. This includes even the fourth blessing, HaTov VeHaMeitiv, so that it not be treated lightly. Magen Avraham, Olat Tamid, and Knesset HaGedolah write this in the name of Rabbenu Shimshon and Rabbi Yitzchak HaLevi. I found, however, that Lechem Mishneh, chapter 2 of Hilchot Berachot, leans toward not repeating the fourth blessing. In truth this requires study because in a doubt concerning blessings one should be lenient. It is not comparable to answering Amen after Boneh Yerushalayim at the beginning of siman 188. Moreover, Responsa Maharshal, siman 44, writes that nowadays the concern of treating it lightly does not apply. Perhaps, however, the original enactment itself established the repetition, like the blessings of Keriat Shema above in siman 63. The matters can be distinguished, and this requires study. The authorities further write that if one did not eat to satiation, he need not repeat Birkat HaMazon. But I found in Sefer HaChinuch on Parashat Eikev that even one who ate less than satiation is obligated to bless, provided he ate a kebeitzah.
