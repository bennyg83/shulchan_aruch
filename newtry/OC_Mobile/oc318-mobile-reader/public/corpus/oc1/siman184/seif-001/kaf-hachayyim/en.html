1) [Seif 1] One who ate in one place must recite the blessing before leaving, etc. This applies when he leaves for another place, but moving from one corner to another is not considered a change of location, even in a large hall, as stated above in siman 178, seif 2, and in our words there, paragraphs 10 and 3; see there. Magen Avraham in this siman, paragraph 1, and Shulchan Aruch HaRav, paragraph 1, write the same. This applies when both corners are in one room, but one should not move from one room to another, as stated in our words there, paragraph 2; see there. Permission to move from one corner to another in one room applies specifically when there is some need. Without a need, he should recite the blessing specifically in the place where he ate. See Chayei Adam, rule 47, paragraphs 5 and 6.
2) Magen Avraham writes there that the same applies when he can see his original place, for this is not considered a change of location. See, however, our words above in siman 178, paragraph 4, where we cited a dispute about this and wrote that initially one should be careful; see there.
3) Magen Avraham writes further there that if one ate in one room and intended to recite the blessing elsewhere in the same house, this intention may be effective. One may rely upon this when the original place is not clean. Eliyah Rabbah, paragraph 2, and Shulchan Gavoah, paragraph 1, cite this. This means that if he cannot eat in the clean place and must eat in an unclean place, in such a case his intention to recite the blessing elsewhere in the same house, in a clean place, is effective. Machatzis HaShekel. See our words above in siman 178, paragraph 23.
4) There: If he acted intentionally, he must return, etc. If he is far away and the food will become digested before he can return to his place, he should recite the blessing where he is. Magen Avraham, paragraph 2; Eliyah Rabbah at the end of paragraph 2; Shulchan Aruch HaRav, paragraph 1; Chayei Adam, rule 47, paragraph 10; Derech HaChaim, paragraph 1.
5) There: If he recited the blessing in the place where he remembered, he fulfilled his obligation. Gloss: This applies specifically according to the Rambam, but according to Rosh, even after the fact he did not fulfill his obligation. See Taz, paragraph 1, who discusses this at length and concludes that as a practical matter we must rule leniently in cases of doubt concerning blessings. After the fact, in every case, he need not recite the blessing again if he already recited it in the second place. If he has not yet recited it, however, he should return to his original place even if he left unintentionally, provided that this is possible and he is not prevented by the group with whom he joined; see there. Eliyah Rabbah, paragraph 1, similarly cites several authorities who hold that after the fact he fulfilled his obligation, and concludes that anyone who acts stringently in this matter is among those whose conduct is astonishing and recites a blessing in vain; see there. Birkei Yosef in Shiyurei Berachah, paragraph 2, Shulchan Aruch HaRav there, Chayei Adam there, and Derech HaChaim there rule likewise.
6) There: According to Rabbeinu Yonah, etc. This is a scribal error, because Rabbeinu Yonah agrees with the Rambam. Meil Shmuel in his glosses on the Rambam; Birkei Yosef in Shiyurei Berachah, paragraph 2.
