And he should bless, etc. The Tur and Shulchan Aruch conclude, 'provided that he is not hungry from the first eating.' The Bach writes that some versions of the Tur read, 'provided that he is not satiated from the first eating,' meaning that if he were already satiated, eating at the second place would not help. The Bach says that version is erroneous because any eating counts as eating so long as he is not disgusted by food, no worse than eating the Pesach offering and afikoman. Birkat Avraham, folio 168, quotes this version and explains it as meaning extremely satiated. In my humble opinion it is therefore too strong to erase this reading entirely. I would preserve it by reading, 'provided that he is satiated from the first eating.' Better still, both versions can be maintained together: 'provided that he is neither hungry nor satiated from the first eating,' meaning that so long as he is not hungry he is considered satiated, as the Levush writes in seif 5. I found this reading in Raavan, siman 188.
