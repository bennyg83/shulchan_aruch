The night of Rosh Hashanah, etc. The custom is to pray from the siddur even though they have not yet recited the blessing Borei Meorei HaEish. Birkei Yosef wrote that this requires analysis according to what he wrote in Birkei Yosef, siman 693, and the later authorities hold that one may not benefit from the light before the blessing; see there. However, there too, regarding Megillah reading, they are not accustomed to say the blessing over the light first; see there. Here, in my humble opinion, there is more room to be lenient, for this is not complete benefit, since part of it is fluent in his mouth. Nevertheless, initially it is proper for one who is fluent in the prayer by heart to do so. However, it appears that as soon as he recites Havdalah in the prayer, that is enough, since in any case he says, Who separates between light and darkness, even though he has not yet recited the blessing over the creation of fire. So is the common practice, that on Motzaei Shabbos people pray from a book by candlelight. However, some do not practice so, and therefore they do not say Veyiten Lecha, because it is not fluent by heart. They also recite Havdalah immediately after prayer in order to precede themselves to the blessing over the light. One who is stringent may be stringent for himself, but one should not rule this way for others.
