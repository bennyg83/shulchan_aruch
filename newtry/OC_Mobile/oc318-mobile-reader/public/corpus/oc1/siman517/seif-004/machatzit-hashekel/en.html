(s.k. 4) To take, etc. To call the non-Jew to his house: that is to say, when the Jew is forbidden to move the money on Yom Tov, therefore he calls the non-Jew to his house and shows him the money, and the non-Jew himself takes the money due him.
And it appears to me in a place where the appraisal, etc. It is permitted to take it without giving money. And likewise it is permitted to mention that [amount of] money, such as "give me for a perutah" or "for two perutos," for this is not naming a sum of money in the manner of buying and selling; rather he informs the seller how much he needs. Which is not so when the appraisal of the bread is not known: one cannot say that he intended to inform him how much he needs, for even if he says "for a perutah" or "two perutos," [the seller] still will not know how much they will give him and whether it will suffice for him. And all this is explained above in siman 323; and see there further details of the laws on this.
