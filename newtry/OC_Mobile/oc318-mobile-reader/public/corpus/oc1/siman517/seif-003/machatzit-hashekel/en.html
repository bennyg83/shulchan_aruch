(s.k. 3) Whose majority, etc. And it appears to me that if a Jew bought, etc., without a seal, and even if he sealed it and the next day found that the seal was spoiled and it appears that it was done deliberately by a person, even so it is permitted, as written there in Yoreh De'ah, since he does not benefit from the exchange, for it is as fine as the first, and we are not concerned that he intends to cause a stumbling. Only in the case of the Maharil, where it was flour of a non-Jew and a Jew had not yet bought it, is there reason to fear an incidental exchange without intent to cause a stumbling. Which is not so when a Jew has already bought: he will not exchange if he has no benefit. And in the book Chemed Moshe he challenged the Magen Avraham, and wrote that since he is a shopkeeper, even if it is as fine as the first, still there is reason for exchange without intent to cause a stumbling: rather, people came to buy flour and his own flour that he had was not enough, and he sold the Jew's [flour]; and afterward he ground today and gave those [bags] to the Jew in place of the Jew's first flour; see there.
