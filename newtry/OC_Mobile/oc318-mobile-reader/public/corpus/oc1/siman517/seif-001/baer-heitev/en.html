Outside. Meaning: not for the sake of a Jew.
Whose majority. That is, that most of those who buy bread and flour are non-Jews; but if most who buy bread and flour are Jews, it is certainly forbidden to take flour unless it is known that it was ground yesterday (Taz). And the Maharil sealed the flour on erev Yom Tov with the non-Jew, and once he found the seal spoiled and forbade taking the bread; and the Magen Avraham wrote: and it appears to me that if the Jew bought flour from erev Yom Tov and left it with the non-Jew without a seal, and on Yom Tov he sees that it is good and fine like the first, it is permitted, for we are not concerned that the non-Jew exchanged it if he does not benefit from the exchange, as written in Yoreh De'ah siman 118; and see siman 515, s.k. 1, what is written there.
Bread. However, it is forbidden to call the non-Jew to his house and give him the money, for that is weekday-like activity; and even through a non-Jew it is forbidden, as written in se'if 2 (Yam Shel Shlomo). And it appears to me that in a place where the appraisal of the bread is known and there is no need to set a price, it is permitted to take it without giving money, as written in siman 323 (Magen Avraham).
