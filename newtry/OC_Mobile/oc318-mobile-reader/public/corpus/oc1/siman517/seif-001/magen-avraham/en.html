Outside the techum. Specifically in a city whose majority are non-Jews; see siman 515, se'if 7.
Flour that was ground today. And there is no [problem] of muktzeh, for wheat is fit to chew or to make roasted grain from it (the Rosh, Ran, and Tosafos). And if you say, if so it should be permitted to send grain on Yom Tov (and see siman 516): one can say that people are not accustomed to make human food from it. And it appears to me that even rye and barley are not muktzeh, even though regarding the beracha they are not considered things eaten raw, as written in siman 208, se'if 4; nevertheless regarding muktzeh they are significant, for even raw meat is not muktzeh because it is fit to chew, all the more so these; and furthermore, for they are completed by human hands. And see siman 325, se'if 4, that some say flour is muktzeh; and one must say that the Mechaber does not hold that reason, but rather [it is] a decree lest one tell a non-Jew to do it for him; and regarding a shopkeeper they did not decree.
Whose majority are non-Jews. And in a city whose majority are Jews it is forbidden to take flour from the non-Jew. And the Maharil sealed the flour on erev Yom Tov with the non-Jew, and once he found the seal spoiled and forbade taking the bread, end quote. And it appears to me that if a Jew bought flour or fish on erev Yom Tov and left it with the non-Jew without a seal, and on Yom Tov he sees that it is good and fine like the first, it is permitted, for we are not concerned that the non-Jew exchanged it if he does not benefit from the exchange, as written in Yoreh De'ah siman 118.
To take bread from him. However, it is forbidden to call the non-Jew to his house and give him the money, for that is weekday-like activity (Levush; Rabbeinu Yeruham; and Beis Yosef). And even through a non-Jew it is forbidden to buy, as written in se'if 2 (Yam Shel Shlomo). And it appears to me that in a place where the appraisal of the bread is known and there is no need to set a price, it is permitted to take it without giving money, as written in siman 323.
