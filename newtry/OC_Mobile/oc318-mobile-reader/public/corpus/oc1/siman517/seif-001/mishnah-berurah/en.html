{1} As was explained above, etc. And see there in the Mishnah Berurah all the practical details of the laws.
{2} Even if it was brought today from outside the techum. That is to say: that it was brought to the non-Jewish shopkeeper; and specifically in a city whose majority are non-Jews, for presumably it was brought for non-Jews, as above in siman 515, se'if 6; but in a city whose majority are Jews, or half [and half], it is forbidden to take from him, for presumably they were brought for Jews, and one must wait until the evening after the first day of Yom Tov, and [wait] the time it would take to prepare them, as above in siman 515, se'if 5.
{3} In a city whose majority are non-Jews. That is, that most of those who buy bread and flour are non-Jews; but if most who buy bread and flour are Jews, it is certainly forbidden to take flour, for presumably it was ground with them in mind, unless it is known that it was ground yesterday.
{4} It is permitted to take, etc. For there is no [problem] of muktzeh, since wheat too is fit to chew at twilight, or to make roasted grain and porridge from it; and the same applies to rye and barley.
{5} To take bread from him, etc. That is to say: even if it is known that the flour was ground today, since he is in a city where most of the buyers are non-Jews, presumably the grinding and sifting were done with them in mind; and if it was known to him that the grinding and sifting were from erev Yom Tov, then it is permitted even in a city whose majority are Jews, for baking is a melacha that is permitted on Yom Tov. And the later authorities wrote that all this deals with a case where the appraisal of the bread was known and there is no need now to negotiate with him over the purchase money; for otherwise, even regarding a Jew it is forbidden, as above in siman 323; and see there the further details one must be careful about regarding a Jew, and the same applies regarding a non-Jew. They further wrote that if the non-Jew does not trust him without giving the money, it is permitted to leave him a security for this; but it is forbidden to call the non-Jew to his house and show him the money so that he take it himself, for that is in any case weekday-like activity. And even through a non-Jew it is forbidden to buy.
{6} That he baked on that very day. See Beis Yosef and Darchei Moshe that some are stringent in this, and in Shulchan Aruch he stated it unqualified to be lenient like the view of those who permit; and Chayei Adam wrote that one should not be lenient except when there is need.
{7} And likewise if it was baked on the preceding Shabbos. That is to say: we do not forbid it because of preparation from Shabbos to Yom Tov, since according to the fundamental law it is permitted to eat even on Shabbos itself (for even though because of the stringency of Shabbos we are stringent above in siman 325, se'if 4, when it is not a pressing time of need, in any case on the Yom Tov that follows it one should not be stringent).
