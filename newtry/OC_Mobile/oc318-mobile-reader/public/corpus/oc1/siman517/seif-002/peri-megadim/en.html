On the Taz: we require a majority of the buyers, and so it is proven and so he ruled many times in the Shas according to the majority of those who eat meat and the like; and [regarding when a non-Jew] attests (mesales), in siman 515, se'if 3, and siman 513, se'if 6: even against a presumption that they were detached [today], he is believed regarding rabbinic [law]; see Terumat HaDeshen, [siman] 83. What he wrote, that there is no "preparation" requirement regarding that of a non-Jew: one can say that refers to dried figs and raisins and the like, where the non-Jew does not set them aside mentally; and the same applies to the melacha of grinding, which was completed by human hands; see siman 325. Only attached [produce] and trapping: they did not distinguish between a non-Jew and a Jew; see siman 515. And on the word "this" in Beis Yosef he raised a difficulty: in a city whose majority are Jews, whatever was brought for the Jews, all are forbidden; if not, this is the explanation: that if it is known that it was brought specifically for one particular Jew, it is permitted for one for whom it was not brought. See Eliyah Rabbah: if he bought fish or produce from erev Yom Tov and left it with a non-Jew, so long as there is no concern that he exchanged them for inferior ones, it is permitted (for we are not concerned that he acts to provoke).
If. A fish caught on Yom Tov became mixed with many fish that were caught from erev Yom Tov, and it is impossible to wait until tomorrow because they will all die: one can say that when it spoils it is nullified, and a "creature" (beriyah) does not apply to live ones, for it was not formed in its prohibition. See Yoreh De'ah 102.
