(s.k. 2) Flour, etc. And one can say that they are not accustomed, etc.: that is to say, even though they are not muktzeh, since they are fit to chew or to make roasted grain, nevertheless, since they are not accustomed, etc., it is forbidden to send them, for there is no joy in this, since they are not fit to derive benefit from in their normal manner.
And it appears to me that even rye and barley, etc. Even though regarding the beracha, etc.: with barley it is explained explicitly in siman 208 that one does not recite over them [when] raw anything but shehakol. But rye is not mentioned there, and one must say that Magen Avraham holds from reasoning that the law of rye regarding the beracha is like barley, for it is a species of barley.
As written in siman 208. That one who chews raw wheat recites before it Borei Peri HaAdamah; and one who chews barley recites before it shehakol, for they are not fit to be eaten raw except by pressing need.
For they are fit to chew, all the more so these; and the later authorities challenged: why need this reason? Let [him] say that barley is animal food, and animal food may be moved and is not muktzeh. And one can say that this is explained above in siman 308, se'if 31, in Magen Avraham s.k. 56: raw meat that is hard, for it is not fit as raw meat (umza), is muktzeh even though one can throw it to dogs; nevertheless, since at Motza'ei Shabbos it will be fit for himself through cooking, his mind is not to throw it to dogs, and it is muktzeh; and the same applies in such a case regarding a chick, and likewise at the beginning of siman 515. And if so, here with barley and rye: if he ground them and made bread from them, the matter is revealed that his mind was not to give them to an animal; and if not that they are fit raw for a person, they would be muktzeh even though they are fit for an animal, as above.
For they are completed by human hands. That is to say: for they belong to a non-Jew, and the non-Jew is able to grind them and bake them on Yom Tov, and in such a case it is not muktzeh. And see Magen Avraham, siman 325, s.k. 10 and 11, on this.
That some say flour is muktzeh. And for that reason the Beis Yosef wrote there in Shulchan Aruch that one should not take bread from a non-Jew on Shabbos except in a pressing time of need. If so, why did the Beis Yosef here state [the law] unqualified to permit and not bring the dissenting view?
He does not hold that reason: that is to say, because of muktzeh.
Rather, in siman 325 he was concerned, when it is not a pressing time of need, according to those who are stringent for another reason, and because of lest one tell a non-Jew, etc. See there in Magen Avraham, s.k. 9. And even though in siman 325 in his commentary on the Tur it implies that he holds the reason is because of muktzeh, etc., nevertheless one can say that in Shulchan Aruch he retracted. And regarding a shopkeeper they did not decree: that is to say, specifically in siman 325, which deals with buying from a non-Jew who baked for himself, then [we say] lest he tell him (and not because of bread of a non-Jew, which is forbidden; see there in the Taz, who wrote that it deals with [bread] made to sell); therefore we decree that he might tell him to bake for a Jew. Which is not so here, which deals with a shopkeeper whose way is not to bake himself, but who buys from a baker: then there is no [concern of] lest he tell him. And for this we are not concerned that he will tell another non-Jew.
