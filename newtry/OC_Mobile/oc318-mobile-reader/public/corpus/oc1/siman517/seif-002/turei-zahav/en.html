And likewise flour, etc. For there is no [problem] of muktzeh, since wheat too is fit to chew at twilight and to make roasted grain and porridge from it; and the grinding is with permission, since [there is] a majority of non-Jews. And it appears to me simply that this "majority of non-Jews" means that most of those who buy bread and flour are non-Jews; but if most who buy bread and flour are Jews, it is certainly forbidden to take flour unless it is known that it was ground yesterday; and the law of when [a non-Jew] attests (mesales) was explained in siman 515. Beis Yosef wrote: and regarding practice it appears that in a place where they have not adopted a prohibition, it is permitted to take even from a non-Jewish shopkeeper any food item, even though it is muktzeh, for regarding something belonging to a non-Jew muktzeh does not apply; and even if it is known that it was brought from outside the techum it is likewise permitted, since it was not brought for this Jew; however, if it is something about which there is concern that it was picked or trapped today, or born today, it is forbidden, end quote. I did not understand his discussion of this at all, for regarding attached [produce] the main prohibition on that very day is because of muktzeh, as above at the beginning of siman 515, and likewise regarding an egg; and if so, in what way will you find muktzeh in the hand of a non-Jew that is permitted? Also regarding flour they needed to say that it is not muktzeh because the wheat is fit to chew; and it is clear that anything that is muktzeh, even if it is in the hand of a non-Jew, is forbidden to a Jew. And it is not comparable to what the Rashba wrote in a responsum, brought by Beis Yosef: that when we say that something not of its species that is attached and was brought from outside the techum is permitted to one for whom it was not brought, that is specifically regarding produce of a non-Jew; but produce of a Jew is forbidden to all, for they are muktzeh, since when they are outside the techum the owners have set them aside mentally, for that of a Jew needs preparation, but a non-Jew needs no preparation, end quote; and the Rama brought it at the end of siman 515. There the muktzeh is on account of the distance of the place, since it is impossible for produce of a Jew that is outside the techum to arrive here at twilight; in that case it is forbidden regarding a Jew even if a non-Jew brought them to him; but that of a non-Jew was not muktzeh at all at twilight, for it is possible for him to bring them for himself and there would be no prohibition at all. And none of this applies here, where Beis Yosef wrote "even though it is muktzeh, even if it is known, etc.," which implies that it is muktzeh in its own right, not on account of being outside the techum; and that you will not find a basis to permit. And one must force an interpretation of his language: that even muktzeh on account of distance of place, not only where the distance is doubtful, but even where it is certainly distant, etc. Also what he wrote, "since it was not brought for this Jew": I do not know why he wrote the word "this," for even if he brought it for any Jew it is forbidden to all Jews; and certainly this is what he meant: even if it is known that he brought it for another Jew, it is permitted for this one, since they did not bring it for him.
