(2) And he also wrote in the Zohar that the blesser must, before saying n'varech she'achalnu, etc., say Hav lan un'varech, for all matters of holiness require zimun — see there. And Magen Avraham brought this siman 187 and wrote that from this the custom is in Ashkenaz language to say Rabbosai mir velin bentshn and they answer Y'hi shem Heaven m'vorach me'atah v'ad olam; end of his words. And it appears better to say in the language of the Zohar Hav lan un'varech. And in Spain the custom is to say Hav lan un'varech l'malka ila'ah kadisha, and those reclining answer Sh'mayim, and he returns and says B'r'shus mori n'varech she'achalnu, etc.; and if there is a kohen among those reclining he says B'r'shus kohen u'mori, etc.; and if another blesses he says B'r'shus rav ba'al habayit u'mori, etc.; and if the son blesses he says B'r'shus avi mori v'rav ba'al habayit u'mori, etc., in Biur Halacha. And see above siman 167 ot 108. And regarding what some practice to say after Hav lan un'varech, etc., B'r'shus malka ila'ah kadisha — see Yad Ephraim ot 4, who wrote the matter should be silenced and one should not say it, and whoever adds detracts — Netan; see there. But Rav Mor Aviv in Biur Or Chayim ot 2 wrote to settle the custom, and wrote that on Shabbat the custom is to add further to say U'v'r'shus Shabbat malketa, and in the sukkah they say U'v'r'shus shiv'ah ushpizin ila'in — see there. And so too Ben Ish Chai parashat Korach ot 1.
