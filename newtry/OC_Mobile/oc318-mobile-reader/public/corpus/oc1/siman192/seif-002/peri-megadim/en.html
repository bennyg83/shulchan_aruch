And Bach wrote the reason for this — see there — and brought Tur's words, that it implies the blesser first says n'varech she'achalnu mishlo uv'tuvo chayinu, and in Shulchan Aruch they omitted it, per Magen Avraham. And Bach wrote further that Rabbeinu Yehuda's proof — if so they should have instituted in birkat hamazon to say first uv'tuvo chayinu; but in truth, since he mentions afterward, one need not mention at the beginning, and such is the custom — that the blesser does not begin uv'tuvo chayinu, but only after the responders he says so. And what is from Tosafot Berachot daf 50 amud alef s.v. amar Rava, that they conclude and say uv'tuvo chayinu — it implies it refers to what follows, that the blesser says n'varech Eloheinu al hamazon she'achalnu mishlo — on this they wrote that with ten the blesser says uv'tuvo chayinu, but with three the blesser should not say first uv'tuvo, meaning. And because one may say as Ein Ayah ot 1 wrote, that he answers afterward uv'tuvo chayinu so one not err that it refers to the host — and they wrote even with ten where there is no room to err since one mentions the Name, nevertheless he says thus; for if not, it would not apply here. And nevertheless it is difficult — with three the blesser should not say, so why say at the beginning with ten, all the more so — see Ein Ayah there. And what Ein Ayah further answered — that it teaches us that with ten one should not say mituvo, one should not say thus from the outset, for it diminishes in the praise of the Omnipresent — Magen Avraham and Levush. And if one says al hamazon — if so he should say mituvo — meaning partial good, for not only on bread, etc.; and this is strained — see ot 5.
