2) The Zohar writes further there that before the person leading the blessing says, 'Let us bless the One from Whose food we have eaten,' etc., he must say, 'Give us permission and let us bless,' because every sacred matter requires an invitation; see there. Magen Avraham cites this at the beginning of the siman and writes that this is the source of the practice in the local language to say, 'My masters, let us recite the blessing,' to which they respond, 'May the Name of Hashem be blessed from now and forever.' It appears preferable to use the Zohar's wording, 'Give us permission and let us bless.' In Sephardic communities the practice is to say, 'Give us permission and let us bless the holy supreme King.' The diners respond, 'Heaven,' and the leader then says, 'With the permission of my teachers and masters, let us bless the One from Whose food we have eaten,' etc. If a Kohen is among the diners, he should say, 'With the permission of the Kohen and my teachers and masters,' etc. If someone other than the host leads, he should say, 'With the permission of the Rav, the host, and my teachers and masters,' etc. If the son leads, he should say, 'With the permission of my father and teacher, the Rav who is the host, and my teachers and masters,' etc. Beis Menuchah. See above, siman 167, paragraph 108. Regarding the practice of saying after, 'Give us permission and let us bless,' etc., 'With the permission of the holy supreme King,' see Yafeh LaLev, paragraph 4, who writes that the practice should be forgotten and the phrase should not be said, for whoever adds thereby detracts; see there. His father and teacher, however, writes in Ruach Chaim, paragraph 2, to justify the custom. He writes that on Shabbos the practice is to add, 'And with the permission of the Shabbos Queen,' while in the sukkah one says, 'And with the permission of the seven supreme guests'; see there. Ben Ish Chai, parashas Korach, paragraph 1, writes the same.
