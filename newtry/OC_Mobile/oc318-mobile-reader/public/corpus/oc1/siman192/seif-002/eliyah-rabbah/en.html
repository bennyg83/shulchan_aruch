(2) [Levush] Baruch Hu, etc. See in Keneset HaGedolah, who wrote in the name of Maharshal and Mateh Moshe and Lechem Chumudot not to say baruch Hu and baruch shemo at all, even with ten; and so in Shulchan Aruch they did not write it. And Bach wrote that one should not say it except when they invite with ten. However, M'livushei Yom Tov wrote this his language: since Tur and Rokeach wrote, one must say that thus it was accepted, and in the Talmud they shortened the nusach; to here. It implies his view is to say it even with three, and he retracted from what he wrote in Lechem Chumudot; and so wrote R. David Abudraham and Maharam, and so is the practice. And so too in Shelah p. 83 he agreed to say it, except there is wonder in his words, for he wrote thus in the name of Kol Bo siman 25, and so wrote in Sefer Aruch Chayim; and I examined Kol Bo itself and they did not mention it at all. And so wrote in Mateh Moshe siman 346 in the name of Kol Bo that one should not say it. And know that even the poskim who agreed to say it — this is in zimun; but when one blesses alone, for everyone he begins Baruch atah Heaven and one should not say baruch Hu and baruch shemo at all. And the reason appears to me as Darkhei Moshe wrote, that they say baruch Hu and baruch shemo so there be a pause between the blessings of zimun and birkat hamazon — and this does not apply alone. And further it appears to me that specifically the blesser says it and not the two who invite with him; and proof appears to me from Abudraham's language, who wrote this his language: and he adds for them baruch Hu, etc.; and so is explained in Sefer Netivot Olam p. 41. Shelah wrote: when the blesser responds Baruch she'achalnu mishlo uv'tuvo chayinu, those reclining should say Amen before the blesser begins Baruch, etc., for here the blessings of zimun are completed; and so I saw from great ones, and so is in the Talmud — that if one enters when they answer Baruch she'achalnu mishlo, he answers Amen; to here. Or Chadash wrote, and it appears to me per this the reason of Darkhei Moshe nearby agrees; to here. And per what I wrote, that only the blesser says baruch Hu and baruch shemo so there be a pause between the blessings and birkat hamazon — one should not wonder about them that they did not mention what Levush wrote in siman 198 that one does not answer Amen; and it is explained there that it is different with one who enters, and they brought his words in Lechem Chumudot and all Acharonim — see there; and so ruled Taz, and it seems thus is the practice. Again I saw in Mateh Moshe, who wrote that such is the custom, even though afterward he wrote that it seems from Rama siman 198 that one answers Amen, for he compares it to all berachot. In my humble opinion it does not imply anything — it refers to one who enters; and you will know that Levush also wrote like Rama there, and even so it seems to him that one does not answer; also his proof from Abudraham is not proof — see there.
