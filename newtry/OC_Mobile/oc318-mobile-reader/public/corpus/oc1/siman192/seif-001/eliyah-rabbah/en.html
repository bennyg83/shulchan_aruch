(1) Uv'tuvo chayinu, etc. Bach wrote that Maharshal settled the custom, for so they practiced in all these countries; but it is difficult to maintain a custom that is not mentioned in any composition among the compositions — that the one who blesses says uv'tuvo chayinu; and therefore the Gaon our teacher Rav Lipp of Prague decreed not to say it, and the world practices from him and onward; to here. And Magen Avraham wrote that one who says it they do not make him repeat, since Maharshal settled the custom, and so wrote Levush; to here. And I wonder about all the great ones mentioned above from what I found in Ba'al Halachot Gedolot chapter Three Who Ate, who wrote this his language: the leader says n'varech she'achalnu mishlo uv'tuvo chayinu; to here — it implies that the one who blesses says it. And behold Taz elaborated and also agreed to Bach's words not to say it, and brought proof from what Tosafot wrote daf 50 s.v. amar, etc., and also with ten, etc., and says uv'tuvo chayinu — it implies that in zimun of three one does not say it; to here. And in truth Tosafot's words are ostensibly puzzling — for whoever mentions his name from mituvo chayinu. But I wonder about Ein Ayah to interpret thus, that if it is impossible with three, they should also not say with ten; and on the contrary, with ten where one mentions the Name more, it is more reasonable not to say it, for one should not err that the blesser is the host of the house — which is not so with three, where one does not mention the Name, one may err that the blesser is the host of the house when he does not say uv'tuvo chayinu. And per this it appears to me to interpret Tosafot's words in what he wrote and says uv'tuvo chayinu — that for emphasis they say, that even with ten where there is no room to err, the blesser says uv'tuvo chayinu; and from this too one may wonder about the great ones mentioned above. However, I have further to interpret Tosafot's words — that it teaches us that even with ten one must say specifically uv'tuvo chayinu and not mituvo; see there and consider carefully.
