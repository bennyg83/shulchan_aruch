N'varech. It is written in the Zohar that one should say Hav lan un'varech, for all kinds of holiness require zimun. And from this the custom is in Ashkenaz language to say Rabbosai mir velin bentshn and they answer Y'hi shem Heaven m'vorach me'atah v'ad olam. The short version of Ba'al Ha-Ma'amar for Rav M'har'r Naftali with the consent of many great ones: the entire first berachah as established. And afterward one says Nodah lecha Heaven Eloheinu al shehinchalta la'avoteinu eretz chemdah tovah u'r'chavah, venatata lanu brit v'Torah v'lechem l'sova b'eretz Yisrael al ha'aretz v'al hamazon. Rachem Heaven Eloheinu aleinu v'al Yisrael amecha v'al Yerushalayim v'al malchut beit David m'shichecha v'tagdil m'heirah kavod habayit v'tanechemenu b'chiflayim b'eretz Yisrael boneh b'rachamav Yerushalayim amen. B'eretz Yisrael emunah ha'El melech ha'tov v'ha'metiv l'chulanu hu heitiv hu metiv hu yeitiv lanu hu gamelanu hu gomelanu hu yigmelenu l'ad b'chesed uv'chen uv'rachamim v'yizkenenu l'ymot haMashiach oseh shalom, etc.; end of his words. And all the more so that one may say until Al yechaser lanu. Bach.

B'eretz Yisrael. And it implies from the Mechaber's words that one should not say baruch Hu and baruch shemo, and so too Mateh Moshe in the name of Maharshal, and so too Bach — specifically in a group of ten, where one mentions the Name, one says baruch Hu and baruch shemo, and not with three or alone. But Tur and Abudraham wrote to say it, and so too Maharal of Prague, and so too Levush, and such is the custom; but alone it is obvious one should not say it, but only begin Baruch atah, etc. Magen Avraham.
