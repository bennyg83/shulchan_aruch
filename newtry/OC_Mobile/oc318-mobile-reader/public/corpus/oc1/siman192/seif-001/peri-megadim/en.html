And he (Ateret Zekenim). In Tur he wrote to say baruch Hu and baruch shemo. Bach brought up from the verse Ki shem Heaven ekra havu godel, etc. — meaning, with ten where one says with the Name, then the responders must say godel so they respond Baruch Heaven; and they add baruch Hu and baruch shemo. Taz challenged: if so, why did he not count it among the berachot — 49 and 2? see there. And for Bach one may say: Rav Asi said Gadelu laHashem iti — plural refers to zimun of three; and R. Elazar said Ki shem Heaven ekra havu godel (Tosefta) — refers to ten. If so, one must say there is some abbreviation of language in Bach and the word etc. see there. And per this, the essence is that the responders answer Elokeinu she'achalnu mishlo; and also baruch Hu and baruch shemo, which is an addition; but in Tur he did not mention this — only the blesser says baruch Hu and baruch shemo. And Ein Ayah (letter bet) that the blesser says baruch Hu and baruch shemo to interrupt between the berachah of zimun and birkat hamazon — if so, the blesser should say it, not the responders, for they interrupt with Amen that they answer after the blesser, as Shelah wrote amud alef and Ein Ayah letter bet. And Magen Avraham to say baruch Hu and baruch shemo with three and all the more so with ten, but not alone; and there it will be explained; and see Ein Ayah shortly further on this. See in letter chet Ein Ayah on this.

And know that the berachah of zimun derives from the verse Gadelu, or Ki shem Heaven, or from the verse Et Heaven Elokecha — this is the berachah of zimun — Berachot 45a and 48b; and it implies it is d'rabbanan and the verse is merely an asmachta. But in Levush siman 199 in Hagahah, an androgynous person is obligated to lead zimun with his kind, for a safek d'oraisa is lechumra. And in Ein Ayah 199:5 in the name of Sefer Keriat Sefar, with ten zimun is d'oraisa, with three not. And that women have permission will be explained in 199 there. And from that workers bless — two berachot — it deals with without zimun; and a practical difference for us too in zimun where each one blesses silently: one should not give a minor to bless the berachah of zimun, for it is d'oraisa; nevertheless one should not give a minor. And another practical difference: if in doubt whether he blessed the berachah of zimun or not — per one who holds it is d'oraisa he repeats; per one who holds d'rabbanan he does not repeat. And why the linkage of language as Poteach l'maan yirbu — in birkat hamazon alone there is no berachah of zimun, unlike something habitual always.
