1) [Seif 1] If three reclined together, they are obligated in Zimun, etc. The Zohar, parashas Balak, 186b, writes: When a person sits at the table, the Shechinah stands there and the Other Side stands there. When the person extends an invitation to bless the Holy One, blessed is He, the Shechinah prepares itself above to receive blessings and the Other Side is subdued. If the person does not extend an invitation to bless the Holy One, blessed is He, the Other Side hears and rustles about in order to have a portion in that blessing. Regarding whether the obligation of Zimun is of Torah or rabbinic origin, see Petach HaDevir, paragraph 4, who concludes that it is of Torah origin even with three; see there. Yafeh LaLev, paragraph 1, however, writes that with three it is rabbinic and the verse is merely an allusion, but with ten, when the Name is mentioned, it is of Torah origin; see there. Eliyah Rabbah, siman 199, paragraph 5, likewise writes in the name of Kiryas Sefer that with ten it is of Torah origin. The words of Olas Tamid there, however, imply that even with ten it is rabbinic; see there. The practical difference is that if they are uncertain whether they made a Zimun, they should make one again. If ten were present, they should repeat the Zimun without mentioning the Name, because there is a dispute and one should not mention the Name in a case of doubt, since in cases of doubt concerning blessings we rule leniently. See Mishbetzos Zahav, paragraph 1.
