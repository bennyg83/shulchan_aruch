And he returns and says, etc. In Tur it is written here Baruch she'achalnu mishlo uv'tuvo chayinu baruch Hu v'baruch shemo; and Maharshal wrote on this that it is an error, for it is not mentioned in the Gemara, nor in Rif, Ra'ash, and Rambam. And Maharach wrote that it is in Rokeach and so too in Abudraham; and it is possible because they said in the chapter Three Who Ate from the Mechilta that with three one leads zimun — R. Avahu, for it is written Ki shem Heaven ekra havu, etc.; and in the chapter Aseret HaDibrot the tanna taught R. Elazar: Ki shem Heaven, etc. — Moshe said to Israel: when I mention the name of the Holy One, blessed be He, you give greatness, etc.; and he derived from this that one must give a berachah, etc.; and this is when they lead zimun with ten and not zimun of three, etc. And it is difficult on this, for if so the tanna should have taught in the Mishnah this distinction between ten and three; therefore it appears there is no basis for this matter from the Gemara and poskim.

One should not say n'varech l'Eloheinu. The reason in Beit Yosef, and this is its language: it appears to me that it is for the reason that will be explained regarding limi she'achalnu mishlo; end of his words. And it is puzzling — behold Tosafot wrote on this, that specifically regarding song and praise it is written with lamed, as Shiru laHashem hodu laHashem; but regarding berachah we do not find this law; end of his words. And if the reason were like limi she'achalnu — meaning that it implies one blesses the host — there would also be prohibition in song and praise for this reason; but certainly limi she'achalnu is different, for the meaning of the language proves it refers to the host, as written nearby; and here the reason is that we do not find this language in the verse regarding berachah, as Tosafot wrote, and so too Rabbeinu Yonah wrote: we do not find lamed adjacent to berachah, as written in m'cholot Barcheu Elohim, etc. Maharshal wrote: nowadays the custom is that also the blesser says n'varech she'achalnu mishlo uv'tuvo chayinu — one reason, so it not appear he refers to the host; but when he says uv'tuvo chayinu the meaning is Him in whose hand is life. And even though from the Talmud there is not such necessity to say it, nevertheless the reason is reasonable, and so I found. And further, since the responders after him say uv'tuvo chayinu, presumably he also said it — for otherwise why would the Talmud need to be precise per Rif's explanation, whether the blesser must return to say Baruch she'achalnu, etc. — let it suffice that he must return so he say uv'tuvo chayinu. And further, how does Rabeinu Yehuda of Barcelona prove that a shaliach tzibbur and one who goes up to the Torah must return to say Baruch Heaven hamvorach — and he brings proof from birkat hamazon — what proof? Perhaps because the blesser did not say first uv'tuvo chayinu, therefore he must return; but learn from this that there is no distinction between the blesser and the responders regarding Amen; end of his words. And these proofs do not decide at all — for what is from Rif's words is in the plain sense of to where he returns; behold it is explained in Rabbeinu Yonah's words there that the distinction is only whether he returns to say n'varech she'achalnu or Baruch she'achalnu. But in any case uv'tuvo chayinu must be in his return, and if so at the beginning certainly one need not say it. And what is from Rabeinu Yehuda of Barcelona is what Tur wrote above siman 57, who brings proof from the blesser's return to say Baruch she'achalnu — so too one who goes up to the Torah a second time says Baruch Heaven, etc.; and Rav Maharshal brings from this proof — let it suffice that it must be because he did not say first uv'tuvo chayinu; and how did Rabeinu Yehuda learn from this for one who goes up to the Torah — this is not proof in truth. And also for one who goes up to the Torah, the responders say what the one going up did not say — namely hamvorach — and the one going up never says l'olam va'ed; and if so, just as you would say in birkat hamazon he returns for saying uv'tuvo chayinu, so too with l'olam va'ed; but certainly the truth is that also in birkat hamazon he must say it a second time literally, like the responders — so too for one going up to the Torah. Also Mateh Moshe raised that uv'tuvo chayinu must be said at the beginning, and such is primary, that it is not in the Gemara and poskim. And what Rav Maharshal wrote that one may err and say he blesses the host — this is not so, for we are not concerned for this error except where there is some proof from the language, as written afterward nearby; and meaning further proof from what Tosafot wrote in chapter Three Who Ate daf 50: and also with ten, if he does not wish to mention al hamazon, the permission is in his hand, and he says uv'tuvo chayinu; end of his words. Learn from this that in zimun of three one does not say uv'tuvo chayinu — for per the context, what he wrote and says uv'tuvo, etc., is connected to what follows.

On the food we ate. Tosafot explained that it implies he blesses the host who feeds him; for if to the Merciful One, why does he mention food — without food there is much to bless; end of his words.

To whom we ate from His own. Rashi explained that it implies, heaven forbid, they are many — this feeds that and that feeds this; and per his words he blesses the host; end of his words. And the reason of mituvo Rashi explained because he diminishes in the reward of the Omnipresent, for it implies a small thing for life; and the reason of chaim is because it implies he excludes himself from the group.

And when they are ten, etc. Beit Yosef wrote, and his reason: since one mentions the Name, there is no room to err and say that al hamazon which they say refers to the host. And it appears to me that for this reason one may also say limi she'achalnu mishlo when they are ten; end of his words. And this is not correct at all — for this language has no relevance when one mentions the Name; for how can he say n'varech Eloheinu limi she'achalnu mishlo — this has no explanation at all, since he already mentions — to whom will he bless? What is this that he should say again limi, etc. And also without lamed one cannot say it — for how can he say Eloheinu mi she'achalnu mishlo, and what sign need one give to Eloheinu blessed be He that he say upon Him mi hu? Therefore this has no basis at all to say thus, in my humble opinion.
