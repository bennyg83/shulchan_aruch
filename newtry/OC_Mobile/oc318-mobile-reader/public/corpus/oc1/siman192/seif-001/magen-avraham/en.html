It is written in Zohar at the beginning of parashat Devarim that one should say Hav lan un'varech, for all matters of holiness require zimun; and from this the custom is in Ashkenaz language to say Rabbosai mir velin bentshn and they answer Y'hi shem Heaven m'vorach me'atah v'ad olam. Bach wrote: one should wonder at the custom of all these countries who say at the first time n'varech she'achalnu mishlo uv'tuvo chayinu, for it is not mentioned thus in the Gemara and in all poskim, etc.; and Maharal of Prague instituted not to say uv'tuvo chayinu; end of his words. And to me it appears that one who says uv'tuvo chayinu they do not make him repeat, since Maharshal settled the custom with good reason, and so too Levush. Shelah wrote that those reclining answer Amen after the one leading zimun finishes Baruch she'achalnu, etc.; and Levush in siman 198 wrote that one need not answer Amen so they understand and hear — and this is strained; and so too in Lechem Chayim siman 19; and in Rashi in Chullin daf 86 he wrote payment for four berachot that he answered Amen after them — it implies that on the berachah of zimun one does not answer Amen, for otherwise there would be five; and one may say that since after Boneh Yerushalayim also the one leading zimun answers Amen, it is not counted — and this is strained, for nevertheless he also receives payment. And in my humble opinion, per what Rabbeinu Yehuda wrote in his s.s. 200, that for the berachah of zimun until n'varech one must answer Amen; and per Rama there one need not answer Amen, and such is the custom. But from Rama's words in Hagahah siman 198 it implies one answers Amen, for he compares it to all berachot; and so too in Abudraham, who wrote in the name of the Yerushalmi that one answers Amen even though they did not eat; end of his words — it implies here that one should not say baruch Hu and baruch shemo; and so too Mateh Moshe in the name of Maharshal; and so too Bach — specifically with ten where one mentions the Name one says baruch Hu, etc.; but Tur and Abudraham wrote to say it, and so too Maharal of Prague in Sefer Netivot Olam, and so too Levush, and such is the custom; but alone it is obvious they should not say it, but only begin Baruch atah, etc. The short nusach of Ba'al Ha-Ma'amar for Rav M'har'r Naftali with consent of many great sages: the first berachah entirely as established; and afterward one says Nodah lecha Heaven Eloheinu al shehinchalta la'avoteinu eretz chemdah tovah u'r'chavah, venatata lanu brit v'Torah v'lechem l'sova b'eretz Yisrael al ha'aretz v'al hamazon. Rachem Heaven aleinu v'al Yisrael amecha v'al Yerushalayim v'al malchut beit David m'shichecha v'tagdil m'heirah kavod habayit v'tanechemenu b'chiflayim b'eretz Yisrael boneh b'rachamav Yerushalayim amen. B'eretz Yisrael emunah ha'El melech ha'tov v'ha'metiv l'chulanu hu heitiv hu metiv lanu hu gamelanu hu gomelenu hu yigmelenu l'ad b'chesed uv'chen uv'rachamav v'yizkenenu l'ymot haMashiach oseh shalom, etc.; end of his words. And all the more so that one may say until Al yechaser lanu. (Bach.) One may say bar'chu. Bach wrote: and if they are three and he said bar'chu he did not fulfill, and he strained to interpret Tur's words — see there; but he did not look at the hints, who wrote explicitly that if he said bar'chu with three he fulfilled; and so too Rabeinu Yerucham; and so too in the Gemara they derive zimun from the verse Gadelu laHashem iti, and from the verse Havu godel — it implies he tells two to bless as Havu godel; and such is primary.

Better. And even though in berachot of Kriat Shema he says bar'chu — that case is different there, since he says haMevorach he did not exclude himself. And he challenged in the responsum of Magen Avraham: if so, in birkat hamazon too he should say bar'chu et Heaven haMevorach, etc. — see there, he answered in the way of derash. And it seems to me that in berachot of Kriat Shema, even though he already fulfilled his obligation he is motzi others; and if so, sometimes he cannot say n'varech; therefore they enacted that forever he says bar'chu — see siman 69. But in birkat hamazon, if he fulfilled he is not motzi; therefore forever he says n'varech, for he too is obligated in the berachah, as stated above.

With lammed. For regarding a berachah it is not written with lammed, as it is written bar'chu amim Elokeinu.

On the food. For it appears as one who blesses the food.

To whom we ate. For it appears as one who blesses the host (ba'al habayit); and even though we say lemi she'asa la'avoteinu, etc. — miracles are different, for the matter is evident that the Holy One, blessed be He, performs miracles.

From His goodness (mituvo). For it implies a small thing; and if so he diminishes in the reward of the Holy One, blessed be He. And that which we say shebanu mituvcha — that question is different, for the questioner does not ask a great question, only like a poor man at the door (Gemara).

Since one mentions, etc. — nevertheless one should not say limi she'achalnu mishlo, for it implies heaven forbid that there are two authorities, and one blesses that god from whom we ate. [Bach] And it appears to me that the comparison also intended this — see there.
