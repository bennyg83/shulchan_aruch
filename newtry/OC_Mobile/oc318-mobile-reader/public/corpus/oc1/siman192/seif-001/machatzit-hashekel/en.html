Magen Avraham wrote. Zohar, etc.; Bach, etc., in the beginning. Meaning, certainly when the blesser returns after they answered Baruch she'achalnu, etc., and the blesser also says Baruch she'achalnu, etc. — it is obvious he concludes then uv'tuvo chayinu. But in the beginning, when he begins to say n'varech, etc., and concludes uv'tuvo chayinu — this is not mentioned anywhere and has no reason.

For Maharshal settled the custom — that if he does not conclude uv'tuvo chayinu, one may say it refers to the host of the house; but when he concludes uv'tuvo chayinu, necessarily he intended Him in whose hand is life. And he brought proof for his words — see Bach and Taz. Shelah wrote that those reclining answer, etc., and he brought proof from siman 198 — when three eat as one and one enters who already began to invite and heard that they answered Baruch she'achalnu, etc., the one who enters answers Amen; and if so, the same applies — those answering Baruch, etc., should answer Amen after the blesser answered she'achalnu, etc.

And Levush, etc., so they understand, etc. — meaning, specifically the one who enters, who need not bless birkat hamazon, he answers Amen; unlike the responders, who need to intend toward the birkat hamazon of the blesser, lest by answering Amen they not intend toward the blesser's berachah — and therefore they do not answer Amen.

And it is strained, for behold it is possible for the blesser to wait not to begin birkat hamazon until after they already answered Amen.

And in Rashi in Chullin, etc. — that this Sadducee who brought Rabbi good news, and as his payment ate with Rabbi, and after the meal Rabbi said to him: a cup of blessing you drink, or forty gold coins you want to take. And Tosafot explained: a cup of blessing you drink — meaning; and Shach explained in Choshen Mishpat siman 382, meaning it did not enter Rabbi's mind that the Sadducee would bless birkat hamazon and drink a cup of blessing; rather Rabbi wanted the Sadducee to go and not be there at the time of birkat hamazon. And if so, what he said forty gold coins you take — why payment for blessing? For behold he would not bless. Therefore Rashi explained payment for four Amens he would answer on his behalf.

And per Rama there, who holds the berachah of zimun is until haZan — if so, the end of n'varech is the middle of a berachah, and one need not answer Amen.

For he compares it to all berachot, as Rama concluded there on what Rabbeinu Yehuda wrote — if one enters and heard that they answered Baruch she'achalnu, etc., they answer Amen. Rama concluded: and so with all berachot that a person hears, he is obligated to answer Amen; end of his words.

It implies it is the end of a berachah, from what he wrote and so with all berachot — for were it not for this, it would be possible to distinguish as Levush wrote above, that the responders are different so they intend, or because Magen Avraham siman 189 s.s.k. 1 wrote that one should answer Amen after haRachaman; and the praise of the Holy One blessed be He is no less than the prayer of haRachaman. But since Rama concluded and so with all berachot, it implies the law of a berachah applies to it.

And so too, etc., even though they did not eat from this — it implies all the more so and a fortiori if they ate. The opposite of Levush's view above.

It implies here that one should not say, etc., since he did not mention it here.

And so too Bach — specifically with ten. And the reason he wrote there: since they derive the berachah of zimun from what is written Ki shem Heaven ekra havu godel l'Eloheinu — and from this verse itself they derive in Yoma that when one hears mention of the Name, one must praise Him and say baruch Hu and baruch shemo. If so, automatically when they invite with the Name — meaning with ten — one says baruch Hu and baruch shemo, for this verse deals with zimun, and specifically when one mentions the Name, for in this verse the Name was mentioned. But alone, etc.; but specifically three — even though they do not mention the Name, nevertheless since in any case they invite, and zimun is derived from this verse Ki shem Heaven ekra, etc., from it they derive to say baruch Hu and baruch shemo — and behold zimun with three is also derived from this verse; therefore also with three is included in this verse. From it from this verse they derive to say baruch Hu and baruch shemo.

(s.k. 1) One may say bar'chu, etc. Bach, etc., and he strained to interpret Tur's words, who wrote with four one may say bar'chu and n'varech is preferable; and if he said bar'chu he fulfilled. And since Bach holds that with three, if he said bar'chu he did not fulfill — necessarily what Tur concluded, and if he said bar'chu he fulfilled, refers to four. And Bach was challenged — behold Tur already wrote that with four one may say bar'chu; therefore he strained in interpreting Tur's words. And therefore Magen Avraham wrote that in his hints he wrote that also with three, if he said bar'chu he fulfilled, and one need not interpret Tur's words literally.
