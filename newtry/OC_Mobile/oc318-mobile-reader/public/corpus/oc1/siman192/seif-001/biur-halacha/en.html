That one of them says n'varech she'achalnu mishlo — it implies from this that one should not say uv'tuvo chayinu, and so wrote Bach and several other Acharonim. And even though Magen Avraham somewhat sides with one who says it that they do not make him repeat it, nevertheless l'chatchila for all it is not proper to say thus, for it has no source in the Talmud; and so too Taz, that this nusach is not proper. However, what seems from Taz, that with ten the one leading zimun may say n'varech, etc., and uv'tuvo chayinu — aside from that there is no reason to distinguish between three and ten, as Ein Ayah questioned — also the source he brought for this from the words of Tosafot chapter Three Who Ate daf 50, who wrote and says uv'tuvo chayinu, and Taz understood it refers to the above — in truth it does not refer to the above, but it is a separate matter, and meaning that one should not say mituvo and should not say chaim but chayinu. And the entire language of this Tosafot is explained explicitly in Tosafot of Rabbenu Yehuda on Berachot, and there it is stated explicitly as our words; and it is known that most of the Tosafot of Berachot is from this Tosafot.
