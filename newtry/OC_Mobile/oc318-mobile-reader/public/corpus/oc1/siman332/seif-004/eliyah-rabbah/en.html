[2] It is permitted to say, etc. Nachalat Tzvi questioned this from siman 307, seif 5, where a rabbinic prohibition was permitted only in a matter that is itself rabbinic and for the sake of a mitzvah. In my humble opinion there is no difficulty, because this case is different, as Mordechai explains at the end of chapter Bameh Behemah: if he is not permitted to do so, he may come to perform the act himself. Taz cites this reason and writes that accordingly the earlier ruling, that one may make the animal run, cannot refer to a case where he fears it may die, and therefore they did not act stringently there. This is puzzling. On the contrary, if he does not fear that it may die there would be greater reason for stringency, because he is not panicked and will not perform a Torah prohibition himself. Perhaps there is a scribal error in his words, and the matter requires study. Moreover, the Tosefta at the end of chapter 17 explicitly states that there is concern that the animal may die.
