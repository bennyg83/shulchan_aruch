One may not assist. The reason Rashi explained is that there is excessive exertion; and in the mishnah they said but we support on Yom Tov — meaning holding the offspring so it does not fall to the ground. And the Rosh wrote that R' Yehuda was uncertain whether this "but we support" applies specifically on Yom Tov or even on Shabbat is permitted; and that which the mishnah teaches on Yom Tov is for emphasis that one may not assist, and Tur wrote to permit even on Shabbat. And Beit Yosef wondered on him, since R' Yehuda was uncertain in it; and it appears superficially that Tur's reason is since in any case there is no d'oraisa prohibition here but only on account of excessive exertion, and in d'rabbanan matters we go lenient in his uncertainty; but it is impossible to say so, for behold in chapter Mefanin it was asked whether each one clears for himself that he does not unload — the Rosh wrote and since its prohibition exists we act stringently; and there it was only on account of exertion; therefore it appears that in Tur's book it was not written thus "R' Yehuda was uncertain," and clear proof is that in his notes there is only a citation of the Rosh's words and he did not mention this uncertainty of R' Yehuda — rather it is certainly a gloss in Asheri and not from his own knowledge:
