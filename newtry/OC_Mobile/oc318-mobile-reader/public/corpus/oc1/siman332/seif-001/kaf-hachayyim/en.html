1) [Seif 1] One may not assist an animal in giving birth, etc., because this entails excessive exertion. Rashi. There is no danger to the animal. Levush. This implies that it is permitted if danger exists. According to the view that assisting birth entails a Torah prohibition because one detaches something from its place of growth, as Mishbetzos Zahav, paragraph 1, writes, however, it is forbidden. Eshel Avraham, paragraph 2. One should therefore be stringent and permit it only through a non-Jew, as stated below in paragraph 3; see there.
2) There: One may not assist an animal in giving birth, etc. Tur writes that one may support it by holding the newborn so it does not fall to the ground and blow into its nostrils, and so forth. Beis Yosef questions him: Rosh writes that Rabbeinu Yerucham was uncertain whether the Mishnah's statement, 'but one may support it,' applies specifically to Yom Tov or even to Shabbos. Since Rabbeinu Yerucham was uncertain, how could Tur state the ruling regarding Shabbos? See there, and see how the later authorities resolve Tur's words. Rashi explains the Mishnah's statement, 'but one may support it,' as referring to Yom Tov, implying that it is forbidden on Shabbos. Rabbeinu Chananel writes this explicitly. Shulchan Aruch likewise appears to adopt this view, since it omits Tur's ruling here and cites it only below in siman 523 regarding Yom Tov, implying that one should be stringent on Shabbos. This is the view of Magen Avraham, paragraph 1. Eliyah Rabbah, paragraph 1, likewise writes that Maadanei Yom Tov and the later authorities agree that none of the birthing assistance permitted in siman 523 may be performed on Shabbos, and even supporting the animal is forbidden. Raavan, siman 375, writes the same. Shulchan Aruch HaRav, paragraph 1, and Chayei Adam, klal 59, paragraph 13, likewise rule this way. This is the view of the later authorities.
3) There: One may not assist an animal in giving birth, etc. If this is its first birth and danger is a concern, or even if it is not the first birth but the delivery is sometimes difficult and presents danger, one may permit assistance through a non-Jew. One must account for the view that assisting birth entails a Torah prohibition, as stated above in paragraph 1, but performance through a non-Jew is permitted, as stated below in seif 4.
