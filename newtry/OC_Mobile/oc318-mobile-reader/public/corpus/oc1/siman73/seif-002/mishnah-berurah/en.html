(5) With his wife, etc.—because one's wife is like one's own body and he is accustomed to her and there is no improper thought; nevertheless turning away faces is required, for face to face there cannot fail to be improper thought. And if the man turns his face alone and the woman remains as she is—even though her face is against his back—nevertheless he is permitted to recite, for again there is no improper thought; provided his flesh does not touch her ervah. But if both wish to recite, then she too must turn her face away; and even if their flesh touches one another they are permitted to recite.

(6) Who forbids—meaning without tallit separation. And according to their words one must be very careful about this in bedtime Shema and in the blessing of Hamapil that there should be tallit separation between them; and then even face to face is permitted, as above.
