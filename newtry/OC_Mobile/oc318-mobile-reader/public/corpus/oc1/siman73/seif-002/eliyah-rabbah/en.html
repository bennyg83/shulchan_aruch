[3] It is proper to be stringent, etc. The Nachalas Tzvi, however, writes that the primary halachah follows the Rif, who permits it, because one may say that in the second baraisa as well, a wife is not included in the category of another member of one's household, just as in the first baraisa. According to Tosafos on Berachos 24, who have the reading 'and his wife was sleeping,' we rely on the baraisa. This is difficult, for the Rosh records Tosafos' reading in connection with the Rif's reasoning, as does the Kesef Mishneh there. It further appears evident to me from Tosafos there that according to the first reading, the objection is from the first baraisa itself. Examine this carefully. Moreover, even according to the reading 'his wife,' since Tannaim and Amoraim dispute the matter and some permit it with one's wife, there is room to permit. Nevertheless, one should be stringent, for I saw in the Agur and in Sefer Zikaron by the gaon Rabbi Yosef HaKohen that leading later authorities agree that it is forbidden. All this applies without a tallis separating them, but with an intervening tallis, all agree that it is permitted.
