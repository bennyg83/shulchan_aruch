[3] And it is proper to be concerned, etc. But Nachalas Tzvi wrote that by law the essential ruling is like the words of the Rif, who permits, because one can say that the other baraita too—a wife is not included in "his household" like in the first baraita; but according to what Tosafos wrote [Berachos] page 24, that we have the version "and his wife sleeps"—we rely on a baraita—until here. And you could wonder: behold, the Rosh wrote following the reasoning of the Rif, the version of Tosafos; and likewise in Kesef Mishnah there. And further it appears to me proven from Tosafos there that with the first version he challenges from the first baraita itself—and study this; and further that even with the version "his wife," etc., nevertheless since tannaim and amoraim disagree, those who permit with one's wife have room to permit; nevertheless one may be stringent, for I saw in Igur and Sefer Zikaron that the Gaon R' Yehuda HaKohen and later great ones agree to forbid—and all this without separating with a tallit; but with separating with a tallit, all permit.
