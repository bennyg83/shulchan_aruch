(Seif katan 3) And from the year, etc. Thirteen years and one day are not required. Once he reaches the beginning of the night corresponding to the day on which he was born, it is sufficient. Magen Avraham writes likewise above, siman 53, seif katan 13.
This is also implied in Yevamos, etc. This is evident from Rashi's explanation of the Mishnah concerning two brothers who married two sisters, where the women were accidentally exchanged at the time of consummation while they were niddos. Each man is liable to bring four sin offerings: one for relations with a married woman, one for his brother's wife, one for his wife's sister, and one for a niddah. The Gemara seeks to establish one interpretation according to Rabbi Shimon, who holds that one prohibition does not take effect upon another unless both prohibitions take effect simultaneously. The Gemara asks: Granted, the three prohibitions of a married woman, a brother's wife, and a wife's sister can arise where the two brothers appoint one agent to betroth the two sisters for them, and the two sisters appoint an agent to accept the betrothals from the brothers. The two agents meet, and the women's agent accepts both betrothals from the men's agent simultaneously. Both betrothals thus take effect at once, and all three prohibitions take effect simultaneously. But how can the prohibition of niddah take effect at precisely the same moment? Is it possible to determine that both women began menstruating at the exact instant the agent accepted the betrothals? The Gemara answers that it can occur where the boys continue bleeding from before age thirteen into after age thirteen for the brothers' liability, and the girls from before age twelve into after age twelve for the women's liability. Rashi explains the case as follows: The two brothers were born on Rosh Hashanah, and the two sisters were born on Rosh Hashanah of the following year. Consequently, when the brothers reached age thirteen, the sisters reached age twelve. Before they became adults, although the girls were niddos, the Torah-level liability had not yet taken effect because they were not yet subject to punishment. The betrothals had occurred before adulthood, but were stipulated not to take effect until adulthood. Thus, the niddah prohibition and the prohibitions generated by the betrothals take effect simultaneously. According to the conclusion, it is unnecessary to say that they betrothed through agents. They themselves may have betrothed the sisters, provided they stipulated that the betrothals would not take effect until adulthood. The prohibitions then take effect simultaneously. The Mishneh LaMelech writes this in Hilchos Ishus. Rashi writes: 'The betrothals and the punishable prohibition of niddah take effect upon them when night falls on Rosh Hashanah of the girls' thirteenth year, which is the boys' fourteenth year,' etc. Since Rashi writes 'when night falls on Rosh Hashanah,' although the case concerns children born on Rosh Hashanah, it follows that completion of thirteen full years for the boys and twelve full years for the girls is sufficient. An additional complete day is not required. Rather, once they enter even a small portion of the following day, thereby completing the years, it is sufficient.
