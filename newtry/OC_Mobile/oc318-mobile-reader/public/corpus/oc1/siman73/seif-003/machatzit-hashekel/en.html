(Siman katan 3) And from the year, etc.—that one does not need thirteen years and one day, but once he reaches the beginning of the night of the day he was born on that day it suffices; and likewise Magen Avraham above siman 55, siman katan 13.

And likewise in Yevamos, etc.—in Rashi's explanation on that which we taught: two brothers who married two sisters, and at the time of the chuppah the women were switched and were niddot—each is liable for four sins: one for a married woman, the second for a brother's wife, the third for his wife's sister, the fourth for niddah. And he sought to establish it with one explanation that it comes according to R' Shimon, who holds ein issur chal al issur except for issur batei achot—that is, two prohibitions take effect at one time. And he challenged: granted, for three prohibitions—a married woman, brother's wife, and his wife's sister—you find it if two brothers were one agent to betroth them to those two sisters, and the two sisters made an agent to receive kiddushin from the brothers, and the two agents encountered each other and the women's agent received kiddushin from the men's agent for one daughter at once—then both kiddushin take effect in one daughter and automatically all three prohibitions take effect at once. But the prohibition of niddah—how do you find it? Is it possible to narrow it that she became a niddah at the very moment the agent received kiddushin? And he answered that you find it if she was in the flow from age thirteen to after thirteen to obligate the brothers, and from twelve to after twelve to obligate the women. And Rashi explained: for example, two brothers were born on Rosh Hashana and the following year on Rosh Hashana two sisters were born—then automatically when the brothers became thirteen years old the women the sisters became twelve years old; and before they were adults, even though they were niddot, nevertheless the prohibition from Torah did not take effect, for they were not yet liable for punishment. And it deals with a case where kiddushin preceded before they were adults, but they handed over the kiddushin on condition that they not take effect until the time of adulthood—and if so, the prohibition of niddah and prohibitions that come from kiddushin come as one. (And according to this, for the conclusion one need not say they betrothed through an agent, but rather they themselves betrothed the sisters, and nevertheless they took effect as one since they said they would not take effect until the time of adulthood—likewise Mishneh l'Melech in Hilchot Ishut, see there.) And Rashi wrote, end quote: and the kiddushin and the prohibition of the punishment of niddah were found to take effect upon them when Rosh Hashana of thirteen for females—which is fourteen for males—darkened, etc., end quote. And since he wrote "when Rosh Hashana darkened," etc., even though it deals with those born on Rosh Hashana as Rashi said there—nevertheless it must be said that already when they are full thirteen years for sons and full twelve years for daughters we do not require one more day; but once a little of the day after that enters, by which the years are complete, it suffices.
