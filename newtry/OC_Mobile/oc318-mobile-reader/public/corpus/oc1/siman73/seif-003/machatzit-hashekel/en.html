And as written in Yevamot, etc. — in Rashi on what we taught: two brothers who married two sisters, and at the time of the wedding the women were switched and they were niddot — each is liable for four sin-offerings: one for eshet ish, one for eshet ach, one for achot isha, and one for niddah; and he needs to establish it for one resolution that it comes like R' Shimon who holds ein isur chal al isur except for isur bas achas — meaning that two prohibitions take effect at once; and he challenges: granted three prohibitions — eshet ish, eshet ach, and achot ishto — you find them if the two brothers were one agent to betroth those two sisters for them, and the two sisters made an agent to accept betrothal for them from the brothers, and the two agents met and the women's agent accepted betrothal from the men's agent for one daughter — then two betrothals take effect in one daughter and thereby all three prohibitions take effect in one daughter; but the niddah prohibition — how do you find it? Is it possible to arrange that she became a niddah at the exact moment the agent accepted betrothal? And they answer that you find it if she flowed on the thirteenth day after the twelfth to obligate the brothers, and from the twelfth day after the twelfth to obligate the women; and Rashi explained for example if the two brothers were born on Rosh Hashanah and the next year on Rosh Hashanah the two sisters were born — then when the brothers became thirteen years old the sisters became twelve years old; and before they were adults, even if they were niddot, the Torah prohibition did not take effect for they were not yet liable for punishment; and it deals that betrothal preceded before they were adults, but they delayed the betrothal not to take effect until adulthood — then the niddah prohibition and prohibitions from betrothal come as one (and accordingly for the conclusion one need not say they betrothed through an agent, but they themselves betrothed the sisters and nevertheless they took effect together since they said not to take effect until adulthood — so wrote Mishneh LaMelekh in Hilchot Ishut — see there); and Rashi wrote: and the betrothal and the punishment prohibition of niddah took effect on them when Rosh Hashanah of the thirteenth year for females arrived, which is the fourteenth for males, etc.; and since he wrote "when Rosh Hashanah arrived," etc. — even though it deals with those born on Rosh Hashanah as Rashi wrote there — nevertheless one must say that it suffices when they are thirteen complete years and twelve complete years, and we do not require one more day; rather once a bit of the following day enters thereby the years are complete — it suffices:
