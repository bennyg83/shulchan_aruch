(א) All things, etc. — the word "all" is not precise, for there are things whose prohibition is rabbinic and they decreed against them during twilight even for a mitzvah need, as explained below siman 309 seif 3 in Shulchan Aruch; and likewise to carry less than four amot — as brought there in Magen Avraham in the name of Rambam — the reason is because they are close to full Torah melacha and they decreed more stringently; and likewise many poskim hold one does not make techum eruvin during twilight even for a mitzvah need; and melacha she'einah tzricha legufah is stricter than other rabbinic prohibitions and is forbidden in every case during twilight, even if we say its prohibition itself is rabbinic — all the more so for Rambam it is d'oraisa. And know that this entire seif does not deal with one who accepted Shabbat upon himself; but if he accepted Shabbat [such as through "a psalm, a song for the Sabbath day" or "for my beloved" in our times] — it is explained above siman 261 that even if it was from erev Shabbat and even if only the community accepted it upon themselves — then Shabbat comes upon him against his will — it is forbidden for him to do any shevut himself even for a mitzvah matter unless through a non-Jew:

(ב) During twilight — and its time is explained above siman 261 — see there; and see in Magen Avraham who was in doubt about motzei Shabbat during twilight — possibly Shulchan Aruch deals only with erev Shabbat, and taking out the day differs from bringing in the day, for in a doubt sanctity did not cease; and in Chayei Adam he wrote to be stringent; and see in Biur Halacha what we brought — Beit Meir decided that by law there is no distinction and even on motzei Shabbat during twilight shevut for a mitzvah need is permitted [and likewise Birkei Yosef wrote thus in the name of Rashba manuscript on Eruvin]; nevertheless for practice he wrote one should be stringent on motzei Shabbat because we are not expert in when twilight time begins — we are not certain it is still day — and see in Biur Halacha:

(ג) And likewise to lower from the tree, etc. — and it counts as a mitzvah need even if he does not need it now so much on Shabbat, because initially one only makes techum eruvin for a mitzvah matter — and if it would be forbidden to lower it and bring it to him his eruv would not take effect; therefore it is a mitzvah need:

(ד) Or to take out from the karmelit, etc. — meaning he intended to acquire residence in the public domain or in the private domain adjacent to the karmelit — and if he would not be permitted during twilight, which is the time eruv takes effect, to take out from the karmelit to bring it to him, his eruv would not take effect; therefore it is permitted:

(ה) And likewise if he was harried, etc. — this comes to explain the "or pressing need" written above — that is, if he was harried and pressed for this — then by presumption he greatly needs it and the time of distress is upon him; therefore they permitted him; and see in Biur HaGra siman 261 that it implies from him that what Rambam permits because he was harried and pressed is when there is great distress, such as when he wants to make an eruv because he is harried and pressed to flee from a non-Jew — in this the matter is equal to a mitzvah matter and they permitted shevut during twilight, even when the eruv rests on the tree or in a karmelit:

(ו) And harried — see what we wrote in siman 261 in Mishna Berurah s.k. 96 in the name of Rashal:

(ז) A lamp on Shabbat — this "on Shabbat" means that he should light immediately during twilight for Shabbat need; but if they ask him to light later on Shabbat itself — this is forbidden even if they asked from erev Shabbat, as explained above siman 307 seif 2.
