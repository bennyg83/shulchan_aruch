(1) All matters, etc. The term "all" is not precise, because there are matters that are rabbinically forbidden and that the Sages prohibited during bein hashmashos even for the sake of a mitzvah, such as that explained below in siman 409, seif 3, in the Shulchan Aruch. The same applies to carrying in increments of less than four amos, as brought there by Magen Avraham in the name of the Maggid Mishneh. The reason is that these acts can readily lead to a complete melacha forbidden according to the Torah, and the Sages therefore decreed more strictly regarding them. Many poskim likewise hold that one may not establish an eiruv techumin during bein hashmashos even for the sake of a mitzvah. A melacha she'einah tzricha legufah is also more severe than other rabbinic prohibitions and is forbidden in every case during bein hashmashos, even if we say that its essential prohibition is rabbinic, and all the more so according to Rambam, who holds that it is forbidden according to the Torah. Know that this entire seif does not discuss one who has accepted Shabbos upon himself. If he has accepted Shabbos, such as through reciting "A psalm, a song for the day of Shabbos" or Lecha Dodi in our times, it is explained above in siman 261 that even if it is still daytime, and even if only the congregation accepted Shabbos, so that Shabbos takes effect upon him against his will, he may not personally perform any shevus even for the sake of a mitzvah, except through a non-Jew.
(2) During bein hashmashos. Its time is explained above in siman 261; see there. Magen Avraham was uncertain regarding bein hashmashos on Motzei Shabbos. It is possible that the Shulchan Aruch discusses only erev Shabbos, because ending the day differs from bringing in the day, for sanctity does not depart because of a doubt. Chayei Adam writes that one should be stringent. See Biur Halachah, where we brought that Beit Meir ruled that strictly speaking there is no distinction, and even during bein hashmashos on Motzei Shabbos a shevus is permitted for the sake of a mitzvah. Birkei Yosef likewise writes this in the name of a manuscript Rashba on Eruvin, that bein hashmashos on Motzei Shabbos is equivalent to bein hashmashos on erev Shabbos. Nevertheless, in practice he writes that one should be stringent on Motzei Shabbos because we are not expert in determining when bein hashmashos begins, meaning the point at which it is no longer certainly daytime. See Biur Halachah.
(3) Likewise, he may lower it from the tree, etc. This is considered a mitzvah need even though he does not greatly need it at that moment on Shabbos, because one may initially establish an eiruv techumin only for the sake of a mitzvah. If it were forbidden to lower it and bring it to him, his eiruv would not take effect. It is therefore considered a mitzvah need.
(4) Or remove it from a karmelis, etc. Meaning that he intended to acquire his Shabbos residence in a public domain or a private domain adjacent to the karmelis. If, during bein hashmashos, which is when the eiruv takes effect, he were not permitted to remove it from the karmelis and bring it to himself, his eiruv would not take effect. It is therefore permitted.
(5) Likewise, if he was occupied, etc. This explains the phrase "or a pressing need" written above. If he was occupied and hurried regarding this matter, it may be assumed that he greatly needs it and that it is a pressing circumstance, and they therefore permitted it. See Biur HaGra, siman 261, from which it appears that Rambam's permission because one is occupied and hurried applies where there is a major pressing circumstance, such as when he wishes to establish an eiruv because he is hurriedly fleeing from a non-Jew. In that case the matter is equivalent to a mitzvah need, and they permitted a shevus during bein hashmashos, even when the eiruv is resting on a tree or in a karmelis.
(6) And hurried. See what we wrote in siman 261, Mishnah Berurah, seif katan 16, in the name of Maharshal.
(7) A lamp on Shabbos. The phrase "on Shabbos" means that the non-Jew should light it immediately during bein hashmashos for a Shabbos need. If one asks him to light it later, on Shabbos itself, this is forbidden even if the request was made before Shabbos, as explained above in siman 307, seif 2. The Mechaber's permission is not limited to the need of the meal. The same applies if one asks him to light in the room where he will sleep, and here too there is room for leniency.
