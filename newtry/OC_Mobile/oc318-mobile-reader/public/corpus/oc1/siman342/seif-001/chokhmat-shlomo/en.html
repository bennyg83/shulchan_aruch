Seif 1. All things forbidden by rabbinic law — they did not decree on them during twilight, etc. — Raavad's view in tractate Eruvin is that for two shevut matters they decreed even during twilight — I questioned him from the Gemara Eruvin chapter 10 daf 98 that challenges there: if you say on a crushed doorpost what is it to me inside four amot what is it to me outside four amot — see there. And it is difficult what it challenges — let us say indeed that they permitted shevut as Rabbi says in the mishnah "there is no shevut standing before Scripture" — perhaps we deal only with one shevut, namely from public domain to private domain, since there is no resting it is only shevut as proven there; but if it is outside four amot which is two shevut matters — four amot in public domain and from public domain to private domain — in this they were stringent even with Scripture; and why does it challenge — and necessarily it is proven there is no distinction between one shevut and two shevut matters. But according to what is written in Sefer Yeshuot Yaakov Laws of Shabbat siman 307 in the name of Shaarei Efraim: specifically when there are two shevut matters together Raavad forbids, but if one after another its law is like one shevut — one can say here it is not together, for first he carries four amot and then from public domain to private domain; therefore it is only like one shevut — examine well. And regarding what they said in Shulchan Aruch "all things forbidden by rabbinic law — they did not decree on them during twilight," etc. — see in Magen Avraham who is in doubt whether also on taking out the day we say they did not decree on shevut during twilight or not; and apparently proof should be brought from what Tosafos wrote in the first chapter of Rosh Hashana daf 12b s.v. "when the sun shone upon him" — "in the Yerushalmi they establish it with a non-Jew" — end of their words. And their intent is certainly that it is difficult for them: how did he harvest on Yom Tov — therefore they wrote that in the Yerushalmi they establish it with a non-Jew. And apparently the matter is astounding: behold telling a non-Jew is still shevut and how did he tell a non-Jew to harvest. And one cannot answer that it deals with "over and command him" improperly, for then there is no need to say "with a non-Jew" — only that he himself transgressed and harvested. And one cannot say the intent is the non-Jew harvested without the Israelite's knowledge, for it is still difficult what Tosafos informed us by saying "in the Yerushalmi they establish it with a non-Jew" — one who examines the Yerushalmi sees it is not as question-and-answer — they establish it with a non-Jew; but the Yerushalmi itself teaches this version: "for vegetables — how does one harvest — an Israelite until it is not yet dark and a non-Jew harvests from when it darkens; one does not tithe from this for that" — end of Yerushalmi. If so the braita itself teaches this case that the non-Jew harvests from when it darkens — and we would know without explaining "with a non-Jew"; and the Yerushalmi wants to inform us a novelty that even if the non-Jew harvests with the Israelite's knowledge it is also found that the law of tithing from new on old applies — only the Yerushalmi teaches one case with a non-Jew that is more common than forgetting or transgressing and harvesting — and this fits in the Yerushalmi itself that teaches one case with a non-Jew; but our Gemara that teaches anonymously and returned and harvested is very difficult on Tosafos — what do they teach us with saying "in the Yerushalmi they establish it with a non-Jew" — does one not know that for an Israelite it is forbidden to harvest on Yom Tov — and necessarily it deals either with a non-Jew or forgetting or transgressing and harvesting; if so what novelty did Tosafos inform us by saying they establish it with a non-Jew — obvious that through whoever it is the harvest is called from new on old. And what relevance does the law of new and old have to the body of the harvest — and what do Tosafos teach us that there they establish it with a non-Jew — and is it not found this law without their establishing it. And apparently it requires study; therefore it appeared that Tosafos hold that what the Yerushalmi says does not mean without the Israelite's knowledge — for then it would explain "without his knowledge" or that he forgot and harvested; why need it explain with a non-Jew — it could teach briefly "and he harvested from when it darkened" and we would know it deals with one of these cases; but the Yerushalmi wants to inform a novelty that even if the non-Jew harvests with the Israelite's knowledge we also find this law of permission — that is, if the non-Jew returned and harvested during twilight of Rosh Hashana it follows Rabbi who holds they did not decree on shevut during twilight; therefore it is permitted to tell a non-Jew to harvest even with his knowledge. But in any case for tithing it is called from old on new; and whether for twilight it is a doubt of day to be lenient to tell a non-Jew to harvest and we establish him for the day — it would also be considered day for vegetable tithing and all would be considered as old. But necessarily taking out the day differs from bringing in the day — and specifically where prohibition was not established Rabbi admits twilight is stringent; therefore here too regarding shevut for harvesting which is bringing in the day and is rabbinic doubt to be lenient and permitted to tell him to harvest; but for tithing to tithe from this for that — since he is certainly obligated in tithes and doubt whether to exempt with old or new — it is rabbinic doubt where prohibition was established and in this it is stringent; and Tosafos' proof from the Yerushalma is clear; and Magen Avraham's doubt is resolved as I wrote — may Heaven grant me time later to explain more — examine well:
