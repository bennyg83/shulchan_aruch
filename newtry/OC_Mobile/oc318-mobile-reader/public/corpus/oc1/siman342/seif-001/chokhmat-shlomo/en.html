Seif 1: All matters forbidden by the words of the Sages, they did not decree upon them during bein hashmashot, etc. NB: The view of the Rav of Bartenura in Eruvin is that where there are two shevut prohibitions, they decreed even during bein hashmashot. This is difficult to me from the Gemara in Eruvin, chapter 10, fol. 98, where it asks: if you say it is an askuppah that is trampled, what difference is there whether it is within four amot or outside four amot? See there. This is difficult: what is the question? Let us say that indeed, although they permitted a shevut, as Rabbi Shimon says there in the Mishnah, "there is no shevut that stands before sacred writings," perhaps that applies only to one shevut, namely from reshut harabbim to reshut hayachid, since without placement it is only a shevut, as is proven there. But if it is outside four amot, where there are two shevut prohibitions, carrying four amot in reshut harabbim and from reshut harabbim to reshut hayachid, there they were stringent even for sacred writings. What, then, is the question? Perforce it is proven that one should not distinguish between one shevut and two shevut prohibitions. However, according to what is written in Yeshuot Yaakov, Hilchot Shabbat siman 307, in the name of Shaarei Ephraim, that the Rav of Bartenura forbids specifically when the two shevut prohibitions occur together, but if they occur one after the other the law is like one shevut, this can be answered. Here they are not together, for first he carries it four amot and afterward from reshut harabbim to reshut hayachid; therefore it is like one shevut. Examine carefully; this is well.
Now regarding what they said in Shulchan Aruch, "all matters forbidden by the words of the Sages, they did not decree upon them during bein hashmashot, etc." NB: See the Magen Avraham, who is uncertain whether even at the outgoing side of the day we say that they did not decree on shevut during bein hashmashot, or not. At first glance one could bring proof from what Tosafot wrote in the first chapter of Rosh Hashanah 12b, s.v. mishetavo alav hashemesh: "In the Yerushalmi they establish it with a non-Jew." Their intent must be that it was difficult to them how he picked on Yom Tov; therefore they wrote that the Yerushalmi establishes it with a non-Jew. But this is apparently astonishing, for telling a non-Jew is itself a shevut, so how did he tell the non-Jew to pick? One cannot say that it speaks where he transgressed and commanded him unlawfully, for then there is no need to say a non-Jew; he himself could have transgressed and harvested. Nor can one say that the non-Jew harvested without the Jew's knowledge, for then what did Tosafot teach us? In truth, one who examines the Yerushalmi will see that it does not establish this as a question and answer, but the body of the baraita itself stated that case: "For vegetables, how so? If a Jew picked before dark and a non-Jew picked after dark, one may not separate terumot and maasrot from this for that." Thus the baraita itself mentioned the case where the non-Jew picked after dark; certainly many other cases are possible, such as where the Jew forgot it was Yom Tov and picked, or transgressed and picked without concern for Yom Tov, for in every case the law regarding maaser is that it is from the new upon the old. The Yerushalmi merely chose one common case, a non-Jew, which is more common than forgetting or transgressing. But our Talmud states generally "and he again picked," so Tosafot's comment is very difficult: what novelty did they teach by saying that the Yerushalmi establishes it with a non-Jew? Does anyone not know that a Jew may not pick on Yom Tov? Perforce it must be either a non-Jew, or one who forgot, or one who transgressed and picked. What novelty did Tosafot teach by saying it is a non-Jew? It is obvious that whoever does the picking, it is called new upon old, for what does the law of new and old have to do with the body of the act of picking? What, then, did Tosafot teach us by saying that there it is established with a non-Jew? Without this, would the law not be found? This apparently requires study. Therefore it appears that Tosafot understood that the Yerushalmi does not mean without the Jew's knowledge; if so it should have said so explicitly, and since it could be found by transgression or forgetfulness, why establish it with a non-Jew? Rather, the Yerushalmi teaches a novelty: even if the non-Jew picked with the Jew's knowledge, this case can occur permissibly. That is, it speaks where the non-Jew again picked during bein hashmashot of Rosh Hashanah, following Rabbi, who holds that they did not decree on shevut during bein hashmashot. Since telling a non-Jew is only a shevut, it is permitted to tell the non-Jew to pick even with his knowledge. Nevertheless, regarding maaser it is called old upon new. Although vegetable maaser is rabbinic and this too is a rabbinic doubt, one might argue either way: if for bein hashmashot it is treated leniently as a doubtful day, so that it is permitted to tell the non-Jew to pick and we treat it as day, then it should also be considered day regarding vegetable maaser and all of it should be considered old. Perforce, the outgoing side of the day differs from the incoming side. Specifically where no issur has been established does Rabbi agree that the doubt of bein hashmashot is treated stringently. Therefore, regarding the shevut of picking, which is the incoming side of the day, it is a rabbinic doubt and we are lenient, so he may tell him to pick. But regarding maaser, when separating from this upon that, since it was certainly obligated in maaser and the doubt is whether it may be exempted as old or new, it is a rabbinic doubt where an issur has been established, and there it is treated stringently. Tosafot thus taught much from this Yerushalmi: regarding telling a non-Jew, bein hashmashot is treated leniently, while regarding separating from new upon old, bein hashmashot is treated stringently. This resolves the doubt of the Magen Avraham, as appears to me now. Perhaps God will grant me another time to explain this further. Examine carefully.
