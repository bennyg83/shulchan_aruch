As for the Beis Yosef's question on the Orchos Chaim from the Yerushalmi, one may say that it holds like Reish Lakish in Sukkah 46b, that on the seventh, once others have fulfilled the mitzvah with the esrog, it is permitted, for it was set aside only for its mitzvah. But the Orchos Chaim, who copied the Yerushalmi, is difficult according to us, for we rule like R. Yochanan that it is prohibited; and likewise, if it became invalid on the first day, it is also prohibited all seven days. The Taz answered that the practical difference is that if the esrog became invalid, it is permitted on the eighth in any event. For according to the strict law it should be permitted on the eighth for us, just as for those in Eretz Yisrael, since we do not say muktzeh on account of the previous day, as Tosafos wrote on daf 10b, s.v. 'ad.' Rather, it is a decree because of sukkah, lest people not listen to us to sit in the sukkah on the eighth, for they will say: today is certainly the eighth. See Beis Yosef and Levush. Even at twilight of the eighth it is prohibited for this reason: esrog is decreed because of sukkah, for even at twilight one must sit in it. Therefore, where the esrog became invalid, for in a comparable case where the sukkah fell there is some leniency in any event, one may at least rely on the lenient ruling regarding the esrog. See siman 649 as to which invalidation applies on the other days. See Eliyah Rabbah and Or Zarua, who wrote against the Taz that the Yerushalmi in chapter Lulav VaAravah, on the Mishnah 'immediately the children snatch their lulavim and eat their esrogim,' does not imply so; see there. See also the Gaon who comments there.
