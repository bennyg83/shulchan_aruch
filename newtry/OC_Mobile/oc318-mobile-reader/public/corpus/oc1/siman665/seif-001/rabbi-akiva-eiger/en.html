Magen Avraham seif katan 1 Because of the doubt concerning the previous day. Their intent appears to be that with an esrog, immediately after he took it and fulfilled the mitzvah with it, its mitzvah has departed; and the reason it is prohibited for the entire seventh day is because, since it was set aside at twilight, it was set aside for the entire day. At twilight it is prohibited because of the doubt concerning the previous day. So it is written explicitly in Rashi's commentary, and this is his wording: an esrog is not fit at twilight after he fulfilled the mitzvah with it in the morning. Now, although the Ran in the first chapter of Beitzah also wrote the principle that we do not say muktzeh because of the previous day, nevertheless here in Sukkah he gave the reason that twilight itself is not muktzeh. This is because it is clear from the Ran that he explains that an esrog on the seventh is prohibited for the entire day because, if one did not take it in the morning, he may take it at twilight, so the whole day is the time of the mitzvah. If so, the difficulty would be: let us say that at the twilight of the seventh it is also prohibited for this reason, and if so on the eighth it should also be prohibited, since it was set aside at twilight because of the mitzvah. Therefore the Ran had to give the reason that twilight itself was not set aside, since if one did not take it in the morning he does not take it at twilight, for it is a doubt in a rabbinic matter. If so, at twilight it is permitted to eat it. And the reason it is not prohibited at twilight because of the rule that since it was set aside for part of the day it remains set aside is that on a weekday we do not say this rule, and it is prohibited only at the time of the setting aside; examine there well. The Eliyah Rabbah wondered: if so, when one designated seven esrogim for seven days, where we rule that with each one he fulfills the mitzvah and eats it on the following day, at twilight of the first day he takes it because of a doubt in a Torah obligation, so should it not be prohibited on the next day? See there. With all due respect to his honor, this is not difficult at all, for the Ran holds that on a weekday we do not say that since it was set aside it remains set aside. According to this, the law would be that on Yom Tov of the diaspora, if one designated seven esrogim for seven days, the esrog of the first day is prohibited on the second day, since he takes it at the twilight of the first day because of the doubt of the first day, and it is prohibited the next day on Yom Tov because of the rule that since it was set aside, like an esrog on the eighth. But perhaps nevertheless it is a whichever-way-you-turn argument: if the first day is Yom Tov, then the second day is weekday and we do not say the rule that since it was set aside; and if the second day is Yom Tov, it is revealed that the first day was weekday and was not the time of the mitzvah. See Tosafos Yom Tov, chapter 3 of Eruvin, and Tosafos on daf 10 of Sukkah. One may also say according to what the Ran wrote in the first chapter of Beitzah, regarding that which he distinguishes that only with muktzeh because of mitzvah do we say muktzeh because of the previous day: from the fact that Rav holds that with each one he fulfills the mitzvah and eats it immediately, it follows that with muktzeh because of mitzvah we do not say this. He answered in the name of the Raah that since he designated them for seven days, it is as though he stipulated that it is set aside only for its mitzvah. If so, one may say that even Rav Asi, according to whom we rule that he eats it the next day, also holds that a condition helps, but he holds that he set it aside for the whole day, which is the duration of that day's mitzvah, for if one did not take it in the morning he may take it all day. Nevertheless, with regard to the following day it is as though he stipulated that it be prohibited only for its day, meaning the entire day; and similarly at twilight of the first day, which is part of the duration of the mitzvah, but no more. Just as Rav holds that it is as though he stipulated that it is set aside only for its mitzvah, so too for R. Asi it is as though he stipulated that it is set aside only for the duration of the mitzvah and its day; examine this well. And although from the words of Tosafos in the first chapter of Sukkah, where they wrote that according to the version that R. Asi holds he eats it the next day, he must hold that we do not say muktzeh because of the previous day, it is proven that Tosafos hold that according to R. Asi it is not considered as though he stipulated, one may say that Tosafos follow their own approach, that of Rashi, that immediately after he fulfilled the mitzvah with it in the morning, its mitzvah departed, and the reason it is prohibited on the seventh is because of the rule that since it was set aside, as above. If so, from the fact that R. Asi prohibits it for the whole day, it must follow that he holds a condition does not help, or that it is not considered as though he stipulated; for otherwise it should be permitted to eat immediately. But according to the Ran's approach, that the reason it is prohibited on the seventh is that it is the time of the mitzvah, for if one did not take it in the morning he may take it all day, and that this is the dispute of R. Yochanan and Reish Lakish, and regarding this we say that the dispute of Rav and R. Asi is also the same, then simply one may say that even R. Asi holds that it is considered as though he stipulated. They dispute only whether it is considered a stipulation for the actual mitzvah alone, or for the time of the mitzvah, namely its day; examine this carefully.
