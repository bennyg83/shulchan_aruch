An esrog on the seventh, etc. But on the eighth it is permitted, and we do not say that since it was set aside at twilight it was set aside for the entire day, for at twilight itself it was prohibited only because of the doubt concerning the previous day (Tosafos and Rosh).
For they were set aside, etc. The Taz wrote that even on the first day, before one took it to fulfill the mitzvah with it, there is a prohibition of muktzeh. This is not comparable to what the Beis Yosef wrote at the end of this siman in the name of the Hagahos Ashri, that if he made it for hoshanah but did not bind it in order to fulfill the mitzvah with it, it is permitted for benefit, for designation alone is not significant. There, as long as he has not performed the act that he must perform, he has not withdrawn his mind from it, etc. Several Acharonim wrote that he erred in this, namely the Maamar Mordechai, the Levushei Serad, and others, for this is a scribal error in the Hagahos Ashri, and it should read: 'but he did not lift it,' etc. So too it appears above in Shulchan Aruch siman 653. The truth is with them, for so it is in the Or Zarua itself, from which the ruling of the Hagahos Ashri is sourced. If so, there is proof to the contrary: even if he made the bundle, as long as he did not lift it to fulfill the mitzvah with it, it is called mere designation, and it is permitted to eat it.
