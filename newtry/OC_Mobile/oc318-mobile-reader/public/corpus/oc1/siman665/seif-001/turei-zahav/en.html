For they were set aside for all seven. It appears obvious that even on the first day, before he took it in order to fulfill the mitzvah with it, there is a prohibition of muktzeh. This is not comparable to what the Beis Yosef wrote at the end of this siman in the name of the Hagahos Ashri, that if he made it for hoshanah but did not bind it in order to fulfill the mitzvah with it, it is permitted for benefit, for designation is not significant. There, as long as he has not performed the act that he must perform, namely the binding, he has not withdrawn his mind from it, and perhaps he will choose another one; therefore muktzeh does not apply to it. But with an esrog that he prepared at twilight to fulfill the mitzvah with it on the following day, he immediately withdrew his mind from it. This is also not comparable to what the Rema wrote in siman 638, that if one designated something for the sukkah but did not sit in it, etc.; there, it was fit for him to sit in it and he did not sit, so he revealed his mind that he did not want it at that twilight, and consequently it was not set aside for that entire day, as it appears to me.
And even if it became invalid, etc. In the Beis Yosef he brought the Orchos Chaim in the name of the Yerushalmi, that if it became invalid on the first day it is permitted to eat it. End quote. One must wonder why it is not prohibited because, since it was set aside at twilight, it was set aside for the entire day. End quote from the Beis Yosef. It appears that the Beis Yosef's question is on the Orchos Chaim; but on the Yerushalmi itself there is no difficulty, for it holds like Reish Lakish in the Gemara, who says that an esrog on the seventh is permitted, because it was set aside only for its mitzvah and not for the whole day. Therefore the Yerushalmi holds that if it became invalid, even on the first day, its mitzvah has departed and it is permitted. Rather, the Beis Yosef wondered why the Orchos Chaim brought this Yerushalmi, since we rule like R. Yochanan, who says that it is set aside for the entire day even after the mitzvah has been performed. It appears to my humble mind to resolve this: the reason the Orchos Chaim brings the Yerushalmi is to learn from it for our ruling, that if it became invalid, it is permitted on the eighth. According to strict law we should have permitted it on the eighth even if it had not become invalid, for the setting aside at twilight of the eighth does not apply to it, as Tosafos wrote on daf 10. Rather, the reason an esrog is prohibited for us even on the eighth is a decree because of sukkah, which is set aside at twilight of the eighth, for if a meal happened to come to him then, he would need to eat it in the sukkah because of the doubt that perhaps it is still day. Therefore, if the esrog became invalid, one may say that we do not decree on the eighth because of sukkah, for even with sukkah there is room to permit benefit from it after the seventh day if it fell, since if it fell even during the seventh it is prohibited only rabbinically. Therefore on the eighth, where there is only muktzeh because of doubt, we leave it at the Torah law and permit. Even according to what the Beis Yosef wrote in the name of the Ran in siman 638, that with a fallen sukkah there is no leniency on the eighth, only that a condition helps, nevertheless there is no basis to prohibit an esrog on the eighth when it became invalid, since in sukkah itself there is a leniency on the eighth when it fell. For this we have support from the Yerushalmi, which is lenient when it became invalid even during the seven days; at least we may rely on it on the eighth. For this reason the Orchos Chaim brought the Yerushalmi, as it appears to my humble mind.
