One recites the blessing. For he recites the blessing over the permitted addition to the flame.
One does not recite the blessing. The Gemara asks: If he recites the blessing over the permitted addition, then a flame transferred from one non-Jew to another should likewise be permitted. It answers that this would indeed be so, but there is a decree on account of the first non-Jew and the original flame. That is, he might come to recite the blessing over a non-Jew's light that was kindled while it was still day, before the permitted flame was added after nightfall, such as immediately after nightfall. See Taz. After the fact he has fulfilled his obligation, since this is only a decree. Magen Avraham. The Taz questions the Maggid Mishneh's ruling that it is forbidden at a gathering because of idolatry: why should one not recite the blessing over the permitted addition supplied by the Jew? He concludes that one may be lenient outside a gathering and stringent at a gathering. This is astonishing, for the Maggid Mishneh did not prohibit it because of idolatry. Rather, he prohibited a case in which a non-Jew lit from a Jew at a non-Jewish gathering, as the Levush explains. If a Jew lit it, even from an actual idolatrous flame of a non-Jew, it is permitted. Eliyah Rabbah.
One does not recite the blessing. Ramban explains that on Motzaei Shabbos it is permitted because of the permitted addition to the flame, which is like a light produced from wood and stones. On Motzaei Yom Kippur, however, one recites the blessing only over a light that actually rested, or over a light kindled from it.
