That he lit on Motzaei Shabbos from a Jew. This teaches that although one does not bless over fire that a non-Jew lit from another non-Jew even on Motzaei Shabbos, for it was lit permissibly, as below, there the reason is that we decree because of the first non-Jew, who lit it on Shabbos, lest one come to bless over his fire. But here this does not apply, and therefore it is permitted. This speaks where the non-Jew did not light the lamp in a gathering of other non-Jews, for otherwise we say that an ordinary gathering of idolaters is for idolatry, and it is forbidden to bless over the lamp, as above in siman 297, seif 2, regarding spices.
Or a Jew from a non-Jew. This means even if the non-Jew lit his lamp on Shabbos, nevertheless it is permitted, for through the Jew's lighting, permitted light was added to part of the light that comes from the first fire, and he blesses over the permitted addition.
But a non-Jew who lit, etc. We do not say here that he blesses over the added permitted light that this non-Jew lit on Motzaei Shabbos, for they decreed because of the first non-Jew. Although even over the lamp of the first non-Jew one could, according to the law, bless, since permitted light was added to it on Motzaei Shabbos, for the nature of fire is always to have new light added to it, the Sages decreed because of the first column, meaning that one might come to bless immediately close to nightfall, while the permitted light had not yet been added, Gemara. The Magen Avraham wrote that after the fact he fulfilled the obligation with this and need not go back and bless, since it is only because of a decree; so wrote the Shaarei Ephraim.
One does not bless over it. If the non-Jew produced fire from wood and stones on Motzaei Shabbos, it is permitted to bless Borei me'orei ha'esh over that fire, for there is no reason to decree here. But if the non-Jew lit a lamp from this fire, there is room to be stringent, for it may be confused and lead to permitting even a lamp lit from a lamp that did not rest, Eliyah Rabbah and Derech HaChaim, not like the Rama of Pano.
One does not bless over a lamp, etc. Because the fire of the non-Jew had labor done with it, as it was lit on Yom Kippur. Although on Motzaei Shabbos one blesses over such a lamp, there the reason is that he blesses over the added permitted flame added by the Jew's lighting, and this addition, since it is born now, is like fire produced from wood and stones on Motzaei Shabbos, over which it is permitted to bless. This is not so on Motzaei Yom Kippur, where it is explained below in seif 8 that one does not bless over fire produced from wood and stones.
