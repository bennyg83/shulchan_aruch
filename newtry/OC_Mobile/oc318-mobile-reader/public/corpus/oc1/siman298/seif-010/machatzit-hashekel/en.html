(Seif katan 10) But, etc. He lit a lamp for a sick person from his own lamp; this should be the text. For that which was taught in seif 5, that if a non-Jew lit a lamp on Shabbos for a postpartum woman or for a sick person, one blesses over it because it rested from forbidden labor, is specifically when the non-Jew lit it from a Jew's lamp that was lit from before Shabbos. But if it was lit from a non-Jew's lamp for a postpartum woman or sick person, one does not bless over it. Granted, what he lit for the postpartum woman and sick person was permitted; nevertheless, since it was lit from a non-Jew's lamp, which is prohibited, we decree because of a non-Jew's lamp. It is no better than a non-Jew who lit on Motzaei Shabbos from a non-Jew's lamp, over which one does not bless, although it was lit on Motzaei Shabbos permissibly, for we decree because of the first non-Jew's lamp, as the Magen Avraham wrote in the following seif katan. The same applies when a non-Jew produced fire from wood on Motzaei Shabbos; although it was lit permissibly, nevertheless we decree because of a prohibited non-Jew's lamp, since a Jew had no involvement in it. But if, etc., from this, meaning from what he produced on Motzaei Shabbos from wood and stones, it is permitted, for to this extent we do not decree, as that would be three decrees.
