On the palms of the hands. This is the wording of the Darchei Moshe: The custom of Israel is Torah, that they are accustomed to bend the fingers and look at the nails, and not to extend the fingers, but only the backs of the fingers, the place of the nails. There is a secret in this, as the Zohar writes. These are his words. And this is the wording of the Kavanos, page 42: The intent of the Zohar is to turn the backs of the fingers, the place of the nails, toward the candle, so that the bending of the fingers is over the thumb, and the thumb should not be visible outside. It is not like those who explain that one should turn the inner side of the fingers and bend them, but rather the opposite. These are his words. So too it is written in the writings. It appears to me that this is why we are accustomed to bend the fingers into the hand, as the Rama wrote, and afterward to extend them and look at their backs, in order to satisfy both views.
