One blesses over the lamp, etc. Because the beginning of its creation was on Motzaei Shabbos, as we say in Pesachim 54: on Motzaei Shabbos the Holy One, blessed is He, gave understanding to Adam HaRishon, and he struck two stones against one another and fire emerged from them. That which we bless on Motzaei Yom Kippur even when it does not fall on Motzaei Shabbos is because it is like havdalah, for the entire day it was forbidden to use this fire even for food preparation, unlike other festivals, and now it is permitted. Therefore, according to the opinion of the some say, one must seek after it just as for havdalah.
Who creates the lights of the fire. For there are several kinds of light in fire: white, red, and green. If he said 'the light of the fire,' the Bach's view is that he did not fulfill the obligation even after the fact. If he said 'who created the lights of the fire,' in the past tense, he fulfilled the obligation.
And one need not seek after it. For we bless only as a remembrance that fire was created on Motzaei Shabbos, as above. Therefore, he can bless over the cup even without a lamp. Whenever it later becomes available to him, when he sees fire and benefits from its light, he should bless Borei me'orei ha'esh. But this is specifically on the night of Motzaei Shabbos and no later, for its time has passed, as below in siman 299, seif 6.
One who does not have a cup, etc. If he estimates that a cup will become available to him at night, and he wants to learn before the cup comes to hand, Rabbi Akiva Eiger inclines in his novellae that he should not bless now over the lamp. It is better that he arrange them over the cup.
