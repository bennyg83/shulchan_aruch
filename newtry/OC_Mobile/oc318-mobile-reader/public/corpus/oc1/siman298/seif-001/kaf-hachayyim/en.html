1) [Seif 1] He recites a blessing over the lamp, etc. This is because fire was first created after Shabbos, as stated in Pesachim 54a. After Shabbos, the Holy One, blessed is He, granted understanding to Adam HaRishon. He brought two stones, rubbed them against one another, and fire emerged from them; see there. See Bach for what he further writes in the name of the Yerushalmi and Midrash Rabbah; see there. Why do we not recite blessings over other things on the anniversary of their creation? Fire differs because its use was forbidden on Shabbos, and it is therefore as though it were newly created now. Responsa Rashba, siman 159; Magen Avraham, paragraph 1.
2) There: Borei Meorei HaEish, etc. One who already recited Borei Meorei HaEish may repeat the blessing to fulfill the obligation of his household. Birkei Yosef, paragraph 1, in the name of Mahari Fragi. Shaarei Teshuvah cites this, and Beis David, siman 491, concludes this way. Toras Chesed writes the same, as cited by Erech HaShulchan, paragraph 1. Shulchan Aruch HaRav, siman 297, paragraph 9, however, cites a dispute and writes that one should account for the dissenting opinion so as not to enter a possible situation of reciting a blessing in vain; see there. Therefore, one who has already recited Havdalah and then comes to recite it for others who do not know how should not repeat the blessing over the fire. Rather, they should recite it themselves, as stated above in siman 297, paragraph 36, concerning the blessing over fragrance when he cannot recite it for them; see there. Ben Ish Chai, parashas Vayeitzei, paragraph 15, writes the same.
3) There: Borei Meorei HaEish, etc. This follows Beis Hillel, because fire contains many lights: a red, white, and greenish flame, as stated in Berachos 54b. If one recited Meorei HaEish without Borei, even after the fact he did not fulfill his obligation and must repeat the blessing. Bach; Eliyah Rabbah, paragraph 1, who writes that this is contrary to Magen Avraham; see there. If he recited SheBara Meorei HaEish, however, he fulfilled his obligation. Gemara there; Bach. Similarly, if he said Bara without the prefix shin, he fulfilled his obligation. Eshel Avraham, paragraph 3. See further the sacred Zohar, parashas Vayakhel, folio 210a, for the reason that we recite Borei Meorei HaEish rather than Me'ir Meorei HaEish; see there.
4) There: Borei Meorei HaEish, etc. You will find that after Shabbos we mention three acts of creation: Borei Peri HaGafen, Borei Atzei Besamim, and Borei Meorei HaEish. They correspond to the three acts of creation in the verse, 'God created man in His image; in the image of God He created him; male and female He created them.' Avudraham; Yafeh LaLev, paragraph 1. Pri Etz Chaim, gate 18, chapter 24, writes that the four blessings of Havdalah correspond to Atzilus, Beriah, Yetzirah, and Asiyah, and to the Divine Names with numerical values 72, 63, 45, and 52. During Hamavdil, one should intend the separation of the husks and the mundane from the holy; see there.
5) There: Or even seek one. Rosh explains that this blessing is merely a remembrance that fire was created after Shabbos. Were it recited because of the benefit of light, one would have to recite it every time he benefited from light. These are his words, cited by Beis Yosef. If you ask why the Sages did not in fact institute a blessing whenever one benefits from light, as they did for other forms of benefit, since one may not benefit from this world without a blessing, Tosafos, Pesachim 53, answers that blessings were instituted only when the body benefits, and not for other forms of benefit. Although a blessing is recited over sunlight each day, that is because it is renewed daily. Bach.
6) Whether one may benefit from light before reciting Borei Meorei HaEish depends upon a dispute, as stated in siman 693. I already wrote in a responsum in the sacred work Be'er Mayim Chaim that Maran's view is also to permit this, even before one has recited Atah Chonantanu; see there.
7) There: He need not seek one. According to the sacred Zohar, parashas Vayakhel, folio 208a, however, which gives an esoteric reason for the blessing over light after Shabbos, it appears proper and correct to seek one; see there.
8) There: But after Yom Kippur, some say one must seek one. This demonstrates that this day is holier than other Yamim Tovim, since kindling a fire is forbidden. Magen Avraham, paragraph 2; Tosefes Shabbos, paragraph 3; Shulchan Aruch HaRav, paragraph 2.
9) There, gloss: One who has no cup for Havdalah recites a blessing when he sees fire, etc. The cup was principally instituted for the blessing of Havdalah, just as a cup was instituted for Kiddush. The blessings over fire and spices are arranged over the cup incidentally to the blessing of Havdalah. Therefore, one who has no cup for Havdalah recites the blessing upon seeing fire, because this blessing is unrelated to the blessing of Havdalah and is recited only because fire was first created after Shabbos, as stated in paragraph 1. The blessing over spices restores the soul and is a blessing over benefit, and may therefore likewise be recited without a cup.
10) There, gloss: And likewise the spices. Levush omits this, and it is also absent from Tur. It is possibly obvious, because the spices restore the soul and because their blessing is a blessing over benefit. Eliyah Rabbah, paragraph 4.
