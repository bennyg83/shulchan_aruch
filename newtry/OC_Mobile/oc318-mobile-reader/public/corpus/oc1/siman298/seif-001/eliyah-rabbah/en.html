[1] Who creates the lights, etc. If he said the singular 'light,' he did not fulfill the obligation [Bach]. Berachos explains that a flame contains many lights; red, white, and greenish. This differs from simanim 167 and 187, unlike Magen Avraham. Olas Tamid cites Mishpat Tzedek: one who intended to bless over the lights of fire, or began 'the lights of fire,' then remembered and concluded over the spices, has fulfilled the blessing over spices and must repeat the blessing over fire. This requires examination in siman 209.
[2] Levush: On the first day, etc. This is incorrect, for that was daylight; this fire was created by Adam HaRishon on Motzaei Shabbos, as stated in Pesachim 52a. Levush himself writes so in siman 624.
[3] Levush: If because of the benefit, etc. We do not normally bless over fire as over other benefits because the body does not directly benefit from it. The blessing over sunlight is due to its renewal each day [Tosafos, Pesachim 53b]. Kol Bo 41 writes that the blessing Yotzer Or also covers this light. Alternatively, the Motzaei Shabbos blessing covers the entire week, since its benefit never ceases: every day it is needed for baking, cooking, and warming oneself.
[4] Levush: Over the lamp, etc. The same applies to spices [Rema]. Levush omitted this, and it is absent from Tur as well, perhaps because it is obvious that spices restore the soul; see siman 299:6. Moreover, their blessing resembles a blessing over benefit.
[5] Levush: Whose light, etc. Kol Bo writes that since the blessing says 'lights' in the plural, a torch is needed, in which many lights are visible. See paragraph 1. One does not bless over oily pinewood called kien because its odor is foul [Sefer Chasidim].
