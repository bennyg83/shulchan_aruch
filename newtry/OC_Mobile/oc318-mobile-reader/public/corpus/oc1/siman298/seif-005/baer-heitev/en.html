For a postpartum woman or an ill person. If there is no danger, it must specifically have been lit by a non-Jew; if there is danger, it may even have been lit by a Jew. Eliyah Rabbah rules that if there is no danger, even a light kindled by a non-Jew is forbidden; see there for his proof, and this also follows from the Levush. If a non-Jew kindled it from his own fire for a postpartum woman or an ill person, one may recite the blessing over it, for the decree applies only while the flame remains in the non-Jew's hand on Motzaei Shabbos. This also appears from Tosafos in Berachos, unlike Responsa Rema MiFano.
Idolatry. This applies specifically when the flame is attached to a coal of idolatry. If, however, a Jew lit a candle from a candle of idolatry, it is permitted, because a flame has no substance. Yam Shel Shlomo at the end of Beitzah; see Yoreh Deah, beginning of siman 142.
