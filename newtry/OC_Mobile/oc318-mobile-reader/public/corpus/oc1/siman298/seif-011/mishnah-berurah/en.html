A synagogue lamp. With regard to the lamp of a beis midrash in our times, it stands to reason that it depends on the place. If people learn there and they light it for this purpose, it is made to give light.
If there is an important person there. Then it is made to give light. When there is no important person, it is made for the honor of the Shechinah. The opinion of the some say holds the opposite, that when there is an important person it is made for his honor and not to give light, and when there is no important person, it is permitted, for it is made to give light. However, if he extinguishes it and lights it again for the need of havdalah, it is certainly permitted according to everyone in every case. It is stated in the Beis Yosef that in our lands they are accustomed to light a special lamp in the synagogue in order to recite havdalah over it. The Eliyah Rabbah wrote that since here the law is made dependent on an important person, it follows that anything similar, such as one who lights a lamp in his home in honor of an important person who came to him, one does not bless over it.
One blesses over it. For it is made to give light for his eating. This speaks where he eats in a house adjacent to the synagogue and the windows are open to it, for it is forbidden to eat in the synagogue, as written in siman 151. It is stated in the Rosh, and the later authorities copied it, that nowadays one does not bless at all over the lamp of the synagogue, because they light it only for honor, for the lamps burn even by day. The intent is the lamps that burn before the lectern, which are only for honor; but those that each person takes in his hand to give light, one does bless over them, Derech HaChaim. Many people stumble in this, taking from the lamps of the lectern for havdalah, and it is not proper to do so. Perhaps when he wants to combine them into a torch, one need not object. It appears obvious that one should not bless over a yahrtzeit lamp, for it is not made to give light but only in memory of the soul of the dead.
That the moon not be shining. For if the moon is shining, the attendant does not need the lamp's light, and it was made only for the honor of the Shechinah.
