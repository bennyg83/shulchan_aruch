Over a torch. Because its light is great. It is good to beautify the mitzvah by using wax. The Magen Avraham wrote this in the name of the Kavanos of the Arizal. He further wrote in the name of Sefer Chassidim that one should not take resinous wood, called kein in the vernacular, because its smell is bad. It appears that this refers to those that give off a bad smell of pitch. Above, in siman 264, it is stated that even a pitch lamp itself may be lit on Shabbos night, and likewise with itran, which certainly smells bad, they would have lit with it were it not for the concern that he might leave and go out in the middle of the meal. Certainly the light of havdalah, for which one need not seek, is no better than the Shabbos lamp, which is an obligation. Perhaps the intent of Sefer Chassidim is that this is not the preferred manner, even though its light is great, because of its bad smell; but it is not worse than another lamp that is not a torch. The Peri Megadim also wrote that if he has no other lamp, he may bless over it.
Another lamp. Because this makes it recognizable that it is for the sake of the mitzvah. It appears that this speaks where he cannot bring this lamp close to the lamp in his house, for otherwise he can fulfill the mitzvah of a torch.
For the need of havdalah. All this is only a mitzvah matter and not indispensable, Maamar Mordechai.
A lamp that has two wicks. A plain lamp mentioned in the Gemara and poskim means an oil lamp, and it teaches us that since their lights meet one another above, it is considered a torch. With our lamp, if several wicks are placed there, it is like one thick wick and is not considered a torch. But if he separates between them with wax or fat, such as those made of wax where the candles are braided together, or he joins two candles together at the time of the blessing, this is also considered a torch. It is implied from the words of the Magen Avraham that he should see that the lights reach one another, for then it is considered a torch.
