Over a torch. He should not take resinous wood, called kien in the vernacular, because its odor is foul. [Mateh Moshe and Shelah in the name of Sefer Chasidim] The Bach wrote that if one said 'the light of the fire' he has not fulfilled the obligation. This requires study, for in simanim 167 and 187 it implies that even if he did not say the exact formula he fulfilled the obligation, and this requires study for practical halacha. One may say that his reason is because he acted like Beis Shammai. It appears from Kisvei HaAri, part 2, section 4, that one should recite Havdalah over a wax candle, and this is also implied in the kavanos of the Ari.
Two wicks. With a wax candle, if two candles are braided together, they are considered a torch, but each one by itself is not considered a torch. The Gemara says: his attendant arose and lit a torch from a lamp. He said to him, why do you need all this, the lamp is placed here. He answered him, the attendant acted on his own. He said to him, had he not heard it from the master, he would not have done it. He answered him, a torch for Havdalah is the preferred mitzvah. The Hagahos Mordechai asks that he should have immediately told him the truth. He answers in the name of the Ri of Corbeil that in truth, initially it is a mitzvah to light a torch for Havdalah, but if a lamp is already placed before him, he need not seek a torch. Therefore Rava understood that he was asking properly, and he answered him that the attendant had acted on his own. The other did not understand, and thought that there is no mitzvah with a torch at all, and therefore asked, had he not heard, etc. Then Rava understood that he did not know at all that a torch for Havdalah is a mitzvah, and therefore answered, etc. These are his words. Now according to his view, the poskim should have written this law, that when a lamp is placed before him he need not light a torch. Therefore one may say that his question was that since there is a lamp, he should have lit another lamp and it would be a torch. Therefore he answered that the attendant acted on his own. Nevertheless, they must be joined together, for then they will have many lights, unlike when each one stands by itself. See what I wrote in siman 433, seif 2.
