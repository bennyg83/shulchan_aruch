[6] Except for the lamp, etc. This implies that this lamp should not be joined to the household lamp to form a torch; Havdalah is made over this single lamp alone. Piskei Tosafos says likewise, but Magen Avraham implies they should be joined. Pesachim 103b relates that a servant lit a torch from a lamp. They asked why, since a lamp was present; after discussion, he explained that a torch for Havdalah is the preferred mitzvah. Hagahos Mordechai asks why he did not immediately give that answer and says that if a lamp is already before him, he need not seek a torch. The servant understood their question differently, whereas they did not know the preference for a torch. I further suggest that the reply hinted the servant was an important person and the lamp was lit in his honor, as below in se'if 11. Magen Avraham asks why the poskim did not record that one need not light a torch when a lamp is present. It seems Hagahos Mordechai agrees that one should light one, but need not seek one; the poskim already state in se'if 1 that one need not search for it. Although one need not search even where no lamp exists, there is then still a mitzvah to obtain one, whereas with a lamp present there is no need at all. Magen Avraham instead says the question was why he did not light another lamp and form a torch, but this does not readily fit the later question. Nor may one object from siman 296:1, where the Havdalah candle is extinguished, for extinguishing it is not obligatory.
