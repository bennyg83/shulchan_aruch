11) [Seif 2] The choicest manner of performing the mitzvah is to recite the blessing over a torch. The word avukah, torch, is explained like chavukah, bound together, because it contains many pieces of wood and many lights. Beis Yosef in the name of Maggid Mishneh, chapter 29. Kol Bo writes that since the blessing says Meorei, 'lights,' in the plural, a torch displaying many lights is required. Eliyah Rabbah, paragraph 5. Nevertheless, even when reciting the blessing over a single lamp, one says Meorei HaEish, because light contains many colors: a red, white, and greenish flame. Shulchan Aruch HaRav, paragraph 4. See above, paragraph 3.
12) There: To recite the blessing over a torch. The Arizal would kindle a wax candle and recite Borei Meorei HaEish over it. He did not recite Havdalah over the lamp in the house, but over a special torch. Shaar HaKavanos, folio 60b. See Pri Etz Chaim, gate 18, chapter 24, which gives an esoteric reason for a wax candle; see there.
14) There: To recite the blessing over a torch. One should not use resinous wood called kien in the vernacular, because its odor is foul. Magen Avraham, paragraph 3; Eliyah Rabbah, paragraph 5; Shulchan Aruch HaRav, paragraph 4. Shulchan Aruch HaRav writes that this applies when another option is available, implying that if no other option is available, it is permitted. Eshel Avraham, paragraph 3, and Mishbetzos Zahav, paragraph 2, write the same.
15) There: Apart from the lamp designated, etc. This implies that one should not join this lamp with the lamp designated for household use to make a torch. Rather, Havdalah is recited over this single lamp alone. Piskei Tosafos writes the same. Magen Avraham, however, implies that they must be attached. Eliyah Rabbah, paragraph 6. All this concerns only the choicest manner of performing the mitzvah and is not indispensable, as stated at the beginning of the seif.
