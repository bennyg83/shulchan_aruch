Who is. See Taz. Princes rise at the end of the second hour, as Chazal state, and spend one hour dressing. See Bach and Peri Chadash. This must vary with the season. Both their sleeping and their dressing depend on the length of the day. When the day is short they hurry to dress. Therefore the hours are proportional hours. The times for Krias Shema, tefillin, in siman 30, and tzitzis, in siman 18, all begin when one can recognize an acquaintance at a distance, according to the Beit Yosef, who holds that they are one measure. Rabbeinu Yonah, cited by the Beit Yosef in siman 18 and here, implies that they are not one measure. See what I wrote in Kaf HaChaim, Taz 1, and here, letter 2. Seven blessings were instituted for Krias Shema corresponding to “Seven times a day I praise You.” Three were assigned to the morning and four to the evening. Levush explains that evening was placed first, as in “there was evening and there was morning,” and also “when you lie down and when you rise.” Since the diligent act early, four were assigned to the evening, leaving three for the morning. Derishah in siman 236 answers that the evening blessing Hashkiveinu also relates to the redemption from Egypt. See there.
In a place. See Taz. According to the Mechaber, one who arose to travel after dawn, before the ideal time for Krias Shema, must wait and recite Krias Shema on the road when he can recognize an acquaintance, even though he could recite Krias Shema and pray at home and join redemption to the Amidah. This is the ruling in siman 89, seif 8. Only when he will travel through danger may he recite Krias Shema at dawn and pray at home. The Bach disagrees and says that even without danger, whenever he can recite Krias Shema and pray at home, he may recite from dawn, because the need to join redemption to the Amidah constitutes pressing circumstances. In siman 89, however, the Bach retracts and explains the Beit Yosef as follows: whenever there is no danger, even if he can recite Krias Shema and pray at home, he should wait and recite Krias Shema on the road when he can recognize an acquaintance, and he should not join redemption to the Amidah. Only danger, or an after-the-fact situation, constitutes pressing circumstances. In a case of danger, when he recites at home, he should put on tefillin from dawn without a blessing. According to our ruling that night is a valid time for tefillin, there is certainly no concern that he testifies falsely. See siman 25, seif 4, and siman 30, Taz letter 1.
Levush wrote. Those who allow the time for Krias Shema to pass because they have no tefillin, claiming that otherwise they would testify falsely, are mistaken. Peri Chadash writes that this is obvious from Berachos 14b, concerning the messenger who acted improperly, and it is likewise stated in siman 66. In my humble opinion, however, one may distinguish as follows. Kesef Mishneh, chapter 1 of the laws of Krias Shema, halachah 13, cited by Taz, seif katan 4, and Magen Avraham, may mean that at the Torah level its time extends all day, while the Sages ordained that after four hours one is merely like one who reads from the Torah, thereby removing from him the Torah mitzvah of Krias Shema. Tosafos to Sukkah 3a, concerning Rabbi Yochanan ben HaChoranis, is similar. [I have now found in Piskei Tosafos to Berachos, section 11, that one who violates a Rabbinic safeguard has not fulfilled the Torah obligation. The evening Krias Shema is different because the Sages themselves said that their limit was only to warn ideally.] It may therefore be that the Sages said that through the third hour he is truly reciting within its proper time and is not called a transgressor. During the fourth hour he recites with its blessings but is called a transgressor and lacks the choicest reward. If the third hour has already passed, perhaps he should wait for tefillin until the fourth hour, for then he would not be a transgressor in the severe sense of testifying falsely. Rabbi Yehudah's messenger acted improperly because he feared that the fourth hour would pass and he would then not fulfill Krias Shema at all, as explained. However, if “from then onward he has not lost the blessings” does not refer directly to Krias Shema, and the time for Krias Shema has already passed, then it is obvious that one should not wait beyond the third hour for tefillin. He should recite Krias Shema within the third hour. Levush therefore needed to state this, and his garments are white as snow. With Hashem's help, this will be explained further below.
