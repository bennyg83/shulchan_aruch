He is [writing] on [Tur] O.C. siman 58. Here the sons of kings stand until the end of the second hour as the Sages said, and remain in their garments one hour—Avodat haKodesh; and Pri Chadash—and it must be said that everything depends on the season, both in sleep and even in their garments: when the day is short they hurry to dress, and therefore they estimate temporally; and here the time of Kriat Shema and tefillin (siman 30) and tzitzit (siman 18)—one hour for them from when one sees his fellow from a distance, etc.; per the Beit Yosef's explanation it is one measure; and R. Yitzchak in siman 18 brought Beit Yosef here—implies it is not one measure; and it is written in siman 25 in Taz (1) and here seif 2 marker 2 from this—that they instituted seven blessings for Kriat Shema: "seven times a day I praise You," etc.; and they took three in the morning and four at night—see Levush: he preceded evening and morning (also "when you lie down and when you rise"), and the diligent precede taking four for Ma'ariv and three for Shacharit—the remainder; and Perishah in siman 236 explains that at night too Hashkiveinu [is said] for "who redeemed Israel," related to [the redemption] in Egypt, etc.—see there.

In place of Tur O.C. 58: when one rose early for a journey after alot ha-shachar and there is not yet the time of Kriat Shema lekhatchilah—even though he could pray and [recite] Kriat Shema in his house and adjoin redemption to prayer—they did not permit him except to wait on Kriat Shema and read on the way when he reaches [the time when] he recognizes his fellow, as in siman 89 seif 8; and if he goes in a place of danger then he recites Kriat Shema under compulsion and prays in his house; but Bach disagrees and says that even without danger, whenever he can [recite] Kriat Shema and prayer in his house, he recites from the outset—this is a case of pressure because of adjoining redemption, etc.; and in siman 89 he retracted his words and Beit Yosef explained that whenever there is no danger, even though he can [recite] Kriat Shema and prayer in his house, he waits on Kriat Shema on the way until he sees his fellow and does not adjoin redemption; and the hour of pressure is danger or bedi'eved. And in danger for one who reads in his house—he puts on tefillin from the outset and does not bless on them; and for us, night is the time of tefillin and certainly there is no "agent who testifies false testimony"; see siman 25 seif 4 and siman 30 in Taz (1)—what is written there:

Levush wrote: those people who pass the time of Kriat Shema when they do not have tefillin—[about one who] testifies false testimony—they err; and Pri Chadash wrote it is obvious from Berakhot 14b: "the agent of poor judgment"—and likewise in siman 66; and in my humble opinion it may be said: and what Mishnah Berurah wrote in chapter 1 of the five [topics] of Kriat Shema (13)—the Magen Avraham brought in Taz (4) and Magen Avraham z"l—that it may be said: from the Torah its time is all day, and the Rabbis instituted that after four hours it is like one who reads in the Torah, and they uprooted from him Kriat Shema deOraita; and likewise in Tosafot Sukkah 3b: R. Yirmiyah (and now I found in Piskei Tosafot Berakhot 11: whoever transgresses a rabbinic fence does not fulfill from the Torah; and Kriat Shema at night is different, for the Rabbis themselves said only to be stringent lekhatchilah)—if so, it may be said that the Rabbis said until three hours he reads actually at its proper time and is not called a transgressor; and at the fourth hour he reads with its blessings and is called a transgressor and has no reward from the best; and if he transgressed the third hour it may be said he waits until the fourth hour so he is not called a transgressor—as one who testifies "[recited] seven times a day"—a more severe matter; and R. Yirmiyah was a poor judgment—he was afraid lest the fourth hour pass and he not fulfill Kriat Shema at all, as stated; and indeed if we say from here onward he did not lose the blessings—for it is not actually standing on Kriat Shema [deOraita], and Kriat Shema has already passed its time—it is obvious he should not wait after the third hour for tefillin and recite Kriat Shema at the third hour; and therefore Levush needed this; and Levush wrote "his garments like fresh snow"—and elsewhere more will be explained:
