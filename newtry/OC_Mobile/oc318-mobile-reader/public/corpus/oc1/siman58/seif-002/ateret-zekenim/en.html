One should hurry to recite it as much as one can. There are those who wrote that if he is on the road or in a place where he has no tzitzit or tefillin, or he cannot put them on because of cold—nevertheless he should hurry to recite Keriat Shema and its blessings in its time, and afterward when he has tzitzit and tefillin he puts them on and blesses over them in order to fulfill the positive commandment of tefillin, and he returns and recites Keriat Shema without blessings while the tefillin are on him—not for the sake of obligation but like one who reads in the Torah; or if he wishes he reads with them another parashah or another psalm—in such a manner he fulfills eighteen [blessings] with putting them on, for their mitzvah is not dependent specifically on Keriat Shema, since they too are positive commandments in themselves (Maharam of Imola), and there are those who disagree with him. One should wait until he comes to an inn even if he will be delayed in coming until after midnight; nevertheless if he wishes to remove himself from doubt of a blessing in vain he may bless Birkat Yotzer Or and Birkat Ahavah Rabbah up to "l'yachedkha b'ahavah," and when he comes to the inn he wraps in tzitzit and puts on tefillin and begins "u'v'karevteinu l'shmekha," etc.; but one should not recite Keriat Shema without tefillin and tzitzit, for they have already discharged the biblical Keriat Shema when he already said "Shema Yisrael," etc., while standing—and it is proper to do so. Nevertheless where it is impossible, the first view is primary as we find in the chapter "He was reading" (Berakhot 14b)—Rav acted this way.
