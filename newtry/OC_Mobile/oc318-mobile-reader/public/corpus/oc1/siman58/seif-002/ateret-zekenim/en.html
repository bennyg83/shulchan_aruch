He should advance the recitation as much as he can. Some write that if he is traveling, or is in a place where he has no tzitzis or tefillin, or cannot put them on because of the cold, he should nevertheless recite Krias Shema and its blessings at the proper time. Later, when he has tzitzis and tefillin, he should put them on and recite the blessing over them in order to fulfill the positive mitzvah of tefillin. He should then recite Krias Shema again without its blessings while wearing the tefillin, not as an obligatory recitation but merely as one reading Torah. Alternatively, he may recite another passage or a different psalm while wearing them. In this manner he fulfills his obligation of donning tefillin, since their mitzvah does not specifically depend upon Krias Shema, for they are also an independent positive mitzvah. This is the view of Maharam Yaffe. Some disagree with him and maintain that one should wait until he reaches his lodging, even if he will not arrive until after midday. Nevertheless, should he wish to avoid the possibility of reciting a blessing in vain, he may recite the blessing of Yotzer Or and the blessing of Ahavah Rabbah through the words, ‘to proclaim Your unity with love.’ When he reaches his lodging, he should wrap himself in tzitzis, put on tefillin, and begin from, ‘You have brought us near to Your Name,’ and continue from there. He should not, however, recite Krias Shema without tefillin and tzitzis, since he has already fulfilled the Torah obligation of Krias Shema by reciting ‘Shema Yisrael’ and ‘Baruch shem kevod malchuso le'olam va'ed.’ It is proper to act this way. Nevertheless, where this is not possible, the first opinion is primary, for we find in Berachos 14b that Rav acted in this manner.
