Seif katan 2—when it begins to shine, the Tur wrote to read it slightly before hanetz so that he finishes "Goel Yisrael" with hanetz, and afterward he brought the language of Rambam that one begins before hanetz, etc., and he estimated this like the measure of one hour before the sun rises, etc.—meaning from the beginning of its reading until the sun rises is one hour. And Beit Yosef challenged in the name of Mahari Abuhav: how did the Tur call this "slightly before," since it is the measure of an hour? And he answered that what Rambam said "until the sun rises" means the end of its rise, and that is not what netz means—for netz means the beginning of its rise, etc.—this is proven from this that hanetz hachama means the beginning of netz, and then he finished "Goel Yisrael." Indeed, in the girsa of Kesef Mishnah "a tenth of an hour"—then netz was the end of the sun's rise, and then one says "Goel Yisrael"; for if it entered one's mind that netz means the beginning of netz, it is difficult—since from the beginning of his reading until the end of the sun's rise there is only a tenth of an hour, nothing remains for Keriat Shema and its blessings except a very short time. There is a practical difference for the law between the girsa and Kesef Mishnah; Kesef Mishnah also agreed to girsa aleph, only that he explains Rambam's intention that from alot hashachar until hanetz hachama is an hour—and according to him also it is proven that netz means the beginning of netz; for if it entered one's mind "the end," this is clear in Rambam brought in Magen Avraham siman 89 seif katan 2 that from alot hashachar until sunrise is an hour and a fifth. And Darkhei Moshe also agreed to girsa aleph, only that he explains the word "third" of Rambam—not dealing from the beginning of its reading but meaning from hanetz until the sun rises, as written here in the hagahah—behold also that netz was the beginning of netz. The end of the matter: for girsa aleph, for all of them its meaning is that one says "Goel Yisrael" at the beginning of netz, unlike girsa bet where one says "Goel Yisrael" at the end of netz; and Kesef Mishnah decided like girsa bet. And on this Magen Avraham wrote that for the law there is no practical difference—even for girsa bet netz is at its beginning; and if you say that then nothing remains except a very short time for Keriat Shema and its blessings—one may say for this girsa that what Rambam said "until the sun rises" means the beginning of netz; for indeed for girsa aleph Mahari Abuhav had to explain that "until the sun rises" means the end, for otherwise the expression "slightly before" is not applicable—unlike girsa bet where it is fine, for this "slightly" is that tenth of an hour they gave him as the measure for Keriat Shema and its blessings, as in Bach there; and all the continuation of the words of Magen Avraham is explained.
