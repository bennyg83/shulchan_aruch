Seif katan 2. From when it begins to shine. The Tur wrote to read it a little before netz, so that he concludes Ga'al Yisrael at netz, and afterward he brought the Rambam's language that one should begin before netz, etc., and measured this as about one hour before the sun rises. This means that from the beginning of the reading until the sun rises is an hour. The Beis Yosef, in the name of Maharai, asked how the Tur first called it 'a little before,' since it is the measure of an hour. He answered that the Rambam's phrase 'when the sun rises' means the end of its rising, not netz, for netz means the beginning of its rising; see there. From this it is proven that netz hachamah means the beginning of netz, and then he finishes Ga'al Yisrael. However, according to the Kesef Mishneh's reading, one tenth of an hour, it must be that netz means the end of the sun's rising, and then he says Ga'al Yisrael. For if one would think netz means the beginning of netz, it is difficult, since from the beginning of his reading until the end of the sun's rising there is only a tenth of an hour, leaving only a very short time for Krias Shema and its blessings. Thus there is a practical difference between the readings. The Kesef Mishneh also agrees with the first reading, but explains the Rambam's intent that from alot hashachar until netz hachamah is an hour; according to him too, netz means the beginning of netz. For if one would think it means the end, the Rambam, brought in Magen Avraham siman 89 seif katan 2, states that from alot hashachar until sunrise is an hour and a fifth. The Darkei Moshe also agrees with the first reading, but explains a third answer: the Rambam is not speaking of the beginning of the reading, but means from netz until the sun rises, as he writes here in the gloss. Thus he too holds that netz is the beginning of netz. In sum, according to the first reading, according to all explanations he says Ga'al Yisrael at the beginning of netz; but according to the second reading, he says Ga'al Yisrael at the end of netz. The Kesef Mishneh rules like the second reading, and on this the Magen Avraham wrote that practically there is no difference; even according to the second reading, netz is at its beginning. If you ask that if so only a very short time remains for Krias Shema and its blessings, that is not so, for according to this reading one may say that the Rambam's phrase 'until the sun rises' means the beginning of netz. According to the first reading Maharai had to explain that it means the end, for otherwise the expression 'a little before' would not fit; but according to the second reading it fits well, that 'a little' is that tenth of an hour, which they gave as the measure for Krias Shema and its blessings. So it appears in the Bach; see there. Thus the whole flow of the Magen Avraham's words is explained.
