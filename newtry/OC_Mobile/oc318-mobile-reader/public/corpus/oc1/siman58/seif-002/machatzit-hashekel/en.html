(Seif katan 2) The measure, etc. It appears in Beis Yosef: from when he begins to read, etc.; this is the correct reading.
This is the language of the Tur: And the mitzvah in its preferred form, etc. is to read it a little before netz hachamah, so that he finishes it and its blessings with netz hachamah and joins prayer to it. The Rambam likewise wrote that its mitzvah is to begin before netz hachamah, so that he finishes reading and blessing with netz hachamah, and this measure is like the measure of one hour before the sun rises. According to the simple meaning, 'before the sun rises' means the beginning of its rising, namely netz hachamah; and the Beis Yosef asked in the name of Maharai how the Tur called this 'a little before' if it is an hour. Therefore Maharai explained that the Rambam means the end of the sun's rising. The Bach and Darkei Moshe discuss the textual readings and explanations, whether it is an hour or a tenth of an hour, and whether netz means the beginning or the end of the sun's rising.
The Bach agreed with the first reading, namely like the measure of an hour. The Bach explained that it means that from alot hashachar until netz hachamah is the measure of an hour, and he brought proof from the Yerushalmi. If so, the Rambam's words 'before the sun rises' mean the beginning of its rising, namely netz hachamah, as Maharai first assumed. What the Tur wrote, 'a little before netz hachamah,' means that the beginning of Krias Shema should be a little before netz hachamah so that he finishes it with its blessings at netz hachamah.
And so wrote in Darkei Moshe, meaning that the Darkei Moshe also agrees with the first reading, like the measure of an hour. But his explanation is not like the Bach and not like Beis Yosef; rather, from netz hachamah itself, without joining the reading time before netz, until the completion of the rising of the entire body of the sun is an hour. It appears to me that regarding the law there is no difference, etc., for even the one whose text reads a tenth of an hour does not mean that from netz until the completion of its rising is only a tenth of an hour.
For certainly, according to everyone, from netz until the completion of its rising is an hour. As the Darkei Moshe wrote, that this is known to one who knows even a little astronomy. Rather, the one whose text reads a tenth of an hour means that the Rambam gives the measure from the beginning of Krias Shema until netz hachamah.
And 'when the sun rises' means the beginning of its rising, as Maharai first assumed. But the one whose text reads the measure of an hour gives the measure from the beginning of netz hachamah until the rising of the whole body of the sun, as the conclusion of Maharai and Darkei Moshe.
