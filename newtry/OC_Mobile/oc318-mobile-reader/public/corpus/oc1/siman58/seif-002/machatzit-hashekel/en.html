(Seif katan 2) The measure, etc.—it implies in Beit Yosef "when he begins to read," etc., thus it should be.

Thus said the Tur: "And mitzvah min hamuvchar," etc.—to read it slightly before hanetz hachama so that he finishes it and its blessings with hanetz hachama and adjoins to it the tefillah, etc.; and so too Rambam in his mitzvot—that one begins before hanetz hachama so that he finishes reading and blessing with alot hashachar. And this measure is like the measure of one hour before the sun rises, etc. And by the simple understanding of what he said "before the sun rises"—meaning the beginning of its rise, and that is alot hashachar; and on this Mahari Abuhav challenged: how did the Tur call this "slightly," since the Tur wrote "slightly before hanetz hachama"? Therefore Mahari Abuhav explained that what Rambam said "before the sun rises" means the entire body of the sun rises, etc., in Beit Yosef; and since it entered one's mind that what Rambam wrote—that it is the measure of an hour until the sun rises—the beginning of the hour is from the beginning of the time of Keriat Shema and the end of the hour is at hanetz hachama. And on this the language of the Tur that called it "slightly" is difficult for him; therefore he explained that at the end it is from when the entire body of the sun rises—meaning that regarding the beginning of the hour it remains as it entered one's mind, namely from the beginning of the time of Keriat Shema; for he did not write in his answer that he also retracted from this. And Kesef Mishnah wrote that this is indeed the principal view, thus it should be.

And Bach agreed to the first girsa—that is, like the measure of an hour. And Bach explained that meaning from alot hashachar until hanetz hachama is the measure of an hour, and he brought proof from the Yerushalmi; if so what Rambam said "before the sun rises" means the beginning of its rise, namely hanetz hachama, and like the view of Mahari Abuhav. And what the Tur said "slightly before hanetz hachama" means the beginning of Keriat Shema shall be slightly before hanetz hachama so that he finishes with its blessings at hanetz hachama, etc.

And so too in Darkhei Moshe—meaning that Darkhei Moshe also agrees to girsa aleph like the measure of an hour; but his explanation is not like Bach and not like Beit Yosef, rather from hanetz hachama literally (without adding the time of reading before alot hashachar) until the completion of the rise of the entire body of the sun is an hour. And it seems that for the law there is no difference, etc.—for even one who has the girsa "a tenth of an hour" does not mean from alot hashachar until the end of its rise is only a tenth of an hour.

For certainly according to all from alot hashachar until the end of its rise is an hour—as Darkhei Moshe wrote, known to one who knows a little in the wisdom of astronomy; only one who has the girsa "a tenth of an hour"—Rambam's intention is to give a measure from the beginning of Keriat Shema until hanetz hachama.

And "until the sun rises" means the beginning of its rise, like the view of Mahari Abuhav. And one who has the girsa "measure of an hour" gave a measure from the beginning of alot hashachar until the rise of the entire body of the sun, as the conclusion of Mahari Abuhav and Darkhei Moshe.
