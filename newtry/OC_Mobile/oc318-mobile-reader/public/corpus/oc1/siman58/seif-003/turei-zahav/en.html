In a place where there are bands of wild animals, etc. Rabbeinu Yonah and Rashba proved this from the fact that here the authorities permit reciting Krias Shema after dawn, whereas below, in siman 89, they rule that one should not recite it at home, but should recite Krias Shema and pray on the road. Here, therefore, the case must be one in which it is impossible to recite on the road. Accordingly, the Tur writes here that he must set out on the road, while below he writes that he wishes to set out, as the Beit Yosef explains in the name of Mahari Abuhav. My father-in-law, the Bach, sought to reject this distinction. He explained that here the person can recite Krias Shema and pray at home before leaving, whereas below he cannot do both. This distinction is incorrect. When there are no bands of wild animals on the road, there is certainly no permission to recite before the proper time for Krias Shema. The Bach himself retracted this view in siman 89.
