3) There: Its time extends until the end of three hours, which is one quarter of the day. Rambam writes in his commentary to the Mishnah, in the first chapter of Berachos: "You should know that all the hours mentioned throughout the Mishnah are seasonal hours. The meaning of seasonal hours is that the day contains twelve hours and the night contains twelve hours. Thus, when it says 'until three hours,' it is as though it said 'until one quarter of the day has passed,' whether the day falls during the summer solstice or the winter solstice." End quote. Beis Yosef. Rama likewise writes this below in siman 233, seif 1. Perishah, paragraph 2; Pri Chadash, end of paragraph 2; Shaagas Aryeh, siman 5, cited by Shaarei Teshuvah, paragraph 1; Shulchan Aruch HaRav, paragraph 3; Chayei Adam, rule 21, paragraph 3; Kitzur Shulchan Aruch, siman 17, paragraph 1. See the gloss Pe'as HaShulchan there, where the author writes that he resolved Pnei Yehoshua's difficulty concerning this in the later edition of his commentary on tractate Berachos. He also cites additional authorities who prove Rambam's position. The gaon and kabbalist Rabbi Yaakov Chagiz, of blessed memory, likewise testifies in his commentary Etz Chaim on the Mishnah that this ruling has been universally accepted throughout Israel, and that seasonal hours are used; see there. Ben Ish Chai, parashas Va'eira, paragraph 5, writes likewise.
