(3) There [its time] extends until the end of three hours, which is a fourth of the day. Rambam wrote in his commentary on the Mishnah, ch. 1 of Berakhot, and said: Know that these hours mentioned throughout the Mishnah are seasonal hours (shaot zemaniyyot). The meaning of "zemaniyyot" is hours such that there are twelve hours in the day and twelve hours in the night; and when he said "until three hours" it is as if he said until the end of a fourth of the day—whether the day be in the season of Tammuz or the season of Tevet, etc. Beit Yosef. And so too Mor Uketzi wrote below in siman 233 seif 1. Perishah, sign 2. Peri Chadash siman 60 sign 2. Sha'agat Aryeh siman 5, and they brought it in Sha'arei Teshuvah sign 1. Raz, sign 3. Chayei Adam klal 21 sign 3. Kitzur Shulchan Aruch siman 17 sign 1. And see Pa'at HaShulchan there, who wrote that he explained what Penei Yehoshua wrote in the later edition on tractate Berakhot, and brought more poskim who proved like the words of Rambam; and also the renowned kabbalist Mahari Chagiz in his commentary Etz Chaim on Mishnayot testified that thus is the accepted practice throughout Israel—that we calculate seasonal hours; see there. And so too Ben Ish Chai, parashat Vaeira sign 5.
