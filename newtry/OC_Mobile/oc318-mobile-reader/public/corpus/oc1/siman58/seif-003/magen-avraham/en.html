Until “upon your heart.” This requires study. This is the Rashba's view, that one must remain standing until “upon your heart.” Yet the Beit Yosef rules in siman 63 that one need stand only for the first verse. His rulings therefore appear contradictory. One can answer with difficulty that he nevertheless holds that when it is impossible to remain standing until “upon your heart,” it is preferable to recite Krias Shema at home from dawn.
He may recite Yotzer Or. As did the members of the Temple watch. Beit Yosef in the name of Rashba. Rashba writes in his novellae that, although one opinion there says that they did not recite Yotzer Or and recited it only when its time arrived, that suggestion is merely a rejection. The conclusion is that they did recite Yotzer Or. Rashba follows his own position, ruling unlike Rav Hai Gaon. However, according to what the Beit Yosef writes in siman 60 in the name of Rav Hai, that the order of the blessings is not indispensable, it appears that they did not recite Yotzer Or until its time arrived. Rashba likewise writes in Responsa, siman 318, that throughout the Temple era Ahavah Rabbah was recited by itself. It therefore appears to me that Yotzer Or should not be recited so early, until one can at least distinguish between blue and white. See what I wrote in seif 5 in the name of Ramban.
