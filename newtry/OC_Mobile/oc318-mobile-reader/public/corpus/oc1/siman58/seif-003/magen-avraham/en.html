One who is compelled. And likewise when there is burial of a dead person in the city, or circumcision, or Erev Yom Kippur when one is at ease in mercy—he may read from alot hashachar (thus Kesef Mishnah, Rambam).

Until "upon your heart." It requires investigation—for this is Rashba's view who holds one must stand until "upon your heart"; but Beit Yosef ruled in siman 63 that one need not stand except in the first verse—and if so his words contradict each other; and it is possible to push aside and say that nevertheless he holds that if one cannot stand until "upon your heart," it is better to read from alot hashachar in his house.

"Is read Yotzer Or." As the men of the watch (Beit Yosef, Rashba)—and Rashba wrote in his novellae; and even though there is one who says there they did not say Yotzer Or, and when the time arrived they would say Yotzer Or—that is merely a deferral in the world; and by conclusion they would say Yotzer Or, etc.—and he follows his view who ruled not like Rav Hai; but according to what is written in siman 60 in Beit Yosef in the name of Rav Hai that the order of blessings does not impede—it implies he holds they did not say Yotzer Or until its time arrived; and so too in Rashba's responsum siman 318 that all the days of the Temple it was called Ahavah Rabbah alone, etc. Therefore it seems one should not say Yotzer Or so much at dawn until one recognizes at least between tekhelet and white—see siman 65 in the name of Ramban.
