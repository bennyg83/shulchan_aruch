The Gemara did not state the greater novelty that both may be recited after dawn, because one should not do so. If he treats that time as night, how can he then treat it as day? The Beit Yosef asks why the Tur did not record this. The Mechaber records it in seif 5. Taz answers that it is obvious. Rabbi Shimon bar Yochai came to teach that dawn begins the time for the daytime Krias Shema. Taz thus teaches us this, while there is no need to teach that one may recite one immediately before dawn and one immediately afterward, because that too is obvious, since dawn separates them as a new time. I do not know, however, why Taz omitted the case of reciting twice, one immediately before sunrise and one immediately after sunrise, which would not be contradictory, for sunrise is not a complete dividing point. Regarding the evening Krias Shema, one who recites after dawn has not fulfilled the obligation after the fact unless he was drunk, ill, or the like, as written in siman 235. This is why the Gemara says that Rabbi Shimon bar Yochai is worthy to rely upon specifically in pressing circumstances. Here, regarding the daytime Krias Shema, even after the fact and without pressing circumstances, even one who was negligent has fulfilled his obligation. See Peri Chadash, who writes that because the Gemara says regarding the second teaching that Rabbi Shimon is worthy to rely upon, the same applies to the first teaching. This is incorrect, because the first teaching of Rabbi Shimon bar Yochai concerns an after-the-fact case. If one recites twice after dawn, once before he can recognize an acquaintance four amos away and once afterward, it appears that this is permitted. I later saw that Eliyah Rabbah, letter 9, cites this in the name of Minchas Kohen, by Rabbi Avraham HaKohen Pimentel, but writes that the Rosh does not imply so. Rabbi Shimon bar Yochai, in the name of Rabbi Akiva, stated one recitation after sunrise, rather than one after the time of recognizing an acquaintance. This may be because it is obvious that the principal beginning of daytime is after one can recognize an acquaintance, and that time therefore separates the two recitations. Between recognition and sunrise, however, one might have thought there is no separation. The Gemara therefore teaches otherwise. The Mechaber's unqualified wording implies unlike Minchas Kohen. This requires study. See what I wrote regarding Magen Avraham. In the abridged rulings of the Rosh it states that if one did not recite the evening Krias Shema, he should wait to recite the morning one until after sunrise. This implies that he may not recite it before sunrise, even after the time of recognizing an acquaintance, unlike Minchas Kohen. See the Beit Yosef, who learns from there that he should recite the evening Krias Shema after dawn and wait before reciting the morning one, rather than reciting the morning one immediately after dawn. Otherwise he actively loses the evening Krias Shema as soon as sunrise occurs. I do not understand this, for it appears that this is only a short time. [Seif 7 says there is compensation, but he nevertheless loses its proper time and has only compensation.] In any case, even if we say that when he waits some time between them and does not recite them consecutively they are not contradictory, once he has treated the time as day, how can he return and afterward treat it as night? This requires study.
