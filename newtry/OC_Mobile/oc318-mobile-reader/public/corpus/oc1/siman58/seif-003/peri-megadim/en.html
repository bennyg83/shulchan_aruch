He went out on Tur 58: the Rosh on mishnah Berakhot 8b: R. Shimon [ben Yochai] sometimes reads once before alot ha-shachar and once after alot ha-shachar—for he did not say a novelty twice after alot ha-shachar, that one should not do so, for from night how can he make day; and Beit Yosef challenged: why did Tur not write thus; and Machabel in Biur haGra wrote it and it seems obvious; and R. Shimon came to inform that from the outset he begins the daytime Kriat Shema; and Taz informed us of this, and there is no need to inform of once before alot ha-shachar and once after alot ha-shachar adjacent—for this too is obvious that there is an interruption of alot ha-shachar. However, that which Taz omitted twice before netz and immediately after netz and they are not contradictory—it may be said why: for sunrise of the sun is not a complete interruption. And here regarding nighttime Kriat Shema bedi'eved after alot ha-shachar—he does not fulfill, for otherwise the sons of R. Gamliel would be bedi'eved; and specifically one who is drunk or sick and the like—as Perishah wrote; and this is what he said: it is better R. Shimon in a time of pressure specifically; and here regarding daytime [Shema], bedi'eved even without pressure—even a deliberate transgressor fulfills—and Pri Chadash wrote: from the other statement, as R. Shimon's statement applies here, so does the first statement; and the first is not R. Shimon discussing bedi'eved. And if he reads two [Shemas] after alot ha-shachar—one before he sees his fellow from afar four amos and one after he sees—permitted; and again I saw in Avodat haRabim letter 9 he brought thus in the name of Minchat Kohen (for the Rokeach Apfimentil) and the Rosh wrote it does not imply thus; and here what R. Shimon said in the name of R. Elazar: once after netz and not once after one sees his fellow—it may be said this is obvious that the essence of the beginning of the day is after one sees, and the time that interrupts is not so after one sees and netz—I would say there is no interruption; we hold thus and the author's conclusion implies not like Minchat Kohen; requires examination; and it is written in Magen Avraham from this. And in summary the Rosh ruled: if he did not read the nighttime [Shema], he waits for the morning [Shema] until after netz—implies before netz, even after he sees his fellow—not like Minchat Kohen. And Abbayi: from there one learns he reads the nighttime [Shema] after alot ha-shachar and the daytime he waits—not that he reads the daytime after alot ha-shachar—for then he loses with his own hands the evening [prayer] immediately when the sun shines; and I did not understand that this implies it is a short time (and in siman 57 some say there are make-up [recitations]—nevertheless he lost its time only make-up)—behold without this, even if we say all the time it interrupts in between and he does not read continuously, nevertheless since he made day how can he return and make afterward night; requires examination:
