(12) One who is compelled—and likewise when there is burial of a dead person in the city, or circumcision, or Erev Yom Kippur when one is at ease in mercy—he may recite from alot hashachar.

(13) Rises early—meaning he has no leisure to wait and delay until the time of recognizing one's fellow, as when he must set out on the road; and likewise other matters like this when he has no leisure as found in Tur.

(14) In any case—if he wishes to read in the middle of the road, and it deals with when his walking time is with the caravan or anything like this, he may continue until the end of the time of Keriat Shema.

(15) He may, etc.—and he also may pray in his house after Keriat Shema and its blessings.

(16) To read it—and he puts on tefillin and does not bless over them before the time arrives of recognizing his fellow at a distance of four amot and recognizing him—as explained in siman 34.

(17) Yotzer Or—and Magen Avraham and Pri Megadim ruled one should not say Yotzer Or so much at dawn until one recognizes between tekhelet and white—and that is the time until one sees his fellow at the aforesaid four amot. Also in Biur HaGra he sides with Magen Avraham; nevertheless lekhatchilah one must wait if he can on account of the mitzvah of tefillin—as explained.
