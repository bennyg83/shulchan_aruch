Seif 3, And one who, etc. This follows from the Gemara there on 8b, where the halachah is ruled in accordance with Rabbi Shimon bar Yochai. The commentators ask that we otherwise rule like the Others and like the vasikin. The Rif and the other poskim explain that Rabbi Shimon bar Yochai refers to an after-the-fact case, or alternatively to pressing circumstances, such as one who must arise early to travel and similar cases, just as the Gemara says regarding Rabbi Shimon bar Yochai's other ruling that it applies in pressing circumstances. They challenged this from the ruling concerning one who arose early, where it says that when the proper time arrives he should recite. The Rashba answers that the case in chapter 1 concerns one who departs from an inhabited place, while Rabbeinu Yonah answers that the members of the caravan will not wait for him, as they explain. The statement in Yoma that one who recites with the men of the watch has not fulfilled his obligation means that he has not fulfilled the choicest manner of the mitzvah, as Beit Yosef explains. See Tosafos 12a, s.v. Mishum, where there is a scribal error and the text should read, ‘as stated in Yoma, one who recites, etc.’ Rashi there likewise explains that the baraisa can be understood as referring to the baraisa in Yoma, meaning that it does not accept Rabbi Shimon bar Yochai's ruling. This is also explicit in the Yerushalmi, chapter 1 of Berachos, where that baraisa is shown to disagree with Rabbi Shimon bar Yochai. The Yerushalmi says regarding Rabban Gamliel that his ruling follows Rabbi Shimon, who taught in Rabbi Shimon's name that one may recite one before dawn and one after dawn. It then asks whether, just as Rabban Gamliel follows Rabbi Shimon regarding the evening, the same applies in the morning, or whether the ruling follows Rabbi Zeira, brother of Rav Chana, that one who recites with the men of the watch has not fulfilled his obligation. This answers the Baal HaMaor's objection to the Rif from that baraisa, for the Rif ruled like Rabbi Shimon bar Yochai, who disagrees with it. The Bavli also indicates this, for Rabbi Yehoshua ben Levi says that the halachah follows Rabbi Shimon bar Yochai. Since a ruling of halachah implies a dispute, the opposing view must be the aforementioned baraisa. Rabbi Yehoshua ben Levi ruled like Rabbi Shimon bar Yochai, and the Rif therefore did so as well. The Tur, however, explains that Rabbi Shimon bar Yochai applies only occasionally, but one who acts this way regularly has not fulfilled his obligation even after the fact. In that type of case the ruling in Yoma applies. This is why the Tur writes that if he recited it after the fact he has fulfilled his obligation and need not recite it again, provided that he is not accustomed to doing so. The Rashba and Shulchan Aruch also appear to rule this way.
And it is also proper, etc. This is derived from the men of the watch, meaning from Reish Lakish, who says that they recited Yotzer Or. This requires study, for the Gemara there concludes that they recited Ahavah Rabbah and not Yotzer Or, since its time had not yet arrived. The Yerushalmi also says so, and the Rambam rules accordingly. According to Tosafos' interpretation, this conclusion would not accord with the halachah.
