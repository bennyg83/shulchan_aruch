(Seif katan 5) "Is read," etc.—and as Maggid Mishneh, etc.—for it says in Berakhot 11b: we learned there—the overseer said to them (the men of the watch—and they are the priests serving in the Temple who were busy all day with the work of offerings, therefore they preceded to read Keriat Shema): bless one blessing—and that is the blessing before Keriat Shema—and they blessed and read Shema and "if then you heed," etc. And the Gemara asked: what is "one blessing"? R' Yehuda said in the name of Shmuel: Ahavah Rabbah; and R' Zerika said in the name of R' Ami in the name of R' Shimon ben Lakish: Yotzer Or. And R' Yitzchak bar Yehuda said: that of R' Zerika was stated by inference, for he said in the name of Rashbi on that mishnah: this teaches that blessings do not impede one another (that if he blessed one blessing alone, the second that he did not bless does not impede him and he fulfills at least through the blessing that he read). If so, if it were Ahavah Rabbah, they would say well—it is proven they do not impede; for since they said Yotzer Or, certainly the time of Ahavah Rabbah has arrived—for even at night the time of Ahavah Rabbah applies, and nevertheless they did not say Ahavah Rabbah; therefore because they did not have time since they were busy with the work of offerings. If so, certainly afterward they also did not say Ahavah Rabbah—for there was no time, and nevertheless they said Yotzer Or; therefore it must be that they fulfilled through the blessing of Yotzer Or. It is impossible that they said Ahavah Rabbah—for perhaps blessings impede one another if he said only one blessing; only the men of the watch in truth read afterward also Yotzer Or, but at the hour when they read Keriat Shema and said Ahavah Rabbah, the time for Yotzer Or had not yet arrived since they preceded to read—and it concludes: and if from inference, what (meaning it is well proven)? And I will always say Ahavah Rabbah, and what is "this teaches blessings do not impede"—the order of blessings of Shacharit? Although they preceded Ahavah Rabbah to Yotzer Or, nevertheless they fulfilled, end of the Gemara. And on this Rashba challenged: behold per the Gemara's deferral they said Ahavah Rabbah and Yotzer Or—on the contrary, they were not permitted then according to that deferral since the time had not yet arrived, although alot hashachar had already risen; therefore Rashba explained that this is only a mere deferral and one cannot prove from inference; but in truth, since R' Zerika said in the name of Rashbi that they said Yotzer Or, presumably they concluded from their rabbis that Rashbi said so explicitly.

That the order of blessings does not impede—but blessings impede one another; that is, if he did not bless only one blessing alone, he does not fulfill through that other blessing either. And it is explained in Beit Yosef siman 60 because he ruled like the Gemara's deferral above—that even for Rashbi they said Ahavah Rabbah and not Yotzer Or since the time had not arrived. When the time of Yotzer Or arrived they said Yotzer Or, and it is not proven except that their order does not impede; see there in Beit Yosef. Behold Rav Hai must hold that they were not permitted to read Yotzer Or although alot hashachar had risen, unlike Rashba.

And so Rashba in responsum, etc.—like the words of Rav Hai and not like what Rashba wrote in his novellae; for he wrote that they would say only Ahavah Rabbah—meaning like the Gemara's deferral, and afterward when the time arrived they said Yotzer Or.
