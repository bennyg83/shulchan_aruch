(Seif katan 5) It is called, etc. And like the men of the mishmar, etc. It is stated in Berachos 11b: there we learned that the appointed officer said to them, meaning to the men of the mishmar, the kohanim serving in the Temple, who were occupied all day with the sacrificial service and therefore read Krias Shema early: bless one blessing, namely one blessing before Krias Shema, and they blessed and read Shema, Vehayah im shamoa, and Vayomer. The Gemara asks which one blessing it was. Rav Yehudah said in the name of Shmuel: Ahavah Rabbah. Rabbi Zerika said in the name of Rabbi Ami in the name of Rabbi Shimon ben Lakish: Yotzer Or. Rabbi Yitzchak bar Yosef said that the statement of Rabbi Zerika was said by inference, for Rabbi Shimon ben Lakish said regarding that Mishnah: this teaches that the blessings do not prevent one another. If one says that they said Yotzer Or, it is well proven that the blessings do not prevent, since if they said Yotzer Or, the time for Ahavah Rabbah had certainly arrived, for even at night it is the time for Ahavah Rabbah, and nevertheless they did not say Ahavah Rabbah, evidently because they lacked time due to the sacrificial service. If so, certainly afterward they did not say Ahavah Rabbah, since they had no time, and nevertheless they said Yotzer Or, so they fulfilled the blessing of Yotzer Or. But if one says they said Ahavah Rabbah, perhaps the blessings do prevent one another, and the men of the mishmar in fact later read Yotzer Or as well; only at the time they read Krias Shema and said Ahavah Rabbah, the time for Yotzer Or had not yet arrived because they were reading early. The Gemara concludes that even if this is by inference, what of it; and it rejects that they may always have said Ahavah Rabbah, and what is meant by this teaches that the blessings do not prevent is that the order of the blessings does not prevent. Although they advanced Ahavah Rabbah before Yotzer Or, nevertheless they fulfilled. On this the Rashba asked: according to the Gemara's rejection, they said both Ahavah Rabbah and Yotzer Or; on the contrary, according to that rejection they were not permitted to say Yotzer Or then, because its time had not yet arrived, even though alot hashachar had already risen. To this the Rashba answered that this is only a mere rejection, showing that no proof can be brought from inference. But in truth, since Rabbi Zerika said in the name of Rabbi Shimon ben Lakish that they said Yotzer Or, presumably he learned it from his teacher that Rabbi Shimon ben Lakish said so explicitly.
That the order of blessings does not prevent. But the blessings do prevent one another, meaning that if he blessed only one blessing, he did not fulfill even that blessing. This is explained in Beis Yosef, siman 60, because he ruled like the Gemara's rejection mentioned above, that even according to Rabbi Shimon ben Lakish they said Ahavah Rabbah and not Yotzer Or, since its time had not yet arrived; and when the time for Yotzer Or arrived, they said Yotzer Or. This proves only that their order does not prevent; see Beis Yosef. Thus Rav Hai necessarily holds that they were not permitted to read Yotzer Or even though alot hashachar had risen, unlike the Rashba.
And so wrote the Rashba in a responsum, etc. like the words of Rabbeinu Hai, and not as the Rashba wrote in his novellae. For he wrote that they would say Ahavah Rabbah alone, implying like the Gemara's rejection, and afterward, when the time arrived, they would say Yotzer Or.
