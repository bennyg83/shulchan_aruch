[5] To advance, etc. The Levush wrote that even if he has no tzitzis and tefillin, he should read and not wait; afterward, when they arrive, he should put them on and read with them Krias Shema, or another passage, or a chapter of Tehillim. The Migdal David wrote at length to challenge the Levush, that it is permitted to wait, and if he was compelled until the time passed, he was compelled. In Ateres Tzvi, siman 66, he rejected his words from the Gemara in Berachos 14, that Rav washed his hands, read Krias Shema, put on tefillin, and prayed, because the messenger had acted wrongly. Rashi explains that the messenger erred by delaying at first in bringing him tefillin, and Rav thought he would not go and that the time of Krias Shema had arrived, so he needed to read it lest the time pass, and when the messenger arrived with the tefillin he put them on. I say that from this very explanation of Rashi the opposite is clear: specifically because he thought the messenger would not go, and perhaps he would not have tefillin even after the time of Krias Shema, he read; but if he had thought the messenger would go, even if the time of Krias Shema would pass he would have waited. I saw that in Chokhmat Shlomo he deletes the words that he thought he would not go; see there. But in all the books it appears, and Beis Yosef and Nachalas Tzvi also quote it so. However, one may explain Rashi's intent otherwise, and this requires study; so too the Lechem Chamudos explains on page 7. Regarding what the Levush wrote that he should read a passage or chapter of Tehillim of Tehillim, he wrote that this is unnecessary, for by putting them on alone he performs a mitzvah; see there. It appears that only if the time of Krias Shema will pass should he not wait, but during the time he should wait for tzitzis and tefillin. Possibly, if the congregation is praying, he need not wait and then pray alone. Although siman 66 implies that he interrupts by putting on tefillin between redemption and prayer, and from siman 111 it is already known that joining redemption to prayer is preferable to prayer with the congregation, nevertheless one may say that the interruption of putting on tefillin or blessing on tefillin is not called such an interruption, since it is for the need of prayer, and is somewhat comparable to 'mix for the oxen' below in siman 163. A proof to this appears to me from Responsa Rashba, siman 185. Nevertheless it seems one should wait in this matter, since even when the time of Krias Shema will pass there is an authority who says to wait, as we stated. I later found in Magen Avraham, siman 66, that he ruled so, although his proofs are rejected within my general discussion there; nevertheless I have explained that as the practical law, the main ruling is to wait. See also siman 100.
