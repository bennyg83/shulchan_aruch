[5] "To precede," etc. Levush wrote: even if he has no tzitzit and tefillin he should recite and not wait; and afterward when they come he will put them on and recite Shema in them, or another section or a psalm—until here. And in Migdal David he expanded to challenge Levush that one may wait; and if one is compelled until the time passes, one is compelled; and in Ataret Tzvi siman 66 he rejected his words, for the Talmud [Berakhot] folio 14 says: Rav Mesharshya raised his hand and recited Kriat Shema and put on tefillin and prayed—because he was an agent who was late, and Rashi explained: the agent spoiled matters by delaying to bring him tefillin initially, and he considered that he would not go and the time of Kriat Shema arrived and he was forced to recite lest the time pass; and when the agent of the tefillin arrived he put them on—until here. And I say: on the contrary, from Rashi's explanation this is proven the opposite: specifically since he considered he would not go, if so perhaps he had no tefillin even after the time of Kriat Shema, therefore he recited; but if he had considered he would go, even though the time of Kriat Shema would pass he would have waited. And I have seen in Chochmat Shlomo he erases those words "and he considered he would not go"—see there; but in all the books it is found, and likewise Beit Yosef and Nachalat Tzvi copied thus. However, one may interpret Rashi's intention: it appears that if the agent of the tefillin had not arrived he would have prayed immediately, for he did not wait for a wonder-worker; and it is difficult for me why he did not wait, behold the time of prayer is later than Kriat Shema by one hour; but rather he considered he would not go; and there is more to interpret in it, and it requires examination; and likewise he interpreted in Lechem Chamudot folio 7. But regarding what Levush wrote that he should read a section or a psalm of Tehillim—he wrote one need not, for in putting them on alone he fulfills a mitzvah—see there; and it appears specifically if the time of Kriat Shema will pass he should not wait; but for a while he should wait for tzitzit and tefillin. And it is possible: if the congregation is praying, he need not wait to pray afterward individually; and even though it appears in siman 66 that one interrupts for putting on tefillin between redemption and prayer, and we already heard from siman 111 that adjoining redemption to prayer is preferable to communal prayer—in any case one may say that the interruption of putting on or blessing on tefillin is not called such a great interruption, since it is needed for prayer and is somewhat like gavila for oxen later in siman 163; and proof for this appears to me from the responsum of Rashba siman 185. And in any case it appears one should wait in this, since even regarding passing the time of Kriat Shema there are authorities who say to wait, as we said. Again I found in Magen Avraham siman 66 that he ruled thus, even though his proofs are rejected in general—see there; and in any case I already explained that for practical halakhah the main thing is to wait.
