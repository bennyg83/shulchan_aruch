The Rosh writes that the two rulings of Rabbi Shimon bar Yochai therefore do not conflict. Likewise, the ruling there in accordance with Rabban Gamliel means that without compelling circumstances the evening Krias Shema may be recited only until dawn, but under compulsion until sunrise; similarly, the morning Krias Shema begins at its normal time as explained above, but under compulsion it may be recited from dawn. Tosafos there, s.v. Le'olam, and in Yoma asks that we otherwise rule like the Others, and that Rabbi Akiva himself stated that the time is when one can distinguish between a donkey and a wild donkey, whereas here the time without compulsion is said to begin at sunrise. Tosafos answers that the novelty is that the evening Krias Shema cannot be recited after sunrise even under compulsion. This is the meaning of the words, ‘since it is still, etc.,’ and the Rosh writes likewise. Tosafos further answers that the novelty is that one cannot recite both the evening and morning Krias Shema during the same period. The Rosh writes in his abridged rulings, and Beit Yosef explains, that one should then recite the evening Krias Shema, since the morning Krias Shema can still be recited afterward. This is the meaning of, ‘And if he was, etc.’ The Tur omitted this because he follows the first answer, while Beit Yosef holds that both answers are accepted as halachah.
