If one was compelled, etc. As is written there explicitly in the Gemara: when he came, etc., in a time of pressure; and so wrote the Rosh; and now they do not argue—the [ruling] of R. Shimon ben Yochai against itself—and likewise that which is ruled there like R. Gamliel: without compulsion until [the time of] the morning Shema, and under compulsion until sunrise; and likewise regarding the morning Kriat Shema without compulsion as mentioned, and under compulsion from [the time of] the morning Shema; and Tosafot challenged there s.v. "forever," etc., and in Yoma: behold we hold like "others," and R. Akiva himself said between a donkey and a wild ass, and here he said without compulsion from sunrise; and he explained there and there that the Gemara teaches us that at night one does not fulfill after sunrise even under compulsion; and this is its meaning: since it still exists, etc.; and likewise the Rosh; and further he explained that the Gemara teaches us that one cannot read both; and he wrote in hints; and Beit Yosef [holds] that one who reads the evening [Shema] of the morning may read it afterward; and this is its meaning: and if he was, etc.; and in Tur he omitted it, for he holds like the first explanation; and Beit Yosef holds that both are true for practical halakhah.
