If one was compelled [and did not recite evening Shema until daybreak]. It is difficult for me from what he wrote regarding the matter of rising—that it is permitted to recite the morning Keriat Shema until three hours since we follow the sons of kings; if so, the evening Keriat Shema should also be read until three hours, since the verse says "when you lie down," and the sons of kings are lying down. Later I found in Milchamot at the beginning of Berakhot, and this is its language: After [the time of] evening prayer, one fulfills [Shema] at the time of night, since there are people who sleep at that hour; and even though they are a minority, since the Merciful One expressed it in singular language—"when you lie down" means your lying down, as they expounded in the first chapter of Berakhot—since there are people who sleep and he too has not yet recited, he may recite until hanetz hachama; and from then onward there is no one sleeping. And if [one objects] because of the sons of kings—they are awake in their beds and do not wish to rise until three hours, etc. And regarding morning Keriat Shema: although it requires morning and is not [valid] all day, since it is written "when you rise," and the majority of people do not rise until one recognizes his fellow; and further because they established the parashah of tzitzit in Keriat Shema, as it is written therein "and you shall see it"—therefore they established its time from when one can recognize between tekhelet and white, and one who reads before then has not fulfilled this mitzvah. And this is what [it means that] one who reads with the people of the watch does not fulfill [properly]—because they precede and read before this time, and the time of tzitzit has not yet arrived, nor the time of the blessing Yotzer Or—end quote. And the Maggid [wrote] in the name of Chaviva [in the] Rashba: Granted, the day is truly day—we follow the sons of kings; but to read the [Shema] of the night during the day because of the sons of kings we do not say, since they are a minority of a minority—end quote. And as has been explained, the principal [matter] in the Gemara implies that the sons of kings sleep until three hours, since David said "My eyes preceded the watches," etc.; and so is the language in the Gemara that all kings sleep until three hours.
