Why is this different from the matter of rising, where one may recite the morning Krias Shema until three hours because we follow the schedule of princes? If so, the evening Krias Shema should likewise be recitable until three hours, under “when you lie down,” since princes are lying down. I later found in Milchamos at the beginning of Berachos: After dawn one may fulfill the evening obligation, because some people are sleeping at that time. Although they are a minority, since the Torah expressed it in the singular, “when you lie down,” it means your own lying down, as expounded in the first chapter of Berachos. Since some people sleep then and he has not yet recited it, he may recite it until sunrise. From then onward no one is sleeping. As for princes, they are awake in their beds and merely do not wish to rise until three hours. Regarding the morning Krias Shema, although it requires morning and is not recitable all day, since the verse says “when you rise,” and most people do not rise before one can recognize an acquaintance, and because the Torah fixed the passage of tzitzis within Krias Shema, where it says “you shall see it,” its time was established from when one can distinguish between blue and white. One who recites earlier has not fulfilled this mitzvah. This is the meaning of the statement that one who recites with the members of the watch has not fulfilled his obligation, because they recited too early, before this time, when neither the time for tzitzis nor the time for Yotzer Or had yet arrived. I also found in the name of Rashba's novellae: Regarding daytime, it is truly day, so we follow princes. But we do not call daytime “night” because of princes, since they are an exceptionally small minority. This appears to me the primary explanation. The Gemara implies that princes sleep until three hours, for David said, “My eyes preceded the watches.” See there. The Gemara's language also implies that all kings sleep until three hours.
