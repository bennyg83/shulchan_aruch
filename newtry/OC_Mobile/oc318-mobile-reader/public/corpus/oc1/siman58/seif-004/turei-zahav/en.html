He has fulfilled after the fact. The Beit Yosef asks why the Tur did not write that when one was prevented by circumstances beyond his control from reciting the evening Krias Shema until after dawn, and he must also set out and cannot recite the daytime Krias Shema at its proper time, he may not recite the two recitations consecutively after dawn, one for night and one for day. It appears to me that this is obvious and did not need to be written at all. Once he treats that time as night, how can he then treat it as day? The Rosh does not say that the Gemara came specifically to teach this point. Rather, he writes that it is not reasonable to act so, indicating that this follows from logic. The Gemara's principal novelty is that one may recite the two recitations consecutively, one before dawn and one after dawn. It teaches that dawn begins a new time, from which point daytime begins for this purpose. Since the Tur has explicitly taught us here that after dawn begins the time for the daytime Krias Shema, and has also taught us in siman 235 that one may delay the evening Krias Shema until dawn, we can easily know that, when necessary, the two may be recited consecutively, since a new time, dawn, separates them. The Beit Yosef therefore writes without basis that the Rosh and Tur were uncertain about this.
