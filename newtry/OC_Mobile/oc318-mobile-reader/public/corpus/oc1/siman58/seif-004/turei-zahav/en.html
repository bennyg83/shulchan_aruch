He fulfilled bedi'eved. In Beit Yosef he challenged: why did Tur not write that if he was compelled from reciting the evening Kriat Shema until after alot ha-shachar, and must also remain on the road and cannot recite the daytime Kriat Shema at its time—for one may not read two adjacent [Shemas] after alot ha-shachar rises and there will be one of night and one of day—it seems this is obvious and he did not need to write it at all; for since he makes that time night, how can he make day; and the Rosh does not say the Talmud teaches us this matter, but rather writes that it is not reasonable to do so—implies from reasoning; and the essence of the rabbinic [teaching] of the Talmud that one may read two adjacent—one before alot ha-shachar and one after it—that it teaches that alot ha-shachar is the beginning of another time; from here onward day begins for this; and since our Rabbi revealed to us here in explanation that after alot ha-shachar is the beginning of daytime Kriat Shema, and also revealed to us in siman 235 that one may wait for the nighttime [Shema] until alot ha-shachar—then we simply know that one may read these two adjacent if needed, since there is a renewed time between them, namely alot ha-shachar; and in vain Beit Yosef wrote that the Rosh and our Rabbi are uncertain about this:
