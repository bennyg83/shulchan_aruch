From dawn. The Magen Avraham below, siman 89, seif katan 8, the Peri Megadim, and the Peri Chadash there explain that dawn occurs before the eastern sky has brightened. I saw that Eliyah Rabbah there disagrees with Magen Avraham and maintains that dawn is when the eastern sky brightens, not earlier. I have now found that he is not alone in this view. Biur HaGra, in Shenos Eliyahu to Berachos chapter 1, on the Mishnah's words “until dawn rises,” writes that dawn means the brightening of the eastern sky. He writes likewise there, s.v. “they enter,” that dawn is when the eastern sky has brightened. Matteh Yehudah also adopts this view. It appears to me that their position is supported by the Rashba, cited by the Beit Yosef in this siman, who writes that once dawn has risen one may properly recite Yotzer Or, as did the members of the Temple watch. The members of the watch recited Yotzer Or a considerable time after slaughtering the daily offering, as Rashi explains to Berachos 11b, s.v. “they blessed,” while the daily offering was slaughtered only after the eastern sky had brightened, as stated in Yoma at the beginning of chapter 3. What proof could there be that the time is called “light” even before the eastern sky brightens? Rather, dawn must mean the time when the eastern sky has brightened. According to this, there is also a leniency after the fact regarding the evening Krias Shema when one was not under duress. One may still question the Rashba's proof, for perhaps immediately after the eastern sky brightened they could not yet recite Yotzer Or, and the members of the watch were delayed by many procedures after the slaughter. Perhaps he holds that the kohanim were vigilant and these procedures delayed them only very slightly.
Without the blessings. His wording implies that if one recited it with the blessings, he has fulfilled those blessings as well. At first glance, in light of what Magen Avraham writes in seif katan 5, it is uncertain whether one has fulfilled the blessing of Yotzer Or after the fact. Nevertheless, it appears that he need not repeat the blessing. We need only take into account the view of Rav Hai Gaon, and perhaps the members of the watch did recite Yotzer, at least regarding ideal practice, as Peri Megadim writes in this siman. See there.
