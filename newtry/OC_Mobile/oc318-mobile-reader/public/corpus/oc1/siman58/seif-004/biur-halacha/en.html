"From when amud ha-shachar rises"—in the words of Magen Avraham later in siman 89 s.k. 8 and Pri Megadim and Peri Chadash there it is explained that the time of the morning Shema is before the east is illuminated; in Avodat haGolah there I have seen that he disagrees with Magen Avraham and holds that the morning Shema is called from when the face of the east is illuminated and not before. And now I have found he is not alone in this, for the Gra also wrote in Shenot Eliyahu on Berakhot chapter 1 on the mishnah "until amud ha-shachar rises" thus: the morning Shema is when the face of the east is illuminated; and likewise he wrote there s.v. "they enter" that the morning Shema is from when the face of the east is illuminated—see there; and likewise in Sefer Mateh Yehudah his view is likewise; and it seems to me there is proof for their words from the words of Rashba and as brought in this siman in Beit Yosef: since amud ha-shachar has risen, he may well recite Yotzer Or like the men of the watch; and behold the men of the watch would say Yotzer Or a long time after the slaughter of the tamid, as is stated in Rashi Berakhot 11b s.v. Barekhu; and the slaughter of the tamid was after the face of the east was illuminated, as is stated in Yoma chapter 28; and what proof is it that light is called even before the face of the east is illuminated? Rather, surely the morning Shema is called from when the face of the east is illuminated; and according to this we also have a leniency regarding bedi'eved for the evening Kriat Shema if one was not under compulsion. And even though there is still [room] to challenge Rashba: from where do we know that immediately after illumination one may say Yotzer Or? Perhaps that place is different, for they would delay for many matters after the slaughter; and perhaps he holds that the priests are diligent and were not delayed thereby, except a very little.

"Without blessings"—it appears from his words that if he read with blessings, he has fulfilled even in them; and at first glance, according to what Magen Avraham wrote in s.k. 5—see there—there is room to doubt whether he fulfilled bedi'eved with the blessing Yotzer Or; but nevertheless it appears one need not return and bless, for we need only be concerned for the view of R. Hai Gaon, lest perhaps they would say the blessing Yotzer [Or] lekhatchilah, as Pri Megadim wrote in this siman—see there.
