[7] It is properly called Yotzer Or, etc. Like the men of the mishmaros; Rashba. But Magen Avraham wrote that one should not say Yotzer Or so early, for this is the view of Rav Hai concerning the order of blessings not preventing fulfillment, as Beis Yosef wrote in siman 60, and as Rashba wrote in Responsa, siman 318, that throughout the days of the Temple they read Ahavah Rabbah individually. This is surprising, for if so the Rashba's words contradict each other. Rather, it appears to me that in the responsa he did not write this as practical law at all, but only brought proof from that answer of the Gemara that Ahavah Rabbah does not open with Baruch because it is short, and not because it is adjacent to another blessing; for if it were because of adjacency, that answer of the Gemara would be difficult. This is clear. Also, what he wrote in the name of Rav Hai, Beis Yosef and Shulchan Aruch already ruled in siman 60 not like Rav Hai; furthermore, initially one must say the blessings before Krias Shema.
