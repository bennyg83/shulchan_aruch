[7] "One reads well Yotzer Or," etc. Like the men of the watch—Rashba. But Magen Avraham wrote one should not say Yotzer Or so early in the morning, for thus appears reasonable to Rav Hai regarding the order of blessings—it is not an impediment, as Beit Yosef wrote siman 60; and as Rashba wrote in a responsum siman 318 that all the days of the Temple they would say Ahavah rabbah individually—until here. And it is astonishing on him: if so, Rashba's words contradict. Rather it appears to me that in responsa he did not write this at all regarding practical law, but brings proof from that variant of the Talmud that Ahavah rabbah does not open with Barukh because it is short, and not because of adjacency—for if because of adjacency of blessings, it would be difficult on that variant of the Talmud; and this is clear. Also what he wrote in the name of Rav Hai—Beit Yosef and Shulchan Arukh already ruled in siman 60 not like Rav Hai; and furthermore lekhatchilah one must say blessings before Kriat Shema.
