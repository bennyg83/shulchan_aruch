Although its time, etc. Kesef Mishneh writes at the end of chapter 1: Some ask why, regarding the evening Krias Shema, we expound “when you lie down” as meaning the entire time people lie down. Regarding the morning Krias Shema, we should similarly expound “when you rise” as the entire time people rise, namely, all day. He answers that indeed we do so expound it, for the Gemara says that one who recites from then onward has not lost the blessings. Were it no longer the time for Krias Shema, the blessings would be in vain. The statement “at the time people rise” is only an asmachta. This is evident, for are princes the majority of the world? Rather, the explanation is as stated. His words require study. First, the blessings are not intrinsically connected to Krias Shema, as written in siman 60, so no proof can be brought from the blessings. Further, according to him, one should be able to recite the blessings even after the fourth hour. Moreover, according to his view, the Torah-level time for Krias Shema would be all day and all night. It would then be a positive mitzvah not caused by time, like prayer according to what is written in siman 106, seif 2, whereas the Gemara explicitly states that it is a time-bound positive mitzvah. It appears to me that the original question does not begin. “When you lie down” properly means the entire time one is lying down. “Rising,” however, applies only when one gets out of bed. Afterward it is no longer called rising, but standing, sitting, or walking. This is also implied by Sefer HaChinuch and stated in Sefer Mitzvos Gadol, positive commandment 18.
