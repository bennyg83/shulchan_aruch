If the third hour passed, etc. The Tur writes here, “if he was prevented by circumstances beyond his control and did not recite,” etc. In truth, the same law applies if he was negligent, as the Beit Yosef writes. The Tur mentions duress because of the conclusion, that even so, from this point onward he has lost the proper time. Kesef Mishneh, chapter 1 of the laws of Krias Shema, writes: Some ask why we expound “when you lie down” as the entire time people lie down, as stated in the Gemara, so that even before sunrise one may fulfill the evening Krias Shema because some people are sleeping then. The meaning cannot be that at that hour people are going to bed, since people do not normally go to bed then. It must mean while they are still lying down. Why, then, do we not similarly expound “when you rise” as the entire time people are rising, meaning all day? He answers that indeed we do so expound it, and therefore one who recites throughout the day has not lost the blessings. However, because redemption must be joined to the Amidah, and the time for the Amidah extends until four hours, corresponding to the daily offering, the time for Krias Shema was fixed at three hours and supported by the verse “when you rise,” at the time people rise. I am astonished that such a holy mouth would say this. Certainly the exposition of “when you rise” is a full exposition, just like “when you lie down.” Furthermore, he rules here that after four hours one has lost the blessings. It appears to me that the question can be answered as follows. Regarding lying down, we say it means while people are lying down, because as long as one lies down the designation “lying” does not change. Rising is different. The designation “rising” changes into sitting, walking, or standing. Therefore one is called “rising” only at the beginning of getting up. This appears straightforward.
