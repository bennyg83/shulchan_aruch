(Seif katan 6) "If he was compelled," etc.—what he wrote regarding standing, etc.—they follow the sons of kings, etc.—meaning: although they are few, nevertheless they follow them and the time of standing is called.

If so—Keriat Shema of the evening, etc.—and that is: if he was compelled and did not read Keriat Shema of the evening, even if hanetz hachama has already risen, he is still permitted to read—for it is still time of lying down relative to the sons of kings. And Magen Avraham's intention is not that he has time for Keriat Shema of the evening until the end of the third hour—for then when would he read Keriat Shema of the morning? For once the third hour has passed, the time of Keriat Shema of the morning has already passed—for we do not say that just as if he was compelled and did not read Keriat Shema of the night and is also compelled to go to a place of wild animal troops we say he reads of the night and not of the day—if so, here too where he did not read Keriat Shema of the night until after hanetz hachama, it should be said he reads of the night and not of the day. One should say that if he did not read at night and must go to a place of wild animal troops, Beit Yosef wrote the reason he reads of the night and not of the day: since both cannot be read before alot hashachar, which are two contradictory matters—if so it is more preferable that he read at night and not lose the certain one of the day; for it is possible that even though he goes to a place of wild animal troops, by chance it may happen that he will be able to read also of the day—unlike if he reads of the day now before alot hashachar he will certainly lose Keriat Shema of the night, etc. Unlike if we say that if he did not read at night he may read until the end of the third hour—for then he will certainly lose of the day; if so what will the Sages accomplish with their enactment to fix reading of the night—and thereby Keriat Shema of the night is fixed, and he loses Keriat Shema of the day through this? And what is more about of the night than of the day? On the contrary, it seems somewhat more to fix Keriat Shema of the day which is now the principal time, than to fix Keriat Shema of the night whose principal time has already passed. Or that Magen Avraham's intention in his difficulty is not that there be time for Keriat Shema of the night until the end of the third hour. And if we say his difficulty is that one must divide those three hours by way of parable—an hour and a half shall be time for Keriat Shema of the night, and the remaining hour and a half for Keriat Shema of the day—who will begin the equal parts? For what difference to me whether the first hour and a half or the last? And behold in all those three hours the sons of kings sleep and rise. And just as one does not divide the time between alot hashachar and hanetz hachama—and if he did not read of the night until alot hashachar rose and wishes to go to a place of wild animal troops—we say he reads of the night and not of the day so there are not two contradictory matters, and we do not say that at the beginning of the time which is from alot hashachar until alot hashachar he reads of the night and at the end of that time he reads of the day—for the reason is that all that time is equal and it is impossible to divide it half for day and half for night—so too in those three hours it is impossible to divide them as explained. Therefore one may say according to his view: since all the poskim agreed that the time of Keriat Shema of the day extends until the end of the third hour, and see Beit Yosef and so in Hagahot Maimuni—if so it is difficult: since the sons of kings sleep until three hours, when will the sons of kings themselves read? And so Tosafot challenged in Berakhot page 2b, s.v. "from the time the poor man enters to eat his bread" is the time of Keriat Shema of the evening for R' Chanina—and Tosafot challenged: if so for R' Chanina the poor man himself, when will he read and pray? See there. If so it must be as Rashi wrote on page 3b in that which it answers for R' Natan who holds three watches in the night—although he said that alot hashachar "my eyes precede the watches" and it is written "at midnight I rise"—and there are not only one watch and a half. And it answers: six of the night and two of the day of the sons of kings—for they stand at three hours, and that is that they sleep only two hours between them; therefore he said "two of the day." And at the third hour they rise.

And the Sages gave Keriat Shema of the morning time until the end of the third hour—for at the third hour the sons of kings read. Therefore every person may read all the third hour, and so I found in Peri Chadash in this siman. From this we found in those three hours two separate times—that is, the first two hours are the time of lying down of the sons of kings who sleep, and at the third hour they rise; if so Magen Avraham's difficulty that one should fix if he was compelled and did not read at night that he read of the night until the beginning of the third hour—for all that time the sons of kings sleep, and at the third hour when the sons of kings rise he reads day and reads Keriat Shema of the day (thus it seems from the words of Hagahot Maimuni ch. 1 of laws of Keriat Shema seif 69 where he wrote in the name of Seder HaYom, and said: I further found that the author of Seder HaYom also ruled that until the end of the third hour is the time of Keriat Shema from the girsa in the Yerushalmi, etc., and kings stand from their beds at two hours and read at the third hour, end of Seder HaYom, etc.). But the beginning of the words of Hagahot Maimuni there implies they sleep all three hours—for he wrote to reject the opinion of those who say the time of Keriat Shema extends only until the beginning of the third hour, from what they said for R' Natan on page 3b, etc., and Hagahot Maimuni rejected: for our purposes the Talmud was not concerned except that David preceded other kings two watches, etc., and why mention the third hour since he found two watches—and so David did not mention in the verse except complete watches, etc. From this it implies they sleep all three hours. But Magen Avraham therefore does not hold thus—for if so his proof would be lost from what he wrote in seif katan 1 that those three hours are counted from alot hashachar from that which it says "two of the day"—and if they were counted from hanetz hachama there would be more than two hours of day, as written there. Per Hagahot Maimuni's words this is not proof—for he was not concerned to mention more than two hours in order to fulfill the verse "my eyes precede the watches" as explained.

And regarding Keriat Shema of the morning—although it requires "morning," etc.—it refers to what he wrote above from this.

However, certainly from when alot hashachar rises it is day; and because its time is not known to everyone they said [in tractate Megillah page 20a]: hanetz hachama—a matter that is clear to everyone—that if one precedes in these he is invalid, and one who delays loses nothing, for preceding them is not a mitzvah and their time is every day. And regarding the daily offering in the Temple, as it is written "in the morning"—they fixed when the east lights up, whose preceding is a mitzvah; and on this he wrote: and regarding Keriat Shema, although it requires "morning," etc.—one should fix the time of Keriat Shema of the morning from alot hashachar like the daily offering.

With the men of the watch who would read from alot hashachar as explained.

"He did not fulfill." Meaning—not in a case of compulsion.

And similarly in Rashba's novellae to resolve the above difficulty—why they did not give time for Keriat Shema of the night for three hours.

Because of the minority. Meaning: although one may read of the night after alot hashachar before hanetz hachama, although it is day—because of the minority of people who sleep at that hour; nevertheless the sons of kings are fewer, for they are a minority of a minority.

For the sons of kings sleep—not like what Milchamot wrote that they are awake in their beds.

"My eyes preceded the watches." And that is per the Gemara's explanation on page 3b for R' Natan: two of the day and six of the night. And therefore since David said "my eyes preceded the watches"—he glorified that his eyes preceded two watches before other kings who stand at three hours. And he attributed the praise to his eyes—implying they sleep; for if the kings were awake in their beds, he would not have attributed the praise to eyes but to his rising.

And likewise is the language of the proof in the Gemara 4b 4a for David: "Guard my soul, for I am pious," etc.—"I am not pious that all the kings of east and west sleep until three hours, and I at midnight," etc.
