(Seif katan 6) If he was compelled, etc. What he wrote regarding rising, etc., that we follow princes, etc., means that although they are a minority, nevertheless we follow them and it is called the time of rising.
If so, the evening Krias Shema, etc. This means that if he was compelled and did not read the evening Krias Shema, even if netz hachamah already occurred, he should also be permitted to read, for it is still the time of lying down for princes. The Magen Avraham's intent is not that he has until the end of the third hour for the evening Krias Shema, for if so, when would he read the morning Krias Shema, since once three hours pass the time for morning Krias Shema has passed. Rather, as Rashi explains in Berachos 3b, the princes sleep only two hours into the day, and in the third hour they rise.
And the Sages gave morning Krias Shema a time until the end of the third hour. Since princes read in the third hour, all people may read throughout the third hour. I also found this in Peri Chadash in this siman. According to this, within those three hours there are two distinct times: the first two hours are the time of lying down for princes, for they are sleeping, and the third hour is when they rise. Thus the Magen Avraham's question is that the Sages should have enacted that one who was compelled and did not read at night may read the night Shema until the beginning of the third hour, and in the third hour, when princes rise, he should read the daytime Shema. He then analyzes the Hagahos Maimoniyos, who cite the Sefer HaMitzvos and discuss whether princes sleep all three hours or rise in the third, and concludes that the Magen Avraham must understand that they rise in the third hour, for otherwise his proof from 'two hours of daytime' would fall away.

Regarding the morning Krias Shema, although we require morning, etc., this refers to what was written above.
Nevertheless, certainly from when alot hashachar rises it is day. Because its time is not known to everyone, they said in Megillah 20a, netz hachamah, which is clear to everyone. If one performed those mitzvos earlier, it is invalid, and one who delays loses nothing, because advancing them is not a mitzvah and their time is all day. Regarding the daily offering in the Temple, where it is written 'morning,' they fixed the time as when the eastern sky lit, for advancing it is a mitzvah. On this he wrote that regarding Krias Shema, although we require morning, etc., they should have fixed its time from alot hashachar as by the tamid.

He did not fulfill his obligation. This refers to a case not involving compulsion.
I also found in the Chidushei Rashba a resolution to the above question, why they did not give a three-hour time for the night Krias Shema.
Because it is a minority. Meaning, although one may read the night Shema after alot hashachar before netz hachamah, even though it is day, because of the minority of people who sleep at that time, nevertheless princes are worse, for they are a minority of a minority.
That princes are sleeping, unlike what is written in Milchamos, that they are awake in their beds.
My eyes preceded the watches. This is according to the Gemara's answer on 3b according to Rabbi Nosson: two hours of daytime and six of nighttime. This is what David meant by 'my eyes preceded the watches,' that he praised himself that his eyes preceded the other kings by two watches, for they rise at three hours. He attached the praise to his eyes, implying that they are sleeping; for if the kings were awake in their beds, he should not have attached the praise to the eyes but to his rising.
So also implies the language of the proof in the Gemara 4a: David said, 'Guard my soul, for I am pious.' Am I not pious, for all the kings of east and west sleep until three hours, and I rise at midnight, etc.
