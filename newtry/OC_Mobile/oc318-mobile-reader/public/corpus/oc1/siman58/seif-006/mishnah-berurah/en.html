He read it. Even if this was through negligence. It appears obvious that what he wrote, 'and did not read it,' is not specific; the same applies even if he previously read all of it in its time without its blessings. He stated it this way for a greater novelty, so that one should not say that since he did not read it at all until now it is worse. So it is written explicitly in Beis Yosef at the end of siman 46; see there. In Responsa Mishkenos Yaakov, siman 77, he questions this where he first read all of it; see there.
The fourth hour. For the blessings are not connected to Krias Shema. Although they were instituted before Krias Shema, nevertheless they are not blessings of Krias Shema, for he does not bless 'Who commanded us to read Shema.' They are like prayer, and therefore their law is like Shacharis, which is only until a third of the day.
The fourth hour passed. Even if he was compelled, nevertheless from then onward he lost the blessings. If he blessed, it is a blessing in vain. See Biur Halacha.
He reads it. See the Levush, who wrote that he may read it. The intent is that it is good for him to read it so that he accepts upon himself the yoke of the kingdom of Heaven, but he is not obligated, for he does not fulfill thereby the positive commandment of Krias Shema, as all the later authorities agree that the time of Krias Shema according to the Torah extends only until three hours, as above. Know that nevertheless, the passage of tzitzis or other verses that mention the Exodus from Egypt are obligatory according to the Torah all day, for according to the Torah no time was fixed for remembering it, as the Shaagas Aryeh wrote in siman 10, that until evening is the time for remembering the Exodus from Egypt.
