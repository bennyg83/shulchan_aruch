The time for Krias Shema, etc. See Mishnah Berurah. Although perhaps they could recite Ahavah Rabbah, as is somewhat indicated by Rashi to Berachos 11b, s.v. “If you say,” etc. [This remains somewhat difficult in light of what Rashi himself writes on that page, s.v. “Yotzer Or,” etc., “what then is the other one,” etc.; this can be resolved only with difficulty.] In any event, Yotzer Or certainly may not be recited before this time. Even according to the Shulchan Aruch, who is lenient below in seif 10, that leniency applies only in pressing circumstances. All the more so according to the Magen Avraham and Peri Megadim there, who rule that even in pressing circumstances it may not be recited. However, everything up to Barechu may be recited before this time. In practice, this ruling makes little difference, because ideally one certainly should not delay putting on tefillin until between Yishtabach and Yotzer. He will therefore naturally recite Baruch She'amar and Pesukei DeZimrah after the time when one can recognize an acquaintance, since the morning time for tefillin begins when one can recognize an acquaintance, and not earlier, as stated above in siman 30, seif 1. [On Shabbos, when only a tallis is worn, this depends on the dispute between the Shulchan Aruch and Rama in siman 18, seif 3, whether one may recite the blessing over it from dawn onward; see there and Biur HaGra.] One who is pressed for time may, for the present, recite Baruch She'amar and Pesukei DeZimrah without tefillin until Yotzer Or. When the time for recognizing an acquaintance arrives, he should put on tefillin, recite the blessing, and continue from Yotzer Or onward.
From when he can recognize, etc. Regarding joining redemption to the Amidah, the words of Rabbeinu Yonah, cited by the Beit Yosef and copied by me in seif katan 5, imply that he cannot do so, because the ideal time for the Amidah, sunrise, has not yet arrived. This can be rejected, however. His statement that another person, who is not concerned with joining redemption to the Amidah, may recite Krias Shema from the time he can recognize, may mean only that he is not concerned with joining them at the proper ideal time. According to Rabbeinu Yerucham, as the Bach and Derishah explain his words in siman 89, it appears that he may join them. In truth, this law depends upon whether, as a matter of law, one may pray before sunrise. Chayei Adam, klal 16, and Derech HaChaim, in the laws of the time for prayer, imply that ideally one may not pray before sunrise. This applies when there is no pressing circumstance, but in pressing circumstances it is certainly permitted, as the Magen Avraham writes in siman 89, seif katan 16.
And the choicest fulfillment of the mitzvah, etc. Know that those who are careful to recite Krias Shema like the vasikin may recite it and pray alone when they have no minyan. More than this is demonstrated by the Mishnah in Berachos 22b, concerning one who descended to immerse, etc. See there. Even if he has no tefillin at that time, he should nevertheless be careful to recite Krias Shema at its proper time, close to sunrise. That Mishnah concerns the practice of the vasikin, as the Gemara there concludes.
Approximately the measure of one hour. See Magen Avraham and Peri Megadim. The Peri Megadim concludes that, since we rule that the interval from dawn until the sun rises is one and one fifth hours, we would have to say that the interval from dawn until one can recognize an acquaintance four amos away is approximately one tenth of an hour, that the interval from recognition until the beginning of sunrise is likewise one tenth of an hour, and that the interval from the beginning of sunrise until the entire body of the sun is above the earth is one hour. See there. His words are astonishing. The Magen Avraham himself, below in siman 89, seif katan 2, avoids this difficulty and explains that when Rambam writes “before the sun rises,” he means before sunrise, namely, before the beginning of its shining, not before the entire body of the sun has risen. Machatzis HaShekel writes likewise there. What is written here, “the measure of one hour,” refers to the interval from sunrise until the entire body of the sun has risen. Furthermore, according to the Peri Megadim, we would be forced to say that “sunrise” is called such even before the sun begins to shine upon the world. Yet we see plainly that one fifth of an hour after dawn, namely twelve minutes, the sun's shining is still not visible at all. I saw a similar view in Eshel Avraham in this siman, citing Responsa Beis David. His language also implies that sunrise begins when the sun starts leaving its place, even though it is not yet visible or known to us. This does not accord with Rabbeinu Yonah, who explicitly writes that sunrise is the time when the sun begins shining upon the mountain tops. It also does not accord with Tosafos to Berachos 9b, s.v. “for Krias Shema like the vasikin.” Tosafos ask from the chandelier in the Temple, from which sparks would emerge when the sun shone, enabling everyone to know that the time for Krias Shema had arrived. As the Rosh writes, this demonstrates that the time for Krias Shema is after sunrise. This too implies that sunrise begins when the sun starts sparkling upon the world, not when it begins leaving its place. Were it otherwise, there would be an additional interval separating Tosafos' view from Rabbeinu Chananel's view, whereas examination of their words shows that their only dispute concerns the word “finish,” whether one should finish Krias Shema and its blessings at sunrise or begin reciting it at sunrise. They do not dispute the meaning of sunrise itself. The practice of the vasikin also proves this. They did not wish to recite Krias Shema and its blessings from the earliest time of recognizing an acquaintance, so that there would not be a long interruption after the recitation and they would still be able to join redemption to the Amidah, as Rabbeinu Yonah explains and the Beit Yosef cites. Tosafos, the Rosh, and the Shulchan Aruch accept this approach, as Biur HaGra writes. Can one tenth of an hour, approximately six minutes, be considered a long time for Krias Shema and its blessings? The Peri Megadim himself ultimately writes that the matter is not clear to him. From all that we have discussed, it is evident that sunrise begins when the first rays become visible upon the world, as we cited in the text from Rabbeinu Yonah and the Aruch, whom Tosafos Yom Tov also cites. [Indeed, it must be clear that this is also the Magen Avraham's intent when he writes, “It appears to me that regarding the law,” etc. He means that the plain meaning of “sunrise” indicates that Krias Shema and its blessings are completed at the first point when the sun begins illuminating the world. It makes no difference whether the interval from the beginning of sunrise until the entire body of the sun has risen is one hour or one tenth of an hour.] Biur HaGra likewise writes in Shenos Eliyahu and in his commentary to this siman that “one hour” is a scribal error and should read “one third of one tenth of an hour.” See the introduction to his commentary and Dammesek Eliezer for the basis and source of his view. His words likewise show that sunrise is the first point when the sun begins sparkling upon the world. We have seen and heard that those who currently follow the practice of the vasikin conduct themselves accordingly. This is also demonstrated as practical law in Magen Gibborim, Shiltei HaGibborim, at the end of seif katan 4. I saw that Tiferes Yisrael writes that sunrise is ten minutes before the entire body of the sun is above the earth, but he does not explain his reason or source. Perhaps this is a scribal error and should read six minutes, namely, one tenth of an hour. I later found this stated explicitly in the Yerushalmi, Berachos chapter 1, that sunrise is the time when the sun begins shining upon the mountain tops, in accordance with Rabbeinu Yonah and the Aruch. The Rashba in the first chapter of Berachos also clearly indicates this. This Yerushalmi is cited by Ramban in Milchamos, chapter 1. All these sources explicitly demonstrate that the law is as we wrote above.
