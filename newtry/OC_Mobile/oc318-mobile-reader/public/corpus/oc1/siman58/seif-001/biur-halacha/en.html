"The time of Kriat Shema," etc.—see Mishnah Berurah; and even though Ahavah rabbah—they could perhaps say, as is somewhat proven from Rashi Berakhot 11b s.v. "if you say," etc. [and it is difficult regarding what Rashi himself wrote on that page s.v. "Yotzer Or," etc.—"the other, what is it," etc., and it may be resolved with difficulty]—in any case Yotzer Or certainly may not be said before this time, even according to Shulchan Arukh who is lenient below in seif 10, namely in a time of pressure; and all the more so according to what Magen Avraham and Pri Megadim ruled there, that even in a time of pressure one may not say [Yotzer Or]; but until Barekhu it is permitted to say before this time; however there is not much practical difference in this law, for lekhatchilah certainly one should not delay tefillin from putting them on until between Yishtabach and Yotzer; and if so, automatically one will say Barukh she-amar and the verses of praise after the [proper] time, so that it will be seen that this is the time of tefillin in the morning—so that it will be seen and not before, as above siman 30 seif 1 [and on Shabbat when they put on only a tallit that is hung, this matter depends on the dispute between Shulchan Arukh and Rama in siman 18 seif 3 whether one may bless on it from plag haMinchah onward—see there and in Biur haGra]. But one for whom the hour is pressing may for now say Barukh she-amar and the verses of praise without tefillin until Yotzer Or; and when the time arrives—so that it will be seen—he will put on tefillin and bless on them and say from Yotzer Or onward.

"From when one sees," etc.—and regarding adjoining redemption to prayer, it appears from the words of R. Yitzchak and as brought in Beit Yosef and as I copied in s.k. 5 that he cannot adjoin, for it is not yet the time of prayer lekhatchilah, which is sunrise. But it may be rejected: the intention in what is written "but another person who is not concerned about adjoining redemption to prayer may recite it from when one sees"—meaning he is not concerned to adjoin at the proper time lekhatchilah; and per the words of Rabbeinu Yerucham, as Beit Chadash explained and Darkei Moshe cited his words in siman 89—see there—it appears he may adjoin. However, in truth this law depends on whether it is permitted by law to pray before sunrise; and see Chayei Adam in the general [laws] 16 and in Derekh haChayim in the law of the time of prayer—it appears that by law one may not pray lekhatchilah before sunrise; and all this when it is not a time of pressure; but in a time of pressure it is certainly permitted, as Magen Avraham wrote in siman 89 s.k. 16.

"And the mitzvah in the optimal manner," etc.—know that those careful to recite like the vatikin may recite and pray individually if they do not have a minyan; and greater than this is proven in the mishnah Berakhot 22: he went down to immerse, etc.—see there—that even if he had no tefillin at that time, nevertheless he would be careful to recite at its time near sunrise; and that mishnah deals with vatikin, as we conclude in the Gemara there.

"Like the measure of one hour"—see Magen Avraham and see Pri Megadim who concludes at the end of his words and wrote: if so, according to what we hold from amud ha-shachar until sunrise is an hour and a fifth, we must say that from amud ha-shachar until the measure that one recognizes his fellow at a distance of four amos is about a tenth of an hour, and from recognition until the beginning of sunrise is also a tenth of an hour, and from the beginning of sunrise until the rising of the entire solar body on the earth is an hour—so his words; see there. And his words are astonishing, for Magen Avraham himself later in siman 89 s.k. 2 guarded himself from this and wrote that Rambam's intention in what he wrote "before sunrise" means before sunrise—i.e., the beginning of shining—and not the rising of the entire solar body; and likewise wrote Magen Avraham there—see there; and what he wrote here "the measure of one hour" means from sunrise until the rising of the entire solar body. And further: according to his words we would be forced to say that sunrise is called even before the sun begins to shine on the world, for we see empirically that a fifth of an hour after alot ha-shachar—which is twelve minutes—still the shining of the sun is not recognizable at all; and similarly I have seen in Eshel Avraham in this siman who cites in the name of a responsum of Beit Yosef, and it also appears from his wording that sunrise is called from when the sun begins to leave its place even though it is not seen and is not yet known to us; and this does not appear from the words of Rabbeinu Yonah, who wrote explicitly that sunrise is the hour when the sun begins to shine on the mountaintops; and likewise from Tosafot Berakhot 9b s.v. "Kriat Shema ke-vatikin," who challenged: from that [passage] of "in the beginning"—when the sun shines sparks come out from it and all would know that the time of Kriat Shema arrived—it is proven that the time of Kriat Shema is after sunrise; and as the Rosh wrote—see there—it also appears that sunrise is from when the sun begins to glitter on the world, not from when it begins to leave its place—for otherwise there would be an intervening period between Tosafot's view and R. Chananel's view, and this does not appear, as is proven to one who examines their words: the practical difference is only in what is written "they finish"—whether the intention is to finish with sunrise or to begin reading with sunrise—but regarding sunrise itself they did not disagree. And further: from the vatikin themselves it is proven that they did not want to recite Shema and its blessings from when one recognizes his fellow—which is the beginning of the time of Kriat Shema—in order that there not be a long interruption after the reading and they would not be able to adjoin redemption to prayer, as Terumat Yeri'im wrote and as brought in Beit Yosef; and this view Tosafot, the Rosh, and Shulchan Arukh agreed to, as the Gra wrote; and that a tenth of an hour—which is about six minutes—is a long time for Kriat Shema and blessings. And Pri Megadim himself wrote at the end that this matter is not clear to him. Behold from all that we expanded it appears plainly that sunrise is called from when the beginning of its shining on the world is recognizable, as we copied in the name of Terumat Yeri'im and the Arukh and as Taz brings them [and in truth it must be clear that Magen Avraham's intention in what he wrote "and in my humble opinion regarding the law," etc., is also as we wrote, and his intention is to say that the plain meaning of the term sunrise proves that finishing Kriat Shema and its blessings is with the first point of the sun that begins to illuminate the world; and what difference to me if afterward from the beginning of sunrise until the rising of the entire solar body is an hour or a tenth of an hour]. And the Gra also wrote in his book Shenot Eliyahu and in his commentary on this siman that there is a textual corruption in what he wrote "the measure of one hour" and it should read a third of a tenth of an hour; and see in the introduction to his commentary and in Sefer Damasek Eliezer the reason and source for his words—see there—which also proves according to his words that sunrise is the first point of the sun that begins to glitter on the world; and thus we have seen and heard that people practice, as practiced today like the vatikin; and likewise it is proven for practical halakhah in Sefer Magen Giborim in Sha'ar haGolah end of s.k. 4. And in Sefer Tiferet Yisrael I have seen that he wrote sunrise is ten minutes before the entire solar body rises on the earth, and he did not explain his reason and source; and perhaps there is a textual corruption and it should read six minutes, which is a tenth of an hour. Then I found it explicitly in Yerushalmi chapter 1 of Berakhot that sunrise is the time when the sun begins to shine on the mountaintops, like R. Yonah and the Arukh mentioned; and likewise in Rashba chapter 1 of Berakhot it is proven explicitly thus, and this Yerushalmi is cited by Ramban in Milchamot chapter 1; from all these it is proven explicitly that the law is as we wrote above.
