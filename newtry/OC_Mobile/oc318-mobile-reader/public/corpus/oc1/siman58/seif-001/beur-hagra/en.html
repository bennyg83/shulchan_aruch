Seif 1, The time for Krias Shema, etc. As Tosafos writes in Yoma 37b, Abaye concedes to Rav Huna that the halachah follows the Others, except that the choicest fulfillment is to act like the vasikin, so as to juxtapose redemption to prayer. Before that time it is impossible to juxtapose them. Rabbeinu Yonah and the Rosh write likewise, unlike the Rambam, who rules entirely in accordance with Abaye. This also appears from the Rif.
One who is accustomed, etc. This is stated in the Yerushalmi and cited in Tosafos in Berachos there and by the other poskim.
And it continues, etc. The Gemara there rules that the halachah follows Rabbi Yehoshua, and even the vasikin concede to Rabbi Yehoshua, as stated there on 25b. See Tosafos there and elsewhere, and the other poskim, as stated in the Yerushalmi there: The one who says that the time is when one can recognize another at a distance of four amos follows the view that it is when one can distinguish between blue and white; but they said that its mitzvah is with sunrise so that one may juxtapose redemption to prayer. Likewise, in Tosefta chapter 1, the Others say: when one can recognize his fellow at a distance of four amos; its mitzvah is with sunrise so that one may juxtapose redemption to prayer.
The end of three hours, etc. The Rambam, Hagahos Maimoniyos, and the other poskim understand that the phrase ‘three hours’ means all three hours, for otherwise it should have said, ‘until the third hour.’ In the Yerushalmi, chapter 1, Rabbi Zeira said in the name of Rabbi Ami: In the days of Rabbi Yehoshua we would go out to a fast and recite Krias Shema after three hours, and he did not protest. Rabbi Yose and Rabbi Acha went out to a fast. The congregation came and recited Krias Shema after three hours. Rabbi Acha wished to protest, but Rabbi Yose said to him: Have they not already recited it at its proper time? Do they recite it now for any reason other than to stand in prayer after words of Torah? He replied: It is because of the uninformed, lest they say that they are now reciting it at its proper time. In chapter 4 there, Rabbi Yose prayed at three hours, Rabbi Chiya bar Abba prayed at three hours, and Rabbi Berechiah saw him recite Krias Shema and pray after three hours. The Yerushalmi asks from the Mishnah that one who recites from then onward has not lost, and answers that he had already accepted the yoke of Heaven at its proper time. Likewise, the Gemara on 7a speaks of the first three hours of the day. This is unlike the Raavad and Rashi, who wrote that the time extends only until the very beginning of the third hour, based on the passage on 3b concerning two hours of the day. See Tosafos to Avodah Zarah 4b, s.v. Bitlas. Their proof is thereby refuted, for the reference is to the entire third hour. Hagahos Maimoniyos offers another answer; see there.
Which is, etc. The Agur writes that the Midrash states that the day on which Yehoshua stopped the sun was at the summer solstice. In Avodah Zarah 25a the Gemara asks how long it remained still and answers twenty-four hours, etc. See what I wrote in siman 443, seif 1.
To recite it a little, etc. This is how the Rambam and Rabbeinu Yonah explain the statement that they would finish it, unlike Rabbeinu Chananel, who explains that ‘finish’ means that they would recite it after sunrise, based on the verse, ‘They shall fear You with the sun,’ as Tosafos cites in his name in Yoma there. Tosafos refutes his view, since that verse refers to prayer, as stated in chapter 4 of Berachos. This is the meaning of, ‘so that he may juxtapose, etc.’ Moreover, at the end of chapter 3 the Mishnah states that if he can ascend, cover himself, and recite before sunrise, he should do so. This is also unlike Rabbeinu Tam's interpretation, which does not follow the practice of the vasikin; see there.
And one who, etc. See Tosafos there, s.v. Kol.
The measure, etc. There is a scribal error here. It should read: a third of a tenth of an hour.
