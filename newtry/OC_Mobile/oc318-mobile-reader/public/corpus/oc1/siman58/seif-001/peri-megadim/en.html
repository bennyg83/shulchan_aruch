Pri Chadash: He warned greatly not to converse in the middle of Kaddish or Kedushah, and brought from Derekh Eretz: R. Chama found Elijah—how many thousands of camels loaded with wrath and anger to pay those [who do so]—brought in Beit Yosef here and in siman 128; therefore it is fitting to be careful about this; and there is no distinction between Kaddish on Tanakh or aggadah or mishnayot if they contain verses. And in Sefer haMagid he expands on this—its place is not here. In Avodat haRabim siman 57 I copied here the explanation of Barekhu "Bless the Lord the blessed One"—so it should not appear as if he removes himself from the community explicitly by saying to them Barekhu as if he does not want to [include himself]; and even though afterward he says Barukh, etc., it is not enough; therefore he says haMevorakh; and nevertheless he returns and says Barukh, etc., for haMevorakh can mean "the Blessed One" in the mouth of all creatures—Avodat haKodesh; but the congregation, why should they say haMevorakh since they already said Barukh, etc.; see Avodat haRabim. As Rashi wrote in Chullin: Israel is more beloved than the ministering angels, who say the Name after two words, and he challenged: the angels also say "Barukh kevod Hashem"; and he answered: since permission was given to Israel—the same applies in Israel: "Barukh Hashem" immediately after one word; and it must be said: since permission was given—meaning the shaliach tzibbur says "Bless the Lord" and this is the meaning of "Barukh Hashem haMevorakh" from the mouth of the shaliach tzibbur and all Israel—on their account.
