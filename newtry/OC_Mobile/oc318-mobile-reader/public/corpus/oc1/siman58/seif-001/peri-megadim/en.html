Peri Chadash. He warned very strongly not to speak during Kaddish or Kedushah. He cited Derech Eretz: Rabbi Chama encountered Eliyahu with many thousands of camels laden with wrath and fury, to punish those who do so. This is cited by the Beit Yosef here and in siman 128. One should therefore be very careful. There is no distinction between Kaddish following Tanach, Aggadah, or Mishnayos, when they contain verses. I discuss this at length in Sefer HaMaggid; this is not the place. I will copy here what Eliyah Rabbah writes in siman 57. The meaning of “Bless Hashem, Who is blessed” is that the chazzan should not appear to exclude himself from the congregation by saying to them, “Bless,” as though he himself does not wish to do so. Although he afterward says “Blessed,” that is insufficient. He therefore says “Who is blessed.” Nevertheless, he then repeats, “Blessed,” because “Who is blessed” could mean “Who is blessed by all creatures.” See Bach. Why, however, does the congregation say “Who is blessed” after already saying “Blessed”? Eliyah Rabbah answers in accordance with Chullin: Israel are more beloved than the ministering angels, for Israel mention Hashem after two words. The Gemara asks that the angels also say, “Blessed is the glory of Hashem,” and answers that once permission is granted, the same applies to Israel. This is difficult, for here they say “Blessed is Hashem” after only one word. We must therefore say that “once permission is granted” means that the chazzan first says, “Bless Hashem.” The congregation then says, “Blessed is Hashem, Who is blessed,” namely, Who was blessed by the chazzan and all Israel.
