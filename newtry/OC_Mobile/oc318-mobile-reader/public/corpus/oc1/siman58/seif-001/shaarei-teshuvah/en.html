Hours. See Baer Heitev and Shaagas Aryeh, siman 5, who discusses the matter incisively at length and likewise concludes that 'until three hours' includes the endpoint, through the end of the third hour. These are proportional hours: three hours equal one quarter of the day. There is no difference between long summer days and short winter days; the day is divided into twelve hours and the night into twelve, and one quarter of the day is the three-hour period for Krias Shema. A congregation that reads Krias Shema late during the summer nevertheless fulfills its obligation if it reads before the end of three proportional hours; before one quarter of the day has passed. See there. Responsa Noda B'Yehuda, siman 3, rules that the synagogue's reading may not be delayed for elderly people who arrive late and would otherwise need to pray individually, when the delay will cause the time for Krias Shema to pass. We do not tell one person to sin so another may benefit; see there.
