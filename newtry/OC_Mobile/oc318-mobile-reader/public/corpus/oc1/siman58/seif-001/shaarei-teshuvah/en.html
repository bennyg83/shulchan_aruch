Be'er Heitev—and see in Sha'agat Aryeh siman 5 who extended in his sharpness and also raised that until three hours means "and until" inclusively—that is, until the end of three hours; and these are temporal hours—which is a fourth of the day; and there is no distinction between summer days when the day is long or winter days when the day is short: we calculate the day as twelve hours and the night as twelve, and a fourth of the day is three temporal hours of Kriat Shema. And a congregation that delays in summer to recite Kriat Shema—if they read before the three hours end, that is, before a fourth of the day passes, they fulfill their obligation of Kriat Shema—peace upon him. And see in responsa Noda biYehudah siman 3 that after the reading there is no [need for] public Torah reading for the sake of elders who delay coming, and they would need to pray individually, since thereby the time of Kriat Shema would pass; and we do not tell a person "sin," etc.—see there:
