Magen Avraham, seif katan 1. That here, according to everyone, his intent is as follows: in siman 233, in Magen Avraham seif katan 3, there are two opinions whether we calculate from alot hashachar until tzeis hakochavim, or from netz hachamah until sunset. On this he brought proof that here everyone agrees that we count from alot hashachar. His proof is from the beginning of Berachos, which asks from the verses about David against each other: it is written, 'My eyes preceded the watches,' implying two watches, which are eight hours, and it is written, 'At midnight I arise,' meaning six hours. The Gemara answers that in truth he arose at midnight, but in comparison with other kings, who sleep until the third hour, meaning that they rise in the third hour, there are six hours of night and two of day, making eight hours; see there. Thus the measure of princes who sleep until three hours means from the beginning of the day, and the time of Krias Shema is likewise because this is the way of princes; if so, it is calculated from alot hashachar. One could reject this, perhaps the three watches of the night continue until netz and the kings sleep two hours afterward. Therefore he brought proof that the three watches must end at alot hashachar, for the Gemara says there that the sign for the end of the third watch is a nursing child, etc. If you ask why a sign is needed, for then it is the beginning of day, the Gemara says the difference is for one who sleeps in a dark house; when he hears this sign, he should rise and read Krias Shema. Tosafot asks that the time of Krias Shema is from when one recognizes his fellow, etc., and answers that since he knows the time of alot hashachar, by the time he rises and prepares himself the time will arrive. Thus the end of the third watch is alot hashachar, and this is clear. The Rav Peri Megadim did not examine this carefully.
