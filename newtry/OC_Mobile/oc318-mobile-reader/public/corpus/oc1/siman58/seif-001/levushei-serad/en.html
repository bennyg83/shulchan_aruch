Magen Avraham seif katan 1—for here according to all his intention is that in siman 253 in Magen Avraham seif katan 3 there are two views whether we calculate the day from alot hashachar until tzeis hakochavim or from hanetz hachama until its setting; on this he brought proof that here all agree we count from alot hashachar. And his proof is from the objection at the beginning of Berakhot on the verses of David that contradict each other—for it is written "my eyes precede the watches" implying two watches which are eight hours, and it is written "at midnight I rise" which is six hours—and it answers: in truth he rose at half the night, but compared to other kings who sleep until the third hour—for they rise at the third hour, when there are six of the night and two of the day, making eight hours. Behold the measure of kings who sleep until three hours is from the beginning of the day, and the measure of time of Keriat Shema is also because thus is the way of the sons of kings—if so we calculate from alot hashachar. But one may reject: perhaps the three watches of the night extend until hanetz and the kings sleep two hours after that. Therefore he brought proof that in any case the three watches end at alot hashachar—for it says there (Berakhot 3a): at the end of the third watch a baby nurses, etc., and why do I need a sign? Then it was already the beginning of the day—and it says a practical difference for one who sleeps in a dark house who does not know the time of Keriat Shema: when he sees the sign of the end of the third watch he rises and reads. And Tosafot challenged: behold R' Eliezer requires until one can distinguish between tekhelet and karti—and one may say: nevertheless, once he knows when alot hashachar will be, then within his rising and preparing himself the time will arrive, etc.—behold the end of the third watch is alot hashachar, and it is simple. And the great Pri Megadim did not look well.
