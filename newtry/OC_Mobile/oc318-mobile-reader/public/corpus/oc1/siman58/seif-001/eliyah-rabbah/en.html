[1] "The time of Kriat Shema," etc. And one blesses two before it and one after it; and in the evening two before it and two after it; and they based them on the verse "seven times a day I praise You" (Tur); and Levush wrote: because evening preceded morning in the order of time, as it is written "and there was evening and there was morning," therefore they took the four—which are the majority—for the evening [service], until here. The explanation: he was troubled why they instituted one more blessing in the evening than in the morning. But I have seen in Shocher Tov siman 6: "Seven times a day I praise You"—R. Yehoshua ben Levi said: these are the seven commandments in Kriat Shema: Yotzer Or, Ahavah, Shema, Ve-hayah im shamoa, Va-yomer, Emet veYatziv, Ga'al Yisrael; and because the mitzvah of tzitzit does not apply at night, they add a blessing—until here. It also requires examination: from Levush it appears he interpreted the verse "seven times a day I praise You" on the seven blessings of morning and evening together; and likewise Beit Yosef explained later at the beginning of siman 236 the words of Tur who brought Yerushalmi: R. Yehoshua ben Levi said on the name "seven times a day I praise You"; and likewise it appears from Rashi's explanation folio 11 and Tosafot at the beginning of Berakhot; and it requires examination: since in Yerushalmi too the words of R. Yehoshua ben Levi are [cited], presumably they interpreted on the seven commandments; and therefore they instituted these blessings so there would be seven by day and seven by night, as the midrash Shocher Tov [says]. However, one may say that he holds there are two readings of this verse: seven commandments and also seven blessings. And in any case we have a broad reason why they instituted four in the evening; but it is possible he holds like Levush that this is for the people of the West who would not say the section Va-yomer in the evening, as is stated in Berakhot folio 14; but for us who say Va-yomer we should not have added a blessing; therefore Levush needed another reason. And in any case one may say: since the mitzvah of tzitzit does not apply at night, we do not count its recitation as a mitzvah; and likewise Rokeach siman 319 wrote this reason in the name of Rabbeinu Tam. And he also wrote a reason in Avudraham in the name of Raavad—may his memory be blessed: they gave the extra [blessing] to the evening because the evening prayer is optional and we want them to be equal.
