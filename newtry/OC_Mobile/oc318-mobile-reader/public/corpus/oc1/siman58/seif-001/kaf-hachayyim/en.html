1) [Seif 1] The time for the morning Krias Shema, etc. Two blessings are recited before it and one after it; in the evening, two are recited before it and two after it. They based these upon the verse, "Seven times a day I have praised You," etc. Tur. See Tikkunei Zohar at the end of tikkun 12 for the reason for these seven blessings. Levush explains why four blessings were arranged at night and three in the morning: in the order of time, evening precedes morning, as it is written, "There was evening and there was morning," etc. They therefore assigned the four blessings, which constitute the majority, to the evening, which comes first, and left the remaining three for the morning. End of his words. Rabbi David Abudraham, page 1b, gives a reason in the name of Raavad: they assigned the extra blessing to the evening because Maariv is optional, and it was necessary to make them equal. Knesses HaGedolah cites this in Hagahos HaTur. Rokeach, siman 318, writes that the reason is that in the morning there is also the mitzvah of tzitzis, unlike at night. Knesses HaGedolah cites his ruling in Hagahos Beis Yosef, paragraph 1; Eliyah Rabbah, paragraph 1. See Shaar HaKavanos, the first discourse on Maariv, page 52c, beginning with the words "Hashkiveinu," and Pri Etz Chaim, Shaar Minchah U'Maariv, chapters 4 and 5.
2) There: When he can recognize a person with whom he is somewhat familiar, etc. If he is very familiar with him, he will recognize him even from a great distance, while if he is not familiar with him at all, he will not recognize him even nearby. Rather, this refers to someone like a guest at an inn who is accustomed to come at intervals. Shulchan Aruch HaRav, paragraph 2, in the name of the Yerushalmi, as cited by Beis Yosef.
3) There: Its time extends until the end of three hours, which is one quarter of the day. Rambam writes in his commentary to the Mishnah, in the first chapter of Berachos: "You should know that all the hours mentioned throughout the Mishnah are seasonal hours. The meaning of seasonal hours is that the day contains twelve hours and the night contains twelve hours. Thus, when it says 'until three hours,' it is as though it said 'until one quarter of the day has passed,' whether the day falls during the summer solstice or the winter solstice." End quote. Beis Yosef. Rama likewise writes this below in siman 233, seif 1. Perishah, paragraph 2; Pri Chadash, end of paragraph 2; Shaagas Aryeh, siman 5, cited by Shaarei Teshuvah, paragraph 1; Shulchan Aruch HaRav, paragraph 3; Chayei Adam, rule 21, paragraph 3; Kitzur Shulchan Aruch, siman 17, paragraph 1. See the gloss Pe'as HaShulchan there, where the author writes that he resolved Pnei Yehoshua's difficulty concerning this in the later edition of his commentary on tractate Berachos. He also cites additional authorities who prove Rambam's position. The gaon and kabbalist Rabbi Yaakov Chagiz, of blessed memory, likewise testifies in his commentary Etz Chaim on the Mishnah that this ruling has been universally accepted throughout Israel, and that seasonal hours are used; see there. Ben Ish Chai, parashas Va'eira, paragraph 5, writes likewise.
4) There: Its time extends until the end of three hours, etc. From what point are those hours measured, and where do they end? Terumas HaDeshen, siman 1, writes that they begin at dawn and end at the emergence of the stars. Levush, in simanim 233 and 266, writes that they begin at sunrise and end at sunset. Maadanei Melech, page 7a, likewise writes this and discusses the subject at length; see there. Knesses HaGedolah, Hagahos Beis Yosef, paragraph 8. Magen Avraham, seif katan 1, however, writes that with respect to the present law, all opinions agree that the hours are counted from dawn; see there. So rule Halachah Berurah, paragraph 1; Yad Aharon in Hagahos HaTur; Eliyah Rabbah, paragraph 2; Shulchan Aruch HaRav, paragraph 3; Shalmei Tzibbur, page 93c; Chesed LaAlafim, paragraph 5; Kitzur Shulchan Aruch there; and Shitilei Zeitim, paragraph 2.
5) There: Which is one quarter of the day. Accordingly, during the winter one must take care to recite Krias Shema early, because the day is short and one quarter of the day is correspondingly short. Beis Yosef in the name of Rabbi Yitzchak Abuhav; Taz, seif katan 1; Solet Belulah, paragraph 1; Ateres Zekeinim, paragraph 1; Eliyah Rabbah there; Chesed LaAlafim there; Siddur Beis Yaakov, in the laws of the time for Krias Shema, paragraph 2. See what we wrote above in siman 46, paragraph 62.
6) There: The preferred performance of the mitzvah is to recite it like the vasikin, etc. Pri Chadash, paragraph 1, writes that initially one is obligated to time the completion of its recitation for sunrise; see there. Birkei Yosef, paragraph 2, cites his ruling and writes that one should be stringent in this manner, since by reciting it like the vasikin one fulfills the obligation according to all opinions; see there.
7) There: The preferred performance of the mitzvah is to recite it like the vasikin, etc. There are people who rise early on winter mornings to snatch Krias Shema and prayer in darkness and gloom, and they call it the prayer of the vasikin. Many unlearned people act arrogantly and pursue reward in this manner, yet no radiance shines upon them; "in darkness they walk." They follow neither this authority nor that authority and do not fulfill their obligation at all. Their practice is nothing but arrogance and an excessive spirit. These are youngsters or elders who are not precise, and they hang empty vessels upon the vasikin. Although we have seen elderly sages who acted this way, they were far more precise, knew the law well, and were highly expert in calculating the proper time, for all their deeds were for the sake of Heaven. Not everyone who wishes to assume the title may do so. Yafeh LaLev, paragraph 1, in the name of Mor U'Ketziah. See Mor U'Ketziah there, where he writes at length in open rebuke of this practice. See also Lev Chaim, volume 2, siman 19, concerning this. A further consequence of this early rising in darkness and gloom is rivalry between one person and another: whoever begins and finishes first is deemed praiseworthy. This also leads them to omit letters, words, and the intentions of the Divine Names. Let the living take this too to heart, so that it not become a stumbling block on the Day of Judgment, G-d forbid. See what we wrote above in siman 5, with Heaven's help, paragraphs 1, 2, and 5.
