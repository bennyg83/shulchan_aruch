1) [Seif 1] The time for the morning Krias Shema, etc. Two blessings are recited before it and one after it; in the evening, two are recited before it and two after it. They based these upon the verse, "Seven times a day I have praised You," etc. Tur. See Tikkunei Zohar at the end of tikkun 12 for the reason for these seven blessings. Levush explains why four blessings were arranged at night and three in the morning: in the order of time, evening precedes morning, as it is written, "There was evening and there was morning," etc. They therefore assigned the four blessings, which constitute the majority, to the evening, which comes first, and left the remaining three for the morning. End of his words. Rabbi David Abudraham, page 1b, gives a reason in the name of Raavad: they assigned the extra blessing to the evening because Maariv is optional, and it was necessary to make them equal. Knesses HaGedolah cites this in Hagahos HaTur. Rokeach, siman 318, writes that the reason is that in the morning there is also the mitzvah of tzitzis, unlike at night. Knesses HaGedolah cites his ruling in Hagahos Beis Yosef, paragraph 1; Eliyah Rabbah, paragraph 1. See Shaar HaKavanos, the first discourse on Maariv, page 52c, beginning with the words "Hashkiveinu," and Pri Etz Chaim, Shaar Minchah U'Maariv, chapters 4 and 5.
