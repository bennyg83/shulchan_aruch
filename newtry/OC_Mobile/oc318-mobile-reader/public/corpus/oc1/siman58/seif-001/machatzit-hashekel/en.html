(C.A.) . . . . R. A.J. D.C. R. R.R. has brought a controversy with the dress from which time it is now considered to be an island of VAT until the stars come out Or from the warm hawk until the sunset:
In the Gemara page 3a in Tosafot, etc.—for it was taught: R' Eliezer says three watches there are in the night. And a sign for the matter: at the beginning of watch one a donkey brays, etc.; watch two, etc.; watch three, etc. And it challenges: what does he count? If the beginning of a watch is considered—for watch one why do I need a sign? It is evening—meaning the beginning of the night is evident. And if the end of a watch is considered—why do I need a sign at the end of watch three? It is day and the beginning of the day is evident. And it answers: in truth the end of a watch is considered, and it was necessary to give a sign for the end of watch three for one who sleeps in a dark house and does not know the time of Keriat Shema—when he sees the sign of the end of watch three he rises and reads. And Tosafot wrote, and said: but R' Eliezer requires until one can distinguish between tekhelet and karti—and one may say: nevertheless, once he knows when alot hashachar will be, before he rises and prepares himself the time has already arrived, etc. And behold certainly Magen Avraham's intention is not to prove that those three hours are counted from alot hashachar and not from hanetz hachama in order to resolve Tosafot's difficulty here—for according to this that those three hours are from alot hashachar, one may say Tosafot's difficulty that R' Eliezer gave a sign through the end of watch three to know alot hashachar, and thereby he knows the end of the time of Keriat Shema—for from the end of watch three which is alot hashachar he has three hours' time—this is not Magen Avraham's way to dispute Tosafot without necessity and to generate from this a new matter, and is this the language of Tosafot? Also this is not sufficient to resolve the difficulty on the Gemara—for the one who answers said "he shall rise and read," implying he gave a sign for the beginning of the time of reading—for "rise and read" implies he reads immediately. And Tosafot challenged well; rather Magen Avraham's intention is that I do not know the time of the end of watch three whether it is alot hashachar or hanetz hachama; therefore Magen Avraham hinted to look in the Gemara and Tosafot—for from those words of Tosafot it is proven that the end of watch three is alot hashachar, for they wrote in their answer: once he knows when alot hashachar will be before he rises, etc.—therefore the end of watch three is alot hashachar. And this matter is the arrangement of what Magen Avraham said afterward in the Gemara regarding that which it challenges from David—and meaning that according to this that the end of watch three is alot hashachar, the words of the Gemara regarding David are proven—that those three hours are counted from alot hashachar—and that is on page 3b where it says: the Rabbis taught—Rabbi says four watches there are in the night, for it is written in one verse "at midnight I rise to thank You" and it is written in one verse "my eyes precede the watches"—how can this be? Rather four watches there are in the night. And from midnight until the end of the night are two watches and the verses do not contradict each other. And R' Natan holds three watches in the night. And the Gemara challenges: if so, for R' Natan the verses are difficult—for from midnight onward there is only a watch and a half; and how did he say "my eyes precede the watches" implying two? And it answers: R' Natan holds like R' Yehuda who said the time of Keriat Shema is until three hours, for thus is the way of the sons of kings to stand at three hours; therefore he said that alot hashachar "my eyes precede the watches" compared to other kings—for since he rose at midnight there are six of the night and two of the day, and Rashi explains two of the day—that other kings sleep (and the third hour should be as Magen Avraham wrote seif katan 6 there)—behold two watches, that is eight hours together. And for R' Natan, since the night is twelve hours and there are only three watches, each watch reaches four hours—if so two watches are eight hours. And the verse "my eyes precede the watches" fits this, end of the Gemara. And since Magen Avraham already proved from the words of Tosafot that the end of the watches are alot hashachar—if so it is impossible that the three hours of the sons of kings are from hanetz hachama—for then there is more than two hours of day—for there is continuation of time from alot hashachar until hanetz hachama (and according to Bach seif katan 2 it is an hour)—if so it must be that the three hours are certainly counted from alot hashachar.
