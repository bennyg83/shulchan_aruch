(Seif katan 1) Three hours; it appears to me that here, according to everyone. Meaning, although at the end of siman 233 he brought the dispute of the Ma'adanei Melech with the Levush from when the day is calculated, whether from alot hashachar until tzeis hakochavim or from netz hachamah until sunset.
In the Gemara, page 3a, in Tosafot, etc. For it was taught: Rabbi Eliezer says the night consists of three watches, and there is a sign for the matter: in the first watch the donkey brays, etc.; in the second watch, etc.; in the third watch, etc. The Gemara asks what he is counting: if he counts the beginning of the watch, why does the first watch need a sign, it is evening and is recognizable; and if he counts the end of the watch, why does the end of the third watch need a sign, it is daytime and the beginning of the day is recognizable. It answers that he always counts the end of the watch, and the sign for the end of the third watch is needed for one who sleeps in a dark house and does not know when the time of Krias Shema is; when he sees the sign of the end of the third watch he should rise and read. Tosafot wrote: but Rabbi Eliezer requires until one can distinguish between blue and green; and they answer that nevertheless, since he knows when alot hashachar rises, before he rises and prepares himself that time has arrived. Certainly the Magen Avraham's intent is not to prove that we count the three hours from alot hashachar in order to resolve Tosafot's question, for it is not the way of the Magen Avraham to disagree with Tosafot without necessity. Rather, his intent is that from our own reasoning we would not know whether the end of the third watch is at alot hashachar or at netz hachamah; therefore he hinted to examine the Gemara and Tosafot, for from Tosafot's language it is clear that the end of the third watch is alot hashachar. This is preparation for what the Magen Avraham writes afterward regarding the Gemara's question from David. Since the end of the third watch is alot hashachar, the Gemara regarding David proves that the three hours are counted from alot hashachar: Rabbi says there are four watches in the night, because one verse says 'At midnight I arise,' and another says 'My eyes preceded the watches.' According to Rabbi Nosson, who says there are three watches, the verses are difficult, for from midnight onward there is only one and a half watches. The Gemara answers that he holds like Rabbi Yehoshua, that the time of Krias Shema is until three hours, for such is the way of princes to rise then; thus six hours of night and two hours of day are two watches. If those hours were counted from netz, there would be more than two hours from alot hashachar, but since the watches end at alot hashachar, the proof is that the three hours are counted from alot hashachar.
