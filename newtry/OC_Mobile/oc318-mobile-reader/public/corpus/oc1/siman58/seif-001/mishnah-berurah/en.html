(1) The time of Keriat Shema—and likewise the blessings of Keriat Shema before it—one also may not say before this time.

(2) A slightly familiar [friend] with him—as a guest who comes at intervals; for if he is very familiar with him, even from far he recognizes him, and if he is not familiar at all, even close he does not recognize him. And this time was fixed for Keriat Shema—even though some people rise from their beds from alot hashachar and we read Keriat Shema in it with "when you rise" from the Torah—therefore ex post facto one has fulfilled, as below in seif 4; nevertheless, since the majority of people do not rise until one recognizes his fellow; and further because they established the parashah of tzitzit in Keriat Shema as written therein "and you shall see it"—therefore the Sages fixed its time from when one can recognize between tekhelet and white—and that is the time when one recognizes his fellow at a distance of four amot.

(3) Until the end—and lekhatchilah it is forbidden to delay until that time [Ner Eliyahu in the name of the Yerushalmi].

(4) Three hours—for so is the way of the sons of kings to rise then, and therefore we still read in it "when you rise." And these three hours are counted from the time of alot hashachar—so wrote Magen Avraham; and the view of the Gra is from the time of hanetz hachama. And regarding lekhatchilah there is no practical difference in this, for in any case it is forbidden to delay—as explained above in sk 3.

(5) That it is a fourth of the day—and there is no difference between summer days when the day is long and winter days when the day is short; we account the day as twelve hours, and always a fourth of the day is the time of Keriat Shema. Therefore one must be careful in winter days to hurry to read Keriat Shema, since the day is short and a fourth of the day is short [Taz]. And indeed in summer days one must be especially careful, for the way of most people is to rise at one time as in winter days—and then Keriat Shema has already passed; for the longer the day, the sooner the time of Keriat Shema comes, and one must complete it entirely in its time. And those who delay Keriat Shema on account of tzitzit or tefillin err; rather they should recite Keriat Shema with its blessings in its time without tzitzit and tefillin, and when they have them they put them on and read with them Keriat Shema or another parashah or a psalm of Tehillim. And even if he doubts lest the time of Keriat Shema pass—likewise he should not wait for tallit and tefillin, for they do not impede each other; but nevertheless he should wait, for anyone who recites Keriat Shema without tefillin is as one who testifies false testimony against himself. And there are people who delay the time of Keriat Shema on account of communal prayer—and this too is not correct. In any case they should be careful to read Shema in its time before prayer and intend to fulfill thereby; as explained at the end of siman 46 in Mishnah Berurah—see there. And one should not delay [going to] synagogue after reading on account of elders who delay coming and would need to pray individually, since thereby the time of Keriat Shema would pass—and we do not tell a person, sin on your account so your fellow gains merit. And on Shabbat and Yom Tov it is very common to pass the time of Keriat Shema because of several matters; therefore the wise person has his eyes in his head to gather a minyan and pray early, or at least to read Shema at its time before standing to pray with the congregation—as explained at the end of siman 46, and see there in Biur HaGra and in Beit Yosef Lekutim there.

(6) That they would prepare—for the essence of prayer lekhatchilah is from the beginning of hanetz hachama, as it is written "They shall fear You with the sun," as will be explained below in siman 89. And if they would begin to read from when one recognizes his fellow, there would be much [time] between this measure and hanetz hachama, and they would have to delay after Keriat Shema and wait until the sun buds, and they would not adjoin redemption to prayer; therefore they would prepare to read close to hanetz hachama and finish with hanetz.

(7) Hanetz hachama—the hour when the sun begins to shine on the mountaintops.

(8) Very [great reward]—and it is assured to him that he is a citizen of the World to Come and will not be harmed all that day.

(9) Measure of an hour—and there are variants that read "a tenth of an hour"; see in Biur HaGra who wrote it must be a third of a tenth of an hour—and see in Biur Halakhah.
