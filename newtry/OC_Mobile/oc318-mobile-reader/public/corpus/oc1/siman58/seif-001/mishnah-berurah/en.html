Who is somewhat familiar with him. Like a guest who comes occasionally; for if he is very familiar with him, he recognizes him even from far away, and if he is not familiar with him at all, he does not recognize him even close by. This time was fixed for Krias Shema, although some people rise from their beds from alot hashachar, and according to the Torah this is called 'when you rise,' and therefore after the fact he fulfills the obligation, as below in seif 4. Nevertheless, since most people do not rise until one can recognize his fellow, and furthermore because they fixed the passage of tzitzis in Krias Shema, in which it is written, 'and you shall see it,' the Sages fixed its time from when one can distinguish between blue and white, which is the time when one can recognize his fellow at a distance of four amos.

Three hours. For such is the way of princes to rise then, and therefore it is still called 'when you rise.' These three hours are counted from alot hashachar; so wrote the Magen Avraham. The Gra's opinion is that they are counted from netz hachamah. Regarding the initial obligation there is no practical difference, for in any case it is forbidden to delay, as above in seif katan 3.
Which is a quarter of the day. There is no distinction between summer days, when the day is long, and winter days, when the day is short; we calculate the day as twelve hours, and one quarter of the day is always the time of Krias Shema. Therefore one must be careful in winter to hurry to read Krias Shema, since the day is short and a quarter of the day is short. In truth, in summer one must be even more careful, for people generally rise at the same time as in winter, and then the time of Krias Shema has already passed, since the longer the day is, the sooner the time of Krias Shema arrives. One must finish all of it in its time. Those who delay Krias Shema because of tzitzis or tefillin are mistaken; rather, he should read Krias Shema with its blessings in its time without tzitzis and tefillin, and when he has them he should put them on and read Krias Shema with them, or another passage or chapter of Tehillim of Tehillim. Even if he is uncertain whether the time of Krias Shema may pass, he should not wait for tallis and tefillin, for they do not prevent one another. Otherwise, he should wait, for whoever reads Krias Shema without tefillin is as though he testifies falsely about himself. There are people who delay the time of Krias Shema for prayer with the congregation, and this too is not according to law. In any event they should be careful to read Shema in its time before prayer and intend to fulfill the obligation with it, as above at the end of siman 46 in the Mishnah Berurah. They should not delay the synagogue reading because of elderly people who come late, for the result is that they will need to pray individually, since through this the time of Krias Shema will pass; we do not tell a person, sin so that your fellow may merit. On Shabbos and Yom Tov it is very common for the time of Krias Shema to pass because of various matters; therefore the wise man has his eyes in his head, to gather a minyan and pray earlier, or at least to read Shema in its time before he stands to pray with the congregation, as above at the end of siman 46; see there in the Biur HaGra and Biur Halacha.
That they would time it. For the main initial mitzvah of prayer is from the beginning of netz, as it is written, 'They shall fear You with the sun,' as explained below in siman 89. If they began reading when one can recognize his fellow, there would be much time between that measure and netz, and they would have to delay after Krias Shema and wait until netz hachamah, and they would not join redemption to prayer. Therefore they timed it to read it close to netz hachamah and finish it with netz.
