1) [Seif 1] A person may make use of bread, provided that he does not render it repulsive, etc. If he does so for medicinal purposes, even something that renders it repulsive is permitted. Shabbos 108b; Magen Avraham at the beginning of the siman. Magen Avraham writes further there that anything needed by a person, even if it is not medicinal, does not constitute a degradation of food. For this reason, one may wash the hands with beverages when water is unavailable, as stated in siman 160, seif 12; see there. Eliyah Rabbah, paragraph 1, writes the same. See below, paragraph 4.
2) There: Therefore, one may not place raw meat upon it, etc. Although there is no prohibition due to blood, since its blood has already been removed through salting, it is nevertheless forbidden because the bread becomes repulsive. Likewise, one may not pass a full cup over it. Even if one is careful that the contents of the cup not spill, it is nevertheless forbidden, lest some spill upon the bread and render it repulsive. Bach. Even roasted meat in its juices is forbidden, for it renders the bread repulsive. Mishbetzos Zahav, paragraph 1.
3) There: Nor may one support the bowl with it, etc. For if some of its contents fall upon the bread, it will become repulsive. Bach, however, explains that this refers to a case in which the back of the bowl is moist or soiled with mud, so that the bread used to support the bowl also becomes repulsive; but we are not concerned that some soup might fall and render it repulsive. See there. Knesses HaGedolah, Hagahos Beis Yosef, paragraph 1, cites this and writes that the Levush agrees with the stringent view; see there. See Maamar Mordechai, paragraph 2, who raises numerous difficulties with the words of Bach; see there. Therefore, one should not depart from the ruling of the Shulchan Aruch, but one should also take the view of Bach into account. Thus, if the bowl contains nothing that would render the bread repulsive if it fell, one must nevertheless not support it with bread until examining its underside, lest it be soiled and render the bread repulsive. Mishbetzos Zahav, paragraph 2, writes that supporting a bowl is permitted when the bread does not become repulsive specifically because it serves the needs of eating, but using bread to support some other object is forbidden. Covering a utensil with bread, however, is permitted; see there.
4) There: For if it falls upon the bread, it will become repulsive. All the more so, one must be careful when washing the hands with the first waters, the middle waters, or the final waters that water not splash upon the bread. Chesed LaAlafim, paragraph 1.
5) There: Even washing that is not for the purpose of eating. Bach asks why this differs from the permission to anoint the hands with fragrant oil to remove grime. He answers that there it is an enactment of the Sages to remove the grime before Birkas HaMazon, since one mentions the Name and requires clean hands. They associated this with the verse, 'You shall sanctify yourselves,' etc.; 'I am Hashem' refers to fragrant oil. For this reason, the final waters are also permitted with wine and all other beverages, because they remove the grime, as stated in siman 181; see there. Taz, paragraph 1, cites this. Magen Avraham, however, proves that anything serving a need, even one that is not a mitzvah, does not constitute a degradation of food. His words were cited above in paragraph 1; see there.
