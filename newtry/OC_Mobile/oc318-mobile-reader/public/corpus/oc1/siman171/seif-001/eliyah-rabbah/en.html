[Levush] A prohibited act, etc. For medical purposes it is permitted in every case. One may sprinkle wine on the ground, make soaking solutions or pickled preparations with it, and anoint the body with wine and oil even when not for medical treatment, because these are human needs. When one sees food lying on the ground, one may not simply walk away and leave it there; he must pick it up, unless there is reason to fear sorcery, such as with a whole loaf. One may not cause food to become prohibited in benefit. Food fit for people should not be fed to animals, because it appears as though one is spurning the goodness of the Holy One, blessed be He. Taanit 20, Magen Avraham. I am uncertain, however, because the Gemara's wording, 'food fit for people, etc.,' may imply that this is not the accepted halachah, which may explain why the poskim omitted it.
[Levush] And some permit, etc. The Shulchan Aruch omits this permissive opinion. The Bach proves that bread is forbidden even if it will not become repulsive, and the later authorities agree.
