(1) His needs: Meaning, the needs of his ordinary use, as will be explained below.
(2) With bread: All the more so with other foods.
(3) But anything that becomes repulsive, etc.: This is prohibited even with other foods.
(4) One may not: If it is done for healing, it is permitted even though the food becomes repulsive, as explained below in siman 328, seif 20. Even apart from healing, if the use serves a human need and people ordinarily use food for that purpose, it is permitted. For this reason, one may sprinkle wine upon the ground and anoint the body with wine and oil, as explained in several places in the Gemara. One may not crush olives in order to wash his hands with the liquid that emerges from them, which is strong and removes filth, because the olives become repulsive thereby and this constitutes wasting food. Gemara.
(5) A full cup: Because it is likely to spill upon the bread and render it repulsive.
(6) If it is, etc.: This too is for the reason stated above. All the more so if the bottom of the bowl is unclean and will render the bread repulsive.
(7) Full, etc.: If it is not full, one may use bread to support it, even though he is using the bread, because a person may use bread for all his needs, as stated above. Likewise, one may cover a vessel with it. Tur.
(8) Which is not needed for eating: Washing before eating would in any event be prohibited according to many poskim. See siman 160, seif 12, for washing is performed only with water. Even here, where the washing is not needed for eating, it is prohibited because it degrades the wine. The same applies to other beverages. In a pressing circumstance, see above, siman 160, at the end of seif 12, and Biur Halachah there.
(9) And one may not throw bread: Beit Yosef and the other poskim explain that bread is regarded as more important than other foods. Therefore, it is prohibited to throw bread even if it does not become repulsive through the throwing, such as when it is thrown onto a clean place, because this degrades the bread.
(10) Which become repulsive through throwing: Such as figs and similar foods that are fully ripe, soft, and crushed by being thrown, thereby becoming repulsive.
(11) Such as nuts, etc.: They are hard and do not become crushed. This refers to throwing them onto a clean place, for otherwise it is prohibited even with nuts, as explained below in seif 4. When one sees food lying on the ground, he may not leave it there but must lift it, unless there is reason to fear sorcery, such as in the case of an entire loaf. Eruvin 64b. The Gemara in Taanis 20b states that Rav Huna holds that food fit for human consumption may not be fed to an animal, and the same applies to birds. Eliyah Rabbah, however, is uncertain whether the halachah follows Rav Huna. See Machatzis HaShekel, who inclines to rule that if one has nothing else to feed them except human food, all agree that it is permitted. Perhaps this is the basis for the common practice of feeding bread to birds.
