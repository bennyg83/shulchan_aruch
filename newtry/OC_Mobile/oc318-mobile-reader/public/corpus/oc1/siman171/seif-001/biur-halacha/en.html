One may not - See Mishnah Berurah regarding what I wrote, “and this is the common practice,” etc. This is evident from the discussion of crushing olives cited below by Magen Avraham, which Chazal prohibited. Although Magen Avraham answers there that soap is available, this is strained, for one may anoint himself with oil and we do not prohibit it because soap is available. Necessarily, where the common practice is to use food in this way, it is permitted, unlike crushing olives. Although they contain oil, people do not ordinarily anoint themselves with the fruit itself. According to all this, we have no source for Magen Avraham’s statement that anything needed by a person is not included in wasting food. On the contrary, one who examines the poskim in this siman will see that washing one’s hands with a beverage is prohibited because it wastes food. We are not discussing fools who wash with a beverage when water is available. Necessarily, whenever people do not ordinarily act in this manner, it constitutes wasting food in all circumstances. Regarding what he cited from Rambam, that wasting food is prohibited only when done in a degrading and rebellious manner, one who examines Rambam will see that washing the hands and similar uses, where the food is ruined, are themselves regarded as degrading and rebellious. According to our analysis, there is no source permitting one to wash his hands with other beverages even when water is unavailable, except for the alternative opinion cited by the Mechaber in siman 160, seif 12, to which Magen Avraham alluded. We already wrote there in Biur Halachah that this view appears only in Or Zarua, and even he states it only tentatively and for a different reason, namely that perhaps we do not consider the wasting of food in a pressing circumstance. This entire matter requires study.
