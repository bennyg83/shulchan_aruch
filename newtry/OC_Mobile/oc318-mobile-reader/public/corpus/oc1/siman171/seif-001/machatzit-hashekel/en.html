Seif katan 1: And one may not throw, etc. For example, an entire loaf, since sorcery is not practiced with a piece of bread.
It is stated in Shabbos 50b that one may not crush olives. Rashi explains: to strike them against a rock in order to sweeten their bitterness. This is prohibited because one wastes the liquid that emerges from them. According to this, one must distinguish between this case and sprinkling wine, soaking food in wine, or making pickled food with it, which are permitted. In those cases, the person benefits from the wine that he sprinkled or used for soaking or pickling. Here, however, he derives no benefit at all from the liquid lost through the striking. Although the olive becomes sweetened, this is not caused by the loss of the liquid but by the striking itself, and it is therefore prohibited.
And the Rif, etc. Because one can wash with soap. In such a case, although the use is not degrading or rebellious but serves a human need, it is nevertheless prohibited because the same result can be achieved without waste through another means. According to the Rif’s explanation, it may be permitted to sweeten the olives. This also appears somewhat from Chiddushei Rashba there.
In Taanis, etc. “And it appears like gluttony,” etc. Rashi concludes: Alternatively, because the Torah has concern for the property of Israel. According to this latter explanation, if one has nothing else with which to feed his animal except food fit for human consumption, it is permitted. Perhaps this is what the common practice relies upon when people feed chickens bread fit for human consumption.
