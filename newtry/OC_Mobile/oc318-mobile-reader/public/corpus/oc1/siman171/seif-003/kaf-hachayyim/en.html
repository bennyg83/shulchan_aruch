3) There: Nor may one support the bowl with it, etc. For if some of its contents fall upon the bread, it will become repulsive. Bach, however, explains that this refers to a case in which the back of the bowl is moist or soiled with mud, so that the bread used to support the bowl also becomes repulsive; but we are not concerned that some soup might fall and render it repulsive. See there. Knesses HaGedolah, Hagahos Beis Yosef, paragraph 1, cites this and writes that the Levush agrees with the stringent view; see there. See Maamar Mordechai, paragraph 2, who raises numerous difficulties with the words of Bach; see there. Therefore, one should not depart from the ruling of the Shulchan Aruch, but one should also take the view of Bach into account. Thus, if the bowl contains nothing that would render the bread repulsive if it fell, one must nevertheless not support it with bread until examining its underside, lest it be soiled and render the bread repulsive. Mishbetzos Zahav, paragraph 2, writes that supporting a bowl is permitted when the bread does not become repulsive specifically because it serves the needs of eating, but using bread to support some other object is forbidden. Covering a utensil with bread, however, is permitted; see there.
