And one may not See Taz, and so writes Magen Avraham, seif katan 1. It appears that one may use bread specifically to support a bowl, because this serves the needs of eating, provided it does not render the bread repulsive. To use bread for work unrelated to eating, such as supporting some object, is prohibited even if the bread does not become repulsive. This applies specifically to bread, for it is comparable to throwing it, according to the answer of Tosafos cited by Beit Yosef. Nevertheless, one may use it to cover a vessel and similar uses, for what difference is there whether it lies here or elsewhere? It appears that covering a vessel with books is prohibited. See siman 154, Magen Avraham seif katan 14, regarding a chest serving as a window, etc. A book is different, because it is evident that he placed it there in order to cover the vessel. Taz there prohibits placing one book beneath another as a support. It may be that all agree that using a book as a covering is prohibited. This requires study. Shmuel states in Berachos 50b that a person may use bread for all his needs. Tur cites this, but Shulchan Aruch omits the word “all,” in accordance with its position that throwing bread is prohibited even where it does not become repulsive, because using it disrespectfully for a purpose unrelated to eating is prohibited. Accordingly, we do not follow Shmuel in every respect. This is the meaning of the Gemara’s question, “In accordance with whom does Shmuel’s ruling follow?” It accords with Rabbi Eliezer. There is no need to elaborate.
