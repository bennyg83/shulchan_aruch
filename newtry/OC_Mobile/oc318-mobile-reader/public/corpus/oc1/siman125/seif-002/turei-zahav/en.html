"And one should raise the eyes," etc. — It seems to me this means they should be closed, but not with eyes wide open, for we rule in the chapter on the mitzvah of chalitzah that one praying must set his eyes downward and his heart upward, etc. And why should we not say thus regarding kadosh? And it is written in the Tur in the name of Sefer HaHeichalot: "Blessed are you to Heaven of the heavens and those who descend from the chariot — if you say and tell My children what I do at the hour when they sanctify and say kadosh kadosh kadosh, and teach them that their eyes should be raised upward to the house of their prayer and they lift themselves upward, for I have no pleasure in the world like that hour when their eyes are raised in My eyes and My eyes in their eyes." And Beit Yosef in the name of Shelah concludes this: at that hour I grasp My throne of glory in the likeness of Jacob and embrace him and kiss him and mention their exile and hasten their redemption. In Midrash Tanchuma, parashat Tzav, it is stated: "and with two he will fly" — from here they enacted to fly on one's legs when the prayer leader says kadosh kadosh kadosh.
