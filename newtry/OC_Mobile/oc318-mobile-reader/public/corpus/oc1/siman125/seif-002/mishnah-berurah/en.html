(4) "To pay attention to one's feet" — as written in siman 95. And one must pay greater attention in kedusha to sanctify the Name, may He be blessed; and in this merit holiness from above will rest upon him. And he should intend to fulfill the verse "and I will be sanctified among the children of Israel." And the Arizal, may his memory be blessed, would warn greatly about this. The text is "as they sanctify" and not "as one sanctifies." Also one should separate somewhat between the word "as they sanctify" and the word "it" that is written afterward, as above in siman 61 regarding Keriat Shema. [Later authorities]

(5) "And one should raise [the eyes]," etc. — For they wrote in the name of Sefer HaHeichalot, may his memory be blessed: "Blessed are you to Heaven of the heavens and those who descend from the chariot — if you say and tell My children what I do at the hour when they sanctify and say kadosh kadosh kadosh, and teach them that their eyes should be raised upward to the house of their prayer and they lift themselves upward, for I have no pleasure in the world like that hour when their eyes are raised in My eyes and My eyes in their eyes. At that hour I grasp My throne of glory in the likeness of Jacob and embrace him and kiss him and mention their merit and hasten their redemption."

(6) "The eyes" — meaning they should be closed, but not with eyes wide open — so the Taz. But many later authorities disagree on this, and Magen Giborim ruled like them.

(7) "And so they shake [their bodies]," etc. — The Shelah wrote that he received by tradition that one lifts one's body and heels upward in Baruch and yimloch as in kadosh.

(8) "And they lift them from the ground," etc. — For it is stated in Midrash Tanchuma, parashat Tzav: "and with two he will fly" — from here they enacted to fly on one's legs when saying kadosh. And Magen Avraham wrote, and not like those who leap and jump; but the Arizal and other later authorities justify the custom, and the custom of Israel is Torah. Nevertheless, where the custom is not thus, it is obvious one should not do so, lest they come to mock and one's reward come through his loss. Bach wrote it should read "and so is written by your prophet." And our version is "as is written by your prophet"; and Magen Avraham wrote it also has support — see there. The later authorities wrote: although answering amen yehei shmeih raba is preferable to kedusha, nevertheless if one already heard Kaddish and has not yet heard kedusha, and it happened to him to answer Kaddish and kedusha — it is better that he answer kedusha in order to fulfill his obligation. Peri Megadim wrote: if he began kedusha and in the middle heard Kaddish, it is reasonable that he not interrupt to answer amen yehei shmeih raba, but rather finish kadosh three times until kevodo; and if he hears it then, he should say amen yehei shmeih raba.

(9) "In the middle of kedusha" — Darkei Moshe wrote that Maharil would not speak from the beginning of kedusha until after amen of El HaKadosh [Rabbi Akiva Eiger].
