(Seif katan 2) "They shake," etc. — as Bach — it should read "and so is written," etc.; for the Tur wrote: "We will sanctify Your name in Your world, as they sanctify it in there above, and so is written by your prophets, and one called to the other and said" — and there are in it 14 words as in the verse "and one called to the other and said" — end of his words. So is the version of Bach in the Tur; and Bach holds that until the words "and one called" there are 14 words; and therefore he wrote that it must say "and so is written." But according to our version that we say "as is written," there are in it only 13 words; and on this Magen Avraham wrote, and our version in the Tur does not have "as is written," etc. — meaning that in Turim printed with Bach it is written in the Tur: "We will sanctify Your name in Your world, as they sanctify it in there above, and one called to the other and said" — end of his words; and if so, perforce one must say that what the Tur wrote "and there are in it 14 words" means from the words "We will sanctify," etc., until the word kadosh — and included within the 14 words are the five words "and one called to the other and said." If so, whether one says "as is written" or "and so is written," in every case the count does not align with the count the Tur wrote. And our version has support, etc. — meaning: if they say "as is written," there are from "We will sanctify" until the Name only [the prescribed] words, as Moshe said in parashat Haazinu, as stated in Yalkut; unlike "and so is written," where there are 22 words until the Name.
