(2) There — "but they are silent and concentrate," etc. — so too in Beit Yosef in the name of Rosh and Tur; and he wrote that it appears one should do thus when the chazzan says "in their abode they praise," etc. — they are silent until he reaches Baruch kavod, etc., and then they answer; and likewise when he says "and in Your holy words" they are silent until he reaches "He shall reign," etc., and then they answer. And one can reject this: Rosh did not limit that they should not say with the chazzan except Nakdishakh and Na'aritzakh — which is like Yitgadal and Yitkadash, etc., that only the prayer leader says on behalf of the entire congregation until he reaches Kadosh, and then the entire congregation answers; but "in their abode," etc., and "and in Your holy words," etc. — it is possible the congregation should say them. And the first [view] appears more [correct] — end of his words. And Eliyah Rabbah wrote, note 1 in Beit Yosef: it appears he is uncertain, and therefore he did not write it in Shulchan Aruch; but Knesset HaGedolah wrote in Hagahot Tur that the custom is that the prayer leader says "in their abode they praise," and the congregation answers Baruch kavod, etc.; and when he says "and in Your holy words" it is written that the congregation answers and says yimloch, etc. — end of his words; and this is the first explanation that Beit Yosef wrote. And they brought it in She'eilot uTeshuvot, folio 138 side b; 163, siman 8, ot 15. However, the Taz in seif katan 1 challenged the words of Maran above — that in Beit Yosef from what he wrote in siman 109, seif 2: if one began to pray with the prayer leader, he says with the prayer leader all of kedushah word for word; and likewise he challenged from what Mor uKetziah wrote, siman 59, seif 3: the custom has spread that even an individual says the order of kedushah in Yotzer — see there — it appears his view is that an individual may say the entire order of kedushah with the prayer leader; and so too Eliyah Rabbah there. And Maamar Mordechai, ot 2, answered the Taz's difficulty: if the entire congregation says it, it appears like frivolity; but nevertheless if they want to say them quietly — it is possible; and see there that he answered further answers — see there. It turns out this matter is a dispute among the later authorities; and therefore we have only the words of the Arizal, who wrote in Shaar HaKavanot, drush 3 of the repetition of the Amidah: one must say with the prayer leader the entire order of kedushah word for word from Nakdishakh and Na'aritzakh until the end of all kedushah; however the two words of Nakdishakh and Na'aritzakh one must say aloud, and the rest of the words until answering kadosh kadosh kadosh one says quietly with the prayer leader; and afterward when one answers kadosh kadosh kadosh, etc., they are aloud, with eyes closed, and leaping upward — end of his words. And so too in Pri Etz Chaim there; Magen Avraham there, seif katan 2; She'eilot uTeshuvot, folio 138 side a, at the time of ot 1.
