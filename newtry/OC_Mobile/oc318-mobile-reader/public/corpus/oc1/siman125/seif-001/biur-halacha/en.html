"The congregation does not," etc. — see in Mishna Berurah; and this is from Machatzit Hashekel. And in the Shulchan Aruch of the Gra he wrote that it is permitted for an individual who wants to say Nakdesh; but if the entire congregation wants to say it, it is permitted by law even not together with the prayer leader; nevertheless ab initio one should not conduct oneself thus. And as in Kaddish, where the congregation answers only after "Yitgadal" that the prayer leader says — so in kedushah ab initio one should answer kadosh and Baruch after Nakdishakh or Nakdesh that the prayer leader said; and so it is implied in Biur HaGra — that this is the reason of Shulchan Aruch that wrote "the congregation does not," etc.: it is implied that even if the entire congregation wants to say it as well, it is not worthwhile ab initio. And in our times, when people have adopted saying Nakdesh — it follows the writings of the Arizal that Magen Avraham brought, or they hold like the Taz in seif katan 1.

"But they are silent," etc., "and then the congregation answers kadosh" — see in Mishna Berurah in what he wrote that one must beware not to lag after the congregation or to precede them, for one does not say a matter of kedusha with fewer than ten; and see above at the end of siman 124 what we wrote there. And I am uncertain regarding the prayer leader: whether he is also obligated to say kadosh and Baruch together with the congregation — for behold he does not remove himself from the category, since he says Nakdesh, etc.; and how can he afterward say it not with ten? And one can say: since the prayer leader stands to discharge the many from their obligation, he is considered like the congregation — that is, for kedushah Chazal required each of the listeners to say kadosh and Baruch by himself. And it is possible that since one standing in prayer — we rule in siman 104, seif 6 — that he yearns and has kavanah for what the prayer leader says in order to fulfill through this — he is considered as one standing to discharge the many from their obligation and may say it even afterward. And if the prayer leader or another individual begins to say kadosh before the congregation finishes their utterance — he is certainly considered like the congregation (Eliyah Rabbah).
