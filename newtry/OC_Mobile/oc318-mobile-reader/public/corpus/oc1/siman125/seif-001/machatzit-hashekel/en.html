Seif katan 1. They are silent, etc. — because it is impossible [to say] a matter of kedusha, etc.; meaning that it was enacted that the prayer leader should say it on behalf of the congregation and be their agent; and when the congregation also says it with him, how can the prayer leader be called their agent — for each one says it himself? Therefore we require at least nine to be silent and the prayer leader be their agent; and all the more so those nine must listen to the prayer leader and not speak other matters.

Even if there is a minyan besides him — it is forbidden. One can say: not to speak other matters; but even to say with the prayer leader is forbidden — for although the prayer leader properly becomes agent for the other nine, nevertheless one who says his own words appears to become an agent, but in truth he does not become agent for anyone.

And it is not called kedusha, etc. — "in their abode," etc., "and in Your holy words," etc. — it is implied that likewise for "in their abode," etc., and "and in Your holy words," etc., the prayer leader alone should say and the congregation should not say with him. And so is the conclusion of Rabbeinu Yonah in his book. But the Taz disagrees and permits the congregation to say them as well — "in their abode," etc., and "and in Your holy words," etc.

What is stated in kamatz — meaning under the alef of "ha'amur" (what is stated).

Before the congregation begins. For the congregation's words Baruch kavod, etc., and likewise yimloch, etc., are the conclusion of the prayer leader's words — the prayer leader says "in their abode, Baruch," and they should answer. Meaning: the band of angels called "Baruch"; and therefore the congregation says what is written "Baruch" — they mean Baruch kavod, etc.; and likewise "and in Your holy words," etc., and the congregation answers yimloch, etc. And if so, how can the congregation precede their utterance before the prayer leader finishes his words?

And it is difficult: how did he bow, etc. — siman 113; and here it is not needed that he bowed only a little, as Magen Avraham wrote in siman 113 — for then he would not have needed to rise at the Name. Since there is no complete prostration; and so it appears somewhat from the words of Maharshal that Magen Avraham brought, siman 127, seif katan 2.

Like adding, etc. — in those places there is an enactment of the Sages to bow; therefore if one adds bowing in Baruch or in Modim, which is similar to the enactments of Chazal, it is like adding; but in another place that is not similar to the place where our Sages enacted — it is not like adding.

"And on every floor," etc. — for he forbade above in siman 113.

And "and thanksgiving" — meaning it is similar to Modim; and therefore it is forbidden.
