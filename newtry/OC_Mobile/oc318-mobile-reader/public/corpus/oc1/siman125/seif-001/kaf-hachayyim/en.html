1) [Seif 1] The congregation does not say 'Nakdishach' with the shaliach tzibbur, etc. The Tur wrote that in Ashkenaz they say a different text: 'Nekadesh es shimcha b'olamecha' [We will sanctify Your Name in Your world], 'just as they sanctify it in the heavens above, as it is written,' etc. However, in Shaar HaKavanos, in the text of the tefillah, folio 51c, he wrote in the name of the Arizal, of blessed memory, and this is his language: In the repetition one should say 'Nakdishach v'naaritzach,' according to the custom of the Sephardim, and not according to the custom of the Ashkenazim, who say 'Nekadesh es shimcha,' etc.; and likewise at Musaf one should not say 'Naaritzach v'nakdishach,' according to the custom of the Ashkenazim, but rather 'Keser yitnu,' etc., according to the custom of the Sephardim. End of his language. And it was cited above in siman 48; see there. So too is written in Shaar HaKavanos, the third discourse on the repetition of the Amidah; Pri Etz Chaim, chapter 2 of Shaar Chazaras HaAmidah; Mishnas Chassidim, in the maseches of She'eiris HaAtzilus, chapter 2, ot 2. And the Tur wrote that this text of 'Nekadesh shimcha,' etc., contains fourteen words, just as there are in the verse 'V'kara zeh el zeh,' etc. And the Bach wrote that accordingly one must say 'v'chein kasuv al yad neviacha' [and so is it written by the hand of Your prophet] and not 'kakasuv al yedei neviacha' [as is written by the hands of Your prophet], etc., for if so there would be only thirteen words; see there. Accordingly, in the text of 'Nakdishach' one must say 'kakasuv,' for if he says 'v'chein kasuv,' there would be fifteen words until 'v'kara zeh,' etc. And see the Magen Avraham, s.k. 2, who wrote that even if one says 'kakasuv' in the text of 'Nekadesh,' there is support from the Chizkuni, parashas Haazinu, that one must say twenty-one words before mentioning Hashem, as Moshe did in parashas Haazinu; see there.
2) There: Rather, they are silent and concentrate, etc. So wrote the Beis Yosef in the name of the Rosh and the Tur, and he wrote: It appears that so should one act when the chazan says 'Le'umasam meshabchim,' etc.: they are silent until he reaches 'Baruch kevod,' etc., and then they answer; and likewise when he says 'Uv'divrei kodshecha' they are silent until he reaches 'Yimloch Hashem,' etc., and then they answer. Yet one may push this aside, for the Rosh excluded them from saying with the chazan only 'Nakdishach v'naaritzach,' which is like 'Yisgadal v'yiskadash,' etc., which only the shaliach tzibbur says in the name of the whole congregation until he reaches 'Kadosh,' and then the whole congregation answer; but 'Le'umasam,' etc., and likewise 'Uv'divrei kodshecha,' etc., the congregation may well say. And the first appears more correct. End of his words. And the Eliyah Rabbah, ot 1, wrote that in the Beis Yosef he appears to be in doubt, and therefore he did not write it in the Shulchan Aruch; but the Knesses HaGedolah wrote in the Hagahos HaTur that the custom is that the shaliach tzibbur says 'Le'umasam meshabchim v'omrim' and the congregation answer 'Baruch kevod,' etc.; and when he says 'Uv'divrei kodshecha kasuv leimor,' the congregation answer and say 'Yimloch Hashem,' etc. End of his language. And this is like the first explanation that the Beis Yosef wrote. And the Shalmei Tzibbur cited him, folio 138b; Kesher Gudal, siman 18, ot 15. However, the Taz, s.k. 1, raised a difficulty against these aforementioned words of Maran in the Beis Yosef, from what he wrote in siman 109, seif 2, that if one began to pray together with the shaliach tzibbur, he says the entire Kedushah with the shaliach tzibbur word for word; and likewise he raised a difficulty from what Moreinu HaRav Moshe [Isserles], of blessed memory, wrote in siman 59, seif 3, that the widespread custom is that even an individual says the order of the Kedushah of Yotzer; see there. From which it appears that his view is that an individual may say the entire order of the Kedushah with the shaliach tzibbur; and so wrote the Eliyah Rabbah there. And the Maamar Mordechai, ot 2, answered the aforementioned difficulty of the Taz: that if the whole congregation say it, it appears like light-headedness; but nevertheless, if they wish to say it silently, it is quite proper; and see there, where he answered with additional answers; see there. It emerges that this matter is subject to dispute; and therefore we have nothing except the words of the Arizal, of blessed memory, who wrote in Shaar HaKavanos, the third discourse on the repetition of the Amidah, and this is his language: You must say with the shaliach tzibbur the entire order of the Kedushah word for word, from 'Nakdishach v'naaritzach' until the conclusion of the entire Kedushah; however, the two words 'Nakdishach v'naaritzach' you must say aloud, and the remaining words, until the response of 'Kadosh, Kadosh, Kadosh,' you should say silently with the shaliach tzibbur; and afterward, when you answer 'Kadosh, Kadosh, Kadosh,' etc., it should be aloud, with closed eyes, and while rising upward [on one's toes]. End of his language. So too is written in Pri Etz Chaim there; Mishnas Chassidim there; Magen Avraham, s.k. 2; Shalmei Tzibbur, folio 138a; Shaarei Teshuvah, ot 1.
