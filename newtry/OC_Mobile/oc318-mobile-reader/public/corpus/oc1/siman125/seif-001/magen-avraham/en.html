"Silent." Also for Kaddish — do not say with the chazzan, for a matter of kedusha is impossible except with ten; one must understand the chazzan and answer after him (Beit Yosef, Rosh). And he wrote in the gloss of Yesh Noflim: even if there is a minyan besides him it is forbidden, and even to learn by heart — unless when the chazzan prolongs in the melody during the melody and not when he cuts the letters. And it is not called kedusha except "We will exalt You," etc., "in their abode," etc., "and in Your holy words," etc.; but the rest of the text they add on Shabbat is not in the category of kedusha, end quote. And see siman 63: on Shabbat they say as the matter stated in kamatz (Seder HaYom siman 475). And the prayer leader should finish "in their abode," etc., "and in Your holy words," etc., before the congregation begins Baruch and yimloch [Seder HaYom siman 476]. Maharil, when he said Baruch and yimloch, would bow and rise at the Name, and it is difficult how he bowed in a place where the Sages did not say — siman 113 (Aleph Hei). And it seems to me specifically when saying "Baruch ata Heaven" or Modim it is forbidden to bow, for this is like adding to the enactment of the Sages; and "on every floor before You you shall bow" is forbidden because it is praise and thanksgiving. But in general it is permitted, for in the middle of a blessing it is permitted to bow. And I received that one lifts one's body and heels upward in Baruch and yimloch as in kadosh (Shelah).
