(1) They established the practice of studying, etc. Some places do not recite Eizehu Mekoman in a mourner's house because Torah study is forbidden to a mourner. This is incorrect. Anything that forms part of the established daily order is not prohibited to a mourner as Torah study, just as was ruled in siman 554, seif 4.
(2) Midrash is like Gemara. They selected this baraisa because it is the beginning of Toras Kohanim, which opens the body of the laws of the offerings, and therefore placed it alongside the passages of the offerings. They likewise selected the Mishnah Eizehu Mekoman and placed it after the passage of the daily offering because the verse states, “In every place incense is burned and an offering is presented to My Name.” Are incense and offerings truly brought in every place? Rather, this refers to Torah scholars who engage each day in the laws of the Temple service. Wherever they may be, Hashem considers it as though they burned incense and presented offerings to His Name. A further reason is that this chapter contains no dispute and is a clear Mishnah transmitted to Moshe at Sinai. Reciting this chapter and this baraisa counts as studying Mishnah and Gemara only for one who understands them. One who does not understand must study and comprehend their meaning. Otherwise, the recitation is not considered Torah study. Prayer differs. Even when the worshipper does not understand, the Holy One, blessed is He, knows his intention. If one recites a Mishnah or baraisa without understanding it, however, this is not called Torah study. Unlearned people in particular must understand the explanation so that through these passages they fulfill the daily obligation to study Scripture, Mishnah, and Gemara. Shelah writes that when reciting Eizehu Mekoman, Bameh Madlikin, or Pittum HaKetores, one should use the traditional chant of Mishnah study. See there.
