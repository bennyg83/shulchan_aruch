(1) They established to recite, etc. — some places do not say Eizehu Mekoman in a house of mourning because Torah study is forbidden there, and this is incorrect; for whatever is seder hayom does not involve Torah study for a mourner, as ruled in siman 554, seif 4.

(2) That midrash is like Gemara — they chose this baraita because it is the beginning of Torat Kohanim, which is the head of the entire section of sacrifices; they placed it with the sacrifices. They likewise chose the mishnah Eizehu Mekoman and placed it after the daily offering, because it is said, "and in every place incense is burnt and offered to My name" — do they in every place burn incense and offer? Rather, these Torah scholars who occupy themselves with the laws of the sacrificial service every day, wherever they are — I consider it as though they burn incense and offer to My name. And further, because in this chapter there is no dispute, and it is a mishnah given clearly to Moses at Sinai. Reciting this chapter and this baraita does not count as study of Mishnah and Talmud except for one who understands; but one who does not understand must study and understand, for otherwise it is not considered study — for specifically in prayer, even though one does not understand, the Holy One, blessed be He, knows his intent; but if one recites the mishnah and the baraita and does not understand, it is not called study. In particular, ordinary people must understand the commentary so that through this they fulfill their obligation of studying Scripture, Mishnah, and Gemara, which a person must study every day. Shlah wrote that when one says Eizehu Mekoman, or "With what does one kindle," or Pitum HaKetoret, one should make the melodic tune of mishnayot; see there.
