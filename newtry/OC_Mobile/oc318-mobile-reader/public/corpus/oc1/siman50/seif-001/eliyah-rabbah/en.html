[1] Eizehu Mekoman, etc. And "VeChalil La'asham." Abudraham wrote: the multitude of the people read "asham" with a weak shin, and this is an error, for according to their reading it suggests "anashim" — "I will call individuals of interest to you" — whereas the correct reading is with a dagesh in the shin, from the context "and a fire for guilt shall not be extinguished"; therefore one should not write a yud between the alef and shin. These are ashamot, etc. There are six ashamot: the first five have the alef of asham attached with a sheva and patach; but the sixth, which is asham talui, the alef is pointed with kamatz because it is a noun. The exposition of this chapter and the exposition of Rabbi Yishmael are explained at length in Abudraham; one who wishes to master them should look there. From the fact that they did not rely on Rashi's commentary in the Mishnayot and Talmud, it seems somewhat that they hold it proper for every person to study his exposition to understand what he says every day — see Magen Avraham. Asham gezeilot, etc. — all of them have sheva and patach attached to the following word, except asham talui, which has kamatz under the shin and alef; and eil nazir has tzere attached to nazir.
