1) [Seif 1] They established the recitation, after the passage of the Tamid, of the chapter Eizehu Mekoman. The reason they instituted reciting the chapter Eizehu Mekoman is that in that entire chapter there is no dispute, and it is a clear mishnah transmitted to Moshe from Sinai. Beit Yosef in the name of the Ra'ah; Ateret Zekeinim. And so it is written in Shaar HaKavanot, in the discourses on the morning prayer, folio 13b, that the reason they were accustomed to say the chapter Eizehu Mekoman and no other is that there is no chapter in all the Mishnayos without a dispute except for this chapter, and it is called settled halacha; end of his words. Now, that which he wrote, that there is no dispute in this chapter, means that no dispute is mentioned within it itself; but among the laws mentioned within it there are some in which there is a dispute elsewhere, for it is written in the chapter Eizehu Mekoman that the Pesach offering is eaten only until midnight, and in tractate Berachos, folio 9a, there is a dispute about this between Rabbi Elazar ben Azariah and Rabbi Akiva: Rabbi Elazar said until midnight, and Rabbi Akiva said until the break of dawn; see there. And see further Tosafot, Pesachim folio 89a, s.v. de'ilu Pesach, etc.; see there. And afterward I saw that the Machazik Berachah wrote so in the name of Maharcha and the Ritva; see there, and Shalmei Tzibbur cited it, folio 63b. And see Peri Megadim, Eshel Avraham, ot 2. And Ateret Zekeinim wrote that this chapter also has 344 words, and together with the title reading it equals the numerical value of Moshe, to indicate that the Oral Torah was given to Moshe from Sinai; end of his words. Shtei Zeitim, ot 1.
