1) [Seif 1] They established to recite after the section of the daily offering the chapter Eizehu Mekoman — the reason they enacted to recite the chapter Eizehu Mekoman is that there is no dispute anywhere in that chapter, and it is a mishnah given clearly to Moses at Sinai. Beit Yosef in the name of the Ra'ah; see Ateret Tzvi; and similarly in Shaar HaKavanot in the homilies on tefillin and tzitzit, page 13b, that they were accustomed to say the chapter Eizehu Mekoman and not another, because there is no chapter in all the Mishnah that is without dispute except this chapter, and it is called a decided halachah — so it ends. Now, what he wrote that there is no dispute in this chapter means that no dispute is mentioned within it; but regarding the laws mentioned in it, some of them are disputed elsewhere — for it is written in the chapter Eizehu Mekoman that the Passover offering is eaten only until midnight, and in tractate Berachos 9a there is a dispute between Rabbi Elazar ben Azariah and Rabbi Akiva: Rabbi Elazar ben Azariah says until midnight, and Rabbi Akiva says until the break of dawn — see there. See also Tosafos Pesachim 99a, s.v. de'ilu Pesach leyom, etc. — see there. Afterward I saw that the Mechaber so wrote in the name of Maharach and Ritva — see there — and Shaarei Tzion brought it in siman 493, 2b; see Peri Megadim, Orach Chayyim, letter 2. Ateret Tzvi wrote that this chapter also has approximately 376 words, and with the reading it amounts to the name Moshe, to say that the Torah given orally was given to Moses at Sinai — so it ends.
