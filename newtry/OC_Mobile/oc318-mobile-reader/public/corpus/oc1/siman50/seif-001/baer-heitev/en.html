Eizehu Mekoman. This is a clear Mishnah transmitted to Moshe at Sinai. It contains 344 words, and together with the act of recitation the total is 345, the numerical value of Moshe. Ateres Zekeinim. Some places have the custom not to recite it in a mourner's house, but this is incorrect. Knesses HaGedolah. The Mechaber likewise rules in siman 554, seif 4. See there.
Mishnah. Magen Avraham writes: It appears to me that this applied specifically in their time, when people understood Aramaic. Nowadays, when people do not understand it, they must study its meaning. Otherwise, it is not considered Torah study. Regarding prayer, even when a person does not understand, the Holy One, blessed is He, knows and understands his intention. If, however, he recites a Mishnah without understanding it, this is not called Torah study. One must therefore learn its explanation. Shelah writes that when reciting Eizehu Mekoman, Bameh Madlikin, or Pittum HaKetores, one should use the traditional chant of Mishnah study. See there.
