Eizehu Mekoman — for it is a mishnah given clearly to Moses at Sinai. It contains 376 words, and with the reading it spells Moshe — see Ateret Tzvi. Some places were accustomed not to recite it in a house of mourning, and this is incorrect — see Knesset HaGedolah. So too ruled the Mechaber in siman 554, seif 4; see there.

Mishnah — Magen Avraham wrote: it seems to me that specifically in their time they understood the language of the Aramaic translation, but now that they do not understand they need to study to understand, for otherwise it is not considered study; for specifically in prayer, even though one does not understand, the Holy One, blessed be He, knows his intent; but if one recites the mishnah and does not understand, it is not called study; therefore one must study the commentary. Shlah wrote that when one says Eizehu Mekoman, or "With what does one kindle," or Pitum HaKetoret, one should make the melodic tune of mishnayot; see there.
