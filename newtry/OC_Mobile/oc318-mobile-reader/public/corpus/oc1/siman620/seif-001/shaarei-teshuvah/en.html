It is good to shorten, etc. Responsa Har HaCarmel, siman 27, wrote regarding an incident that occurred on Yom Kippur during the repetition of the prayer aloud, at the piyyut 'LeKel Orech Din': a fire broke out in the city, may Hashem protect us, and everyone fled from the synagogue; the shaliach tzibbur also uprooted himself from his place and went home, and he also interrupted with words and other forms of interruption. This is considered compulsion, since ten people did not remain listening to the shaliach tzibbur. Since he paused the amount of time needed to complete the entire prayer, he returns to the beginning. The measure of 'paused to complete all of it' is the measure of the prayer without the piyyutim. Here, since he was in the first three blessings, even if he paused only a small amount of time he also returns to the beginning, because the first three blessings are considered one blessing, and he returns to their beginning. So too here, according to what was stated above, since it is compulsion because of panic and distress, he must return to the beginning. In my humble opinion, regarding interruption in the first three blessings everyone agrees that this is so even if the interruption was not due to compulsion. Only where we require a pause long enough to complete all of it in order to require returning to the beginning do the authorities distinguish between an interruption due to compulsion and one not due to compulsion; see there. This is unlike the first three blessings, where even if he did not pause, etc., he stops; and so too where he paused in the middle of a subject, to return to the beginning of that blessing alone, there is no distinction between an interruption due to compulsion and one not due to compulsion. However, the piyyut that they already said need not be repeated when he returns to the first three blessings. But 'Zochreinu' and 'Mi Chamocha,' since they are part of the blessing formula, he must say them again. Although if he forgot them he need not return, here is different; see there.
