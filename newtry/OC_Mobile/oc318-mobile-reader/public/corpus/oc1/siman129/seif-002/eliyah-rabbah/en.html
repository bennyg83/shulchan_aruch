(2) And therefore, etc. — on Yom Kippur, and the same applies to all fasts per Levush; and it seems he follows his reason that on all fasts there is no nesiat kapayim at Mincha because of lo pelug — and I already wrote this does not exist; and if so they need not the reason "if he ascended," etc., for they are fit for nesiat kapayim; and so in Shulchan Aruch and Rama they did not mention other fasts at all. Also per his view one may distinguish between Yom Kippur and a complete public fast known to all, versus other fasts where it is not so known that everyone fasts. And this seems to me the reason of R' Yaakov Weil, who wrote in Lekutim siman 41: those fasting on Rosh Chodesh Nisan until noon and praying in congregation — not to say Elokeinu, etc., for it is not fit for nesiat kapayim, lest they err as on Mincha of other days — until here. And we are because even if he ascended he descends, for it is not so known as I explained; even if one says on all fasts it is known, nevertheless here that they do not fast the whole day it is not so known — so it seems to me. Unlike Magen Avraham, who wrote that Maharal R' Yaakov Weil disagrees and holds that even on Yom Kippur they do not say Elokeinu; and per us who hold like Rama, they say also per the view of Maharal R' Yaakov Weil. And per my humble opinion they do not disagree, as we said; and further that even on Yom Kippur at Mincha Bach wrote it does not seem a proper custom that they say Elokeinu — see there; and do not add to him; and see above end of siman 127. He wrote in Sefer Tzeda LaDerech: every kohen who blesses is blessed, and those who curse him shall not see light, as it is said "and those who curse you I will curse" — until here. And it seems he was precise: it should have said "and those who curse you I will curse" as written "and those who bless you shall be blessed," but he explained "I will curse" that light will be lacking from them. He wrote in Shiyurei Keneset HaGedolah: the custom is to begin ne'ilah prayer so there will be time to bless Birkat Kohanim before the stars emerge — until here. And see below siman 623, for it implies that even if there was time by day to lift hands at ne'ilah there is no nesiat kapayim, lest it be confused in other years, for they always finish at night — and service is comparable to avodah.
