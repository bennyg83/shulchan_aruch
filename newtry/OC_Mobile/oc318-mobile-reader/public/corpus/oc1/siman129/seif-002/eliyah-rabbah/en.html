Therefore, etc. This refers to Yom Kippur, and the Levush says the same applies to all fasts. He follows his own view that on all fasts there is no Birkat Kohanim at Minchah because of the principle not to distinguish between cases. I have already written that this is incorrect. If so, there is no need on other fasts for the reason that 'if he ascended, etc.,' because those times are inherently fit for Birkat Kohanim. The Shulchan Aruch and Rama do not mention other fasts here at all. Even according to the Levush, one can distinguish between Yom Kippur and a full communal fast, which everyone knows is observed, and other fasts where it is less obvious that everyone is fasting. This seems to me to explain Rabbi Yaakov Weil's ruling in his Likkutim, siman 41, that those who fast on Rosh Chodesh Nisan until midday and pray together should not say Eloheinu, etc., because the time is not fit for Birkat Kohanim and people may confuse it with Minchah on ordinary days. The reason is that here, even if a kohen ascended he would descend, since the fast is not widely known. Even if one holds that ordinary public fasts are known, a fast only until midday is certainly less known. This excludes Magen Avraham's interpretation that Rabbi Yaakov Weil disagrees and holds that even on Yom Kippur one does not say Eloheinu. According to our ruling like the Rama, Magen Avraham therefore says it should also be said in Rabbi Yaakov Weil's case. In my humble opinion they do not disagree, as explained. Moreover, even regarding Minchah on Yom Kippur the Bach writes that saying Eloheinu is not a proper custom; see there. We should not extend the leniency beyond that. See above at the end of siman 127. Tzeidah LaDerech writes that every kohen who blesses is blessed, while those who curse will not see light, as the verse says, 'and the one who curses you I will darken.' It appears he derives this because the verse should have said 'I will curse,' parallel to 'those who bless you are blessed,' but instead it means that I will diminish their light. Shiyarei Knesset HaGedolah writes that his custom is to arrange Ne'ilah so there is enough time for Birkat Kohanim before nightfall. See below siman 623, which implies that even if it happens that there is still daylight at Ne'ilah, there is no Birkat Kohanim, lest people confuse that year with other years when Ne'ilah ordinarily ends at night, and song is analogous to the Temple service.
