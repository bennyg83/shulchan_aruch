And although the Rosh in Hilchot Tefillin siman 16 brought in the name of a Gaon that a blind person is exempt from tefillin and also from tzitzit—see there—this view is rejected and we do not rule thus, but as our master wrote, may his memory be blessed, and his commentators—see the Rosh there and Ma'adanei Yom Tov, letter 6, as in Knesset HaGedolah in Hagahot Tashbetz, letter 1, and Yad Aharon in Hagahot Tashbetz, siman 206, letter 1. And what Rabbi Chaim Palaggi wrote in Lev Chaim siman 100: although we rule that a blind person is obligated in tzitzit, nevertheless he should not bless on it because there is an opinion that a blind person is exempt, and we are in doubt—Ben Avraham already wrote on his words in Parashat Lech Lecha, letter 15, that the widespread custom is that a blind person blesses on the tallit, and where there is a custom we do not say "we are in doubt"—see there. And in his book Rav Pe'alim, part 2, siman 7; and so too we wrote in our humility above, siman 7, letter 2, in the name of several poskim that where there is a custom we do not say "we are in doubt"; and so too Taz siman 46, letter 7—see there. And likewise the widespread custom among all decisors is to rule that where there is a custom we do not say "we are in doubt":
