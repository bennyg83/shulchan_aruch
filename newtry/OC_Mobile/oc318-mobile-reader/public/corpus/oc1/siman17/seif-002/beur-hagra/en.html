Section 2, "Women, etc." And although in the Jerusalem Talmud Kiddushin, and Tosafos brought it—Megillah 2b, s.v. "because which is it," etc., Rabbi Ila said, etc.—if so, there is no proof from the aforementioned that even the Rabbis hold that nighttime clothing is exempt; our Gemara does not hold like that Jerusalem Talmud, as explained above, Menachot 41a; and also the anonymous Gemara in Kiddushin mentioned above that they are exempt follows Rabbi Shimon:

And servants. Chagigah 1a, and so forth:

"Nevertheless, etc." This follows Rabbi Yosei and Rabbi Shimon, who say that women may voluntarily perform leaning on a sacrifice. Although the anonymous Mishnah in Rosh Hashanah 32b is not according to them, nevertheless an actual ruling carries great weight, as in Chagigah 16b and Eruvin 96b. The Rambam also ruled this way, not like the Jerusalem Talmud in Berakhot, which says that Yonah's wife was made to return and that the Sages protested against Michal. See above, siman 589, section 6.

"And to recite a blessing, etc." This is from what is stated in Bava Kamma 87a, "At first, etc." If it were so, he would lose the blessings. But the Rambam holds that one is not permitted to recite a blessing, as stated in section 3 and as he wrote explicitly there. His reason is that they cannot say "and commanded us," for even regarding matters in which one is obligated rabbinically the Gemara asks, "And where did He command us, etc.," in chapter 2 of Shabbat. There is no proof from a blind person, because he is obligated rabbinically. The proof is from what is stated in Pesachim 116b: "A blind person is exempt, etc.; the Rabbis hold, etc." That is to say, since they are obligated rabbinically, they can therefore discharge others' rabbinic obligations, as stated in Berakhot 20b; therefore he can recite a blessing, as stated in chapter 2 of Shabbat and chapter 4 of Sukkah regarding the Chanukah lamp, and in Megillah 24b: "Rabbi Yehudah says: anyone who has not, etc." This implies that if he has seen, he may discharge others' obligation. But a woman is exempt even rabbinically, as stated in Berakhot there and Sukkah 2b. See Tosafot in Eruvin and in Rosh Hashanah there, and Hagahot Maimoniyot, chapter 3 of the Laws of Tzitzit.

"Since, etc." Menachot 41a: "Granted, etc." See Tosafot there, in the comment beginning "All are obligated in tzitzit, etc."

"They are obligated because of doubt." Rosh Hashanah 29a.

"Without a blessing." See what I wrote in siman 688, section 4.

"And according to, etc." For we rule like Rabbi Yosei and Rabbi Shimon, who say that women may voluntarily perform leaning on a sacrifice. The first opinion holds that since the anonymous Mishnah in chapter 4 of Rosh Hashanah follows that view, it is an anonymous Mishnah against a dispute in a baraita, and the law follows the anonymous Mishnah, as stated in chapter 4 of Yevamot and as stated in Eruvin 96a: "No; Rabbi Meir holds, etc."
