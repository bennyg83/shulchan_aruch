Since it is not an obligation upon the person. The gloss upon this should read: “since it is only an obligation upon the person,” so that it not contradict what is written below in siman 19, where they state that tzitzis is an obligation upon the person. Mahari and Lechem Chamudos. It is possible, however, that no emendation is necessary. Rama's intent in writing that it is not an obligation upon the person is that a person is not obligated to make tzitzis for a tallis unless he wears it. Only when he wears the tallis does he become obligated to place tzitzis upon it. Women do not ordinarily wear a four-cornered garment, and if a woman were to wear one it would appear arrogant. Nachalas Tzvi.
Women who wish to do so may recite the blessing, but it is preferable that they not recite it. Tur in the name of Rabbeinu Tam. I am surprised that he did not write the same regarding shofar in siman 589, but instead wrote that although women are exempt, they may blow and recite the blessing, and one should not protest. Some maintain regarding a minor that, since nowadays everyone is careful to purchase a garment for himself, one must likewise purchase a four-cornered garment for a minor in order to educate him, just as one does for himself. The entire purpose of educating a minor is that he become accustomed from childhood to fulfilling the mitzvah of tzitzis, as he sees his father do. Bach. I heard that some are stringent to inspect the tzitzis even when the tallis is folded, because Shmuel maintains that garments stored in a box are obligated in tzitzis, and some authorities rule like him. Rif, however, writes that Shmuel's ruling is not accepted.
