Since it is only an obligation of the person. {Rama: On this matter, the correct reading is "since it is only an obligation of the person," so that one should not question what is stated later in siman 19, where they wrote there that tzitzit are an obligation of the person (Mahari and Lechem Chayyim), and perhaps there is no need to emend anything; and the Rabbi's intention in his gloss that wrote "and it is not an obligation of the person" means that one is not obligated to put tzitzit on a tallit if he does not wear it, but only when he wears the tallit is he obligated to put tzitzit; and it is not the custom of women to wear a four-cornered garment, and if a woman were to wear one it would appear as yuhara (showing off) (Nachalat Tzvi).}

Women, if they wish, may recite a blessing; and it is better that they not bless (Tur in the name of Rabbeinu Tam). And I wonder why he did not write similarly about the shofar in siman 589, but rather wrote that even though women are exempt, they may blow and recite a blessing and no one objects to them doing so, as explained above. And some say regarding a minor: since now everyone is careful to purchase for themselves, therefore one must also purchase for the minor a four-cornered garment to educate him, like for oneself—that all education of a minor should be that from his youth he be accustomed to fulfilling the mitzvah of tzitzit when he sees his father doing so (Bach). I heard that there are those who are stringent to check the tzitzit even when folding the tallit, because Shmuel holds that a folded garment is obligated in tzitzit, and some poskim rule like him; and the Rif wrote that Shmuel's view is not accepted.
