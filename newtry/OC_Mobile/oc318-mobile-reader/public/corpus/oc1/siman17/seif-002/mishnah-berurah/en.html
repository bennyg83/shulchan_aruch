(3) That time causes — for at night is not the time for tzitzis, and every positive mitzvah whose time causes exemption women are exempt from them even rabbinically, for the entire Torah is compared to tefillin, as it is written in them “so that the Torah of God shall be in your mouth,” and just as they are exempt from tefillin, which is compared to Torah study, as it is written therein “and you shall teach them to your sons and not to your daughters,” so they are exempt from every positive mitzvah whose time causes exemption. And slaves we derive by verbal analogy from “to her” from woman, for every mitzvah from which a woman is exempt, a slave is also exempt:

(4) And to bless on it — for even one who is not commanded and fulfills has reward, and it is appropriate to say “Who has commanded us,” since the men were commanded; they too have reward:

(5) And it is not an obligation of the person — with this one resolves why women bless on lulav, which is also a positive mitzvah whose time causes exemption, and one answers that it is different there because it is not an obligation of the person — for even a man has no biblical obligation to acquire a four-cornered tallis; only if he wraps himself is he obligated to make tzitzis on it, unlike lulav, for regarding a man it is an obligation of the person, being an obligation of the body. And know that we rule regarding tzitzis: obligation of the person and not obligation of the object — and both are for leniency. Obligation of the person for leniency — to exclude obligation of the object, that whenever he does not wear the tallis, even if it has four corners, he is exempt from tzitzis. Not obligation of the person — that he is not obligated to buy himself a tallis in order to become obligated in tzitzis; only if he has a four-cornered tallis and wears it is he then obligated in tzitzis. See siman 19:

(6) Obligated out of doubt — for a Torah doubt is treated stringently, as Beit Yosef wrote; and from this it follows that a matter whose obligation is only rabbinic — such as a borrowed tallis after thirty days, as above in siman 14 — or a garment whose obligation is only due to doubt — such as a garment half open and half closed, as above in siman 10, seif 7 — they may go in it without tzitzis. And see in Peri Megadim that he wrote similarly again; and perhaps one should be stringent in all this because of appearances, as stated in Shaarei Challah there:
