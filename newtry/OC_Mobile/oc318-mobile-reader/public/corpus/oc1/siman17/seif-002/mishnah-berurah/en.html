(3) Caused by time. Night is not the time for tzitzis. Women are exempt even D'Rabbanan from every time-bound positive commandment, because the entire Torah is compared to tefillin, regarding which the verse says, “so that the Torah of Hashem shall be in your mouth.” Just as women are exempt from tefillin, which is compared to Torah study, concerning which the verse says, “You shall teach them to your sons,” and not to your daughters, so they are exempt from every time-bound positive commandment. The exemption of servants is derived by a verbal analogy from women: every mitzvah from which a woman is exempt, a servant is likewise exempt.
(4) And recite a blessing over it. Even one who is not commanded but performs a mitzvah receives reward. It is therefore appropriate to say “and commanded us,” since men were commanded, and women too receive reward for performing it.
(5) And it is not an obligation upon the person. This answers why women recite a blessing over lulav, which is likewise a time-bound positive commandment. Lulav differs because, for a man, it is an obligation upon the person, meaning a bodily obligation. Tzitzis, however, is not an obligation upon the person in that sense. Even a man has no Torah obligation to purchase a four-cornered tallis. Only if he wraps himself in such a garment must he attach tzitzis. Know that regarding tzitzis we rule both that it is an obligation upon the person and that it is not an obligation upon the person, and both rulings are leniencies. It is an obligation upon the person as a leniency, excluding an obligation upon the garment: so long as he does not wear the tallis, even though it has four corners, it is exempt from tzitzis. It is not an obligation upon the person in the sense that he is not obligated to purchase a tallis in order to become obligated in tzitzis. Only if he owns a four-cornered tallis and wears it does he become obligated. See siman 19.
(6) They are obligated because of doubt. A doubt concerning a Torah obligation is treated stringently. So writes Beit Yosef. This implies that regarding something whose obligation is only D'Rabbanan, such as a borrowed tallis after thirty days, as explained above in siman 14, or a garment whose obligation itself is doubtful, such as a garment exactly half open and half closed, as explained above in siman 10, seif 7, they may wear it without tzitzis. See Peri Megadim, who writes a similar point. Perhaps one should nevertheless be stringent in all these cases because of appearances, as stated there in seif 8.
(7) Without a blessing. Since the essential obligation arises only from doubt, regarding a blessing, which is D'Rabbanan, we follow the lenient rule. See below in siman 67, in Mishnah Berurah, where, with Hashem's help, several principles concerning this will be explained in the name of the poskim.
(8) Male and female characteristics. An androgynos is likewise of doubtful male or female status. See Artzos HaChayim, which brings proofs from several sources that the practical halachah treats an androgynos as doubtful, not as an independent category.
