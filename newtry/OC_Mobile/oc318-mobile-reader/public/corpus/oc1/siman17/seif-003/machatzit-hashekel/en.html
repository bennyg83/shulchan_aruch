(Note 3) “Tzitzis” — meaning to acquire, etc. Bach, Mordechai, siman 24, etc. Meaning that in our Talmud it states: a minor who knows how to guard tefillin — his father is obligated to buy him tefillin; a minor who knows how to wrap is obligated in tzitzis. And since it does not also teach regarding tzitzis that his father is obligated to buy him a tallis, it implies specifically that if the child already has a tallis his father must fix tzitzis for him, but he need not buy him a tallis — except that Mordechai brought in the name of the Yerushalmi that it teaches that his father is obligated to buy him a tallis, and ruled accordingly, except that if our Talmud disagreed we would not set aside our Talmud and rule like the Yerushalmi. Therefore the Magen Avraham wrote, siman 24, that it is not so much an obligation, meaning our Talmud does not disagree with the Yerushalmi except specifically regarding tefillin, for the father is obligated to buy himself tefillin. And since his son reached the age of education, the baraita teaches explicitly an obligation that his father must buy him tefillin, for he must educate him that the son perform the act of the mitzvah like the father. But regarding tzitzis, for the father himself — if he does not have a four-cornered tallis he is not obligated by law to buy himself a four-cornered tallis, only that it is good and proper to buy one for himself. And the same applies to his son: if he reached the age of education, it is the path of honesty and goodness to buy him a tallis, for a son who reached the age of education has all the law of the father. And truly this is the Yerushalmi’s intent that his father must buy him a tallis — from the side of honesty and goodness, not from the side of law. Nevertheless our Talmud was not comfortable using the language of obligation for what is only the path of goodness and propriety, since it is not a complete obligation; therefore it teaches “obligated in tzitzis” — for certainly if he already has a tallis there is an obligation on the father to fix tzitzis for him as for himself:
