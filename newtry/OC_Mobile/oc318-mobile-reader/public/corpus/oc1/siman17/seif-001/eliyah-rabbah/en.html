[1] And it is not an obligation of the person, etc. In Eliyahu Zuta I wrote an explanation that lulav differs, for a man is commanded to take a lulav for himself; but here, even a man is not commanded except when he has a garment—then he puts tzitzit on it, as stated below in siman 24. And that which is written in Shulchan Aruch in siman 19 that tzitzit are an obligation of the person means to exclude obligation of the garment, so that one should not say that even when the tallit lies in a box one is obligated in tzitzit; but in truth a man is not obligated to buy himself a tallit for tzitzit. The Levush appears to have been troubled by contradictory statements in Shulchan Aruch, and therefore understood that there is a scribal error in Shulchan Aruch and one must read "since it is only an obligation of the person," and from this one must resort to all the forced distinctions he extended between lulav and tzitzit. So too understood Lechem Chamudot page 103, and they did not analyze this properly; so too Nachalat Tzvi, and in Levush himself at the beginning of siman 24. Further, the Levush wrote that Michal daughter of Saul is different, for she was a king's wife whom everyone recognizes; and Nachalat Tzvi questioned: whence that Michal wore tzitzit? If because it states in Eruvin 96 that she laid tefillin, he would think that presumably she also wore tzitzit. If so, Tosafos there should be questioned—for they wrote for the one who says it is not optional, the reason is that tefillin require a clean body; see there—if so, why did they protest regarding tzitzit? So far. And I have not reached the bottom of his view, for the Gemara did not challenge that they should have protested only regarding tefillin; but regarding tzitzit, in truth they need not have protested. Nevertheless the Levush's words do not seem correct for this reason, for I already wrote that he does not mean "obligation of the person" except to exclude obligation of the tallit. And I am further troubled by Yoreh De'ah siman 182: even in one of the garments it is forbidden for a woman to change, even if it is recognizable in her other garments—and it is strained to reconcile. And more will be explained there that only when she wears it for identification and adornment does the prohibition of a woman wearing a man's garment apply; see there.
