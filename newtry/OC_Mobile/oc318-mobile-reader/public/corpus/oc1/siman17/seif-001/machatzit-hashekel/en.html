Se'if katan 1: “And recite a blessing over it.” This is because even one who is not commanded, etc. If women received no reward for the mitzvah, it would not be appropriate for them to bless and offer praise over it. Regarding a mitzvah for which one receives reward, however, it is fitting to praise Hashem for it: because He commanded us, we can hope to merit the reward for the mitzvah, even when one serves not for the sake of receiving reward. The fact that reward is granted for a mitzvah enables us to recognize the mitzvah's great value and to praise Hashem for drawing us close to it and sanctifying us through His mitzvot. This would not apply if we received no reward. Magen Avraham therefore writes that even one who is not commanded but performs the mitzvah receives reward. The Talmud states that one who is commanded and performs is greater than one who is not commanded and performs. If one who is not commanded received no reward, how could the Talmud compare the two and say that the commanded person is greater? They would not be comparable at all, since one would receive reward and the other would receive none.
The phrase “and commanded us” is also understandable. It does not constitute speaking falsely, since the men were commanded. The words “and commanded us” refer to the Jewish people as a whole, who were commanded concerning the mitzvah.
“And they too receive reward.” This means that women recite a blessing over the mitzvah concerning which the men were commanded, because through that command women at least receive the reward of one who is not commanded but performs. Had the men not been commanded concerning it, then even if we assumed that one who performed it would receive the reward of one who is not commanded but performs, this is not one of the rationally self-evident mitzvot. If Hashem had not commanded us concerning it, we would never have imagined that this mitzvah reflected the Creator's will, blessed is He, and we would not have performed it at all. Consequently, no one would have merited any reward for it.
