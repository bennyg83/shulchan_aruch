(Section 1) And to bless on it. For even women, etc.—for if women did not receive reward for it, it would not be appropriate to bless and give praise for that mitzvah. Granted, for a mitzvah for which one receives reward, it is fitting to give Him praise for it, for through our being commanded in it we hope to merit the reward of the mitzvah—even a worker who does not intend to receive payment. Nevertheless, for a mitzvah on which reward was given, through this we recognize the greatness of the value of the mitzvah and give Him praise that He brought us near to that mitzvah, and through this He sanctified us in His mitzvot—unlike if we did not receive reward; and therefore he wrote that even one who is not commanded and performs receives reward. For we say in the Gemara: greater is one who is commanded and performs than one who is not commanded and performs; and if not that even one who is not commanded and performs receives reward, how could it be appropriate to say "greater is one who is commanded," etc.—for they have no similarity at all, for this one receives reward and that one does not receive reward:

And this is resolved: “and commanded us” means there is no issue of speaking falsely, since the men were commanded; and “and” refers back to “and commanded us” regarding the entire community of Israel who were commanded in it:

“And they too have reward.” Meaning that women also bless on what the men were commanded; thereby women in any case receive reward, like one who is not commanded but fulfills — for were the men not commanded in it, even if one who fulfilled it would receive reward like one not commanded but fulfills, nevertheless this is not among the intelligible mitzvos. And had God not commanded us in it, we would not have conceived that the Creator’s will lies in that mitzvah, and we would not fulfill it at all, and consequently they would not earn any reward:
