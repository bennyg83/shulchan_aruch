Se'if 3. In the gloss: and it is invalid even in pressing circumstances, though there is one who says it is valid, and therefore the custom in these countries is, etc. Note: In my humble opinion, this does not depend on that reasoning, but rather on what we read in the Talmud, Sukkah 37b: Rabbi Shimon holds that these are for the walls, while palm leaves and leaves of a thick tree are for sechach. This implies that myrtle leaves and leaves of a thick tree are both for sechach. In the Yerushalmi, Sukkah chapter 3, halachah 4, we read: Rabbi Yasa, when he came up here, saw, etc., until: did he not hear what Rabbi Simon said in the name of Rabbi Yehoshua ben Levi, 'and that they should announce and proclaim,' etc.? It is not myrtle and it is not a thick tree; rather, myrtle is for the sukkah and a thick tree is for the lulav. These are its words. See the Korban HaEdah's explanation. According to this, in any event it is proven in the Yerushalmi that myrtle-wood means without avos, and it appears that this means two and one, while 'thick tree' means an actual complete myrtle, three upon three. According to this, since our Talmud says that myrtle leaves, etc., and leaves of a thick tree are for sechach, it is proven that even a hadas shoteh, which is two and one, is valid for sukkah. Since it is valid for sukkah, it is valid for lulav according to us, for Rabbi Yehudah requires the four species that are in the lulav. Therefore, since the plain Talmud holds like Rav Kahana, so we rule. However, Tosafos had a difficulty: according to Mar bar Rav Ashi, who said that such a myrtle is a hadas shoteh, how will Rabbi Yehudah explain what the verse says, 'myrtle leaves,' since it is invalid for sechach? Therefore Tosafos wrote that according to the initial assumption, which held that this is a hadas shoteh, Rabbi Yehudah would also establish this as referring to the walls, meaning that even myrtle leaves are for the walls, and only leaves of a thick tree are for sechach. This explains well the wording of Tosafos, 'he would establish it,' and they did not write, 'Rabbi Yehudah establishes it as for the walls.' Their intent is that certainly according to what we rule, like Rav Kahana, myrtle leaves are also for sechach; only according to the reasoning of Mar bar Rav Ashi would he establish even myrtle leaves as being for the walls. According to us, myrtle leaves are for sechach, proving that we rule that two and one is valid. We rely on this as practical halachah. So it appears to me correct and clear. Examine this, for I have been brief.
