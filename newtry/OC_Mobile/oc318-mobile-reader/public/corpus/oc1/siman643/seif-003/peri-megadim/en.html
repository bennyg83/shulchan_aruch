On the other days. See Turei Zahav. The words of Maharam of Rothenburg in the Tur, 'even on a weekday, etc.' Nevertheless, one can say that the Mechaber wrote according to the Rambam that one recites Kiddush standing and says sukkah and afterward Shehecheyanu, and on the second night the opposite, as in siman 661, but in any event the berachah of sukkah is standing. If so, it is obvious that on the other days one recites sukkah before Hamotzi, before he sits, unlike Maharam in this respect; see Bayis Chadash. Rather, the custom is after Hamotzi, while seated, like the Rosh; automatically, according to the custom, one also recites Kiddush seated. The Turei Zahav, of blessed memory, did not explain it this way.
