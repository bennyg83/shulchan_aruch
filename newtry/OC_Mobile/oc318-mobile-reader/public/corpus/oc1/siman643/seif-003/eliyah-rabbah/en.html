[3] And afterward he recites, etc. If the sukkah was already made and standing with its sechach, he recites Shehecheyanu and afterward leishev ba-sukkah, as written in siman 661; this is the Magen Avraham in the name of the Bayis Chadash. This requires analysis, for in the Gemara, Sukkah 46, it says that if it was made and standing, he recites Shehecheyanu when he sits in it. If so, he certainly recites Shehecheyanu afterward. On the contrary, the opposite is reasonable: when it was not made and standing, and he recites Shehecheyanu at the time of making, then he recites Shehecheyanu and afterward leishev ba-sukkah, since he no longer needs Shehecheyanu for the sukkah. So too the Bayis Chadash concluded in this language: what the Gemara said, sukkah and afterward Shehecheyanu, is only where he did not recite Shehecheyanu at the time of making, unlike Rashi's explanation. Therefore it appears to me that there is a scribal error at the beginning of the Bayis Chadash's words, and it should read: if the sukkah was not made and standing, etc.; then he recites Shehecheyanu for the sukkah at the time of making it. What he wrote, 'unlike Rashi's explanation,' appears to me to mean: to exclude Rashi, who explained that the Gemara speaks of a sukkah already made and standing. He explains that it speaks even where it had not been made, and nevertheless he did not recite Shehecheyanu then, because he wanted to attach it to the cup. So it appears to me. However, it is astonishing that the Bayis Chadash explained the Tur this way, for the Rosh wrote that if one recited Shehecheyanu at the time of making, he is exempt from Shehecheyanu of the day. At the end of siman 641 I wrote about this; see there.
