3) Ibid. And sukkah, and afterward Shehecheyanu, etc. There is no distinction between whether the sukkah had been made previously or whether he made it before the festival. Since he did not recite Shehecheyanu when making it, he recites the blessing over the sukkah and afterward Shehecheyanu. As for what the Bach wrote, distinguishing that if it had already been made and was standing with its s'chach, its law is like that of the second night, when one recites Shehecheyanu and afterward the blessing over the sukkah, as written in siman 661, and the Magen Avraham cited his words at the beginning of the siman, the later authorities have already disagreed with him. Nehar Shalom, sec. 1; Bigdei Yesha; Eshel Avraham; Levushei Serad. See Eliyah Rabbah, sec. 3, regarding what he sought to emend in the words of the Bach. Chayei Adam, klal 79, sec. 3, wrote that the Bach's ruling to recite Shehecheyanu and afterward the blessing over the sukkah applies specifically when he recited a blessing over the sukkah at the time he made it; see there. However, regarding the essential law where he recited the blessing at the time of making it, see above, siman 641, sec. 7, where we wrote that there is a dispute whether he should again recite Shehecheyanu over the festival, and where there is a doubt regarding blessings, one is lenient; see there.
