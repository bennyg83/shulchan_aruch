Because. See Turei Zahav, Sukkah 56a and 46a. The Bayis Chadash, of blessed memory, also explained that because Shehecheyanu refers back to Yom Tov and the sukkah, he gives the reason that Shehecheyanu comes after the sukkah, unlike Rashi's explanation on 56 regarding a sukkah already made and standing. Therefore, if one said Shehecheyanu at the time of making, he again says on Yom Tov the Shehecheyanu of Yom Tov first and afterward leishev ba-sukkah; or if it was made and standing, see Magen Avraham, and with Hashem's help it will be explained there. What is written in the Gemara, that the obligation of the day is preferable, and that both are needed, means that first, Shehecheyanu also relates to the sukkah, yet nevertheless frequency is preferable, and therefore he says, etc.; see the Bayis Chadash here and in siman 661. Therefore on the second night, when Shehecheyanu does not relate to Kiddush, the Rosh concludes that Shehecheyanu comes and afterward sukkah, for the frequent comes first, as in siman 661; see there. One can add flavor from the Mishnah, 'here is matzah and chametz,' which is a refutation; see Tosafos 56a, s.v. ve-hilchasa. The Rosh explains that the halachah is when it was not made and he did not recite Shehecheyanu at the time of making, that he recites sukkah and afterward Shehecheyanu, because it relates to both; on the second night, Shehecheyanu and afterward sukkah, like the plain Mishnah 'here is matzah and chametz.' See the Yerushalmi in Perek HaChalil, halachah 1, where there is an answer to this; see there. But our master David, the Turei Zahav, of blessed memory, explained that the Tur refers to Rav Ashi's statement that Rav Kahana arranged them over the cup, Sukkah 46a. This is as written in the Chiddushei Ritva there, that certainly, by the law of the Gemara, it is a mitzvah to recite Shehecheyanu immediately at the time of making the sukkah, with Shehecheyanu on the making, and on the first night to say leishev ba-sukkah and Shehecheyanu on the festival, and the Shehecheyanu of making the sukkah does not exempt it. Tosafos there, s.v. nichnas, the Rosh there in chapter 4, siman 3, and the Ran there, 22b, s.v. ve-im, wrote as a possibility that Shehecheyanu for making the sukkah exempts the Shehecheyanu of the festival; it implies that the halachah is not so, but that even if he recited Shehecheyanu at the time of making, he recites two berachos upon entering. See Eliyah Rabbah, siman 641, subparagraph 1. Rather, Rav Kahana speaks where it was already made and standing, or where he forgot to recite Shehecheyanu at the time of making. As the baraisa teaches, when he enters, immediately upon entering he recites two berachos, leishev ba-sukkah and Shehecheyanu, the Shehecheyanu being for the sukkah of making; or possibly, when it is made and standing, he recites Shehecheyanu on the fulfillment of the mitzvah of sukkah. With Hashem's help, this will be explained in Magen Avraham. Rav Kahana arranged the two berachos specifically over the cup because Shehecheyanu also refers back to the Kiddush of the day; therefore he waited and arranged everything over the cup of Kiddush. This is what the Tur and Shulchan Aruch wrote, that Shehecheyanu refers back to the Kiddush of the day and to the mitzvah of sukkah. If the intent were that the reason Shehecheyanu comes afterward is because it also relates to the sukkah, it should have said simply that Shehecheyanu also refers back to the sukkah, and that would suffice. Rather, it says that it refers back to the Kiddush of the day and to the sukkah, and therefore he waits until Kiddush for Shehecheyanu. But Shehecheyanu comes after sukkah because the obligation of the day is preferable, according to his view in siman 661 that on the second night as well one says sukkah and afterward Shehecheyanu. From what is written in the baraisa on 46a, 'he says Shehecheyanu; when he enters to sit he says sukkah,' and it does not mention Shehecheyanu of Yom Tov, one can say that Shehecheyanu of Yom Tov is said in the marketplace. This is not so where he did not say Shehecheyanu at the time of making, or where it was already made and standing; in those cases one can say that Shehecheyanu for the sukkah, at fulfillment of the mitzvah, requires two berachos specifically, sukkah and afterward Shehecheyanu, so that Shehecheyanu also relates to the sukkah. According to this, on the second night Shehecheyanu comes and afterward sukkah, and this supports the Rosh in siman 661. Understand this. Regarding whether, if he said Shehecheyanu at the time of making, he should say it on Yom Tov, it implies in Chiddushei Ritva that he should say the Shehecheyanu of Yom Tov, but this still requires analysis. See siman 641 and Eliyah Rabbah.
