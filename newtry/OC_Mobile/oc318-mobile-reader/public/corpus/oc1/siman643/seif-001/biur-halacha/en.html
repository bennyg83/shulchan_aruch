And afterward Shehecheyanu. See Mishnah Berurah, where I wrote that there is no difference whether the sukkah had already been made and standing with its s'chach, etc. Even in that case, the Shehecheyanu blessing relates to the sukkah, meaning fulfillment of the mitzvah of sukkah. This is the consensus of the later authorities, including Levushei Serad, Eliyah Rabbah, Nahar Shalom, Chayei Adam, and Bikkurei Yaakov, unlike what the Magen Avraham wrote in the name of the Bach. Some later authorities write that the Bach too agrees, but there is a scribal error in his words, as is evident to one who studies them. What we wrote, that this applies because he did not bless at the time of making it, means that if he blessed when making it, the Shehecheyanu now applies only to the festival; therefore he should bless Shehecheyanu and afterward sukkah. According to Tosafot and the Rosh, in that case he need not recite Shehecheyanu at all, because the Shehecheyanu he recited before the festival on the sukkah automatically helps for the festival as well, as we wrote in siman 641.
The Magen Avraham further writes that if he did not recite Shehecheyanu on the first night, he recites it all seven days, citing the Gemara. I do not understand, for we rule that even on the second night he recites Shehecheyanu because of the festival. If so, even if he did not recite it on the second night, he recites it all seven days because of the festival. This is unlike Shehecheyanu for sukkah, for if he recited it before Sukkot at the time of making the sukkah, he fulfilled it; therefore on the second night he need not recite Shehecheyanu because of the sukkah, having fulfilled it the previous day. But regarding Shehecheyanu for the festival, if the second day is the festival, he does not fulfill with yesterday's Shehecheyanu even if he intended also for the festival. In truth the Gemara does not mention the first night; its wording is, "if he did not bless today, he blesses tomorrow or another day." One can say that outside Eretz Yisrael, regarding the second night too, this language applies. Derekh HaChayim writes this explicitly, and it requires study.
