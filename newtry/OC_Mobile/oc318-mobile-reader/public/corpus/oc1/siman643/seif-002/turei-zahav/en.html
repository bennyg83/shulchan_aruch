According to the Rambam, he recites Kiddush while standing. The Beit Yosef explains his reasoning as follows. The Rambam understood the blessing 'to dwell in the sukkah' as referring to actual sitting. To recite the blessing before performing the mitzvah, he would bless while standing before sitting. On the first night, however, he cannot recite the blessing until after first reciting Kiddush. He therefore had to recite Kiddush while standing so that he could also recite the sukkah blessing while standing, thereby reciting the blessing before performing the mitzvah. End quote. The Rosh challenges the Rambam that, according to this, the blessing should be recited before entering the sukkah, because entry is the mitzvah rather than sitting. If one ate while standing, he would still be obligated to recite the sukkah blessing. Rather, the mitzvah of sukkah consists of eating and spending time there, and if one recites the blessing after sitting, it is still properly considered before performing the mitzvah. This is similar to the Yerushalmi in the chapter HaRoeh, which states that after one has wrapped himself he says, 'to wrap oneself in tzitzis.' Although he is already wrapped, he recites the blessing because the mitzvah continues for as long as he remains wrapped. The same applies to sukkah, where the mitzvah continues throughout the meal. The phrase 'You shall dwell in sukkos' means that one should remain there, as in the verse, 'The people remained in Kadesh,' etc. End quote.
In my humble opinion, the Rosh's challenge to the Rambam presents no difficulty. It does not appear at all that the Rambam interprets 'to dwell in the sukkah' as meaning that the mitzvah consists of literal sitting. If so, how would he interpret 'You shall dwell in sukkos'? Were we commanded to sit literally throughout all seven days? Rather, the Rambam also understands it as an expression of establishing oneself and remaining there. However, this establishment is recognizable only through actual sitting, by which one demonstrates that he wishes to remain there. As long as he stands, this is not recognizable, for perhaps he will immediately turn around and leave. It is certainly not recognizable merely through entering. Therefore, the blessing does not apply until he prepares to sit, because the beginning of sitting is the beginning of the recognizable indication that he wishes to remain. He must recite the blessing before performing the mitzvah, even before beginning the act. This is what the Sages meant when they said that one who enters in order to sit recites the blessing. It would be inappropriate to recite the blessing before entering, because entry would then intervene before the mitzvah begins, namely before the recognizable establishment. This would not merely be before performing the mitzvah, but excessively early.
The Rosh, however, holds that since the mitzvah continues throughout the day, every moment is considered before a further performance of the mitzvah. Although it would be preferable to recite the blessing entirely before the act so that even the beginning of the mitzvah would be preceded by the blessing, since the primary mitzvah is eating, it is better to place the blessing immediately before the primary mitzvah. Regarding tzitzis, one may ask why the Yerushalmi says that the blessing is recited after wrapping, rather than entirely before performing the mitzvah. One can answer that there too the primary mitzvah occurs when one takes hold of the tzitzis, which is possible only after first wrapping oneself. It is therefore better to place the blessing immediately before taking hold of the tzitzis, even though that occurs after wrapping. Although the wording of the blessing refers to wrapping, this is proper because the mitzvah requires him to remain wrapped throughout the day, just as with sukkah.
The Rambam's position is thus beautifully protected from the Rosh's challenge. Their disagreement is only this: According to the Rambam, one recites the blessing before sitting, because sitting begins the establishment in the sukkah. According to the Rosh, it should be recited before the primary mitzvah, which is eating. According to the Rambam, this law applies throughout all seven days of the festival, and one should recite the blessing before sitting down to eat. If he enters the sukkah some time before the meal, he likewise recites the blessing before sitting. If he later eats, even after a lengthy interval, he does not recite another sukkah blessing, because the Rambam does not accept the reasoning that eating is the primary mitzvah. According to our practice, however, which follows Rabbeinu Tam that eating is the primary mitzvah, the earlier sitting does not matter, and one recites the blessing only at the time of eating, before the meal. This appears to me to be the correct explanation of their words.
