(4) He recites Kiddush standing, etc. According to his view, sitting is the beginning of performing the mitzvah, and one must recite the berachah before performing it, meaning before the beginning of the mitzvah act. He should practice this way all seven days. The Rosh holds that one should attach the berachah to the primary mitzvah, which is the eating, as written at the end of siman 639, that the custom is to recite the berachah only over eating.
(5) Leishev ba-sukkah, and he sits, and afterward recites Shehecheyanu. This comes to exclude reciting Shehecheyanu before he sits. Although the berachah of Shehecheyanu itself may, according to all opinions, be recited standing, nevertheless it is not proper to do so for another reason: one must attach the performance of the mitzvah to the berachah. Since he recited leishev ba-sukkah, he must sit immediately. Regarding whether several householders may recite Kiddush in the sukkah at once, see above, siman 488, Mishnah Berurah subparagraph 8. It is proper to say the berachah of sukkah aloud, drawn out in melody like the other berachos of Kiddush. The same applies to all other berachos: one should accustom himself to say them in a loud voice, for the voice arouses intent. This is from the Shlah.
