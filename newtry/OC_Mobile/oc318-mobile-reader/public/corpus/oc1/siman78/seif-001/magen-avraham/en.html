Or wait until they are absorbed in the ground, at the distance that will be explained below in siman 82; so is explicit in the glossed Shulchan Aruch of the Shach's author. Difficulty: in siman 90 he ruled one should not wait until they bring water, lest he delay long enough to finish all of it and need to return to the beginning — all the more so here, waiting until absorption, which is a large measure. One could say: regarding Kriat Shema, some say that even if he delayed long enough to finish all of it he need not return, as Rabbeinu Shimon wrote in siman 65; therefore we are not concerned lest he delay. But in tefillah all agree one must return to the beginning; therefore we are concerned lest he delay. Nevertheless, the reverse is more plausible: in Kriat Shema he should not wait, for if he delays there will be doubt whether he must return to the beginning — unlike tefillah; therefore it seems in Kriat Shema they are not particular whether he returns to the beginning, for it is like one reading Torah. See Terumat HaDeshen siman 82. Rama wrote in responsum siman 98: one who has an illness that constantly drips urine against his will — if so he could never pray; it is permitted to make a garment around his limb, the upper garments should be clean, he may enter the synagogue and pray; when urine drips he pauses and then prays — provided he did not need to drip when he began to pray, even though he knows that during his prayer he will need to drip — see there where he elaborated his reason.
