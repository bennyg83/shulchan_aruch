He was reading — see Mishna Berurah what I wrote regarding tefillah; and this is from the Peri Megadim. He also wrote that perhaps even if through this the time for tefillah will pass, nevertheless he waits; and all the more so according to what we wrote above that perhaps at the time of urination it is from the Torah — one should be stringent.

Distances from them — and regarding tefillah see Beit Yosef, who rules like Rashba: since he is already standing in tefillah he need not distance at all from urine, which is rabbinic; but below in siman 90, seif 27, regarding a child urinating in the synagogue, it implies he rules like the other authorities who require distancing in tefillah as well, 4 amot, or that he should be silent until they bring him water to pour on the urine — and this is not comparable to removing garments, for the Rabbis were lenient in tefillah even though they are tofeach al menat lehatpiach, as we wrote in Mishna Berurah, because that is a great interruption to go and remove them and put on other clothing under them — unlike walking, which is not called such a great interruption. So too silence; Levush explains that here anyway his garments are also soiled with urine and he cannot remove them in the middle of his prayer; therefore they were lenient here too that he should not distance from urine that dripped from him onto the ground. It is appropriate to be stringent, for the second and third approaches brought in Peri Megadim are both not like Levush; and also in Rashba's responsum itself it implies he is lenient even when his garments are not soiled. So too in Meteh Yehudah; and so is proven in Tosafos. At any rate, regarding a child urinating, it seems from Peri Megadim one should not deviate from the Shulchan Aruch's ruling there; and so it seems in responsa siman 90 — see there.

And so is the main ruling etc. — see in Mishna Berurah what I wrote after the fact regarding tefillah; and although from the gloss in siman 90 seif 27 it seems that after the fact one returns to the beginning in tefillah through delay — see Peri Chadash there who disagrees with the author's main ruling; and although one should not rely on him, as we wrote before, nevertheless after the fact regarding returning to the beginning there is doubt: after Rashba and Tosafos who are lenient lechatchilah not to distance in the middle of tefillah — if so he was not then under duress; and perhaps nevertheless one should return for the reason that without that, Ri"f and Rambam hold that in tefillah one returns even without duress, as Magen Avraham wrote similarly in siman 65 seif 2 — see there.
