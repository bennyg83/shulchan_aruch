1) [Seif 1] If he was reciting and urine began flowing down his legs, etc. A person suffering from a stone ailment, due to which urine continually drips involuntarily, and who would otherwise never be able to pray, may wrap a garment or sponge around his organ so that his outer garments remain clean, enter the synagogue, and pray. When the urine drips, he should pause, and afterward continue praying. This applies specifically when no urine was dripping when he began praying, even though he knows that it will begin dripping during his prayer. Responsa Rema, siman 98. Panim Chadashos writes likewise in the name of a responsum of Maharam of Lublin, siman 98. Knesses HaGedolah cites it in Hagahos HaTur, as does Sheyarei Knesses HaGedolah, siman 80, Hagahos HaTur, paragraph 2, in the name of Responsa Rema. Peri Chadash likewise cites that responsum; Magen Avraham, s.k. 2; Eliyah Rabbah, paragraph 3; Olas Tamid, paragraph 1; Shulchan Aruch HaRav, paragraph 4; Shiyurei Taharah, paragraph 5. Contemporary physicians advise fastening an animal bladder around his organ so that the urine will drip into it. Kerem Shlomo.
2) Ruach Chaim wrote that it is a mitzvah to warn elderly unlearned people, most of whose garments are not clean, concerning the recitation of Kerias Shema, prayer, and blessings. Drops of urine descend from the residue left when they urinate, yet they do not take care to wait and clean themselves thoroughly or to change their trousers, because old age makes them prone to this. ‘Who asked this of your hand, to trample My courtyards’ and come pray in the holy house while wearing garments soiled with urine? Some are affected because of intestinal illness. A Torah scholar, however, has his eyes in his head and keeps both his person and garments clean. If any doubt arises, he should immediately replace and change the garment. Undoubtedly, regarding him it was said by the Divine Presence: ‘He smelled the fragrance of his garments, blessed him, and said: See, the fragrance of my son is like the fragrance of a field that Hashem has blessed.’ See Sefer Chasidim, siman 817, and Responsa Maharam Mintz, siman 81; consider this carefully.
