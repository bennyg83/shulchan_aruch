And if urine fell on the ground, he distances from it 4 amot, etc., or waits until it is absorbed in the ground (Ri, chapter 2 of Berachos, fol. 15). Specifically for Kriat Shema he may go and distance himself; but during tefillah, when he is forbidden to move from his place, he pauses when urine is flowing onto his knees. If he is in a place where urine is absorbed in the ground, he waits until it is absorbed; if he is in a place where urine is not absorbed in the ground, since he is standing in the middle of tefillah and cannot distance himself 4 amot and also does not have a revi'it of water he can pour on it, he prays opposite it and is not concerned — for from the Torah it is forbidden only opposite the exposed member alone, and the Rabbis decreed to annul it through a revi'it of water or through distancing 4 amot; therefore, when standing in the middle of tefillah they did not decree, since it is not possible for him to move from his place (Beit Yosef, Rashba, and Rosh).
