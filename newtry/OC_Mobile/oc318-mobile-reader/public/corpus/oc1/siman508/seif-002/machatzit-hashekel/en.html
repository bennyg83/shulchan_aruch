(s.k. 2) Because etc. And even old ones, etc.: for we say on folio 34 regarding that which the mishnah taught, "one may not perform libun on the tiles," what is he doing? Rabbah bar bar Chanah said in the name of Rabbi Yochanan: here we are dealing with new tiles, because one must test them so that they not crack in the fire; and we are concerned lest they crack, and he exerts himself unnecessarily. And some say: because one must harden them. And the Rambam wrote as Beis Yosef copied his wording here in Shulchan Aruch. And the Maggid Mishneh wrote on this, and these are his words: And our master did not mention new ones; therefore it appears that he interprets "and some say" [to mean] that even old ones are forbidden, because one hardens them at every moment that one performs libun on them. And because they said "because one must test them," they said "because one must harden them." Alternatively, this is what it means: because one must perform libun on them so thoroughly until they become hardened, etc.; end quote of the Maggid Mishneh. And the distinction between the first explanation and the second explanation is this: according to the first explanation, they always become hardened; even if one performs libun on them several times, nevertheless with every libun they become more hardened. And according to the second explanation, there is a limit to their hardening, and some become hardened in one time, and some in two times, and some in three times, each according to what it is. Therefore even old ones: perhaps they have not yet been fully hardened, and now will be the completion of the hardening, or an aid to the hardening that comes afterward; and so the Lechem Mishneh explained the Maggid Mishneh's words. Nevertheless one must say: what advantage does the second explanation have over the first? For according to the first explanation, that which was said "because one must harden them" is not precise, and he said so only by juxtaposition with saying "because one must test them," as the Maggid Mishneh wrote; and the intent is: it is well if you say that in one time it becomes hardened, then that which was said "because one must harden them" is fine, for it is not yet finished, and it is not a utensil until after the first libun, when it has been hardened and the utensil is finished; but if you say that at every moment it becomes more hardened, the language "because one must" does not apply, for even after the libun its hardening is not finished so as to be after the libun a complete utensil, [any more] than before the libun. And he should have said "because one hardens them," and he needed to say [the other wording] by juxtaposition with saying "because one must test them," he said so. But according to the second explanation, that there is a limit to its hardening, and sometimes it becomes hardened in one time or two: if so, after the completion of the hardening the name "utensil" applies to it, and that which was said "because one must harden them" until the completion of the hardening is fine, and we do not know if now is the completion of the hardening, etc., as above. However, the Lechem Mishneh's words there are puzzling in my humble opinion, for he wrote that according to the Maggid Mishneh's words the Raavad's critique in chapter 9 of Hilchos Shabbos is settled: for it is stated there in Beitzah: We learned there [in a mishnah], one brings the fire, etc., and one sets the pot, etc.; all of them are liable. And it objects: one who sets the pot, what is he doing? And Resh Lakish said: here we are dealing with a new pot, and they touched upon it because of libun of tiles. And the Rambam in chapter 9 of Hilchos Shabbos copied the mishnah as it stands and did not copy that it deals specifically with a new pot, and the Raavad critiqued him: Resh Lakish establishes it specifically as a new pot. And on this the Lechem Mishneh wrote that the Rambam follows his own view, for he holds that even with an old one there is [an issue of] chissum; and these are his words: the Maggid Mishneh wrote, and it appears that according to our master's view there is no distinction between new ones, etc.; and it appears that according to our master's view, that which the Gemara answered, "here we are dealing with a new pot, and they touched upon it because of libun of tiles," that is according to the version that one must test them; but according to the version that one must harden them, even if they are not new. And now the Raavad's critique of our master in chapter 9 of Hilchos Shabbos is settled, etc.; end quote. And I did not understand: for of necessity, when Resh Lakish established it as a new pot and because of libun of tiles, of necessity he holds the reason is because of chissum; for if [the reason were] because one must test them, there is no liability for a chatas, for it is only a mere concern, and even if it cracks there is nothing but unnecessary exertion, as Rashi wrote, but there is no melacha for which one is liable. And for this reason the Rosh ruled like the one who holds that one must harden them, since Resh Lakish of necessity holds thus, for because one must test them there is no liability for a chatas; see there. And further, according to his words that even with an old pot there is [an issue of] chissum: if so, it is impossible to cook on Yom Tov, for even with an old one there is chissum; and regarding this one cannot say that he holds as Magen Avraham wrote in the name of the Ran in siman 507, s.k. 11, that where the chissum and the use come as one, it is permitted. But the first difficulty is certainly difficult. And of necessity one must distinguish between tiles and a pot: for even though with tiles even old ones have chissum in them, nevertheless with a pot specifically a new one has chissum, and not an old one, as I proved above; and so appears the Rama's view, for here he agrees with the Mechaber, who holds that even old tiles are forbidden, and above at the end of siman 502 regarding a pot he wrote that specifically a new one is forbidden. And therefore the Maggid Mishneh was silent in Hilchos Shabbos about settling the Raavad's critique, not like the Lechem Mishneh, who forgot [that] the Maggid Mishneh relied on what he wrote in Hilchos Yom Tov, that this is not so, as above.
