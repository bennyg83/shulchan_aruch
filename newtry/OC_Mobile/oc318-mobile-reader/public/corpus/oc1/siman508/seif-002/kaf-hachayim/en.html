Because it hardens them — explanation: to harden them; for in the first heating they strengthen and harden; therefore even though for Yom Tov need too, for behold they roast on them — nevertheless, since in this heating it becomes a vessel, it is forbidden — Rashi Beitzah 34a. And it implies this refers to new tiles; and he returns to what is written there, in the gemara — here we deal with new tiles. And so Ran there; and so Rambam there in explanation of the mishnah; and so Rashba in Avodat HaKodesh there. And it implies: old tiles and new stones are permitted, as Rambam wrote in explanation of the mishnah there; and so Ri in chiddushim that one may whiten old tiles to roast on them; and so in Avodat HaKodesh there. But Kol Bo and Or Zarua there wrote: one does not whiten tiles nor new stones, etc. — implying new stones are also forbidden, but old ones are permitted, even tiles. However Rambam in his composition chapter 3 of laws of Yom Tov law 11 wrote anonymously: one does not whiten stones (and Maharam and Beit Yosef's text: tiles) to roast or bake on them because it hardens them, and did not distinguish new from old; and Maharam wrote there: it appears he explains what the gemara says here — we deal with new tiles because they need inspection; and some say because they need hardening — that is the question; and some say even old ones are forbidden because it hardens them at every hour one whitens them, see there and Bach Beit Yosef. And so Beit Yeshash chapter 4 of Beitzah siman 19 — there is concern to forbid even old ones, see there. And Ohr Sameach note 1 wrote: thus is the language of Shulchan Aruch, that anonymously one should not distinguish first time from second time, for always there is concern of hardening, see there. And so Taz s.k. 1 — even old ones forbidden. And so Magen Avraham s.k. 2. Rabbi Zalman note 1 — however Ateret Tzvi note 2 wrote to wonder at the words of the poskim mentioned who wrote to forbid old ones, since Rambam permits explicitly in explanation of the mishnah, and so Kol Bo in his name, etc.; and further Rashba, Ramban, Ran, and Ri also permit, and so from Rashi; and therefore he wrote that Rif, Rambam, and Tur hold old ones are permitted; and that they did not write it in explanation — they relied on what is written 'new oven and stove,' etc.; and in Tur he also relied on pots above, and wrote here the mishnah in its form in the usual manner — and this is clear, end of his words; and so Nahar Shalom, see there. For practical ruling it appears: since Rambam wrote explicitly in explanation of the mishnah to permit old tiles, and so from Rashi, and so is the simple meaning of the gemara, and so explicitly Rashba, Ramban, Ran, Ri, Kol Bo, and Or Zarua, and the language of Rambam, Tur, and Shulchan Aruch — we can explain as Ateret Tzvi and Nahar Shalom wrote, so Rambam's words do not contradict each other; for a time of need one may be lenient with old tiles, but if possible without that one should be stringent. And likewise with new stones one should be stringent, since it is explicit in Kol Bo and Or Zarua and Levush, and so is the text in Rambam before us and in Shulchan Aruch; and one who is cautious also with old stones — praiseworthy.
