1) [Se'if 1] Stones may not be heated until white-hot, etc. This is the reading in our editions of Rambam. Maggid Mishneh and Beis Yosef, however, imply that Rambam's text reads 'tiles.' This is also the reading in the Talmud and all the halachic authorities. Stones, however, may be heated, because hardening does not apply to them. Magen Avraham, subsection 1; Shulchan Gavoah, subsection 2. This is also the text in Rambam's Commentary on the Mishnah. See there. Rashba's Avodas HaKodesh, Beis Moed, gate 2, subsection 6, likewise reads 'tiles.' This is also the text in the commentaries of Rabbeinu Chananel and Meiri, and in Rokeach, siman 299. Shulchan Aruch HaRav, subsection 1, writes likewise. Kol Bo, siman 58, however, mentions both: 'Neither tiles nor new stones may be heated until white-hot, etc.' Orach Chaim, page 88c, uses the same wording as Kol Bo. Levush writes likewise, and Shaarei Shamayim rules accordingly. See also the following subsection.
2) There. Because this hardens them. This means that it makes them hard. Their first firing strengthens and hardens them. Therefore, although this is also needed for Yom Tov, since food is roasted upon them, it is nevertheless forbidden because this firing forms a utensil. Rashi, Beitzah 34a. This implies that the law applies specifically to new tiles and refers back to the Talmud's statement there, 'Here we are dealing with new tiles.' Ran there writes likewise, as do Rambam in his Commentary on the Mishnah and Rashba in Avodas HaKodesh there. This implies that old tiles and new stones are permitted, as Rambam writes in his Commentary on the Mishnah there. Rabbeinu Yerucham, section 2, likewise writes that one may heat old tiles until white-hot to roast upon them. This also appears from Avodas HaKodesh there. Kol Bo and Orach Chaim there, however, write, 'Neither tiles nor new stones may be heated, etc.' This implies that new stones are also forbidden, while old ones are permitted, even if they are tiles. Rambam, however, writes without qualification in chapter 3 of Hilchos Yom Tov, law 11: 'Stones may not be heated until white-hot to roast or bake upon them, because this hardens them.' The text of Maggid Mishneh and Beis Yosef reads 'tiles.' He makes no distinction between new and old. Maggid Mishneh writes there that Rambam appears to explain the Talmudic passage as follows: The first opinion says that the case concerns new tiles because they must be tested, while the alternative opinion says that even old tiles are forbidden because they are hardened each time they are heated. See there. Beis Yosef cites him. Yam Shel Shlomo, chapter 4 of Beitzah, siman 19, likewise writes that one should be concerned and forbid even old tiles. See there. Olas Shabbos, subsection 1, writes that this also appears from the unqualified wording of Shulchan Aruch, which makes no distinction between the first and second firing, because hardening always applies. See there. Taz, subsection 1, likewise forbids even old tiles, as do Magen Avraham, subsection 2, and Shulchan Aruch HaRav, subsection 1. Eliyah Rabbah, subsection 2, however, expresses surprise at these authorities for forbidding old tiles, since Rambam explicitly permits them in his Commentary on the Mishnah, and Kol Bo cites him accordingly. Furthermore, Rashba, Ramban, Ran, and Rabbeinu Yerucham also permit them, and this appears from Rashi's explanation. He therefore writes that Rif, Rambam, and Tur also hold that old tiles are permitted. They did not state this explicitly because they relied upon their discussion of new ovens and stoves, etc.; Tur also relied upon the earlier law of pots. Here they merely copied the Mishnah in its original form, as is their practice. This is clear. These are his words. Nehar Shalom writes likewise. See there. As a practical ruling, since Rambam explicitly permits old tiles in his Commentary on the Mishnah, this appears from Rashi and the plain wording of the Talmud, and Rashba, Ramban, Ran, Rabbeinu Yerucham, Kol Bo, and Orach Chaim explicitly write likewise, while the wording of Rambam, Tur, and Shulchan Aruch can be explained as Eliyah Rabbah and Nehar Shalom explain it so that Rambam's rulings do not contradict one another, one may be lenient with old tiles in a case of need. If it is possible to avoid using them, one should be stringent. One should likewise be stringent regarding new stones, because Kol Bo, Orach Chaim, and Levush explicitly forbid them, and this is the reading in our editions of Rambam and Shulchan Aruch. One who is careful even with old stones will be blessed.
