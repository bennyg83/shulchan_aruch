Because. See Taz; Beitzah 34a; and see Beis Yosef: since the Rif, the Rambam, and the Tur did not distinguish, and the baraisa there deals with a new pot, the same applies to an old one according to the reason that one must harden them; see Lechem Mishneh, chapter 3 of Hilchos Yom Tov, halacha 11. However, in siman 502 an old pot is permitted for cooking on Yom Tov, and one need not [require] a metal vessel, for it is with water; and even so a new one is forbidden by reasoning, or [where] he sets the pot [on the fire] and immediately afterward puts in water, and there is no measure of chissum at all except by means of water, and therefore specifically a new one [is forbidden]; and see Eliyah Rabbah; and according to what I wrote, it settles somewhat. And a new earthenware frying pan for frying in fat and the like on Yom Tov: see Yoreh Deah 121, and in Hilchos Pesach, siman 451; even though one can say a frying pan is significant like an empty [vessel], nevertheless one does not perform such thorough libun in the fire [that] it is considered chissum, and with an old one it is permitted in any case; and likewise a new earthenware frying pan to put in a swept oven for pot-roast: with an old one it is permitted. See Rashi, Beitzah 33a: "one may not take out fire," he added a vav, and "one may not perform libun on tiles," he omitted a vav, for there [in that mishnah] it is on account of nolad and muktzeh; see siman 501; and here, according to the latter version, [the reason is] to harden them, not unnecessary exertion that would be akin to muktzeh; and understand.
