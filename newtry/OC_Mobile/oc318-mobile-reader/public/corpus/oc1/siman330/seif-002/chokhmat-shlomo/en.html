Seif 2, non-Jew may not deliver on Shabbat, etc. NB: See Magen Avraham s.k. 4 what he wrote that Karaites permitted since they observe Shabbat, etc. It appears this is specifically where there is no d'oraisa prohibition as Tosafos brought Magen Avraham below seif 27 — then since only d'rabbanan prohibition, permitted because of hatred since the gemara's reason does not apply because they also observe Shabbat; but where d'oraisa prohibition — hatred permission does not help as Tosafos wrote — then also for Karaites forbidden to desecrate Shabbat since they have no Israelite status and behold they are moridin velo ma'alin; all the more not to desecrate Shabbat for them. So it appears Knesset HaGedolah's intent. But this too requires study — it appears hatred applies only to idolater who are numerous against us and we are delivered into their hands — there hatred applies; but Karaites who are few and we are not delivered into their hands — no hatred concern at all. And behold at first glance it would prove permitted to desecrate Shabbat for Karaites even in d'oraisa melacha from mishnah that stated Jewish woman may not deliver non-Jewess — implies to Kutit permitted as challenged end of chapter 1 regarding may not rent bathhouse to non-Jew — behold to Kutit permitted; so too here one can infer. But this is not one — mishnah deals weekday not Shabbat. And further one can say mishnah teaches novelty that not only for Kutit whom law moridin velo ma'alin certainly may not deliver — also for non-Jew whom law not moridin velo ma'alin also may not deliver because she delivers son to idolatry. Also one can say it stated so for seifa: but non-Jewess may deliver Jewish woman — to inform even non-Jewess not suspected in this manner of bloodshed, all the more Kutit; and though Kutit suspected also of bloodshed as above daf 15b — nevertheless non-Jewess is worse than Kutit and is greater novelty. Therefore it appears not to desecrate Shabbat for Karaites — requires study. I again saw in first law I wrote 'without Shabbat desecration' — my intent was Avnei Nezer's words where so explained; but in rest of my words he did not notice. And behold for one who examines sugya Avodah Zarah 26b there is proof it is not precise that only non-Jewess stated — also for Kutit forbidden; for if we say Kutit permitted — difficult in gemara's challenge why do needs there in reverse according to R' Meir alone; if taught nursing one would think in this R' Yehuda forbids because he rubs eye from outside; but for midwife R' Meir agrees permitted; if taught midwife one would think in this non-Jewess forbidden but Kutit permitted; but in nursing one would think even Kutit forbidden — it teaches even in nursing non-Jewess forbidden but Kutit permitted; from this gemara holds no distinction between Kutit and non-Jewess — requires study. And behold in Tosafos' words themselves that deal when fetus already uprooted — and otherwise would be d'oraisa melacha — requires study. And in my humble opinion it appears there is no d'oraisa liability here, since we rule melacha she'einah tzricha legufah is exempt; if so the gemara Tosafos brought for proof deals when his intent and purpose of act was to detach fetus from animal — therefore liable for detaching; but non-Jew midwife's intent is not to uproot fetus from its growth only to save mother; if possible to leave fetus in womb longer it would be preferable — if so similar to extinguishing lamp for sick to sleep, or digging pit and he needs only its dirt, or extinguishing wooden coals lest they damage — all these are melacha she'einah tzricha legufah and exempt for those who hold melacha she'einah tzricha legufah exempt; so too if intent not to take fetus only save mother — melacha she'einah tzricha legufah, exempt; therefore forbidden because of hatred. With this I stood on gemara's words: Rav Yosef thought to say delivering infant on Shabbat for payment permitted because of hatred, etc.; and why relevant on Shabbat for payment more than free — on weekday Rashi explained well that free she can say for my sustenance I need, meaning if she does not deliver she will do other work and profit; but on Shabbat otherwise she may not do melacha and Shabbat payment forbidden — she cannot say for my sustenance I need; if so why relevant on Shabbat for payment more than free — say also free hatred applies here. At first glance one can say: free — since weekday forbidden, hatred not relevant — therefore they decreed Shabbat because of weekday. Also one can say it is disgrace to Shabbat that they say weekday forbidden and Shabbat permitted — disgrace to Shabbat like Beit HaLevi at beginning of Beitzah — see there. But more it appears in my humble opinion: at first glance one would say whoever does melacha on Shabbat for payment should be exempt from Torah, since no agency for sin — liability only on sender and agent does not intend melacha body only to receive payment — melacha she'einah tzricha legufah exempt. But it appears as sheluda — nevertheless since agent acts per sender and sender needs melacha body — considered melacha machshavah; if so here too: midwife's mind may be to deliver fetus and considered intent on fetus; but mother in danger certainly intent more to save herself than fetus birth — if midwife free then midwife acts per herself — perhaps intent on fetus and here d'oraisa melacha and hatred does not help; even if intent only mother's salvation — still if she acts per herself, whether intent save mother or fetus — no d'oraisa melacha since intent to take payment is melacha she'einah tzricha legufah for her; if we say intent per employer's mind — non-Jew mother's mind certainly to save herself more, and also melacha she'einah tzricha legufah; and in any case melacha she'einah tzricha legufah; therefore only d'rabbanan and rabbinically permitted because of hatred — so one can say in this; if this reasoning not correct in examiner's eyes — this at least appears correct: this is melacha she'einah tzricha legufah and all d'rabbanan, and from d'rabbanan permitted because of hatred. Requires study. And see what I wrote in these laws in my composition Avodat Avodah for tractate Avodah Zarah part 2 section 101 siman 150 responsum to Skalit — see there — requires study.
