(א) The newborn who was born, etc. — Magen Avraham wrote that in all of them there is distress; and since all these are not melacha they did not decree on them — and this is from Tosafos at the end of chapter Mefanin and in She'eilotot of Rav Achai parashat Shemos it implies that preventing cutting the cord can be danger to the infant — end of his words there: what about cutting the cord on Shabbat, etc., for I ask regarding one fetus — do we say perhaps he can be careful [meaning tying the cord alone] and danger will not come, or perhaps sometimes unawares danger comes, or perhaps the halacha is like R' Yosi whose reason is plausible that sometimes it tears and it is doubt of life-threatening danger and doubt is resolved leniently; and he concludes from R' Nachman's statement that halacha is like R' Yosi; and so Ran's chiddushim there, in the mishnah — if they do not cut it there is danger — see there; and so Meiri in the mishnah there; and in Rambam chapter 2 of Hilchot Shabbat it is explained more than this that it is a general rule in danger for all these matters — if they prevent the newborn, after he considered in halacha 11 the matters done for the newborn he concluded because it is danger to him if they do not do all these for him; and accordingly it is obvious that even in d'oraisa melacha they desecrate when impossible another way; and the intent of the gemara in these melachot is not because they are d'rabbanan — for in truth they do them on the day he is born even where d'oraisa prohibition is done through them, such as heating hot water to wash or to salt the signs needed for salting, or regarding lefofei to cut cloth for swaddling, or to bring a knife through public domain to cut the cord [except burying placenta — forbidden to bury in earth because it is only a segulah, as Meiri wrote and Rambam omitted in his composition]; and Rambam's intent there in what he wrote in hot water that was heated on Shabbat — even if heated through Israelite [see chapter 2 of Hilchot Milah in She'eilot of Lunil scholars and his responsum there] [gloss: and know that in Rambam's words these that imply lefofei for the newborn there is concern of danger if they prevent — this settles very well R' Nachman's statement that from here they lefofei for the newborn who was born; but behold it says on daf 66 lefofei infants on Shabbat is fine — behold on every Shabbat permitted [and see below what we wrote on this in the name of other early authorities]; and there is no shvus matter at all — what is the novelty here; but according to Rambam it is fine, for there the gemara informs us that because there will be distress if they prevent, therefore the Rabbis did not decree; and so Rambam infers in chapter 21 of Hilchot Shabbat halacha 41 — see there; and here the gemara informs us that on the day he is born lefofei is among other essential needs of the newborn; and the difference is that it is permitted to transgress d'oraisa melacha for this]. And in any case it appears obvious one should be lenient l'chatchila to heat hot water through non-Jew to wash the infant; and as I wrote in Mishna Berurah; and afterward I found in Sefer Nazer Yisrael in the name of responsum of Shevut Yaakov he also wrote so.

(ב) Or doubt seven-month, etc. — This law the Mechaber copied from Tur; and as it appears its source is as Beit Yosef wrote — from that which Shabbos 135 baraita: seven-month they desecrate for him, etc.; eight-month they do not desecrate; doubt seven-month or eight-month they do not desecrate; eight-month — behold he is like a stone, etc.; and Tur explains the baraita's intent is regarding actual Shabbat desecration to heal and sustain him — for if he explained the baraita deals regarding circumcision on Shabbat he would not infer from this that they do not desecrate, for from doubt we do not circumcise on Shabbat day; but for Shabbat desecration for healing we desecrate from doubt as we rule in fallen wall — doubt alive doubt dead we clear away; and apparently in the gemara there it is proven the baraita refers to circumcision, and so Rashi there; and one can say Tur also agrees, only he holds the baraita includes both from that which it says there general desecration language; and one cannot ask on daf 36 that challenges from this baraita to circumcise in any case, and needed to establish it regarding facilitators of circumcision and according to R' Eliezer more — it would have been better to establish it regarding actual Shabbat desecration alone per the baraita's plain language; or that the gemara holds the baraita is taught regarding circumcision; and one can say if he had answered so he would challenge from the first baraita that says there his foreskin, etc., certainly pushes aside Shabbat and not doubt pushes aside Shabbat; but from Rambam who did not copy this law only in Hilchot Milah it implies he holds this baraita is taught only regarding circumcision. And behold in Behag laws of milah regarding doubt eight-month he wrote explicitly: and regarding permitting Shabbat we desecrate from doubt, for we say all doubt of life-threatening danger is resolved leniently; and also Ittur in laws of milah brings his words for halachic ruling; and as it appears there Rav Hai Gaon also wrote so; and Ittur did not argue there only added that where nails were completed even if one separated from it immediately — also this is within doubt, for it remained; and so Meiri there on daf 136 also wrote as all Behag's words; and he wrote: however regarding desecrating Shabbat all agree they desecrate — see there; and so in Sefer HaEshkol when he copied the baraita doubt seven-month they do not desecrate he wrote on this — end of his words: explanation — to circumcise him; meaning they did not intend to explain it refers to all desecration; but one can reject that perhaps they deal where hair and nails were completed [and so it appears somewhat in Ittur there — see there]; but where they were not completed it is more reasonable to say he is eight-month. And behold from the sugya in Erchin 'she sat on the birth-stool and died' brought in Sefer HaHashlamah — we are lenient from doubt to bring knife through public domain — Magen Avraham answered that there it deals when we know her months were completed; and further Magen Avraham brings this depends on two answers brought in Torat HaAdam to Ramban regarding her fetus that smelled — for answer one, Behag's words are clear that even for newborn before forty days it is mere water — nevertheless they desecrate; if so all the more for one born to the world; and he is doubt that they desecrate; but according to what Ramban explained there the reason for one sitting on birth-stool — she is considered like birth and only the door is locked before him — if so at least we say specifically when her months were completed — see there [and behold according to Magen Avraham's words this is automatically rejected — perhaps he goes according to Behag there that he holds they desecrate even for fetuses, and all the more there]; and in my humble opinion this does not depend on this at all — for even according to this answer Ramban did not say there only that for fetuses they do not desecrate because Israel is not commanded regarding fetuses; and one sitting on birth-stool is not called fetuses; and like newborn it is water — not so for newborn perhaps Ramban also agrees that although they are not executed on him from doubt because of 'and the congregation shall judge,' etc., nevertheless they desecrate from doubt because doubt of life-threatening danger is resolved leniently; and conversely one can also reject somewhat — for there if they feed his mother on Yom Kippur presumptively she will eventually give birth a viable son who will observe many Shabbats, therefore the Torah permitted; as Ran wrote — not so here who was already born and we see hair and nails were not completed — it is reasonable he is eight-month and why desecrate Shabbat for him. And know that according to Magen Avraham above who said this depends on the two answers — from this Ritva in chiddushim of Niddah 4:44 as Behag's view — even for fetus they desecrate — from this they desecrate also for doubt eight-month.
