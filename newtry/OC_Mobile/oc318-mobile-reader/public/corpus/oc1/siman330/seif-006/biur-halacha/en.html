All thirty — The Mechaber's source is Rambam there — see there; and in truth the Maggid himself wrote initially as simple matter that the law of bonfire is like all needs of the living creature; and on all her needs we derive three times she has them; if so what is bonfire's superiority over all other needs of the living creature — only afterward he brought from Raavad's words that in his view until thirty days she is endangered for a bonfire — see there; and in truth all this is only according to Raavad's explanation of Rambam's words — for that which he did not permit bonfire for a sick person deals with illness without danger; and correspondingly living creature permitted also after twenty-seven — for her law is also like sick without danger; and nevertheless they may also make bonfire for her; and therefore because for bonfire she is always endangered. But in truth it is very difficult to explain the gemara's words — for that which it says it thought for a living creature yes, for a sick person no — is not a sick person without danger; if so for most poskim who hold that for the gemara's conclusion it is likewise for a sick person — it would be permitted to make bonfire even for sick without danger, and this is unheard of for any posek to be lenient [and one can say the gemara's intent is specifically through a non-Jew only — this is not so, for it brings Avodah Zarah proof from bloodletting and he became chilled, and there certainly even through Israelite as all poskim imply; and sick is preferable to that in their view, as Rashi explained]. Also Rambam's language is not precise accordingly — why does he infer and say but they do not make for a sick person to warm even for healing — forbidden, for that is illness without danger — unless we strain that even through non-Jew they do not make, and this is not in the plain sense of his language — see there; and also in Shiltei HaGibborim chapter Mefanin it is clear he holds it deals with sick with danger; and so in Rambam's chiddushim brought in Shoshanim LeDavid that Rambam's view that they do not make bonfire for sick deals even with sick with danger — and he counts the houses — see there; and therefore one need not strain that it deals with living creature within forty; and properly it deals within twenty-seven or within three; and so it also appears somewhat in Yerushalmi from Shmuel's statement that they make bonfire for her — it refers to the time they desecrate Shabbat for her; for in Yerushalmi chapter Mefanin on the mishnah 'they desecrate Shabbat for her' Shmuel said they make bonfire for her even in Tammuz season — it implies it refers to the time before twenty-seven; and also according to what Rabeinu Chananel understood in chapter Hader regarding infant whose hot water was spilled — there they say let him warm through non-Jew by his mother; and they ask his mother eats dates; and answer she is a prophetess that it mentions her; and R' Chananel explained there cold seized her, etc. — see there; and accordingly she certainly needs hot water to wash and to drink to warm her body; and nevertheless they permitted only through non-Jew because it deals on the eighth day; and one should not distinguish between bonfire and bathing — for Raavad himself does not distinguish — see there in his words [and also in the gemara that Shmuel said living creature until forty — the gemara did not find support except for immersion, and did not say for bonfire; and one can reject that otherwise Shmuel's two statements are unnecessary]. But since the Maggid wrote he nullifies his view before Raavad's view — therefore I searched further and found proof for our words from early to early — Rav Achai Gaon, who certainly if he saw Raavad would perhaps also nullify his view before him — end of his words in parashat Shemos: a woman who gave birth on Shabbat, even if her three days fell on Shabbat, even though she said I am healthy and do not need bonfire and hot water, etc. — that baraita is not a baraita, and we make bonfire and hot water for her, etc., and all needs done for a living creature, etc.; behold also bonfire is included in three and seven and forty; and Ramban brings it in Torat HaAdam — see there; and so Rabeinu Yerucham at the beginning of his book — end of his words: they make bonfire for a living creature on Shabbat on those days we said they desecrate through Israelites; and on those we said they do not desecrate they do through non-Jew — end of his words; and since we found no implication in any posek alone from Raavad that the law of bonfire is stricter than all needs of the living creature — and all copied for the introduction of these laws from the statement of Nehardeans who said living creature three, seven, and forty — it is very difficult to be lenient to make bonfire through Israelite until forty; and it requires study for practical law.
