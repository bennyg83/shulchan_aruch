[6] All three days, etc. We do not calculate these as full twenty-four-hour periods, as stated in siman 617 by the Magen Avraham. Issur VeHeter HaAruch, at the beginning of rule 59, does not appear to hold this way. This will be explained below.
[7] [Levush] If it appears, etc. This implies that ordinarily one does not desecrate Shabbos. The Shulchan Aruch does not appear to say this. Perhaps the Levush wrote this only to exclude a case where her companions also say that she does not need it, in which case one does not desecrate Shabbos. But when her companions do not know, one does desecrate Shabbos, unlike Nachalat Tzvi. Likewise, if her companions say she does not need it while she remains silent, Shabbos is desecrated. So rules the Bach.
[8] [Levush] Ordinarily we desecrate Shabbos, etc. This is so even when her companions do not know. But if they say that she does not need it, one does not desecrate Shabbos because she is silent. If she says that she does not need it and a physician says she does, one does not desecrate Shabbos, according to the Bach. The Shiltei Giborim in Perek Mefanin is uncertain even in that case, so one should be lenient regarding danger to life. Responsum 66 of the Radbaz likewise implies that one desecrates Shabbos. It appears to me that even if she is eating ordinary human food we still say that at that moment she may not fully sense her condition. Although the Rashba, cited by the Beit Yosef, writes regarding the law within three days that perhaps that particular incident simply occurred that way, the Bach brings a complete proof from Eruvin 68, where the Gemara suggests that she may be confused and not know what she is eating. That case was after three days, as the Taz writes. In truth, the Taz was imprecise, because Rashi there explains that it could be even after seven days, and warming was done through non-Jews. The Taz's statement that if her mind is clear, eating cold food is effective, does not appear from the Gemara, the Bach, or the Ran in the name of the Ramban in Perek Mefanin. Rather, regarding all women we permit based on this concern. It is obvious that if the woman after childbirth is actually ill, even after seven days, she is no worse than any other patient.
