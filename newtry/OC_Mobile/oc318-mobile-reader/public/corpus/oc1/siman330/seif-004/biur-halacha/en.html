All three days — And from when do we count three days — Lechem Mishneh chapter 2 wrote from the gemara it appears that from womb opening we count those three days; but from Rambam's language in halacha 13 it appears from birth we count for three days; and he wrote that so it appears — otherwise if two days of dripping blood occur how is the law — see there in Lechem Mishneh where he expanded; but in Rambam's explanation of mishnayot it is clear from womb opening we count — end of his words: they do not begin desecrating Shabbat until she sits on the birth-stool and blood begins to drip, and from that hour for completion of three days they desecrate, etc.; and in my humble opinion Rambam's words are so: a living creature from when blood begins to drip until she gives birth they desecrate; and likewise after birth they desecrate; and all these desecrations continue until three days; and his words in the halacha accord with his words in the explanation. And accordingly if sitting on the birth-stool lasted one day we desecrate for her only two days after birth; and nevertheless if it continued close to three days and she did not give birth this is not in that category, for truly the intent of the gemara is on birth as is usual for most women; but when birth is very difficult she becomes sick in other limbs of her body — and this is known that all these measures stated in the gemara were said only when she has no other illness besides being in childbirth, as the early authorities wrote — otherwise these measures do not apply at all; and for practical law it requires study in all this.
