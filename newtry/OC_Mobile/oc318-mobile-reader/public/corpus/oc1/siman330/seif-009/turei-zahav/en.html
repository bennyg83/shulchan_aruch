They straighten the limbs of the newborn, etc. In the Tur it is written asovei yanuka — meaning they straighten, etc. The explanation of his words: at the end of chapter Chavis they taught they do not set the spine of a minor, and they establish it regarding vertebrae of the spine; and Rashi explained vertebrae of the spine that dislocated one from another — this is what the Tur wrote that asovei yanuka in this manner that we mentioned is forbidden after the day of birth; but on the day of birth everything is permitted, for they relied on the verse in Yechezkel. And that which he wrote afterward but to wrap him in his garments, etc. — this is the dispute of R' Nachman and R' Shimon in chapter Kol HaKelim regarding asovei yanuka, and this is lefofei yanuka at the end of chapter Chavis; and although there all agree it is permitted, that is on the day of birth; but afterward R' Nachman and R' Shimon disagree, and the halacha is like R' Shimon that it is permitted even afterward — and this is the view of the Rosh, not as Tosafos wrote in chapter 25 that asovei yanuka is not lefofei yanuka; if so, what does the Shulchan Aruch write here 'they straighten the limbs of the newborn' — that is specifically on the day of birth, as stated.
