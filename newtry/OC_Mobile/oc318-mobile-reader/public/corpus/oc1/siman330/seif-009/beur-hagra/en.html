Seif 9, they straighten. According to Rashi's explanation Shabbos 123a and Rav Sheshet; and so Tosafos there s.v. asovei — that this is not lefofei, for R' Nachman himself permitted there Shabbos 129b; and so Rashi there s.v. melapefin; and therefore it is also not vertebrae of the spine that dislocated one from them — for they taught they only set as written here in Shulchan Aruch, and Rashi and Tosafos there and there did not distinguish between day of birth or afterward; and there 147b s.v. bechumrei Rashi wrote it deals after the day of birth — for if on the day of birth we say there 129 'all that is stated,' etc.; and it must be that Rashi retracted from what he wrote at the end of Mefanin that this was not asovei — for R' Nachman said that on the day of birth even setting is permitted; and so the Rosh at the beginning of chapter Kol HaKelim — asovei is lefofei, and after time, etc.; and at the end of Chavis he also wrote as Rashi there that on the day of birth everything is permitted; if so lefofei is always permitted like Rav Sheshet; and 'they straighten' is specifically setting on the day of birth. Taz, Bach, and Magen Avraham. And Shulchan Aruch chapter as Tosafos and as Rashi explained in chapter 25 and chapter Mefanin — and 'they straighten' is always permitted, for asovei is not lefofei; and this is what is written here 'they straighten,' etc., and in seif 10 'permitted,' etc., and there is no distinction between them; and so it appears in the gemara, for it says asovei and does not contradict lefofei as in other places; and that which they do not set — he did not write at all because he was uncertain — for according to Tosafos there is no distinction between day of birth or afterward, for always forbidden; and for the Rosh on the day of birth permitted; and as Rashi wrote at the end of Chavis; and so Ran; and from Rambam's words who did not distinguish — from this always forbidden like Tosafos; nevertheless even for the Rosh lefofei is permitted, even to straighten limbs — and this is asovei that is permitted forever. Magen Avraham s.k. 19.
