(א) And they kindle, etc. — See what Mishna Berurah wrote in the name of Tosafos Yeshanim in Yoma; and accordingly that which we say in the gemara 'if she needed a lamp' — the intent of the gemara is not if the midwife says or she says only that night arrived and certainly she needs a lamp to settle her mind; and so Bach at the beginning of the siman — see there; rather accordingly it is not similar to one who needs a lamp to one who needs oil — for there it certainly implies when the skilled midwife says oil is needed; and it is strained. But from Ran at the end of Yoma s.v. ve'ein osin and these matters were also brought in Torat HaAdam to Ramban — it appears they explain per the plain sense, that the midwife says she needs a lamp; and one can reject their difficulty there from the end 'if she needed oil.' And behold from Rambam in chapter 2 of Hilchot Shabbat halacha 11 it appears he explains the baraita another way so everything is resolved — namely in an ordinary sick person with danger or a woman in childbirth from the time the womb opens they certainly kindle the lamp and we need no detail; only the baraita deals before opening the womb when she cries in her pains — therefore we suspend on 'if she needed a lamp,' meaning she says she needs; and therefore at the beginning of the chapter in halacha 2 he summarized 'and they kindle the lamp' because presumptively she needs a lamp when one is not sleeping; whereas here he wrote when she needs a lamp at the hour she cries in her pains; and in halacha 13 he begins there to explain the law for a living creature from when blood begins to drip.

(ב) Lamp — And here an expert is not required as for a sick person on Yom Kippur, for here the woman in childbirth can become endangered more through fear that perhaps they are not doing properly what she needs than a sick person from hunger [Tosafos].
