1) [Seif 1] Shabbos is desecrated for her, etc. Therefore, when a woman reaches her ninth month, she should prepare everything before Shabbos so that Shabbos will not need to be desecrated. Sefer Chassidim, siman 855; Magen Avraham, paragraph 1. They should take care to light a lamp for her before Shabbos with enough oil to last until morning. Ben Ish Chai, Parashas Tetzaveh, paragraph 14.
2) There: Shabbos is desecrated for her, etc. Know that a woman who miscarries after forty days has the same status as a woman giving birth regarding desecrating Shabbos and Yom Kippur. Responsa Mayim Chaim, branch 9; Eshel Avraham, paragraph 1.
3) There: A midwife is summoned for her, etc. This applies even when she is three parsaos away. If the patient cannot walk, she may be carried to the midwife, because possible danger to life overrides Shabbos. Mishbetzos Zahav, paragraph 2. A chachamah is the woman who receives the child at birth, called a midwife in Hebrew. Rambam in his Commentary to the Mishnah.
4) There: A midwife is summoned for her, etc. Even if another midwife is present but this one is more skilled, it is nevertheless permitted. Tiferes Yisrael; Pis'chei Olam, paragraph 1. A Jewish woman who delivers Jewish children on Shabbos may collect payment through the courts. Responsa Mahari Bruna, siman 114. If twins were born and she demands two fees, when both were born within the period required for a brief utterance she receives only an additional payment. If they were two separate births, even on the same day, she receives two fees. If each was born within the period required for a brief utterance and the birth canal remained open as initially, she receives half the fee for the second birth. There, siman 115. See above, siman 306, paragraph 36.
5) There: A lamp is lit for her. This applies even if no practical use of its light is required, but only to give her peace of mind. The Baraisa's statement, 'if she needed a lamp,' does not mean that it is not lit when she does not need its light. Rather, it excludes daytime, when no lamp is needed, or a nighttime place where a lamp is already present. Whenever she lies in a dark house, or at night when there is no light from either a lamp or the moon, a lamp is lit in every case. Beis Yehudah, siman 59; see there. One who lights a lamp for a woman giving birth need not fast, as stated above in siman 329, paragraph 25.
6) There: Even if she is blind. Even if the women attending her do not need a lamp and could provide all necessary care without one, a lamp is nevertheless lit for her. Although she cannot see, her mind is more settled when she knows that a lamp is present, because she says that if she needs something, her companion will see and provide it. Levush. Nevertheless, when a non-Jew is available, it is preferable to have the non-Jew act, because a woman giving birth is treated more leniently than another ill person in this regard, although in several respects her law is more stringent. Eshel Avraham, paragraph 2. See below, paragraph 8.
7) Tosafos write that an expert is not required here as one is required for an ill person on Yom Kippur. A woman giving birth is more likely to become endangered by fear that those attending her may not properly provide what she needs than an ill person is to become endangered by hunger. Taz, paragraph 1.
8) There: The manner is changed. Even according to the view in siman 328, seif 12, that no change is required, this case differs. The pains of childbirth are natural, and not even one woman in a thousand dies from giving birth. The Sages therefore were stringent and required an unusual method. Maggid Mishneh, chapter 2, halachah 11; Magen Avraham, paragraph 3.
9) If she needs oil, another woman should not place oil in her hair, come to the woman giving birth, and squeeze it out. Rather, she should bring the oil in a utensil suspended in her hair. It is preferable to carry unusually without adding another prohibition than to add the prohibition of squeezing. Beis Yosef; Magen Avraham there. See above, siman 320, paragraph 110, regarding squeezing hair; see there.
10) There: The manner is changed. This applies only when the unusual method will not delay the patient's need at all. Maggid Mishneh there. This is also stated above in siman 328, seif 12, in the gloss.
11) There: Suspended in her hair, etc. Since this is not the ordinary manner of carrying, no Torah prohibition applies. Olas Shabbos, paragraph 2.
