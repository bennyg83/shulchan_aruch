After zimun. It is implied in the Tur that specifically for Hallel it is a mitzvah to return, but for Birkas HaMazon its law is like the other days of the year; see siman 137. And there are those who want to join also for Birkas HaMazon, and if so he must not eat the afikoman in his house, but rather in the place where he [the other] recites Birkas HaMazon, as is written in siman 184: for if he eats the afikoman separately, it is like one who eats two Pesachim. And so wrote the Kol Bo, and so it appears in Abudraham, for zimun is like a mitzvah presently at hand and it is permitted to uproot [one's place], as is written in siman 178 at the end of se'if 2 in the gloss. And it appears to me that even according to those who forbid eating two meals at night, as is written in siman 484, that is specifically when he recited Birkas HaMazon in between; but when he did not recite Birkas HaMazon in between, even though he went to another place, it is like one meal (and so it appears in the Tur). However, according to what the Beis Yosef wrote in siman 178, it is forbidden to leave in the middle of his meal unless he first recited Birkas HaMazon; see there. And we practice as the Rama wrote. And the Tur wrote that even for Hallel it is enough that his wife and children respond after him. And so it appears in Tosafos, Sukkah folio 38, who wrote that women are obligated in this Hallel. And the Maharil wrote that a minor should not say Hodu, but Ana he may say, for they respond after him what he says. And Mahariv wrote: even though we say in the Gemara that a curse shall be upon him who demeans his Maker by appointing such agents, that is specifically at the beginning of the chapter. And in the Agudah on Sotah, siman 21, he wrote that if a minor says Yomar na etc., one responds after him what he says. And at the end of Pesachim it is written that if there are only two there, both of them should say Hodu. And the Agur wrote that it is a mitzvah [to have] three adults on both nights, end quote. And certainly l'chatchilah one should perform the mitzvah in its choicest form, but there is not so much insistence, for women also join for this, as written above.
