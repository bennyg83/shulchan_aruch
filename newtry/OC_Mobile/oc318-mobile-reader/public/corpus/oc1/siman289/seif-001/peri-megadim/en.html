If it is beer. See Taz. His difficulty is based on an unnecessary blessing. Regarding precedence, however, even HaMotzi precedes Borei Peri HaGefen, as explained in siman 211, seif 5. Nevertheless, one can say that for Kiddush wine has special importance and therefore comes first, whereas with respect to an unnecessary blessing, etc. He answers that the blessing is unavoidable, because bread exempts all beverages. One can say that their practice was to drink wine before the meal to stimulate the appetite. His question, however, concerns only beer, etc. What he wrote, that one should intend not to exempt the bread, is difficult. In siman 206, seif 2, the Mechaber rules that only where one specifically intended does one item exempt another, whereas without intent it does not. All the more so, beer should not exempt bread, for even with items of equal status intent is required. Moreover, the flow of his language implies that the reason one should intend not to exempt the bread is so that it will be evident that the Shehakol is being recited for Kiddush. If he intended the Shehakol to exempt the bread, it would no longer be evident that he recited Shehakol because of Kiddush. But even without this consideration, l'chatchila each item requires its appropriate blessing. See Magen Avraham, siman 206, seif katan 2, and the Gemara, where the alternative blessing is effective only b'dieved.
Know that the daytime Kiddush is d'rabbanan, yet women are obligated in it. As Magen Avraham wrote in siman 291, seif katan 11, regarding all matters of Shabbos, men and women are equal even in rabbinic matters. Whoever is included in rabbinic observance of Shabbos, including muktzeh and shevus prohibitions, is likewise included in remembering Shabbos through the daytime Kiddush. See siman 597, Magen Avraham, seif katan 3. One who fasts on Shabbos does not recite the rabbinic Kiddusha Rabbah in the morning, but at night, when Kiddush is by Torah law, one can say that he must recite Kiddush. We wrote about this in Peri Chadash. Regarding whether one who does not himself drink may recite Borei Peri HaGefen in the morning to discharge others, see siman 273, Taz, seif katan 3, and siman 167, seif 7, Magen Avraham there. Beit Yosef there cites two opinions concerning Borei Peri HaGefen of the morning Kiddush; see there. At first glance, since daytime Kiddush was instituted because of oneg Shabbos, perhaps it resembles the rule in siman 205 that one cannot discharge others, or perhaps there is not such a significant difference. See Magen Avraham and Taz there in siman 167. This still requires further study. The Rama in siman 273, seif 4, rules that one may discharge others in the morning.
What the Mechaber wrote concerning a prepared bed applies to one whose practice is to sit upon a prepared bed. According to our practice, if the bed is in the room where one eats, he should arrange it in the morning. See Magen Avraham, siman 302, seif katan 6, and see the Prisha and Shiyarei Knesset HaGedolah regarding this.
If in the morning he has no wine and has only bread, so that he may not eat other foods before reciting HaMotzi, as Magen Avraham wrote in seif katan 4, one can say that he may recite HaMotzi to discharge others even though he does not eat with them. See siman 273, Taz, seif katan 3, and Magen Avraham, siman 167, seif katan 1. According to the Taz there, seif katan 17, however, he cannot discharge them because there is no distinguishing feature.
