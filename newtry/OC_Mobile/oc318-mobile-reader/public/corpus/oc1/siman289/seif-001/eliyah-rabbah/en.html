[1] And a bed prepared. Meaning because their practice was to sit upon the bed (Perishah). Be'er Sheva, p. 119, writes that this refers to a bed prepared before Shabbos or one in which he slept on Shabbos. Otherwise it is prohibited to make it, and he should merely spread a sheet over the bed without moving the cushions. Magen Avraham in siman 302 writes that if the bed stands in the room where he lives and leaving it unmade is degrading, he may make it because this is a Shabbos need. It would seem, however, that since spreading a sheet is possible, the degradation is not so great. Rokeach siman 55 states: "They prepare ten couches, so that if he needs to recline he may recline on one of them." Perhaps the same applies when it is his practice to sleep on Shabbos.
[2] And he should recite a blessing, etc. Kol Bo, siman 39, writes that the early authorities had the custom after Kiddush to say, "Blessed is He Who sanctifies Shabbos," without Hashem's Name or kingship. Some have the custom before Kiddush to begin with "The Children of Israel shall observe Shabbos," and after Kiddush to begin aloud Tehillim 23, "Hashem is my shepherd," followed by Tehillim 24, "The earth and its fullness belong to Hashem," and Tehillim 104, "Bless Hashem, my soul." Some begin from "He causes grass to grow" and then break bread. It appears that one who wishes to follow the custom of reciting these chapters should do so after HaMotzi, because we wash our hands before Kiddush and their recitation would constitute an interruption. Rokeach, siman 54, writes: "After they have eaten their fill, some sing zemirot and praise to the Holy One, blessed is He. Therefore, it is proper to study some Torah before eating." One should make the daytime meals somewhat more substantial than the nighttime meal. See siman 271, seif 3.
