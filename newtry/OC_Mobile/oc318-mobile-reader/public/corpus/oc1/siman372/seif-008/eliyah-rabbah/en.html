For then its weight, etc. Moreover, heavy pieces of wood placed adjacent to the wall reduce its height because their weight fixes them in place, even if they belong to another person, are resting on the ground, and were not expressly nullified there, since they are not normally moved, as stated in Avodat HaKodesh. See siman 370, seif katan 2. If a ten tefachim high partition of reeds stands between two courtyards and ladders lean against it, even if the ladders themselves are strong, since they rest on the reeds and removing them would cause the partition to fall, they do not reduce the wall. If ladders face one another and rest against each other, or rest on stone posts rather than the partition, they do reduce it, because one's foot can ascend by them, ibid.
Seven and a small amount high over a length of four, etc. Meaning, along the length of the wall. See seif 9.
Whether for making an eruv, etc. This is when another ladder is also placed on the other side.
A bench, etc. The text of the Shulchan Aruch reads 'basin,' and so it appears in Eruvin and in the Rashba and Rabbeinu Yerucham, meaning a receptacle.
