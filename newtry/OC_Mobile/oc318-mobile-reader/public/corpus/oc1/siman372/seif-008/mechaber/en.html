A wall between two courtyards ten high for both, and one placed against the wall a ladder four wide and likewise opposite it in the second courtyard — even if the ladders are not opposite each other — if there are not three tefachim between them they are considered like an opening. If they were separated from opposite each other three tefachim — if the wall is' four wide they are still considered like an opening; and if it is not four wide they are not considered like an opening. In what ladder did they speak — when it has four rungs; but less than this — no, unless its sides and rungs are heavy so its weight fixes it. If the wall is only ten high and one erects a ladder seven and a bit high over a width of four adjacent to' the wall — it permits both to use upon it and to join together, since three do not remain to the top of the wall. If one removed a section from the top of the wall to reduce it from ten — if there is in it a width of four along the wall it helps both to make it like an opening to join together and regarding that one may use the entire wall; and if there is not four it is not considered like an opening to join together, and likewise it does not help to use the entire wall — one uses only opposite the place that was reduced:
