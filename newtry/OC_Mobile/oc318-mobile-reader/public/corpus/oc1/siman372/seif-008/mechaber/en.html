If a wall between two courtyards is ten high for both, and a ladder four wide was placed against it, and similarly in the second courtyard—even if the ladders are not directly opposite—if less than three handbreadths apart they are considered' like a doorway. If three apart: if the wall is four wide, still considered a doorway; if not, not. This applies to a ladder with four rungs; fewer do not suffice unless its sides and rungs are heavy so its weight fixes it in place. If' the wall is only ten high and one erects a ladder seven and a bit high, four wide, adjacent to the wall, it permits use of the wall and joint eruv, since less than three remain to the top. If one removed a section from the top to reduce it below ten: if four wide, effective for doorway and use; if not, ineffective except opposite that place.
