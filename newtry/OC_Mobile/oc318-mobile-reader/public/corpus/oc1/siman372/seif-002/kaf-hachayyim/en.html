14) [Se'if 2] And it is forbidden to carry from it, etc. Similarly, within it one may carry only within four amos, as stated above in siman 346, se'if 3. See there.
15) There. Into another karpeif, etc. Beis Yosef writes in the name of Rashi that a karpeif larger than beis sasayim that was not enclosed for residential use is called a single domain relative to another such karpeif or relative to a courtyard. Accordingly, one may carry two amos in one and two amos in the other, transferring from one to the other. See there. Perishah, subsection 4, however, writes that the Tur's wording implies that nothing at all may be carried from a karpeif larger than beis sasayim into a courtyard, for this resembles carrying from a karmelis into a private domain. See there. Ma'amar Mordechai, subsection 3, writes likewise, and we wrote the same above in subsection 4 in the name of Mishbetzos Zahav. See there.
16) There. Into another karpeif, etc. This means another karpeif that is likewise larger than beis sasayim. If it is smaller, it is an actual private domain, and it is forbidden to carry from it into a karpeif larger than beis sasayim, for that constitutes carrying from a private domain into a karmelis. Tosefes Shabbos, subsection 5.
17) There. Only two amos, etc. This is because carrying from one karmelis to another is permitted, as stated above in siman 346, se'if 3. All this applies to utensils that began Shabbos within those areas. If utensils began Shabbos in a house and were brought into a karpeif by a non-Jew or in a similar manner, it is forbidden to take them into another karpeif. See above, subsection 3, and examine the matter carefully.
