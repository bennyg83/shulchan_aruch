(117) A ditch between two courtyards. This means that no partition separated them other than the ditch itself.
(118) Ten handbreadths deep, etc. Just as a partition ten handbreadths high separates domains, a ditch of this depth also divides the domains. The ditch must extend throughout the entire length of the courtyards, from one end to the other. If a section of four handbreadths is missing, that place is considered an entrance, as explained below in seif 17.
(119) And four handbreadths wide. If it is narrower, one can easily step from one edge to the other and it is not considered a separation. The entire area of the ditch must be four handbreadths wide, as is evident below in seif 17.
(120) Straw and stubble. This refers to material fit for animal food. If it is spoiled and unfit for animal food, its law is like earth, and it is considered nullified even without an explicit declaration.
(121) So long as he has not nullified it. He must explicitly declare that he will not remove it from there. Some say that even if he did not say so verbally, but resolved in his heart to leave it there, it is automatically nullified.
(122) Filled with earth, etc. This means throughout the entire length of the ditch, or at least for more than ten amos. Otherwise, the filled area is considered an entrance, and if they wish they may make separate eruvin. The requirement that it be filled with earth is not literal. The same law applies if the fill reduces the depth to less than ten handbreadths.
(123) Even without specification. Earth and stones placed in a ditch are ordinarily presumed to have been left there permanently.
(124) See above, siman 358, seif 2. The Rama there cites two opinions regarding one who nullified the material for one Shabbos but intends to remove it afterward. Some say that because he does not intend it to remain there permanently, it is not considered nullified and is worse than an unspecified placement, so the residents cannot make one joint eruv. As a matter of practical halachah, since this is a matter of rabbinic law, one may rely upon the lenient view. They may make one eruv together, or separate eruvin.
(125) To make it into a storehouse there. This means for a period of time, with no intention of taking from it. Magen Avraham questions this ruling, and Beur HaGra agrees with him. Only untithed produce, which may not be moved on Shabbos, is considered nullified without specification. Other produce, from which one may take on Shabbos, is not ordinarily considered nullified.
