(54) Up to ten amos, etc. Ten amos itself is included.
(55) It is considered an entrance. Therefore, if they wish they may make separate eruvin, and if they wish they may make one eruv together, as stated above in seif 4.
(56) Provided it is not breached. If the wall is breached across its entire width, even if the opening is less than ten amos, the courtyards must make one joint eruv.
(57) Across its entire width. Some portion must remain on each side, or a strip four handbreadths wide and ten handbreadths high must remain on one side. Otherwise, it is considered fully breached. See Magen Avraham. See also siman 363, seif 2, in the Mishnah Berurah, where this matter is discussed.
(58) More than ten amos, etc. Even if the remaining wall on both sides is many times greater than the fallen section, the entire opening is treated as a breach rather than an entrance.
(59) Together. If each courtyard made its own eruv, they prohibit one another. If they constructed a tzuras hapesach at the breach, however, it is considered an entrance and they may make separate eruvin if they wish.
(60) If there is not between them, etc. The same applies if the entire partition is ten handbreadths high except for a section wider than ten amos that is less than ten handbreadths high. That section is considered a complete breach, and the courtyards must make one joint eruv.
