(113) If they wish, they may make separate eruvin. The prohibition against climbing an ordinary tree on Shabbos is only from the words of the Sages, and during bein hashemashos, when the eruv takes effect, the Sages did not apply their decree concerning a shevus.
(114) A tree is ineffective. Since it is forbidden to climb the tree on Shabbos, and that prohibition itself arises from Shabbos, it cannot serve to permit something for Shabbos, for the two principles would contradict one another.
(115) An asheirah is effective. Since no Shabbos prohibition applies to climbing it, we are not concerned with the separate prohibition of idolatry. It is nevertheless considered an entrance whose access is merely blocked by an external prohibition.
(116) Dry. It has no fruit, leaves, or branches, so there is no concern that one may detach anything. Strictly speaking, it would therefore be permitted to climb it on Shabbos were it not for the prohibition of an asheirah. See siman 336 above, which states that one should refrain from climbing even a dry tree. That restriction is only a safeguard lest people become lenient regarding other trees. Eliyah Rabbah inclines toward ruling in accordance with the first opinion, which is the view of many poskim.
