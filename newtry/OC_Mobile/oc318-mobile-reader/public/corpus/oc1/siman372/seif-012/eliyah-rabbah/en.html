To permit its use to him, etc. But not to permit them to join in one eruv unless it reaches the top of the wall. See seifim 8 and 9, as stated by Nachalat Tzvi and Magen Avraham. The Bach, however, writes that it is obvious that they may also join in one eruv when the other courtyard likewise made a corresponding arrangement. Perhaps the Bach refers to a case where it reaches the top. It appears to me that if the ladder reaches the top of the wall, that suffices, as written in seif 14.
The Levush writes: The wall is ten tefachim, etc. This is a scribal error and should read twenty tefachim.
