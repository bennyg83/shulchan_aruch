A house situated between two courtyards and open to both, and it joined in an eruv with both—but the courtyards did not join with each other—one may not carry items that were in houses from one courtyard to the other via that house. If there is a wall between two courtyards ten handbreadths high, or if the ground of one courtyard is five handbreadths higher and a five-handbreadth partition was built to complete ten, they cannot join in one eruv. If the wall is ten high for both and produce is on top, the residents of both courtyards may lower it into their courtyards and raise produce that was in the courtyards onto it, and lower it into the other courtyard—whether the wall is four handbreadths wide or not. But produce that was in a house: if the wall is four wide, it is' forbidden to raise or lower it; if not four wide, it is permitted (provided they do not exchange items, as above Siman 346:1). If the wall' is ten high for one courtyard but not the other (e.g.,' one ground is higher), the one for whom it is not ten high may use it even with house-items; the other may not use it even with courtyard-items—provided the wall is four wide.
