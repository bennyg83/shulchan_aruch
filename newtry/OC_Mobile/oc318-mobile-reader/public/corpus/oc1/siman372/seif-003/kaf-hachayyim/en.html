18) [Se'if 3] Two courtyards whose residents wish to establish an eruv together, etc. This means that there is an entrance or window between them, as stated in the following se'if. See the end of siman 365 and siman 387, which imply that if there is no window between them, then even if they participated in the shituf for the alleyway they may not carry through a hole. The same law would therefore apply to our courtyards and houses. Magen Avraham, subsection 3. Even HaOzer, however, questions this. Since they participated together in a shituf, they are all deemed a single courtyard, so why should they not carry from one to the other just as they may carry from one house to another within a single courtyard? See there. This also appears to be the opinion of Tosefes Shabbos, subsection 6. Chayei Adam, rule 72, subsection 12, Beis Meir, and Atzei Almogim likewise disagree with Magen Avraham. See there. This is also the opinion of the later authorities.
19) There. One person brings it on behalf of everyone, etc. The same applies when a father and his children who receive their sustenance from him live in one courtyard, or a teacher and his students who receive their sustenance from him. We rule that whenever no other residents live in the courtyard, they need not establish an eruv, as stated in siman 370. If they wish to establish an eruv with the adjacent courtyard, one person represents all of them. Beis Yosef. He may even provide the food from his own property. Shulchan Aruch HaRav, subsection 4.
