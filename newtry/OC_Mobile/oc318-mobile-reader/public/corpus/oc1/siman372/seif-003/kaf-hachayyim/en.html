3) There. Regarding utensils that began Shabbos within them, etc. This means that the utensils were placed there before the onset of Shabbos. It excludes utensils that began Shabbos in a house and were mistakenly carried into a courtyard, which may not be carried into another courtyard when the residents did not establish an eruv. Minchas Pittim likewise writes that if utensils came into a courtyard permissibly, such as by being worn, it is forbidden to take them into another courtyard. Within that same courtyard, however, once they have arrived there they may be carried throughout it, according to Rashi on Shabbos 130 and Tosafos on Eruvin 91a, beginning with the words 'And utensils.' Gaon Yaakov at the beginning of the chapter Kol Gagos in Eruvin writes this as obvious. Rashba in Avodas HaKodesh, however, maintains that even if the utensils were brought into the courtyard permissibly, they may be carried within it only four amos, as in a public domain. See there. See Rabbi Akiva Eiger's novellae to the chapter Kol Gagos, Mishnah 2, and Beis Meir, siman 346, who writes that if a household utensil was carried into another house whose residents did not establish an eruv with the first house, all opinions agree that it may be carried throughout the second house. This is contrary to Even HaOzer, siman 366. See there.
