1) [Se'if 1] Roofs and courtyards, etc. The reason is that their use is neither exclusive nor constant, and an eruv chatzeiros is required only for utensils from the house. Beis Yosef in the name of Rashi; Taz, subsection 1; Magen Avraham, subsection 2. A roof means the upper surface of the roof. Magen Avraham, there. That is, there is no additional roofing above it, for otherwise it would be included in the category of an upper story and would be forbidden. The discussion also concerns a roof that does not project beyond the house's partitions. If it projects, see its law above in siman 345, se'if 16, and below in siman 374, se'if 4.
2) There. And karpeifos. See above, siman 346, se'if 3.
3) There. Regarding utensils that began Shabbos within them, etc. This means that the utensils were placed there before the onset of Shabbos. It excludes utensils that began Shabbos in a house and were mistakenly carried into a courtyard, which may not be carried into another courtyard when the residents did not establish an eruv. Minchas Pittim likewise writes that if utensils came into a courtyard permissibly, such as by being worn, it is forbidden to take them into another courtyard. Within that same courtyard, however, once they have arrived there they may be carried throughout it, according to Rashi on Shabbos 130 and Tosafos on Eruvin 91a, beginning with the words 'And utensils.' Gaon Yaakov at the beginning of the chapter Kol Gagos in Eruvin writes this as obvious. Rashba in Avodas HaKodesh, however, maintains that even if the utensils were brought into the courtyard permissibly, they may be carried within it only four amos, as in a public domain. See there. See Rabbi Akiva Eiger's novellae to the chapter Kol Gagos, Mishnah 2, and Beis Meir, siman 346, who writes that if a household utensil was carried into another house whose residents did not establish an eruv with the first house, all opinions agree that it may be carried throughout the second house. This is contrary to Even HaOzer, siman 366. See there.
4) There. Regarding utensils that began Shabbos within them, etc. Utensils that began Shabbos in a house may not be carried from a courtyard into a karpeif not enclosed for residential use, even if the courtyard and karpeif belong to one person. Rashi on Eruvin at the end of folio 23; Tosafos at the end of folio 76; Magen Avraham, subsection 1. This applies even if the karpeif is smaller than beis sasayim. Tosefes Shabbos, subsection 1; Eshel Avraham, subsection 1; Shulchan Aruch HaRav, subsection 1. It is forbidden to carry from a karpeif enclosed for residential use into a karpeif larger than beis sasayim that was not enclosed for residential use. Similarly, if one karpeif is smaller than beis sasayim and the other larger than beis sasayim, and neither was enclosed for residential use, it is forbidden to carry from one to the other, just as it is forbidden by Rabbinic law to carry from a private domain into a karmelis, and an eruv is ineffective. If both are larger than beis sasayim and neither was enclosed for residential use, however, it is permitted to carry from one to the other within four amos. Similarly, it is permitted to carry from such a karpeif into an actual karmelis, as stated in siman 346, se'if 3. It is forbidden to carry from a courtyard into a karpeif larger than beis sasayim that was not enclosed for residential use, or in the reverse direction, even one amah, because this resembles carrying from a private domain into a karmelis. Mishbetzos Zahav, subsection 1. See below, subsection 15.
5) There. Or to a roof. This means from his courtyard to another person's roof.
6) There. Or to the top of the wall between them. This means the wall between two courtyards. See below, se'if 6.
7) There. Even if it is much higher than it. We do not say that this causes them to be divided into two domains. This excludes the opinion of Rabbi Meir in the Mishnah.
8) There. And from the roof to a karpeif. The same applies from a courtyard to a karpeif, and from one person's karpeif to another person's karpeif. Shulchan Aruch HaRav, there.
9) There. Which is not larger than beis sasayim. If it is larger than beis sasayim, it is included in the category of a karmelis, since it was not enclosed for residential use, as stated below in se'if 20. Consequently, it is forbidden to carry from it into a private domain. See above, siman 346, se'if 3, and our words there in subsection 27.
10) There. Or into an alleyway that was rectified, etc. Its law is likewise that of a courtyard whose residents did not establish an eruv, rather than that of a karmelis, as stated in siman 388. Shulchan Aruch HaRav, there.
11) There. Even if they did not establish an eruv in it. This means that the courtyards did not participate together in a shituf for the alleyway. Shulchan Aruch HaRav, there, writes likewise. See below in siman 388.
12) There. Into another courtyard. The same applies to another roof or another karpeif, as explained above.
13) There. We are not concerned that one might carry, etc. This is because that would constitute a decree upon a decree. Levush; Tosefes Shabbos, subsection 4.
