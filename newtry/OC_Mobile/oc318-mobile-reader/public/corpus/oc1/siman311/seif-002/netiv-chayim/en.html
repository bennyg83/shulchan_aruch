(s.k. 22) It refers only to tithe. It appears to me: it is difficult — the novellae of the Rashba write explicitly that if he intended for planting it is forbidden; and according to his view it should be difficult against him from the gemara of the Aruch daf 45, wheat that one sowed in the ground — what is the law to gather and eat; and further it is difficult: if when intending for planting for shevi'it and tithe they are concerned — for it teaches "one who buries," implying specifically if he intended the burying alone and not planting; and if on Shabbat one takes even when intending for planting — if so it should have taught explicitly "and one takes on Shabbat even if he intended for planting," like Tosafos deduced s.v. part of it is exposed, see there; however the words of the Beit Yosef are sound and stand: specifically for a turnip and radishes — if he intended for planting one does not take, for it is usual that they increase from the moisture of the ground; and if so they grew due to the intent of planting; unlike wheat, even when they intended for planting, and requires study.
