[4] [Levush] And likewise they did not permit, etc. Malbushei Yom Tov says the text should read "and likewise it appears to me they permitted here, etc." His words are surprising. The gloss is correct as written: they did not permit removal through a non-Jew except in a fire or when a corpse in the house is decomposing and creating an odor.
[5] [Levush] But if it decomposed, etc. This implies that it may be moved even from one bed to another. Tur and Bach, however, make clear that this is prohibited, because the Sages were more stringent regarding direct movement than regarding carrying out.
[6] [Levush] Nevertheless, if, etc. According to Tur, if the corpse is close to decomposing, even if the household has somewhere else to go, it may be carried out so that it not decompose (Magen Avraham). In my humble opinion it is possible Tur agrees that this is prohibited, and permission was given only when there is concern it will decompose and become degraded before them and they cannot leave. Rama and Levush imply this, for the opinion cited above is Tur's view, yet they rule here without qualification to prohibit.
[7] [Levush] Only by means of a loaf, etc. Magen Avraham writes that if no loaf is available, Tur prohibits because direct movement of a corpse was not permitted without a loaf or child. In my humble opinion Tur may agree that where neither is available movement is permitted because of human dignity. The requirement of a loaf or child applies only when available, so that what can be remedied should be remedied.
[8] [Levush] And some say the opposite, etc. The first view appears primary to me, because Rashba and Ran decide accordingly, and Tzedah LaDerech and Sefer Zikaron of the Gaon Rabbi Yosef HaKohen rule likewise.
[9] And some permit, etc. Bach questions Shulchan Aruch, because Rambam and Tur themselves conclude that Rav Chisda prohibits it. In truth that is the law, and Rabbenu Yerucham, netiv 28, rules likewise, as Shibbolei HaLeket also implies. Nevertheless, it appears to me that Shulchan Aruch's intent is that according to the opinion that nowadays, when we have no biblical public domain, one may rely on leniency. It is obvious that according to the prohibiting view it is prohibited even without a child. Perishah goes further and writes that even Rambam permitted only through a child, because the corpse is treated as subordinate to the child. This requires study.
