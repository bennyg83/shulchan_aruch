(א) Permitted to take him out — for great is human dignity that overrides a lav of the Rabbis; and what is written "and if they had a place" — that is Rambam's view and his reason, for he holds the main permission is on account of dignity of the living, for the living too are disgraced when the dead person putrefies and is disgraced; therefore when they have a place to leave — there is no dignity of the living — we do not take him out, except for a dead person who came on a ship mentioned at the end of the seif — there is great disgrace for the dead when gentiles gather around him, and they were concerned for his dignity; unlike here where there is not so much disgrace for the dead since he is placed where they do not see; and further since he already putrefied. And know that according to those who say as stated above who are lenient when close to putrefying — even if they have a place to go out — permitted to take him out so that he not putrefy:
(ב) Only via a loaf — for carrying out that cannot be remedied otherwise — permitted; but moving that can be remedied via a loaf — they remedy:
(ג) Better to take him out, etc. — see in Eliyah Rabbah who decided like the first view because many poskim hold like it; but nevertheless it appears to me when he has no loaf and child — permitted to take out even without loaf and child, for even per the first view there is no decisive ruling that is for delay; see in Magen Avraham s.k. 8 and in Eliyah Rabbah s.k. 7:
(ד) Even according to R' Yehuda — the reason is it is melacha she'einah tzreichah legufah, for he does not need the dead person and there is only a rabbinic prohibition according to R' Shimon whom we rule like; but regarding a dead person they did not decree on account of human dignity; but the first view holds they did not permit except karmelit which has no Torah root; unlike here where there is melacha upon it, only she'einah tzrichah legufah — they did not permit even for a dead person; and see Taz and likewise in Eliyah Rabbah the practical law is like the first view because many poskim hold like it; and especially melacha she'einah tzreichah legufah too is not clear, for he is exempt — there are those who rule like R' Yehuda who is liable upon it; see at the end of siman 334:
(ה) Via a child — specifically when he is somewhat grown, for the living carry themselves apply to him and there is only a rabbinic prohibition, and regarding a dead person they did not decree; unlike a very small child [see siman 308 s.a. 71 and in Mishna Berurah there] who is equal to a loaf and one is liable for taking them out themselves:
(ו) And likewise they are permitted, etc. — like via loaf and child — meaning as they permitted via an Israelite with loaf and child, they permitted via a gentile even without loaf and child; and it is implied somewhat from this language that specifically to karmelit; but to reshus harabbim, which is forbidden according to most poskim via an Israelite even via loaf and child — even via a gentile forbidden; and so in Magen Avraham and Eliyah Rabbah he sides in the name of several poskim to be lenient also in reshus harabbim:
(ז) For the need of kohanim — that they could be in their houses that were under one roof with the dead person, for moving the dead person via loaf or child was not permitted except for the need of the dead person; and the Acharonim wrote that indirect moving — namely turning him from bed to bed — is permitted for the need of kohanim even without loaf and child, for behold it is indirect moving for the need of his place which is permitted, as below in seif 5; but nevertheless both in this and in what Rama permits via a gentile — specifically when the relatives agree, for regarding disgrace of the dead there is none in this, for what is it to him whether they purify him in this house or another; but they cannot compel them to take the dead person from the house where he died [unless in a place where the custom is to carry him immediately to the place where they purify him — he can compel them]; and even if the synagogue was under one roof with the dead person and the kohanim are delayed thereby from going to the synagogue, which is a mitzvah — this is dignity of the relatives that they purify and attend to the needs of the dead in their house; unless through falling, for there is no dignity — some say they can compel them to take out from the house for the kohanim; and per the manners explained above [all this is explained from the words of Magen Avraham and Eliyah Rabbah; and see what Magen Avraham further wrote on this matter]:
(ח) For the need of a mitzvah — shvus of shvus for the need of a mitzvah is permitted, as above in siman 307 seif 5; and see there that for a great need too it is considered like the need of a mitzvah; therefore here we permit via a gentile for the need of kohanim; and so it is implied from the GRA:
