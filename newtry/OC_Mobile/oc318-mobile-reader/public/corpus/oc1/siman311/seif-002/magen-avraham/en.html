(א) That he is close to putrefying. And specifically via a loaf or a child; but if there is no loaf or child — forbidden, for they were stricter regarding the prohibition of moving than regarding the prohibition of carrying out, as is written in seif 1 — Tosafos Sukkah daf 36 and see siman 312 s.s.a:

(ב) Permitted to take him out. For great is human dignity that overrides a lav of the Rabbis, as is written in siman 13 and siman 687 seif 2; and the Maggid Mishneh wrote: because of the dignity of the living he speaks, and in order to remove the odor from them they permitted — end of his words in the name of Ra'avad — meaning they did not permit carrying out on account of the dignity of the dead, for there is not so much disgrace when the dead person putrefies — see what is written in seif 1:

(ג) We do not take him out. For there is no dignity of the living; and dignity of the dead too there is not, since he is placed in a place where they do not see; and furthermore since he already putrefied; and per the Tur's view — if he is close to putrefying, even if they have a place to go out — permitted to take him out so that he not putrefy:

(ד) Only via a loaf. For carrying out that cannot be remedied otherwise — permitted; but moving that can be remedied with a loaf — they remedy; but nevertheless if he has no loaf or child — permitted according to Rambam and the Tur; forbidden according to [others], for they did not permit the prohibition of moving without a loaf or child:

(ה) Even according to R' Yehuda. For it is melacha she'einah tzreichah legufah — see siman 337:

(ו) Via a child. Specifically when he is somewhat grown, as is written in siman 308 s.a. 71:

(ז) Not via a loaf. For nevertheless he will be liable for the loaf since it is not ancillary to the dead person:

(ח) They assemble there. And it is great disgrace — see what is written in seif 1:

(ט) To tell a gentile. For it is a shvus in which there is no action; and what Rabbenu Yerucham challenged — that if so it should be permitted to tell a gentile to bury a dead person on Shabbat — the Ramban already expanded on this in Sefer HaEshkol daf 26: karmelit is different, for everyone knows it is not within melacha; but to desecrate Shabbat upon him even via a gentile — forbidden on account of the dignity of the dead, lest they say Shabbat was desecrated for him; therefore even to take him out via a gentile to place him in a grave made since yesterday — forbidden, even though it is shvus of shvus and they do not observe any dignity at all in a matter of prohibitions that become publicized even of the Rabbis — end of his words; and so Tosafos in Bava Kamma daf 88 and in Gittin daf 18 — see siman 337 thus; if so it is implied that to reshus harabbim it is forbidden even via a gentile; and even per the one who says melacha she'einah tzreichah legufah is exempt — nevertheless its essence is d'oraisa, as the Tur wrote in the name of Rabbeinu Tam:

(י) And they are accustomed to move. In truth Maharil ruled that it is permitted to move via a loaf or child for the need of the living; and so is the custom — end of his words; except that in Hagahot Maimoniyot he forbids via loaf or child; and it appears to me that indirect moving is permitted for the need of kohanim, as Hagahot HaKunteres wrote — that for the need of his place it is permitted; but nevertheless the kohanim cannot compel the relatives to take him out, even if the kohanim need to enter the synagogue, for it is a mitzvah — so in responsum of Maharil siman 69 — unless through falling [Bach]; and see siman 371 seif 4; and the Mechaber deals with when the relatives are not particular; and regarding disgrace of the dead there is none, for what is it to me in this house or in another house; but the kohanim want to be in their houses — and so in Maharil there; on a weekday permitted to take him out so the kohanim may enter their houses if the relatives are not particular, or in a place where the custom is to carry immediately to the place where they purify him — he can compel them; but nevertheless it appears to me that if there is a kohen who is ill who cannot leave his house — they compel the relatives to take the dead person from his house so that he not transgress a d'oraisa prohibition; and what is written in Shach Yoreh De'ah siman 372 that it is rabbinic — he was not precise; see siman 343:
