(א) To anoint the dead and to rinse him — anointing is with oil and rinsing with water — meaning with cold water; and the reason in all this that they permitted is that this is not within moving but mere contact since he does not move a limb:

(ב) From beneath him — but forbidden to remove the dead person from upon the cushion, for that is actual moving; and people practice raising him from upon the cushions via a loaf even where there is no concern lest he putrefy — one must protest against them; only to remove the cushions from beneath him is permitted; and in some places they practice straightening his eyes and aligning his limbs so they not contort, and they say it is in the Zohar that it is dangerous; and even though this matter has no root in law, for in the Mishnah it states we do not straighten the eyes of the dead, and likewise not to move a limb — and all the Rishonim sealed so and as ruled in Shulchan Aruch — nevertheless where they practice one does not protest since they have support from the Zohar; but in truth in the Zohar danger is not mentioned except regarding eyes; if so to align limbs they have no support whatsoever; and in Eliyah Rabbah he wrote that where they practice to be lenient in this because they fear lest the limbs contort and there be disgrace for the dead — they should place a loaf [or another object permitted to move, as stated] on those limbs and press upon it until the limbs extend:

(ג) Lest he putrefy — on account of heat of the cushions and mattresses; and permitted to sprinkle vinegar on the garments of the dead so he not putrefy until Motzei Shabbat and they can occupy themselves with his purification [Acharonim]:

(ד) That he not move, etc. — and if the dead person was soiled with mud and excrement that he is repulsive in the eyes of viewers — permitted to move him via loaf and child and to wash him with water by hand and not via a cloth on account of squeezing; but otherwise forbidden in all this, and even via a gentile forbidden:
