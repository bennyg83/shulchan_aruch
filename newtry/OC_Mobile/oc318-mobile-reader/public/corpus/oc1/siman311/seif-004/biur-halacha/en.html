Some say, etc., except for a naked corpse, etc. — see Beit Yosef who challenged on this that the garment should be nullified relative to him; and see Magen Avraham who resolved that the garment is not nullified automatically since he will strip it off; and in my humble opinion there is much to question in this law: first, is the Gemara dealing with one naked [unless we force that it deals where they dressed him in shrouds from erev Shabbat and there was no time to bury him]; and further, in the Gemara it is stated explicitly that one who takes out a person who is dressed in garments is exempt even for the garments because the garments are secondary to him; and Eliyah Rabbah already challenged thus; and see there what he rejected in the name of Maharal that specifically regarding a living person, but regarding a dead person he is destined to strip them from him — therefore they are not nullified relative to him and are like a loaf and infant and like what Magen Avraham wrote; but all this is very forced, for behold his garments when he is dressed in them certainly need them for bodily use more than his bed on which he lies after death [for he cannot lie naked], and nevertheless regarding his bed also, although he will certainly remove it in the end, nevertheless it is proven in the Gemara that all the time he lies on it, if he wishes to move him he needs a loaf or infant or to turn it from bed to bed, that is moving from the side, and they did not permit because of the bed itself [for it did not become a base as Rosh and Mordechai wrote] but certainly although he will remove it from it, nevertheless all the time he lies on it it is considered secondary to him and nullified relative to him; if so all the more his garments — all the time he is dressed in them they are secondary to him and nullified relative to him, since he needs them now and they are not considered like a loaf and infant:
