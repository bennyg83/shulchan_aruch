Two people come, etc. In the Gemara they say they shall do so specifically because there is no partition for the corpse, only for the living — for a movable utensil is only for the need of a movable thing; and Beit Yosef challenged: since we rule that a movable utensil [may be used] even for the need of a thing that is not movable, he should not need this at all but can make a temporary partition for the corpse; and it appears to me that so too Tosafos wondered: what prohibition is there if each brings a bed to sit on; and further, why bring a bed first and then a mat — let them wait until they warm above and bring bed and mat together. And it appears the rabbis were strict to make a recognizable sign that it is for the need of the living, as much as possible — end of his words; and the reason for this stricture appears to be that they made distance in the matter of need of the corpse, for since a person is distraught over his dead we are concerned lest they move directly; therefore they made a recognizable sign as much as possible so all will sense the prohibition of moving the corpse; and therefore the Tur's conclusion here also is resolved — that it appears as if done for the need of the living, that they do not desecrate Shabbat for the corpse even in a d'rabbanan prohibition — end of his words; he intended what we said of the distance:
