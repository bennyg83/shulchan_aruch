(א) By them. He explained that one places it with him and moves it with him:

(ב) That one turns it over. And if he was alive at bein hashemashos — permitted afterward to move the bed, for it did not become a base — the Rosh; and see siman 266 seif 9; and the Me'or wrote that even if he died at bein hashemashos the bed did not become a base, for the bed is not needed for the dead person and in any case one need not place him except on the ground, as we learned: they remove the cushion from beneath him:

(ג) In that same domain. This the Beit Yosef wrote — for since Rambam wrote "and a fire fell in a courtyard in which there is a dead person" — it is implied that to take him out is forbidden, for here there is no disgrace of the dead when he burns; and because of the reason that if you do not permit him he will come to extinguish — we do not permit carrying out; and all the more so per the one who says melacha she'einah tzreichah legufah is exempt — see siman 334; and even though in Mordechai it is implied that regarding fire they permitted more — the Beit Yosef does not hold so, but rather as is written in seif 2 — and he is disgraced among the living, etc.; and if they have a place to go out, etc. — specifically because of the dignity of the living it is permitted — so explicitly in Beit Yosef; and therefore he also wrote regarding a dead person lying in the sun, etc. — in that same domain as stated — not like the parsah; and in Rambam it is implied that the law of fire and sun are equal, as is so in the gemara:
