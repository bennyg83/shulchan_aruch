1) [Seif 1] A corpse that is lying, etc. Burying a meis mitzvah does not override Shabbos, as is explicit in Sanhedrin 35. The same applies to Yom Tov, as Tosafos writes in Nazir 47a. The pious Rabbi Yosef Molcho, responsa manuscript, siman 15; Birkei Yosef, paragraph 1. See below, siman 526.
2) There: If there is a loaf, etc. Whatever can be moved permissibly is preferable. This itself honors the deceased, since Shabbos is not desecrated on his account whenever a permitted method is available. Tosefes Shabbos, paragraph 1.
3) There: He moves it by means of them. This means that he places the loaf or child beside the deceased and moves it together with him. Magen Avraham, paragraph 1. It need not literally be placed upon him, because that would disgrace the deceased. Eshel Avraham, paragraph 1.
3) Regarding the practice of moving a corpse only by means of a loaf or child that was present there before Shabbos, there is no basis upon which to rely. Be'er Eishek, siman 52. Divrei Ben Shlomo writes the same. Yad Aharon, Hagahos HaTur.
4) There: That he turn it from one bed, etc. The ruling mentions the usual case, because a corpse ordinarily lies upon a bed. The same applies when it lies upon a mat on the ground: one rolls the mat so that the corpse falls onto the adjacent mat. Chiddushei Ran; Pis'chei Olam, section 3.
5) There: That he turn it from one bed, etc. If he was alive during twilight, the bed may afterward be moved, because it did not become a base for a forbidden item. Rosh and Meiri write that even if he was dead during twilight, the bed does not become a base, because the bed does not need the corpse. On the contrary, the corpse should be placed upon the ground, as the Mishnah teaches that one pulls the cushion from beneath him. Magen Avraham, paragraph 2; Eliyah Rabbah, paragraph 1; Tosefes Shabbos, paragraph 3. See above, siman 266, paragraph 44, for what we wrote in the name of Baal HaMaor. If he died during twilight and one intended to leave him there throughout Shabbos, he should nevertheless turn him from bed to bed, using every permitted option available because the law is subject to a dispute, instead of moving him directly. Regarding a corpse lying in the sun, however, for which indirect movement is forbidden, there is no practical difference concerning the bed. Eshel Avraham, paragraph 2.
6) There: For it is indirect movement. Although indirect movement is considered movement when performed for the benefit of the muktzeh item, as stated in seif 8, the Sages permitted it in a fire because a person is distraught over his deceased relative. If it were not permitted, he might extinguish the fire. Tosefes Shabbos there.
7) There: He may move it directly. Although extinguishing in this case is labor not needed for its own purpose, so there is no concern for a Torah prohibition, the Sages nevertheless permitted direct moving. If one needed the extinguished material to make charcoal, the prohibition would be from the Torah, and not everyone knows this distinction. Ran writes similarly. Tosefes Shabbos, paragraph 4. See below, at the end of siman 334 in the gloss, and our words there, with the help of Heaven.
8) There: All this applies within the same domain. This implies that taking the corpse into another domain is forbidden. The later authorities question the Shulchan Aruch, however, because even to prevent disgrace it is permitted to take the corpse into another domain, as stated in seif 2, and this should certainly be permitted because of a fire. Levush writes that a scribal error entered the Shulchan Aruch. Alternatively, its meaning is: 'All this applies within the same domain,' meaning that the instruction first to seek permitted ways to lessen the prohibition by using a loaf or child applies when the danger from the fire is not so immediate. If the fire is dangerous, they should not first seek a way to lessen the prohibition, lest the fire intensify and the corpse burn. Rather, they should take it out directly by the methods explained in seif 2; see there. Bach and Taz, paragraph 1, likewise permit taking it from one domain to another because of a fire, just as when decay threatens in seif 2; see there. Eliyah Rabbah, paragraph 13, writes this in the name of several authorities and agrees with them in practice. Tosefes Shabbos writes the same, and this is the view of the later authorities. Magen Avraham, paragraph 3, writes that there is no disgrace when the corpse burns, but the later authorities have already challenged him. Machatzis HaShekel attempts to defend Magen Avraham based on Rema in Yoreh De'ah, siman 363, who permits placing lime upon a corpse and so forth. Pachad Yitzchak, paragraph 1, rejects this comparison: there the purpose is specifically to decompose the flesh, whereas here a fire would consume everything, and there is no greater disgrace; see there. This is especially so according to the sacred Zohar and Arizal, who teach that an aspect of the nefesh remains within the body through the mystery of the ethereal remnant of the bones so that it may endure until the resurrection, as stated in Eitz Chaim, Gate 18, Gate of the 288 Sparks, chapter 1; see there. If it were burned, Heaven forbid, no suffering could be greater. See also Shaar HaMitzvos, Parashas Vayechi, and examine it carefully.
9) There: A corpse lying in the sun, etc. The same applies when it lies in a confined place where the relatives cannot sit beside it. Agudah, chapter Kirah; Eliyah Rabbah, paragraph 2.
