In the Mechaber seif 2, via a child — it appears to me: the reason of the Ramban that the corpse becomes a base for the child, and the one who takes out there via the bed is exempt even for the bed, for the bed is subsidiary to him, as stated in the mishnah in chapter Hametzunim daf 93b, see there; and with this it is also fine according to Rabbeinu Avigdor, who below seif 4 brings proof from the residue of cups and does not bring straightforwardly that what is it to me whether a loaf and child on the corpse or its garments — rather necessarily that I place upon it that the corpse becomes a base for a child or for food and utensils; but with its garments it is the reverse, for behold its garments are subsidiary to the corpse and it is like the residue inside the cup that the cup serves its residue; and therefore one can resolve the question of the Shelah that Magen Avraham s.k. 16 can say that King David, peace upon him, needed to move him from domain to domain and it was not permitted except in the manner the Ramban wrote — to place upon him a child so the corpse will be a base for the child and exempt even for the bed that is the corpse; and what they said in Shabbat daf 30b that the Sanhedrin answered him "your father — place upon him a loaf or a child" is a strengthened expression; and "loaf" he mentioned merely as an example, only "child" for taking from domain to domain; and this does not apply via its garments, and requires study. And that which they did not permit a corpse on a bed except via indirect moving and not to move directly via the bed — because it is not comparable to a child, for there the corpse becomes a base for a child, unlike here where the corpse is on a bed and he is liable even for the bed; and it is also not comparable to cup residue that do not come out with rinsing except via pouring and washing, and likewise the garments of a corpse; unlike upon the bed, which is not batel against the bed, for they can shake them off from the bed, as I found reasonable.
