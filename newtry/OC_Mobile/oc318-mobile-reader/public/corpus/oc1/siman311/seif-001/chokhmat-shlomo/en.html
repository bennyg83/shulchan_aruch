Seif 1, a corpse lying in a place where one fears for it due to fire, etc. It appears to me: behold from the language of the poskim it is implied that every corpse is permitted to move because of fire or another disgrace. However behold in Midrash Rabbah parashat Vayikra chapter 5 there is, and this is its language: R' Avin said — an incident with a butcher in Tzipori who was feeding treifot and nevelot to Israel; once he ate and drank and became drunk and went up to the roof of the house and fell and died; the dogs began to lick his blood; they came and asked R' Chiyya — what is the law to remove him from before them? He said to them: it is written "and you shall be holy people to Me; flesh in the field — torn — you shall not eat, you shall throw it to the dog" — this one who was stealing vessels and feeding nevelot and treifot to Israel, etc. — from their own they are eating, end of his words; and see in Mechilta there that he explained the question was whether it is permitted to remove him from before them, etc. If so it is proven from there that for a wicked person who feeds treifot and the like, one does not desecrate Shabbat and Yom Kippur on his account to move him because of disgrace; for necessarily it deals via a loaf or a child, for without a loaf and child even if a proper person died it is forbidden; and necessarily it deals via a loaf and child; and even so they forbade to move him — and it is proven that for a wicked person we are not concerned for his disgrace. And even though one does not derive halacha from aggadah — that is only when the midrash expounds thus as halachic method or expounds not based on an incident; but when there is an actual incident and nothing in the Talmud proves the opposite, certainly we derive the law from it, since it is explicit in it that he ruled thus halacha lema'aseh. And therefore it appeared that so is the law: for a known wicked person one does not desecrate Shabbat because of his disgrace — only regarding fire and the like where there is concern that a person will be agitated over his corpse and may come to actual desecration of Shabbat — in this one does not distinguish between wicked or not; but where there is no concern he will come to d'oraisa melacha, it appears one does not desecrate Shabbat to move the corpse via a loaf and child, as explicit in the midrash mentioned, and requires study.
