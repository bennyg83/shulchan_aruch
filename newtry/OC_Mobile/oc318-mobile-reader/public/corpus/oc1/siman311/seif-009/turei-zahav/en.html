(א) Buried fruits. In the Gemara it also mentions a hot coal buried in coals — Tosafos beginning of chapter Kol HaKelim wrote it deals when he left it to take it; but if he left it there all Shabbat it is a base for forbidden matter as below regarding a stone on the mouth of a barrel; and Beit Yosef wrote thus, and Rosh in responsum also wrote; and further: if so, even in "leaves and it is there" the hot coal did not become a base, only for baking need and to remove it afterward — end of his words. And it is possible this argument is also to refute that he buried in straw that the hot coal did not become a base for the straw but for what cooks in the straw and to remove it afterward. And it is possible the poskim hold thus, and therefore they did not distinguish between forgetting and leaving; and although it is possible they relied on what they distinguished regarding a stone on the mouth of a barrel — therefore we conclude: since muktzeh is d'rabbanan and there is a side to say the poskim are lenient thus we rule — end of his words. And I did not understand this, for if so the entire law of base for forbidden in clarity falls, for everywhere we could say so; also regarding a stone on the mouth of a barrel we could say even in "leaves" it is not a base, for he only left it until he needs to remove it. And further, above siman 309 near the end Beit Yosef wrote in the name of writings of Maharai that even if he leaves incidentally as people are accustomed to place in a box these things on those — it is a base, since he knows this will rest on the object on Shabbat as written there in rulings siman 193; and according to what appears to me that Rosh did not write thus except regarding a hot coal on coals — there there is necessity to take it after baking, for it will spoil if it remains, and this does not apply in other places. And per the reason Rama wrote shortly that in foods there is no base for forbidden matter, and it is from Kol Bo — this reason applies also to this seif in explanation; but it was not settled for me from where we say thus to be lenient, for Tosafos wrote explicitly there is base in foods; and on whom shall we rely to contradict their words. And it appears certainly all poskim hold thus: specifically with intention to take them on Shabbat — for the presumption of one who buries fruits so they will soften is that he intended to take from them what he will need when fit for eating; therefore the poskim did not need to mention he will take from them on Shabbat, for the presumption is so; also Rama's words are explained thus as stated shortly; and even if he left them there to be all Shabbat — meaning as long as he wishes to eat from them and what remains he will eat another time — in this he said properly that in foods there is no base, for certainly he intended to eat from them as long as he wishes, for the presumption is for this; but one who resolves clearly he will not eat from them on this Shabbat and buries them for that intention — specifically it appears forbidden even in moving from the side, as Tosafos' words, that it is like placing a stone on the mouth of a barrel as appears to me; and we learn here that Rosh's view is like the author of Terumot siman 309 that "leaves" is not considered when he thinks to take it on Shabbat; and this is also Rabbenu Tam in Tosafos; and Beit Yosef wrote that the author of Semag also said so, and they are fit to rely upon where there is need to move at least by moving from the side the menorah they kindled on Shabbat when there is need for its place and there is no gentile there:
