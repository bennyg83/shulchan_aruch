The later authorities question the Shulchan Aruch, however, because even to prevent disgrace it is permitted to take the corpse into another domain, as stated in seif 2, and this should certainly be permitted because of a fire. Levush writes that a scribal error entered the Shulchan Aruch. Alternatively, its meaning is: 'All this applies within the same domain,' meaning that the instruction first to seek permitted ways to lessen the prohibition by using a loaf or child applies when the danger from the fire is not so immediate. If the fire is dangerous, they should not first seek a way to lessen the prohibition, lest the fire intensify and the corpse burn. Rather, they should take it out directly by the methods explained in seif 2; see there. Bach and Taz, paragraph 1, likewise permit taking it from one domain to another because of a fire, just as when decay threatens in seif 2; see there. Eliyah Rabbah, paragraph 13, writes this in the name of several authorities and agrees with them in practice. Tosefes Shabbos writes the same, and this is the view of the later authorities. Magen Avraham, paragraph 3, writes that there is no disgrace when the corpse burns, but the later authorities have already challenged him. Machatzis HaShekel attempts to defend Magen Avraham based on Rema in Yoreh De'ah, siman 363, who permits placing lime upon a corpse and so forth. Pachad Yitzchak, paragraph 1, rejects this comparison: there the purpose is specifically to decompose the flesh, whereas here a fire would consume everything, and there is no greater disgrace; see there. This is especially so according to the sacred Zohar and Arizal, who teach that an aspect of the nefesh remains within the body through the mystery of the ethereal remnant of the bones so that it may endure until the resurrection, as stated in Eitz Chaim, Gate 18, Gate of the 288 Sparks, chapter 1; see there. If it were burned, Heaven forbid, no suffering could be greater. See also Shaar HaMitzvos, Parashas Vayechi, and examine it carefully.
