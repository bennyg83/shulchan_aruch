1) [Seif 1] Any blessing that does not mention the Name and kingship, etc. Since the first blessing of Shemoneh Esrei contains the phrase, 'the great God,' this is considered equivalent to kingship. Some say that because one says, 'God of Avraham,' it is considered equivalent to kingship, since the world had not yet proclaimed Him King over them until Avraham Avinu came. In the first blessing of the abridged seven, although kingship is not stated, the phrase, 'the holy God, like Whom there is none,' is considered kingship. Beis Yosef in the name of Tosafos and Rosh. Rokeach, siman 363, writes: Every blessing that gives thanks to the Holy One, blessed is He, mentions the Name and kingship. Shemoneh Esrei, however, is not thanksgiving for enjoyment or a mitzvah, but a request for a person's needs. Since a person should always arrange the praise of the Holy One, blessed is He, and then pray, the Sages did not institute kingship in it. Beis Yosef cites this. Regarding the blessing Elokai Neshamah, according to those who say it is not adjacent to Asher Yatzar, although it contains no kingship, since it mentions that the Holy One, blessed is He, creates souls, breathes them into bodies, afterward takes them from the bodies, and afterward returns them, there is no greater mention of kingship than this. Beis Yosef; Levush. See further above, siman 46, paragraph 3, and siman 113, paragraph 1.
