Seif katan 2: where there is no established service, and so forth. Every document, and so forth. The messenger does not deliver the letter directly to its owner. Therefore, where there is an established postal office there is no concern even if the intended recipient is not at home, because the messenger delivers the letter only to the postal office, which is permanently established. Where there is no established office, however, if he does not find the postal official he will have to deliver it to the intended recipient, and perhaps that person too will not be at home. Although the gentile will then perform the act on Shabbos, this is permitted and we do not initially apprehend such a possibility. It is therefore permitted to give the letter to the gentile while it is still day. Here too, since he may find the postal office in the town and be able to reach it before nightfall, it should be permitted.
Accordingly, if the writing is that of a gentile, the seal affixed to the letter must likewise be a gentile's seal.
From this we learn, and so forth. This does not refer to the immediately preceding statement, but rather to the underlying rule that where there is no established postal office it is forbidden because the messenger may have to go after the recipient on Shabbos. All the more so may one not tell him, “See to it,” and so forth, for then he will certainly have to travel on Shabbos.
