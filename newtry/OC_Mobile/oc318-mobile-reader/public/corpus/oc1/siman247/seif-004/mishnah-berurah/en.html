For the gentile does so on his own, and so forth. This means that the Jew does not compel him to do so. Rather, the gentile voluntarily agrees to carry the letter, and we assume that he does so because of a benefit he previously received from the Jew and therefore acts for his own advantage.
It is proper to be stringent. This wording implies that it is merely preferable to take this view into account, and the Taz likewise appears to accept the first view. In Darkei Moshe, siman 252, however, the Rama appears to accept the dissenting view. The Levush and Eliyah Rabbah in this siman also imply that carrying it without payment is forbidden. It is therefore proper to stipulate at least some small payment for the task. Once the gentile accepts that sum as his wage, the arrangement is considered a fixed fee, as the Chayei Adam also writes.
