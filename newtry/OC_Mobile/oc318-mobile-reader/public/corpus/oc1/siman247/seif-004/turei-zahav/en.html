Some disagree, etc. This refers to what the Beit Yosef writes in siman 252 in the name of the Shibolei HaLeket. In my humble opinion, this is extremely surprising, because it would certainly seem that the non-Jew exerts himself for his own benefit. We maintain in many places concerning a gift that, had the recipient not provided some satisfaction to the giver, the giver would not have given him the gift. So too here: when this non-Jew transports the item without payment, it is certainly compensation for some benefit the Jew previously conferred upon him, and it is therefore as though he received money from the Jew. It is no less favorable than the case stated immediately afterward, where the non-Jew initiates the arrangement with the Jew, etc. Yet the Shibolei HaLeket there does not mention this permission where the non-Jew initiates the arrangement.
