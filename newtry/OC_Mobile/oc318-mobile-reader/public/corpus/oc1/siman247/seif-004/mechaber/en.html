If the non-Jew carries the letter without payment, it is permitted even if it was given to him on Friday, because the non-Jew does this of his own accord merely to show gratitude to the Jew for a benefit he previously received from him, and it is therefore regarded as though a price had been fixed. {Rama: Some disagree and hold that whenever the non-Jew does it without payment it is forbidden, and it is proper to be stringent. But where the non-Jew initiates the matter and tells the Jew that he will go for him without payment, his intent is certainly for the benefit he previously received from the Jew, and it is permitted. [Beit Yosef]}
