Seif katan 1: a letter, and so forth. This teaches that where there is no established postal service it is forbidden, whereas where there is one it is permitted. This entire distinction does not apply to other forms of labor.
It is permitted, and so forth. Even on Friday, according to Rabbi Moshe Alashkar. This responsum is not available to me. In my humble opinion, it addresses a case in which the messenger can reach the destination before nightfall. Nevertheless, with a letter of this kind it is forbidden where there is no established postal service, because perhaps he will not find the recipient at home and will have to go after him on Shabbos. The same concern seemingly applies to objects, lest he not find the person at home. Even so, the authorities are more lenient with objects than with a letter. Indeed, regarding a letter too, the Magen Avraham asks in the following seif katan why we should be concerned that he may not find him, and how this differs from siman 252. He answers that because the messenger carries a document belonging to a Jew and this is recognizable, there is a special concern. That consideration does not apply to objects, and therefore they are permitted.
