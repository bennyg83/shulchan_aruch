1) [Seif 1] A person may send a letter, etc. A letter is specified to teach a greater novelty: although the Jewish person's handwriting is recognizable in the non-Jew's possession, it is permitted when a fixed fee was set. Bach; Olas Tamid, paragraph 1. Magen Avraham, paragraph 1, writes that a fixed fee is required for all types of labor, as stated in siman 252. A letter is specified to teach that if there is no established postal service, it is forbidden to send it even on Sunday; see there. Specifically a letter may not be sent even on Sunday when no fixed fee was set and there is no established postal service, because the Jewish person's writing is recognizable. Other utensils and articles that are not recognizable, however, may be sent even on Friday and even without a fixed fee, provided they can reach there while it is still Friday. Eshel Avraham, paragraphs 1 and 2; see there. Shulchan Aruch HaRav, paragraph 6, writes the same. See Eliyah Rabbah, paragraphs 1 and 3, and our words above in siman 246, paragraph 20.
