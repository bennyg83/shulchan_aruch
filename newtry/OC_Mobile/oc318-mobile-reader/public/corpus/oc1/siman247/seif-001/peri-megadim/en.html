Provided that. See the Taz. A stipulated fee need not necessarily be a precise sum, as is evident from seif 2 and from the responsum of the Rashba, for whenever the Jew mentions that he will pay the gentile it is permitted. The Tur, however, writes that one must agree upon a known amount. This may mean an amount commonly known in that place, where there is at least an approximate established rate, so that the gentile relies upon receiving it. If the amount is entirely unknown, perhaps even mentioning that he will pay him does not suffice. See the Levush concerning hiring him without specifying terms. Regarding the Mechaber's statement that one must not tell him to travel on Shabbos, if the gentile independently says that he will travel on Shabbos it is permitted. See the Beit Yosef and Levush, and see siman 307 in the Taz, seif katan 3, and the Magen Avraham here, seif katan 2.
Now. The Mechaber rules in accordance with the Rif and Rambam that when no fee was stipulated it is forbidden even from Sunday. The baraita on Shabbos 19a states that letters may not be sent on Friday unless a fee was stipulated, implying that with a stipulated fee it is permitted even on Friday, but without one it is always forbidden. The earlier baraita states that utensils may not be rented on Friday, but may be rented on Wednesday or Thursday; similarly, letters may not be sent on Friday, though on Wednesday or Thursday it is permitted. According to Beit Shammai this concerns the resting of one's utensils even when they reach the gentile's house, while letters sent for a stipulated fee are nevertheless forbidden on Friday because the gentile appears to be the Jew's agent, but are permitted on Wednesday or Thursday. Rabban Shimon ben Gamliel's household was even more stringent than Beit Shammai. This is why the Rif and Rambam omit that ruling in chapter 6 of Hilchot Shabbos, halachah 16. See the Maggid Mishneh, and Rabban Gamliel was likewise especially pious. In chapter 20, see the Maggid Mishneh, who infers from the word 'always'; in the Rif the word is 'entirely.' The fact that the Rosh cites the baraita concerning renting utensils and letters and nevertheless quotes the Rif's word 'entirely' is not conclusive. The word 'always' can likewise mean whether or not the messenger can reach the recipient's house. Still, the Rif and Rambam's omission of the permission on Wednesday and Thursday concerning letters somewhat supports this reading, though it is not decisive because they do record the baraita's rule concerning Friday. The Rambam, however, does provide evidence, for he changed the Rif's expression 'entirely' to 'always.' The Nesiv Chaim explains 'always' to mean even when the messenger can reach the recipient's actual house before Shabbos, because perhaps the recipient will not be home. In his longer work he did not decide the issue. Here the Mechaber rules like the Rambam and Rif, and in siman 246, seif 1, he likewise rules that the earlier baraita follows Beit Shammai. At first glance the Rama's rulings appear inconsistent, for there he writes that the latter opinion is primary, which would mean that the baraita is authoritative; why, then, does he write here merely that one may rely upon the lenient authorities when necessary? The Rif does not explicitly forbid it. This can be resolved as explained there: nevertheless, in siman 246 the Rama ruled stringently, and here too he was unwilling to be lenient except specifically where there is a need.
