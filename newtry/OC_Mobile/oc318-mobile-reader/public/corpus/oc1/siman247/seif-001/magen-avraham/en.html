A letter. The same rule applies to all forms of labor: a fixed fee is required, as stated in siman 252. The Mechaber mentioned a letter in order to teach that if there is no established postal service it is forbidden, as explained below. It is permitted to send one's utensils and belongings with a gentile even on Friday. See Rabbi Moshe Alashkar, Magen Avraham, siman 246.
If there is no established postal service. This means an appointed official to whom every letter is brought, who receives it and forwards it to the person for whom it was sent. If that official is not permanently stationed in the city to which the letter was sent, but only travels there occasionally for business, it is forbidden, lest the addressee not be found at home and the postal official likewise not be present, requiring the messenger to pursue him on Shabbos. Although other articles may be given to a gentile whenever he can complete the task before nightfall, even without a fixed fee, and it is permitted even if he later performs it on Shabbos, as stated in siman 252, seif 2, here the gentile carries a document in the Jew's handwriting and the Jew's involvement is recognizable, so people may say that he gave it to him on Shabbos to transport. Accordingly, if the letter is written in a gentile's script it is permitted. From this we learn all the more that when sending it on Friday one may not tell him, 'See that you are there on Sunday,' or on Monday, and the like, where it is known that he cannot arrive unless he travels on Shabbos. This is as though he explicitly told him to go on Shabbos, and it is forbidden even where a fixed fee was stipulated. See siman 307, seif 4.
The house nearest the wall. This means the city to which it was sent, namely the first house inside the city. So writes the Kesef Mishneh, rejecting what was written in the Beit Yosef; Rabbi Levi ibn Chaviv, responsum 105, and the Bach likewise rule this way.
Even if there is no established postal service. This applies even when it is known that he cannot arrive there before Shabbos. It appears to me that according to the first view, if the journey is short, such as one day's travel, and he sends it on Monday or Tuesday, it is permitted, for even if he does not find the recipient at home he has ample time to seek him out. See what I wrote in siman 252, seif 2.
