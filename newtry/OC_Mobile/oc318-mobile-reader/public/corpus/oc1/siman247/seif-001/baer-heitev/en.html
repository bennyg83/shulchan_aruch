Fixed a price. For then he labors on his own account and not for the Jew. The same applies to every type of labor: a fixed price is required, as stated in siman 252. In Eliyah Rabbah, however, he challenges the Magen Avraham on this point and concludes that with a letter, utensils, or money, it is permitted even if no price was fixed, since nowadays we do not have a full-fledged public domain; see there.
That he will go on Shabbos. He must also remove it from the Jew's house before nightfall. It is forbidden to say, “See that it is there on Sunday or Monday,” when it is known that this is impossible unless he travels on Shabbos; even if a price was fixed, it is forbidden. Magen Avraham.
Postal station. This means that whenever there is no postal station, and the letter must be delivered personally into the hands of the addressee, one must be concerned that the person may not be at home and that the messenger will follow him on Shabbos. A postal official, however, is permanently stationed for this task. This is what is called a postmaster.
To the wall. To the city to which it was sent, meaning the first house within it. Bach; Magen Avraham.
On Thursday. This is permitted even when he knows that the messenger will be unable to reach the destination before Shabbos. It appears to me that even according to the first opinion, if the journey is short, such as a one-day journey, and he sends it on Monday or Tuesday, it is permitted. Even if the addressee is not at home, the messenger still has ample time to go after him. Magen Avraham.
