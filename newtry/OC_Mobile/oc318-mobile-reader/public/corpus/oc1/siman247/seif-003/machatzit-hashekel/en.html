Seif katan 3: the house adjacent, and so forth. The first house. The Bach writes that even if we know that the postal office is at the far end of the town, we rule leniently on the possibility that the official may be found in the first house. One must explain why here we rely leniently on a doubt, whereas where there is no established postal office we do not rely on such a doubt and instead are concerned that he may not find the recipient at home.
He rejects what the Beit Yosef wrote, namely, that an established postal office in the town means in the town where the Jew lives, from which all letters are sent to the official. According to that explanation, the requirement that the messenger reach the house adjacent to the wall means, stringently, that before Shabbos he must reach the house adjacent to the wall at the opposite end, namely the end of the town, since he may not find the recipient at home and the recipient may be in another house at the far edge of the town.
