Seif katan 5: although, and so forth. The wording implies that this is obvious, and so forth. Since it states, “although, and so forth, it is forbidden,” it implies that there is another case in which the prohibition is even more evident. The ruling means: not only in that other case is it certainly forbidden, but even though he intends, and so forth, it is nevertheless forbidden. What is the case in which prohibition is more evident? It must mean where the gentile does not anticipate reciprocal compensation but acts gratuitously. The Magen Avraham therefore concludes by referring to siman 4, seif katan 7, where it is necessarily evident that the Rama did not interpret the Beit Yosef's words in this manner.
