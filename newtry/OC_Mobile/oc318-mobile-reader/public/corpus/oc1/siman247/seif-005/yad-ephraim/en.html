Seif katan 8. “Even if he pays him wages,” etc. See what Eliyah Rabbah writes concerning this. The Magen Avraham's statement that the law of techum is likewise D'Rabbanan means as follows. At first glance one could have distinguished between a non-Jew who travels as the Jew's agent, in which case the Jew instructs him to go beyond the techum, and a non-Jew who travels on his own initiative regardless. The Magen Avraham therefore says: what difference does it make that he travels on his own? By giving him the letter to carry from one domain to another, the non-Jew performs that labor for the Jew. Although we do not have a full-fledged public domain and the prohibition is only D'Rabbanan, namely carrying in a karmelis, it is nevertheless forbidden. This is comparable to your ruling that it is more severe when he travels as the Jew's agent than when he travels on his own, because when he acts as the Jew's agent the prohibition of techum is incurred through that agency. Since even a D'Rabbanan prohibition is forbidden in that case, so too, when he travels on his own but the Jew gives him a letter, he performs a D'Rabbanan labor for the Jew and it is forbidden. The Magen Avraham's further reference to siman 266, where an allowance is made because of financial loss, means that since the Jew faces a loss, we are concerned that he will not restrain himself regarding his property. The Sages therefore decreed lest, because of his loss, he carry the object four cubits in a public domain.
