For a year, etc. - The reason is that although he fixes his wages for the entire year, this is nevertheless not regarded as a fixed-price arrangement. The principal reason that fixing a price is effective is that the Jew has no practical interest in the work being done on this particular day, since if it is not done today it can be done another day, and the non-Jew works for his own benefit in order to hasten completion of his undertaking. Here, however, since he is hired for all kinds of work, it is almost certain that he will be needed for some other task on the following day. The Jew therefore benefits from the non-Jew's labor on Shabbos, and the non-Jew is considered his agent for this purpose. According to this reasoning, the Mechaber should have forbidden it even from Sunday, consistently with his ruling above in seif 1 where no price was fixed. However, because the proposition that such an arrangement is not considered fixed-price is not entirely settled, as appears in the Beit Yosef above in siman 244 and here in this siman, he relied in this case upon the permissive authorities cited by the Rama in seif 1 and prohibited it only on Friday.
