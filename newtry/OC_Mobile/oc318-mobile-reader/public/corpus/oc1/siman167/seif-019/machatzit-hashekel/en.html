He therefore had to explain that the throat is not free, etc. Nevertheless, Tosafos in Pesachim, cited above at the end of siman 166, write according to Rabbi Yochanan that those who recited Kiddush in shul need not recite a new blessing over wine when they drink at home, because changing location is not an interruption. Tosafos add that although everyone fulfills Kiddush when even one person drinks, to be exempt from a new blessing over wine at home each listener must actually drink from the Kiddush cup. Otherwise, there is an interruption between the blessing and his drinking. Thus, although the one reciting Kiddush tasted, it is still considered an interruption for the listeners who did not taste.

Furthermore, throughout the year, etc. If we infer from the fact that bread is not placed before each person throughout the year that one may interrupt, why is salt placed before each person throughout the year? Necessarily, this is to prevent an interruption. We see that throughout the year as well, the Sages were concerned and instituted a measure to avoid interruption.
Until the one who breaks bread gives him, etc. Although salt is placed before each person so that there not be a lengthy interruption while he asks for salt after receiving his portion, and because placing a little salt before everyone involves little effort, they required it. Placing bread before each person, however, involves greater effort, and the interruption until the one who breaks bread gives him a portion is brief. Therefore, they were not concerned throughout the year.
Therefore they doubled the expression, etc., immediately after the blessing, at once. The words "immediately" and "at once" are synonymous. They are doubled to emphasize that the act should follow the blessing as closely as possible.
