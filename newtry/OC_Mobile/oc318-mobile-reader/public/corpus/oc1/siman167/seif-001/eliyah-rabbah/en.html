[Levush] And they attached it by a kal vachomer, etc. In Torat Chaim on Sanhedrin 102 I found a significant allusion in breaking the bread for HaMotzi. Just as bikkurim are separated from the standing crop, terumah from grain, and challah from dough, so after baking one separates the first portion for Hashem. Therefore one should cut from the place that baked first, just as with bikkurim the Sages say that a person enters his field and sees the first fig to ripen. This is also why it is a mitzvah to break a whole loaf, because then he separates the first portion of the bread. If part has already been cut away, what remains is merely leftover. Another reason is that the table resembles the altar, and everything associated with the altar had to be perfectly whole. The altar itself required whole stones, and both kohen and offering were invalidated by a blemish. Therefore the cup for Birkat HaMazon should also be whole, the wine should not be defective, and the cup should be full.

However, in the responsa Shaar Ephraim I found that as long as the burned part has not been cut away, it is called a whole loaf for lechem mishneh. If challah was removed from a whole loaf, its law is like a sliced loaf. I elaborated to prove this in a responsum at the end of my Eliyahu Zuta. The Magen Avraham wrote that with our bread one cuts above. It seems that this refers to where dirt is stuck below, and so it appears from the Bach. It also seems that this speaks of a slice, for with a whole loaf one should cut around it, though this can also be done with a slice. The precise way of cutting and marking before the blessing on Shabbos and Yom Tov is discussed by the Maharshal, Bach, Toras Chaim, and Maharil. The Maharil would prepare the cut so that the remainder was still recognizable at the time of the blessing. Regarding the size of the HaMotzi piece, there are views in the Yerushalmi whether it is a kezayis or less than a kezayis. In practice, whether for oneself or for others, one should give a kezayis, thereby satisfying all opinions. The Maharil's custom of eating half a kezayis has support from Sefer Yereim that less than half a kezayis is nothing. At a large meal, the piece distributed should come from the slice separated for HaMotzi. The Magen Avraham wrote that even with a slice one should not cut completely before the blessing, so that it appear larger. The Darkei Moshe wrote that one should break opposite the split, for that is where it began to bake. The Shiyarei Knesses HaGedolah wrote amounts of pieces corresponding to the Torah readers: on Shabbos seven, on Yom Tov five, on Rosh Chodesh and Chol HaMoed four, and on a weekday and at Shabbos Minchah three.

The laws of Maharann state that one who leaves space between the words bread and from the earth is assured that he will not be harmed at that meal. Avudraham explains that HaMotzi bread means food, as in the phrase making bread, since bread itself does not come from the earth, but grain from which bread is made. Similarly with the manna, it says that Hashem would rain down bread, although actual bread did not fall. Toldos Yitzchak explains that we bless HaMotzi bread because in the future Hashem will bring forth actual bread, as at the end of Kesubos, that Eretz Yisrael will produce ready cakes. This also explains why we say bread from the earth and not from the ground, since earth alludes to Eretz Yisrael. For this reason the Bach wrote that we say HaMotzi, which can also imply the future, as an allusion to the future. This is the meaning of the statement that from a person's blessings it is recognizable whether he is a Torah scholar. Avudraham also explains that the wording follows the verse, and Kol Bo writes that it is considered as if He brought forth bread, since the wheat kernel contains no true waste, for even the bran is fit with it, as the poor eat it.

The Shelah wrote that one should have more intent in the table blessing than in other blessings, because food and drink bring a person to coarseness of nature and haughtiness of heart; therefore he must contemplate so that his heart is drawn toward desire for Hashem, blessed is He, and not toward the desire for food and drink. Maharil wrote in the laws of Shabbos that whenever he mentioned the Divine Name in the HaMotzi blessing, he raised the bread with both hands until he finished mentioning the Name, and then lowered it as before; on a weekday he raised the bread, and on Shabbos the two loaves.
[Levush] and it is possible, etc. The phrase dry bread implies that only where he intends to eat dry, unclean bread does it require salt, but in the first case, clean bread such as ours, there is no mitzvah at all. From the Rama it implies that the mitzvah applies to all cases, and so I found explicitly in Mateh Moshe. Perhaps the Levush's further statement, that the verse says, etc., also refers to the first case. From the Rama it implies that he is permitted to wait, whereas from the Levush it seems possible that there is an obligation with clean bread. What is said in Berachos, that this does not require waiting, refers after he has already blessed HaMotzi. See Responsa Beis Yaakov, siman 168.
