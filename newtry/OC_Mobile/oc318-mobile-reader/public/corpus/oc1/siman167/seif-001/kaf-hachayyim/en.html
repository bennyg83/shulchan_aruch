1) [Seif 1] One should break the bread at the place where it is well baked. The holy Zohar, parashas Eikev, folio 272b, similarly writes that one must break it from the place where it was thoroughly baked, because baking completes the fruit, and this corresponds to Vav. At the beginning of folio 121b in parashas Mishpatim, it writes that 'the place where it was thoroughly baked' excludes burnt bread and means the place that has the best flavor; see there.
2) There: At the place where it is well baked. Maseches Derech Eretz Rabbah, chapter 6, teaches: a person should not break the HaMotzi bread from the soft place, but from the hard place. The Beit Yosef cites this. The Levush writes the same, as do Magen Avraham, paragraph 1, and Shulchan Aruch HaRav, paragraph 2. Even one who prefers the soft place should break the bread only from the hard place. Although we generally say that a preferred food takes precedence, this case is different because we seek to demonstrate the bread's desirability. Halachos Ketanos, volume 2, siman 188; Beis Menuchah, paragraph 14.
3) However, an elderly person who cannot eat from the hard portion should break the bread from the soft portion. Halachos Ketanos there; Yad Aharon in Hagahot HaTur; Beis Menuchah there. If this elderly person is the host or the leading person at the meal, who must break bread for the others, one should break a portion for him from the soft place and portions for the others from the hard place. Since they intended to fulfill their obligation through his blessing and he broke portions for them from the hard place, the act of breaking was likewise performed at the hard place. See Beis Menuchah, paragraphs 15 and 16, regarding this. According to what we have written, the matter is clear. Examine this carefully.
4) There: At the place where it is well baked. Hagahos Maimoniyos, chapter 7 of Hilchos Berachos, writes in the name of Sefer HaMitzvos that one should break the bread from its underside, because that side bakes first when it adheres to the hot oven. This is the custom in France. In Germany, however, the custom is to break it from the upper side, because they say that side bakes first. R' Meir would make one cut from both sides in order to satisfy all opinions. The Beit Yosef cites this. This is what the Rama, of blessed memory, means when he writes, 'With our bread, one should break it at the side,' etc. Our bread is thick, and a person can break it from either the upper or lower side. Therefore, he should break it from the side and cut through both the upper and lower portions in order to satisfy all opinions. The distinction made by the Taz, paragraph 2, between their bread, which was baked in an oven, and our bread, which was baked on the oven floor, is astonishing. Even regarding their bread there is a dispute as to which side bakes first, as written in siman 254, seif 5, and the Taz there, paragraph 2; see there. Maamar Mordechai, paragraph 3, raises the same question and leaves the Taz's words requiring further study; see there. The later authorities write without qualification that for every loaf one should make a single cut through both the upper and lower sides in order to satisfy all opinions. Levush; Bach, who writes that this is the custom; Siddur Beis Oved in Mekor Chaim, paragraph 1; Shulchan Aruch HaRav, paragraph 2; Chayei Adam, klal 42, paragraph 11. It appears that when one begins cutting the bread before the blessing, he should begin from the bottom and continue until he reaches the upper side. He should not complete the cut at the upper side, so that the loaf appears completely whole from above when he recites the blessing. After the blessing, he should break the portion still attached above, leaving it attached strongly enough that if one holds the smaller portion, the larger loaf rises with it, as stated in the Shulchan Aruch. See below, paragraph 11.
5) There, in the gloss: He should cut slightly from the upper and lower sides. The same applies if he has no whole loaf and a piece of bread is brought before him. The Beit Yosef writes in the name of Rashi, of blessed memory, that he should not break it and recite the blessing from its middle, but from the edge of the bread; see there. He should cut one piece from the intact side, through both the upper and lower sides, in order to satisfy all opinions. See below, paragraph 12, and siman 168, paragraph 16.
6) There: He should cut slightly, etc. One should cut opposite the split in the loaf, because the dough was pressed until it split on the side opposite the place where baking began. Darkei Moshe, paragraph 1, in the name of Or Zarua; Magen Avraham, paragraph 2; Eliyah Rabbah, paragraph 2; Shulchan Aruch HaRav there.
7) There: He should cut the portion, etc. The Tur writes that some do not wish to cut the bread at all before the blessing, so that it will be completely whole when the blessing is recited. My master and father, the Rosh, of blessed memory, writes that this does not appear correct, because the delay while cutting the bread constitutes an interruption between the blessing and eating. Rather, he should cut it but not separate the portion until after the blessing. This is from Tosafos on Berachos 39b; see there. It follows that the requirement to cut before the blessing applies specifically to thick, hard bread that takes time to cut and is cut with a knife, as is known. Soft or thin bread, which can be torn by hand without delay, should be cut only after the blessing of HaMotzi, so that it remains completely whole during the blessing. I afterward saw that Shulchan Aruch HaRav, end of paragraph 3, and Kitzur Shulchan Aruch, siman 41, end of paragraph 3, write the same.
8) The Levush writes that if one has a whole loaf, it is a mitzvah to break bread from a whole loaf so that he blesses Hashem, may He be blessed, over the whole loaf that He gave him. This is a manner of honor and praise for Him, may He be blessed. Eliyah Rabbah, paragraph 2, comments that for this reason he heard that one should not break bread from a loaf whose portion was burned until it is inedible. If there is no other whole loaf, it is better to cut off the burned portion, because it is not respectful. However, he found in Responsa Shaar Efraim, siman 1, that as long as the burned portion has not been cut away, the loaf is considered whole regarding lechem mishneh; see there. Responsa Chacham Tzvi, siman 62; Beit David, siman 80; and Makom Shmuel, siman 48, write the same. Erech HaShulchan, siman 274, paragraph 1, however, disagrees with Shaar Efraim and writes that we rule that anything standing to be cut is considered already cut; see there. Since the matter is disputed, if one has another loaf, he should not use the burned loaf for HaMotzi or for lechem mishneh. If he has no other loaf, the better practice is not to remove the burned portion until after the blessing of HaMotzi, since many authorities consider it a whole loaf. When he cuts the HaMotzi portion, however, he should cut from the well-baked place. If scraping off the burned area would still leave the loaf looking whole, he should scrape it beforehand.
9) There: He should cut the portion for breaking. This is an initial preference, to avoid even a slight interruption. After the fact, however, it is obvious that cutting is not considered an interruption at all, just as saying 'take and bless' does not require repeating a blessing. Know this, because the Rosh did not disagree with Tosafos' statement that on Shabbos it is proper to recite the blessing before cutting. This proves that cutting after the blessing is not an interruption. Bach; Shiyurei Knesset HaGedolah, Hagahot HaTur, paragraph 1. The Taz, paragraph 3, likewise writes that it is only the most preferable practice; see there. Shulchan Aruch HaRav, paragraph 3, writes the same.
10) There: So that if he holds the portion, etc. The Rosh writes this in chapter Keitzad Mevarchin and brings proof from what we learned in chapter 3 of Tevul Yom. R' Meir says that if one holds the larger part and the smaller part rises with it, the smaller part is considered like the larger. R' Yehuda says that if one holds the smaller part and the larger rises with it, the smaller is considered like the larger. The halachah follows R' Yehuda. Hagahos Maimoniyos, chapter 7 of Hilchos Berachos, similarly writes this in the name of Rabbeinu Peretz. The Beit Yosef cites it and writes that although Hagahos Maimoniyos afterward cites R' Baruch, who says that one need not be concerned about this, he is a lone authority against Rabbeinu Peretz and the Rosh; see there. Therefore, the Shulchan Aruch rules this way, and the later authorities agree.
11) There: So that if he holds the portion, etc. On weekdays, Maharil, of blessed memory, would cut a ring-shaped cut, leaving the center attached. He would break the piece from bottom to top. On Shabbos, he customarily used a thin semolina loaf for breaking bread. Since it was thin, it did not require a preliminary cut. He placed his eight fingers on its upper surface and his two thumbs underneath, recited HaMotzi, and at the conclusion of the blessing bent it from below upward until it broke. Derashos Maharil, laws of HaMotzi; Shiyurei Knesset HaGedolah, Hagahot HaTur, paragraph 4; Olas Tamid, paragraph 2; Magen Avraham, paragraph 2; Eliyah Rabbah, paragraph 2. See above, end of paragraph 4. Below, in paragraph 32, we will explain according to the Arizal how one should hold the bread during the blessing on weekdays and on Shabbos; see there.
12) There: So that if he holds the portion, etc. Even when he has only a piece, he should not cut it completely before the blessing, so that it will appear larger. This is the custom. Magen Avraham, paragraph 4; Eliyah Rabbah there; Yad Aharon in Hagahot HaTur. If challah was separated from a whole loaf, its law is that of a piece. Eliyah Rabbah there. See above, paragraph 5, and below, siman 168, paragraph 10.
13) There: He should leave it attached to the loaf and begin the blessing. During the blessing itself, however, it is forbidden to cut, because one may not occupy himself with anything while reciting a blessing. Even a minor task is forbidden, as written below in siman 191, seif 3, and in what we write there, with Heaven's help.
14) There: And he should begin the blessing. If one recited the blessing over a loaf intending to cut it on one side, but was unsuccessful and had to cut it from another side, Riaz holds that he must repeat the blessing. Before repeating it, he must say regarding the first blessing, 'Blessed is the Name of His glorious kingdom forever and ever.' Shiltei HaGibborim, chapter 3 of Berachos, cites him; Knesset HaGedolah, Hagahot HaTur. Olas Tamid, paragraph 2, however, writes that this requires practical study. Maamar Mordechai, paragraph 4, likewise writes that according to what is written below in siman 206, seif 6, it is obvious that he need not repeat the blessing. This is true not only according to the Rama's words there, but even according to Maran's view; see his full discussion. See above, siman 140, seif 3, and what we wrote there in paragraph 15, and examine carefully. Birkei Yosef, paragraph 12, cited by Shaarei Teshuvah, paragraph 22, writes that one who recited HaMotzi and then dropped the portion from his hand should break another portion and repeat the blessing. We do not rule this way, because the matter is disputed in siman 206, seif 6, and we rule that a doubt concerning blessings is treated leniently. Rather, he should break another portion without a blessing, since he intended to eat more from this bread.
15) There: He should not break a small portion, etc. Regarding the size of the portion, some say it should be an olive's volume and some say less than an olive's volume. Rokeach in the name of the Yerushalmi; Beit Yosef. The Rama, of blessed memory, writes in the gloss that one should give each person an olive's volume, implying that he rules according to the opinion that the HaMotzi portion must be an olive's volume. This also appears at the beginning of siman 475, which requires an olive's volume for HaMotzi, and it must be eaten continuously without interruption; see there. Olas Tamid, paragraph 11, similarly writes in the name of the Shelah that before speaking one must eat an olive's volume, for less than an olive's volume is not considered eating; see there. Shaar HaMitzvos, parashas Eikev, likewise writes that the HaMotzi portion must be an olive's volume; see there. Magen Avraham, paragraph 7, writes the same and says that one should initially be stringent; see there. So too the Bach in siman 475; Yad Aharon in Hagahot Beit Yosef to this siman; Yafeh LaLev, paragraph 2. This applies when one intends to eat more, in which case he should initially eat an olive's volume continuously after the blessing of HaMotzi. Regarding the blessing itself, however, even less than an olive's volume requires HaMotzi, as written below in siman 168, seif 9. Dagul Mervavah writes the same; see there. It is obvious that Dagul Mervavah refers to the initial preference, since his comment addresses Magen Avraham. After the fact, once one has recited the blessing and tasted even a small amount, if he then interrupted with speech or the like, he need not repeat the blessing. This is unlike Beis Menuchah, paragraph 16, who discusses the matter at unnecessary length; see there. See below, paragraph 45.
16) There, in the gloss: On Shabbos he should not cut, etc. The same applies on Yom Tov. Shulchan Aruch HaRav, paragraph 3. The reason is that his hand might slip and completely sever the portion before he finishes the blessing, leaving him without lechem mishneh. Levush. Tosafos on Berachos 39b write the same. Tashbetz, siman 323, however, gives another reason: when he cuts, crumbs fall from the loaf and it is no longer whole. Eliyah Rabbah, paragraph 3. The Mordechai there in chapter Keitzad Mevarchin writes that perhaps the piece will fall and he will no longer have lechem mishneh. Magen Avraham, paragraph 5, cites him and writes that this implies that if the piece did not completely fall away, it would still be lechem mishneh. The Agudah on Chullin, siman 150, however, writes in the name of Maharan that it is considered lechem mishneh only when one holds the cut portion and the loaf rises with it. If he cut more deeply, it is no longer lechem mishneh. Magen Avraham writes that this also appears from Shiltei HaGibborim there on the Mordechai and from the Rama's wording that if he cut more deeply, it is considered a piece; see there. See above, paragraph 10.
17) There, in the gloss: Until after the blessing, etc. The reason is that Shabbos requires lechem mishneh. Although cutting between the blessing and eating could constitute an interruption, since it is not a complete interruption and is done in honor of Shabbos so that the loaves remain lechem mishneh, the Sages were lenient. Olas Tamid, paragraph 3. See above, paragraph 9.
18) There: Until after the blessing, etc. Those who are meticulous customarily score the loaf with a knife before the blessing. Bach in the name of Maharshal; Ateres Zekeinim; Magen Avraham, siman 274, paragraph 1; Eliyah Rabbah, paragraph 2. Yeshua Yaakov, however, writes that this is improper. Tzelach in his novellae to Berachos 39b likewise writes that it is better not to score the loaf at all. Beis Menuchah, paragraph 18, cites him; see there. Therefore, the proper practice appears to be to prepare thin loaves for HaMotzi on Shabbos so that one will not need to score them with a knife before the blessing and will not delay cutting between the blessing and eating. On weekdays as well, one should do this when possible, so that he blesses over a completely whole loaf. See above, paragraph 11.
19) There: He must give from the portion, etc. Meaning, the primary mitzvah is to give each person bread from the portion he cut, rather than from the loaf that remains. One must take care with this at a large meal. Mateh Moshe and Maharil; Magen Avraham, paragraph 6. The words of the Arizal, of blessed memory, in Shaar HaKavanos, folio 72b, however, imply that one should cut from the loaf over which he recited HaMotzi and give to the others, rather than giving from the portion he cut for himself; see there. Although that passage discusses Shabbos, there is no distinction in this respect between Shabbos and weekdays. If one were required to give everyone from the first portion, it would have said that he should cut a large piece, take an olive's volume from it for himself and an egg's volume for his wife, and then distribute to the other diners. Instead, it implies that the first portion should be solely for him. For the others, he returns and cuts a separate portion for each person from the loaf itself over which he recited HaMotzi. See the following paragraph and examine carefully.
20) Shiyurei Knesset HaGedolah writes in Hagahot HaTur, paragraph 6: 'How many pieces should one cut at HaMotzi? I found the mnemonic hazag: five pieces on Friday night, corresponding to the five words in the verse May Hashem shine His countenance; seven pieces at the morning meal, corresponding to the verse May Hashem raise His countenance; and three pieces at the third meal, corresponding to the verse May Hashem bless you. I further saw it written that on Shabbos one cuts seven pieces at every meal, corresponding to the seven people called to the Torah on Shabbos. On Yom Tov one cuts five pieces, corresponding to the five people called to the Torah. On Rosh Chodesh and Chol HaMoed, one cuts four pieces, corresponding to the four aliyos. On weekdays, one cuts three pieces, corresponding to the three aliyos. Some also say that at the third Shabbos meal one cuts three pieces because it follows Minchah, when three people are called to the Torah.' Eliyah Rabbah cites this at the end of paragraph 2. According to the Arizal, of blessed memory, however, there is no basis for this. Shaar HaKavanos, folio 72b, states: 'Afterward, break bread from the right-hand loaf, which corresponds to the Yod of Havayah and to Chochmah, from which the finest nourishment is drawn. First break an olive's volume and eat it yourself. Intend that this olive's volume corresponds to the Yod of Havayah in Zeir Anpin. Then break a second portion, the volume of an egg, and give it to your wife. Intend that it corresponds to the final Yod of Adnus in the Nukva of Zeir Anpin, and intend that they unite to form the interwoven Name. Afterward, break bread and give it to the other diners seated there.' The requirement to give one's wife an egg's volume applies specifically on Friday night, because illumination is then drawn to the aspect of Malchus, as stated in Shaar HaKavanos there. During the Shabbos day meal, however, one should break for her only an olive's volume, like the other diners. It follows that one should break portions only for himself, his wife, and the diners if any are present. If there are no other diners, he should not break additional portions. This is so on Shabbos, and all the more so on weekdays, when he should not break more than an olive's volume if there are no other diners.
