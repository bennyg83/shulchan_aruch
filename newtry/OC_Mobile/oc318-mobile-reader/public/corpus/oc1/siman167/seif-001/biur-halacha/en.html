And with our bread. He specified our bread because, according to one reading of the Gemara, Hamotzi should be recited over the place where the bread began to bake first. The ovens used in the time of the Gemara were constructed so that the bread was attached to the oven walls. It was understood that both the upper part of the loaf and the lower part attached to the oven wall began baking at the same time. One could therefore break the loaf wherever he wished, either from its upper or lower part. In our ovens, however, the bread rests on the oven floor, which is heated first by the fire beneath it. Some say the bread begins baking first on the lower side, while others say the upper side bakes first because it faces the hot air. Rama therefore writes that before completing the blessing one should cut slightly from both the upper and lower sides, meaning from the side of the loaf, so as to satisfy both views. See Levushei Serad and Beur HaGra, who question this explanation, since the same disagreement about which part bakes first would also apply to the ovens of the Gemara. They therefore leave Rama's use of the word "our" requiring study. Know also that although Rama states his ruling without qualification, it has the status of a minority opinion. According to the Mechaber, who rules that one should break from the place that baked well, the principal requirement is only to break from a hard part, whether from the upper or lower side, and not from the middle. The Mechaber follows the final version in Rashi, which reads that one breaks from where the bread is well crusted. Rama follows the authorities whose version reads that one breaks from where it baked first, as Beur HaGra explains. The later authorities accepted Rama's ruling because it satisfies all opinions.
He must cut slightly. See Mishnah Berurah. Accordingly, it is obvious that where breaking the bread after the blessing will cause no delay, such as with a small, thin loaf, there is no need to cut it at all before eating. Shulchan Aruch HaRav writes likewise.
