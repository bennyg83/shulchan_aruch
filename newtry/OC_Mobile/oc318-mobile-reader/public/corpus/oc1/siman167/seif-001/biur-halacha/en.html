(א) And with our bread—he specified our bread because in the gemara [one girsa] one must bless hamotzi from where the bread began to bake. Behold, the ovens in the time of Shulchan Aruch—their way was to stick the bread to the oven walls; and they held that there, between the top part of the loaf and the bottom stuck to the oven walls, both begin baking at one time; therefore he breaks from wherever he wishes, from the top of the loaf or the bottom of the loaf. Unlike our ovens, where the bread is placed on the oven floor, which heats first from the fire beneath it: some say the bread begins baking there first; some say the upper side bakes first, since it faces the air. Therefore Rama wrote he should cut a little from the top and bottom [that is, from the sides of the bread] before completing the berachah, to fulfill both views; see Levush and Beur HaGra, who wondered on this—for those two opinions disputing which place bakes first apply also to ovens of the Talmudic era, and Rama's mention of our bread remains with difficulty. Know also: although Rama mentioned his words in general, it is as if written, and some say—for the Mechaber's view, who rules where it baked well we are particular, the main point is only that he break from the hard place on the top of the loaf or its bottom, not from its middle; he rules like the last version in Rashi, who has in the gemara from where the bottom crust formed; and Rama holds like those who have in the gemara from where it began on the bottom crust, as Gra and Acharonim wrote. Acharonim codified Rama's words that with this we fulfill all views.
