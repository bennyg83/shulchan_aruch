One breaks the bread at the place, etc. This means that after washing his hands for eating, when he wishes to bless on the bread and eat, he breaks it for the honor of the blessing at the place where it baked well, meaning the hard place and not the soft middle.
And with our bread. See the Beur HaGra, who questions the wording our bread, since the same law applies to their ovens as well, where they would attach the bread to the sides of the oven. See Biur Halacha.
At the side of the bread. The Gemara states, according to one reading, that one breaks from the place where the bread begins to crust and bake. Some authorities hold that this is the upper side of the bread facing the air of the oven, and some say the lower side cooks first because it rests on the floor of the oven; therefore he should cut from the side in a way that includes the upper and lower parts and thereby satisfies both. Some say it is good to cut at the side opposite the split, for on that side it began to bake and the dough was pressed until the opposite side split. With a slice, he may break at the cut place and need not break at the whole side, but he should cut from the upper and lower side. If the loaf was burned in one place, see the later authorities.
And he should cut, and must, etc. The word must explains what was written initially, that he should cut. Strictly, one blesses and afterward slices the loaf, so that the whole blessing is on a complete loaf, which honors the blessing. Yet in order that there not be a long delay between the blessing and eating, which would appear as an interruption, the authorities wrote that initially it is good to cut slightly into the loaf before beginning the blessing, with the amount of cutting being slight as explained below, then bless, and afterward finish separating the slice from the bread.
Slightly. Even with a slice, he should not cut it completely before the blessing, so that it appears larger.
For otherwise, etc. Meaning, if he cuts into the loaf so deeply that when he holds the slice the loaf does not rise with it and it breaks into two, it is considered sliced and separated from the loaf. Even though if he holds the loaf the slice rises with it, this does not make it considered whole.
And he should leave it, etc. This returns to what was written at the beginning, that he cuts the HaMotzi slice.
Small. Meaning smaller than a kezayis, as stated by the Levush. See Beur HaGra and Eliyah Rabbah.
More than a kebeitzah. This is when he slices it in order to eat immediately from that slice itself. But this does not include cutting a large piece from the loaf and then cutting from that piece itself a small piece for HaMotzi, for that does not appear like gluttony, as explained below at the end of this siman in the Taz.
And on Shabbos. The same applies on Yom Tov. Since one must bless over lechem mishneh, we are concerned that the whole slice may break off before the blessing, or at least that he may cut so deeply that when he holds the slice the loaf will not rise with it, making it fully sliced, while we require lechem mishneh. Therefore it is better not to cut at all. Although this will cause some delay between the blessing and eating due to the cutting, it is not considered an interruption, since it is for the sake of eating; only on weekdays are we stringent to do the matter in the best manner.
It does not harm. Meaning, he has not thereby lost lechem mishneh, for whenever he holds the slice and the rest of the loaf rises with it, it is considered whole.
But on Shabbos, etc. Meaning, on Shabbos it does not appear like gluttony, for he does this out of love for the Shabbos meal, to increase the meal.
Or if he eats, etc. Meaning, even on a weekday.
From the slice. Meaning, the primary mitzvah initially, when discharging others through his blessing, is to give bread from the slice that he cut to each one of them and not from the remaining loaf. One should be careful about this at a large meal, when one person discharges all through his blessing, and should cut a HaMotzi slice large enough to distribute from it to all the participants.
A kezayis to each one. So that he should not appear stingy, as above at the end of the seif. In Darkei Moshe the wording is a kezayis or slightly less. Some say further that, aside from this reason, it is also good initially for a person to eat a kezayis at the time of the HaMotzi blessing.
