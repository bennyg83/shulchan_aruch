The place and time of breaking the bread, and who breaks it – containing 20 sections: One should break the bread at the place where it is well baked. He should cut the piece to be broken, and he must cut it slightly, so that if he were to hold the slice, the rest of the loaf would be lifted with it. For if not, it is considered as already sliced. He should leave it attached to the loaf and begin the blessing, and after he concludes the blessing, he should separate it—so that the blessing concludes while the bread is still whole. And he should not break off a piece that is too small, because that appears stingy, nor a piece larger than the size of a kebeitza (an egg), because that appears gluttonous. {Rama: (And with our bread, one should break it on the side of the loaf and cut slightly from the top and bottom—Hagahot Maimoni, chapter 7 of Hilchot Berachot). Rema: On Shabbat, one should not cut the loaf before the blessing, so that the loaves remain whole (Tosafot and Mordechai, chapter "Keitzad Mevarchin"). Nevertheless, if he forgot and cut it as on a weekday, it is not harmful (Mordechai). And it seems that the restriction not to break more than a kebeitza applies only on weekdays and when eating alone, but on Shabbat or when eating with many people—and he must give each person a kezayit—it is permitted to break off as much as needed. See also Siman 274.}
