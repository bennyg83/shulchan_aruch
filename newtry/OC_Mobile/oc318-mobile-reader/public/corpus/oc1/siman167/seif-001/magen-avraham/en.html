In the place where it was baked. Meaning, in the hard place, and not in the soft place.
At the side of the bread. This follows the other explanation, which holds that one breaks from the place where the bread begins to crust and bake. Some say this is the upper side, and some say it is the lower side; therefore one satisfies both views. The Darkei Moshe writes in the name of the Or Zarua that this means opposite the split, for that side began to bake and the dough was pressed until the opposite side split. Rashi writes that with a sliced loaf one should break only from the edge of the bread and not from its middle. With our bread one should cut above; see what I wrote at the end of siman 168, seif 3. The Maharil would cut around the loaf like a ring, leaving it attached in the middle, and would then slice from bottom to top. With a thin cake, he would place his eight fingers above and his two thumbs below, recite the blessing, and slice.
And he should cut. For if he cuts only after the blessing, it appears as an interruption.
It is considered sliced. It seems to me that even with a slice one should not cut it fully before the blessing, so that it should appear larger. This is the practice; see the beginning of siman 168.
But on Shabbos he should not. For perhaps the slice will fall and he will not have lechem mishneh. This implies that if it did not fall completely it remains lechem mishneh. However, the Agudah in Chullin, siman 150, writes in the name of Maharam that this is only when, if one holds the slice, the loaf rises with it; if he cut more than that, it is not lechem mishneh. This also appears from Shiltei Gibborim on the Mordechai, from below in siman 168, and from the language of the Rama, who writes that cutting as on a weekday does not harm, implying that if he cut more, it is called sliced.
From the slice. Meaning, the primary mitzvah is to give bread from the slice that he cut to each person, and not from the remaining loaf. One must be careful about this at a large meal, as stated by Mateh Moshe and Maharil.
A kezayis to each one. At first glance this implies that there is an obligation to eat a kezayis, and so it appears at the beginning of siman 475, that one must press himself to eat two kezaysim at once; see what I wrote there. I have not seen people practice this. The Beit Yosef also wrote in the name of the Rokeach that less than a kezayis suffices. Nevertheless, one should be stringent initially.
