The laws of Maharann state that one who leaves space between the words bread and from the earth is assured that he will not be harmed at that meal. Avudraham explains that HaMotzi bread means food, as in the phrase making bread, since bread itself does not come from the earth, but grain from which bread is made. Similarly with the manna, it says that Hashem would rain down bread, although actual bread did not fall. Toldos Yitzchak explains that we bless HaMotzi bread because in the future Hashem will bring forth actual bread, as at the end of Kesubos, that Eretz Yisrael will produce ready cakes. This also explains why we say bread from the earth and not from the ground, since earth alludes to Eretz Yisrael. For this reason the Bach wrote that we say HaMotzi, which can also imply the future, as an allusion to the future. This is the meaning of the statement that from a person's blessings it is recognizable whether he is a Torah scholar. Avudraham also explains that the wording follows the verse, and Kol Bo writes that it is considered as if He brought forth bread, since the wheat kernel contains no true waste, for even the bran is fit with it, as the poor eat it.
