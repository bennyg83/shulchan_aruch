4) There: At the place where it is well baked. Hagahos Maimoniyos, chapter 7 of Hilchos Berachos, writes in the name of Sefer HaMitzvos that one should break the bread from its underside, because that side bakes first when it adheres to the hot oven. This is the custom in France. In Germany, however, the custom is to break it from the upper side, because they say that side bakes first. R' Meir would make one cut from both sides in order to satisfy all opinions. The Beit Yosef cites this. This is what the Rama, of blessed memory, means when he writes, 'With our bread, one should break it at the side,' etc. Our bread is thick, and a person can break it from either the upper or lower side. Therefore, he should break it from the side and cut through both the upper and lower portions in order to satisfy all opinions. The distinction made by the Taz, paragraph 2, between their bread, which was baked in an oven, and our bread, which was baked on the oven floor, is astonishing. Even regarding their bread there is a dispute as to which side bakes first, as written in siman 254, seif 5, and the Taz there, paragraph 2; see there. Maamar Mordechai, paragraph 3, raises the same question and leaves the Taz's words requiring further study; see there. The later authorities write without qualification that for every loaf one should make a single cut through both the upper and lower sides in order to satisfy all opinions. Levush; Bach, who writes that this is the custom; Siddur Beis Oved in Mekor Chaim, paragraph 1; Shulchan Aruch HaRav, paragraph 2; Chayei Adam, klal 42, paragraph 11. It appears that when one begins cutting the bread before the blessing, he should begin from the bottom and continue until he reaches the upper side. He should not complete the cut at the upper side, so that the loaf appears completely whole from above when he recites the blessing. After the blessing, he should break the portion still attached above, leaving it attached strongly enough that if one holds the smaller portion, the larger loaf rises with it, as stated in the Shulchan Aruch.
