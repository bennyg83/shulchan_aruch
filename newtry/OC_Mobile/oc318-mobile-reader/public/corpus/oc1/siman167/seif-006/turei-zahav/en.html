(א) Bring salt, etc.—it seems that even for us whose bread is kneaded with salt and one need not bring salt as written in Shulchan Aruch 168—nevertheless since it is pleasing to him to eat by dipping further in salt, certainly it is not interruption. And even though Bach wrote that it seems from Tosafot's words this would be interruption—I do not know from where he infers so, for Tosafot wrote only that one does not make prohibition if he did not bring since the bread is fine; but if it pleases him that they bring him salt, certainly it is not interruption—only that afterward Bach brought in name of Rashba's responsum that if his intent was initially to eat without relish and afterward he said bring relish—it is interruption and he must return and bless; Bach already rejected this from halachah because we hold even if he spoke in something not needed for the slice of motzi but for meal needs it is not interruption. And so it appears to me proven from gavil le-torai that it is not interruption. And Bach rejected this and said gavil le-torai applies to slice of motzi since it is forbidden to taste until he gives his animal—for there is no prohibition on mere tasting—for if so why did they say here forbidden to eat until he gives etc. and did not say forbidden to taste as this language in first chapter Shabbat daf 9—forbidden to taste until he prays, and forbidden to taste until he separates—but here they forbade only meal eating; and behold proof in scripture it is written and you shall eat and be satisfied—and in explanation they said on this and again and you shall eat and be satisfied—for ostensibly it is hard why he brings here and be satisfied unless to teach that initially it was only eating that brings to satiety; and so my teacher my father may his memory be blessed for berachah—not specifically for slice of motzi.

(ב) Already all fulfilled, etc.—this Bach brought in name of Rokeach and disputed him and rejected his proof with good reason—for Rokeach brings proof from that they bless and give infant to drink—and that is no proof for there he blesses intending that the infant drink alone and since he drank it is not berachah in vain; but here each is liable to bless in order to eat, and when one blesses for all it is as if each blesses for himself; and when he interrupts between his answering amen and his tasting it is interruption and he must return and bless—and this seems clear to me—end of his words. And indeed Bach's words are clear as sun at noon—for how can one say they fulfill through what this blesser ate of slice of motzi—behold we are in berachat hana'ain and each must bless on his benefit; and though what those reclining taste from slice of motzi is beloved mitzvah by itself not obligation—nevertheless obligation is upon him to bless on what he will eat afterward; and all who eat without berachah—it is transgression in his hand; and since he interrupted in speech before his benefit, the berachah of this one left and he benefited without berachah—and per force better his friend's berachah than his own. And further that through friend's berachah he fulfills he must answer amen as Rama wrote earlier; and if so the essence of his fulfilling is through saying amen and it is as if he blessed himself; and if so he must not interrupt between his amen and his eating. And Maharach brought proof to Rokeach's ruling from that Rashi explained on take, bless that it is not interruption—and one can say like Taz's enactment above; and in any case l'chatchila one should not interrupt as Rambam in Hagahah wrote even if the cutter ate.
