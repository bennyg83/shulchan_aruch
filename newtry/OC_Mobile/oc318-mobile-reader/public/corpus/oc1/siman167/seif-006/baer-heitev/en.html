For eating. One must eat the measure of a kezayis before speaking. If he placed the bread of HaMotzi into his mouth and chewed it, he may not speak until he swallows it. If he spoke while still chewing, he need not repeat the blessing. Shelah. See Magen Avraham and Halachos Ketanos, part 2, siman 33. See Taz, seif katan 6, who notes that the wording “to taste,” and so forth, was not used; see there. This requires study, for Gittin 62a states that one may not taste food until he has fed his animal. Rabbi Moshe Chagiz answered that a major rule in the Gemara is that one may rely only on a teaching stated in its proper context. The Taz therefore carefully asked why this was stated here, in the context of blessings. Examine this carefully. This is the text of the Baer Heitev before me.
Salt. Even according to our practice, where our bread is kneaded with salt, this nevertheless does not constitute an interruption. Taz.
An interruption. Taz and Magen Avraham disagree and rule that it does constitute an interruption. Perach Shoshan, Orach Chayim, principle 1, siman 14, rules similarly. Taz concludes that a person should conduct his household by instructing each individual to recite HaMotzi independently if he will speak before eating the bread of HaMotzi. The head of the household who breaks the bread should intend to discharge only those who will not speak before eating. Consequently, there is no concern of a blessing recited in vain. See there. See also Ginnas Veradim, principle 1, siman 43.
