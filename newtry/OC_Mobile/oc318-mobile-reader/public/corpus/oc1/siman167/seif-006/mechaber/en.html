He should eat immediately and not speak between the blessing and the eating. If he did speak, he must recite the blessing again—unless the speech was related to matters connected to what he blessed over. For example, if he blessed over the bread and before eating said,"Bring salt" or "Bring a condiment," or "Give so-and-so to eat," or "Give food to the animal," and the like—he does not need to bless again.Rema: Nevertheless, ideally one should not interrupt at all (Kol Bo). And the ruling that if he spoke idle words he must bless again applies only if the one who broke the bread spoke before he ate. But if he spoke afterward, it is not considered an interrupting speech—even if the other participants have not yet eaten—since they already fulfilled their obligation through the eating of the one who broke the bread. For they are not required to eat from his piece; they do so only to enhance the mitzvah (Rokeach and Or Zarua).
