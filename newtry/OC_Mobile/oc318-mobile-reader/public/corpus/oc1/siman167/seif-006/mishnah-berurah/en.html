He should eat immediately. Initially, it is forbidden to interrupt even with silence for longer than the time needed to speak, as below in siman 206, seif 3; see there.
And he should not speak. Until he finishes chewing a little of the slice and swallows it, since the Shulchan Aruch rules below at the end of siman 210 that one who merely tastes, if he did not swallow, does not need a blessing. If so, the essence of the blessing is on swallowing. Similarly, if many are present and each has his own loaf and blesses for himself, he should not answer Amen to his fellow's blessing before he tastes from his own blessing. Nevertheless, if he transgressed and spoke while chewing before swallowing the slice, about matters not needed for the meal, it requires study whether he must return and bless. The Chayei Adam inclines that even if he swallowed only the taste extracted in his mouth from the chewing, he need not return and bless. However, he need not specifically eat a kezayis before speaking; even if he ate less than a kezayis after the blessing, it is considered eating for this matter, and he may speak afterward if needed. Where there is no pressure, it is good first to eat a kezayis.
And if he spoke. Even one word.
Relevant matters. Even if he interrupted with words that are not needed for the HaMotzi slice itself but are for meal needs, and even if he spoke to bring utensils needed for the meal, it is not an interruption.
On which he blessed over the bread, etc. Even with our bread, which is clean and seasoned with spices, where the Shulchan Aruch ruled above that one need not wait until salt and relish are brought, nevertheless, since he wishes to eat the bread by dipping it in salt and relish, this is considered a meal matter.
Give so-and-so to eat. Whether he is poor or wealthy, and even if he did not say to give him from the HaMotzi slice, but told members of his household to give him a loaf so that he can bless for himself, nevertheless it is a meal matter.
Food for an animal. This too is a meal matter, for it is forbidden to taste before giving food to one's animal. The Magen Avraham wrote in the name of Sefer Chasidim that regarding drinking, a person precedes the animal, as it is written by Rivkah, who told the servant to drink and then said she would water his camels.
He should not interrupt at all. In all these matters; and even if the bread was not clean, so that he needs the salt, it is also forbidden to interrupt before he swallows a little.
Who spoke. This means the one breaking the bread before he ate.
But afterward, etc. Meaning, according to the Rama, even if the listeners, who were fulfilling their obligation through his blessing, spoke afterward before tasting, there is no concern after the fact, since the blessing of the one who blessed has already taken effect. Initially, however, each listener is certainly forbidden to interrupt before tasting. This is only the Rama's view, but nearly all later authorities disagree and hold that the listener is no better than the one blessing himself; just as if he spoke before tasting he returns and blesses, so too listeners who interrupted with speech before tasting return and bless. Know further that if the one blessing spoke before tasting, even if the listeners did not speak at all, it appears from the authorities that they no longer fulfill their obligation through that blessing, as I wrote in Biur Halacha, and the practical law requires study.
