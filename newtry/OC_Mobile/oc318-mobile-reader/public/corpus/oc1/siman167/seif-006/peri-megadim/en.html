Fine bread. See Taz. Berachos 40a states, “This does not require delay.” Rashi explains that “this” means this bread, and “delay” is like the expression “Moshe delayed”; the Gemara used a refined expression. It is possible that even if he intends to eat it with a relish, since fine bread does not require a relish we follow the ordinary practice of most people and he need not delay. From this it would follow that with coarse bread, which everyone eats with a relish, even if he intends to eat it dry his personal preference is disregarded, and he must bring a relish before reciting HaMotzi. Rambam, however, in chapter 7 of Hilchos Berachos, halachah 3, writes that the law depends upon his intention to eat it dry, following the Aruch’s interpretation. Beit Yosef appears to explain that the phrase “this does not require” refers to the person himself, who says: I do not need a relish, because I can eat even coarse bread without one, and his preference is not disregarded. If so, the wording of Shulchan Aruch, “fine bread, or if he intends to eat it dry,” is difficult, for it appears to combine two contradictory approaches. Bach already raised a similar difficulty and answered that Shulchan Aruch rules like Rambam, and the two cases are equivalent: regarding fine bread, everyone agrees that no relish is necessary, whereas with coarse bread we follow the person’s intention. Taz, however, understands the Rosh differently. The Rosh cites one interpretation that the word means delay and another that it means, “This does not require a relish.” Taz does not accept Beit Yosef’s explanation, for then the essential point is missing, and it should have said, “I do not intend to eat it with salt.” Rather, both interpretations discuss fine bread. What practical difference is there between them? According to the first interpretation, the Gemara says only that no prolonged delay is necessary, but if the relish is ready one must wait briefly even for fine bread. The word “this” means that this law, namely the need to wait, applies only briefly and not as an actual delay. According to the second interpretation, “this bread,” meaning fine bread, does not require any waiting at all, whereas bread that is not fine requires waiting and delay. Tur follows the second interpretation, that fine bread requires no waiting at all. Rambam likewise follows the second interpretation, for he says that if one intends to eat coarse bread dry he need not wait, and does not merely say that prolonged delay is unnecessary. This shows that no waiting at all is required. There is thus no practical disagreement between Tur and Rambam. Tur uses the Gemara’s formulation concerning fine bread, while Rambam speaks of the person’s intention. Shulchan Aruch therefore rules in accordance with both, and Bach’s difficulty is resolved. Heaven forbid that I should speak against our master, but there appears to be support for Bach. Rambam would not ordinarily omit what the Gemara explicitly states and instead introduce an entirely new case. Rather, Rambam follows the Aruch’s interpretation, as Beit Yosef explains at length, and the Rosh also intended the Aruch’s interpretation. Kesef Mishneh explains, “This does not require,” as referring to the bread, not to the person, meaning: I intend to eat it this way. See Beit Yosef. The word translated as “delay” may also mean a relish, and Derishah explains it as something tasteless. So too in the Targum to Iyov 6, “This does not require a relish in order that it not be called tasteless.” See there.
