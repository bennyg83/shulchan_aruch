[15] [Levush] he is satiated, etc.—Lechem Chamudot, daf 64, wondered on Levush—for this is Beit Yosef's language; Kolbo wrote: if he finished his meal and cannot eat more, he need not bless—it implies if he can eat more he returns and eats and blesses on it first; and this is possible per Raavad, when he forgot and put liquid in his mouth; but poskim do not agree with him, as will be explained in siman 172; therefore even if he can return and eat he need not; until here. Also his own words contradict—for at the end he wrote only when he wishes to eat, and per him the matter does not depend on will but on ability, and he should have written only when he is able; until here Lechem Chamudot; and similarly Mahari Yom Tov. And in my humble view I settle everything: certainly even if he is satiated, he can still eat a little; but he cracked on him—for since he is already satiated, and it is reasonable to Levush, who wrote like Kolbo and cannot eat, that he is completely satiated and cannot eat at all—on this Beit Yosef disagrees; but Beit Yosef agrees that if satiated, nevertheless we require that he still be able to eat; for if he is not satiated, even if he has no intent to eat more, he must bless and eat—and this is what Levush wrote, only when he wishes to eat; and so Bach understood Beit Yosef's words, for he wrote on Beit Yosef's words: and it appears even if his eating would not be coarse eating, cracking on food—even so, since he finished his meal, he need not bless again.
