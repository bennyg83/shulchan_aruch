But it is permitted to give, etc. This is astonishing to me. The dispute is that Tosafos, in the name of the Sar of Coucy, permits the participants to taste first if the one breaking bread has already given them their portions, whereas Rambam and Tur make no distinction and hold that in every case they may not taste first unless each is eating from his own loaf. Beit Yosef and Rama rule stringently. Nevertheless, one should not initially permit the one breaking bread to distribute their portions before he eats while they then wait, for that distribution is an interruption. According to the Sar of Coucy it is not an interruption because they eat immediately. According to the stringent ruling, however, they must wait, and the distribution becomes an interruption between the blessing and the eating of the one breaking bread, whose eating is the primary obligation. Above, seif 6, it was ruled that even saying “Take and bless” may not initially interrupt. Furthermore, seif 1 states that on a weekday one should cut the loaf before HaMotzi so that the cutting will not interrupt. All the more so, cutting portions for those who certainly may not yet eat is an interruption. One should not do this, and we have never seen anyone follow such a practice. Bach likewise writes that the one breaking bread should initially be careful to cut his own portion first. He explains that this is to remove the participants from the dispute among the authorities, but this is imprecise. If they wait until the one breaking bread tastes, there is no dispute. The concern is rather the interruption I have explained.
