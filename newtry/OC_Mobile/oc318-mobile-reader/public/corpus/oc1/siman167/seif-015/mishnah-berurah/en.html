Those reclining may not, etc. This is when they fulfilled their obligation through the blessing of the one breaking bread. See Peri Megadim, who inclines that even if he did not intend to discharge them and each one blesses for himself, since all need the loaf of the one breaking bread, it is not respectful to taste before he tastes.
Permitted to taste. Meaning, if they wish to cut from the loaf of the one breaking bread and taste before him.
But it is permitted, etc. This is not an interruption, for all of this is a need of the meal.
To each one his portion, etc., and they should wait. They should wait from eating until he tastes first. Even if he explicitly gave them permission for this, some say it still does not help. The Taz's view is that it is not proper for the one breaking bread to distribute each person's portion before tasting, since they are not permitted to taste until he tastes first; this distribution is therefore considered an interruption for him between the blessing and eating, which is forbidden initially. Rather, he should see to taste first and then distribute to each person. So wrote other later authorities.
He eats from his own loaf. Each of them has his own complete loaf.
They are permitted to taste first. Even though they all fulfill their obligation through the blessing of the one who blessed.
And if it is Shabbos. The same applies to Yom Tov.
There must be, etc. For if each participant does not have lechem mishneh before him, then they must rely on the one breaking bread, who has lechem mishneh before him, and taste from that bread; they are therefore not permitted to taste before him.
