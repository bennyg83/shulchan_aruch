Seif katan 15. “Nevertheless, one should not permit,” etc. Bach writes that it is initially forbidden because they might become impure first, and whether it is permitted is a dispute, as Taz ultimately cites in his name. Taz, however, holds that since the law has been established stringently that they must wait, they will certainly act properly and this concern falls away. Nevertheless, one should not permit it initially for another reason, because it constitutes an interruption. It is possible that the word “nevertheless” is a scribal error, and the text should read: “For this reason, since we rule stringently like those who disagree with the Sar of Coucy, one should not initially permit,” etc.
