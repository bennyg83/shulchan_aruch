There Nihyah with a kamatz. This is past tense, meaning that it already came into being. The second opinion is to pronounce it with a segol, as a present-tense form, applying both to the past and to the future, like borei, which is a present-tense form, and we do not say bara. The Gemara, however, states that borei too is not a present-tense form but a past-tense form, and the blessing applies only to the past. The reason we do not say bara is because of the Scriptural expression. According to this, nihyah should be pronounced with a kamatz. The Yerushalmi, however, implies that it disagrees with our Talmud. It explains that Beis Shammai, who say, “Who created the light of fire,” using the past tense, do so specifically because fire was created on Motzaei Shabbos. Regarding something that is constantly renewed, however, etc. This indicates that it understands borei as a present-tense form and that the blessing applies both to the past and to the future. This is the continuation of Magen Avraham’s words.
