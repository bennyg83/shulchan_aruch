Or many, etc. All this concerns bread. The law regarding wine and other fruits is explained in siman 213.
One recites the blessing for all of them. Even if all the participants know how to recite the blessing themselves, it is nevertheless an enhancement of the mitzvah for one person to recite it and discharge them all, because “in the multitude of the people is the King's glory.” Maadanei Yom Tov, in the chapter Keitzad Mevarchin, siman 33, writes this and states that Ramach also ruled so.
Without reclining. Magen Avraham cites Shiltei Giborim that if some reclined, one person recites the blessing for all, even for those who did not recline. Peri Megadim writes that according to our practice, where sitting is equivalent to reclining, the same applies if only some sat. In my humble opinion, the principal statement of Magen Avraham in the name of Shiltei Giborim is very difficult. Below, siman 193, seif 2, it is explicit that even the third person must join their fixed meal through reclining at least at the end of the meal, and for us through sitting. This is explicit in the Rosh in the chapter Sheloshah SheAchlu, which is the source of this law. It is well known that the Rosh and other poskim, and the unqualified ruling of the Shulchan Aruch, understand the Mishnah as referring both to HaMotzi and to Bircas HaMazon, with the same law applying to both. This therefore requires considerable study.
Our manner of sitting. Standing is not effective. Tosafos, Berachos 43a, at the end of s.v. ho'il, states this; Beit Yosef cites it in siman 296, and Bach rules likewise. The case in seif 12, where standing suffices, is different because there they are seated upon animals.
Unless they sat at one table, etc. See Magen Avraham, seif katan 26, who explains that when they are in a house containing a table but do not all eat at the same table, it is evident that they do not intend to establish a joint meal, and their original designation of a common place is nullified. The Gra writes in his commentary that the Mechaber copied “at one table” inadvertently from the preceding discussion. In truth, one table or one tablecloth is not required where they initially agreed to eat together, or where the householder eats with his household. Whenever they sit and eat together, meaning that they are not scattered, one here and another there, they combine, as in seif 12, contrary to Magen Avraham.
