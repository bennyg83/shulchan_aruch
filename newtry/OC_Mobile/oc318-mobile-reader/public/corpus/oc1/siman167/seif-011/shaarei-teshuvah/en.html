An interruption. See Baer Heitev. See Panim Meiros, part 2, siman 5, regarding people sitting together, each with his own cup before him for Kiddush, who wait to recite the blessing over wine until everyone has recited it. One who recites the blessing first answers amen to his fellow’s blessing, and only afterward do they recite Kiddush. They do not act properly, and through this interruption they cause the first blessing to be in vain. Since he interrupted between the blessing and tasting, it constitutes an interruption, as some poskim rule regarding answering amen after the blessings of Krias Shema. He must therefore recite the blessing again, and his first blessing was in vain. Accordingly, one should not interrupt by answering amen. The same applies to HaMotzi: when each person has his own loaf before him, he should not answer amen to his fellow’s blessing until he has tasted after his own blessing. See there.
