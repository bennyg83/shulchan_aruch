Seif 11: If there were, etc. This is the Tur's wording. See Tosafos, 45a, s.v. im, etc. This is why he writes “two or many,” since regarding Bircas HaMazon there is a distinction between two and three. Beit Yosef.
Specifically, etc. Mishnah, 42a. See Tosafos there, s.v. heseibu; Gemara, 40b, etc.; and Rosh there.
Or, etc. Yerushalmi, cited by Rosh and Rabbeinu Yonah there: Rabbi Yehoshua ben Levi said, “By oath, the Mishnah teaches this regarding guests, but not regarding a householder in his own home.” Rabbi Chiyya taught: “Even a householder in his own home.” Rabbeinu Yonah rules like Rabbi Yehoshua ben Levi.
But, etc. Mishnah there.
And if, etc. Gemara there.
We shall eat here. Rosh and Rabbeinu Yonah. See Rashi, s.v. beduch, etc., “for they established, etc.” The Gemara spoke of the usual case.
Nowadays, etc. Tosafos in the aforementioned gloss explain that we are not accustomed to reclining. Rabbeinu Yonah further writes that this applied specifically in their times, when each person had his own table and cloth before him. In our times, however, when all eat at one table and from one cloth, reclining is unnecessary. Tosafos there write similarly. This is why the Shulchan Aruch says, “at one table, etc.” Below, however, it says, “unless they sat at the table, etc.”
And according to our practice, etc. Beit Yosef writes that without sitting there is no fixed association at all. This is also implied by the Baraisa there, “They sat down to eat, etc.,” which the Gemara explains as a case where they said, “Let us go, etc.” Even so, sitting is required, and there is no fixed association without sitting. The same applies to a householder with members of his household. See what I wrote in siman 213.
At one table, etc. This requires study. Why should our practice differ from theirs, where a fixed association is created by sitting alone, as explained above? Likewise, the inference drawn from the Tur in siman 174 concerns sitting alone. See Tosafos, 43a, end of s.v. ho'il. The Shulchan Aruch likewise writes in seif 12, “If they were, etc.” It appears that this is a scribal error, and that the wording was carried over from the preceding phrase. Beit Yosef also did not write it, unlike Magen Avraham.
