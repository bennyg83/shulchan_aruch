[Levush] that are not needs, etc. See the Beit Yosef, who cites many authorities that only needs of the HaMotzi itself are permitted, but writes that the Rambam does not seem so. I do not know how one could move from what is explicit in the authorities because of an implication in the Rambam. The Prishah writes that the Rambam need not disagree with all those great authorities; giving food to a person is certainly no worse than giving food to an animal, and if one says to give food to a person who needs it, even a wealthy person, this is a mitzvah and precedes feeding one's animal. So too it appears from Shibolei HaLeket, Taniya, and Avudraham. At first I thought to establish the law that one must return and bless, but because of doubt I investigated further and found that the Rosh, Agudah, Raavan, and Semak imply that anything needed for the meal is not an interruption. Therefore one should not return and bless.
[Levush] bring salt, etc. Meaning, even with clean bread; and give so-and-so, even if he is wealthy. So wrote the later authorities.
[Levush] that it is forbidden to eat, etc. Some explain that just as the Holy One, blessed is He, first provides and prepares food for the creatures and only afterward prepares it for man, so too every person must do this in his home; this is from Rabbeinu David Avudraham. Drinking is permitted before one's animal, as we find with Rivkah, who said to drink and then that she would water his camels, as stated in Sefer Chasidim. This now explains Rashi on Chukas, that from the command to give drink to the congregation and to their animals we learn that the Holy One, blessed is He, has compassion on their property. Otherwise one could ask that perhaps it teaches that they should water their animals first. There is another answer, but this is not the place to elaborate.
[Levush] between answering Amen, etc. The Lechem Chamudos writes that this is not precise, for hearing is like answering, and when one tastes without answering Amen he also fulfills his obligation. This is also explicit below at the end of siman 213.
[Levush] but the one breaking bread, etc. The same law applies to one of those reclining, as the Knesses HaGedolah wrote, and so it appears from the Lechem Chamudos based on the words of Tosafot that I cited.
[Levush] that is, etc. The Rama's reason is like the latter view, and in Darkei Moshe he wrote that the words of the Rokeach appear correct, since once one person tasted from the slice, all have fulfilled, similar to tasting from the cup of blessing, as written below at the end of siman 271. Malbushei Yom Tov also wrote that we see daily practice initially in this manner regarding the blessing on wine during the meal. So too wrote Lechem Chamudos, who cited Tosafot in Pesachim regarding Kiddush. But this is astonishing, for Tosafot later write that regarding wine they did not fulfill unless they drank, since otherwise it is an interruption. This supports the stricter view that the listeners should not interrupt before they themselves taste.
[Levush] and he cannot correct, etc. However, Maadanei Melech, daf 64, wrote that he is exempt even for the past, because as long as he is still engaged in the matter it is properly called before performance. See below, siman 168, seif katan 18, what I wrote in the name of the Kesef Mishneh.
[Levush] he is satiated, etc. Lechem Chamudos wondered at the Levush, for the Beit Yosef cites Kol Bo that if he completed his meal and cannot eat more, he should not bless, implying that if he can eat more he should go back, eat, and bless first. The Beit Yosef says this is possible according to the Raavad, but the authorities do not agree, as will be explained in siman 172; therefore even if he can return and eat, he need not do so. Lechem Chamudos also notes that the Levush's own words seem contradictory, for he ends by saying, when he wishes to eat, though according to him it depends not on desire but on ability. In my humble opinion, all can be resolved: certainly even one who is satiated may still be able to eat, but food is repulsive to him because he is already full. The Levush understood Kol Bo's phrase cannot eat as meaning that he is completely full and unable to eat at all, and on this the Beit Yosef disagrees. But the Beit Yosef agrees that we require satiation, even if he still can eat somewhat; if he is not satiated, then even if he does not intend to eat more, he must bless and eat. This is why the Levush wrote when he wishes to eat. The Bach also understood the Beit Yosef this way. I am also puzzled by the Beit Yosef's statement that this is possible according to the Raavad, for in siman 172 he cites the Raavad as speaking of one who intends to eat more. It is also difficult to identify the authorities who disagree. The Rashba's novellae imply that even if one cannot eat, he blesses. Moreover, Kol Bo and Baal Halachos Gedolos rule like the earlier authorities. Therefore I say that even if he does not intend to eat, he must go back, bless, and eat; however, if he is so satiated that food is repulsive to him, it appears that he should not return and bless, for the intent of Baal Halachos Gedolos and Kol Bo can be explained as referring to one unable to eat because food is repulsive to him.
He fulfilled, etc. All this is only after the fact. Even if he did not mention bread, but said only Master of this, the Beit Yosef and Bach wrote that he fulfilled his obligation. See below, siman 214, where if he omitted only the word universe, he must return and bless. According to this, it seems to me that he must specifically say King of the universe, and so it appears from the Bach and Prishah.
