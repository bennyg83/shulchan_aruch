16) There, in the gloss: On Shabbos he should not cut, etc. The same applies on Yom Tov. Shulchan Aruch HaRav, paragraph 3. The reason is that his hand might slip and completely sever the portion before he finishes the blessing, leaving him without lechem mishneh. Levush. Tosafos on Berachos 39b write the same. Tashbetz, siman 323, however, gives another reason: when he cuts, crumbs fall from the loaf and it is no longer whole. Eliyah Rabbah, paragraph 3. The Mordechai there in chapter Keitzad Mevarchin writes that perhaps the piece will fall and he will no longer have lechem mishneh. Magen Avraham, paragraph 5, cites him and writes that this implies that if the piece did not completely fall away, it would still be lechem mishneh. The Agudah on Chullin, siman 150, however, writes in the name of Maharan that it is considered lechem mishneh only when one holds the cut portion and the loaf rises with it. If he cut more deeply, it is no longer lechem mishneh. Magen Avraham writes that this also appears from Shiltei HaGibborim there on the Mordechai and from the Rama's wording that if he cut more deeply, it is considered a piece; see there.
