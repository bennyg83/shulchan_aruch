There. The statement that borei implies the past does not mean that we recite borei rather than shebara because the latter is certainly past tense while one must use the participle. That is incorrect, for borei too can certainly refer to the past. We do not say shebara, although all agree it is past tense, because we adopt borei, the Scriptural expression, as Tosafos writes there. Blessings should always refer to the past. Magen Avraham then writes that Rashba's novellae cite the Yerushalmi that even Beis Shammai agrees one says shebara; they dispute only regarding fire. The printed words “except as written” are a scribal error and should read “regarding fire,” which is not renewed every year. Regarding something renewed, even Beis Shammai agrees one blesses borei, a participial form. Nevertheless, our Talmud is primary.
