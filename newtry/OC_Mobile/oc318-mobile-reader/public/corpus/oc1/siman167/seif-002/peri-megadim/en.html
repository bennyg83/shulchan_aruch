(א) And regarding our bread, etc.—the Taz has difficulty in understanding and explanation is needed, for in Talmudic times too the bread did not float in the air of the oven on a ladder and the like, but rather stuck to the oven wall—see Shabbat 38a until the bottom face hardens, see there in Rashi and Tosafot; only that there in Rashi and Tosafot on ibaya lehu there are disputing views whether the faces stuck in the oven baked first or the faces opposite the fire baked first—per Rashi and Yerushalmi—and this is possible in their ovens; but in ours it is reasonable that the bottom stuck to the ground baked first for all, since there is no fire in the oven as is known; and it is possible that nevertheless there is doubt, but rather Peri Megadim letter 2 explains the Taz that the Rambam there rules like Yerushalmi—Rif to be lenient—and we are like Tosafot who require both—and all the more so in our ovens that stick to the ground of the oven baked first; and why did he be stringent to cut from bottom and top—from the bottom alone suffices—rather as Bach wrote that there is ash or sawdust beneath it and it is not so beautiful, and therefore he cuts from both together because below baked first and above is beautiful and proper; and one can load this onto his words though his language is not so precise, for he has difficulty from what the Rambam wrote and our bread—why discuss ours; and on the contrary one can say in their bread there is doubt, not in ours that presumably where it sticks to the ground it bakes beautifully; rather as Peri Megadim holds that below bakes beautifully and Rif to be lenient and we like Tosafot—if so for motzi, below alone suffices only for Bach's reason, not so in theirs in the air of the oven meaning there is no ash etc. they are equal that bottom alone suffices, but what he concluded and there are views whether bottom etc. is difficult. And Levush in Peri Megadim 65 ruled that the bottom stuck cooks last and therefore on Shabbat he was stringent until the bottom hardens and Piskei Dishah added until what is inside cooks—that is the main point, not bottom and top for bottom is obvious also top (unlike Taz whom Rama disputes with Bach); and why did he write in motzi bottom and top and in Hagahah there some say—but it is explained in Peri Megadim that bottom cooks later and if so in ours top alone suffices and it is also fine that there is no ash and sawdust.

(ב) And we conclude the law: bread baked on vegetables or what they call bagel or platzil on mardeh in the oven—ostensibly he should cut from the bottom since there it baked first per Taz nicely also and there is no ash and sawdust; but the reverse is so, for in such a case it hurries to bake from the upper side because in earthenware and placed on mardeh or vegetable it impedes baking, and all the more in platzil where there is light from one side where it hurries to bake more than the top. And it will be explained further in Peri Megadim and here there is no need to extend. And in Magen Avraham 2 it will be explained further on this, see there.
