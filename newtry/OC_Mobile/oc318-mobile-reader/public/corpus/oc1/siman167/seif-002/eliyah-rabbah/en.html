However, in the responsa Shaar Ephraim I found that as long as the burned part has not been cut away, it is called a whole loaf for lechem mishneh. If challah was removed from a whole loaf, its law is like a sliced loaf. I elaborated to prove this in a responsum at the end of my Eliyahu Zuta. The Magen Avraham wrote that with our bread one cuts above. It seems that this refers to where dirt is stuck below, and so it appears from the Bach. It also seems that this speaks of a slice, for with a whole loaf one should cut around it, though this can also be done with a slice. The precise way of cutting and marking before the blessing on Shabbos and Yom Tov is discussed by the Maharshal, Bach, Toras Chaim, and Maharil. The Maharil would prepare the cut so that the remainder was still recognizable at the time of the blessing. Regarding the size of the HaMotzi piece, there are views in the Yerushalmi whether it is a kezayis or less than a kezayis. In practice, whether for oneself or for others, one should give a kezayis, thereby satisfying all opinions. The Maharil's custom of eating half a kezayis has support from Sefer Yereim that less than half a kezayis is nothing. At a large meal, the piece distributed should come from the slice separated for HaMotzi. The Magen Avraham wrote that even with a slice one should not cut completely before the blessing, so that it appear larger. The Darkei Moshe wrote that one should break opposite the split, for that is where it began to bake. The Shiyarei Knesses HaGedolah wrote amounts of pieces corresponding to the Torah readers: on Shabbos seven, on Yom Tov five, on Rosh Chodesh and Chol HaMoed four, and on a weekday and at Shabbos Minchah three.
