The one who blesses hamotzi, etc.—for in the Gemara there is a dispute: Rabanan hold hamotzi and R' Nachman holds motzi; and they say that in motzi all agree it implies past and the berachah refers to the past, but in hamotzi they dispute whether it implies past and they ruled like Rabanan. And Tosafot wrote: even though in motzi all agree it implies past, Yerushalmi explains so as not to mix the heads of the letters—haolam motzi—and even though in lechem min haaretz there is mixing of letters, it is different there for scripture writes lehotzi lechem min haaretz; and in first chapter of Pesachim there is similarly in berachat biur chametz a dispute whether to say levar or al biur, and they say levar—all agree it implies the owner of removal and the halachah is al biur; and Rosh wrote there: even though for levar all agree and for baal biur they dispute—because they say in Berachot from a person's berachot it is recognizable if he is a talmid chacham, and one who blesses on biur is recognizable as a talmid chacham and knows that al biur too implies future as they say in chapter Keitzad—Rabbis praised R' Zeira regarding Rav Zevid that he is a great man and expert in berachot; they said bring to me—for I came they brought him bread; he said let my master taste something; he opened and said motzi lechem min haaretz; he said this is what they say about him that he is a great man—for if he had said hamotzi we would have heard the reason and heard that halachah is like Rabanan who say even hamotzi (so wrote my ancestor Mahari bar Betzalel)—but he said motzi, what does he teach us? In motzi all agree it implies past—apparently he must show us innovation in his berachot; and therefore they practiced to bless on biur to show us innovation that so too it implies future and that one not err to say al biur for the past; and if he blessed thus he did not fulfill—end of his words. And it is difficult for me from Perek She'alu daf 22 from dispute of R' Akiva and R' Yehuda whether to say baruchu et Heaven hamvorakh—for R' Akiva holds one says baruchu et Heaven hamvorakh; and it is there in Gemara Refram bar Papa came to synagogue of Bei Givar, stood and read in the sefer and said baruchu et Heaven and was silent and did not say hamvorakh; all cried baruchu et Heaven hamvorakh; Rava said foolish pot—meaning earthenware vessel—with the dispute why do you need it (Rashi: better to hold R' Yehuda's words for even R' Akiva concedes when he said hamvorakh it is better, only he need not); and behold the world practices like R' Yehuda—if so the Gemara—and why did Rava rebuke Refram for he acted well to show that this too fulfills; and what is different from that of how do they bless where the one who said motzi intended to remove himself from dispute as we say explicitly there in Gemara, and nevertheless R' Zeira disputed him because it is recognizable from a person's berachot if he is talmid chacham—and perhaps Refram here too acted for this reason like R' Akiva to show innovation that there is no difference between them—for here all concede in hamvorakh it was like there in motzi all concede—and why not say Refram acted well to show in his berachot that he is talmid chacham as R' Zeira wanted? Fine per Tosafot's explanation that here is different due to mixing of letters—it works; but per Rosh who wrote for reason he must show innovation and this is proven from that of Pesachim—it is hard. And one can answer that one of two is required—either show innovation or act according to all; and fine that case of R' Zeira acted well to show innovation that halachah is to say hamotzi like Rabanan who dispute R' Nachman—and though it is simple halachah is like majority, perhaps halachah is like R' Nachman for his reason is reasonable that he brings proof hamotzi implies future as written hamotzi etchem mitachat sivlot Mitzrayim—and when Moshe said thus he had not yet taken them out, but Rabanan resolve that verse as explained there in Gemara—necessarily for R' Nachman it is better; thus he teaches innovation that halachah is like Rabanan; and this is clear in words of R' Zeira who said if he said hamotzi he stated the reason that it implies there must have been reason for this—and this is the essence of the innovation; and similarly in dispute on biur it was reasonable to say levar for all concede and they dispute baal biur—necessarily it was reasonable to say as all concede, thus there was innovation in what he said al biur; but there in hamvorakh therefore he did not say like all but came to say innovation that halachah is like R' Akiva who need not hamvorakh—and this is no innovation for we hold halachah like R' Akiva even against his colleagues; and since there is no extra innovation, better to say like all; therefore Rava was angry and said if so why did Rava need there in Perek She'alu above to rule halachah like R' Akiva regarding that there is no difference between many and individual—one can say that there is the first clause of the mishnah there that follows R' Yehuda HaGelili and perhaps halachah is so; it teaches us like the other anonymous mishnah that follows R' Akiva—as seems right to me to resolve.
