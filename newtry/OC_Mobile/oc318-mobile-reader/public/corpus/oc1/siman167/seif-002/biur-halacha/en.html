(א) And they will say an artist, and I will call on all the blessings when he hears from Israel, an artist who is oppressed because of a bee here who wants to go out with a blessing must listen to their hearts The blessing [as he has written himself] and the testimony of an artist who is not obligated to hear so that he will be an artist

(ב) And the one blessing must intend, etc.—see Mishnah Berurah for the reason, which is simple; and so is explicit in Avnei Nezer. And behold, although they brought this from Yerushalmi, no posek [except Avnei Nezer] found this matter [and in particular what Avnei Nezer proves—that without this they do not fulfill their obligation—contradicts the law explained below in siman 213, as the article already noted; see there]. And indeed, anyone who blesses any berachah himself does not need to answer amen at all; on the contrary, one who answers after him amen is a boor, as explained in the Talmud; and although here it is not an interruption at all, nevertheless why should he answer amen [and although I settled this somewhat in Mishnah Berurah, nevertheless it is strained]. And indeed Lechem Chamudot, chapter 3 She'achlu, and similarly Keneset HaGedolah—it appears they found this law difficult and diverted this law to another reason: so he know when to break, because it is forbidden to break until amen finishes from the mouths of the answerers, as below in seif 16; but from simple reasoning it is clear like the reason I wrote in Mishnah Berurah. And were I not afraid, one could say there is a printing error in Yerushalmi and it should read: your intent removes your obligation—and the intent is we require intent of listener and speaker, as below in siman 213; accordingly Avnei Nezer's law is not mentioned at all in Yerushalmi. Behold, I later found in Sefer Charedim that he has this girsa in Yerushalmi; see there; and it requires study.
