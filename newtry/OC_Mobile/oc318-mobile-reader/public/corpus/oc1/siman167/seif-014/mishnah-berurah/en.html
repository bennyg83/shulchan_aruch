Many. The same applies to two. The case is where the host of the meal is not with them, or where all are hosts of the meal and are eating together.
The greatest among them. Meaning greatest in wisdom, out of honor for him.
Breaks bread. And discharges the others through his blessing. Even in a place where the custom is that each one blesses for himself over his own loaf, nevertheless the custom is that the greatest breaks bread first, as stated by the Peri Megadim. A groom on his wedding day breaks bread even if someone greater than he is among those present.
To give him precedence. As it is written, and you shall sanctify him; Chazal said this means for every matter of sanctity, to open first and bless first.
A Torah scholar precedes him. It is forbidden for the Torah scholar to give the kohen precedence before himself, and by doing so he disgraces the Torah. It is stated below in siman 201 that this is specifically if he gives him precedence as a rule of kohen status, meaning because he is a kohen. But if he gives him permission to bless not because of his kohen status, he may do so.
It is good to give him precedence. It is stated in the Gemara that one who is careful about this merits length of days.
The host is with them. Meaning, the owner of the meal who is eating with them.
He breaks bread. He precedes even a kohen and a Torah scholar, so that he will break generously.
Even if the guest is greater. The Lechem Chamudos wrote that today the custom is to honor the greater person, and the Magen Avraham agreed. Several later authorities question this. However, at a meal where each participant eats from his own loaf and blesses for himself, certainly the proper practice is for the host to honor the greater person to break bread first, since the reason of generosity does not apply.
He should first say, etc. Even if the greatest person is breaking bread, or the host, because of humility he must request permission, as though the others are greater than he and it is fitting for them to bless. When a kohen is present, whom there is a mitzvah to precede, if another wishes to break bread, and the same applies to Birkas HaMazon, he must obtain permission from him; it does not help merely to say with the permission of the kohen unless the kohen actually granted him permission.
