The greatest among them. The Gemara implies that the groom breaks bread on the day of his wedding, even if someone greater than he is present, as the Beit Yosef cites in the name of Tosafot.
A Torah scholar precedes him. It is forbidden for the scholar to give him precedence.
It is good to give him precedence. One thereby merits length of days.
The host breaks bread. The custom is to honor the greater person, for the concern of generosity does not apply when he is not giving him a large slice sufficient for the entire meal, as stated by Lechem Chamudos. See the end of seif 11. So too it appears from the Gemara, for Rabbi Abbahu honored Rabbi Zeira to break bread.
With permission of my teachers. The custom is to request permission from a kohen, as stated by Lechem Chamudos, and this is proper, for there is a mitzvah to give him precedence; if so, it is forbidden to bless without his permission. This was also established in the hymn beginning Let us thank Your name. Nevertheless, it does not help for one merely to say, with permission, if the kohen did not grant permission.
