Also Mahari Yom Tov wrote on this Levush, and this is his language: we see the practice daily that l'chatchila we do so for wine-berachah within the meal; until here. And similarly Lechem Chamudot, daf 49, concluded this language. Again I found in Rokeach's words in Tosafot Pesachim 100b, and this is his language: the hands of kiddush have fulfilled even though they only drank if the one who blesses tastes, or even one of them; until here. And it is a great wonder on a sage like him that he did not examine Tosafot Pesachim 101a, and this is its language: even the hands of wine have not fulfilled—and it deals with those who drank in the synagogue; although for kiddush they fulfilled by hearing, as I explained above, for wine he does not exempt unless he drank, for it is interruption, as we say regarding hamotzi that if he interrupted he must return and bless; until here. Behold explicitly that for wine-berachah and hamotzi berachah all must taste without interruption. The reason appears to me: specifically for kiddush, which is a mitzvah, one can taste for all and all fulfill; but hamotzi and wine-berachah—each hears the berachah so that he himself may eat, and he must eat or drink immediately. Accordingly Rama's proof from the cup is strong; and so is the Levush's proof from hamotzi. And the first view in Levush is like the first view in Rokeach below.
