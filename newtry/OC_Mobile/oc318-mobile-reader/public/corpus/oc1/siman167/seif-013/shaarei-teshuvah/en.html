Malka, Marei. See Baer Heitev. See Bach, siman 165, who implies that if one recited Borei Minei Mezonos over bread, he did not fulfill his obligation. Beis Yehudah, siman 41, rules likewise. Birkei Yosef writes that Beit Yosef, Taz, and Eliyah Rabbah in the name of Derishah hold that he did fulfill his obligation. Even HaEzer, siman 168, likewise explains Beit Yosef. Birkei Yosef inclines to say that perhaps even Bach agrees. See there in Shiyurei Berachah, and in siman 207, where he writes that Rema MiPano in Alfasi Zuta likewise holds this view. There he also writes that if one recited Borei Peri HaAdamah over bread, he fulfilled his obligation, though Raah and Ritva disagree. See there. Birkei Yosef further writes: If one washed his hands, recited HaMotzi, and then learned that the water used for washing was invalid for netilas yadayim, he must wash again. He should immediately eat less than a kezayis, since according to Tashbetz and those who agree with him, such an amount does not require washing. He should then wash again and complete his meal. If, after reciting HaMotzi but before tasting anything, he washed his hands again, Bnei Chiya was uncertain whether another blessing is required. Birkei Yosef writes that it appears no new blessing is required, for this is no worse than the instruction, “Take, bless,” discussed there. See there.
