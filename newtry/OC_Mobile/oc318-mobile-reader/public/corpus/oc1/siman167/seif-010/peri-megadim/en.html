He does not repeat the blessing. See Taz, who excludes the reading in Rambam, chapter 4, halachah 2 of Hilchos Berachos, that HaMotzi is a Torah blessing. See Beit Yosef and Perishah regarding the a fortiori inference from Berachos 35a and 21a, and Magen Avraham, seif katan 7. According to that reading, when Berachos 21a lists Bircas HaMazon and Krias Shema as Torah obligations and prayer as Rabbinic, HaMotzi is not expressly classified. Its Torah status would derive only by an a fortiori inference, which the Gemara calls “from the words of the Sages.” One might argue that Chazal have authority to uproot a Torah law by requiring passive nonperformance, but not through an active violation, as explained in Yevamos 90b. With Bircas HaMazon they could maintain their enactment in a case of seminal impurity through passive nonperformance. With HaMotzi, however, if such a person may not even bless mentally but nevertheless eats, would the Torah obligation be actively uprooted because of Ezra's enactment? This may be compared to tzitzis on Shabbos in siman 13, where one may wear a four-cornered garment in public. Wearing is not itself forbidden, for the Torah did not say, “Do not wear without tzitzis,” but rather commanded, “You shall make tzitzis.” The positive command is therefore suspended passively for human dignity. The same applies here. The Torah did not say, “Do not eat without a blessing,” but imposed a positive obligation to bless first. One who eats without HaMotzi neglects a positive command, but the eating itself is not forbidden. It is enough that HaMotzi should be like Bircas HaMazon: just as the eating does not become prohibited because one failed afterward to recite Bircas HaMazon, so too before eating. Furthermore, as I wrote in several places, there are three reasons why punishment is not imposed through an a fortiori inference: this is the received rule given to Moshe; one may distinguish degrees of severity and not infer from them; and perhaps the very severity means there is no atonement through lashes, or perhaps further analysis will uncover a refutation, so we prohibit but do not punish from doubt. Accordingly, even if this is a true a fortiori inference, one still may not repeat the blessing from doubt, lest some refutation exist and he violate “You shall not take Hashem's Name in vain,” according to the view that this prohibition is Torah-level. See simanim 215 and 67. The Mechaber did not conclude, as the Tur did, that HaMotzi is not Torah-level. It is therefore possible that he holds it is Torah-level through the a fortiori inference, yet one still cannot repeat it for the reason stated. This also explains why he should eat a kezayis, as Magen Avraham, seif katan 7, writes. Taz therefore concludes that HaMotzi is only Rabbinic; see Magen Avraham there. Levush writes a refutation to the a fortiori inference. See Rashi, Chullin 7b, s.v. batza, which implies that HaMotzi concerns a kezayis, and the remarkable conduct of Rabbi Pinchas ben Yair, who was careful not to eat a kezayis from others.
