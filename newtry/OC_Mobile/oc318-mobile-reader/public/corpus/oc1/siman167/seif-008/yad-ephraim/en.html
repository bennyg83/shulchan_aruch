Magen Avraham note 18—but to drink is permitted, and proof from Rivkah etc.—this requires study—for it would be better to bring proof from Divine statement in chapter Chukat and you gave drink to the congregation and their animals; and I saw in Ohr HaChaim there that he addressed this and wrote to answer that in time of danger man precedes—even in that of Rivkah when there is danger or distress man precedes. (And see Nedarim daf 80 in dispute of Rabanan and R' Yehuda on verse and to their animals and to all their beasts; and in Even HaEzer in Beit Shmuel siman 80 and what Yad Ephraim wrote there and in responsum part Even HaEzer; and to my view the reason forbidden to taste etc. is because every person must not hold himself righteous for sometimes he has no merit except that he eats through merit of animals as stated in Midrash in incident of Alexander—and it cites verse man and beast You save Heaven. And on this one can say per what is established at beginning of chapter Keitzad Mevarchin—that when you gather your grain you do not do will of the Omnipresent—this refers to and I will give grass in your field for your animal and through that merit also you and you shall eat and be satisfied; and therefore since through animal's merit he also eats, it is fitting to precede it first, and he derives this from that verse itself and I will give etc. And behold this applies specifically regarding eating through natural way; but if through miracle there is no reason the miracle should be through animals' merit—and so too in Binyan Ariel on verse to die there we and our city, see there—and if so certainly the miracle is for man's sake and then animal is secondary; therefore man precedes properly. And therefore in bringing forth water from the rock where there is no place to hang on animals' merit, the Merciful One commanded Moshe and you shall give drink to the congregation and their animals; and it is precise what he said and you brought out for them—meaning for their sake and merit therefore they precede; and per this there would be room to prove from here the reverse—that where it is natural way animal precedes for drinking too; and practical difference if he interrupted between washing and motzi and said to give animal to drink—and perhaps only animal's eating one precedes before his eating, but animal's drinking one does not precede before eating as verse implies and I will give grass etc. that on its eating the stringency came to precede animal; and per this one can say if we say for drinking too one must precede—if he wanted to drink and interrupted in speech to give animal to drink he need not return and bless; but custom of world is not to be stringent in drinking at all even those meticulous in eating matter; and per what is written one can give reason specifically in eating that rain of heavens is needed to satiate face of earth with produce, and Midrash says rain does not descend except through animals' merit; but for drinking abundant heavens on the earth—not dependent on animals' merit and the stringency found no place to rest to precede animal in drinking; therefore one who interrupts must return and bless. Again I found in Bereishit Rabbah and Perishah Maharal who brought Rashi's words in Chukat from here that the Holy One had pity on their property, see there; and per what is written in Levush Ateret Zahav—one need not say for preceding—for certainly miracle was made through man's merit—and I shortened; and nevertheless majority of world are careful in this even for eating; and it seems because mostly they have slaves and hired workers entrusted with animals and poultry and they are warned and stand to give them food in morning early—and presumption they do their agency. And this requires study: if one relies on his servants in this always and now it occurred he interrupted saying gavil le-torai—it seems for him it is interruption; and one who has no one to serve him and he himself must be careful in this since Sages said forbidden to man etc.; and see Sefer Charedim wondrous matter from our holy rabbi the Ari on this matter.
