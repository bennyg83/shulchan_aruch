(א) Already the Taz wrote that for those who benefit each must bless—nevertheless he fulfills through his friend's blessing; and what he wrote that one must answer amen—in Berachot 42b b'dieved even without answering amen he fulfilled. And what he wrote in the name of Bach—his intent is since Rashi on Berachot 40a explains the one who cuts before he cuts etc. and did not explain plain cuts—and even among those reclining if one said to his friend at his place take, bless it is not an interruption—learn from this that Rashi meant specifically the cutter, but another after the cutter cut and tasted, even not for meal needs, is not interruption. And what he wrote regarding donning tefillin—one can say a mitzvah of obligation resting upon him is interruption, not so optional eating and berachah of praise and thanks when one praises the Master of all on behalf of all (his agent is as himself) and the cutter ate more—not interruption for those reclining; and similarly matzah eating on Pesach night—if the cutter tasted and others interrupted they must return and bless for it is like berachat hamitzvot. And Taz made an enactment: if it occurred that those reclining interrupted to bless hamotzi and the cutter intends to exempt whoever will not interrupt between his hearing and his eating—then he fulfills necessarily and there is no berachah in vain—even per Rokeach for in such a case he did not fulfill through hearing for he and the cutter intended only that he not interrupt between hearing and eating. And Eshel Avraham 12 to Magen Avraham—the cutter too, if one of the reclining tasted—all fulfilled per Magen Avraham; and see Levush—l'chatchila forbidden and b'dieved fulfilled; and behold also Rambam in Hagahah concedes l'chatchila one should not speak even if the cutter ate.

(ב) And know: if the cutter interrupted with speech between blessing and eating and others ate afterward—one can say they fulfilled through his blessing since they did not interrupt with speech; and from this I would say to her Rama 62 in Hagahah—since he takes others out with his blessing even though he did not intend to still drink wine and this cup is water—and matter of 173 in Magen Avraham 7—in unwitting case he takes them out even though he did not enjoy—here too when intentional and interrupted—nevertheless his berachah l'chatchila is fine and he did not transgress enactment of Sages for his intent was to bless and enjoy too, only that afterward he interrupted—others fulfilled through his blessing. And we have come to the dispute of Rokeach and Bach as said.

(ג) If he made kiddush on bread and blessed hamotzi and finished kiddush and afterward wine became available and he blesses on it—this requires study—for one can say it is not interruption for the essence of the mitzvah is on wine—and it will be explained in siman 271; and in our chiddushim we wrote from this.
