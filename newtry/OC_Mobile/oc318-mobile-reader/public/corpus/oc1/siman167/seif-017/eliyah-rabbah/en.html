[17] [Levush] One fulfills the obligation with the berachot of the other, etc. Olat Tamid wrote as follows: meaning, even if he did not intend to discharge them with his berachah, nevertheless they fulfill the obligation; and so is proven from seif 13, end quote. I also found in the Perishah that he wrote: according to the Rokeach, since even where they did not fix themselves together they fulfill the obligation with intent, if so, where they did fix themselves together they fulfill the obligation even without intent. He holds like the opinion in the laws of Rosh Hashanah, siman 589, that one does not need intent to discharge and to fulfill; end quote. But I am astonished, for the Shulchan Aruch and Levush ruled below in simanim 193, 213, and 589 that we require intent to discharge and to fulfill. I also found in Lechem Chamudot, p. 56, that he wrote plainly that intent is required even where they fixed themselves together; and so it is implied from Rama seif 2, which I brought in seif katan 26. The Rokeach presents no difficulty at all, for he speaks specifically after the fact, as the Levush wrote explicitly in this seif and seif 13; but where they fixed themselves together it is permitted even at the outset with intent to fulfill and discharge. However, it appears to me that perhaps where they fixed themselves together, although they need intent to fulfill, nevertheless he need not explicitly intend to discharge them, for presumably his intent is so, since they fixed themselves together, similar to the shaliach tzibbur blowing shofar below in siman 589. With this, the Taz's question in seif katan 13 is resolved: why did he need to mention in seif 13 "if the one reciting the berachah intended," etc.? See there, and examine carefully.
