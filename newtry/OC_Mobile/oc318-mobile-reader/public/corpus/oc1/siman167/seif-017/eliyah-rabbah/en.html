[Levush] one fulfills through the other's blessings, etc. Olas Tamid explains that even if the one blessing did not intend to discharge the others, they nevertheless fulfill their obligation, and he says this is proven from seif 13. I found similarly in the Prishah, who wrote that according to the Rokeach, if they did not establish themselves they fulfill with intent; then where they did establish themselves, they fulfill even without intent, following the view in the laws of Rosh Hashanah, siman 589, that intent to discharge and fulfill is not required. But this is astonishing, for the Shulchan Aruch and Levush rule below in simanim 193, 213, and 589 that intent to discharge and fulfill is required. Lechem Chamudos also writes plainly that intent is required even when they established themselves, and so it appears from the Rama in seif 2. The Rokeach is not difficult, for he speaks only after the fact, as the Levush states explicitly here and in seif 13. Where they established themselves, it is permitted initially with intent to fulfill and discharge. Nevertheless, it seems possible that when they established themselves, although they need intent to fulfill, the one blessing need not expressly intend to discharge them, for presumably that is his intent since they established themselves, like a shaliach tzibbur blowing shofar in siman 589. This resolves the Taz's question why seif 13 needed to mention if the one blessing intended.
