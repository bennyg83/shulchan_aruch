Seif 17: The one who breaks the bread, etc. This follows one explanation in Tosafos cited above. The Yerushalmi, chapter 6, teaches: The one reciting the blessing extends his hand first, unless he wishes to accord honor to his teacher or to one greater than he in Torah, in which case he may do so. When Rav broke bread, he would taste with his left hand and distribute with his right. This accords with the first explanation of Tosafos, that the discussion concerns the piece cut from the loaf. The entire passage there likewise concerns that piece, for it then states concerning Rav Huna, “This elder said, ‘Recite the blessing, elder, recite the blessing,’ and this does not constitute an interruption between the blessing,” etc. Earlier it likewise states that those reclining may not taste anything, etc., which Tosafos cited as explained above. It is also evident there that one may distribute the bread before eating, as stated above in seif 15 in the gloss, from the conduct of Rav, etc. He acted out of piety so that they would not need to wait for him, as the commentator explains there. It is likewise evident there contrary to the Sar of Coucy. It also appears from there that regarding the piece cut from the loaf, one may honor another, etc. This resolves the objection of the Sar of Coucy, who wrote, “And so it appears below,” etc. Rambam likewise rules this way.
