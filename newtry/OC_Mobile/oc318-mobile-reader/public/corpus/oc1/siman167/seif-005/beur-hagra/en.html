Seif 5: And if, etc. As Rashi explains there.
Or, etc., or, etc. So write the Tur and Hagahos Maimoniyos in the name of Rabbeinu Chananel's explanation.
Or if he intended, etc. This follows the Aruch's explanation that beshesh means a relish, as in, “Can tasteless food be eaten without salt?” Meaning, something that requires a relish. He said that this bread does not require a relish, and since he does not intend to eat it with a relish, he need not wait for one. So it appears. The Aruch writes: “There is no ruling, etc. ‘Can tasteless food be eaten without salt?’ Its Targum is: just as something is not eaten beshesh without salt. Anything eaten with bread is called beshesh; in the language of the Mishnah it is called liften, and in Rabbinic language it is called beshesh. Furthermore, in Eretz Yisrael they place mallow in a pot with eggs and peppers, cook it, and call the dish beshesh. Another explanation: one who recites the blessing need not delay and seek a relish, but may bless immediately because the bread contains salt. Another explanation: because it is fine bread and requires neither salt nor a relish.” End quote. Hagahos Maimoniyos writes that Rabbeinu Chananel's explanation also follows the Aruch. See the Tur. This is the meaning of “or if it is, etc.” The reason is that since it is not eaten without salt and a relish, he must wait for them so that he not interrupt between the blessing and eating. This follows the aforementioned verse, “Can tasteless food, etc.” Correspondingly, “seasoned” refers to a relish, and “or, etc.” refers to salt. Rambam therefore writes, “or if he intended, etc.,” because the reason is the concern for interruption, as explained. Rambam's words may also be sustained according to Rashi's explanation.
Nevertheless, etc.; and it is, etc. Tosafos, end of s.v. heviu, etc.
