That they bring before him. If there are many participants, he must wait until it is brought before each one, as stated in the Gemara. See what I wrote at the end of seif 6.
He need not wait. In all these cases, if he wishes to wait, he may do so. Or perhaps he is not permitted to interrupt, as in Darkei Moshe. According to what I wrote in seif 6, that even something not needed for the HaMotzi slice is not an interruption between the blessing and eating, then between washing and the blessing it is permitted even initially; therefore the Shulchan Aruch wrote the matter here without qualification.
It resembles the altar. Therefore it is forbidden to kill a louse on the table, as stated in Sefer Chasidim, siman 102.
And it protects. The Beit Yosef wrote in the name of Tosafot, citing the Midrash, that when Israel sit and wait for one another until they wash their hands, while they are without mitzvos, the Satan prosecutes them, and the covenant of salt protects them. See what I wrote in siman 166, that it is good to speak words of Torah rather than sit idle, and better than both is not to interrupt at all.
See the end of siman 170. Meaning, although we do not practice eating salt after the meal because our bread contains salt, nevertheless there is a mitzvah to place salt on the table. The kabbalists wrote to dip the slice of HaMotzi in salt.
