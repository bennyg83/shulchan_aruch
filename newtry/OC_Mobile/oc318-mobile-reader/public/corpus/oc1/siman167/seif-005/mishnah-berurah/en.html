He should not break, etc. Consequently, one must be careful not to begin the blessing, and if he blessed, he must wait a little before eating until salt is brought.
Before him. If many are present, he must wait to bless until it is brought before each one, because he is discharging them through his blessing and they must taste immediately after the blessing.
Salt or relish. This is so that it should be eaten with flavor, out of honor for the blessing. Also, so that afterward he should not need to interrupt between the blessing and eating to request salt or seasoning, which is forbidden initially. Even if there will be some delay until they bring it, it is proper to wait before blessing. What is written regarding clean bread means wheat bread and the like.
Dry bread, etc. There is no lack of honor for the blessing here, since he does not intend to eat salt or relish at all during this meal. The second reason, interruption, also does not apply, because he does not intend to request salt or relish afterward at all.
He need not wait. Nevertheless, if he wishes to wait before the blessing until they bring salt or relish before him, he may do so, and there is no concern for an interruption between washing and HaMotzi, since it is a need of the meal, as stated by the Magen Avraham and other later authorities.
It resembles the altar. As Chazal said, when the Beis HaMikdash stood, the altar atoned for a person; now a person's table, when he gives from his bread to the poor, atones for him, as it is written, this is the table that is before Hashem.
And the eating. Meaning, that a person eats to strengthen his powers, and through this he will be healthy and strong for the service of Hashem.
And it protects. It is stated in the Midrash that when Israel sit and wait for one another until they wash their hands and they are without mitzvos, the Satan prosecutes them, and the covenant of salt protects them.
And see the end of siman 170. Meaning, although it is explained there that for us, since our bread contains salt, we do not practice eating salt after the meal, nevertheless it is a mitzvah to place it on the table. The kabbalists wrote to dip the HaMotzi slice in salt three times.
