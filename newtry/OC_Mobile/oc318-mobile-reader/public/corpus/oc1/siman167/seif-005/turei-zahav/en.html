(ב) Clean bread, etc.—Tur wrote and if it is clean bread he need not wait; and so R' Chananel and Rambam; and Bach challenged why he wrote thus for Rambam did not write so but wrote only if they intended to eat dry bread; and to me it seems to explain Tur's words because Rosh wrote: he said to him there is no judgment requiring six—some say delay until they bring relish for salt because Moshe tarried; and some say this does not require relish—he eats spice without salt—we translate as one who does not eat in six because there is no salt—end of his words; and it is not Rosh's way to bring explanations with no practical difference—necessarily there is practical difference: per first explanation there is no distinction between bread and bread—all need relish, only one need not delay for bringing relish—for what he wrote first until they bring before him relish means if he is ready only lacking a bit of bringing; unlike where delay is needed and there in that incident of Rabbah bar Rav Shmuel it was a case that if he wanted to wait for relish there would be delay in the matter—therefore he said he need not delay for this; and the words there is no judgment do not refer to bread but to the essence of the law that relish is needed—he need not delay; and per second explanation that said he need not relish at all—it implies there is distinction between bread and bread—and there was clean bread—therefore he need not relish at all even if before us. And this explanation agrees with our Tur; and so from Rambam's words who wrote to distinguish if they intended to eat dry bread—then he need not relish at all—learn from this not like first explanation; rather each recorded a particular separately—for Rambam the particular was if bread is dear to him that he eat only bread alone he need not relish at all; and for Tur the particular was if it is clean bread; and therefore Shulchan Aruch ruled like both.
