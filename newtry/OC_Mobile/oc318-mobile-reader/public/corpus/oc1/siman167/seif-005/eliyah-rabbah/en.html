He wrote that the tefillah and the blessing of the table are more than other blessings, because the food and the feast bring a person to the grossness of nature and the height of the heart, and therefore one must observe so that the desires of the Omnipresent, may the Omnipresent be blessed, be attracted to his heart and not to the side of the desires of food and feasting. He wrote from the Ri'el the laws of Shabbat to the world when the Omnipresent mentioned in blessing the one who takes out a handful of bread with both hands up until he finishes the remembrance and lowers it for the first time in the sand of Harim Hafim on the Shabbat of the two Gods:
