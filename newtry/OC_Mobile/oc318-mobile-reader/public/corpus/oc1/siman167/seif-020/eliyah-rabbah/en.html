[Levush] if after the fact, etc. Olas Tamid wrote that he does not know the Levush's source, and that it seems permitted even initially. But I have already written that there is a complete proof for the Levush, for otherwise the words of the Shulchan Aruch would contradict one another. Olas Tamid also overlooked Lechem Chamudos, daf 70, who brought proof from the responsum of the Rashba cited by the Beit Yosef in siman 213. See also Beit Yosef in siman 174. Furthermore, Piskei Rekanati, siman 80, writes that even after the fact he does not fulfill his obligation, and the Magen Avraham's view also inclines this way and he expresses wonder at the Beit Yosef.
In one place, etc. Malbushei Yom Tov asks about people sitting together on a moving wagon who say, 'Let us eat.' Does the wagon join them into one fixed group? In my humble opinion there is proof from what I wrote in siman 2 in the name of the Taz, that one who sits in a wagon is considered like one riding. As long as the wagon is moving, they are like riders traveling and there is no fixed meal. See below siman 94, seif 4. Afterward Magen Avraham was printed, and he writes that since all three sit together as one group perhaps they do combine. This requires study. On a ship, however, it is obvious that there is a full fixed meal. A servant too can fulfill his obligation with the host's HaMotzi even if he did not formally join them at the table. See above, end of siman 89 and siman 94, where wagon and ship appear equivalent, though one can distinguish them. It seems proper to be lenient also in a wagon where they intend to join, because after the fact people fulfill their obligation in any event, and the matter is only d'rabbanan.
