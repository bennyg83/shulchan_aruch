Even on Shabbos, when he is obligated, etc. This refers to all three meals. Although they are obligatory, the obligation is not upon him as a mitzvah in itself, but so that he should enjoy the Shabbos meal. The mitzvah itself does not create an absolute obligation, for if he enjoys fasting, he need not eat, as stated in siman 288. Therefore these blessings are included among other blessings over benefit.
Although he does not taste. The same applies when he has already fulfilled the mitzvah of eating matzah and has already recited Kiddush over wine. The reason in all these cases is that the blessing of Kiddush and the eating of a kezayis of matzah are obligations of mitzvos incumbent on the person; automatically all are included in the rule of guarantorship, as above. The same applies on the first and second nights of Sukkos, when one is obligated to eat a kezayis of bread in the sukkah; he may discharge another even though he does not taste himself.

Before, etc. The Shelah wrote that one should also leave a little from the HaMotzi slice to eat after his meal, so that the taste of HaMotzi remains in his mouth. One should not feed an animal, bird, or non-Jew from the HaMotzi slice, or from the piece that touched the HaMotzi slice. Meaning, from the same slice that he first cut from the whole loaf he then cuts a small piece for HaMotzi; that slice as well should not be fed to them, for this is not honor for the mitzvah.
