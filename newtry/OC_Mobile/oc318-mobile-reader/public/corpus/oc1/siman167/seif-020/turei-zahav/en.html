"And he may not recite the blessing," etc. The explanation is that we rule that with all blessings, once a person has fulfilled his own obligation he may nevertheless discharge the obligation of others, except for blessings over enjoyment. If he already fulfilled such a blessing, he may not recite it merely for others. Therefore, regarding HaMotzi over matzah and Borei Pri HaGafen over the wine of Kiddush, these are not treated simply as blessings over enjoyment, because there is a mitzvah incumbent upon him. He may therefore discharge others even when he does not taste together with them. The bread of Shabbos is different. Although there is an obligation to eat it, the blessing remains a blessing over enjoyment, and one who is not eating may not discharge others. The reason appears to be that bread is a person's principal ordinary food and eating bread is not uniquely an act of mitzvah, since he eats bread on weekdays as well. Matzah and the wine of Kiddush are different because there is a special change in their use at that time. "From which he broke." Bach writes that the portion of bread upon which the mitzvah was performed should be treated with special respect and should not be fed to an animal, bird, or other inappropriate recipient. The portion should be consumed in a respectful manner by one for whom it is fitting. I have seen many who are careful with remnants of bread used for a mitzvah in this way. I brought a further proof in my work on Rashi, Parashat Bo, regarding the verse, "their kneading bowls bound in their garments," etc., that there is an expression of cherishing a mitzvah in what remains from matzah and maror.
