2) There: Throughout the day, whenever he relieves himself, etc., he recites Asher Yatzar. If one drank a laxative and intends to continue evacuating his bowels until the waste has been fully expelled, since he has not diverted his attention from relieving himself, he should not recite Asher Yatzar until the evacuation is complete. Halakhot Ketanot, part 1, siman 86; Shulchan Aruch HaRav, ot 6; Kitzur Shulchan Aruch, section 4, ot 6. The work Kerem Shlomo writes in the name of the responsa of Mahara’af that he should mentally contemplate the blessing; see there. Yad Aharon, of blessed memory, in Hagahot HaTur, however, disagrees with Halakhot Ketanot and holds that he should recite the blessing each time; see there. The Chida, of blessed memory, cites his words in Birkei Yosef, ot 2, and writes that the widespread custom is to recite the blessing each time and that the position of Halakhot Ketanot is not accepted. Kisei Eliyahu, ot 3, writes likewise, as do Shalmei Tzibur, page 45, and Zachor LeAvraham, Hilkhot Berakhot, ot 50, in the name of the Chida, of blessed memory. Ruach Chaim also writes: ‘The present custom is to recite the blessing each time, since one does not know whether he will need to relieve himself again, and in any event he diverts his attention from it. Moreover, although the established rule is that when the halachic authorities disagree whether to recite a blessing one does not recite it, where the people have customarily recited it one does not invoke the rule that a doubtful blessing is omitted, because custom is decisive, as is explained by Birkei Yosef, Even HaEzer, siman 219, and Nehar Shalom there, se’if katan 3,’ etc. Pnei Yitzchak, ot 7, writes likewise, concluding that the view of Beit Oved, ot 3, which applies the rule of doubtful blessings and says to wait until he has finished, is not accepted; see there. Shaarei Teshuvah, ot 1, likewise cites Shalmei Tzibur. Shaarei Teshuvah concludes, however, that in a case of severe diarrhea, when he feels that he will immediately need to relieve himself a second time, it is prohibited to recite the blessing, as stated below in siman 92, where it is prohibited even with respect to words of Torah; see there. Our custom is therefore to recite the blessing each time, in accordance with the Chida and those who agree with him. The rule that a doubtful blessing is omitted does not apply against an established custom, as Ruach Chaim writes. Zachor LeAvraham, part 1, Hilkhot Berakhot, ot 60, writes likewise in the name of Terumat HaDeshen, siman 34; Radbaz, part 1, siman 290; and Maharashach, who cites the Radbaz, part 3, page 14b. Zachor LeAvraham concludes that this appears to be the accepted ruling. Ben Ish Chai, parashat Lekh Lekha, ot 15, writes likewise.
