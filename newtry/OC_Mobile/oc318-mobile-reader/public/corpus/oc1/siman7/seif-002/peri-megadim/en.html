Must — Taz: Here it implies that he already urinated a second time; on this basis he challenged that the author himself in 178:1 and 2 agrees as he agrees in one blessing, and likewise the author and the Rav in the gloss there 2 — if one is obligated to bless Birkas HaMazon first before he eats, not so when he passed and ate again, for everyone agrees one blessing suffices — and this is why he wonders how the author established this here in the Shulchan Aruch. And what he wrote regarding Musaf — it has no compensation in Mincha is an unusual expression; and according to Rashi, who says until seven hours — the matter is ready. But for us, who establish like the Rabbis, Musaf [can be said] all day; if the whole day passed, then it has no compensation at night, as stated in 178:6 and 186:1 — see there. And the distinction the Taz makes between here and eating is that there is no fixed [time]… Therefore if he diverted his attention from eating — for the author in 178:1 and 2 he must bless first Birkas HaMazon — so too here if he passed and ate again he must bless twice Birkas HaMazon; but for us there, 2 in the gloss, never needs twice Birkas HaMazon. And here if he diverted his attention, the obligation of blessing falls immediately; therefore the Be'er Heitev was silent and I concede here to the author. And what he wrote in Yoreh De'ah siman 19 — there it deals with when many birds were before him and he blessed and slaughtered one — you must bless on the second slaughter separately; and after the first slaughter was removed, he must complete it all — meaning covering before he slaughters again. And similarly for eating: he said "come let us bless," for it is forbidden to eat unless he blessed HaMotzi; nevertheless he need not bless twice Birkas HaMazon (178 in gloss), for in Birkas HaMazon there is no completion of the act except praise and thanks, and one thanks suffices once — not so… the Taz holds that the essence in Birkas HaMazon depends on him, that if he wishes he may still eat with HaMotzi and bless afterward Birkas HaMazon once; nevertheless it is not clear how it is proved from there, for on the contrary, since it writes praise and thanks, it implies the essence of the distinction of blessings of praise — thanks suffices — and similarly Asher Yatzar is praise. And see Levush, who gave a reason to distinguish, that here the second blessing comes of itself in human nature, not so Birkas HaMazon which one enjoys willingly; therefore one need not bless twice. For halachah we establish doubtful blessings leniently, like Acharonim and Nosei Keilim and Shelah and Magen Avraham and Bach and his teacher the Chassid, and Eliyah Rabbah letter 2 agreed to the essence of Shelah and Taz; and still doubtful leniency in Rabbinic [matter] and serious "do not bear" [false oath]. And if he wishes without God's name and kingship, he may say the matter is in his hands. The first [blessing] is for the obligation and the second for compensation, like prayer below 178:1. However for the author, who blesses twice with God's name and kingship, there is no compensation here, for all his day is its time and forever.

And know that if one urinated and diverted his attention and did not bless, and afterward wishes to urinate — it appears he must bless first for what passed: one reason is that it is a double doubt — perhaps the halachah follows the author's ruling and perhaps the halachah in 178 like the author, that even at a meal if he diverted attention he must bless first Birkas HaMazon, and all the more so here. And see Chayei Adam chapter 4, halachah 6, that double doubt is for stringency and he must bless. However we were uncertain whether a double doubt helps for blessings per the Rambam that doubtful Biblical [violation] is permitted… or serious "do not bear" (and this will be explained elsewhere in the introduction to the Laws of Blessings). Also Rama with the author — no doubt. Nevertheless here one may say it is certainly so that he must bless first Asher Yatzar per all opinions, as I wrote. And see Levush and "see what is written below" from this.

Question: That which we establish per the ruling of the Shulchan Aruch all day — even if he relieved himself and wiped and rubbed and prays immediately — he does not bless Al Netilat Yadayim; if the opportunity arises to read [Kerias Shema] — one doubt. Furthermore, if he wishes to taste before prayer something whose dipping is in liquid and the like — can he bless Al Netilat Yadayim? Answer: I will answer briefly: examine Tur Orach Chayim 473, what he wrote "he shall urinate and rub," etc. — meaning that he should bless Al Netilat Yadayim; and Beit Yosef, Bach, and Prisha wondered about him (Taz 473 letter 7 and Darkei Moshe there). And if we say it is a double doubt obligating a blessing, as Chayei Adam wrote chapter 4, halachah 6 — if so, it would be a double doubt: perhaps the halachah is that one whose dipping is in liquid blesses, and perhaps the halachah is as the Acharonim in siman 4; and if so, for us regarding prayer the Darkei Moshe wrote here that because of doubtful blessings we establish per the author's ruling, and here there would be a double doubt — except that since the author did not give this advice in 473:6 to wash and rub, it implies that even a double doubt does not help. This is what we wished to explain, and it will be explained further in the Laws of Blessings on this. And here one should not elaborate.

What the author wrote in seif 2 — urinated and did not rub for preparation — I had thought only one hand, the one he is accustomed to rub with; and this fits well that incident of the day that Beit Yosef brought for a meal — one takes one hand and rubs it; and that of Kiddush — Bach explained that for the conclusion, one hand; and he rubs only with the left hand, and this is not so, for to wipe it is forbidden with the right, but rubbing is permitted, as evident siman 43 regarding tefillin in synagogue — see there; and therefore one hand applies in both. And the Gemara in Yoma states the law; but from piety for preparation, and all the more leaving a fixed bathroom, there is doubt in any case because of ruach ra, as stated above, and see Magen Avraham on this.

Eliyah Rabbah in the name of Maharshal in responsa — Asher Yatzar before Birkas HaMazon because it is more frequent — see there. However immediacy was not given to Asher Yatzar, for it is impossible that he must wash his hands first; and even to urinate without rubbing — we do not disagree with Magen Avraham on this.
