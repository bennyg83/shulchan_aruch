[2] One is obligated to bless twice, etc. And Bach extended [his words] to challenge the Levush and Shulchan Aruch that one need not bless Asher Yatzar twice. And Nachalas Tzvi, Olas Tamid, Shelah, and Magen Avraham agreed—see there. And it is not difficult at all, as the Levush himself answers his words. And likewise Taz extended [his words] to strengthen the ruling of Shulchan Aruch and Levush—and this is the main [view]. However, he wrote in Sefer Mekor Chaim that specifically when he left the bathroom and returned from his place he is considered to have changed his mind; but if he has still not moved from his place, he is not called one who changed his mind. Rashal wrote in a responsum, siman 97: One who ate something that obligates upon it the concluding blessing Borei Nefashos Rabos and forgot, and went to urinate, and remembered before blessing Asher Yatzar—he should bless Asher Yatzar first and afterward Borei Nefashos Rabos, because the frequent comes first—until here. But one should not say the reason that Borei Nefashos Rabos is an interruption between netilah and the blessing of Asher Yatzar, for there is no prohibition to interrupt; the blessing of Asher Yatzar does not depend on netilah, for if he did not rub [his hands] he need not perform netilah according to many poskim, and even so he blesses Asher Yatzar. And see below, siman 165, and below, siman 227, seif 3—laws belonging to this siman. See in Eliyahu Zuta and Lechem Chamudos, page 73.
