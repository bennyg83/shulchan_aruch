One who drank a laxative and has in mind to evacuate his bowels until the filth is cleared from them—since he did not distract his mind from relieving himself, therefore he should not bless Asher Yatzar until after he finishes evacuating—Leket Yosher, part 1, siman 86; Rebbe Tzaddok, letter 6; Kitzur Shulchan Aruch, siman 4, letter 6. And in the book Kaf Rachashim he wrote in the name of a responsum of Maharaf that he contemplates it in his heart—see there. However, the Yisa Berachah, may he rest in peace, in Hagahos Turi disagreed with Leket Yosher and held that he should bless each time—see there. And Chida, may he rest in peace, brought his words in Birkei Yosef, letter 2, and wrote that so is the widespread custom—to bless each time—and there is no concern for the view of Leket Yosher; and likewise Kitzur Shulchan Aruch, letter 3; and likewise Shulchan HaTahor, page 45; and the compiler in Hilchos Berachos, letter 50, in the name of Chida, may he rest in peace; and likewise Ruach Chayyim, and these are his words: And the custom now is to bless each time, for one does not know whether he will relieve himself again another time, and by law he distracts his mind from this—and apart from the law, we establish that wherever the poskim disagree whether one blesses, he blesses, and the people are accustomed to bless—one should not say in this case "safeik berachos lehakel," for custom is primary, as is explained from the words of Birkei Yosef, Orach Chayyim, siman 219, and in the book Nefesh HaShulchan there, seif katan 3, etc.—until here. And likewise our teacher Penei Yitzchak, may he live long, there, letter 7, and he concluded not like Be'er Yitzchak, letter 3, who wrote "safeik berachos lehakel" and he should wait to bless until he finishes—see there, for this does not exist—until here. And likewise Sha'arei Teshuvah, letter 1, in the name of Shulchan HaTahor; and he concluded there in Sha'arei Teshuvah, and these are his words: However, it appears that with strong diarrhea, when he feels that he will immediately need to relieve himself again, it is forbidden to bless, as below, siman 92, that even in words of Torah it is forbidden—see there, etc.—until here. And so is our custom to bless each time, as Chida and his colleagues [wrote]; and one should not say in this case "safeik berachos lehakel," for in a place of custom we do not say "safeik berachos lehakel," as Ruach Chayyim wrote. And likewise the compiler, part 1, in Hilchos Berachos, letter 60, in the name of Teshuvos HaRadak, siman 34, and Radbaz, part 1, siman 271, and Maharshach brought them from Radvam, part 3, page 14b; and the compiler concluded there that it appears this is agreed upon; and likewise the rabbi the son of Avraham Chaim, may he live long, parashas Lech Lecha, letter 15.
