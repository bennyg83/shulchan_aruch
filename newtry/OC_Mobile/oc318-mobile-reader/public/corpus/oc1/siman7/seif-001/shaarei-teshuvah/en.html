"Asher Yatzar." See Baer Heitev concerning one who drinks a laxative beverage, etc. Shalmei Tzibbur writes that the widespread custom is to recite Asher Yatzar each time; see there. However, it appears that where one has severe diarrhea and feels that he will immediately need to relieve himself again, it is forbidden to recite the blessing, as explained below in siman 92, where even Torah study is forbidden under such circumstances; see there. This is also evident from what Shibbolei HaLeket writes in the name of a manuscript responsum of Mahara Nachum. He was asked about one who relieved himself or urinated and did not recite Asher Yatzar: how long does he retain the ability to recite it? He concludes that if he remembers before he again begins to feel the need to relieve himself, he should certainly recite the blessing. If he remembers only after that urge has begun again, he should not recite it. All the more so in the present case. Regarding what was written concerning one who leaves the restroom, see Eliyah Rabbah, where it is evident from siman 165 that he need not perform an additional washing; see also siman 227. Nevertheless, some are stringent upon themselves, as written above in siman 4 in the name of Machazik Berachah, that senior rabbis had the practice of washing 3 times after leaving the restroom. See there.
