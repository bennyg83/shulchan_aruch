1) [Seif 1] Throughout the day, whenever he relieves himself, etc., he recites Asher Yatzar. If one ate something requiring a final blessing, in the amount of a kezayit, and forgot to recite it before urinating, he should first recite Asher Yatzar because it is more frequent. Furthermore, Asher Yatzar has no minimum measure, whereas a final blessing requires the measure of a kezayit or a revi’it. It is therefore proper to recite Asher Yatzar first. Olat Tamid, se’if katan 1, in the name of the Responsa of Maharshal, siman 97; Ateret Zekenim, siman 165. Magen Avraham there, se’if katan 3, writes likewise in the name of the aforementioned authority, as do Sovea Semachot on this siman, ot 2; Eliyah Rabbah, se’if katan 2; Shulchan Aruch HaRav, ot 7; Erekh HaShulchan, siman 165, ot 1, in the name of Matteh Moshe, siman 365; Rabbi Akiva Eiger on this siman; Zachor LeAvraham, Hilkhot Berakhot, ot 1; Siddur Beit Yaakov, ot 6; Ruach Chaim, ot 1; Pnei Yitzchak, section alef, ot 10; and Ben Ish Chai, parashat Vayetze, ot 13. If he urinated before Birkat HaMazon, Rabbi Akiva Eiger writes in his novellae here that it requires consideration which should take precedence. The words of Ruach Chaim there, however, imply that Asher Yatzar comes first; see there. This is how one should act, since we are concerned that he may forget.
