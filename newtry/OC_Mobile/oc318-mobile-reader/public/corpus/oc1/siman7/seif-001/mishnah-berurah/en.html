(1) One blesses, etc. — and even if a long time passes after relieving himself, he is nevertheless obligated to bless. If he did not bless Asher Yatzar until he wishes to urinate again, the Peri Megadim holds that before he urinates he should bless Asher Yatzar for the first urination; but in Sha'ar HaTzedek he agreed in the name of several later authorities that if he remembered after he began to feel the urge, he should not bless again — see there.

(2) Asher Yatzar — Maharshal wrote in responsa siman 97: one who ate something obligating a concluding blessing and forgot, and urinated, and remembered before he came to bless — which comes first? He answered that Asher Yatzar comes first because it is more frequent. One who drinks a laxative, intending from the outset to evacuate his bowels until the filth is cleared from them — therefore some say he should bless only after finishing the evacuation, and some say he blesses each time; the custom follows the latter view. However, with strong diarrhea where he feels he will immediately need to evacuate again, it is forbidden to bless, as below siman 92, where even Bedi'eved it is forbidden [Sha'ar HaTzedek].

(3) To pray immediately — although we establish in siman 233, seif 2, that for Mincha prayer and likewise for Ma'ariv, even if he does not know that they became soiled, he must wash his hands, and all the more so when he relieved himself — nevertheless he does not bless Al Netilat Yadayim except in the morning. And see in Sefer Eretzot HaChayim, who wrote that the majority of poskim hold that if he relieved himself for major needs and afterward wishes to pray, he blesses Al Netilat Yadayim. Nevertheless, Lechatchilah one should not deviate from the ruling of the Shulchan Aruch; and see in Sefer Ma'aseh Rav.

(4) That he rubbed on them — the splashes of urine; and the same applies for major needs when he relieved himself and wiped.
