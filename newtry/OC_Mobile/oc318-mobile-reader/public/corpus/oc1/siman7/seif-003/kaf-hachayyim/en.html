3) [Seif 2] If he urinated and did not rub, etc., he need not wash his hands except because of, etc. Likewise, if he defecated but did not wipe himself, he need wash his hands only for cleanliness. This is evident from the words of Maran, of blessed memory, in Beit Yosef, siman 613, and from the Bach there. Although in this siman the Bach writes that one who relieves himself and does not wipe has the same law as one who wipes, because it is presumed that he touched soiled flesh, this applies only because he washed his body; the Bach’s words here must be understood likewise. This follows the first opinion cited by the Tur. The Rosh, however, does not hold this way, as the Bach writes in siman 613, and Maran, of blessed memory, rules accordingly in siman 613, se’if 3. Shulchan Gavoha there, ot 9, writes likewise. Regarding what the Taz writes there, se’if katan 2, from the Tur’s words in siman 554, see Nehar Shalom there, who writes that this proves nothing, because that passage concerns a case in which one rubbed; this is clear. Yeshuiot Yaakov, ot 1, nevertheless writes that one should be stringent even if he did not rub, since we are concerned that his hands may have touched the urine while he was urinating; see there. Shulchan Aruch HaRav, ot 2, writes that if he was careful not to touch his nakedness, he need wash only because of cleanliness; see there. All this concerns one who relieved himself or urinated in a field or a similar place. If, however, he entered a permanent privy, he must wash his hands because of the impure spirit that rests on the hands in a privy, as stated above in siman 4, ot 65. See also Peri Megadim, siman 613, Mishbetzot Zahav, se’if katan 2.
