3) [Seif 2] One who urinated and did not rub, etc.—he need not perform netilas yadayim except because of, etc. And likewise if he relieved himself for adults and did not wipe—he need not perform netilas yadayim except because of cleanliness, as is explained from the words of Maran, may he rest in peace, in Beit Yosef, siman 213, and Bach there. And although in this siman Bach wrote that one who relieves himself and did not wipe—his law is like one who wiped, for there is a presumption that he touched filthy flesh, or alternatively because he soiled his body—and so too must be said according to Beis Yosef in this siman; that is, in the view of Yisa Berachah that Tur brought. However, the Rosh did not hold so, as Bach wrote in siman 213; and Maran, may he rest in peace, ruled so in siman 213, seif 3; and likewise Shulchan Gavoah there, letter 9. And what Taz wrote there, seif katan 2, from the words of Tur, siman 554—see Nefesh HaShulchan there, who wrote that nothing is lacking from what is discussed there when he rubbed, and this is simple. However, Yeshuos Yaakov, letter 1, wrote that one should be stringent even without rubbing, for we are concerned lest he touched his hands at the time of urinating—see there. And Rebbe Tzaddok, letter 2, wrote that if he was careful not to touch his nakedness, he need not perform netilah except because of preparation—see there. And all this discusses a case such as when he relieved himself outdoors or urinated on the face of the field and the like; but if he entered a permanent synagogue, he must perform netilas yadayim because of the evil spirit that dwells on the hands in the synagogue, as written above, siman 4, letter 65. And see Peri Megadim, siman 213, in Mishbetzos Zahav, seif katan 2.
