1) [Seif 1] One may not travel more than three parsaos on Friday, etc. This is measured from the beginning of the day. Rambam, chapter 30, halachah 11. Since we maintain that an average person walks ten parsaos in a day, the travel time would extend from morning until shortly before one third of the day, leaving two thirds of the day to prepare the needs of the meal. According to this, it appears that even one traveling by wagon or riding a horse should not travel beyond this measure, so that time remains to prepare the meal. Bach, however, writes: It appears that this law applies specifically to one traveling on foot, but the common custom is to be lenient when traveling by wagon or riding a horse. Leave them be; it is preferable that they act unknowingly rather than intentionally. Nevertheless, one must take care not to travel until evening, even when hurrying to reach his city and home, because people frequently stumble and desecrate Shabbos by failing to reach the city until night. Rather, he should spend Shabbos in a village one or two parsaos from the city and not desecrate Shabbos. These are his words. Ateres Zekeinim, paragraph 1; Magen Avraham, paragraph 1; Eliyah Rabbah, paragraph 3. See below, paragraph 5.
2) There: More than three parsaos, etc. If traveling as far as three parsaos will not leave him sufficient time to prepare, and he would need to travel farther, he should not set out. Eliyah Rabbah, paragraph 1.
3) There: And he will be able to prepare, etc. In these countries, most people prepare meals in abundance, and therefore they are not careful about this. Agudah, end of Sukkah; Magen Avraham, paragraph 2. Eliyah Rabbah, paragraph 3, however, writes that he did not see any of the great early halachic authorities mention this. It appears that he means that in his opinion one should be stringent in accordance with the Shulchan Aruch and should not rely upon the fact that people prepare extra food.
4) There: So that he will reach his home, etc. Many transgressions can result from this. When he arrives close to Shabbos, his host, or even those in his own home, may cook additional food for him and desecrate Shabbos. All the more so, he may be delayed on the road until it is actually dark, leading to numerous Shabbos violations involving carrying out, bringing in, leaving the techum, and the requirement that his animal rest. A person should therefore take all this to heart and not allow the evil inclination to entice him by saying, 'The day is still long and the road is good.' Chayei Adam, klal 1, paragraph 7.
