(1) More than three parasangs: measured from the beginning of the day. This is approximately one third of the day, since an average person walks ten parasangs in a day. One traveling by wagon or riding a horse, who moves more quickly, may travel much more than three parasangs until one third of the day. Bach appears to permit one traveling by wagon to continue even after midday, provided that he stops for Shabbos while much of the day remains, enough to prepare Shabbos needs.
(2) To his home: meaning, to the place where he will observe Shabbos.
(3) When he is traveling home: Even if he intends to enjoy Shabbos more at home, it is forbidden, lest they not know of his arrival and have prepared nothing for him. In these countries most people prepare Shabbos provisions generously, and therefore people are generally not careful about this, whether traveling home or staying as a guest. Magen Avraham in the name of Agudah. Nevertheless, many later authorities write that one should initially take care not to walk or travel until close to evening, since this frequently leads to Shabbos desecration. A host, or even members of his own household, may add cooking for him when he arrives close to Shabbos. He may also fail to reach his lodging or home before nightfall, resulting in carrying, entering or leaving Shabbos boundary, and violating the obligation that his animal rest. A person should therefore take all this to heart and hasten to stop even in a village. He should not allow the evil inclination to tell him that much of the day remains and the road is good. If he nevertheless traveled until nightfall and was within a city’s boundary during twilight, he may walk throughout the city, as stated in siman 416. He must descend from the wagon or horse; see siman 266, where this will be explained, with God’s help.
(4) This applies when he is, and so forth: meaning, at the place where the three parasangs end. So writes Levush; see Eliyah Rabbah.
(5) That is not, and so forth: Stopping on the road is unsafe, since all roads are presumed dangerous. Therefore, even if he has sufficient provisions for Shabbos, he may travel from there until reaching a safe place.
(6) Any number of parasangs: Nevertheless, he should take care not to delay his arrival until close to evening, as stated above in seif katan 3.
