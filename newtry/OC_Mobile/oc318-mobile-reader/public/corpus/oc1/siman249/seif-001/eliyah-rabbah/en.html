[2] [Levush:] Or he has provisions with him, and so forth. This means that at the place to which he is going he has provisions, although at the place he will ultimately reach he may have none; he is nevertheless permitted to go because of concern for danger. But when he carries his food with him, he may travel even through a secure area. This appears correct to me, and this is how the Rivash explains it at the end of siman 101. This excludes the view of the Olas Tamid, who forbids travel through a secure area even when one carries food, for according to the second formulation, the law follows the fact that he relies upon his host, and yet travel is forbidden. He evidently did not see the Rivash cited above. It also appears that he was imprecise, for according to the second formulation the case does not concern one carrying food at all. This is likewise demonstrated by Maharshal’s glosses to Sefer Mitzvos Gadol, positive commandment 30. Moreover, the Ran in the first chapter of Shabbos explains that the reliance is upon the host, not, as Rashi explains, upon what he carries with him on the road. See there. I likewise found in the Perishah in the name of Maharshal: “According to all opinions, if he prepared his meal and took it with him, it is permitted. Nevertheless, it appears to me that he should not continue until evening, lest he fail to reach lodging while it is still day and travel excessively.” If he in fact continued until nightfall and is near the city’s Shabbos boundary, he may enter the city, for we rule in siman 400 that he may enter it and traverse all of it. In any event, however, it is forbidden to remain seated in the wagon or upon the horse. See siman 266, seif katan 9.
[3] [Levush:] Or he sent, and so forth. Magen Avraham wrote in the name of the Agudah that in these countries most people prepare food generously, and therefore people are not careful about this restriction. I have not seen any of the great early authorities mention this. Nevertheless, one traveling by horse or wagon may, according to the Bach, be lenient and travel more than three parasangs, but he must not continue until evening.
