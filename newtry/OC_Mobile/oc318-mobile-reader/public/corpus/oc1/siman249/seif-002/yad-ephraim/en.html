Magen Avraham s.k. 3 — and it is a complete mishnah; it is a textual error here and should read above where he wrote that an engagement feast is a mitzvah feast even on erev Pesach; and it is a complete mishnah in Pesachim daf 49, etc., as should be written; and one could say there it deals with chutz la'aretz, for in chutz la'aretz it is forbidden to make an engagement feast, as stated in siman 646; and if so, it cannot be established there in chutz la'aretz — such as if he has rolled dough, etc.; and therefore it deals with erev Pesach; if so, all the more so erev Shabbat, as proven from the laws of erev Pesach that states even for R' Yosi who is lenient on erev Shabbat and yom tov on erev Pesach because of the obligation of matzah — he agrees on erev Shabbat.
And what Magen Avraham wrote — and further difficult, that in siman 646, etc. — meaning, if so it is difficult on Bach who wrote that the same applies to a wedding feast, etc. — for it implies since he marries on erev regel he also makes the feast then; and even though in siman 646 it is stated that he makes the feast on the regel — this is to say it is permitted even in such a case that he makes the feast on the regel, and there is no prohibition because of ein ma'arivin, etc.; but nevertheless the conclusion of the matter that the Gemara says it is permitted to marry on erev regel — meaning even if he makes the feast then too it is permitted.
And what he wrote — and further difficult, since he betrothed on erev Shabbat, etc. — ostensibly requires study — how is it mentioned here that he betrothed on erev Shabbat. And it is possible it refers to Bach's words who compared a wedding feast to an engagement feast; and in weddings it certainly deals with one who married today — and if so, in engagement in such a case it is certainly permitted, for it is not possible on another day lest another precede him; and if so, again the time of the feast is today; and therefore Magen Avraham wrote that he explained the Yerushalmi — an engagement feast is forbidden if he betrothed beforehand, etc. And requires study — for if so, what is the superiority of an engagement feast, for below in siman 646 it is stated that if he feasts not at the time of betrothal there is no mitzvah; and on the contrary, this itself teaches that in such a case it is not in the category of a mitzvah feast upon which joy of betrothal applies — and therefore forbidden on erev Shabbat; and here in Shulchan Aruch too it deals in such a case that he already betrothed. And it is further possible there is a distinction between that in siman 646 that deals with one who already feasted an engagement feast but now eats with his fiancée — this is not a mitzvah but a feast of gifts, as explained in siman 644 on erev Shabbat. Not so here — even though it deals with a feast not at the time of betrothal, nevertheless he has not yet feasted an engagement feast at all, and it is the first feast with him; and nevertheless forbidden on erev Shabbat since it is not at the actual time of betrothal — if so, its time is not fixed today; not so when he betroths on erev Shabbat — permitted to make the feast too. And nevertheless requires study — for in chutz la'aretz we hold it is permitted to betroth, and nevertheless forbidden to make a feast at the time of betrothal; and we do not say since he betroths today it is a fixed time like milah and pidyon haben. And even though there in milah and pidyon haben the reason is there is no joy — nevertheless this reason was needed only to permit even not at its time, as explained in Taz s.k. 4; but at its time it is permitted since its time is fixed, as Tosafot explain there; and if so, here too we could say thus when he betroths in chutz la'aretz — it is called a fixed time for the feast, as Magen Avraham wrote here regarding betrothal on erev Shabbat — requires study. And see in Magen Avraham siman 551 s.k. 34 who wrote that a feast not at the time of betrothal — there is none; and nevertheless it is a mitzvah feast; and afterward he wrote that in siman 644 it implies it is not a mitzvah feast on erev Shabbat; and in my humble opinion one must distinguish as he wrote.
And what Magen Avraham wrote in the first chapter of Taanit, etc. — it is possible he means: since we see they permitted betrothal even on the ninth of Av lest another precede him — all the more so on erev Shabbat it is permitted for this reason, and he has no reason to defer waiting for another day; if so, again the time of the feast is fixed and permitted on erev Shabbat.
And what he wrote — and requires study on Shulchan Gavoah, etc. — see Darkei Moshe siman 251 who brought the words of Shulchan Gavoah who wrote thus in the name of Yerushalmi; and Darkei Moshe wrote the custom is simple to betroth on erev Shabbat; and he wrote that Shulchan Gavoah explains what Yerushalmi said — "this that you say engagement feast" — meaning he wants to betroth and also make the feast today, and then it is forbidden because he must toil at the feast; but to betroth alone — such as the feast is already prepared from yesterday, or he does not want to make the feast now — permitted; and in such a case Shulchan Gavoah deals when he wants to make the feast now too — then forbidden to betroth because of evasion; and so it appears from what Shulchan Gavoah wrote on this; and likewise in Darkei Moshe one should reduce so they busy themselves with Shabbat needs — end quote; and they brought Rama at the end of siman 251 — it appears he holds that also in betrothal the reason is lest he be preoccupied at the feast and unable to deal with Shabbat needs; but betrothal itself is obvious there is no toil and preoccupation, for no time is needed for this; and it is possible this is Magen Avraham's intent in what he wrote in the first chapter of Taanit, etc. — meaning it is impossible to explain Yerushalmi's intent that the main prohibition in betrothal is because he must toil in feast needs — behold in betrothal alone there is no prohibition on erev Shabbat at all from betrothal itself; and regarding this one cannot say "even on the ninth of Av," etc. — for this applies specifically if the reason for permission on erev Shabbat is lest another precede him — one can say even on the ninth of Av it is permitted for this reason; not so if we say originally there is no place of prohibition in betrothal on erev Shabbat except because of the feast — and if he wants to make the feast specifically at the time of betrothal, they did not permit him even to betroth — if so, what is "even the ninth of Av" that it says here — we permit betrothal because lest another precede him:
