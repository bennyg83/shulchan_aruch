It is forbidden to establish, etc. Rambam's wording is not so; rather the rabbi author of Shulchan Aruch mixed in the explanation of the Rav HaMagid. And thus Rambam in chapter 30: It is forbidden to establish a feast and drinking on erev Shabbat because of honor of Shabbat, and it is permitted to eat and drink until darkening; and nevertheless from honor of Shabbat a person should refrain from minchah onward from establishing a feast so he enter Shabbat desiring to eat — end quote; and the Rav HaMagid explained it, and thus: It appears from our rabbi's words that a feast and drinking one is not accustomed to on weekdays is forbidden to do on erev Shabbat, and the entire day is included in the prohibition; and he proved this from the statement in Gitin chapter HaSholeach — two families, etc.; and to eat and drink without establishing a meal — even a meal one is accustomed to on weekdays — all day is permitted to begin, as explained in chapter Erev Pesach and like R' Yosi; and to establish a feast one is accustomed to on weekdays — a mitzvah to refrain from the ninth hour onward, for our rabbi holds that even though we hold like R' Yosi who said "he eats and walks until darkening" — this is specifically without establishing a meal, as above per our rabbi's view — and this is correct — end quote. And Tur wrote and thus: It appears that even before minchah one should not establish, for erev Shabbat he speaks generally — for he learned in chapter HaSholeach: R' Yosi — two families were in Jerusalem; one established a feast on erev Shabbat and one on Shabbat — meaning at the time of beit hamidrash — and both were uprooted — end quote. And per the Rav HaMagid's explanation — no difficulty on what Tur wrote, for he makes two passages in Rambam's words — in the first it deals with a feast one is not accustomed to on weekdays — this is forbidden all day; and in the last it deals with accustomed, etc.; and what the rabbi wrote "even a feast one is accustomed to," etc. — the wording "even" is not settled for me, for it deals specifically with accustomed; and it appears regarding a feast he said "even" — meaning even a non-casual feast is permitted by law, and the prohibition is only because of a mitzvah; but this distinction of "accustomed" is not well settled in my mind, for we do not find this, and Rambam should have mentioned it in his explanation; and further — "and nevertheless," etc. — it is difficult, for it should have said "but to establish a feast one should refrain"; and further — why did Rambam mention first feast and drinking, and in the last mention only feast; and further — first he said generally "because of honor of Shabbat" and in the last "so he enter Shabbat," etc. — he certainly did not write this change for nothing; and indeed they are two matters, as Rosh wrote in chapter Erev Pesach, and thus: It is possible that even though R' Yosi is permitted to begin and is not concerned he will enter when hungry — nevertheless when it darkens he must stop because of honor of Shabbat — end quote. And it appears to me to explain Rambam's words in this way: First it was difficult for him — we hold like R' Yosi that he eats and walks all day; and it is stated in chapter HaSholeach that one family was uprooted because of establishing on erev Shabbat; and it does not suffice to answer this difficulty and say R' Yosi does not deal with establishing — for if there is permission without establishing even all day, there is no reason the family should be uprooted because of establishing; rather the answer to this is that that family established a feast and drinking — they set themselves to drink in the manner of drunken drinking — and therefore they were punished; and therefore Rambam mentioned: forbidden to establish feast and drinking — and this all day, for through getting drunk he will nullify Shabbat entirely; and if so there are two precise points here: first — without establishing, even eating and drinking, there is no prohibition; and second — even with establishing a feast, it is permitted as long as he does not also establish drinking; therefore he afterward said: and it is permitted to eat and drink until darkening — this is the first precision; and afterward he said: and nevertheless a person should refrain from establishing a feast — meaning even without establishing drinking, which is permitted by law, for he will not get drunk and will not nullify honor of Shabbat entirely — nevertheless a person should refrain himself from establishing even a feast alone so he enter Shabbat desiring — as it appears to me, correct in explaining Rambam's words. But Tur did not explain thus; rather he explained that Rambam speaks in one passage and the last is explanation of the first — to say not specifically all day but from minchah onward; and even though he first said generally erev Shabbat — nevertheless he intended the mixture of the day — meaning from minchah onward; and similar to this we find in siman 552 — Tur wrote in the name of Rambam: "From yesterday we did not eat even a lentil stew on the ninth of Av"; and Tur wrote on this that this is regarding the interrupting meal; and further he explained in the last of the first — that he said forbidden — not a full prohibition but a mere mitzvah that he refrain himself, etc.; and on this Tur challenged — erev Shabbat generally implies all day, for we say two families established — one on erev Shabbat and one on Shabbat; and that one on Shabbat — meaning at the time of beit hamidrash — and this is in the morning, for they do not establish midrash after minchah — but certainly in the morning he speaks, for their way was to expound; and if so, that one on erev Shabbat too is in the morning; and by this Tur's wording "meaning beit hamidrash" is well settled; and Mahari Weil forced this, as Beit Yosef wrote; and according to what he wrote it works out:
