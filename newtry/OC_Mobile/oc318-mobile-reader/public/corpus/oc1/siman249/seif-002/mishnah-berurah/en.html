(7) It is forbidden to establish: The Gemara relates that a family in Jerusalem regularly held a meal on Friday and was uprooted.
(8) On Friday: The same applies on the eve of a Festival, since there is likewise a mitzvah to honor and enjoy a Festival. Peri Megadim.
(9) An engagement feast: If he became engaged previously, he may not hold the feast on Friday. Although an engagement feast is a mitzvah feast, he should have held it earlier. If he became engaged on Friday, however, he may hold the feast, since becoming engaged on Friday is permitted lest another precede him; the feast is consequently permitted as well and is considered like a circumcision feast or redemption feast whose time is fixed. Likewise, if the wedding took place on Friday, the feast may be held. Nevertheless, initially, where possible, it is proper to postpone the feast until the next day or another day, even if the engagement occurred on Friday. All this concerns an engagement feast. A feast held for our customary matchmaking agreement is not a mitzvah feast, as Peri Megadim writes in siman 444. It should therefore not be held on Friday even if the match was concluded that day. Eating sweets when the conditions are written is not considered a meal.
(10) The honor of Shabbos: Some explain that, because of the preoccupation with the feast, people will not attend to Shabbos preparations.
(11) So that he enters Shabbos, and so forth: Even if he holds the feast in the morning, since he eats more than usual he will no longer eat the evening meal with appetite.
(12) A circumcision: Even if it is not performed on the eighth day, such as when the child was ill then and it was postponed, its time is still considered fixed, for every moment is its proper time and it is forbidden to leave him uncircumcised even one day. The same applies to redemption of the firstborn after its proper time. Since, as a matter of law, from the thirtieth day onward the obligation rests upon the father at every moment, he may also hold the feast.
(13) It is permitted: It too is a mitzvah feast and is not deferred because of Shabbos meal. Nevertheless, initially it is a mitzvah to hold it early in the morning out of honor for Shabbos, as explained in siman 695:2 concerning the Purim feast; see there. After the fact, it may be held even after Minchah.
(14) And to eat and drink, and so forth: This does not include drinking so much of various beverages that he becomes satiated, for that certainly spoils his appetite, as experience demonstrates. At times intoxication may cause him to miss Shabbos meal entirely. It is therefore a mitzvah to refrain from this, at least from the ninth hour onward.
(15) Even, and so forth: This means “and even.” Certainly, without formally establishing a meal, eating is permitted all day and there is not even a mitzvah to refrain. Even when establishing a meal, if it is not lavish but only of the kind he ordinarily eats on weekdays, it is legally permitted all day; nevertheless, it is a mitzvah to refrain, and so forth.
(16) From establishing a meal: If he began, he need not interrupt.
(17) From the ninth hour onward: These are seasonal hours, namely the final quarter of the day. Nevertheless, during winter, when the days are very short, it appears to be a mitzvah to refrain from establishing even an ordinary meal before the ninth hour whenever he estimates that it will leave him without appetite at night.
