An engagement feast. The same applies to a wedding feast, since it could have been held on another day, as Bach writes. This requires study. In siman 444:7 the Shulchan Aruch rules that an engagement feast is a mitzvah feast even on Pesach eve, when such a feast is forbidden during Chol HaMoed. All the more so should it be permitted on Friday, which is more lenient, as stated in the Gemara and in an explicit Mishnah. Moreover, in siman 546 he rules that one may marry on the eve of a Festival, as stated explicitly in the first chapter of Moed Katan. Further, since the man became engaged on Friday, Friday is the proper time for the feast. One cannot object that he should not have become engaged that day, because there is concern that another may precede him. Rokeach, siman 30, cites this Jerusalem Talmud. It appears to me that its meaning is as follows: “It is forbidden to become engaged on Friday” refers to an engagement feast, meaning that if he became engaged previously, he may not hold the feast on Friday. “But becoming engaged is permitted” means that he may become engaged on Friday lest another precede him, and he may then also hold the feast. The Jerusalem Talmud in the first chapter of Taanis concludes with Shmuel’s statement that even on the Ninth of Av one may become engaged; see there. The ruling of Shiltei HaGibborim in chapter 22 of Shabbos, that it is forbidden to become engaged on Friday, therefore requires study, since all agree that it is permitted. The Jerusalem Talmud in the first chapter of Kesubos forbids marrying on Friday because of the honor of Shabbos, which implies that it disagrees with our Talmud. See Even HaEzer siman 63.
The honor of Shabbos. Chiddushei Hagahos cites Rabbeinu Chananel that, because of the preoccupation with the feast, they will not attend to Shabbos preparations.
Redemption of the firstborn. If the proper time for redeeming the firstborn has passed, it is forbidden to hold the feast on Friday. Circumcision, however, even when its time has passed, such as when the child was ill on the eighth day, is deemed to have a fixed time, for every moment is its proper time and it is forbidden to leave him uncircumcised. This appears from Tosafot at the end of Moed Katan 8. See what I wrote in siman 568:2 and siman 546, and see Yoreh Deah siman 266 in Shach and Taz, where some prohibit performing a delayed circumcision on Friday.
It is permitted. If both meals can be maintained, that is preferable; if not, Shabbos meal is deferred, according to Levush. Bach writes that it is a mitzvah to begin before the tenth hour. Shelah writes that his teacher, Maharam Shmuel of Lublin, was a sandak on Friday and declined to attend the feast because a quorum was present without him; Shelah responded to him. See there, and see siman 551:10. Olas Shabbos writes that the statement that rejoicing exists only with meat applies specifically when the Temple stood and the meat was from peace offerings. He overlooked Sanhedrin 70. Maharam Tiktin and Mordechai to Taanis write: if laundering was permitted for the honor of Shabbos, all the more so circumcision, which takes precedence over Shabbos. There the discussion concerns the parties to the circumcision, for whom it is a festival; see siman 559. Darkei Moshe cites Or Zarua: it is a mitzvah to eat Shabbos meal with appetite, and since bread is the principal meal, one may not eat pastries on Shabbos before the meal, lest he be unable to eat the principal Shabbos meal with appetite. Or Zarua follows his own view that eating after Minchah on Friday is forbidden. According to us, however, since we rule that eating after Minchah is permitted, it follows that one need not eat with appetite, and pastries may therefore be eaten while it is still day. Darkei Moshe further cites a responsum that, after Kiddush on Friday night, pastries were brought before Rabbeinu Kalonymus; he ate them, recited the blessing over grain foods and the after-blessing, in order to complete one hundred blessings, and then they ate bread for the meal. I do not understand Darkei Moshe, for Or Zarua forbade this only on Shabbos itself, excluding the incident involving Rabbeinu Kalonymus. It appears to me that Or Zarua is correct, for there is at least a mitzvah to eat with appetite. According to our practice of washing before Kiddush, it is plainly forbidden to eat before the blessing over bread. Furthermore, the blessing over bread precedes the blessing over grain foods, as stated in siman 211:5, and even on Shabbos one may not cause an unnecessary blessing, as stated at the end of siman 215.
