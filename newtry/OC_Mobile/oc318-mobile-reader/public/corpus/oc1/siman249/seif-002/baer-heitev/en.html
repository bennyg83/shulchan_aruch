Engagement. The same applies to a wedding feast, as stated by the Bach. This requires study, for in siman 444:7 he wrote that an engagement feast is a mitzvah feast even on Pesach eve, all the more so on Friday, which is more lenient. A further difficulty is that in siman 546 he wrote that it is permitted to marry on the eve of a Festival. A further difficulty is that, since he became engaged on Friday, Friday is the proper time for the feast. The Rokeach, siman 30, cites this Jerusalem Talmud, and it appears to me that its meaning is as follows: It is forbidden to become engaged on Friday. This statement refers to an engagement feast, meaning that if he became engaged previously, he may not hold the engagement feast on Friday. But becoming engaged itself is permitted, meaning that one may become engaged on Friday lest another precede him; in that case it is also permitted to hold the feast. See Magen Avraham there, and see siman 444:6. See also Responsa Chavos Yair, siman 70, for an extended discussion of what constitutes a mitzvah feast. Regarding one who buries his dead on Friday, whether he is obligated in the meal of consolation, see Yoreh Deah siman 371. See also Even HaOzer, who resolves the Magen Avraham’s objection to the ruling of the Shulchan Aruch. Eliyah Rabbah rules that if one became engaged on Friday and the feast can be postponed until the next day or another day, it is forbidden to hold it on Friday; the later ruling concerns a case in which postponement is impossible.
Redemption of the firstborn. If the proper time for redeeming the firstborn has passed, it is forbidden to hold the feast on Friday. Circumcision, however, even when its time has passed, such as where the child was ill on the eighth day, is considered to have a fixed time, for every moment is its proper time since it is forbidden to leave him uncircumcised. This appears from Tosafot to Moed Katan 8; see Magen Avraham. See also Yoreh Deah siman 266, in the Shach and Taz, where some prohibit performing a delayed circumcision on Friday.
