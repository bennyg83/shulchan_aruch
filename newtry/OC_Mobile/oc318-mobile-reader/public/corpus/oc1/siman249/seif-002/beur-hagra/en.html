Seif 2: To which he is unaccustomed. This is demonstrated by Pesachim 99b: “He may continue eating,” and so forth.
And even if it is. This is from the Jerusalem Talmud.
And throughout the day. For the statement there concerning Friday is unqualified.
And a meal whose time has arrived. Even an engagement feast is permitted if he has already become engaged, as stated in Pesachim 49a. The same applies even to a wedding feast, as stated in the first chapter of Moed Katan, and all of these are permitted on the eve of a Festival.
And to eat. This follows Pesachim 99b, in accordance with Rabbi Yose, for the Mishnah there follows his view. It must refer to eating without formally establishing a meal, as explained below; this also follows from the wording, “he may eat,” and so forth. It must therefore concern the sort of meal he ordinarily eats, as explained above.
But a mitzvah. There, on 100a, the law follows Rabbi Yose regarding interrupting a meal. Rabbi Yose says there, “One need not interrupt,” which implies that one may not begin. There, however, the discussion concerns formally establishing a meal. See Tosafot there in the name of the Baal Halachos Gedolos.
