(19) Must fast, etc. — and as if one accepted an unspecified fast on other weekdays, whose law is specifically until nightfall, and it does not help that he prayed Maariv beforehand, as below in siman 562 seif 1 and 2; the same applies here, and it is not considered a prohibition that he enters Shabbat afflicted, even though on Shabbat itself it is forbidden to fast:
(20) If he did not specify — then he is permitted even while the day is still great; and likewise if he conditioned to fast only until after minchah gedolah, his condition is effective:
(21) And there are those who say he does not complete, etc. — this view holds that both for an individual fast and for a communal fast such as the tenth of Tevet falling on erev Shabbat, he does not fast except until he leaves the beit knesset, for then they have already accepted Shabbat in prayer, which is prayed from plag haminchah onward [this is from 10:45 onward and the hours are seasonal — see below siman 261]; and by this is called completion of the fast what he fasts until after accepting Shabbat, and the same applies on yom tov until after accepting yom tov [and on a weekday too, this view does not call completion of the fast until nightfall]; and Rama rules that for an individual fast — meaning one who decreed a fast upon himself — one may rely on this view not to complete, nevertheless l'chatchila it is better to specify thus at the time of accepting the fast, and b'dieved this is not grounds for rejection; and for a communal fast it is stricter and one must complete according to law, and even a condition does not help, for the matter does not depend on him alone; but a community that decreed a fast on themselves anew for some matter is not so stringent and a condition helps:
(22) In an individual fast, etc. — an individual fast is called one who accepted upon himself to fast on this erev Shabbat, or whose custom is to fast every erev Shabbat, or erev Rosh Chodesh falling on erev Shabbat [meaning those people who do not observe Yom Kippur Katan on the Thursday before it, for those who observe it also fast on Thursday — Rama in siman 418]; or who fasts on the day his father and mother died, or the 20th of Sivan, or who fasts the fasts explained in siman 580. Magen Avraham wrote that if one fasts every erev Shabbat and completed the first time, he must conduct thus always, and the same applies to all the above, for presumably all this is without explicit condition that he does not intend always to complete — we say his intention is to conduct thus always. And l'chatchila it is better not to complete the first time when it falls on erev Shabbat, as Rama wrote, so he need not always complete. And if at first when he began to fast — a day his father and mother died, or erev Rosh Chodesh and the other fasts above falling on a weekday — and on weekdays they do not complete, therefore some poskim hold that even when it later occurs on erev Shabbat he must also complete until nightfall, for presumably his intention is always to conduct thus, unless he explicitly conditioned that he does not intend to conduct thus always; and some dispute this and hold that presumably he did not accept upon himself to complete even on erev Shabbat, and therefore one who suffers may rely on those who are lenient and eat immediately after leaving the beit knesset even though the day is still great:
(23) Until nightfall — and even the above view agrees to this, for behold even on Shabbat itself one can begin and fast a dream fast, as below in siman 288, and certainly he completes until night. And behold some poskim dispute and hold that even for a dream fast it suffices to wait until they leave the beit knesset for maariv, for this is called completion; but the later authorities agreed with Shulchan Aruch not to be lenient in this, for since it is because of danger — to nullify and regret a bad dream — perhaps it will not help to nullify it if he does not complete until night:
