A fast for a troubling dream. It appears that even the second opinion agrees that a fast for a troubling dream must be completed, since the later authorities permit such a fast even on Shabbos itself. See Knesses HaGedolah and Yad Aharon. It appears to me that one who fasts every Friday and completed the first such fast must continue doing so forever. The same applies to one who observes the fasts listed in siman 580, to one who fasts on the eve of Rosh Chodesh when it falls on Friday, and to the twentieth of Sivan when it falls on Friday, as stated by Magen Avraham. Regarding a yahrzeit that falls on Friday, the Bach wrote that if, when he first began fasting, the date occurred on Friday, he should take care to accept the fast upon himself on condition that he not complete it. Since a yahrzeit fast is like a vow, once he did not complete it initially, he need not complete it thereafter. But if the first occurrence was on a weekday and he completed the fast, it became like a vow, and he must complete it even when it later falls on Friday. We therefore learn that if the first yahrzeit fell on Friday and he did not complete the fast, he need never complete it when the yahrzeit falls on Friday. If he completed it then, or if the first yahrzeit fell during the week, when he was legally obligated to complete it, he must complete it whenever his yahrzeit falls on Friday. The Taz disagrees and writes that conduct is not deemed a vow unless he intended to observe it permanently. One who is lenient therefore loses nothing, especially if completion causes him distress. See there, and see Yoreh Deah siman 402, seif katan 12, and what is written there. Common practice follows the Bach. See Responsa Nachalas Shivah, siman 57 [58].
