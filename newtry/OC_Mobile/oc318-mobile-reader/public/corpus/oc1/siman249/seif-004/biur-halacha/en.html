It appears obvious that, according to the Mechaber, if he was already obligated to observe a fast and chose to discharge it on Friday, he does not fulfill his obligation if he fails to complete it properly and must fast another day. Even according to the Mechaber, who did not cite Maharam’s view in the main text even as an alternative and therefore evidently holds that accepting Shabbos in prayer is not considered completion, this applies only to one who accepted an ordinary fast, whose presumed duration is until nightfall and which he must therefore complete. Regarding a communal fast, however, such as the Tenth of Teves falling on Friday, the Beit Yosef appears inclined to rule like the authorities who explain that the Gemara’s statement “they fast and complete” means that one may complete it if he wishes. That passage also concerns communal fasts. Yet Rama’s unqualified ruling that a communal fast must be completed, without mentioning their view, indicates that he follows Rokeach and Raavyah, cited by the Beit Yosef, who require completion. Darkei Moshe states that Tolaas Yaakov agrees. I found no proof for this in the Ran cited there. He then writes that Maharil also requires completion of a communal fast. The reference given in Shulchan Aruch is Maharil, responsum 33, which states that after the evening prayer there is proper completion on Friday for an individual fast, but that when a public fast was proclaimed on Lag BaOmer because of a decree, he did not permit eating during the day, although it was Friday, except for those who had stipulated. On a weekday, completion is at full sunset, meaning nightfall. Nevertheless, Rama’s omission of the authorities cited by Beit Yosef, who make completion optional, is difficult. Maharil himself writes in responsum 157 that his custom for an individual Friday fast was to continue until after the evening prayer, following Maharam, but that for a communal fast he personally was stringent to complete it like several great German authorities, while telling others that either practice was acceptable when done for the sake of Heaven. What he wrote in responsum 33, that he did not permit eating, means only that he did not expressly permit it; he did not impose a prohibition either. Rama’s gloss does not sound this way. Perhaps he ruled stringently because the common practice followed Maharil’s personal stringency, and because Rokeach, Raavyah, and Tolaas Yaakov likewise require completion.
And some say that he should not complete it. See Mishnah Berurah. According to this opinion, when one returns from the synagogue hungry, it is improper to wait until nightfall solely because of the fast. The Raavad, cited by the Beit Yosef, implies that immediately after sunset, once he has accepted Shabbos, the fast is considered complete even if he has not yet prayed. It appears to me that this opinion and the Raavad’s reasoning apply only when one accepted the fast in ordinary language and not as a vow. If he prohibited food upon himself for an entire day through the language of a vow, all agree that he may not eat until dark, when the day ends. See Yoreh Deah siman 215 and siman 220:1.
