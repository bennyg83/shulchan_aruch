If he did not specify, etc. The law implies — specifically if he already specified unspecified; but l'chatchila he should not say unspecified so he completes — why should he accept upon himself at first to fast part of Shabbat:

And there are those who say he does not complete. This is the view of Maharam in Tur regarding other fasts; and regarding a dream fast he brought Beit Yosef in the name of Hagahot Orah Chayim in the name of Or Zarua — he wrote regarding a dream fast one need not wait except until leaving the beit knesset; and in responsum Maharil siman 33 in the name of Ravyah he wrote that even on a weekday one need not complete; and in what Rama wrote to distinguish between individual fast and communal — there is no ruling for a dream fast; but the custom is simple among us to complete for a dream fast even on erev Shabbat. And regarding yahrzeit — R' Shlomo Luria wrote in responsum siman 9 that they accustomed to complete on erev Shabbat, only it was accepted — if the first yahrzeit occurred on erev Shabbat, then the custom is not to complete; and he gave a reason per responsum of Mahari Weil — that the day one's father died is considered like a vow, and on Shabbat the vow does not take effect; but if it falls on a weekday, then the vow takes effect and he is liable to complete also on erev Shabbat; and he concluded — and proof, for even one who accepted upon himself an unspecified fast on erev Shabbat is obligated to complete — end quote. And in my humble opinion it is wondrous what he wrote — that hour when Shabbat enters from the day of Shabbat he is exempt from fasting — even though he already fasted on a weekday; and further — it is not called the way of a vow unless he intended to conduct thus always, as explained in Yoreh De'ah siman 214; and further — since he brought proof from one who accepts an unspecified fast who must complete, and we do not hold thus, as Rama wrote here — therefore I say one who is lenient not to complete on erev Shabbat did not lose, especially one who suffers in his fast should not suffer at the beginning of Shabbat; and further it appears what he wrote in the Gemara — those of the house of the Rav who sit fasting every erev Shabbat, and Tur brought it — this is specifically those who recognize in themselves that after the fast they will be hungry to eat; not so those who are weak after the fast and eating is not so comfortable for them — they should not fast; and proof from siman 695 regarding Purim falling on Sunday — see what he wrote there:
