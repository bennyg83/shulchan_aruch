If he found the congregation that already recited, etc. — the reason is that it is more preferable to pray with the congregation than to connect geulah to the Maariv prayer; and if he came to the synagogue after Barekhu and can pray Minchah and finish when the congregation begins Maariv — one must examine what he should do; for one cannot learn from this law that also in this he finishes Minchah and afterward prays again immediately with the congregation and afterward recites Shema — for here it is different, for he has only one prayer before him — regarding this we say it is better he pray it with the congregation and afterward Shema; not so here, for behold he has two prayers before him — and therefore he prays the first time individually; if so, let us say he should wait until the congregation reaches Maariv prayer and pray with them, and afterward recite Shema and pray Maariv — and he gains connecting geulah to prayer; and it appears to me that also in this we do not concern ourselves with connecting geulah to prayer, but he prays the two prayers one after the other — meaning the second with the congregation; for how can we say he pray his Minchah with the congregation? Behold he will not have partnership, for they take that time for night — for behold they pray Maariv and he still takes it for day since he prays Minchah; and even in what the congregation prays Maariv immediately after Minchah we say it is two voices that contradict one another — for now they establish day and immediately establish night; rather in prayer they were lenient, as the Rosh wrote beginning of Berachot — meaning not at one time; but here, at one time he wishes to participate with them, and truly they have no partnership — and it is as if we pray an entirely different prayer; and one should learn this distinction from what Terumat HaDeshen wrote siman 4: for one who delayed praying at Maariv time Minchah until the congregation answers Barekhu — he ruled there: if he already answered Barekhu with them, he has made it holy — he should not pray Minchah there and pray Maariv twice; but if he did not answer with them, he can pray Minchah — only that he loses [something] likewise. So too here: since he waits for Maariv prayer, his intent is to have partnership with them, and truly there is no partnership here; and an example for this we find in Tosafot chapter Arvei Pesachim daf 105 s.v. venim'a, etc. — but according to R' Yehuda he does not challenge, etc. — so too here there is no partnership, for the times are divided; and if your soul wishes to say that even though the prayers are not equal, nevertheless there is benefit since in any case the congregation also prays — we too say there is benefit that he say immediately the Minchah prayer while the congregation is engaged in the blessing of Shema — for then it is in any case a time of favor before Him, blessed be He, and the prayer of this individual is accepted more at that time; and so is in Hagahot Menahem Nissel, and these are his words: if he came to the synagogue after Barekhu and began Minchah and the congregation begins Maariv prayer — it is better he pray the 18 of Maariv with the congregation, etc. — end quote; and one should not be precise from the language "and he began Minchah" — that it deals specifically b'dieved — for if so he should have written l'chatchila he should not begin Minchah until the congregation prays Maariv; rather certainly he did not descend to this, but his intent is: if the matter occurred that at the time of Maariv prayer of the congregation he finished this one's Minchah prayer — he prays again the 18 of Maariv with them — in my humble opinion, clear.
