(Seif katan 4) "The King," etc. — "Who reigns in His honor." It is possible the reason is as Rashi wrote, and they are words of Midrash Rabbah in Megillat Esther on "Who reigns from India," etc. — that he reigned of himself and was not from royal seed and was not fit for kingship (Shulchan Aruch).

"That yimloch is language of request" — meaning that his intent in saying yimloch should not be language of prayer that he prays that He reign, etc. — meaning to bring the redemption near; rather his intent should be from language of praise that certainly so it will be when He redeems us, speedily in our days.

The word tamid below — I did not know its reason. And in Perishah it is brought in Sefer Eliyah Rabbah: on the contrary, that one should read it above.
