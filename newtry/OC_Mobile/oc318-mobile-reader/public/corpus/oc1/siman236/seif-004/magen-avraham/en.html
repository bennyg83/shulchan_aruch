"The King in His honor." In Sefer Amek HaMelekh he calls out against those who say "Who reigns in His honor," etc. — only "The King," etc.; however in the text of Rambam it is written "Who reigns," etc., and so in Tur; and Peri Megadim wrote that yimloch is language of request — for there is no prayer in the conclusion of a blessing — and one must explain it as praise that the Holy One, blessed be He, reign forever; and Be'er Heitev wrote that one should read the word tamid below — "May He always reign," etc.
