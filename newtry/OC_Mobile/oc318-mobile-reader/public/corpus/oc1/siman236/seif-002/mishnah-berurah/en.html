(4) Between the geulah of Maariv and prayer — for even at Maariv it is a mitzvah to connect geulah to prayer.

(5) And even those accustomed to say, etc. — and in Tur he concludes with this; and that which they are accustomed to interrupt with the verses Yir'u eineinu and Kaddish — because in early days their synagogues were in the fields and they were afraid to remain there until the time of Maariv prayer; and they enacted to say these verses that have in them 18 mentions corresponding to the 18 blessings in Maariv prayer, and they leave with Kaddish. And now that they returned to pray Maariv in synagogues, the first custom was not abolished; and nevertheless one should not interrupt with other matters; and there are among the great ones who were accustomed not to say them — end quote; and with this we understand the words of the Mechaber.

(6) One should not interrupt, etc. — for those verses are not called an interruption, since they were enacted to say them — behold they are like a long geulah (Tosafot).

(7) Need of prayer — and not specifically to read and announce that it is Rosh Chodesh, which is d'oraisa, or to announce dew and rain that if they err they must repeat; rather even to announce on miracles is fine; and even though if they err they also need not repeat — nevertheless it is a need of prayer. And specifically in this place one may interrupt; but when he stands in the blessing of Shema between the sections, it is forbidden for him to announce anything.

(8) To say Barekhu — for this too is a need of prayer.

(9) And see above siman 69 — meaning that stated in the Rama gloss it is explained that specifically at Maariv we are lenient for this, but not at Shacharit.
