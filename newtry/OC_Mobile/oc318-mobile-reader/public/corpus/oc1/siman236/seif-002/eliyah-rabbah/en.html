[Levush] As though he fulfilled, etc. Perishah says he derives this from the verse "seven times a day," implying as though he learned all day.
[Levush] And it is somewhat related to the conclusion, etc. I found that "Hashem Tzevaot is His Name" is also related to the conclusion, as the verse says, "Who makes darkness and dawn, Hashem Tzevaot is His Name." Emek Berachah writes that one should pause between "according to His will" and "Who creates day." Perishah writes that in "the living and enduring God, continually He shall reign," and similarly in Yiru Eineinu, "the King in His glory, continually He shall reign," the word "He shall reign" begins a new thought. Magen Avraham, citing Emek Berachah, says instead that the word "continually" should be read with what follows.
[Levush] In the past tense, etc. Above, at the end of siman 66, where he writes that Emet VeEmunah was enacted regarding the future redemption, one must say he refers there to the words Emet VeEmunah themselves, whereas here he discusses the principal theme of the blessing. Bach writes in siman 66 that our Yom Tov practice of concluding the Maariv piyyut with "King, Rock of Israel and its Redeemer" requires study, because this sounds like the present tense "Who redeems Israel." He suggests saying "and redeemed it" without a vav in a form that clearly refers to the past. In my humble opinion, the Yerushalmi cited by the Tur at the end of siman 66 implies that even the wording "its Redeemer" can refer to the past. See below what I wrote in the name of Taz. The Tur and Levush, however, do not seem to support this. Magen Avraham's answer that Rashi explains the redemption of Maariv as also referring to the future is imprecise, because the Tur himself writes that above. Rather, the explanation is as I have given, and so appears from Perishah.
