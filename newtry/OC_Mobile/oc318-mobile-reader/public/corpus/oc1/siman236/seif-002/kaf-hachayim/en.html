(2) There, in Ashkenaz one blesses two, etc. — the first: "Who by His word causes evening," etc., and its conclusion and "Who separates between day and night" — Blessed are You, Heaven, Who causes evening. And this is the Sephardic text. And the Ashkenazic text: "Who separates between day and night — Heaven of hosts is His name; living and enduring Heaven, may He always reign over us forever and ever; Blessed are You, Heaven, Who causes evening." And R' Yechiel wrote: and more correct is the Sephardic text, for this is not a kind of conclusion adjacent to the conclusion; and to settle the custom, the Rosh would say that we pray "Heaven will reign forever and ever" and the verse will be fulfilled "and it shall come to pass at evening time there shall be light" — and it is somewhat like a conclusion adjacent to the conclusion (Tur). And Taz brought seif katan 1 and Magen Avraham beginning of the siman, and so Bach wrote a reason to settle the custom — see there. "Heaven of hosts is His name" is also a kind of conclusion, as it is written (Amos 4:13) "He makes dawn and darkness, Heaven of hosts is His name" (Eliyah Rabbah, letter gimel). And now the people of Sepharad are accustomed to say after "Who separates between day and night — Heaven of hosts is His name," "Blessed are You, Heaven, Who causes evening" — because it is mentioned in Sha'ar HaKavanot daf 52 side b, and he gave a reason in sod — see there.
