Seif katan 2: They are accustomed, etc.; and Ma'amar Mordechai writes that one must sit, etc. Those who stand reason as follows. In earlier times, synagogues were in the fields, and people feared prolonging Maariv at night because the synagogue was far from inhabited areas. They therefore instituted, in place of the Amidah, the eighteen verses beginning Baruch Hashem Le'olam, which contain eighteen mentions of the Name Havayah corresponding to the eighteen blessings, and together with them they instituted the concluding blessing Yir'u Eineinu. Even now, when our synagogues are within the city and we recite the full Amidah, the original enactment to recite these eighteen verses in place of the eighteen blessings remains in force. This is the reason Rama writes that meticulous people stand, since these verses were originally instituted in place of the Amidah itself. Those who disagree argue that for this very reason one should sit. If one stands, it appears that he intends to fulfill the Amidah through these verses; when he subsequently recites the Amidah, it then appears as though he prays the Amidah twice. Nor can it be considered one extended prayer, because Kaddish intervenes between them. So writes Taz. Eliyah Rabbah writes that even those who stand should not stand facing the wall, for then it appears exactly as though they are praying the Amidah.
The correct version is “Save us, God of our salvation,” excluding our version, “Save us, Hashem our God, and gather us,” for that would create nineteen mentions of the Name Havayah. In my humble opinion, this argument is not sufficient, for one could say that one Name corresponds to Bircas HaMinim. Although the Tur and Rosh write that there are eighteen mentions corresponding to the eighteen blessings, they may not have been exact in their wording, since the Amidah is called “eighteen blessings” because that was the original enactment of the Men of the Great Assembly, whereas Bircas HaMinim was added later in Yavneh. They therefore also referred generally to eighteen mentions corresponding to them. One may further say that the twofold phrase “Hashem, He is God” is counted as one. Eliyah Rabbah likewise writes that it is counted as one, according to his approach. Nevertheless, one may still follow Magen Avraham and say, “Save us, God of our salvation.”
For this is the wording of the verse in I Chronicles 16, where the Name Havayah is not mentioned, and so writes Eliyah Rabbah. In Tehillim 106, however, it says, “Save us, Hashem our God, and gather us,” and so forth.
Then he said, etc. He said it standing. Since he had not adjoined redemption to prayer in order to pray with the congregation, when he later recited Krias Shema with its blessings he said these verses standing, so that this should somewhat resemble adjoining redemption to prayer. The verses would thus resemble the Amidah to some extent. Although he had already prayed with the congregation, the verses could now be regarded as standing in place of the Amidah and as a voluntary prayer. Although a voluntary prayer requires a novel element, as explained above in siman 107, adjoining redemption to prayer is itself considered a novel element.
