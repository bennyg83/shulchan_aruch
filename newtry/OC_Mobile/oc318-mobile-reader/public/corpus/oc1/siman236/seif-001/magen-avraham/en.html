It is written in Amek Berachah that one must pause between "...in the expanse as He desired" and "Who creates day and night" (Shulchan Aruch). That which we say "May He always reign over us forever and ever" — even though it requires a kind of conclusion adjacent to the conclusion — one can say we pray that Heaven reign and the verse be fulfilled "and it shall come to pass at evening time there shall be light," etc. (Tur). Bach wrote: that which we conclude "Blessed are You, King, Rock of Israel and its Redeemer" when saying Maariv is not correct, for we say in chapter Arvei Pesachim regarding Shema "Redeemer of Israel" — on redemption that passed — end quote; and however, in the first chapter of Berachot Rashi explained that specifically Shacharit refers to redemption that passed, but evening geulah refers also to the future, as it is written "to declare in the morning Your kindness and Your faithfulness in the nights" (Shulchan Aruch) — if so, one should not deviate from the custom. Need of prayer — and furthermore Maariv prayer is optional (Rashba), and siman 167; and so is the practice, not like Maharshal (Bach); and nevertheless it is possible specifically on Rosh Chodesh, which is d'oraisa, or "grant dew and rain" — for they return him if he did not say it; not so Chanukah and Purim, where they do not return him if he did not say it — it is possible they do not announce; and nevertheless the custom is to announce, and so Radbaz; and it appears to me that between the sections we rule as at Shacharit — specifically between geulah and prayer some are lenient because Maariv prayer is optional; but between Shema and its blessings it is obligatory for everyone.
