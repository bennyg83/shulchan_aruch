He wrote (Magen Avraham): he wrote (Be'er Heitev) that one must pause between "...in the expanse," etc. — meaning that one must continue the word "as He desired" to what is above it: "and arranges the stars in their watches in the expanse as He desired." As we say at the end of Shabbat: "There is no mazel for Israel" — and Tosafot wrote that sometimes through great merit Heaven changes the mazel; and so we find that the Holy One, blessed be He, said to Abraham: "What is your opinion that righteousness stands in the west? I will establish it in the east" — behold that sometimes it is His will, blessed be He, to confound the constellations and stars, and it is fitting that as He desires, blessed be He, He arranges them in the expanse. But if one continues the word "as He desired" below — "as He desired, Creator of day and night" — it cannot be, for certainly He has the ability to change day to night or vice versa, to darken the sun and it will not shine; but He already promised, blessed be He, "day and night shall not cease" — only by way of happenstance, as in the darkness of Egypt, when there was darkness in the whole world only in Egypt; and likewise the stopping of the sun for Moses and Joshua and Hezekiah was only by way of happenstance. Not so confounding the arrangement of the stars — that is certainly done in every generation for the righteous of the generations, and they are among the hidden miracles.

It appears that we pray, etc., "and it shall be at evening time there shall be light" — and meaning: "and for the nations He covers with darkness" — and it is properly a kind of conclusion "Who causes evening" — meaning that evening shall be for the nations; it is comparable to "Who causes evening" every day.

He wrote (Bach) in siman 66 and see there (Taz).

As we say in chapter Arvei Pesachim daf 117 side b: R' Yehuda — Shema and Hallel [conclude with] "Redeemer of Israel" — the prayer "Redeemer of Israel" is mercies — Rashi explained: they are mercies, for we pray for the future — until here his language. But Shema and likewise Hallel of Passover night is praise for the redemption of Egypt that passed; and Bach concluded there, and these are his words: rather one must say ve'go'alo without a vav (between gimel and alef), for it sounds well regarding redemption that passed — end quote.

However, in the first chapter of Berachot daf 12 side a, Rava said: Rav Chisda said in name of Rav: whoever did not say emet veyatziv in the morning and emet ve'emunah in the evening did not fulfill [his obligation of] the 18 [blessings], as it is written "to declare in the morning Your kindness," etc.; and Rashi wrote, and these are his words: as it is written "to declare in the morning Your kindness," and the blessing emet veyatziv concludes regarding the kindness He did for our forefathers when He took them out of Egypt, etc.; and the blessing emet ve'emunah speaks also of the futures that we hope He will fulfill for us His faith and His promise to redeem us immediately from kings, etc. — end quote. However, Tosafot there wrote, and these are his words: after they cited Rashi's language they wrote the above, and these are his words: and at the end of the blessing it returns to the redemption of Pharaoh in order to connect to "Redeemer of Israel" — end quote; behold the conclusion in Maariv is also for the future; and so too Perishah, who challenged the Tur above siman 66 — he cited the above language of Rashi, and in this siman wrote that one must say "Redeemer of Israel" and not "and its Redeemer," because it refers to redemption that passed; and he answered from his own reasoning, for he holds that even though the blessing speaks of future redemption, nevertheless the conclusion must be on redemption that passed — brought in Sefer Eliyah Rabbah (Shulchan Aruch).

(Seif katan 1) Need, etc. — and in siman 167 seif 6: that which is a need for a meal is not an interruption between netilat yadayim and HaMotzi; the same law applies to that which is a need for prayer.

Not like Maharshal, who wrote: since nowadays we accepted Maariv prayer as obligatory, the reason of Rashba has departed — therefore one may not interrupt with an announcement. And Bach disagrees: the main reason of Rashba is because it is a need of prayer, and this applies also for us; and what Rashba wrote that Maariv prayer is optional is only a branch and not the main reason.

Specifically on Rosh Chodesh, which is d'oraisa — meaning on Rosh Chodesh: even though we hold that at Maariv if one forgot Yaaleh VeYavo they do not return him, nevertheless Rosh Chodesh itself is d'oraisa. And likewise "grant dew and rain" — even though it is not d'oraisa, nevertheless it is a matter of superiority that if they did not say it they return him; not so Chanukah and Purim, which are d'rabbanan — and also if one did not say Al HaNissim they do not return him.

And it appears to me that between the sections, etc. — but between Shema and its blessings it is obligatory for everyone, even though he already agreed above that the reason of Maariv being optional is only a branch and the main reason is because of the need of prayer; and this applies also between the sections. Nevertheless, his intent is that it is better for him to interrupt with an announcement after Yir'u eineinu than to interrupt between the sections — for after Yir'u eineinu there are poskim who permit interrupting for all matters, for in any case they interrupt between geulah and prayer by saying the verses Yir'u eineinu, as the Tur wrote in their name; not so between the sections — even for Maariv, everyone agrees one may not interrupt; if so, it is preferable to interrupt between geulah and Maariv prayer than to interrupt between the sections.
