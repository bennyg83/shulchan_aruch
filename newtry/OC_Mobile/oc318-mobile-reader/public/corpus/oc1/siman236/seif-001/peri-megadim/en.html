Two blessings before it. See Taz and Tur. See Tehillim 119. Bach concludes that the phrase “for Your righteous ordinances” alludes to seven righteous ordinances contained in the three paragraphs: the tefillin of the hand, the tefillin of the head, mezuzah, and the four tzitzis. See there. This is difficult, because that count follows Rabbi Yishmael, whereas according to the Sages tzitzis is a single mitzvah, and the halachah follows them. See the chapter HaTecheles in Menachos.
One may instead say that tzitzis is one mitzvah, bringing the count to four; “you shall love Hashem” is one; “you shall speak of them,” namely Krias Shema twice, once by day and once by night, constitutes two mitzvos; and Torah study is a separate mitzvah. Alternatively, “I will provide grass in your field” is itself words of Torah. There is no need to elaborate.
Regarding Tur’s statement, in Sefarad they say, “Who distinguishes between day and night, blessed are You, Hashem,” whereas in Ashkenaz they add, “Hashem of Hosts is His name, the living and enduring God, may He reign over us forever and ever.” The phrase “Hashem of Hosts is His name” resembles the conclusion, as Eliyah Rabbah writes from the verse, “Who makes dawn into darkness, Hashem of Hosts is His name,” Amos 5:8. “The living and enduring God” likewise resembles the conclusion, “He will reign,” referring to the final redemption, “and He will illuminate for us.” This is the meaning of “Who distinguishes between day and night”: darkness for the wicked and light for Israel.
