(1) In the evening, etc. — and the custom is to say "and He is compassionate" before Maariv prayer (Tur siman 237 — see there the reason). And know that what is explained above in siman 54 in Mishna Berurah seif katan 14 in name of the Acharonim — that after Barekhu its law is like the middle of a section [for the main Barekhu that the shaliach tzibbur says — the intent is the blessings that bless the world afterward, and since the world answered Barekhu it is as if they began the blessing, as Eliyah Rabbah and Peri Megadim wrote there — see there] — it is obvious one should not distinguish between Shacharit and Maariv; and according to this one must be very careful not to talk after Barekhu of Maariv, even before he began to bless the first blessing if his intent is to pray Maariv then with the congregation — and alas, many people fail in this.

(2) Two, etc. — "Who by His word," etc., and "With abounding love" — and it does not open with "Blessed," for it is adjacent to the first. And one must pause between "...in the expanse as He desired" and "Who creates day and night" — for "as He desired" refers to what is above. The custom of Sepharad is not to say "Heaven of hosts is His name, living and enduring Heaven," etc., but they conclude immediately after "Who separates between day and night" — "Blessed are You," etc. — so that it be a kind of conclusion adjacent to the conclusion; and so is correct. And see in Tur that our custom is also a kind of conclusion — that we pray that Heaven reign and it be fulfilled "and it shall come to pass at evening time there shall be light for us and darkness for the enemies of Heaven"; and that which we say "Heaven of hosts is His name" — the Acharonim also wrote because it is written "He makes darkness and dawn, Heaven of hosts is His name."

(3) And two after it — the first: emet ve'emunah — and it does not open with "Blessed," for it is adjacent to its companion; and Shema is not an interruption — and it concludes "Blessed are You, Heaven, Redeemer of Israel" and not "and its Redeemer" as in prayer, because it was enacted on redemption that passed (Tur). And on Maariv of Yom Tov they are accustomed to conclude "King, Rock of Israel and its Redeemer" — and many challenge this; and many Acharonim settle the custom; and nevertheless it is better to conclude "Redeemer of Israel" as on the rest of the days of the year, as we wrote above in siman 66 in Mishna Berurah seif katan 33. And the second blessing: Hashkivenu — and it concludes "Who guards His people Israel forever" — because at night they need guarding from harmful forces; and even though we hold one must connect geulah to prayer even at Maariv, as below in seif 2 — nevertheless Hashkivenu is not an interruption, for it is like a long geulah (Gemara). And Tur Yeshanim wrote the reason: when Heaven passed to smite Egypt, Israel were afraid and prayed to Heaven that He fulfill His word that He not give the destroyer permission to come to their houses to smite; and corresponding to that prayer they enacted to say Hashkivenu — therefore it is like a kind of geulah.
