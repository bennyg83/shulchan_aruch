Seif katan 3: He should pray with them even though they pray before nightfall. Meaning, not only will he fail to adjoin redemption to prayer if he prays with the congregation, but they are also praying before nightfall, whereas if he prays alone he can pray at night. Although the beginning of siman 235 explains that praying with the congregation is preferable even when they pray before nightfall, the novelty here is that even though both disadvantages are present, namely that they pray before nightfall and that he will not adjoin redemption to prayer, prayer with the congregation is nevertheless preferable.
But he should not wait, etc. One might have thought that since in any event one of the two prayers, Minchah or Maariv, must be prayed individually, why should he pray Minchah alone and Maariv with the congregation, thereby failing to adjoin redemption to prayer and also praying Maariv before nightfall? Let him do the reverse: wait until the congregation begins the Maariv Amidah and then pray Minchah. Although his prayer would not be together with theirs, since they are praying Maariv while he prays Minchah, it would at least be at a time when the congregation is praying. He could then pray Maariv at night and adjoin redemption to prayer.
Regarding Shacharis and Mussaf. Meaning, if he prays Mussaf while the congregation is praying Shacharis, this is not considered praying at a time when the congregation is praying.
Because they contradict one another. The congregation praying Maariv treats the time as night, whereas he, by praying Minchah, necessarily treats it as still daytime.
See the end of siman 263, that one may not pray Minchah in a synagogue where the congregation has already accepted Shabbos, because the two practices contradict one another. See there.
And furthermore, prayer with the congregation, etc. Meaning, even if one were to say that such a case is considered praying at a time when the congregation is praying, it is nevertheless preferable for him to pray Minchah alone and Maariv with the congregation. The latter is actual prayer with the congregation, which is superior to praying Minchah while they pray Maariv, which would only be considered prayer at the time the congregation is praying, as explained above.
See the end of siman 109 regarding an analogous case involving Kedushah: “An individual standing in the Amidah who reaches the place of Kedushah while the congregation is reciting Kedushah DeSidra does not recite Kedushah with them, because the two Kedushos are not equivalent,” and so forth.
