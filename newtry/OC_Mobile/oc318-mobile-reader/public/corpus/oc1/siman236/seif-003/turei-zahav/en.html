Because they were enacted in place of prayer. In my humble opinion it appears that one who says them seated is fine; for if one says them standing, he intends that this be in place of the 18 [blessings] — if so, he has fulfilled Maariv prayer — and how will he pray Maariv afterward a second time? It is like one who prays and returns and prays — for Tur wrote siman 107 that it is like offering two tamid offerings; and one can say it is considered a long Maariv prayer; and as we find regarding Hashkivenu that it is a long geulah, as is found in the Gemara first chapter of Berachot — this is not so, for they say Kaddish before Maariv prayer, and this indicates interruption; what was said before this, and proof from what the Tur wrote this siman, and these are his words: Rav Netronai wrote from words of the Sages: one may only [pray Maariv] in the evening with two before and two after; and since the halachah is like Rav who said Maariv prayer is optional, the later authorities enacted that after "Who guards His people Israel forever" one say verses that have songs and praise, and say a blessing after them, and interrupt with Kiddush — meaning prayer is finished — end quote; behold before us that Kaddish indicates interruption; what was said at first — and how was it considered Maariv prayer? After Kaddish it is fine if one says seated — it indicates a long geulah like Hashkivenu, for behold there is no interruption between them, and it is literally like Hashkivenu; not so if one wishes to consider it in place of the 18[-blessing prayer] — in my humble opinion; afterward I found so in Mateh Moshe in name of Maharil and Maharshal.
