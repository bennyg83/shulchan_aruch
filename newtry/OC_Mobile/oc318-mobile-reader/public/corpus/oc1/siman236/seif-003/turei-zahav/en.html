For they were instituted in place of prayer. In my humble opinion, one who recites them while seated acts properly. If one who stands intends these verses to serve in place of the Amidah, he has thereby fulfilled Maariv; how then may he subsequently pray Maariv again? He would resemble one who prays and then repeats the prayer, which the Tur in siman 106 compares to offering two daily offerings. Nor can it be said that both together constitute one extended Maariv, analogous to Hashkiveinu being considered an extension of redemption, as stated in the first chapter of Berachos. This is untenable because Kaddish is recited before the Maariv Amidah, and that demonstrates that what preceded it has ended. A proof appears from the Tur in this siman, who writes in the name of Rav Natronai: “According to the Sages, one may recite at night only two blessings before Krias Shema and two after it. Since the halachah follows Rav, who holds that Maariv is optional, the later authorities instituted that after Shomer Amo Yisrael La'ad one should recite verses containing songs and praises, followed by a blessing, and then interrupt with Kaddish, meaning that the prayer has concluded.” Thus, Kaddish clearly marks the conclusion of what came before. How, then, could those verses be considered part of the Maariv recited after Kaddish? If they are recited while seated, however, they may be viewed as an extension of redemption, like Hashkiveinu, since there is no interruption between them. They are then exactly analogous to Hashkiveinu. But if one wishes to regard them as standing in place of the Amidah, the difficulty remains. This appears correct to me. I later found this position in Mateh Moshe in the name of Maharil and Maharshal.
