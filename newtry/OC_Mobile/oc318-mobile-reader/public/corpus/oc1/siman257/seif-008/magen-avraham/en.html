They touch the coals. According to the Rama in siman 253, seif 1, it is permitted even when they touch the coals, since the pots are not insulated in garments. In some places, after lighting the fire and cooking the pots, they rake away the hot ashes, place the pots there for the following day, and cover them with cold ash. Although the stove adds heat, it is nevertheless permitted, because it is forbidden only when the pot is placed there for insulation, and the ground is not suitable for insulation, so there is no concern, as stated by the Rosh. Tosafos give this latter reason alone, that the ground is not suitable for insulation. Maharshal writes in a responsum that hot ash does not add heat unless coals are mixed into it. Sefer HaTerumah writes that even if one finds a few coals in it the following day, it is permitted b'dieved. See seif 1. Tosafos on folio 48 write: ‘Our stove has heat only because of the fire, and it continuously cools, whereas olive refuse adds heat on its own.’
It is permitted according to all opinions. This applies specifically to a pastry or types of legumes that cook quickly, as written in siman 254. Raw meat, however, does not require sealing with clay. According to the Beit Yosef, sealing with clay is ineffective, as written there. In places where the custom is not to seal with clay, it is forbidden to place legumes or dough products in the oven unless they will reach ma'achal ben Derusai before nightfall, unless the coals were covered with ash, as written in siman 253. Maharshal was very stringent in his responsum; see there. Go out and observe how the people conduct themselves. See what I wrote at the end of siman 259. Shiltei Giborim on the Mordechai writes in the name of Tosafos: ‘Why may one not insulate in material that adds heat? It is a decree lest one insulate in hot ashes. Here, however, where the coals are visible, there is no concern that one will insulate in them.’ End quote. Nevertheless, it is no more lenient than leaving food on the fire.
And it is a mitzvah to insulate. One for whom hot food is harmful may eat cold food, as stated by Mateh Moshe.
