There are those who say that if he forgot and insulated, etc. See Magen Avraham, who writes that this applies specifically when the food was fully cooked, but if its cooking was completed on Shabbos it is forbidden, for it is certainly no more lenient than leaving food on the fire. I did not copy this ruling because, according to our ruling above like the opinion that leaving food at the stage of ma'achal ben Derusai is permitted, the same should seemingly apply here. The Tosefes Shabbos already raised this difficulty, while the Peri Megadim labored greatly to resolve it, but his resolution is not convincing. Because of the seriousness of the matter, it appears to me that Magen Avraham did not wish to adopt two leniencies together, namely, to rule in our case like the opinion equating insulation with leaving food on the fire, and also to rule regarding leaving food on the fire like the lenient opinion there. It is sufficient to be lenient here only when it was fully cooked. See Chayei Adam, who likewise records this opinion as practical halacha, specifically when it was fully cooked, perhaps for the reason we have stated. Nevertheless, where necessary, one may possibly rely b'dieved, when it was done unintentionally, even if it had been cooked only to the stage of ma'achal ben Derusai, in accordance with Tosefes Shabbos. This is also implied by Shulchan Atzei Shittim. I saw that Biur HaGra writes that this opinion is rejected, see there. That is with respect to the strict letter of the law. Nevertheless, it is very difficult to rule stringently b'dieved, since the Rama himself relies upon the lenient authorities b'dieved when one insulated for the following day. The same should apply where he forgot, for the rationale of both cases is one and the same, as the Gra explains.
