One may not insulate. After the dish has been cooked, he wishes to insulate it in something that will preserve its heat, as stated in siman 253, seif 2, in the Rama's gloss. The reason is that perhaps he will find his pot cold and bring it to a boil, thereby becoming liable for cooking. Even according to the opinion that he is not liable for cooking because it was already cooked once, there is nevertheless reason to decree lest he heat it on the fire and stoke the coals, as stated by the Rosh. See siman 318, seif 15.
One may insulate in it. Ordinarily, pots are boiling at bein hashmashos, so the foregoing concern does not apply. See siman 609, where according to the Rama it is forbidden to insulate for Motzei Shabbos. This requires study, for in siman 254, at the end of seif 5, he wrote that it is permitted to bake bread for Motzei Shabbos. Perhaps the Rama holds that even the prohibition of insulation itself is a novel stringency, since it is permitted to place incense and sulfur before Shabbos, as stated at the beginning of siman 252, even though this is for weekday use. Therefore, we should accept the stringency as stated but not extend it further.
That adds heat. This is a decree lest one insulate in ashes containing coals and stoke them on Shabbos.
The dish is forbidden. Since the ruling is stated without qualification, it appears that he holds it is forbidden even when done unintentionally. This is also implied by the Rama's gloss. Although in siman 253 he permits a case where one forgot and left food on the fire, insulation is treated more stringently.
Or it became reduced. This refers to the law after the fact. L'chatchila, it is forbidden even when continued cooking harms the food, as stated in seif 7, unlike the Olas Shabbos.
When it merely retains its warmth. The same applies to one who insulated on Shabbos in something that does not add heat; after the fact it is permitted, since it merely retains its existing warmth.
It is permitted to eat. This applies specifically to a dish that was fully cooked. If its cooking was completed on Shabbos, it is forbidden, as is implied by the Hagahos Mordechai, for it is certainly no better than food left on the fire.
For the night. This opinion holds that once food has reached ma'achal ben Derusai it is permitted, as stated in seif 7. When one insulates for the following day, it is permitted even if the food has not reached ma'achal ben Derusai, because he will not stoke the coals, since in any event it will cook by the next day.
