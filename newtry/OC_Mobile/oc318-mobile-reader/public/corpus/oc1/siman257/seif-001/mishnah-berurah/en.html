One may not insulate, etc. After the dish has been cooked, he wishes to insulate it in something that will preserve its heat, such as pillows, cushions, or the like. The Sages forbade doing so on Shabbos, lest he find his pot cold and bring it to a boil, thereby becoming liable for cooking. Even according to the opinion that he is not liable for cooking in this case, since it was already cooked once, there is nevertheless reason to decree lest he heat it on the fire and stoke the coals, thereby becoming liable for kindling.
But during doubtful nightfall. See below, siman 261, seif 1, in the Rama's gloss, and what is written there.
One may insulate in it. Ordinarily, pots are boiling at bein hashmashos, so the foregoing decree does not apply.
In something, etc. This refers to insulation for Shabbos. Regarding insulation for Motzei Shabbos, it is evident from the Rama's gloss in siman 609 that one may not insulate in any manner. The Magen Avraham writes this, but questions it and concludes that it is only a stringency that people adopted, not the strict law. See the Levush and Eliyah Rabbah there regarding what they wrote on this matter. According to them, it is certainly permitted to insulate for Motzei Shabbos, and there is not even a custom there to be stringent.
That adds heat. This is a decree lest one insulate in ashes containing coals and stoke them on Shabbos.
Even while it is still day. The Peri Megadim writes that even if he insulated it before midday, he nevertheless commits a prohibition by leaving it insulated for Shabbos and not removing the insulation before nightfall.
Or it became reduced. This refers to the law after the fact. L'chatchila, it is forbidden even when continued cooking harms the food, as stated in seif 7 by the later authorities.
When it merely retains its warmth, etc. The reason is that his action did not benefit the food at all. The Magen Avraham writes that the same applies to one who insulated on Shabbos in something that does not add heat. After the fact it is permitted, even though he committed a prohibition, because it merely retains its existing warmth.
Some say that if he forgot, etc. The same applies if he erred regarding the law and thought it was permitted; this too is considered unintentional.
It is permitted to eat. The reason for this opinion is that it equates insulation with leaving food on the fire. The Mechaber ruled above, in siman 253, seif 1, that if one forgot and left food on the fire, even a dish that is mitzamek v'yafeh lo is permitted to eat. The Magen Avraham, Taz, and Gra hold that according to the Mechaber it is forbidden to eat even when the act was unintentional until Motzei Shabbos, and this is why he stated the law without qualification. Nevertheless, in a case of need one may rely on this opinion, which is supported by many later authorities. This is especially so if the food was fully cooked, since in any event some are lenient below in seif 7 in the Rama's gloss. Therefore, after the fact, when the act was unintentional, one should not be stringent.
But when he insulates, etc. The primary reason the Sages forbade insulation in something that adds heat is lest one insulate in glowing ashes and stoke the coals. Therefore, when he insulates for the next day, this opinion holds that the decree does not apply. Even if the dish has not reached ma'achal ben Derusai, it will cook throughout the night on its own, so he will not stoke the coals. This opinion likewise holds that if the food is fully cooked, it is permitted to insulate it, because he will not stoke the coals. Therefore, it is permitted even when insulating for the night, in accordance with the lenient opinion below in seif 7 in the Rama's gloss.
That he not do so regularly. This implies that if he does so regularly, it is forbidden even after the fact. The reason is that strictly speaking this opinion has no basis, as the Gra and other poskim write. They likewise write that the lenient opinion below has no basis in law. Therefore, the Rama wrote there that one should not follow this practice in other places.
