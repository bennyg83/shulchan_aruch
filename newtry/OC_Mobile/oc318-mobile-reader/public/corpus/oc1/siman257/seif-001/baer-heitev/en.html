On Shabbat. Meaning, after the food was cooked, he wants to insulate it in something that will preserve its heat, as written in siman 253 seif 2 in the gloss. The reason is lest he find his pot cold and reheat it. But during twilight, ordinary pots are hot. With something that adds heat, even on Friday it is forbidden, lest he insulate in remetz, meaning ash with coals; this is forbidden lest he stoke the coals after dark.
Forbidden. Meaning even inadvertently. Although in siman 253, where one forgot and left it, it is permitted, nevertheless regarding insulation they are more stringent. Magen Avraham.
In its heat. The same applies to one who insulates on Shabbat in something that does not add heat, which is permitted, for it merely remains in its heat. Magen Avraham.
To eat. This is specifically food that was fully cooked; but if its cooking was completed on Shabbat, it is forbidden, for it is no better than shehiyah. Magen Avraham; see Taz.
Permitted. For he will not stoke, since in any case it will be cooked by tomorrow.
