The cooked dish is forbidden, etc. This appears to mean even when it was already fully cooked, and even when he insulated it unintentionally. This is implied by the fact that the Mechaber follows it with, “There are those who say,” which discusses a case where it was done unintentionally and the food was fully cooked. Evidently, the first opinion forbids even in that case. In truth, however, the source of this law in the Ran, chapter Kirah, does not imply anything conclusive. It is possible that the intent is specifically where it was done intentionally, similar to the incident of the people of Tiberias and to the law above in siman 253 concerning one who forgot and left food on the fire. Although one can distinguish insulation as more stringent, what source do we have to add this stringency, particularly when it was done unintentionally? Moreover, according to that reading there would be no dispute between the “there are those who say” and the first opinion, and it is preferable to minimize disputes. It therefore appears to me that one may be lenient.
And it improves it, etc. It appears to me that here, b'dieved, they permit the food when it is mitzamek v'ra lo, because his action did not benefit it. In truth, however, it is forbidden l'chatchila to insulate it, and seif 7 discusses that case. Olas Tamid raised a contradiction between the rulings of the Shulchan Aruch and was forced to explain that “improves it” was not stated precisely. There is no difficulty at all, and this is also implied by Magen Avraham.
It remains at its warmth, etc. The same applies to one who insulates on Shabbos with a material that does not add heat: it is permitted, since it merely remains at its warmth.
There are those who say, etc. Taz writes that there is no dispute here with the first opinion, because the source in Hagahos Mordechai explains that the case is specifically where the primary insulation consisted of permitted materials, with only a few burning coals, etc. If, however, the primary insulation consisted of material that adds heat, it is forbidden, as in the incident of the people of Tiberias. End quote. He was not precise, for Hagahos Mordechai, chapter Kirah, concludes: “Alternatively, there it was done intentionally.” End quote. This makes clear that there is no distinction. Even when the primary insulation adds heat it is permitted if done unintentionally, while the incident of the people of Tiberias, where it was forbidden, concerned an intentional act. This is clear.
And b'dieved, etc. I am astonished how the expression b'dieved can apply together with “provided he is not accustomed to doing so” in a case of unintentional insulation. The Rama, who wrote this, had first written: “There are those who say that all this is forbidden only when he does it for the nighttime meal, but when he insulates for the following day, it is permitted to insulate before Shabbos with material that adds heat.” It is concerning that case that he wrote, “And b'dieved, etc.” I subsequently saw that Malbushei Yom Tov writes that words are missing here, etc.
