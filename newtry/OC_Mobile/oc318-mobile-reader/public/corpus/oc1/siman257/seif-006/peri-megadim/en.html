And insulation. See Taz. The oven mouth is sealed with clay. According to the Rama in siman 254, seif 1, in the gloss, because the seal serves as a reminder, actual insulation with raw food is permitted. One might argue that insulation is more stringent, since even what is permitted regarding leaving food on the fire and returning it is forbidden regarding insulation, for the Sages decreed more strongly lest one stoke the coals. If so, sealing should likewise be ineffective. This is not correct, for Taz does not appear to mean that. Rather, he refers to a case where the oven is not sealed with clay. Regarding leaving food on the fire, according to us, once it has reached ma'achal ben Derusai, and when it is mitzamek v'yafeh lo, it is permitted according to the second opinion in siman 253, seif 1. One must take care that the pot not touch the oven, for whenever no airspace intervenes it constitutes insulation. Nevertheless, I have not merited to understand this. What difference does it make that one side adheres to the oven when all the other sides are uncovered and separated by air? Once part of it is uncovered, it should no longer constitute insulation. According to our practice in siman 253, seif 1, it is permitted even when it touches the coals. See Eliyah Rabbah, letter 18. With God's help, this will be explained further in siman 259, in Magen Avraham, in the laws of leaving food on the fire and insulation. I subsequently saw that the words of Taz can be explained according to Maharshal's responsum, siman 60, which I cited in Magen Avraham, letter 9. In a sealed oven, insulation is forbidden even though there is no concern that one will stoke the coals, because the seal serves as a reminder. Insulation is nevertheless more stringent: the Sages forbade olive refuse and similar materials, where the concern of stoking does not apply, as a decree because of hot ashes. All the more so may they have forbidden a sealed oven because of hot ashes. The Rama, however, holds that because the oven does not touch the walls of the pot it is permitted. It follows that when it does touch, it is forbidden. This still requires further study. See what I wrote in Magen Avraham, letter 19. With God's help, this will be explained further in siman 259 in the laws of leaving food on the fire and insulation. This is not the place to elaborate.
