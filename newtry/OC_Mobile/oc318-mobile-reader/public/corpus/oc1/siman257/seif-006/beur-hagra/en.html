Se'if 6: With something that does not. As stated in the first clause there: 'Even though they said,' etc. The Gemara's statement, 'Now,' etc., 'to bring to a boil,' etc., concerns something that does not add heat, for the decree is lest he bring it to a boil, as stated there on 34a according to Rashi's reading.
So that it should not. The Rambam explains it this way, like every case of insulation in the Mishnah and Baraisa there, for the entire chapter discusses insulation for the purpose of warming. Furthermore, this follows from the Gemara's statement there: 'Had we learned only from the Mishnah, I would have said that this applies only to something,' etc. According to Rashi's explanation, however, there is nothing whose normal manner of insulation would fail to warm more than water does in the summer. Rather, the explanation is as stated above and as the Gemara says there: 'Just as one may not,' etc., 'so too,' etc., 'Rabbi,' etc.
But with something that adds heat, etc. This follows from the beginning of chapter 4, where he saw that he had placed a jug, etc., and moved it, etc. He said to him: 'What is different,' etc. See Tosafos there, 48a, s.v. 'What is different': 'This is difficult,' etc. Rabbeinu Yitzchak, however, wrote that the incident there concerned insulation. The proof is that the Gemara brings the case of one hot-water urn atop another, which concerns insulation, as is evident from its inclusion among the laws of insulation. The Tosefta there explicitly teaches: 'One may insulate a hot-water urn atop another hot-water urn, and a pot atop another pot.' If the incident there concerned mere placement, the Gemara should have cited the Baraisa: 'A person may bring a jug of water and place it opposite the fire,' etc. Rather, he wished to insulate both vessels in something that does not add heat, such as where it became uncovered on Shabbos, when it is permitted to cover it. He thought it was permitted because we rule that one may insulate a cold item, but Rabbah forbade it because placing it atop the hot-water urn is like insulating it in something that adds heat. On Shabbos they permitted only placing one hot-water urn atop another in order to preserve its heat, as explained above.
