But with a material that adds heat, etc. See Tosefes Shabbos, who writes that sand is permitted even though it is among the materials that add heat, because it adds heat only to hot items, whereas it cools cold items.
Even to insulate something completely cold, etc. See Biur HaGra, who brings the source from Shabbos 48. Rava observed the servant of the Reish Galusa placing a pitcher of cold water upon the mouth of a kettle of hot water, and he prohibited it because it generates heat and is comparable to a material that adds heat. Thus, with something that adds heat, even a cold item is forbidden; see his words there. Nevertheless, one must understand that it is not literally classified as a material that adds heat, for if it were, it would be forbidden even before Shabbos. How, then, could the Shulchan Aruch rule below in siman 258 that it is permitted before Shabbos? Biur HaGra there also states explicitly that it is forbidden only on Shabbos. It appears that it is not literally called a material that adds heat. Rather, because it generates heat in the cold item, the Sages treated it like a material that adds heat for this purpose, so that it is at least forbidden on Shabbos. Damesek Eliezer explains similarly; see there in siman 258.
