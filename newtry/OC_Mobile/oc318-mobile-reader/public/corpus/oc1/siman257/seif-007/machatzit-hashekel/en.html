Tosefes Shabbos writes: ‘This follows the view of Hagahos Mordechai, who rules like the first opinion at the beginning of siman 253, that even regarding leaving food on the fire it is forbidden unless the food was fully cooked. According to our ruling, however, which follows the alternative opinion there and Chananya, that it is permitted once the food had reached ma'achal ben Derusai before Shabbos, this reasoning cannot be stated.’ End quote. In my humble opinion, if so, Magen Avraham should not have stated the matter without qualification. It therefore appears that Magen Avraham holds that even according to our ruling like the alternative opinion in siman 253, here the food must be fully cooked. The Beit Yosef writes in seif 7 that regarding insulation even mitzamek v'ra lo is forbidden, and the Rama writes that this is the primary ruling. Yet regarding mitzamek v'ra lo, even the first opinion at the beginning of siman 253, and likewise the Mechaber there who is stringent and requires food left on the fire to be fully cooked, concedes that mitzamek v'ra lo is permitted. The reason, as Magen Avraham writes there, is that the Sages were more stringent regarding insulation than regarding leaving food on the fire. Thus, insulation is more stringent l'chatchila than leaving food on the fire, even according to the first opinion in siman 253, which is the Mechaber's view there. Magen Avraham therefore holds that b'dieved as well one should not be more lenient than the law of leaving food on the fire according to the first opinion, namely the Mechaber's view at the beginning of siman 253. When Magen Avraham writes that it is ‘no more lenient than leaving food on the fire,’ he means leaving food on the fire according to the first opinion at the beginning of siman 253.
