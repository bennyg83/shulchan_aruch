Fully cooked, etc. This is not comparable to leaving food on a stove, which according to all opinions is permitted when the food is fully cooked, as stated in siman 253. Ordinarily, insulation is primarily intended for the next day and requires more stoking so that the dish not cool. Therefore, in every case we are concerned that he may stoke the coals if he insulates in glowing ashes, and because of this the Sages decreed against every material that adds heat, as explained above. Even when he insulates for the night, the Sages did not distinguish within their decree. Leaving food on a stove is different, because ordinarily it is intended only for the night. Since the food is left uncovered, it will not cool in a short time, and there is no concern that he will stoke the coals.
Even when continued cooking harms it. After the fact, all opinions permit food that is mitzamek v'ra lo, as stated above in seif 1.
And some are lenient, etc. This refers to insulating before Shabbos in something that adds heat. But to insulate on Shabbos in something that does not add heat is forbidden according to all opinions, even when the food is fully cooked and continued cooking harms it, as stated by the Gra and Peri Megadim.
Completely raw. This opinion holds that since the food is raw, even if he insulated it in glowing ashes, stoking would not cook it sufficiently for the night meal. By the next day it will cook even without stoking, so there is no concern that he will stoke the coals.
