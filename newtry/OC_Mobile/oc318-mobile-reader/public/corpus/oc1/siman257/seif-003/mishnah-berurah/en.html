Or sesame dregs. Although olive dregs and sesame dregs are equal regarding insulation within them, there is nevertheless a difference between them. With sesame dregs, and likewise with all the materials mentioned here, it is forbidden only to insulate the pot within them. If he insulated it in a permitted material, such as a basket of wool or the like, he may place the basket on them. On olive dregs, however, it is forbidden to place it, because they are hotter and add heat upward through the basket to the pot, as stated in the Gemara.
And straw. This applies whether it is long or cut into fine pieces. Grape pomace is the waste product of a winepress.
And soft materials. Any soft substance is called mochin, such as cotton, soft wool plucked from an animal, and fibers scraped from worn garments.
All three. Straw and grape pomace are counted as one item.
When moist. Moist materials produce much more heat than dry ones. The unqualified wording of the Mechaber implies that they are forbidden whether naturally moist or moistened by something else. Several later authorities, however, agree that the prohibition applies specifically when they are naturally moist, since these generate more heat than materials moistened by liquid that fell on them after they had dried.
Garments. The same applies to pillows, cushions, and fleeces of wool.
And produce. Such as wheat and legumes.
Or other feathers. The text first mentions dove wings because that was the common example in the Gemara.
And flax tow. Fine fibers shaken out from flax.
In stones. This means small stones from which fire is struck, called firestones. The statement that this is uncommon means that it is entirely uncommon to insulate in stones, because they break the pot or spoil the food. Regarding something in which people sometimes insulate, even if it is uncommon, the Sages issued their decree. The Maharshal writes in a responsum that hot ashes do not add heat and one may insulate in them, unless coals are mixed into them. Eliyah Rabbah, Tosefes Shabbos, and other later authorities cite this ruling. The same applies to any material that does not add heat; even if it is hot, insulation in it is permitted.
