And straw. And grape skins; see Taz, Bach, and Magen Avraham, letter 10. The Rambam, chapter 4 of Hilchos Shabbos, omits straw, and the Levush likewise omits it, but the Mechaber mentions it. See Eliyah Rabbah, letter 8, who writes that this is indeed the halacha: moist straw also adds heat. It appears that this applies whether the straw is long or has been cut into fine pieces. In our novellae at the beginning of chapter Kirah we discussed at length the meanings of straw, stubble, and chopped straw. Regarding salt, the unqualified wording implies that both mined salt and salt extracted from water add heat. Regarding sand, see Tosafos, Shabbos 38b, s.v. “No,” that cold sand cools and hot sand heats; sand heated by the sun heats even after it has cooled. See also the Yerushalmi at the beginning of Bameh Tominin: cold sand cools, hot sand heats, and straw is a custodian. The Gaon who comments there explains that it neither cools nor heats. This supports the Rambam, who omitted straw, for if the reference were to dry straw, why single out straw? In the Ran at the beginning of Bameh Tominin, Krakow edition, it appears that the reading is “the sand that we learned is a custodian,” meaning that the sand taught in the Mishnah as adding heat acts like a guardian, preserving it according to what was entrusted to it. The text would accordingly refer to sand, not straw. See there; we discussed this in our novellae. Magen Avraham writes many laws concerning stones. With God's help, this will be explained in Magen Avraham, letter 11. Magen Avraham, letter 10, will also explain the resolution of the phrase “when all three,” etc. This is not the place to elaborate.
