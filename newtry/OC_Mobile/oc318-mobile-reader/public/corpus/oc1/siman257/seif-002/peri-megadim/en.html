It is permitted. It is permitted to eat it; see Taz. Eliyah Rabbah, letter 4, writes that Hagahos Mordechai concludes, “Alternatively, there it was done intentionally,” etc., and Tosefes Shabbos writes similarly. Nevertheless, Taz holds as practical halacha primarily in accordance with his first explanation, that whenever one insulated with material that adds heat, it is forbidden even if done unintentionally. The people of Tiberias thought it was permitted, which indicates that they acted unintentionally, and even so the Sages forbade the water to them in Shabbos 38b because of insulation. They even forbade the water that had entered the pipe before Shabbos and had become heated before Shabbos; see siman 326, seif 3. The difficulty is that according to the opinion permitting b'dieved when one used material that adds heat, why did they forbid the people of Tiberias the water that entered the pipe and became heated before Shabbos? Hot water is mitzamek v'ra lo. With God's help, this will be explained in siman 326. This is not the place to elaborate.
And what the Rama wrote. The Rama's qualification, “provided that he is not accustomed to doing so,” refers to one who insulated for the following day. Words are missing there in the Levush; see Eliyah Rabbah, letter 5. What the Mechaber writes in seif 3 is that a fully cooked dish is forbidden on Shabbos when insulated in material that does not add heat. Even when fully cooked and mitzamek v'ra lo it is forbidden, lest he find his pot cold and bring it to a boil, etc.
