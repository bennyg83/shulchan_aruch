It causes it to cook. Magen Avraham writes that when it is insulated in a material that does not add heat, it plainly does not cause any cooking at all. Rather, the case is the manner described in seif 8: the pot stands upon a stove with coals beneath it, covered with ash, for otherwise, according to the Mechaber, it would be forbidden to place there a pot that is not fully cooked, and the pot is covered above with clothing arranged so that it does not touch the pot. As explained in seif 8, insulation in this manner is permitted, and adding a covering then causes additional cooking. Beit Me'ir disputes this and writes that the plain meaning of the Tur and Shulchan Aruch indicates that they refer to every case. Nachal Shalom writes similarly. Beit Me'ir explains that when one insulates something boiling that remains at a boil and continues cooking, even if the insulating material does not add heat but merely preserves its warmth and boiling state, every extension of the boiling and cooking causes the food to cook further. Adding another covering therefore causes cooking. This is proven from Rashi on folio 51, who writes that one removes the woolen coverings if he fears that his dish will become scorched, even though woolen coverings do not add heat. End quote. Biur HaGra likewise clearly holds like Beit Me'ir. At the beginning of seif 2 he brings proof from the teaching of our Rabbis that this applies even when the food is fully cooked; see there. I therefore did not record the words of Magen Avraham as practical halacha.
