Se'if 4: And it became uncovered after nightfall. So wrote the Sefer Mitzvos Gadol, Sefer Mitzvos Katan, and Sefer HaTerumah. The wording 'and it became uncovered' is not exact, for even initially it is permitted, as stated below. Rather, even in such a case it is permitted specifically when it became uncovered after nightfall. The Sefer Mitzvos Gadol cites the Yerushalmi, which states that this applies specifically after nightfall. Its wording at the end of chapter 4 is: 'If it became uncovered while it was still day, he may cover it while it is still day. If it became uncovered after nightfall, he may cover it after nightfall. If it became uncovered while it was still day and the day became sanctified upon him, it was taught: one may not insulate after nightfall.' In our Gemara, at the end of chapter 2 of Shabbos, there are those who say: 'Nevertheless, they said that one may not insulate,' etc. The Rif reads the passage in the reverse order, and so does the Rambam, explaining that the concern is lest it boil, meaning that the pot may come to a boil and he will need to uncover it and then cover it again. With something that adds heat, it is certainly forbidden even if it was covered before Shabbos. The Gemara asks: if so, it should also be forbidden during bein hashmashos, etc. The Rambam explains that during bein hashmashos it is permitted even with something that adds heat. The Ra'avad objected that bein hashmashos should not be more lenient than before Shabbos. Therefore, the Ran explains that the Gemara means: during bein hashmashos it should be forbidden even with something that does not add heat, because of the concern that it may boil and he may uncover it during bein hashmashos, etc.
Likewise. This is what the Rosh wrote in the name of Rabbeinu Yonah; see there, for Rabban Shimon ben Gamliel explains the view of the first Tanna.
