(א) And it is forbidden, etc. — and possibly there is support, etc. — apparently what was asked for Magen Avraham who wrote possibly — plainly Shach Yoreh De'ah 191 note 6: all agree regarding a gentile or apostate when there are not two river banks, even rabbinic prohibition does not apply; but regarding Israel even without two river banks, although no lifnei iver, nevertheless rabbinically forbidden, for one must separate from prohibition — all the more so not to aid oneself; unlike Rama there who brought two contradictions; likewise from Magen Avraham's own words below siman 357. However it seems different here, for Magen Avraham doubts the case here — feeding one who did not wash hands: he gives into the eater's mouth and the eater does not touch it; nevertheless Chazal required netilah lest he touch; also gemara Chullin 107b discusses whether netilah required — although conclusion requires netilah and as Shulchan Aruch wrote, nevertheless its prohibition is lighter than if the eater himself ate without netilah, for he certainly touches; if so there is reason to permit feeding when no two river banks — one can say this is not called aiding oneself; on the contrary he saves him from prohibition, for if he does not feed him he will eat himself and commit a more severe prohibition as above; see Rambam permitted to give, etc.; there wrote doctor may give — so from end of Magen Avraham's words, and it implies specifically for medical need.
