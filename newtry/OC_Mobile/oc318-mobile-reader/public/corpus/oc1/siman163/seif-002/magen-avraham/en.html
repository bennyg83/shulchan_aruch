And it is forbidden to feed him. It appears to me that this applies specifically when one gives him food belonging to the giver. If the piece belongs to the eater and one merely hands it to him, it is permitted, for even if he did not hand it to him, he could take it himself. This is unless they stand on opposite sides of a river, as stated in the first chapter of Avodah Zarah. See what I wrote in siman 347. It is possible, however, that there remains a prohibition against assisting transgressors. See Tosafos, Gittin 61a, and Avodah Zarah 55a. See also Rambam, at the end of chapter 10 of Hilchos Maaser, who writes that one may place demai belonging to an am haaretz into the hand of a sick am haaretz, but not into his mouth. Certainly, if the produce is definite tevel, even placing it into his hand is forbidden. Likewise, if the demai belongs to the physician, it is forbidden.
