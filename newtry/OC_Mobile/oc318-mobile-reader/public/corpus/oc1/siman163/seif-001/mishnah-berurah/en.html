(1) If there is no water, etc. Yam Shel Shlomo writes that those who wipe their hands with the moisture of grass when they have no water commit a transgression, because they treat washing the hands lightly. Their blessing is also in vain, for this is not washing at all. Peri Megadim cites this ruling.
(2) Available, etc. When water is available, eating even with a cloth is forbidden, lest he come to touch the food.
(3) Ahead of him at a distance greater than, etc. This means that when traveling, he estimates that he will not find water even beyond four mil, and he now wishes to eat. In that case, they permitted him to eat by wrapping a cloth around his hands. If, however, he estimates that he will obtain water immediately after four mil, he must wait until he reaches that place. Regarding water behind him, where returning involves additional exertion, a distance of one mil is sufficient: if he estimates that he will not find water within one mil, he may eat with a cloth. The later authorities write that the same applies when he is uncertain whether he will find water within four mil ahead or one mil behind him. If he desires to eat, he may also use a cloth. Guards of gardens and orchards who would need to leave their post to obtain water are required to go only less than one mil, like one traveling backward, because they cannot abandon their watch. Chayei Adam and the other later authorities agree that one sitting at home is likewise required to exert himself for water only within less than one mil, like one traveling backward. Only regarding water ahead of a traveler, where he is already going in that direction, did the Sages require him to wait as far as four mil.
(4) He should wash his hands, etc. The text should read “he should wrap,” meaning that he wraps his hands. Maamar Mordechai.
(5) In a cloth. Or in any other material. Gemara. See Biur Halachah. According to the Mechaber, it is reasonable that he must specifically wrap both hands, for even if he eats with one hand, he may touch the food with the other. According to Rama, however, there is room to be lenient. This requires study.
(6) In a liquid. Washing was also required for this, as stated above in siman 158. See what we wrote there in Mishnah Berurah. Eliyah Rabbah writes that where one has no cloth, he may eat even without a cloth, since he cannot obtain water within the required distance. In truth, however, this case is uncommon. Are we dealing with naked people who possess no kerchief with which to wrap their hands? He is certainly obligated to do so. The Aruch, who requires a cloth, is not a lone opinion, for Rabbeinu Chananel, in his commentary to Pesachim 47a, which has now become available to us, explicitly writes the same.
(7) With a spoon. The later authorities had difficulty with this ruling, for what is Rama’s source? A cloth is different because the hand is covered. Here, the hand remains uncovered, and there is greater concern that he may touch the food. Eliyah Rabbah resolves this only with difficulty. Therefore, if he has a cloth, he should specifically wrap his hand in the cloth.
