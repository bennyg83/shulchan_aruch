1) [Seif 1] If water is not available before him, etc. The Tur writes: 'It is forbidden to eat without netilas yadayim, even if one wishes to wrap his hands in a cloth or eat with a spoon so that he does not touch the bread.' The Beit Yosef comments that in chapter 8 of Chullin, 107b, the Gemara concludes that a cloth was permitted for those who eat terumah but was not permitted for those who eat non-sacred food in purity. Rashi explains that a cloth was permitted for those who eat terumah because kohanim are vigilant and will not touch it. It was not permitted for those who eat their non-sacred food in purity because they are not accustomed to guarding themselves as kohanim are. The Beit Yosef writes that this is also the view of the Rosh, that a cloth was not permitted for those who eat non-sacred food in purity, and all the more so for ordinary non-sacred food. This is likewise the view of Rabbeinu Yonah at the end of chapter Eilu Devarim, Sefer Mitzvos Gadol, and Sefer Mitzvos Katan. The Rambam, however, writes in chapter 6 of Hilchos Berachos that a person may wrap his hands in a cloth and eat bread or food dipped in liquid. His view appears to be that only those who eat their non-sacred food with the purity of sacred food or terumah were not permitted to eat with a cloth, but ordinary non-sacred food is permitted. The Ra'avad questions the Rambam and holds like the Rosh and Rabbeinu Yonah; see there. The Beit Yosef concludes that as a practical halachah, one should not permit eating non-sacred food through a cloth, since many great authorities disagree with the Rambam. However, if water is unavailable for one mil behind him and more than four mil ahead, he may eat specifically through a cloth, following the Aruch. This is the ruling we accept. These are his words here in the Shulchan Aruch. The Levush and Bach agree, and the later authorities write the same. See below, paragraph 9.
