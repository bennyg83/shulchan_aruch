[11] Seeds, etc. For the Omer and the Two Loaves (Rashi, chapter HaZahav). Shulchan Aruch and the authorities imply that in every circumstance Shabbos wages are prohibited. Shibbolei HaLeket, siman 31, writes that if a Jew rents an animal or utensils to his fellow by the day, he may not receive payment for Shabbos. The same applies to renting a room.
[12] There is no responsibility, etc. If it is stolen or lost (Ran, chapter HaShoel). This implies that for negligence he is liable like an unpaid custodian.
[13] [Levush] Or for the week, etc. Meaning that he omits the wage for one day of the week, namely Shabbos (Malbushei Yom Tov).
[14] It is considered a day laborer, etc. Beit Yosef cites Shibbolei HaLeket similarly, but comments that the other authorities do not appear to agree. Perhaps he did not see Ran in chapter 5 of Ketubot, who explains that if the employer dismisses him in the middle of the week, he need not pay for the whole week but only for the days worked, and therefore it is considered Shabbos wages. Ran proves this from Hagahot Maimoniyot. The same applies to renting a room (Magen Avraham). In my humble opinion this resembles one who says, "Twelve dinars per year, one dinar each month," in Choshen Mishpat siman 312, seif 15, where there is doubt whether we follow the first expression regarding a leap month. The same doubt should apply here. Ran's case there concerns a rebellious wife, where an amount is actually added for each day, but he does not discuss this rental case. This also explains why Responsa Rabbi Aharon Sasson, siman 73, remains uncertain here. It also appears to me that the worker cannot be dismissed in the middle of the week when hired for a month merely because the wage was stated as so much for each day rather than as a lump sum. This still requires study. Magen Avraham further writes that interest bearing loans must be structured so that if repaid in the middle of the week the borrower pays for the whole week or pays nothing for that week, rather than calculating by days. Similarly, a penalty assessed at a fixed amount per day includes prohibited Shabbos payment, whereas a debt reduced by a certain amount each day is not considered Shabbos wages. It appears to me that regarding interest, if from the outset they specified payment by the week and not by the day, then even if in practice the lender later accepts less than a full week's payment, this is merely a waiver by him. Rashi in the chapter Af Al Pi explains that the prohibition of Shabbos wages is a decree because of buying and selling. Beit Yosef in siman 585 asks that buying and selling itself is rabbinic, creating a decree upon a decree. In my humble opinion he overlooked Beitzah 37a, which says that various acts were decreed because of buying and selling, and Rashi and Tosafot explain that this is not a double decree because all are part of one decree.
