It is called day labor. So too the Bedek HaBayis wrote in the name of Shibolei HaLeket, and he wrote concerning it that this does not appear from the words of the poskim. Perhaps he did not see the words of the Ran, who wrote that the reason is that if he dismisses him in the middle of the week, he need not pay him for the whole week, but only for the days that passed. Therefore he is called a day laborer, and it constitutes wages for Shabbos. This is also evident from the Gemara. The same applies to one who rents a room to his fellow. It appears to me that the same applies to those who lend with interest, that they must lend in such a manner that if he repays in the middle of the week, he pays for the whole week, or pays nothing at all, and they should not calculate by the days. For even regarding a rebellious spouse, whom they penalized that as long as he remains rebellious he gives such-and-such per day, nevertheless it is forbidden to give the portion for Shabbos, for that is wages for Shabbos. The same applies to every comparable penalty. But if they reduce from the debt such-and-such each day, that is not called wages for Shabbos in such a case. So it appears from the Gemara in the fifth chapter of Kesubos.
