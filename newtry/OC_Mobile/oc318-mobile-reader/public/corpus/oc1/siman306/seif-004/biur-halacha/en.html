To guard seeds — see Bava Metzia 58a, that specifically when they acquired from his hand; otherwise there is no law of guarding in what is attached to the ground; and even if he were a weekly or monthly hire he is not liable for what is attached to the ground when stolen:

He gives him, etc. — see Mishna Berurah, that he can deduct from his wages if he did not guard on Shabbat, as stated plainly; and even though in Chayei Adam, general 60, law 8, it appears that when he deducts from him if he will not guard on Shabbat, it is no longer considered absorption — it is not clear at all; and his proof that he brought from Ketubot 64b, s.v. "it appears," is no proof at all, for there he cannot retract from his quarrel in the middle; and therefore certainly they enacted that it should count for each day separately — unlike in our matter, where he hired him for a full month; and is it because he will deduct when he does not guard some day that it will count as a day hire? Certainly the law is thus: one who is a monthly or weekly hire — if some day of work is lacking, he can deduct from him, for he accepted upon himself to guard a full month or week; and nevertheless his wages are called Shabbat wages in absorption, as stated plainly:

And he stipulated to pay him, etc. — see in Mishna Berurah that this means he stipulated with the worker that he will pay him per the calculation of the days — this wording indicates that he will be able to dismiss him in the middle, etc., as is proven in Darkei Moshe for one who examines there; and I saw in Eliyah Rabbah that to him it appears he cannot dismiss him in the middle of the week, since he hired him for a month and told him only to pay him each day, etc., and not at the end — until here. And behold, from this it appears he thought that Rama's intent is: from that which he promised to pay at the end of each day and not at the end of the week or month like other hires that are paid only at the end — it is evident he can dismiss him each day; and therefore he is a day hire; and on this Eliyah Rabbah properly disputes: why should we say he can dismiss in the middle; and he retracted from what he hired at first for a month — better to say he did not retract at all, only that he obligated himself to pay at the end of each day to the worker, like other obligations a person can impose upon himself; and like the matter they said: a gift, a borrower is like a lender. But in truth it appears to me plainly that in this manner Rama also agrees to Eliyah Rabbah; and Rama's intent is as Darkei Moshe wrote, and as we copied in Mishna Berurah:

He is called a day hire — and likewise in Darkei Moshe in the name of Shibulei Leket; and he wrote on it: it does not appear so from the words of the poskim; and it is possible he did not see the words of the Ran, who wrote the reason: if they dismiss him in the middle of the week he need not pay him for the whole week, only for the days that passed — therefore he is called a day hire, and it is Shabbat wages — until here, Magen Avraham; and from his words it appears that this law in Shibulei Leket is the words of the Ran; and what he challenged against Beit Yosef because he did not see the words of the Ran — in my humble opinion one need not say thus about Maran Beit Yosef, for in truth the law in Shibulei Leket is a different law, thus: in Darkei Moshe he wrote: Shibulei Leket wrote that if he says such-and-such you will give me per month that he will come such per day — it is also like Shabbat wages; it does not appear so from the poskim I wrote nearby — until here; meaning: Shibulei Leket holds that what he concluded "that he will come such per day" is retraction from what he said at first that he hired him for a month; and Beit Yosef holds that what he concluded "that he will come such per day" is mere calculation — that the worker calculates how much he will profit each day; and Beit Yosef holds that this is like one who says: give me my wages for ten days — that even in this he hints at day wages for Shabbat, nevertheless since he did not claim explicitly the wages of each day separately, it is permitted; the same here in our matter: certainly at the end when he claims he will claim for the whole month or week — what difference is it to us that in the middle he calculated how much is due him for each day? And this is what Beit Yosef concluded and wrote: it does not appear so from the poskim I wrote nearby. But the law in Darkei Moshe that he proved from the Ran is a different law, thus: the Ran wrote at the end of "Even though": if he hired him for a month, if he stipulated with him that he will pay per the calculation of the days — he is called a day hire, and it is Shabbat wages, for he can retract in the middle of the month — therefore it is not called monthly hire — until here; and this wording refers to a hire who stipulated with the worker that he will pay per the calculation of the days, and this indicates he can dismiss him [but the worker need not stipulate, for without that the worker can retract even in the middle of the day, as the poskim wrote]; and he holds that since the employer can dismiss in the middle, therefore he is only like a day hire; and Beit Yosef too possibly does not dispute this; nevertheless, the essence of Darkei Moshe's law that he brought as proof from the Ran is not compelled, for there one can say the enactment was l'chatchila for each day separately, as written in Sefer Ma'amar Mordechai — see there; and see Turei Zahav:
