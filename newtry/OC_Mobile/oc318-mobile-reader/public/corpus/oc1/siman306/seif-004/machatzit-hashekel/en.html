(s.k. 4) Stood, etc. — his and theirs — for animal and utensils are likewise like the feet of the owners, that is, with that person with whom the utensils and animal were at twilight, likewise like his feet; and a person cannot take them beyond the place where those owners were permitted to walk; and if he gave the animal to a shepherd who dwells five thousand amot to the west side of the town, and thus the owner of the animal dwells in the town — the owner of the animal is permitted to walk to the west side two thousand amot, and the animal is like the feet of the shepherd and is permitted to walk from the shepherd's place two thousand amot; and the animal is distant from the town three thousand amot; and thus if the animal stands within the third thousand — behold it is outside its techum and outside his techum, that is, of the owner of the animal; even so he calls to it.

But forbidden, etc. — outside its techum. Meaning in the aforementioned case: if the owner of the animal placed an eruv to the west side, thus the owner of the animal is permitted to walk to the west side four thousand amot, and the shepherd did not place an eruv toward the town — thus the animal is not permitted to walk more than two thousand amot; thus when the animal stands within the third thousand it is outside its techum, but it is within the techum of the owner of the animal, and the owner of the animal is permitted to walk there to the place of the animal; but nevertheless forbidden to bring it by hand, only to call to it specifically.

And it deals with 397 — so it should read.
