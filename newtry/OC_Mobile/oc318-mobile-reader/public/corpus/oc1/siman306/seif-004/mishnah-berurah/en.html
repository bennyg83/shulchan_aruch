(15) One who hires workers — it deals when he hired him for days that for each day he guards he will give him such-and-such, and he guarded also on Shabbat; and he says he need not pay him for Shabbat day; and see Tur, for it is proven that even if he wishes to give him, it is forbidden to hire to take the wage unless he gives him as a gift [Peri Megadim].

(16) He does not give, etc. — rabbinically, a decree because of buying and selling.

(17) There is no liability, etc. — that if damage occurred to them on Shabbat he is not obligated to pay.

(18) He was a Shabbat hire — that is, for the entire week together; and therefore he gives him Shabbat wage; and meaning that even if the worker returns within the time, he does not deduct Shabbat wages from the time that passed, since it is swallowed with the other days [and this is what the Mechaber concludes, that the worker can say "give me wages for ten days," for it deals when the worker returns in the middle of the second week]; and consequently he is obligated to guard also on Shabbat, and if loss occurred he is obligated to pay; and it appears that even if no loss occurred, only he did not guard on Shabbat day, he may deduct from his wage as if he did not guard another day; and see Biur Halacha.

(19) To pay him each day, etc. — that is, he stipulated with him that he will pay him per the count of days; and this language indicates the employer can retract in the middle of the week and need not pay him for the entire week, only for the days that passed; and therefore it is called a day hire, and forbidden for him to accept Shabbat wage at the end, even if he pays him for the entire month together; and see below s.k. 20; and Magen Avraham wrote that thus is the law when renting a room to his fellow for a month and he stipulated with the renter that he will pay per the count of days — likewise forbidden to take Shabbat rent, and the reason as above; and likewise those who lend with interest to a gentile for a year or month must be careful not to stipulate with the borrower in stam that he will pay per the count of days that he holds the money, for this language indicates that whenever he pays him, even if he pays in the middle of the week, he calculates the interest per the count of days; and therefore each day is considered separately, and he will need to deduct at the end Shabbat wages for all the Shabbatot; rather he should stipulate with him that if he pays in the middle of the week he pays the interest for that entire week or does not pay interest at all for this last week.

(20) It is called a day hire — and in Sefer Shevut Yaakov he disputes and holds that from the Ran there is no proof for this law, for in our case even according to his words he can retract and remove him in the middle of the week, nevertheless presumably he will not retract in the middle, and it is a monthly hire and afterward the Shabbat wage is called swallowed and permitted; and also in Sefer Avnei Rozel he tends that there is doubt in this law — see there his reason.

(21) Or he should say, etc. — and what merchants are accustomed to hire a Jew on erev Shabbat to guard the calves from theft — the matter is difficult how the merchant transgresses lifnei iver, since he knows he will certainly accept Shabbat wage; therefore proper he stipulate with the guard that he guard also on erev Shabbat day and motzei Shabbat certain hours, and then it is like Shabbat hire swallowed and permitted [Chayei Adam].
