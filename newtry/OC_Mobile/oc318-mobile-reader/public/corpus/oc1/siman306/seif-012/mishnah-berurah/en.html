(48) About a lost object — so one who knows of it will come and tell, for returning a lost object is a mitzvah; and to tell one who knows of it that he should return it — this too is in the category of matters of Heaven and permitted; and they are accustomed to announce also about a gentile's lost object because of ways of peace; and in a place of danger it is obvious permitted; and the Acharonim wrote that likewise permitted to announce on Shabbat about theft.

(49) Even if, etc. — that the returner cannot return today, nevertheless permitted in order to publicize the matter on Shabbat, which is a time of assembly for the many.

(50) Communal bans — and likewise if they excommunicated him in a dream, they release him on Shabbat, for it is Shabbat need that he not remain in his excommunication.

(51) It is like communal affairs — it appears that if it is for individual need to release what the congregation banned him, it would be forbidden when not for Shabbat need; but from what he wrote in Bedek HaBayit in the name of Rabbeinu Yerucham, and likewise from the responsum of Rashba brought below in Beit Yosef siman 341, it appears that even for individual need they release him, because on a weekday he cannot gather the congregation easily to release him; and likewise the Acharonim tend toward this law. Beit Yosef wrote end siman 307 about what they practice at fairs that the beadle calls from the scroll one who is banned by the court for his debts — it is like communal affairs, for without this debts and all commerce would be lost, and no teacher has power to abolish what they practiced to be lenient because of many great ones.

(52) But, etc. — and Magen Avraham proved that the law of ban and its release is equal: for an individual it is forbidden to ban if he transgressed a sin unless he desecrated Shabbat, which is like for Shabbat need; and for communal need permitted even if not for Shabbat need, for if they do not ban on Shabbat they will not assemble another day and the matter will be nullified; and in Sefer Avnei Rozel he tends that even for communal need it is not worthwhile to ban on Shabbat, because it has blessing written upon it; and see Sefer Zechor le'Avraham in laws of Shabbat letter 18, where he brought from Kenesset haGedolah that the simple custom is to be lenient, as the words of Magen Avraham above. And in responsa Devarim Ribbot it is found that a sage may excommunicate on Shabbat for his honor.
