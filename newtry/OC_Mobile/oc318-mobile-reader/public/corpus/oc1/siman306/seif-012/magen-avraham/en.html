About a lost object. Returning a lost object, and telling someone who knows of it, is a mitzvah. Nowadays they are accustomed to announce a non-Jew’s lost object on Shabbos because of the ways of peace, though I do not know where this is alluded to. But where there is a concern of danger, it is obvious that it is permitted.
Communal bans. The same applies if he was placed under ban in a dream, as stated in Radbaz, part 1, siman 118.
Like communal affairs. This implies that if it is an individual need, it is forbidden to release the ban if it is not for a Shabbos need. In Bedek HaBayis he wrote in the name of Rabbeinu Yerucham, path 17, part 7, that the reason is that on a weekday he cannot easily gather the congregation and they will not be able to release him, so it is like a husband’s annulment of his wife’s vows, which is permitted, as written in siman 341. The Bach wrote at the end of siman 307 that the custom at fairs, where the shamash reads from a written document who has been placed under ban by beis din because of his debts, may be justified because of loss. It must be announced on Shabbos, when all gather and know to compel him in his city to pay; otherwise the debts and all commerce would be lost. Furthermore, it affects the public and is considered communal affairs. Therefore no authority has the power to abolish this custom, which they practiced leniently before several great authorities.
For the need of Shabbos. This resembles releasing vows, as the Maggid Mishneh writes. It implies that the same applies to a communal matter, which is permitted, as I wrote above in the name of Rabbeinu Yerucham. The Rama requires study, since he wrote ‘but,’ etc., for the law of a ban and its release are equal in this regard. So too Sefer Divrei Rivos, siman 308, writes that a sage may place someone under ban on Shabbos for his own honor. Rashdam, siman 8, wrote that it is permitted to place under ban one who desecrated Shabbos, and so too is written in Sefer Chasidim, siman 409. See Sefer Shiyarei Knesses HaGedolah, page 49.
