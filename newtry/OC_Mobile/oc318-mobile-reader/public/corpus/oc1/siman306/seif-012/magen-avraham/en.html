About a lost object — for returning a lost object, and to tell one who knows of it, is a mitzvah; and they are now accustomed to announce a gentile's lost object on Shabbat because of ways of peace; and I did not know where this is hinted; but in a place where there is danger of the matter, plainly permitted.

Communal bans — and likewise if they excommunicated him in a dream [Radbaz ch. 1 letter 118].

Like communal affairs — it implies that if it is individual need forbidden to release the ban if not for Shabbat need; and in Bedek HaBayit he wrote in the name of Rabbenu Yerucham year 17 ch. 7 the reason: on a weekday he cannot gather the congregation easily and they cannot release him for him, and it is like a wife's vows that are permitted, as written siman 341; Beit Yosef wrote small seif 307 about what they practice at fairs that the beadle calls from the scroll one banned by the court for his debts — there is room to settle the custom because of loss, for they need to announce on Shabbat when all assemble and know to compel him in his city to pay; and without this debts and all commerce would be lost; and moreover it touches the many and is communal affairs; and therefore no teacher has power to abolish this custom they practiced to be lenient before many great ones — end of his words.

For Shabbat need — resembles permission of vows [Maggid Mishnah]; and it implies that likewise for the congregation it is permitted, as above in the name of Rabbenu Yerucham; and it requires study on Rama who wrote "but," etc., for behold the law of ban and its release is equal in this; and so in Sefer Devarim Ribbot siman 308: a sage may excommunicate on Shabbat for his honor; and Rashdam siman 8 wrote permitted to excommunicate one who desecrated Shabbat; and so in Shulchan Aruch HaRav siman 499; and see in Sefer Shiyurei Yehudah page 49.
