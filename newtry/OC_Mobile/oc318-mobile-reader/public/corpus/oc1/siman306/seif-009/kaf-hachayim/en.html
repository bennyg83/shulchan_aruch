Gloss: And likewise it is forbidden to stroll, etc. — it implies that even within the techum it is forbidden; but from the words of Tosafot that I wrote nearby (above letter Heh) — that where the matter is not evident it is permitted within the techum — one can permit strolling to find a horse, etc. See there letter Bet; and likewise Magen Avraham s.k. 3, that it is forbidden only specifically where the matter is evident. And likewise Eliyah Rabbah letter Gimel: specifically at the end of the techum or where the matter is evident. And likewise Turei Zahav letter Bet. However, the Rav Chayei Adam, general 60 letter Bet, wrote that this is called being occupied with one's affairs on Shabbat, even though the matter is not evident at all — it is forbidden.
