[9] And this is the main ruling, etc. This is because the Rama indicated that this is the view of the Ran, Mordechai, Maggid Mishneh, and Hagahot Maimoniyot. In truth, the Beit Yosef wrote so as well. But I am surprised, for specifically a shevut is prohibited, whereas regarding what is prohibited because of "finding your affairs," they agree that it is permitted. If so, Rashi explained in Beitzah 36 that buying and selling are prohibited because of "finding your affairs" and "speaking speech." However, according to Rashi's second explanation, that alternatively it may lead to writing a document, the matter depends on another dispute. But perhaps everyone agrees that even a shevut is prohibited for the sake of a mitzvah. There is another difficulty: in seif 6 the Shulchan Aruch and Levush wrote simply that mentioning the purchase price is forbidden, while here Rama and Levush wrote it as a dispute; and in Beit Yosef it is clearly implied that he treats them as one matter. This difficulty, however, seems to me answerable: seif 6 is speaking where it is possible even without mentioning the purchase price. Although the Beit Yosef implies that even where it is impossible it is forbidden, nevertheless the Shulchan Aruch can be explained this way. See seif katan 18.
