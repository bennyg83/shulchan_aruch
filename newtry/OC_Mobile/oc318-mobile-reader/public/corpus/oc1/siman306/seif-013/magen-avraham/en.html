To announce on Shabbos. In Constantinople they are accustomed to announce whoever wishes to rent a house or buy one, but the responsa of the Geonim forbid this, as quoted by the Knesses HaGedolah.
And if not, he will lose, etc. It is forbidden specifically in such a case, because it is like judging a case. But if he announces generally, ‘Anyone who has a claim should come and state it,’ even though there is no enactment in the city that if he does not state it his claim is void, nevertheless this is not like judging a case. If there is a place where they practice this way, we do not protest against them. Nevertheless, initially one should not practice this way, because it is weekday speech, as the Rivash writes.
To announce wine. The Levush wrote: I have not seen them practice this way, for in all places they announce even the purchase price, and perhaps they consider it a matter of Heaven for the need of kiddush. According to what I wrote in seif 3, this is not a valid permission. In Yam Shel Shlomo, fifth chapter of Beitzah, siman 8, he wrote that one should announce before Barchu, and if he forgot until after Barchu, he should not mention the purchase price. In Maharil he wrote that even to announce who the seller is is forbidden. It appears to me that since in his place good wine was common, it was not a mitzvah need. As for other beverages, it is obvious that they are forbidden.
