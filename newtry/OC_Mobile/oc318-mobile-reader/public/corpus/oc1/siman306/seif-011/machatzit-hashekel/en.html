(s.k. 11) And to pledge, etc. — rather we hold thus: when one assigns a valuation upon himself, etc. — this is not the language of the Ran, rather the language of the Ran; and "one may not assign valuations" — for it is not a known object, etc.; and intentionally Shulchan Aruch changed Magen Avraham's language of the Ran to reject the Beit Yosef's answer, as will be explained.

And on Beit Yosef two [words] — thus the Beit Yosef: it appears to me that assigning valuation of another person is fine for buying and selling, for it is as one who acquires this person from another since he vowed to give his valuation to hekdesh — end of his words; and this is fine when assigning another person, but when assigning oneself this reason does not apply, for it is not applicable to say he acquired himself; and therefore Magen Avraham copied above in the Ran's question "when one assigns valuation upon himself," etc., as above; and perhaps the Beit Yosef holds that also when assigning oneself it applies that it is as acquiring oneself.

And according to him forbidden to dedicate a Torah scroll on Shabbat, etc.; and Taz s.k. 2, thus: rather he should hold it in the house and dedicate it orally — meaning he should dedicate it orally from erev Shabbat, and on Shabbat bring it to the synagogue, for thus was their custom to bring their donations to the synagogue on Shabbat; and therefore in any case he dedicates it orally; and then bringing on Shabbat is permitted.

As written in Choshen Mishpat siman 95: even though we hold one does not swear on hekdesh, nevertheless for our hekdesh for the poor or synagogue or Torah scroll or the like, one swears, for ordinary people have rights in them.

That the dedicator also has a share in it — if so, according to this reason permitted to dedicate a Torah scroll on Shabbat.
