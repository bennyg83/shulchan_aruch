In their script — see in Mishna Berurah; and because this law is relevant to many matters, I said to explain this matter. Behold, Rama copied in the name of Or Zarua that writing other than Assyrian script is only rabbinic; and I examined there in Or Zarua in Laws of Shabbat and saw he wrote there that it is not called writing from the Torah except Assyrian script and Greek script; and the essence of his foundation is in the Yerushalmi according to his explanation — and as we will bring below; and even though in our matter this explanation leans toward stringency, that we do not permit even through a gentile except in their script, and for this reason Rama copied his words in order to be concerned for his view — nevertheless, so as not to cause mishap in many matters, I am compelled to elaborate here and explain that all poskim dispute this. Behold, in Shabbat 103 there is in the Mishnah: one who writes in any language is liable — and this teaches explicitly that the intent is in any language literally; for if the Mishnah's intent were only to include Greek writing, it should not have said "in any language" [and even though no proof is needed for this, nevertheless in order to clarify the matter I did not refrain: behold, in Megillah 8b there is in the Mishnah: there is no difference between scrolls and tefillin and mezuzot except that scrolls are written in any language, etc.; and the Gemara: they are written only in Assyrian script — Rabban Shimon ben Gamliel: even in scrolls they did not permit that they be written except in Greek]; and I saw in Or Zarua that he brought proof there for his view from that which is stated in the Yerushalmi there: what of "in any language" — even Alef-Alfa [and in Or Zarua the girsa is further Beita-Veita, and it is a scribal error — it should read Beit-Beita, as in the Mishnah in Shekalim ch. 3: in Greek it is Alpha, Beta, Gamma]; and from that the Yerushalmi adjoins "even Alef-Alfa Beit-Beita" it appears specifically when they wrote in our script or Greek script, but in another script it is not considered melacha; and in truth its explanation in the Yerushalmi is very puzzling: first, behold we learned in the Mishnah "in any language" — how does the Yerushalmi explain for us that the intent is Assyrian or Greek? And further, according to its explanation, what is "even Alef" that the Yerushalmi says — it should have said Alpha Beta Gamma; and further, according to its explanation the Yerushalmi contradicts itself within the space of speech, for it is stated stated in the Mishnah: one who writes two letters, whether of one name or of two names or of two signs, in any language, is liable — R' Yosi: they did not make two letters liable except because of marking, etc.; and the Yerushalmi says on this: who is the tanna of signs? R' Yosi — for the Yerushalmi holds signs are from the language of "sign" and means two signs merely that indicate some matter, as Rav Hai Gaon explained, brought in Maggid Mishneh there in Korban Edah; and on this it says R' Yosi holds the liability of writing is because of marking, and even on two scratches merely he is liable; and afterward it says: what of "in any language" — even Alef-Alfa; and if the explanation is as he says, the Mishnah contradicts itself — at first "two signs" of R' Yosi, and what it said "in any language" intending Assyrian and Greek is not R' Yosi, for according to R' Yosi certainly he is liable in any language, for it is no less than marking merely, for which R' Yosi makes liable; and also that the Yerushalmi's words after this clarify R' Yosi's words — see there; and how can we say that at first it deals with R' Yosi and at the end with R' Yosi, and in the middle the Yerushalmi interrupts and says not according to R' Yosi — especially since the Mishnah contradicts itself, as we wrote; and further, why should Shabbat matters differ from gittin and other documents, where it is stated explicitly in Gittin 19 that it is in any language, and so ruled in Even haEzer siman 126; and there too it is written "and he shall write for her" — behold it is the manner of writing; and therefore there must be another explanation of the Yerushalmi's words; and it appears to me the Yerushalmi's intent is: at first it asks from "two signs" — whose tanna is it? R' Yosi; and afterward it asks: what is the inclusion of "in any language" — meaning, what is the extension, since even marking merely makes liable, and any language is no less than marking merely; therefore it says "even Alef-Alfa" — meaning, that at first it was written Alef-Bet and he added a letter in each word and it became Alpha Beta; and one might have thought this is inferior, for his addition accomplishes nothing — and it teaches he is liable, for it is writing in any language; and in Greek Alef is not a word at all until he completed that other letter; and likewise in Bet he completed another letter and it became Beta; and even though we require that the two letters be customary and read together, the Yerushalmi holds that this too is included, after the two words are adjacent; and per the Yerushalmi it appears the Mishnah deals when the body of the writing was Assyrian, only the language is Greek; and the extension is as stated; but in truth it is plain that even if the body of the writing were also Greek or of any language, he is liable — as we rule regarding gittin in Even haEzer siman 126, that in any script and any language it is valid; and even though there it is written "and he shall write" [gloss: further we can say the Yerushalmi's intent is to hint at what Rabbenu Yerucham wrote in Netiv 12 part 14: if one letter is from this language and one letter from another language he is liable — and he brought no source for this; and it is possible he explains the Yerushalmi "even Alef-Alfa" according to its plain sense — that is, one letter he wrote the form in our script and one letter the form in Greek script; and the same in another language; and I saw another explanation on the Yerushalmi in the margin of the Talmud, and it is not clear — for then it should have said "even Alef-Alef"; and further, it appears in our Gemara that Galician is not included in language]. The conclusion of our words: know that for every script of every nation one is liable from the Torah; and see that all the Rishonim hold thus: Rambam ch. 11 of Laws of Shabbat wrote explicitly that one who writes in any script and any language is liable; and so it is evident from Rashi on the Mishnah that for every script and language he is liable; and likewise Alfasi and Rosh copied the Mishnah "in any language" liable as it stands, and did not emend it at all from the Yerushalmi's words; and according to Or Zarua's explanation they should have brought it to teach that specifically Greek and not another script [and this alone proves explicitly, as the Rosh wrote in ch. "One who injures" that on their script there is also a Torah prohibition, only that because of settling Eretz Yisrael they permitted telling a gentile]; and likewise the Ran wrote explicitly on the Mishnah that for every script of the seventy languages he is liable; and likewise Meiri wrote in these words of the Ran; and likewise it is evident from Rabbenu Yerucham in Netiv 12 part 14; and likewise from Tosafot Gittin 8b regarding one who buys a house from a gentile, that their script is also a Torah prohibition, only that because of settling Eretz Yisrael they permitted telling a gentile in this [and this proof Magen Avraham also brought in siman 340]; and likewise it is evident from Rashba in Bava Kamma ch. "One who injures," and Ramban, and Ritva in ch. "R' Eliezer on circumcision," like the aforementioned Tosafot; and likewise from Ba'al haItur it is evident as we said, that he brought from this sugya that they write upon him "ono" — melacha of the Torah, permitted through a gentile where there is mitzvah need; and likewise evident from many poskim who copied the aforementioned Mishnah as it stands; and Magen Avraham in siman 340 wondered on Rama's words here — see there what he elaborates and wonders on Hagahot Maimoniot who wrote similarly. And we will further explain in Tosafot Nofach: one cannot exempt writing in other languages on the ground that even in the holy tongue there are poskim who hold that if the crowns of Shaatnez Getz are missing he is exempt — how much more so in other languages that have no Assyrian form at all: first, we rule above in siman 36 that the crowns do not invalidate, and automatically he is liable on Shabbat in every case; and even the poskim who hold that in Assyrian writing, if a crown is missing it is invalid and automatically exempt for Shabbat — that is specifically in writing a Torah scroll, where they hold it is not called writing without the crown Shaatnez Getz; but regarding gittin, behold it is ruled in Even haEzer that they write Assyrian writing without crowns; and therefore, as Sefer haTerumah distinguishes regarding writing a Torah scroll it is different — and even according to Riba, who holds that regarding gittin too we require the letters be crowned, he also agrees that in another language it is valid, and one cannot say there "missing a crown," since such is the manner of writing there; and as stated in the Mishnah [Gittin ch. 7]: a bill of divorce written in Hebrew and witnesses in Greek, etc.; and as all this Mordechai wrote in Laws of Ketanot — see there. The conclusion of our words: know that all the Rishonim go in one view, like the plain sense of the aforementioned Mishnah, that for every script and every language he is liable; and the words of Or Zarua and Hagahot Maimoniot ch. 1 of Laws of Tefillin that they brought in the name of Rabbenu Yoel are a lone view, rejected by all these many authorities; and see below in siman 340 in Peri Megadim, who somewhat reconciles Rabbenu Yoel's view brought in Hagahot Maimoniot from Magen Avraham's difficulty that in any language he is liable; and Peri Megadim strained that he will explain the Mishnah "in any language" meaning only regarding the language alone, but the body of the writing perhaps requires specifically Assyrian — see there; and he did not see Rabbenu Yoel's words, master of this view brought in Or Zarua, who explains explicitly in "in any language" regarding the body of the letters, and includes only Greek; and further, what he brought from gittin that it is written "and he shall write," and it is evident in Gittin 19, and so ruled in Shulchan Aruch Even haEzer 126, that it is written in any script — the same for Shabbat, as in the name of all the Rishonim; afterward I found in Noda BiYehudah Mahadura Tinyana siman 33 that he too wrote as we said, that every script is from the Torah; and he concluded that in any event Or Zarua is alone in the matter and we found no companion for him:
