To buy a house. In Matzas Shmurim, part 1, siman 31, he wrote in the name of the Rivash and the Rosh that it is forbidden to buy, unless he bought it before Shabbos, in which case it is permitted to tell a non-Jew to write on Shabbos.
In Eretz Yisrael. Syria is like Eretz Yisrael for this matter, as the Rambam writes in chapter 6. From the Gemara in the first chapter of Gittin the Rambam was precise and wrote ‘for this matter,’ for as a matter of law Syria is outside Eretz Yisrael, as the Rambam wrote at the beginning of Hilchos Terumos. Aram Naharaim, Aram Tzova, Achlav, and the like are called Syria and are not like Eretz Yisrael, etc.; all are like Syria by enactment of the Sages, etc. What is Syria? From Eretz Yisrael and below, opposite Aram Naharaim and Aram Tzova, all along the Euphrates until Bavel, such as Damascus, Achlav, Charan, Magbab, and the like, until Shinar and Tzahar, are like Syria. But Acco is outside Eretz Yisrael like Ashkelon. Nevertheless, this requires study, for the baraisa holds that an individual conquest is called a conquest, and if so it is forbidden to give them settlement in the land because of the prohibition of ‘do not show them favor’; therefore one may violate a D'Rabbanan prohibition. But since the Rambam ruled that an individual conquest is not called a conquest, why should a D'Rabbanan prohibition be set aside for the sake of another D'Rabbanan matter? This is even more difficult, for in Yoreh Deah siman 151 we conclude that one may even initially sell houses to non-Jews in Syria, and certainly one should not be permitted to buy from them on Shabbos. This requires study. Perhaps he holds that in any event there is some mitzvah in buying houses. Hagahos Ashri, second chapter of Moed Katan, in the name of Or Zarua, writes: How does he do it? He shows him purses of dinars and the non-Jew signs, etc. This implies that it is forbidden to give him money.
