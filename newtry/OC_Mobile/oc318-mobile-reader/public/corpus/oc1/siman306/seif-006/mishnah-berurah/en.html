(25) Matters of Heaven, etc. — for it is written "if you turn back your foot from Shabbat from pursuing your affairs," etc.; and they expounded: your affairs are forbidden, matters of Heaven permitted.
(26) Accounts — and permitted to calculate what he needs for a mitzvah meal [Magen Avraham].
(27) And to pledge tzedakah — for the poor or for the synagogue. And to donate some object for the synagogue they are also accustomed to be lenient, even though we say one may not assign valuations [i.e. say "the valuation of so-and-so is upon me"] and one may not dedicate on Shabbat, as is brought below siman 339 — that is specifically dedication for the altar or Temple maintenance; but our dedications are permitted, for there is no sacredness upon them, only they are profane; and nevertheless l'chatchila proper to be careful when dedicating some known object on Shabbat, such as a Torah scroll or crown for a Torah scroll, that he dedicate it orally from erev Shabbat, even between himself and himself; and then even if he brings it on Shabbat to the synagogue it is only publicizing the dedication he dedicated from erev Shabbat. And to pledge money for tzedakah and not a known object — permitted even l'chatchila according to all, for the reason forbidden to dedicate is because it is like buying and selling, removing the object from his domain to another domain; and this does not apply to money; and likewise when he does not designate the object on Shabbat, only says stam "I vow a Torah scroll," even though he has one in his house, permitted according to all, for it has not yet left his domain since he did not designate specifically this one.
(28) And to supervise communal affairs — to examine and investigate, for communal needs are like mitzvah need. And permitted even to go to taverns where gentiles gather in order to supervise for the sake of the many. And the poskim wrote that telling a gentile to do melacha and not another shevut for communal affairs was not permitted — only supervision and involvement in matters without melacha, which is forbidden for individual need because of "and speak a matter"; this was permitted to speak for a mitzvah or for communal need [Magen Avraham and Shaarei Teshuvah]; and see below siman 307 s.k. 5 and above siman 276 seif 2 in the gloss.
(29) And to arrange the child — it should read: the young girl.
(30) Or a trade — for he too is engaged in mitzvah, for if he has no trade he engages in theft.
(31) But to hire him, etc. — as above seif 3 in the gloss, as the opinion of those who forbid there.
(32) How much will he give — rather he should say stam "say on behalf of one who blessed."
(33) To pledge tzedakah — and even the chazan if wealthy, since they give him this so he prays before the congregation, it is mitzvah need. And regarding announcing mitzvot in the synagogue — some forbid and some permit, for buying and selling does not apply except to an acquired object; and where they practiced leniency one should not protest. And acquisition of places in the synagogue, and likewise acquisition of etrogim from the congregation after their mitzvah is finished, when they acquire them for eating or smelling — according to all forbidden; and likewise to buy a shofar or etrog on Yom Tov from the seller of etrogim — forbidden [Magen Avraham and Shaarei Teshuvah]; and see below siman 323 seif 4. And behold buying and selling, whether by mouth or by handing over, is forbidden by decree lest he come to writing. And likewise forbidden to give a gift to his fellow, for it resembles buying and selling, since it leaves his domain; but as a gift it is permitted when for Shabbat and Yom Tov need, as written siman 323 seif 7; and likewise for mitzvah need; and likewise forbidden to give collateral to his fellow unless for mitzvah or Shabbat need [Magen Avraham in the name of the poskim]; and he wrote further that therefore what they are accustomed to give utensils as a gift to the groom who expounds is not proper; however what they obligate somewhat to give him money — perhaps permitted, since it is in his power to retract; and see Avnei Rozel.
