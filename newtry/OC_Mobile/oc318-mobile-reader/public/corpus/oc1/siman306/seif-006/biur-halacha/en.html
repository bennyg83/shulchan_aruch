And to teach him a book or a trade — know that the tannaim disputed this matter in the Gemara: some hold that a father must teach his son Torah and also teach him a trade [and likewise if he teaches him to know commerce — so too in She'iltot]; and the tanna R' Nehorai said: I leave every trade in the world and teach my son only Torah, for all trades in the world stand for a person in his youth, and in old age he dies cast in hunger; but Torah stands for a person in his youth and in his old age, as it says, etc.; nevertheless, this one who teaches his son a trade — all agree he must teach him from the outset, and also while he is occupied in his craft, Torah and fear of Heaven; for otherwise he will find in his craft itself many matters of theft prohibition, and also many other pitfalls and breach of religion entirely, God forbid; and especially in our days, when everyone scatters to many places where observant Israel is not found — if fear of Torah and observance of mitzvot is not fixed in his soul from youth, he is liable, God forbid, to desecrate Shabbat in complete melachot for which there is liability of stoning. And even in Torah study, which is known to shield [from suffering] and pray from sin — they said: any Torah without work [and likewise any occupation is also included in work, as stated in She'iltot] — in the end it comes to naught and draws sin; how much more so study of craft without Torah — certainly it draws many sins and also breach of religion entirely, God forbid:

And specifically to speak if he wishes to hire, etc. — behold, from this wording it appears that to hire him without qualification, even without mentioning a sum, is forbidden; and from the end of the passage it appears the opposite — that specifically when mentioning a sum it is forbidden; and see Biur haGra, who noted this, as above in seif 3 in the gloss; and likewise in Beit Yosef he hung this on the aforementioned — it appears at first glance likewise that specifically when mentioning a sum it is forbidden — needs study:
