Accounts — and permitted to calculate what he needs for a mitzvah meal [Ketubot 5a].

And to pledge tzedakah — and even though one may not dedicate, as written siman 339 seif 4 — that is a known object, for it appears like buying and selling; but they did not forbid to obligate oneself by speech to the sacred; but it is difficult to me: behold when one assigns valuation upon oneself he also obligates himself and it is forbidden — end of Ran's words; and on Beit Yosef two [words]: and according to him forbidden to dedicate a Torah scroll on Shabbat for the synagogue; but in Kol Bo he wrote that specifically dedication for the altar or Temple maintenance is forbidden, for it leaves his domain to the domain of the sacred; but one who vows a Torah scroll or crown is permitted, for they have not yet designated anything leaving their domain — end of his words; it appears to me his intent is that our dedications are profane, as written in Choshen Mishpat siman 95, and every Israelite has a share in them; if so, it did not leave his domain, for the dedicator also has a share in it, as above — not like Beit Yosef.

And to supervise — Maggid Mishnah ch. 24 wrote: telling a gentile and not another shevut for communal affairs was not permitted — only supervision and involvement in matters without melacha, which is forbidden for individual need because of "and speak a matter"; and this was permitted to speak for a mitzvah or for communal need — end of his words; and so siman 339 seif 4.

Or a trade — for he too is a mitzvah, for if he has no trade he engages in theft.

How much will he give — rather he should say stam "do for me one who blessed."

To pledge tzedakah — and nevertheless it requires study: if the chazan is wealthy, then it is not considered tzedakah; and it should be said: since they give him this so he will pray, it is mitzvah need; as when they pledge money for synagogue maintenance: Yeshuot Yaakov ch. 5 of Beitzah wrote: announcing mitzvot — whoever gives more acquires it; it appears like extra buying and selling, and it would seem forbidden unless it is said that buying and selling does not apply to a mitzvah matter, only to an acquired object; and all this is only to settle the custom under pressure; but acquisition of places in synagogue, and likewise acquisition of etrogim from the congregation after their mitzvah is finished, is forbidden [and during their mitzvah he does not acquire the etrog itself, only to fulfill with it, even on the first day, for he must return it; but to buy an etrog or shofar on Yom Tov — it appears to me forbidden; it deals with 586]; and clear to me: as the pious of Austria practiced, whatever he stated orally to give, he gives to tzedakah, even if his fellow acquired the mitzvah — if so, it is like cutting sums for mitzvah need, and not buying and selling at all, and permitted even acquisition of places and houses from tzedakah; and every God-fearing person should be careful in this — end of his words; and in Shaarei Tzedek on tractate Eruvin 22b he also questioned acquisition of mitzvot; and in tractate Bava Kamma he wrote a person can sell his matzot to another: buying and selling, one by mouth and one by handing over, is forbidden by decree lest he write [Rambam ch. 23]; Beit Yosef wrote siman 527 that Mordechai holds forbidden to give something as a gift to his fellow on Yom Tov and Shabbat unless for mitzvah need — end of his words; and plain for Shabbat need it is permitted, as written siman 323 and 526; and so in Eruvin 71a that acquiring permission on Shabbat is forbidden even without a kinyan act [and in Choshen Mishpat small seif 254]; and so in Gittin 77a that even to acquire by chazakah is forbidden — see there in Rosh; and so Ritva in Ketubot 4a: they seized movable property for her ketubah — meaning from erev Shabbat; for on Shabbat forbidden, for it is as one who acquires, acquisition on Shabbat forbidden even for a mitzvah matter unless the hour is pressed, etc. — end of his words; and this is correct; but not needed, for here it is not full collateral, only that he should rely on it; and it is like the case of one who borrows for wine and leaves his cloak with him — end of his words; it implies even collateral forbidden to give for a profane matter; and so in Yalkut parashat Beha'alotekha 216: even though it counts "gives collateral and takes" among Pesach matters that override Shabbat — it implies forbidden for a profane matter; see nearby; and moreover behold hekdesh resembles a gift and is forbidden; and so in Rif ch. 5 of Yom Tov and Rambam end of Laws of Sale; and it deals with 307 seif 11 what he wrote; and if so it requires study what they are accustomed to give utensils as a gift to the groom who expounds on Shabbat;
