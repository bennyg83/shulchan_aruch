Accounts. It is permitted to calculate what is needed for a mitzvah meal, as stated in Kesubos 5a.
And to pledge charity. Although one may not consecrate, as written in siman 339, seif 4, that refers to a specific object, which appears like buying and selling. But they did not forbid obligating oneself by speech to the sacred. However, the Ran writes that this is difficult, for when one vows his own valuation he also obligates himself, and that is forbidden. See the Beit Yosef, who answers this. According to him, it is forbidden to consecrate a plate on Shabbos for the synagogue. But the Kol Bo wrote that only consecration for the altar or for Temple maintenance is forbidden, since it leaves his domain and enters the domain of the Most High; however, one who vows a Torah scroll or a crown is permitted, because they have not yet designated any particular item that leaves their possession. It appears to me that his intent is that our consecrations are non-sacred property, as written in Choshen Mishpat siman 95, and all Israel has a share in them. If so, it has not left his domain, since the one who consecrates also has a share in it. This appears correct, unlike the Beit Yosef.
And to supervise. The Maggid Mishneh, chapter 24, wrote that instructing a non-Jew, or any other shevus, was not permitted for communal affairs alone. What was permitted is only supervision and involvement in matters without performing melacha, which is forbidden for individual needs because of the verse concerning weekday speech. This was permitted for a mitzvah or for communal needs, and this is compelled by the Gemara. So too it appears from siman 339, seif 4.
Or a trade. This too is a mitzvah, for if he has no trade he will engage in theft.
How much he will give. Rather, he should say generally, ‘Make a Mi Shebeirach for me.’
To pledge charity. Nevertheless, it requires study: if the chazzan is wealthy, this is not considered charity. One must say that since they give it to him so that he will pray, it is a mitzvah need, similar to pledging money to build a synagogue. The Yam Shel Shlomo, fifth chapter of Beitzah, wrote that announcing mitzvos, that whoever gives more will merit them, resembles buying and selling more strongly, and it would have seemed forbidden, unless we say that buying and selling does not apply to a mitzvah matter, but only to an object being acquired. All this is only to justify the custom with difficulty. But purchasing seats in the synagogue, and likewise purchasing the esrogim from the congregation after their mitzvah is completed, is forbidden. During the time of their mitzvah he does not acquire the esrog itself, but only the right to fulfill the mitzvah with it, even on the first day, for he must return it. But to buy an esrog or a shofar on Yom Tov appears to me forbidden; see siman 586. The clearest practice in my eyes is like the custom of the pious men of Austria, that whatever one verbally undertook to give, he gives to charity even if his fellow acquired the mitzvah. If so, it is like fixing sums for the sake of a mitzvah and is not comparable to buying and selling at all. It is therefore permitted even to purchase seats and houses from charity funds, but every G-d-fearing person should be careful about this. The Shiltei Gibborim, in Avodah Zarah, also questions the purchase of mitzvos, and in tractate Bava Kamma he wrote that a person may sell his mitzvos to another. Buying and selling, whether verbally or through transfer, is forbidden as a decree lest one write, as the Rambam states in chapter 23. The Beit Yosef wrote in siman 527 that the Mordechai holds that it is forbidden to give an item as a gift to his fellow on Yom Tov or Shabbos unless it is for a mitzvah need. It is obvious that for Shabbos needs it is permitted, as written in simanim 323 and 516. So too it appears from Eruvin 71a, that acquiring rights on Shabbos is forbidden even without a kerchief acquisition. See Choshen Mishpat at the end of siman 254. So too it appears from Gittin 77a, that even acquiring through taking possession is forbidden; see the Rosh there. The Ritva likewise wrote in Kesubos 4a concerning giving movable property as security for her kesubah, meaning that he gave it to her on Shabbos.
