[7] And he may say, etc. Olat Tamid wonders why Shulchan Aruch limits this to a mitzvah need, since for an optional purpose siman 307, seif 8, permits saying, "Go to such-and-such city, etc." This is surprising, because both laws appear separately in the Gemara on 150a and in Tur. The difficulty therefore would be on the Gemara and Tur themselves. The answer is simple: here the statement is worse because he asks the person to bring him a particular object, whereas below the case concerns merely walking somewhere.
[8] Tomorrow, etc. But on Shabbos itself they did not permit it even through a non-Jew, even if he instructed him before Shabbos to do it on Shabbos (Beit Yosef; Ran). If the non-Jew did it on Shabbos, the item is prohibited. Raavan, siman 388, writes likewise.
[9] And this is primary, etc. Rama marks this because Ran, Mordechai, Maggid Mishneh, and Hagahot Maimoniyot hold so, as Beit Yosef indeed writes. It surprises me, however, that only a rabbinic melachah is prohibited, whereas regarding something forbidden merely because of "pursuing your affairs" they agree it is permitted for a mitzvah. Rashi on Beitzah 36b explains buying and selling as prohibited because of "pursuing your affairs" and "speaking words." According to Rashi's second explanation, that it may lead to writing a document, the matter depends on another dispute, but perhaps all agree that for a mitzvah even a rabbinic prohibition remains prohibited. Another difficulty is that in seif 6 Shulchan Aruch and Levush state without qualification that mentioning the purchase price is prohibited, whereas here Rama and Levush present a dispute. Beit Yosef explicitly appears to treat them alike. This can be resolved by explaining seif 6 as a case where mentioning the price is unnecessary, even though Beit Yosef appears to prohibit it even when necessary. Shulchan Aruch can nevertheless be interpreted this way. See seif katan 18.
[Letter 9*] [Levush] Immediately on Shabbos, etc. This implies that telling him to go tomorrow is permitted, as above. Even if he goes today, what does that matter, since the Jew did not instruct him to go today, as explained above.
[10] [Levush] So that she not become distraught, etc. According to this, it should be permitted even through a Jew when a non-Jew is unavailable, and certainly for the relatives themselves. Moreover, Mordechai compares it to making an acquisition on Shabbos, which is rabbinic, and techumin are likewise rabbinic. Issur VeHeter HaAruch, rule 59, permits hiring a messenger for him, etc. Agudah, chapter HaShoel, writes without qualification that one sends for his relatives and does not mention a non-Jew. It likewise speaks simply of a sick person and does not require that his condition have suddenly worsened. The same appears in Hagahot Maimoniyot, chapter 2, and Piskei Recanati, siman 103.
