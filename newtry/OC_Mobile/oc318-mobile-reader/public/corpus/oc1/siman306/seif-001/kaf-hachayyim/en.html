1) [Seif 1] Even regarding something for which he performs no labor there. If a non-Jew shows a Jewish person a gold coin on Shabbos and asks him to say whether it is good or bad, deficient or whole, and how much it is worth, it is absolutely forbidden to tell him on Shabbos. This is considered a commercial activity and is forbidden on Shabbos. Lev Chaim, volume 3, siman 70; see there. Orach Chaim, paragraph 1.
2) There: Such as inspecting his property, etc. This applies specifically when his purpose is recognizable, as stated in siman 307, seif 9; see there. Magen Avraham, paragraph 1; Tosefes Shabbos, paragraph 1. Nishmas Adam, klal 60, however, writes that directly pursuing one's affairs, such as inspecting one's property, is forbidden even within the techum and even when the purpose is not recognizable. He writes that this is the view of Tosafos, Rambam, Sefer Mitzvos Gadol, Tur, and the Shulchan Aruch; see there. If his purpose is entirely unrecognizable and he does not complete his objective on Shabbos, but merely positions himself so that he will be ready after Shabbos, it is permitted. Chayei Adam there, paragraph 1.
3) Therefore, a person may not walk through his fields and gardens to see what they require or how their produce is developing, and the same applies to his other property. He thereby engages in his affairs on Shabbos, and it is forbidden even when his purpose is entirely unrecognizable. Chayei Adam there, paragraph 2.
4) There: Or walking to the city gate, etc. This is because bathhouses were outside the wall, as stated in the first chapter of Megillah, folio 6b; see there. Bach; Perishah, paragraph 1.
5) There: Or walking to the city gate, etc. This is forbidden even within the techum. Tosafos there explains that his purpose is recognizable: he is not strolling but intends to enter a bathhouse after Shabbos, and bathhouses were ordinarily located at the city gate. Regarding the other cases, such as hiring workers or bringing produce, only walking to the end of the techum is forbidden, while walking within it is permitted. Olas Shabbos, paragraph 1; Eliyah Rabbah, paragraph 2.
6) There: Likewise, one may not wait until nightfall at the edge of the techum, etc. This means approaching the edge of the techum on Shabbos and waiting there until nightfall so that he will be near the workers. He may, however, wait until nightfall for something permitted on Shabbos, such as bringing his animal. Although the animal is outside the techum and may not be brought on Shabbos, he may call it, as stated in seif 2. Even if it is so far beyond the techum that it cannot hear his voice, since a possible permitted method exists in the world if there were watchmen's huts, he may wait there even though no such huts exist. This law is explained at length at the end of siman 203 in Taz; see there. Levush Serad. If a river interrupts the route, one can say, 'If only there were a bridge there.' Mishbetzos Zahav, paragraph 1. See siman 339, seif 7.
7) There: To bring his animal. This is because an independently permitted method exists: if watchmen's huts were present, he could walk and bring it. Taz, paragraph 1; Magen Avraham, paragraph 2. Since watchmen's huts would provide a permitted solution, he may wait until nightfall even when none are present. Tosefes Shabbos, paragraph 3. Regarding a lamb, however, which he could not bring even if actual partitions were present because an animal may not be moved, he may not wait until nightfall for it. Olas Shabbos, paragraph 3. Regarding watchmen's huts, see below, siman 398, seif 6.
8) There: Some say that if the animal is not, etc. Levush does not introduce this as 'some say,' but states it without qualification. Eliyah Rabbah, paragraph 4, writes that this is correct because all opinions appear to agree; see there. Tosefes Shabbos, paragraph 4, writes the same. Although no dissenting opinion is found, the Shulchan Aruch commonly introduces a law not found in other halachic authorities with the words 'some say,' as Sema writes in Choshen Mishpat, siman 16; see there. Machazik Berachah, paragraph 4, writes the same; see there. Mishbetzos Zahav, paragraph 1; Maamar Mordechai.
9) There, gloss: The same applies regarding strolling, etc. This implies that it is forbidden even within the techum. From the words of Tosafos cited above in paragraph 5, however, that walking within the techum is permitted when one's purpose is not recognizable, it is possible to permit strolling in order to obtain a horse, etc. Olas Shabbos, paragraph 2. Magen Avraham, paragraph 3, similarly writes that it is forbidden only when the purpose is recognizable. Eliyah Rabbah, paragraph 3, writes the same: only at the end of the techum or when the purpose is recognizable. Tosefes Shabbos, paragraph 2, writes the same. Chayei Adam, klal 60, paragraph 2, however, writes that this is considered engaging in one's affairs on Shabbos and is forbidden even when the purpose is entirely unrecognizable. Nishmas Adam there writes the same; see there.
10) There, gloss: The same applies regarding strolling, etc. Those who customarily refrain from strolling in the daily gathering place of merchants act properly. There is support for this in Pesachim 51: one may not sit upon the benches of non-Jews in Akko. Responsa She'eilas Yaavetz, siman 167; Birkei Yosef, paragraph 1.
11) One who went there to seek payment from a non-Jewish debtor and pressured him to provide a guarantor or security can be judged favorably. The aforementioned authority; Birkei Yosef, paragraph 2.
12) If the aforementioned act caused a mishap and some desecration of Hashem's Name, the community may fine him even though he committed no prohibition. The aforementioned authority; Birkei Yosef, paragraph 3.
13) Insuring a ship in which one has vast wealth is forbidden even through a non-Jew. The aforementioned authority there; Birkei Yosef, paragraph 4.
14) Responsa Rabbi Akiva Eiger, siman 159, questions whether one who performs an act of acquisition on Friday subject to a condition may fulfill that condition on Shabbos, since this is like performing the acquisition on Shabbos. His grandson cites this in Responsa Ksav Sofer, siman 46, and himself concludes that it is forbidden even to perform the acquisition on Friday in a manner that causes it to take effect automatically on Shabbos. All the more so, when an acquisition is made contingent upon fulfilling a condition, fulfilling that condition on Shabbos is forbidden; see there. Responsa She'eilas Shmuel, siman 26, distinguishes as follows. If on Friday he said that the acquisition takes effect retroactively from now upon fulfillment of the condition, he may fulfill the condition on Shabbos, because the sale takes effect retroactively from Friday. If he merely said 'if,' so that the acquisition is completed when the condition is fulfilled, he may not fulfill the condition on Shabbos and thereby complete the sale. It is also obvious that this constitutes pursuing one's affairs; see there. Accordingly, it appears that the case using 'from now' is permitted only when the purpose is unrecognizable. Otherwise, it is forbidden because of pursuing one's affairs. Pischei Teshuvah.
