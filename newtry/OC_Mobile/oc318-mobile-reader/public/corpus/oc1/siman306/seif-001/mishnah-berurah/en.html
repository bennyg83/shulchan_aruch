(1) Examining his property — and specifically where the matter is recognizable, such as standing by his field that needs plowing or harvesting and the like, for the thing is recognizable that he stands there to examine its needs; and likewise to go and wait at the town gate, since it is customary that bathhouses are outside the town near the town gate, it is recognizable that for the bathhouse need he waits; but if the matter is not recognizable, it is in the category of reflection on his business affairs, which is permitted.

(2) One may not wait, etc. — that is, to bring himself close on Shabbat until the end of the techum and wait there so he will be close to the place of the workers to hire them on motzei Shabbat; and the reason is that everything forbidden to do on Shabbat, even if it is a rabbinic prohibition, is forbidden to wait at dusk for it.

(3) To bring his animal — for even though it now stands outside the techum and it is impossible to bring it on Shabbat, nevertheless this is not called waiting at dusk for a forbidden thing, since if there were watchmen [burgin — booths of the guards] close to one another within seventy amot and four handbreadths, it would be permitted to bring it even if it extended this way several mil from the end of the town, because all this relates to the town; and since there is a remedy through watchmen, therefore it is permitted to wait at dusk even without watchmen, for waiting at dusk is forbidden only for something for which no remedy can be found for the prohibition.

(4) And some say that if, etc. — all agree on this, and he used the language "and some say" because thus is the Mechaber's manner in a place where this law is not explained explicitly among other poskim.

(5) That he is not permitted to bring — meaning even if there were complete partitions it would in any case be forbidden because of muktzeh, and it is found that he waits at dusk for a thing forbidden to do on Shabbat, and it is forbidden.

(6) That it is forbidden to move — and also in this there is no prohibition because of "pursuing your affairs," only when the thing is recognizable that he intends for his needs, or when he waits at dusk at the end of the techum for this; but if it is not recognizable, only as one going to stroll, it is permitted even though his intent in this going is to find a horse and the like [Acharonim].
