"Examines his property," etc., or "goes," etc. — that is, even within the techum — so all the Acharonim agreed. And behold, in Mishna Berurah I copied that specifically where the matter is evident — and that is from Magen Avraham and the other Acharonim who all agreed thus, and not like Chayei Adam, who was stringent in this in every case. And behold, I saw in Nishmat Adam there, who elaborated on this and wrote that this law of Magen Avraham depends on a dispute: this is only according to Ramban and Rashba and Ritva; but Rambam and Semag appear not to hold thus, only that going and examining property is actually making one's business affairs, and forbidden in every case. And what he challenged there from Eruvin (38:) from a braita, "a person should not walk at the end of his field," etc. — he did not challenge except from the end there; and on this he answered there: the matter is evident; but that which is "goes and examines his field" — we do not require that the matter be evident specifically, for in every case it is forbidden; and in truth, even if for him it is as the words of Rambam and Semag, behold it is a rabbinic matter, and we can rely to be lenient upon all these many authorities who are lenient explicitly; and also the Ran in the chapter "One who waited at dusk," s.v. "one may not stroll," wrote explicitly likewise; and also in Rashi in Eruvin 39, s.v. "the matter is evident," it is evident that he holds like the plain sense of the sugya — that the Gemara's difficulty there was on both of them, and on both he answered there "the matter is evident"; behold, also when walking at the end of his field to know what it needs — we forbid only because the matter is evident, and not like Chayei Adam. [And what he wrote that from the Shulchan Aruch too it appears they are stringent in every case — I did not know his proof, for behold this law flows from that which is stated in the Gemara: "a person should not walk at the end of his field to know what it needs"; and it appears there in Rashi, s.v. "evident," that when he stands and looks at the matter of his field it is included in "the matter is evident"; and from this, that the Shulchan Aruch forbade without qualification to examine property, is also in a manner where presumably the matter is evident, and like the Gemara's wording there "the matter is evident"; but if in truth it is not evident at all — it is permitted.] And also one who examines according to Rambam and Semag there itself — there is no proof that they dispute all these many authorities who are lenient explicitly; and why should we make a dispute between them? And therefore I ruled as the view of all the Acharonim who copied like the words of Magen Avraham:

And likewise one may not walk at dusk, etc. — Gemara 150; and see below in siman 397 seif 9 in Magen Avraham s.k. 13, that here it is forbidden even where the matter is not evident — because of this; and the reason — see there; and many Acharonim wrote that the reason for the prohibition is because walking at dusk itself presumably indicates that he intends to do something forbidden on Shabbat:
