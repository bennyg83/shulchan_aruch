[1] [Levush] What they require, etc. Or how their fruits ripen (Semag).
[2] At the boundary, etc. Specifically at the end of the techum, where his intent is evident. Within the techum it is permitted. The case of standing at the city entrance is likewise evident because the bathhouses are before the city gate (Tosafot, Shabbos 150a). Maggid Mishneh, chapter 24, adds that if he stands inside a field needing plowing or cultivation, his purpose is evident. Magen Avraham in siman 307, seif katan 13, objects from Eruvin 39a, which explicitly says that even at the edge of the techum his purpose is not evident, because if he is a Torah scholar we say his study drew him there, and if he is an ignoramus, etc. In my humble opinion that passage concerns one who waits on Yom Tov that falls on Friday in order to establish an eruv, where his purpose is not apparent. But if he waits on Shabbos close to nightfall, we certainly infer that he intends to prepare for weekday activity. Magen Avraham's objection from Rashi, that it is prohibited to position oneself for something forbidden on Shabbos, likewise refers to a case in which the purpose is evident. Proof is that making an eruv itself is prohibited on Shabbos. His concluding remarks there are also unclear.
[3] To find a horse, etc. Specifically at the end of the techum, or wherever his purpose is evident, as above.
[4] [Levush] And specifically, etc. Shulchan Aruch cites this in the name of "some say." Levush correctly states it without qualification, because it appears to me that all authorities agree. Proof is from the Gemara on Shabbos 150a, which answers that the case concerns rotten straw that is muktzeh.
