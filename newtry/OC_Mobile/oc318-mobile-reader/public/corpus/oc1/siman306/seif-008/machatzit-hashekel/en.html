(s.k. 8) And some, etc. — like regarding wage of translators, for they said in the chapter "The place where they practiced" daf 50: four perutot have no sign of blessing, and one of them is wage of translators. Rashi explained: they stand before the sage on Shabbatot and hear from his mouth words of Torah and announce to the many — end of his words; even though the translators do a mitzvah and their wage according to this view is permitted, nevertheless he does not see a sign of blessing. However below siman 585 the Beit Yosef wrote: one who takes wage to blow shofar on Rosh HaShanah, or in order to pray, or to translate on Shabbatot and Yom Tov — he does not see from that wage a sign of blessing; and he wrote on this Magen Avraham, thus: but there is no prohibition, since it is mitzvah need — end of his words; and the Peri Chadash questioned him there, and likewise questioned in this siman Sefer Teshuvah — behold the general rule, for the Rama holds everywhere the Beit Yosef's essential first view; if so, since here he brought the first view that forbids wage of chazanim, how did Magen Avraham write plainly that there is no prohibition. And in my humble opinion there is no difficulty, and first I will explain whence Magen Avraham holds that the Beit Yosef below siman 585 holds there is no prohibition — and this is because the Tur below complained about the custom of Spain that they flee from the mitzvah of blowing shofar to the many until they need to hire one from the market to blow, and he did not know whence they found permission for this, for it appears to him forbidden, etc., that it is forbidden to take Shabbat wage; and the Beit Yosef brought in the name of Mordechai who wrote in the name of Rashi permission for chazanim, for they give them wage to fulfill the mitzvah to pray; and the Beit Yosef questioned: is it in order to fulfill mitzvot to desecrate Shabbat. And he answered: since Rashi in Ketubot regarding a rebellious wife wrote the reason for prohibition of Shabbat wage is a decree because of buying and selling — and behold buying and selling itself is only a rabbinic prohibition — how was Shabbat wage decreed because of buying and selling, it would be a decree upon a decree; or there is no prohibition at all, only a mere stringency. And therefore to fulfill the mitzvah it is permitted; and he wrote further: and so in the chapter "The place where they practiced" the Sages taught: four perutot have no sign of blessing, and one of them is wage of translators; and the reason the Gemara explains is because it is Shabbat wage; and impossible that there is any prohibition in the matter, for otherwise it would not say "have no sign of blessing," which implies there is no prohibition, only they do not see a sign of blessing, etc. — end of his words; and if so, it turns out that the Tur and those who stand in his view who hold that even in a place of mitzvah there is prohibition — therefore they did not consider this at all as a proof from what the chapter "The place where they practiced" states, that he does not see a sign of blessing, and it did not appear to them to infer from it that there is no prohibition; and therefore it is plain in my eyes that the Tur and those with him that this is not at all a necessary inference; but the Beit Yosef, since he himself wrote that the inference of Rashi in Mordechai is from what the chapter "The place where they practiced" states "have no sign of blessing," which implies there is no prohibition — therefore impossible that the Beit Yosef holds the Tur's view that even in a place of mitzvah there is prohibition; it would not have been for him to be silent and write "he does not see a sign of blessing," and place a stumbling block before the examiner lest he infer from this that there is no prohibition, as Rashi inferred from this in the chapter "The place where they practiced"; and all the more the Beit Yosef should have explained that there is also prohibition and say that the Beit Yosef relied on himself on what is written in this siman here that there is prohibition — it appears to Magen Avraham strained; nevertheless the truth is so, that the Beit Yosef below holds there is no prohibition; and that which they questioned — behold here he brought the first view of those who forbid — one can answer according to what Shach wrote Yoreh Deah 62 Rama 2 in general rules of Orach Chaim: if the Mechaber adopted everywhere the essential first view, for what purpose did he bring a second view; and one of his answers is that there is a difference in a rabbinic prohibition in a time of pressure — one may rely on the second view; if so, also here it appears to the Beit Yosef that in a time of pressure one may rely on the view of those who permit to give wage to fulfill the mitzvah; and if so it comes out well what the Beit Yosef holds below that there is no prohibition in the wage they give to the blower, for there is a time of pressure; and in what the Tur wrote that they did not find one who would wish to blow until they needed to hire one to blow — there too is a time of pressure; and moreover impossible for the congregation to find a blower; otherwise why should they spend the expense of a sage and also bring themselves into a matter of prohibition; it would be for one who wishes to blow to blow for free; therefore it is a case where they do not find a blower for free — if so, you have no greater time of pressure than this, and one may rely on the view of those who permit; unlike here, where the essential view is like those who forbid wage of chazanim, even though there too it deals with a case where they do not find one who will pray for free (even though it is possible someone would wish to pray for free, but his voice is not as pleasant as one who wants wage, as our eyes see); but even if it is a real time of pressure that they have no one at all who will pray for free, nevertheless there is prohibition; nevertheless there is a difference in time of pressure, for they can hire him with swallowing, for certainly there is no prohibition, unlike regarding blowing where swallowing does not apply — it is called a time of pressure, as above; and even though the Beit Yosef generally below siman 585 together with the blower also prayer and translation, and regarding them there is no time of pressure, for it is possible with prayer through swallowing, as above — nevertheless since he wrote "he does not see a sign of blessing" and did not write prohibition, since he grouped them together and he does not see a sign of blessing — behold it includes all three; unlike prohibition, which does not include all three, for regarding the blower there would be prohibition, and likewise the translator; and he wished to write a matter common and including all of them.

And all the more there siman 585 the essential law is the blower, and regarding the blower there is no prohibition, as above; and therefore he only wrote "he does not see a sign of blessing" and did not write prohibition.
