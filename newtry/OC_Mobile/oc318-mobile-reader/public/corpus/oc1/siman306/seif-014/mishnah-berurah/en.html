(56) That they took out his daughter — he mentioned custom of the matter, and likewise another may desecrate Shabbat to save her from the great prohibition; and all this deals when they took her out, a gentile from his house by coercion, and therefore they permitted him to set his feet on the path to save her, for we hold if he is not negligent he is obligated to do a small prohibition so his fellow should not do a great prohibition; but if she was negligent, her father has no [obligation] to desecrate Shabbat for her, for we do not say to a person "sin so your fellow may gain merit"; and specifically for a d'oraisa prohibition; but for a rabbinic prohibition, Avnei Rozel's opinion is to be lenient to transgress in order to save her.
(57) Beyond three parasangs — that is, even though there is in this a d'oraisa prohibition for some poskim; and likewise if he will need to desecrate Shabbat thereby with some of the 39 melachot, likewise fine, for when you will completely change the religion and desecrate Shabbat and serve idolatry all your days — if he desecrates Shabbat once it is called a small prohibition against this. Magen Avraham wrote: it requires study if the daughter is minor and they want to remove her from Israel, whether the adult will sin for her, for we hold the court is not commanded to separate her from prohibition; or perhaps like pikuach nefesh, that they desecrate Shabbat for the minor, saying "desecrate one Shabbat for him so he will observe many Shabbatot" — likewise here; and in Avnei Rozel his opinion is to be lenient that if he does not strive to save her she will remain thus also in adulthood.
(58) And see above siman 328 seif 10 — in the gloss where it is found that one they want to coerce to transgress a sin once they do not desecrate Shabbat to save him; and there is no contradiction here, for there it deals when they want to coerce him to transgress once, and therefore even if it is a great sin such as idolatry, one need not desecrate Shabbat to save him, for desecrating Shabbat is also like idolatry; unlike here where she will remain an apostate and desecrate Shabbat forever. Kenesset haGedolah wrote that to go beyond techum on Shabbat to avenge his father from murderers is permitted; and Magen Avraham and Shaarei Teshuvah wondered on him, for it is no better than other mitzvah need, that it is forbidden to go beyond techum for it.
